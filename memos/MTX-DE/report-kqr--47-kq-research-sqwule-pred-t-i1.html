Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. Over the next 18 months, the evidence favours MTU meeting or raising guidance rather than reducing it.

Initial FY2027 guidance is highly likely to exceed current consensus for both operating profit and free cash flow. Sell-side revision breadth looks set to stay flat in the near term and then turn modestly positive. The main route to a reduction is Geared Turbofan (GTF) fleet management, with V2500 volume erosion and unfavourable business mix as secondary risks. This is an analytical read of the evidence, not a recommendation.

What consensus assumes. The FactSet consensus of 19 November 2026 estimates revenue of €10,742 million, operating profit of €1,561 million, EPS of €20.51 and free cash flow of €733 million. For FY2026, consensus adjusted EBIT of €1,439 million sits near the top of the €1.35 billion to €1.45 billion guidance range. The consensus dividend is €4.50 per share, against €3.60 the prior year.

Little room remains for upside on FY2026 EBIT, so any beat will come from cash metrics and FY2027 framing. Track record. Since 2015, MTU has exceeded its initial operating profit guidance in eight of the last ten years. FY2024 adjusted EBIT of €1,050 million compared with initial guidance of €890 million.

In FY2026, the EBIT target was held at €1.35 billion to €1.45 billion while the cash conversion rate was raised twice. This pattern supports a "meet on earnings, raise on cash" base case. Run-rate quality. Nine-month adjusted EBIT was €1,042 million, up five percent.

It included an unadjusted customer bankruptcy charge of €25 million to €30 million. Jefferies noted that underlying commercial OEM EBIT was eight percent ahead of consensus excluding the charge. Headline guidance therefore understates earnings quality. Cash conversion above 60% is the most probable guidance surprise.

Powder-metal compensation payments are expected to end in 2026, cutting annual outflows by about $250 million from 2027 and pushing cash-conversion guidance well above sixty percent. The FY2026 target was already lifted to between 55% and 60%. Underlying free cash flow conversion was 80% in the first half of 2026.

Resolving the GTF conflict. The sections point in opposite directions on cash. They can be reconciled because compensation payments and pre-financed shop-visit drag are different cash items. Compensation is expected to stop after 2026, which supports the FY2027 cash guide.

The sluggish recovery path implies extended exposure to pre-financed shop-visit costs through 2028. The grounded fleet has stalled near 592 aircraft. The brief cites roughly 530, but the body evidence is used here. The €1.2 billion of pre-financed receivables only becomes a cash tailwind from 2029.

Cash guidance can therefore rise in 2027 without implying full normalisation. Contrary evidence includes Turkish Airlines' elevated grounded count persisting until mid-2027. 3 Peer read-across is supportive but not uniform. GE Aerospace expects commercial services to grow at a low double-digit rate in 2027. StandardAero's 2027 commercial capacity is already fully booked.

Against this, Safran's spare-parts growth slowed to 17% in the third quarter from 30% in the first half. Lufthansa Technik's 8.6% operating margin was considered light. HEICO's PMA parts carry a 30% to 40% discount to OEM list prices. Demand is firm.

The risk lies in pricing and mix, not volume. Mix is the main limiter on margins. The OEM segment missed third-quarter profit expectations on supply constraints, and group adjusted EBIT margin was 14.4%. Management lowered commercial OEM revenue growth guidance to a mid-to-high single-digit percentage.

This explains why management may guide conservatively even with strong demand. The 2030 targets are €13 billion to €14 billion of revenue and an adjusted EBIT margin of 14.5% to 15.5%. Together they imply growth without material margin expansion. Revisions and medium-term drivers.

Each five-cent move affects revenues by about €100 million. Buybacks are not a driver: they remain opportunistic and the 40% payout target is suspended. The convertible bond due 18 March 2027 is largely de-risked, since a new €600 million 2033 convertible repurchased 92.82% of the legacy debt.

Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. MTU Aero Engines guidance and consensus trajectories over the next eighteen months depend heavily on resolving the Geared Turbofan fleet management challenges.

Investment relevance and EPS trajectory

1.
Critical inquiry: "how likely is company guidance to be raised, met or reduced at the earnings events over the next 18 months, and how likely are sell-side consensus forecasts, and the net breadth of revisions, to move higher or lower over the same period?"
2.
Significant earnings impact: MTU Aero Engines is highly exposed to the aftermarket cycle, which makes up about 90% of its total profits. This high concentration means that any shifts in spare parts pricing or shop visit volumes will directly determine the company's earnings per share over the next three years. Consequently, investor sentiment remains highly sensitive to how aftermarket dynamics offset the normalization of new engine deliveries.
3.
Shifting investment focus: At MTU Aero Engines, the primary investment case is transitioning from a pure earnings recovery story into a long-term cash generation narrative. Progress in resolving the Geared Turbofan fleet management issues acts as a vital catalyst for cash flow progression through 2028. Accordingly, near-term capital allocation choices remain constrained by pre-financed engine shop visits that will drag on cash performance until the end of 2028.
4.
Projected margin contractions: Group operating margins at MTU Aero Engines are projected to contract slightly as the product mix shifts. Specifically, the original equipment manufacturer segment will face headwinds from a higher share of lower-margin series engine deliveries and a lower ratio of highly profitable spare engines. This mix normalization is expected to lower group margins by approximately 50 basis points over the 2025 to 2028 period.
5.
Prolonging financial pain: The company has entered a period of several years of severe financial pressure due to the Geared Turbofan powder metal issue. While MTU Aero Engines offers direct access to the attractive Airbus narrowbody segment, the cash costs of airline compensations have pressured its free cash flow. Consequently, the group has implemented dividend reductions to manage these significant liquidity burdens over the medium term.

Financial sensitivity and engine exposures

6.
Manageable risk outlook: MTU Aero Engines management considers the current risk situation driven by the fleet management plan to be entirely manageable through structured mitigation measures. The operational and financial impacts have been carefully integrated into the company's long-term corporate planning and public forecasts. Nonetheless, any unexpected escalation of these technical challenges could further impair net assets and cash generation.
7.
High geared turbofan exposure: MTU Aero Engines holds an 18% program share in the Pratt & Whitney Geared Turbofan engine family. This program represents the company's largest commercial engine venture, with over 14,000 orders and commitments placed globally. Consequently, the operational ramp-up of the GTF MRO network directly impacts the commercial maintenance segment, where the GTF accounted for approximately 45% of nine-month revenues.
8.
Resilient legacy aftermarket: The mature IAE V2500 engine program remains a highly resilient driver of aftermarket demand for MTU Aero Engines. Approximately half of the active V2500 fleet has not yet reached its second shop visit, which typically requires heavier work scopes and extensive parts replacement. This transition to second overhauls is expected to provide a solid pricing tailwind that supports segment profitability as the older fleet matures.
9.
Expanding widebody partnerships: MTU Aero Engines participates in GE Aerospace widebody programs, maintaining a 6.65 percent program share in the GEnx and a 10% share in the GE9X. The company manufactures key turbine center frames for these high-thrust engines, representing significant long-term production and maintenance volume. However, delays in the Boeing 777X entry-into-service have pushed back initial GE9X deliveries, negatively impacting near-term commercial original equipment revenue growth.
10.
Contrary evidence: Turkish Airlines expects a significant increase in groundings of its Airbus A320neo fleet in 2026 due to ongoing engine turnaround delays at Pratt & Whitney. This negative development contradicts broader market expectations that GTF aircraft-on-ground levels are steadily improving. Such persistent bottlenecks could prolong airline compensation payments and continue to drag on MTU Aero Engines' cash conversion rates.
11.
Other side of the story: While some airlines report worsening GTF groundings, RTX and MTU Aero Engines have demonstrated that their powder metal inspection plan remains firmly on track. A 39% increase in commercial maintenance output during late 2025 has driven stored fleet counts down by over 20% from their historical peaks. Therefore, the gradual resolution of the fleet management campaign is more likely to yield medium-term cash flow benefits than persistent financial liabilities.
