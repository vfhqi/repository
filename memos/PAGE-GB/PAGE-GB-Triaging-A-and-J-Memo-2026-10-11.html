[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = C

Thesis. PageGroup is a cyclical recovery with a self-help overlay, bought after its first leg: the company has right-sized for a trough it has been in for three years, the hiring cycle has turned in the United States, Asia Pacific and Southern Europe but not in France, Germany or the UK, and at 212.0p the shares have already paid for the part of the recovery that has happened. The mechanism is proven and the modal return of about 13 percent a year over eighteen months and 16 percent a year over three years is short of the 20 percent bar, because the second leg is a bet on European timing that this week's environment argues against.

  • The change that matters is external and cyclical, and it has turned in only half of the business.
    • White-collar permanent hiring demand is the one force that moves this company's earnings, and it is still falling in the markets that are about half of gross profit.
    • Group fees: gross profit fell 2.4 percent at constant currency in H1 2026 to 385.2 GBPm
      • The Q2 2026 decline narrowed to 0.2 percent from 4.9 percent in Q1 2026.
    • Europe: EMEA gross profit fell 6.9 percent at constant currency in H1 2026
      • France fell 13.0 percent in H1 and 12 percent in Q2
      • Germany fell 4 percent in Q2
      • The UK fell 8.2 percent to 91.1 GBPm.
      • EMEA was 53 percent of group gross profit in FY2025.
    • The markets that have turned: the United States recorded its seventh consecutive quarter of growth, accelerating to 5 percent in Q2 2026 from 1 percent in Q1, with conversion back to four offers in five
      • Asia Pacific gross profit rose 9.2 percent at constant currency in H1 2026 (Greater China up 14 percent, Japan up 17 percent)
      • Spain rose 9 percent and Italy 7 percent in Q2 2026.
  • The change inside the company is real, delivered and of self-help size, not transformational size.
    • It is what a good operator does in a trough, which makes the cyclical drop-through larger but does not drive the earnings on its own.
    • Cost base: about 40 GBPm of annualised savings since the strategy launch, confirmed by the chief financial officer at the H1 2026 results, on a cost base of about 750 GBPm (gross profit 769.5 GBPm less operating profit 20.9 GBPm, FY2025, our arithmetic), so about 5 percent.
    • Capacity: fee earners down 7.5 percent in FY2025 to 4,968 and a further 1.6 percent in Q2 2026 to 4,914, 24 to 27 percent below peak
      • Management de-layered in April 2025.
    • Perimeter: the clerical Page Personnel brand, 20 percent of net fees, exited in Latin America, Asia Pacific and the UK
      • 75 percent of fees now under the single Michael Page brand.
    • Price: permanent fee rates at record levels three years into a volume trough, average fees up 14 percent on mix.
    • What is absent: no chief executive change (Nicholas Kirk, an internal appointment, in his fourth year), no guidance increase (FY2026 guided in line with consensus at about 28 GBPm of operating profit), shareholder returns cut rather than raised (the interim dividend cut 72.8 percent to 1.46p).
  • 'Financial Outputs' have turned on the profit line and not yet on the fee line, cash or the balance sheet.
    • The engine of the case is operating gearing, and it is visible in two consecutive halves.
    • Profit: H1 2026 operating profit 9.7 GBPm from 2.1 GBPm in H1 2025
      • Conversion 2.5 percent from 0.5 percent.
    • Productivity: gross profit per fee earner up 3.7 percent at constant currency to 77.4 thousand GBP in H1 2026, the highest since 2022.
    • Cash and balance sheet: free cash flow was minus 13.5 GBPm in FY2025 on reported net income of 9.0 GBPm
      • Net cash of 31.4 GBPm at FY2025 became net debt of 7.2 GBPm at 30 June 2026 excluding leases
      • The minimum cash buffer was halved to 25 GBPm.
    • Sell-side: consensus earnings per share for FY2027 rose 14.3 percent over three months to 2026-10-02 after falling 45.7 percent over twelve
      • The raises are concentrated in FY2027 and FY2028, not in FY2026, so they are a bet on the turn, not a report of it.
  • Along the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain the internal change has reached the financial statements and the sell-side's estimates have begun to move; the external change has reached the operating metrics in half the markets and has not reached the company's actions at all in France, Germany or the UK, because the company can only wait for it.
    • The layer that decides the case is European demand, which the company does not control.
  • What we think it earns: earnings per share of 2.9p in FY2025 rising to 4.4p in FY2026 (the guided path), 7.9p in FY2027, 13.5p in FY2028 and 17.2p in FY2029, on a modal path in which France and Germany return to gross profit growth in the first half of 2028 rather than in 2027.
    • Against consensus: our FY2027 figure of 7.9p sits below the consensus median of 10.1p, because consensus needs gross profit growth of about 3 percent and conversion of 6.6 percent in FY2027, which the United States and Asia cannot deliver alone.
    • The engine: conversion from 2.7 percent in FY2025 to 5.4 percent in FY2027 and 8.2 percent in FY2028 on our path, applying management's own 70 percent flow-through from gross profit to operating profit in FY2027, about 50 percent in FY2028 and about 35 percent from FY2029, because growing fees beyond current capacity needs consultant hiring that dilutes margins.
  • What it costs: 212.0p, or 14.8x the 24-month-forward consensus earnings per share of 14.36p, the 80th percentile of the stock's five-year range and above its ten-year median of 13.8x
    • On our own 24-month-forward earnings of 12.1p the multiple is 17.5x, above the ten-year 75th percentile of 16.1x.
    • Already priced: the shares rose 60.1 percent over six months and 37.9 percent over three on the Q2 2026 beat of 13 July 2026 and Deutsche Bank's target raise to 355p of 9 September 2026
      • The cost savings, the United States and Asian conversion recovery, the Southern European turn and a European turn in 2027 are in the price.
    • Not priced: a European turn that slips to 2028, which is our modal path
      • And, in the other direction, a full return to the 16 percent through-cycle conversion, which is the bull branch.
  • The 'Modal return' is a C at this price, and earnings growth carries all of it.
    • At the stock's ten-year median 24-month-forward multiple of 13.8x, the eighteen-month value including cash returns is 254.8p (plus 20.2 percent, 13.0 percent a year) and the three-year value 334.4p (plus 57.7 percent, 16.4 percent a year), against the 20 percent bar.
    • The swing factor: at today's 14.8x held, the eighteen-month rate is 15.6 percent a year
      • At the ten-year 35th percentile of 12.3x it is 4.8 percent a year.
    • The cost base has no margin: if the incremental flow-through is 55, 40 and 28 percent instead of 70, 50 and 35 percent, with tax at 38 percent instead of 33 percent, the eighteen-month return is minus 3.9 percent a year and the three-year return 7.4 percent a year, both F on the framework's anchors, and this Building Block would fall to D.
    • Where the case clears the bar: at about 194p on the modal path at 13.8x
      • At about 173p on the conservative 12.3x anchor.
  • The pattern it fits is 'Trough on Trough', in shape rather than crisply.
    • Every condition that reads the company passes at B or A
    • The two mandatory conditions that read the price, a bottom-quartile multiple and capitulation, fail, because the share price moved first.
    • The pattern was there, and the entry was six months ago.

  • The case therefore is a late-timed cyclical recovery whose mechanism is proven, whose first leg is in the price, and whose second leg depends on French and German hiring turning within eighteen months.

    • It is compelling in mechanism and not in price.

  • Rating = C, because the change is material in direction but not yet in size at this price: the internal forces are delivered and the operating gearing is real ('Change Forces' at C), the profit line has turned while the fee line and cash have not and the sell-side is ahead of the European evidence ('Financial Outputs' at C), and the modal return sits at 13 to 16 percent a year, in the C band and short of the bar ('Modal return' at C).

    • The parts combine without one binding: no Element is at D, none above C, and the case for each is the same case, a cyclical recovery whose first leg is in the price.
    • Not D: D was live on the external force reading at D
    • The mechanism from conversion to profit is proven in the United States and in the FY2021 recovery, when adjusted EBIT rose from 22.2 GBPm in FY2020 to 175 GBPm in FY2021.
    • Not B: the return does not clear the bar and the force that would make it clear is not the company's.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The case against, at its strongest: the price is a false friend, and the recovery it pays for is masking a structural decline.
      • Record fee rates mask a structural fall in placement volumes
      • The localised United States recovery (construction and manufacturing, not financial services, which shows no recovery) masks a European business whose two largest markets have fallen for three years and are still falling.
      • Disintermediation: the Page Personnel wind-down removes 20 percent of fees that corporate direct sourcing and artificial-intelligence agents were taking anyway, and the same forces are moving up the salary ladder: one expert call reports clients securing 90 percent of salary-level and executive roles through LinkedIn
        • Jefferies' channel check says large recruiters will be net losers from automation and clients will demand a share of the productivity gain.
      • Cost rigidity: support staff grew 11 percent since 2019 while fee earners fell 16 percent, so the cost base is rigid where it matters and the 70 percent flow-through is a stable-market number the company has not earned since 2022
        • Conversion has fallen from 11.8 percent to 2.7 percent against a peer median of 5 percent.
      • Cash: cash has fallen from 131 GBPm in FY2022 to 31 GBPm, the dividend has been cut twice and the buffer halved.
      • Price: at 14.8x 24-month-forward earnings and 110 percent above their low, the shares pay for a recovery that Morgan Stanley values at 110p and Jefferies' bear case at 90p
        • The rise came on a sector relief rally this week's environment reading attributes to the artificial-intelligence narrative, not to recruitment demand, in a week when the sector is Underweight, Fragility is Double Underweight, French spreads are 152 basis points over Germany and the European Central Bank is expected to raise rates in December 2026.
    • What we concede.
      • The European volume decline is real and unturned, and our modal path already pushes its turn to 2028.
      • The support-cost oddity is unexplained by management and is the strongest single fact in the bear case
      • Our drop-through sensitivity shows it removes the whole return at this price.
      • The cash position is thin enough that a late recovery means a third cut.
      • The multiple is full on our path, and the market has paid for the first leg.
      • The artificial-intelligence disintermediation rate is unquantified in any period, so neither side can prove its reading from the numbers.
    • Why we hold our view, which is neutral rather than bearish: the mechanism is proven, not conjectured.
      • Adjusted EBIT rose from 22.2 GBPm in FY2020 to 175 GBPm in FY2021 on gross profit up 44 percent
      • In 2025 and 2026 the United States went from conversion at about 60 percent to four in five and seven quarters of growth without a surge in vacancies.
      • The bear's structural reading has to explain why the same company and the same consultants recovered in the United States and would not in Europe.
    • The structural-decay evidence rests on the clerical end, which is being exited, and on expert calls about generalists.
      • The professional end is growing at the top (Page Executive up 15 percent at constant currency in Q2 2026 at record fees) and fee percentages have held at 20 to 35 percent through the trough, which is not what a disintermediated agency's pricing looks like.
    • The bear's valuation anchors are inconsistent with each other.
      • Morgan Stanley's 110p is 9.5x next-twelve-month EBITA on trough earnings, a multiple it set on an artificial-intelligence narrative in January 2026
      • Jefferies' 90p is 1.2x EV-to-net-fees at the bottom of the cycle
      • Both price a permanent trough, and the framework's own test, the 24-month-forward multiple on normalised margins, puts the stock at about its ten-year median, neither cheap nor expensive.
    • The cost rigidity caps the conversion ceiling; it is not a reason the conversion will not rise.
      • At a 10 percent conversion the company earns about 16p a share (our arithmetic, on FY2025 gross profit of 770 GBPm, 33 percent tax, 5 GBPm of interest and 311m shares) against 2.9p now, and that is what our modal path assumes by FY2029.
    • Where the case against would win: a United States conversion relapse below four in five with demand stable, which would say the problem is structural rather than cyclical
      • Or a further leg down in France, which would push the modal turn to 2029.
      • So we concede the timing and the price and hold the mechanism: the right answer is not that the recovery will not come, it is that at 212.0p the modal return for waiting for it is a C, and the case clears the bar only below about 194p or on evidence that Europe has turned.
