Every analyst figure below is a real one from our files; none of our own numbers appears in a question. ★ marks the topics to lead with.

Terms. EBITA: adjusted earnings before interest, tax and amortisation of acquired intangibles, the company's profit measure. Organic: growth in units owned in both periods. Leverage: net debt to EBITDA, against the company's 3.0 times ceiling. Earn-out: a deferred payment to a seller that depends on the acquired unit's later profit; a write-back is a gain booked when that payment is cut. Cash conversion: operating cash flow over EBITDA.

1. Attendees

Bengt Lejdström, Chief Financial Officer. He was Chief Executive from January 2024 to mid-2025, so he ran the group when the 2024 and early 2025 acquisitions were made, and now sits beside his successor.

2. Objective and higher intent

  • Objective: judge whether the proof point the memo set for adding (fourth-quarter cash and year-end leverage, reported 10-Feb-2027) is likely to be met, before it is reported.
  • Higher intent: the case is that a cleaned-up group now compounds, provided growth turns into profit and cash and the acquisition engine runs inside the debt ceiling. In August the Chief Executive was confident on both. The third quarter then showed growth of 13% turning into profit growth of 6%. The call should show whether August's confidence has survived that quarter.
  • What would move the position. Towards adding before February: named, dated reasons for the profit gap; a fourth-quarter cash release like last year's; discipline on deal prices with examples. Towards holding back or cutting: a new reason for the gap each quarter; the ceiling described as flexible; write-backs traced to recent deals.

3. Strategy

He signs the numbers, so cash, debt, deal funding and earn-outs are his ground. Let the two analysts argue for you: one sees structural cost pressure, the other raised its numbers. Use Anders Mattson's August words as the yardstick. Questions that touch his own record as Chief Executive go through "some analysts ask".

7. Topics (check-pick list)

T1 ★ Growth that is not reaching profit. "Sales grew 13% organically in the third quarter and profit 6%. RedEye calls it structural cost pressure and has cut its long-run gross margin. DNB Carnegie raised its numbers anyway. Which of them has understood the quarter?"
- Pick from: "How much of the gap ends in the fourth quarter, in kronor?" / "The pricing project had 'no effect yet'. When does it?" / "What would the first half of 2027 need to show for you to say the gap has closed?"
- Positive: two or three named items with amounts and end dates. Negative: "mix", or a different reason from the one given in October.

T2 ★ The fourth quarter against the analysts. "DNB Carnegie has SEK 292m of EBITA in the fourth quarter, a margin above 21%. After the third quarter, what has to go right to get there?"
- Pick from: "Is the cash release shaping up like last year's fourth quarter?" / "In August Anders told us about a fifth of the companies were not where he wanted on cash. Still a fifth?" / "Which companies would you least like us to ask about?"
- Positive: relaxed, specific, cash already coming in. Negative: distancing from the analyst's number; "it depends on December".

T3 ★ The debt ceiling and the deal pace. "DNB Carnegie has leverage falling to 2.0 times by the end of next year. You ended September at 2.95 times against a ceiling of 3.0. Some people say the ceiling, not the pipeline, now sets how many companies you can buy. Is that fair?"
- Pick from: "Which ratio does the board hold you to, with or without earn-outs?" / "For the right company, would you go through 3.0 times?" / "Half your sales are in sterling and the debt is in kronor. What does a weak pound do to that ratio?" / "Is new equity off the table at this share price?"
- Positive: one ratio, a firm ceiling, a path below it without help. Negative: two ratios; "the ceiling is a guideline"; equity not ruled out.

T4 ★ Price and discipline in acquisitions. "In August the message was high conviction in SEK 100m of acquired profit a year. The first half delivered 35. Meanwhile Indutrade is paying about 8 times and Halma 10 to 12. Are you losing companies on price, or choosing not to pay?"
- Pick from: "What was the last deal you walked away from, and why?" / "At what multiple does your 20% return stop working?" / "Analysts put no acquisitions in next year's SEK 1,169m. If you deliver the SEK 100m, how much sits on top?" / "Is the pipeline better or worse than in August?"
- Positive: a real example of saying no; multiples unchanged. Negative: "up to 7 times" drifting higher; pace blamed on sellers.

T5 ★ The earn-out write-backs. "Some analysts are asking why earn-out write-backs reached about SEK 90m in nine months, nearly double all of last year. One reading is prudent accounting. The other is that companies bought in 2024 and 2025 are missing their plans. Which companies are behind it?"
- Pick from: "Are those the same companies that are behind on cash?" / "Without the write-backs, what did profit grow?" / "Is there more to come in the fourth quarter?"
- Positive: names the units, one-off reasons. Negative: "a normal part of the model"; no names.

T6 A more demanding owner. "Anders told us in August that Sdiptech must become a more demanding owner, and that some managing directors thought head office had been erratic: first profit, now cash. From the finance seat, are the managing directors with you yet?"
- Pick from: "How many managing directors have changed this year?" / "How is GAH since its managing director was replaced?" / "With about 35 units on different systems, how do you know a subsidiary's numbers are right between audits?"
- Positive: evidence of change, with numbers. Negative: "it takes time".

T7 Water & Bioeconomy. "In July the steer for Water & Bioeconomy was 21% to 22%. The third quarter was 20.3%, and Anders called the minimum wage in the United Kingdom a major challenge. Is 21% to 22% still the number to hold you to, and by when?"
- Pick from: "Is it one company or the whole segment?" / "Would you sell a company that cannot get back there?"
- Positive: a date and a plan. Negative: the steer quietly lowered.

T8 What worries them now. "In August Anders said he was not worried about the existing companies, only about buying the wrong ones. After the third quarter, would he still say that?"
- Pick from: "Which risk has moved up the board's list most this year?" / "Lifco has reported soft transport products in the United Kingdom. Are you seeing it?" / "Safety & Security at 36% was called unsustainable. What is sustainable?"
- Positive: same answer, calmly. Negative: the existing companies are now on the list.

T9 The sceptics and the analysts. "Anders said sceptical investors want three years of 15% growth with stable margins before they believe it. One year in, what would you show them?"
- Pick from: "What do the funds that sold this year tell you?" / "Only two houses publish numbers. Which understands the business better?"
- Read it this way: if he prefers the lower house, expectations are too high.

T10 February's building blocks. "In February you restate the building blocks. RedEye has organic growth fading to about 3% by 2029; your range is 5% to 10% for profit. Is there any reason to move it?"
- Pick from: "A return on capital above 15% 'in one or two years' was said in February. Still?" / "Do the swaps closing in 2027 help the interest bill?"

The final, difficult question. "You ran the company until mid-2025 and now sit beside your successor while he changes how it is run. What is he doing that you would not have done?"
