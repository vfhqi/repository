[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = D

Thesis. Bank Pekao is a self-help case inside a good franchise, arriving after the market has paid for it. At PLN 276.5 the shares price a 3.6% to 3.7% net interest margin, a 34% cost ratio and a legislated tax refund at the top of the stock's own five-year range, so the modal return is about 4.6% a year over eighteen months and 9.1% a year over three years against the 20% bar. The same facts clear the bar only below about PLN 210 to 225.

  • The change is internal and real; the dominant external force is a headwind on the largest revenue line.
    • A proven private-sector operator was placed on Poland's second-largest bank by the 2024 political reset, and the external forces acting on the bank point both ways.
    • Cezary Stypułkowski, in office since 2024-10-05 after fourteen years running mBank, where the cost-to-income ratio reached 27% in mid-2024, runs a 2025-2027 strategy with hard targets: return on equity above 18%, a cost-to-income ratio below 35%, a cost of risk of 65 to 75 basis points and a 50% to 75% payout.
    • The internal forces are strong on three of the framework's twelve (a chief-executive change, a resourced strategy, visible standards improvement), with a fourth mooted (a PLN 15bn to 20bn capital release through the PZU reorganisation)
      • Guidance was reiterated, not raised.
    • Against them, Polish policy rates have fallen 200 basis points since May 2025 to 3.75%, and the net interest margin, on about 75% of operating revenue, fell from 4.2% in FY2025 to 3.65% in Q2 2026
      • The bank corporate tax rate rose from 19% to 30% in January 2026
      • Fines, a court ruling and higher levies landed in the same year.
    • Demand is the tailwind: Polish GDP grew 3.9% year on year in Q2 2026, corporate loans grew 14% and cash loans 14.8% to 2026-06-30.
  • 'Financial Outputs' have reached the operating metrics and the sell-side's outer years, not yet earnings per share.
    • The change shows in the cost ratio, the volumes and the mix
    • The financial statements show it only beneath the tax step.
    • The cost-to-income ratio excluding the Bank Guarantee Fund held at 34.0% in 1H 2026 through a double-digit rise in IT costs
      • Net fee income grew 11%
      • Cash-loan sales were a record PLN 3.2bn gross in Q2 2026.
    • 1H 2026 net profit fell 16% to PLN 2,748m on the tax step
      • Consensus earnings per share fall from PLN 26.73 reported in FY2025 to PLN 22.77 in FY2026, then recover to PLN 26.36 in FY2027 and PLN 28.90 in FY2028.
    • The sell-side has raised FY2027 and FY2028 estimates by 4.2% and 5.8% over three months and FY2028 by 10.6% over six, after cuts in the twelve months to spring 2026
      • No broker has cut since the Q2 results.
  • The change has travelled all six layers of the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain, with the fifth layer masked.
    • Resources, choices and actions are in place, the key operating metrics have moved, the sell-side's estimates have moved, and the financial statements will show the change only as the tax rate steps down to 26% in 2027 and 23% in 2028.
  • What we think it earns is a tax refund followed by mid-single-digit growth.
    • Our modal path sits on consensus: earnings per share of PLN 22.8 in FY2026, 25.8 in FY2027 and 29.1 in FY2028, then about 5.5% a year (30.7, 32.4, 34.2 for FY2029 to FY2031).
    • From the FY2026 trough to FY2028 that is about 13% a year, inside the framework's 12% to 20% band
      • About 5.7 points of it is the tax rate moving from 30% to 26%, which investors do not extrapolate.
    • From the last reported year, FY2025, earnings per share compound at about 2.9% a year to FY2028, because the tax rate ends higher than it started even after the step-down.
  • What it costs is the problem.
    • The shares have risen 66.6% in twelve months to 2026-10-06 and trade at 9.78x the 24-month-forward earnings of PLN 28.28, the 92nd percentile of the stock's own five-year range (median 7.67x) and above its ten-year median of 8.70x.
    • The multiple is industry-driven: of the 95 banks in the dashboard sector the great majority sit at the 80th to 100th percentile of their own ten-year ranges, so a sector multiple has been applied to a company whose return on equity is falling from 21.4% in FY2025 to 16% to 18%.
    • Against Polish peers the shares are a 6% discount to the group's average of about 10.4x, not cheap.
  • The return does not clear the bar on the modal path, and has no cushion beneath it.
    • Valuing the shares at 9.0x the earnings two years beyond each horizon, with dividends at a 70% payout, gives PLN 295.9 at eighteen months (4.6% a year) and PLN 358.7 at three years (9.1% a year).
    • Cash returns carry the return: dividends of PLN 54.5 over three years are 19.7% of the price and more than the whole price gain
      • Earnings growth adds about 6% a year on the 24-month-forward figure
      • The return from re-rating is negative (9.78x to 9.0x).
    • Even at a held 9.78x the three-year return is about 11.7% a year, a D
      • At the ten-year 75th percentile of 10.30x it is 13.3% a year, a C
      • Nothing short of the ten-year 90th percentile of 12.60x reaches 20%.
    • A plausibly worse drop-through from pre-provision profit to earnings per share (a 65 basis point cost of risk, a policy rate 50 basis points below 3.25%, the tax step to 23% not taken) takes FY2028 earnings per share to about PLN 26.9 and the eighteen-month return to −0.3% a year.
  • The pattern it fits is 'Major Corporate Change' at a C, with 'Compounder' as a C secondary.
    • A commercial operator is extracting the cost and mix improvements a strong, under-managed franchise allows, but the pattern's payoff, an earnings-per-share trajectory upgrade, is being supplied by the tax code rather than by the company.
  • Where the chain holds and where it breaks.
    • It holds from resources to the sell-side's estimates
    • It breaks at the price, because the re-rating that a 'Major Corporate Change' usually earns has already happened for sector reasons.
    • The one unpriced positive is the PZU capital release, which no broker models
      • We put it at about 38% (a 50% chance the enabling legislation passes by about Q1 2027, times a 75% chance it then completes at a parity that reaches Pekao's minorities) and leave it out of the modal path.

  • The case is therefore a well-run bank at a full price, not a 20%-a-year investment from PLN 276.5.

    • We park it on return, not on quality, and would re-open it below about PLN 210 to 225, on a tabled reorganisation bill with a parity set, or on an FY2028 consensus above about PLN 33.

  • Rating = D, because the thesis is not material enough at this price: moderate, mixed input forces ('Change Forces' C) drive outputs that improve from a tax-depressed trough but compound at only about 2.9% a year from the last reported year ('Financial Outputs' C), and from a multiple at the top of the stock's own range the modal return is about 9.1% a year over three years ('Modal return' F)

    • The framework's question, 20%-plus a year in the modal case, is answered no.
    • The Elements bind in sequence: the internal forces would carry a C or B thesis in a cheaply rated bank, and it is the valuation that converts a C set of outputs into an F return, so the Building Block sits at D, above the return Element because the forces and outputs are real and would carry a different rating at a different price.
    • Not C, because C needs the modal return within reach of the bar at some plausible multiple, and even at a held 9.78x it is about 11.7% a year
    • Not F, because the fundamental change is genuine and the price at which the case clears (about PLN 210 to 225) is within the stock's own twelve-month range (52-week low PLN 164.06).
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The strongest case against our D is the constructive sell-side read after Q2 2026: Pekao has shown it can hold a 34% cost ratio through an IT catch-up, volumes are growing double digits in the right segments, the margin has bottomed at 3.65%, the tax rate falls 7 points over two years, the payout is 75%, and the PZU reorganisation is a PLN 15bn to 20bn option worth 21% to 28% of the PLN 72.6bn market capitalisation that no broker models
      • On that view earnings per share grow about 13% a year to FY2028 with a 6% yield, and 9.8x for a bank earning a 17% to 18% return on equity is not expensive against Polish peers at 10x to 12x.
    • We concede every operating fact and the yield.
    • Our response is arithmetic.
    • The 13% is measured from a trough the tax step itself created
      • The three-year rate from the last reported year is about 2.9%
      • Once the step-down is complete in FY2028 the growth rate falls to the mid-single digits that a 3.6% margin, 8% volume growth and a sub-35% cost ratio produce, and the multiple at 9.78x, the 92nd percentile of five years, already discounts that path.
    • The capital release accrues to a merged entity at a parity not yet set, through legislation not yet passed, while the minister examines alternatives
      • At our 38% odds and PLN 57 to 76 a share of gross value its expected value is PLN 22 to 29 a share, which lifts the three-year rate by roughly 3 points if all of it reached Pekao's holders, to about 12% a year, still a D.
    • On the sell-side's own numbers, consensus earnings and a held 9.78x, the three-year return is about 11.7% a year.
    • We hold the D.
Change Forces: are the input change forces sufficiently strong? (E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because the internal forces are genuinely strong (a chief-executive change with a proven record, a resourced strategy, visible standards improvement, a mooted capital release) but the dominant external force, policy-rate normalisation, is a headwind on about 75% of operating revenue and the regulatory and fiscal forces are also headwinds, so the net input change is moderate and mixed, not the clear, strong, one-directional change a B needs.
  • The two sides: the internal forces rate B and the external forces rate C
    • The parent sits at C because the external headwind acts on the largest line in the profit and loss account, the net interest margin, while the internal forces act on costs, mix and fees, which are smaller lines.
  • Not B, because B was live on the strength of the leadership and strategy change
    • It fails because the framework's screen rule (at least three forces strong, or chief-executive change plus a guidance increase) is only partly met: the chief executive changed but guidance was reiterated, not raised, and the bank acknowledges it is short of its return-on-equity target in 2026.
External forces: are there sufficiently strong external forces acting on the company, new or intensifying, and in which direction? (RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because the external forces are strong but point both ways: demand (credit volumes, EU-funded investment, GDP growth of 3.9% in Q2 2026) and macro are tailwinds, while rates, regulation, taxation and competition for deposits are headwinds
    • The net direction is slightly negative for the next eighteen months and slightly positive thereafter as the tax step-down and the rate pause work through.
  • Direction by force: demand a tailwind
    • Competition a headwind in deposits and neutral in lending
    • Disruption a mild headwind
    • Political and regulatory a headwind
    • Supply side neutral to a mild tailwind through wages
    • The company's own cycle past the margin peak with volumes rising
    • Macro a tailwind acting less on this company than on the universe because Polish rates are already low relative to the cycle peak.
  • Not D, because the demand and macro tailwinds are real and measured (loans +8.5% to +10% year on year, GDP +3.9%) and the rate headwind is now largely delivered: the 3.65% margin of Q2 2026 against 4.2% in FY2025 already captures most of the 57 basis points of compression Morgan Stanley projects for 2026 to 2028.
    • Not B, because the headwinds sit on the largest revenue line.
Political and regulatory: is a change moving demand, pricing or cost? (KQ 4, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = D
  • Rating = D, because political and regulatory change is moving cost and revenue against the bank on several fronts at once (the bank corporate tax rate from 19% to 30% in 2026, a PLN 119m UOKiK fine, a CJEU ruling that cut net interest income by PLN 126m, the Bank Guarantee Fund charge up 25.2%, a proposed reserve tax of up to PLN 2bn a year for the sector), and the one dated relief, the tax step-down to 26% and 23%, is legislated but politically exposed ahead of the 2027 election.
  • Tax: the bank corporate income tax rate rose from 19% to 30% from January 2026 and is scheduled to fall to 26% in 2027 and 23% in 2028
    • The step is 1,100 basis points against a Western European banking average of 20% to 25%.
  • Fines and rulings: a UOKiK fine of PLN 119m for mortgage credit-holiday practices, under appeal
    • A PLN 11m UOKiK penalty on foreign-exchange clauses
    • The CJEU ruling on non-interest consumer loan costs cut 1H 2026 net interest income by PLN 126m
    • PLN 202m of consumer-protection provisions in 2025.
  • Levies and buffers: Bank Guarantee Fund contributions of PLN 458m in 1H 2026, up 25.2%
    • The countercyclical buffer from 1% to 2% in September 2026
    • A KNF long-term funding ratio of 40% from December 2026
    • A proposed special tax on reserves of up to PLN 2bn a year for the sector.
  • Legislation the case needs: the PZU-Pekao reorganisation requires amendments to several financial laws
    • The deadline has moved from mid-2026 to 2027-12-31, and the State Assets Minister is exploring alternative structures.
  • Not F, because the direction is partly dated relief (the tax schedule) and the Swiss-franc litigation tail is clearing faster than expected (annual Swiss-franc provisions down to about PLN 0.2bn in 2026-2027 from PLN 0.66bn in 2025).
    • Not C, because the sum of live interventions is larger than any one bank elsewhere in the universe faces, by the research's own outlier call.
Macroeconomic: is a macro force acting on this company more than on the universe? (KQ 7, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because the macro force acting on this company more than on the universe is the Polish policy-rate path, and it is now a mild, well-telegraphed headwind (a hold at 3.75% until April 2027, then 50 basis points of cuts to 3.25% by end-2027 on Morgan Stanley's path), while Polish growth is a mild tailwind relative to Western Europe.
  • Rates: the NBP reference rate has been 3.75% since the March 2026 cut, after 200 basis points of cumulative cuts since May 2025
    • A terminal 3.25% is expected by mid- to late 2027.
    • The bank's sensitivity is 10 to 15 basis points of margin per 100 basis points of cuts
    • A 50 basis point cut lowers net income by about 2.2% on Morgan Stanley's model.
  • Inflation: headline 4.0% in September 2026 from 3.4% in August, core stable at 3.3%
    • The research reads this as reducing the pressure to cut further.
  • Growth and fiscal: GDP +3.9% in Q2 2026, slowing below 3% in 2027 as Recovery and Resilience Facility funding concludes
    • A fiscal deficit near 7% of GDP in 2026 and 2027.
  • Not D, because the rate path from here is shallow and the margin sensitivity is modest
    • Not B, because the force still points down on the largest revenue line.
Revenue and demand cycle: where is the company's own cycle, and is it turning? (KQ 6, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because the company's own cycle is past the margin peak and in the early part of a volume upswing: the net interest margin fell from 4.2% in FY2025 to 3.65% in Q2 2026 and management says it has bottomed, loan growth is accelerating to double digits in the target segments, and FactSet's standardised quarterly revenue series shows the top line down 7.7% year on year in Q2 2026, so the cycle is turning in volume but not yet in revenue.
  • Margin: 4.2% in FY2025
    • 3.8% to 3.9% in 1H 2026
    • 3.65% in Q2 2026, down 9 basis points quarter on quarter
    • Management claims the bottom, with the rate cuts "already priced in"
    • Morgan Stanley sees about 3.6% by FY2028, so the remaining compression is small if rates follow the modelled path.
  • Revenue: FactSet's standardised quarterly revenue (not the company's operating income) was PLN 5,610m in Q2 2026, −7.7% year on year, after −6.5% in Q1 2026 and −4.1% in Q4 2025
    • The company's own operating income of PLN 8,541m in 1H 2026 runs roughly flat on FY2025's PLN 17,165m.
  • Net interest income: PLN 3,302m in Q2 2026, flat quarter on quarter, −4% year on year, in line with consensus.
  • Not D, because the volume side is turning up and the margin decline has slowed to single basis points a quarter
    • Not B, because revenue growth is still negative year on year.
Demand: is end-market demand changing, structurally or cyclically (a swing with two-plus years of visibility)? (KQ 1, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because end-market credit demand in Poland is rising cyclically with two-plus years of visibility: corporate lending is in an investment-led recovery and consumer-finance demand is strong, though mortgage growth is subdued and the EU-fund taper after 2026 caps the horizon.
  • Economy: Polish real GDP grew 3.9% year on year in Q2 2026 against 3.5% in the prior quarter, driven by public and EU-funded investment
    • Projected 3.3% to 3.7% for 2026 and below 3% in 2027 as Recovery and Resilience Facility funding concludes.
  • Sector credit: new mortgage agreements up 50% year on year to 102,600 loans totalling PLN 47.4bn in the first four months of 2026
    • Credit Information Bureau loan sales up 31% year on year and cash-loan sales up 16% in 1H 2026
    • The sell-side bar is 8% to 9% annual loan growth for 2026 to 2028.
  • Pekao's volumes: total loans PLN 196.6bn at 2026-06-30, +8.5% year on year on Morgan Stanley's measure and +10% on management's
    • Corporate +14% to PLN 123.3bn
    • Cash loans +14.8%
    • Mortgages +2.9%.
  • Headroom: private-sector loans at 32% of GDP
    • Against it, the Jefferies caution that the Polish consumer behaves like a developed-markets consumer, which makes high-velocity credit expansion harder to repeat.
  • Not A, because the force is cyclical and public-investment-led, the EU-fund taper from 2027 is dated, and the research's own direction call is that growth decelerates to 2.8% in 2027.
Competition: is competitive intensity easing or tightening? (KQ 2, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because competitive intensity is tightening moderately on both sides of the balance sheet: deposit competition from mBank (deposits +21%) and PKO BP (retail deposits +12.5%), and consolidation (Erste buying Santander Bank Polska, UniCredit entering through Vodeno), against Pekao's rational-pricing stance.
  • Deposits: mBank's customer deposits grew 21% year on year to PLN 248bn in 1H 2026
    • PKO BP's retail deposits +12.5%
    • Pekao's retail deposits +4.0% to PLN 160.2bn.
    • Pekao's deposit beta fell to 36% from 50% in 2020
    • The business digest flags a reversal as a weak signal.
  • Lending: Santander Bank Polska noted corporate lending spreads down to 18 basis points on individual transactions and asked whether Pekao was driving the compression to gain share
    • Erste Group's acquisition of Santander Poland creates a formidable mid-market competitor
    • UniCredit targets a 20% return through Vodeno.
  • Not D, because sector pricing is still described as rational, Pekao's loan-to-deposit ratio is 69% and the sector's 61% to 62%, so no bank needs to buy deposits aggressively
    • The research's "price war" is an alternative reading, not an observed fact.
Disruption: is a disruption force strengthening or being disproved? (KQ 3, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because disruption to the deposit franchise is strengthening at the margin in two forms, retail savings migrating to mutual funds (Pekao TFI assets +25.3% to PLN 45.7bn; PKO BP's retail funds +32.3% to PLN 85.9bn) and fintech payment and credit platforms (Allegro Pay and PayPo with 90% of limit-based buy-now-pay-later; Revolut benchmarking Poland), and neither threatens the corporate franchise.
  • Savings migration: every zloty in Pekao TFI is a zloty not funding the loan book at bank margins
    • The earnings review calls it the non-obvious outlier, and the business digest's early-warning signal is a contraction in retail current accounts relative to term deposits.
    • Current accounts were 75.2% of client deposits at June 2026 and roughly 68% of individual deposits.
  • Not D, because the migration lifts fee income (fund distribution drove the 11% fee growth), so the net profit effect is partly offsetting, and incumbent scale and depositor trust remain the entry barrier the research names.
Supply side: input costs, availability, concentration? (KQ 5, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because the bank's inputs are cheap and getting cheaper where it matters: deposit costs fell with the deposit beta at 36%, Q2 2026 interest expense was PLN 694m against PLN 1,076m a year earlier, wholesale funding is available at a 3.625% fixed coupon for the EUR 750m senior non-preferred and 4.0101% for the EUR 500m Tier 2, and enterprise wage growth has moderated to 5.8%
    • The exception is IT wage and licence inflation.
  • Wages and IT: enterprise wage growth 5.8% year on year in 1H 2026, the lowest since 1H 2020
    • 5.6% in August 2026 against 6.6% expected.
    • The tech sector's net employment outlook is +28% for Q3 2026 and other administrative expenses rose 12% on IT.
    • Cloud suppliers are concentrated in Kyndryl, Google Cloud Poland and Microsoft Ireland.
  • Not A, because the IT cost line is rising at double digits and the concentration in a few cloud suppliers is a named exposure.
Catch-all: any other material external dynamic, positive or negative? (KQ 8, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) optional Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because one material external dynamic is not caught above: the sector-wide European bank re-rating.
    • Of the 95 banks in the dashboard sector the great majority carry own-history price-to-earnings percentiles of 80 to 100, so the multiple on Pekao is being set as much by the European sector as by Poland
    • A tailwind that has already acted and is therefore now a risk, read in full under the re-rating and mark-to-market items.
Internal forces: are there sufficiently strong internal change forces under way? (RA 2, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because at least three of the twelve internal forces are strong and under way (a chief-executive change with a proven commercial record, a resourced strategy with hard targets, and a standards improvement visible in costs and governance), with a fourth force mooted (balance-sheet utilisation through the PZU capital release), so the framework's screen rule for a strong internal change is met on the count of forces, though not on its second route, a chief-executive change plus a guidance increase.
  • False-positive checks: no caretaker chief executive (a fourteen-year chief executive of mBank)
    • Not a presentation-only transformation (voluntary redundancies, branch cuts, a completed PeoBIZ 2.0 migration, fee tariff changes, an audit with clawback consequences)
    • No selling of crown jewels
    • No dangerous leverage (CET1 14.8%)
    • No debt-funded buyback.
    • Empire-building is the live risk: a 30% to 40% domestic market share ambition and a possible Alior acquisition.
  • Not A, because the change has not raised guidance, the chief executive defends a "slow reorganisation" pace, no insider owns shares, and the largest force, the capital release, is contingent on legislation.

Summary: Written Summary of the Investment Case (not rated) (E 3, BB 4)
The case in 8 to 10 sentences (the thesis as tested), kept in full at both ends of the record (Part 1, E 3, BB 4)
  • Bank Pekao is a self-help case inside a good franchise, arriving after the market has paid for it.
  • A proven private-sector operator, Cezary Stypułkowski, was placed on Poland's second-largest bank by the 2024 political reset and is doing what his record says he does: holding the cost-to-income ratio at 34.0% excluding the Bank Guarantee Fund through an IT catch-up, shifting the loan mix toward cash loans and mid-corporates (both growing about 14% a year), tidying the perimeter and the governance, and defending a 3.65% net interest margin against policy-rate cuts with fixed-rate mortgages and hedges.
  • The outputs have reached the operating metrics and the sell-side's outer-year estimates but not yet earnings per share, which fall about 15% in FY2026 because the bank corporate tax rate rose from 19% to 30%
    • Earnings recover about 13% a year to FY2028 as the rate steps down to 26% and 23%, and from the FY2025 base of PLN 26.73 they compound at only about 2.9% a year to our FY2028 of PLN 29.1.
