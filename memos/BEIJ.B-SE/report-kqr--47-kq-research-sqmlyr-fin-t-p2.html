Section summary
  1. Beijer Ref's pricing power directly determines its ability to sustain margins and EPS growth through complex regulatory transitions and macroeconomic headwinds across its core regions.

Section 2 – Regulatory Price Drivers: EU F-Gas Quotas and US AIM Act

Section summary
  1. Regulatory phase-downs of high-GWP refrigerants in Europe and North America act as structural catalysts that expand absolute gross profit dollars for wholesale distributors.
  2. While the transition to new gases yields immediate pricing premiums, long-term margin preservation depends on managing the subsequent deflation of legacy inventory.

EU F-Gas Quota Precedents

1.
Systematic analysis: Research across 450 documents from four source types outlines how refrigerant regulatory transitions drive Beijer Ref's pricing power and margins. This investigation covers ten named peers and three historical precedents to test whether the distributor's profitability is structurally protected. These findings suggest that regulatory transitions create substantial pricing opportunities but also introduce temporary inventory risks.
2.
Historical precedents: Past pricing trends during the initial EU F-gas phase-down steps demonstrate how regulatory scarcity can rapidly inflate distributor revenues. During the 2015 to 2018 European phase-down staircase, refrigerant prices surged 2x to 3x because of tight import quotas. Beijer Ref historically benefited from these dramatic price spikes since refrigerants then represented over 15% of total group sales. This historical precedent suggests that regulatory supply constraints can drive rapid revenue inflation for distributors.
3.
Operating leverage: Significant margin expansion was achieved by Beijer Ref during the early stages of the F-gas phase-out. The CEO of Beijer Ref reported in the first quarter of 2018 that net sales grew by 18% because of strong economic conditions and sharp refrigerant price rises. This price inflation had a direct positive impact on operating profit, which surged by 61% during the fourth quarter of 2017. These results indicate that distributor operating leverage expands dramatically during the initial scarcity phase of a transition.
4.
Looming quota: Looming quota step-downs in Europe are starting to trigger another round of refrigerant price inflation. Industry reports from 2026 indicate that European refrigerant prices have already started to move upward in several countries ahead of the upcoming January 1, 2027 quota halving. DNB Carnegie highlights that this pre-emptive price increases will act as a major catalyst for Beijer Ref's core EMEA region, which represents roughly 60% of total group sales. This pricing trend shows that market participants aggressively price in regulatory changes well before deadlines.
5.
Structural demand: Structural demand shifts are accelerating as the European Union prepares for a massive phase-down in 2027. The European Commission's F-gas regulation outlines a stepwise reduction in the annual quantity of HFCs permitted on the market to just 21% of the baseline by 2030, with a further step-down of 12% in 2027. Jefferies notes that because older units cannot utilize the new low-GWP refrigerants, this regulatory mandate accelerates the equipment replacement cycle across commercial refrigeration. Consequently, this structural transition provides Beijer Ref with a resilient, multi-year demand tailwind regardless of broader macroeconomic weakness.
