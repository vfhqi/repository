[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = D

Thesis. Neste is a cyclical margin recovery with a self-help overlay and a regulatory demand step beneath it. At 31.93 EUR the shares already pay for the recovery and for the self-help, so what is left is a re-rating on an earnings base that falls from next year, and the modal return of 15.6 percent a year over eighteen months and 11.0 percent a year over three years does not clear the 20 percent bar.

  • The change is real, broad and has already travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain.
    • A new chief executive (15 October 2024) and chief financial officer (early 2025) chose retrenchment and delivered it: a cost programme at a EUR 594m run-rate against a EUR 350m target, the hydrogen project cancelled, 510 positions cut, the leverage ratio from 37.8 percent to 29.9 percent.
    • The outputs followed: a renewable sales margin of USD 1,223 a tonne, a refining margin of USD 25.8 a barrel, comparable EBITDA of EUR 1,203m in Q2 2026 against EUR 341m a year earlier, and FY2026 consensus EPS up 350 percent in twelve months to 3.77 EUR.
    • There is no layer left for the change to reach.
  • The strongest force on the outputs is external and transient.
    • About 40 percent of the Q2 2026 margin expansion came from the distillate squeeze that followed the Middle East conflict and the Strait of Hormuz closure, and a ceasefire was negotiated at the end of Q2 2026.
    • The brokers already assume Porvoo's refining margin normalises to a real USD 12 a barrel by 2028 against USD 25.8 in Q2 2026.
    • The internal change explains perhaps EUR 594m of a EUR 2,500m rise in EBITDA
    • The crack and the mandate explain the rest.
  • The demand step beneath the case is structural, but it is a floor on volume and not on margin.
    • The German abolition of double-counting adds 1.5 million tonnes of physical demand from January 2026
    • The US volume obligations are finalised at 8.86 billion RINs for 2026, up 65 percent
    • Renewable diesel demand grows at over 10 percent a year to 2030.
    • Against that, capacity of the same order arrives in 2027 to 2028: Rotterdam itself, Enilive, Repsol, US plants running above nameplate, up to 3 million tonnes of potential Chinese SAF with no anti-dumping duty.
    • The 2024 trough, at USD 242 a tonne with the same mandates in prospect, is the evidence that the margin can fall far below the floor the bull assumes.
  • 'Financial Outputs' peak in the current year.
    • Consensus has EPS falling 23.8 percent in FY2027 and 7.3 percent in FY2028
    • Our modal path has FY2027 at 2.45 EUR, 28 percent below FY2026's 3.41 EUR, because we take the 2027 renewable margin at USD 900 a tonne where consensus implies about USD 975 and because the term contracts leave the diesel component open.
    • Fiscal-year EPS compounds at minus 7.7 percent a year from FY2026 to FY2029 on our path.
  • The price already pays for the known catalysts.
    • The shares have risen 97.2 percent in twelve months to 31.93 EUR
    • The German step, the US obligations, the cost programme, Rotterdam in 2027 and the Q4 cash inflection are all in the brokers' notes and in a median target of 37.00 EUR.
    • The multiple, 12.1x consensus 24-month-forward EPS of 2.6325 EUR, is the 21st percentile of the stock's own ten-year history because the market expects the earnings to fall, which is also our reading.
  • What the case offers from here is a re-rating, and it is only partly ours to earn.
    • On the modal path (a 2027 renewable margin of USD 900 a tonne settling at USD 800; Rotterdam starting in 2027 and adding 1.3 million tonnes; a multiple of 14.0x) the value including dividends is 39.65 EUR at 2028-04-07 and 43.70 EUR at 2029-10-07: 15.6 percent a year and 11.0 percent a year.
    • The re-rating from 12.1x to 14.0x is the whole of the return above the dividend
    • Earnings growth on the 24-month-forward base is 3.7 percent
    • The cash return is 4.5 percentage points over eighteen months.
    • The two conditions the re-rating needs, the margin floor and Rotterdam on time, are each probable at 65 percent and together at 42 percent, and the probability-weighted multiple of 12.6x gives 8.0 percent and 7.4 percent a year.
  • The chain holds on the inputs and breaks on the return.
    • Strong forces (B), outputs delivered to a peak with a negative forward slope (C), and a return that fails the bar at both horizons (D): the return binds.
    • The eighteen-month modal return would clear the bar at 30.2 EUR, 5 percent below the market
    • The three-year return would clear it only at 25.3 EUR.

  • The case is therefore not compelling at this price.

    • The self-help is in the numbers, the strongest force is fading, and the three-year horizon fails on every version of the path.

  • Rating = D, because the change is material in its inputs and has already been fully expressed in the outputs and in the price, so that the change expected from here is a decline in earnings from a peak, offset only partly by Rotterdam's volume, and the modal return of 15.6 percent a year over eighteen months and 11.0 percent a year over three years (8.0 and 7.4 percent on the probability-weighted multiple of 12.6x) is below the 20 percent bar at both horizons.

    • Not C, although C was live: the 'Change Forces' are a B, the near-term outputs are as strong as any in the universe, and the eighteen-month clearing price of 30.2 EUR sits only 5 percent below the market
    • We hold D because the framework's question is whether the expected change is material enough to drive the return from today's price, and at 31.93 EUR it is not, and because the three-year horizon fails on every version of the path.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The bull's case is that the market is mispricing a structural step-change as a cyclical peak.
    • RED III, the German physical step of 1.5 million tonnes, Spain from 2027, the EU anti-dumping duties on Chinese biodiesel and the ReFuelEU ramp put a mandate floor under European renewable margins near USD 1,000 a tonne
      • Rotterdam adds 24 percent to volume into that floor from 2027
      • At USD 1,000 a tonne and Rotterdam volumes FY2028 EPS is about 3.4 EUR, the 24-month-forward figure at April 2028 about 3.6 EUR, and at 14.0x the value is 50 EUR plus dividends, 38 percent a year.
    • Rothschild & Co Redburn and DNB Carnegie hold this view, and Jefferies says the enterprise value is below asset replacement cost.
    • What we concede: the mandate floor is real, physical and in force
      • The 2027 contract round is being struck in Q4 2026 against a spot of USD 1,000 a tonne or more
      • The market has been wrong about Neste in both directions inside two years, with a consensus target of 12.23 EUR in early 2025 and 40 EUR by September 2026
      • And if the margin resolves toward USD 1,000 a tonne, our modal path and this rating are wrong by a Building Block.
    • Why we hold our view: the floor the mandates set is a volume floor, not a margin floor.
      • The margin is set by capacity against the mandate, and capacity (Rotterdam itself, Enilive's two plants, Repsol's 1.5 million tonnes, the US plants above nameplate, up to 3 million tonnes of Chinese SAF without duties) is arriving into the same window as the demand step.
      • The 2024 experience of USD 242 a tonne with these mandates already legislated shows the floor can be far below USD 800.
      • Term contracts leave the diesel component open, so the crack's fading reaches realised margins within a year.
      • The bull's own evidence, record peer margins at Diamond Green Diesel and Rodeo and USD 1,223 a tonne at Neste, is the evidence that the industry is at the top of its cycle.
      • We put the bull branch at possible, not probable, and the case does not clear the bar without it.
