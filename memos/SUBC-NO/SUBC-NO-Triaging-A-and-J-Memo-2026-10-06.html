[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = D

Thesis. Subsea 7 is a well-run deepwater installation contractor at the top of a supply-constrained cycle, and at NOK 329 the shares already pay for the margin plateau and the merger cash that are the whole of what is left. The change that built the case has run through every layer of the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain to the sell-side's estimates; what remains for the next eighteen months is a return of about 5 percent a year on our modal path, a quarter of the 20 percent bar.

  • The external change is real, unusually strong for this industry, and mature.
    • A fixed global fleet of twenty high-specification rigid pipelay vessels, owned by nine operators and dominated by three contractors, met a multi-year rise in deepwater capital spending, and because a newbuild now costs USD 750m to 900m with a 50 percent deposit and no yard slot, the extra demand went into price rather than into capacity.
    • Backlog stood at USD 13.6bn at the second quarter of 2026, covering 95 to 97 percent of 2026 revenue, 72 percent of 2027 and 38 percent of 2028
      • The three contractors are booked through 2027 to 2028.
    • The same research that establishes the force dates its plateau: global offshore final-investment-decision capital expenditure peaks around 2027 at USD 149bn, Petrobras's 2026 to 2030 plan declines sharply after 2028, and management itself expects an offshore wind sanctioning lull to weaken Seaway 7 in 2028 and 2029.
  • The internal change is a corporate event, not an operating change.
    • The nil-premium 50-50 merger with Saipem, signed in July 2025 and cleared unconditionally in Brazil on 23 June 2026, is a strategy change of the first order, but it has produced no output yet and the chief-executive succession of 1 July 2026 was designed to produce none.
    • Guidance was raised twice in 2026, from a 22 percent adjusted EBITDA margin to 23 percent to about 24 percent, and shareholder returns stepped up to about NOK 33 a share across 2026 including EUR 555m of pre-completion specials
      • Both are the cycle's output passed through, not a new engine.
    • The merger awaits the European Commission's Phase 2 decision by 26 November 2026 and the Australian regulator by mid-November 2026
      • Either party may cancel if required divestments exceed EUR 500m.
      • We put completion on current terms at about 70 percent, probable.
  • 'Financial Outputs' have arrived rather than are arriving.
    • The adjusted EBITDA margin rose from 15.9 percent in 2024 to 20.9 percent in 2025 and is guided to about 24 percent for 2026
    • The balance sheet moved from net debt to net cash of USD 190m at June 2026
    • Earnings per share roughly double in 2026
    • The sell-side has raised FY2027 EPS by 35.5 percent in twelve months.
    • From here the path is a plateau: our modal EPS compounds at 5.3 percent a year from FY2026 to FY2028, consensus at 2.2 percent and 4.9 percent for FY2027 and FY2028, and our path eases after 2028 as Petrobras's plan declines and wind pauses.
    • Revenue growth has already decelerated from +16.3 percent in 2023 and +14.5 percent in 2024 to +3.6 percent in 2025, with consensus at +1.6 percent for 2027
      • The improvement left is in the margin, worth about one percentage point, and below EBITDA.
  • The change has travelled all six layers of the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain.
    • It shows in the company's resources (a fixed fleet, an internal successor), its choices (merger, bidding selectivity, two guidance raises), its actions (pricing up 3 to 5 percent a year, distributions), its key operating metrics (utilisation 87 percent, segment margin 26.5 percent in the second quarter of 2026), its financial statements (group margin 24.4 percent, free cash flow USD 478m in the quarter) and the sell-side's estimates.
    • The one force not yet past layer three is the merger.
  • What we think it earns: adjusted EPS on the brokers' basis of about USD 2.71 in FY2026, 2.95 in FY2027, 3.00 in FY2028, then 2.97, 2.86 and 2.86 to FY2031, the later years including our half-share of merger synergies
    • In NOK at the 9.62 the price pairing implies, about 26.1, 28.4, 28.9, 28.6, 27.5 and 27.5.
    • We sit 3 percent above the consensus median for FY2026 and about 10 percent above for FY2027, because we take the margin to 24.5 percent on the roll-off of backlog priced from 2024 in a seller's market
      • We sit below DNB Carnegie's 25 percent because Brazil per-well pricing is softening and one expert reports delivery margins guided down to 5 percent.
  • What it costs: NOK 329.0, or about 12.6 times the 24-month-forward consensus EPS of about NOK 26.1, against the stock's own five-year median of 11.27x, its two-year median of 10.34x and a peer median of 9.8x
    • The 62nd percentile of five years and the 70th of two.
    • Only Saipem, the merger partner, matches the multiple at 12.6x
      • The four nearest peers by business sit at about 9.6x.
    • The specials have been known since March 2025, the 24 percent margin is in consensus at 23.8 percent, and the shares are +69.8 percent over twelve months
      • The price has moved before the events it waits for.
  • The return does not clear the bar, and the cash leg is what holds it up.
    • On our modal path the eighteen-month value including cash returns is NOK 356, 5.4 percent a year
    • The three-year value is NOK 347, 1.8 percent a year.
    • Cash returns of about NOK 32 over eighteen months carry the return, and about 60 percent of that cash is the merger-conditional special
      • Earnings growth adds 8 points
      • A de-rating from 12.6x to 11.5x takes 8.7 points away and is the swing factor.
    • Hold the multiple at 12.6x and the eighteen-month figure is 11.4 percent a year, still a D
      • Take it to the five-year 75th percentile of 13.2x and it is 14.7 percent a year, a C, on a branch we put at about 28 percent.
    • A plausibly worse drop-through from EBITDA to EPS (tax and minorities at the reported 37 percent instead of our 33 percent, net finance at USD -25m) takes the eighteen-month return to 0.2 percent a year and the three-year to -0.6 percent a year.
  • The pattern it fits is 'Demand Inflection', crisp on its conditions and late in its life.
    • The mandatory condition passes, oligopoly and fixed-cost leverage are textbook, and one-to-many scalability fails because one vessel-day serves one customer
    • The EPS trajectory upgrades the pattern promises have mostly been delivered.

  • Where the chain holds and where it breaks.

    • It holds from the force through the estimates without a weak link
    • It breaks at the price, because materiality is judged for the expected change from here, and from here the market's forward window reaches the years the research itself flags.
    • The case therefore is a good company at the top of its cycle, fully priced: compelling as a business, not compelling as an investment from NOK 329.

  • Rating = D, because the change is material but delivered: strong forces (B) have driven outputs that are now very good and improve only a little further (C), and at NOK 329 the return they leave over eighteen months and three years is well below the bar (F).

    • Not C, because a C needs the eighteen-month return to hold near 12 percent a year, which requires the multiple not to move at all from a level above the stock's own median while the market's window reaches 2029 to 2030
    • We judge a modest de-rating more likely than that.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.

    • The strongest case against our view is the bull's, in its strongest form: the market is still mis-pricing a structural change in the supply side of deepwater installation.

    • The case against, at its strongest.

      • Three contractors become two of consequence.
      • Saipem7 would hold 40 percent of the world's rigid pipelay vessels and 60 to 65 percent of SURF
      • No newbuild can be ordered under USD 750m and three years
      • The standalone trades at a 9 to 10 percent free-cash-flow yield (consensus free cash flow USD 973m on a USD 9,872m market capitalisation) and the combined group at 5.4x EV/EBITDA against large-cap services at 10x
      • EUR 300m of synergies are not in the price
      • DNB Carnegie's 25 percent margin would add about 10 percent to FY2027 EPS
      • Backlogs reaching 2030 to 2031 say the plateau is a floor, not a peak.
      • On those premises a multiple of 13 to 14x on about NOK 29 of earnings gives NOK 380 to 410 plus NOK 32 of cash by April 2028, 14 to 20 percent a year, and the three sell-side targets of NOK 400 to 440 (DNB Carnegie, Rothschild & Co Redburn, Jefferies) say so.
      • The risks research reads low short interest as strong cycle conviction.
    • What we concede.
      • The structure, which we rate A
      • The cash yield, which is real and covers the ordinary dividend about twice
      • The record, ten quarters without a miss
      • That today's multiple is not a peak-cycle multiple
      • That the one quantified mis-modelling in the research (the margin line) runs in the bull's favour
      • And that if the merger completes and the market re-rates the group to 13x, our 'Modal return' is wrong by a full rating and the entry level is today's price.
    • Why we still hold our view.
      • First, the merger partner trades at the same 12.6x, so the exchange creates no arbitrage and imports a lower-margin, higher-variance half: an 11.1 percent group margin on EUR 15.5bn across offshore, onshore engineering and drilling, an 8 percent guidance cut this year, and a USD 3.8bn arbitration claim.
      • Second, the sell-side's own mean target is about 10 percent above the price at NOK 362.87 and its consensus margin is flat at 23.8 percent through 2028, so the street's numbers do not describe a 20 percent-a-year stock either.
      • Third, the stock paid a median 10.34x over the two years in which it delivered this upcycle's upgrades, and a market that paid 10x for rising earnings is unlikely to pay 13x for flat ones.
      • Fourth, the research the bull relies on dates the plateau and names the 2029 decline.
      • Fifth, the framework's own warning is that growth above 40 percent very likely returns to base rates and disappoints, and this year's EPS doubling is that pattern.
    • Where the bull would prove us wrong first.
      • Saipem7 trading above 13x on a completed merger with earnings unchanged, which is our Invalidate level on the de-rating risk
      • Or a record 2027 intake carrying 2030 execution dates, which would say the plateau is longer than the research dates it.
Change Forces: are the input change forces sufficiently strong? (E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because an external force of unusual strength (fixed vessel supply against a multi-year deepwater capital cycle, sector-level, with backlog visibility to 2028) is joined by two genuine internal forces (a transformational merger and a second guidance raise within a year), and the combination has already propagated through to the sell-side's estimates.
  • External force: twenty high-specification rigid pipelay vessels worldwide, no J-lay or reel-lay newbuilds under construction, three contractors booked through 2027 to 2028, clients paying to reserve capacity
    • The direction is a tailwind on demand, supply and competition.
  • Internal forces: the Saipem7 merger (signed, 8 of 16 approvals at July 2026), two guidance raises in 2026, distributions stepped up to about NOK 33 a share for the year
    • The chief-executive succession is orderly continuity.

  • Not A, because the external force is a cycle the research itself describes as plateauing at an elevated level, with Petrobras's spending declining after 2028, and the largest internal force, the merger, is agreed but not delivered.
  • For the case: the forces are strong enough for a 'Demand Inflection'
    • Their age, not their strength, is what the rest of the record turns on.
