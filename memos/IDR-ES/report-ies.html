Indra (IDR-ES): what the nine-month results do to the existing case

These results leave the operating and valuation core of our case where it was, because the case was written with the third-quarter numbers already in hand, but they weaken it modestly at the edges: the momentum readings the case leaned on have rolled over, and the results admit three readings the case does not hold.

What changed

  • Group. Nine-month revenue was EUR 4,645m (+28.6%, organic +16%) and EBIT EUR 454m (+42.4%, margin 9.8%). Third-quarter revenue of EUR 1,466m missed consensus of EUR 1,510m by 2.9%; third-quarter EBIT of EUR 137m missed EUR 147m by 6.9%. The case had all of this.
  • The miss was broad. Against consensus, third-quarter EBIT was 15% light in Defence (EUR 62m), 26% in Space, 9% in Minsait and 28% in Mobility. Only Air Traffic Management beat (revenue EUR 147m, +39%, EBIT 13% ahead). The case frames the quarter as a Defence phasing event.
  • Defence margin. 14.2% in the quarter against 18.2% a year earlier; 17.3% excluding TESS, against 21.3% excluding TESS in the first half. Eurofighter and FCAS revenue was EUR 80m in the quarter, of EUR 400m over nine months. The case assumes the ex-TESS margin runs near 20% and treats the quarter as phasing.
  • Orders. Defence intake was EUR 169m in the quarter (down 33%, book-to-bill 0.39x) and Defence backlog slipped to EUR 11.48bn from EUR 11.75bn in June. Nine-month Defence intake was still up 80% at EUR 1,713m. Group backlog was EUR 22,165m.
  • Cash. Reported free cash flow EUR 1,692m; comparable EUR 70m. Adjusted net debt EUR 431m after EUR 1.81bn of net prepayments and factoring.
  • The fourth-quarter ask. Guidance was reiterated. It requires more than EUR 246m of EBIT, more than EUR 305m of comparable free cash flow and 28% revenue growth in one quarter.
  • Land vehicles. The review reads 101 cumulative vehicles by late September, 18 more announced, as ahead of schedule against a 2026 target of 90. The case reads the same 101 as behind a contracted 138, with a penalty. The review's own quarterly figures (43 in the first half, 17 in the third quarter) show the delivery rate slowing.
  • Sell-side. Bernstein cut FY26 revenue to EUR 6,970m and EBIT to EUR 698m, both now under the guided floors, yet raised FY26 EPS to EUR 3.04 from EUR 2.65, FY27 EPS to EUR 3.61 from EUR 3.13 and its target to EUR 73 from EUR 68, entirely on financial income and tax. Morgan Stanley held Overweight and EUR 70.
  • One correction to our own evidence. The case attaches a 0.89x book-to-bill to Minsait. The review gives 0.89x as Capgemini's third-quarter figure and gives Minsait's nine-month intake as down 1%.

The challenge

Reading one: the miss is organisational, not phasing. Two fighter programmes can explain Defence. They cannot explain four divisions missing on profit in the same quarter. That quarter ended with five senior executives removed and the management committee cut by a third. The case treats the reorganisation as something that widens the range of outcomes later; it does not link it to the quarter just reported. If it is the cause, the fourth quarter inherits it, and the case's 85% odds on the floors are too high: an Outperform-rated broker already forecasts both floors missed. For this to be right, the non-Defence shortfalls must share a cause with Defence rather than being four coincidences. Against it: the fourth quarter is seasonally the heaviest. That defence covers cash and revenue better than it covers a broad profit miss.

Reading two: the ex-TESS margin has stepped down, and that is the line the case says matters most. A fall from 21.3% to 17.3% excluding TESS is four points on the book where, on the case's own arithmetic, each two points is worth EUR 0.18 of FY27 EPS. The review attributes part of it to a lower FCAS contribution, not only to timing. If the fighter work is where the high margin sits and its run-rate is lower after the programme reset, the ex-TESS margin settles nearer 17% to 18% than the case's 20%, and the full-year 18% to 19% Defence guide is out of reach. For this to be right, fourth-quarter fighter revenue must stay well below the first-half pace.

Reading three, in the other direction: the order "peak" is one lumpy quarter, and the cash unwind goes with it. The case builds two pillars on the same event: orders have peaked, so the share price clock has turned; and the prepayment balance is a one-off stock that reverses from FY27. Both rest on EUR 169m of intake in a single quarter of a state-award series, against nine-month intake up 80% and a further Spanish programme round the review describes as still to come. The case itself calls Defence capacity-limited, not demand-limited; in such a business book-to-bill is a weak clock and conversion is the true one. If the next round lands with the same pre-financing terms, the advances refill, the interest income Bernstein has just capitalised persists, and consensus EPS drifts up rather than being cut. For this to be right, new awards must outrun the offsets. The review cannot confirm the terms.

We do not find a fourth. The space and Minsait weakness are readings the case already holds.

What it does to the case

  • Current momentum (B, 78): more pressure, clearly. It was set on sell-side momentum pinned A and relative strength at the 71st percentile. The pins now read C and C, relative strength is at the 49th percentile, the three-month return is -6.3% and the one-month EPS revision is zero.
  • Guidance conservatism and the record of delivery (C, 62): more pressure. A broker below both floors, and reading one.
  • Forecast financials (B, 80): more pressure from reading two; partly offset below the line.
  • Scope for upgrades (D, 30): the same. Operating estimates down, non-operating up is the rotation the case forecast; reading three is the risk to its direction.
  • Setups' Fit/Crispness (C, 66): the same, but its evidence is contested. The land-vehicle shortfall it cites is read the opposite way by the review; the Defence revenue shortfall is confirmed, at 20% against consensus.
  • Thesis's Change Forces' Strength (B, 76): the same; slightly less pressure if reading three holds.
  • Case Riskiness (D, 36) and Foundations' Robustness (D, 58): the same to slightly more.
  • Price. EUR 58.8 on 13-Nov-26, below the 50-day average and 5.6% above the 200-day. It has not approached the EUR 44 to 48 zone where the case says the return clears the bar. The multiple is still at the 97th percentile.

What would settle it

  • The second review, four working days on. It should carry the wider broker response: whether others follow Bernstein below the line, and whether operating estimates settle under the floors. It will carry no new company disclosure, so it cannot test any of the three readings.
  • The capital markets day (25-Nov-26 on the case's calendar). The sell-side expects capacity timelines, margin progression and a Minsait roadmap. A divisional margin bridge would test reading two.
  • Fourth-quarter results (late February 2027 on the case's calendar). EBIT against EUR 246m and the spread of the result across divisions test reading one. Fighter revenue against the EUR 80m quarter and the ex-TESS margin test reading two. Defence intake and the prepayment balance test reading three.
  • Not committed by the company: vehicle acceptances against contract, the offset schedule for the advances, the pre-financing terms of the next programme round, and IRIS2 margins. Reading three and the land-vehicle dispute may stay open for that reason.
