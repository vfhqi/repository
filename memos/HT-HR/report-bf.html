EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF) 22 findings

Hrvatski Telekom represents a high-quality European telecommunications incumbent operating within a structurally advantaged Croatian market characterized by rational competitor conduct, steep entry barriers, and superior capital efficiency. The company commands a dominant 54% revenue market share in a consolidated triopoly structure, maintaining a profit-favoured, vertically integrated position across retail connectivity and B2B ICT services.

The investment case centers on predictable earnings generation supported by contractual revenue visibility exceeding 85%, mechanical margin expansion driven by scale advantages, and disciplined capital allocation yielding over 90% dividend payout ratios while maintaining zero bank debt. The company delivered 3.6% revenue growth and 37.7% Adjusted EBITDA AL margin in FY2025, with operating profit margin improving 1.4 percentage points year-over-year in H1 2026 to 15.2%.

Plausible problems to monitor

Following a comprehensive analysis of Hrvatski Telekom's operational, financial, and competitive positioning across ten analytical dimensions, the evidence reveals three fundamental plausible problem to monitor where the company's metrics or structural exposure fall materially below peer medians or create latent vulnerabilities relative to industry base rates. These signals do not constitute immediate threats to the investment thesis but merit continuous monitoring as potential constraints on sustained margin expansion, capital efficiency, or long-term competitive positioning over the 18-to-36-month analytical horizon.

The first plausible problem to monitor centers on persistently elevated capital intensity during the FTTH expansion cycle, which places near-term pressure on free cash flow conversion. CAPEX AL intensity peaked at 23.6% of revenue (EUR 131.9 million) in H1 2026 due to accelerated FTTH and 5G network rollouts, substantially exceeding the 18-19% maintenance capex benchmark observed among established European scale leaders and aligning instead with the 28% intensity profile typical of infrastructure-building challengers.

This divergence reflects HT's dual imperative to simultaneously complete gigabit fiber coverage to 1 million premises while maintaining 5G Standalone upgrade momentum, but it creates a meaningful drag on equity cash generation. The prolonged peak in FTTH CapEx AL intensity above 23% of revenue paired with inflation in network equipment costs could suppress free cash flow equity conversion below 60%, restricting management's capacity to sustain capital returns above 100% of net income.

While management guidance projects moderately lower CAPEX AL intensity in FY2026, the absence of explicit numerical targets and the ongoing strategic priority of rural fiber M&A absorption suggest that capital intensity normalization may lag initial expectations. [Cross-ref: Section 2, Section 4, Section 9]

The second plausible problem to monitor manifests through near-term working capital absorption dynamics that temporarily compress operating cash flow, signaling potential liquidity stress if inventory buffering or receivables collection cycles fail to normalize. Net working capital expanded in H1 2026 due to EUR 61.5 million in inventories, reflecting proactive chipset procurement against supply constraints, while working capital absorption of EUR 51.7 million in H1 2026 reduced operating cash flow to EUR 153.7 million from EUR 189.6 million in H1 2025.

This EUR 36 million year-over-year operating cash flow decline occurred despite stable underlying profitability, creating a 27% sequential cash conversion drag relative to industry peers who typically maintain near-zero or negative net working capital positions. A structural lengthening of enterprise customer payment terms combined with persistent supply chain inflation could permanently lock up working capital, compressing free cash flow equity conversion below 60% over the N18M horizon.

Although management attributes this absorption to tactical chipset inventory buffering and FTTH capex invoicing timing, the magnitude of the swing and its concentration in receivables and inventory suggests vulnerability to either demand-side payment delays or supply-side cost pass- through constraints. [Cross-ref: Section 2, Section 8]

The third plausible problem to monitor emerges from regulatory exposure to wholesale price compression in non-deregulated rural fiber zones, which threatens to erode the margin tailwinds anticipated from FTTH footprint expansion. HAKOM adjusted the benchmark WACC for HT's regulated wholesale products to 4.82%, directly setting wholesale access tariff ceilings, while HT's copper network nationwide and its fiber infrastructure in noncompetitive regional markets remain subject to regulated wholesale price caps.

This regulatory framework creates a two-tier margin structure where HT enjoys commercial pricing freedom across 72 deregulated urban municipalities but faces strict return caps in precisely the rural geographies where incremental FTTH expansion is concentrated. Accelerated competitive fiber overbuild by Telemach or aggressive wholesale price resets by regulator HAKOM in non-deregulated zones could compress HT's fixed service revenue growth and erode its scale-driven margin advantage over the N36M horizon.

The risk is amplified by the fact that rural fiber economics already exhibit structurally lower density and higher maintenance costs per premise passed, meaning that any wholesale rate compression would directly impair return on invested capital for the fastest-growing segment of HT's infrastructure footprint. While HAKOM's track record has been broadly constructive and the regulator recently granted urban deregulation, the 4.82% wholesale WACC sits materially below HT's 16.5% group ROIC, creating a permanent wedge that constrains monetization of regulated fiber assets. [Cross-ref: Section 3, Section 4, Section 7, Section 9] These three plausible problem to monitor collectively underscore that Hrvatski Telekom's near-term financial profile exhibits temporary capital intensity and working capital pressures that modestly degrade cash conversion metrics relative to fully mature European infrastructure peers, while its long-term margin expansion trajectory faces structural regulatory headwinds in precisely those rural zones where incremental fiber investment is most concentrated.

Importantly, none of these signals constitute existential threats to the core investment thesis centered on dominant market share, superior network quality, and disciplined capital allocation, but they do suggest that equity free cash flow growth may lag EBITDA AL expansion by 200-300 basis points annually until the FTTH buildout cycle normalizes post-2027 and working capital positions revert to structural equilibrium.

Investors should monitor quarterly disclosure of CAPEX AL intensity guidance, days sales outstanding in trade receivables, and any HAKOM consultations regarding rural wholesale rate adjustments as leading indicators of whether these plausible problem to monitor are transitory cyclical phenomena or emerging structural constraints on throughcycle returns.
