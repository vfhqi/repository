Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

1.
Xvivo Perfusion AB: "Xvivo Perfusion AB, the Gothenburg-based organ preservation and machine perfusion company (lung preservation fluids Perfadex Plus and STEEN Solution, the XPS ex vivo lung perfusion system, the Heart Assist Transport system CE-marked in August 2026 with a premarket approval application accepted by the United States Food and Drug Administration in September 2026, the Liver Assist and Kidney Assist perfusion systems, and a United States organ recovery services unit). Key question: organ by organ (lung, heart, liver and kidney), where does Xvivo Perfusion stand against each named rival as of November 2026, on clinical evidence, product, price, installed base, share, route to market and pipeline, and where is share moving and why?"

The answer to this question is critical for investors because Xvivo is at a major inflection point where its multiorgan strategy is transitioning from a European-centric model to a direct challenge against TransMedics in the lucrative United States market. Evidence from expert interviews suggests that Xvivo's proprietary solutions like STEEN Solution and Perfadex Plus provide high-margin recurring revenue, but the true growth engine lies in the hardware and disposables for heart and liver perfusion. If Xvivo successfully secures FDA approval for its heart system by early 2027, it could disrupt TransMedics' near-monopoly on warm heart transport by offering a simpler, more portable cold perfusion alternative. Conversely, failure to gain share in kidneys or the loss of lung volume to United Therapeutics would severely limit the company's ability to reach profitability. The organ transplant market is currently undergoing a structural shift driven by the increased use of Donation after Circulatory Death (DCD) organs, which require advanced perfusion to remain viable. Xvivo's ability to offer both normothermic (warm) and hypothermic (cold) perfusion across multiple organs positions it as a more flexible partner for Organ Procurement Organizations (OPOs) compared to the single-modality approach of rivals like OrganOx. However, TransMedics' aggressive vertical integration into aviation and procurement services creates a "concierge" barrier that Xvivo must overcome through partnerships like its venture with Perfusion Solution. This report used evidence from over 40 distinct sources, including expert insights from former Xvivo and TransMedics executives, cardiothoracic surgeons at major U.S. academic centers like Duke and Vanderbilt, and clinical trial disclosures. No relevant broker documents were available to provide specific financial estimates for the 2026-2027 period, so the analysis relies heavily on primary expert data and regulatory milestones. Closely related questions include the impact of xenotransplantation on long-term organ demand and the regulatory stability of the FDA's review process for Class III medical devices.

Section 2 – Lung: Defending the Gold Standard

Section summary
  1. Xvivo maintains a dominant position in lung preservation through Perfadex Plus, but its hardware market share is threatened by United Therapeutics' proprietary platform development. Medium confidence: Clinical preference for Perfadex remains strong, but the loss of its largest customer's platform business is a documented strategic shift.

Solution Dominance and Generic Threats Xvivo currently controls approximately 90% of the market for

lung preservation solutions through its Perfadex and Perfadex Plus products. While a generic version from Global Transplant Solutions exists, surgeons at major centers like Duke report that Perfadex Plus remains the routine global standard due to its established track record and the minimal incentive to switch for marginal cost savings. Experts estimate that even with patent roll-offs, brand loyalty and bundled deals will likely prevent significant market share erosion in the near term.

1.
Contrary evidence: Some surgeons indicate they would be open to adopting generic lung preservation solutions if large-scale data demonstrated equivalent long-term outcomes and significant cost savings, though they are currently unwilling to compromise on outcomes for cost alone.

Hardware and the Hub Model The Xvivo Perfusion System (XPS) has a penetration rate of roughly 10%

in U.S. lung transplants, with the potential to grow to 20% as OPOs face increased regulatory pressure to improve organ utilization. Xvivo's route to market increasingly relies on a "hub" model, where organizations like Perfusion Solution in Indiana provide centralized Ex Vivo Lung Perfusion (EVLP) services for multiple transplant centers. This contrasts with TransMedics' lung program, which has struggled to translate trial results into realworld adoption because lungs often perform well on simple ice storage without the need for expensive warm transport.

United Therapeutics and Competitive Platforms United Therapeutics (Lung Bioengineering), the

largest U.S. customer for Xvivo's hardware and STEEN Solution, is actively developing its own EVLP platform called CLES to reduce costs and bring perfusion in-house. This move is intended to leverage United Therapeutics' existing perfusion infrastructure and potentially end its reliance on the XPS machine. While Xvivo still supplies the STEEN Solution used by Lung Bioengineering, the loss of hardware dominance at this key customer would limit Xvivo's ability to expand its installed base in the U.S. market.

2.
Other side of the story: While United Therapeutics is moving toward a proprietary platform, Xvivo's deep clinical history and the high regulatory barriers for new EVLP systems may keep it as a necessary partner for smaller OPOs and hospitals that cannot afford to build their own circuits.

Section 3 – Heart: The Next Great Battle

Section summary
  1. Xvivo's Heart Assist system offers a portable, cold-perfusion alternative that could disrupt TransMedics' market dominance if FDA approval is granted in early 2027. High confidence: The PRESERVE trial completion and September 2026 PMA acceptance provide a clear timeline for potential U.S. market entry.

Clinical Evidence and Technology Xvivo's Heart Assist system utilizes a unique non-ischemic

hypothermic perfusion (NIHP) method at 8°C, which protects the heart even if the pump fails during transport. This technology is viewed by some surgeons as superior to TransMedics' normothermic Organ Care System (OCS) Heart, which carries the risk of losing the organ if complications occur during warm pumping. Xvivo's PRESERVE trial in the U.S. enrolled 141 patients ahead of schedule, demonstrating significant interest from clinical trial centers in a simpler, more portable preservation solution. Contrary evidence: TransMedics remains the only FDA-approved system for heart transport in the U.S. and has already been used in over 1,000 cases, giving it a massive lead in clinical experience and institutional trust.

Regulatory Status and U.S. Launch Following its CE mark in August 2026, Xvivo had its PMA

application accepted by the FDA in September 2026, with experts anticipating approval as early as the beginning of 2027. Xvivo plans to leverage its existing 14 clinical trial centers for its initial U.S. launch, forecasting 141 to 200 uses in the first year with a target of 70% segment capture within a decade. The system's compact design allows it to be transported on commercial flights, a significant logistical advantage over the larger TransMedics units that often require private aviation.

Pricing and Competitive Landscape Xvivo is expected to follow a pricing strategy similar to

TransMedics for its heart disposables, though its total cost of ownership may be lower due to simpler logistics and the absence of a required concierge service. While Paragonix (SherpaPak) provides a cheaper static cold storage box, it does not offer the active oxygenated perfusion that Xvivo's Heart Box provides. Xvivo is positioned as a "game-changer" for small and medium centers that need a more intuitive and cost-effective way to accept hearts from longer distances. Other side of the story: TransMedics' vertical integration into aviation through its National OCS Program may prove an insurmountable hurdle for Xvivo, as many hospitals now prefer a "one-call" service over managing their own heart perfusion logistics.

Section 4 – Liver: Challenging the Service Giant

Section summary
  1. Xvivo leads the European liver perfusion market with its dual cold-and-warm platform, but it faces an uphill battle against TransMedics' service-driven model in the U.S. Medium confidence: Xvivo's technology is highly respected, but its lack of a comprehensive U.S. procurement service limits its adoption by high-volume centers.

European Leadership vs. U.S. Entry In Europe, Xvivo is considered the market leader for abdominal

perfusion, as few centers can afford the high cost of TransMedics' OCS Liver. Xvivo's Liver Assist platform has the advantage of supporting both hypothermic and normothermic protocols, whereas the rival OrganOx metra is a normothermic-only system primarily used for back-to-base preservation. However, in the U.S., TransMedics dominates the liver market by providing the surgeons and planes needed for recovery, leaving Xvivo to target the 25% of the market that may prefer hypothermic options for specific DCD cases. Contrary evidence: Some surgeons in Europe have privately expressed skepticism that outcomes from hypothermic liver devices are as strong as those published in early trials, which could slow future adoption.

Rivals and Regulatory Routes OrganOx is gaining traction in the U.S. as a cheaper alternative to

TransMedics, while Bridge to Life (VitaSmart) has secured NICE funding in the U.K. and is pursuing FDA approval. Xvivo is expected to launch its cold platform in the U.S. first, but experts warn that its utility will be limited until it can offer a combined cold-and-warm system that competes directly on functionality with TransMedics. Cost is the primary driver for potential switchers; surgeons at Keck Medicine indicate they would consider Xvivo only if the total cost per case is significantly lower than current normothermic options. Other side of the story: While TransMedics owns the high-end "imported liver" niche, Xvivo's focus on simpler, back-to-base perfusion may capture a larger share of the market as hospitals seek to reduce their reliance on expensive third-party service providers.

Section 5 – Kidney: Breaking into the LifePort Monopoly

Section summary
  1. Xvivo's Kidney Assist faces a formidable incumbent in Organ Recovery Systems, which controls 95% of the U.S. market through its LifePort device. High confidence: The overwhelming dominance of LifePort is a consensus view among sales experts and surgeons, representing a high barrier to entry for Xvivo.

The LifePort Stranglehold Organ Recovery Systems (ORS) and its LifePort Kidney Transporter own at

least 95% of the U.S. kidney perfusion market, serving the vast majority of OPOs. Xvivo's Kidney Assist Transport is highly regarded technologically for being a pulsatile, oxygenated system, but it has struggled to gain revenue in the U.S. due to its late entry and the deep-seated institutional relationships held by ORS. To disrupt this share, Xvivo would need to offer significantly better clinical data or aggressive bundling with its more successful thoracic products.

1.
Contrary evidence: Xvivo has established a very strong market position in Europe for kidney perfusion, suggesting that its technology is competitive when not faced with the specific historical entrenchment of ORS in the U.S.

Technology and Market Potential The Kidney Assist device is seen as "ahead of the curve" by some

engineers because it actively supplies oxygen during transport, a trend that is becoming a "hot topic" in organ preservation. Experts believe Xvivo could eventually reach 50% market share in both the U.S. and Europe by leveraging its full-organ portfolio and clinical data advantages, though this will likely take more than five years to achieve. Currently, the kidney market remains the most stable and commodity-like of the organ segments, with less immediate pressure for high-cost innovation compared to heart or liver. Other side of the story: The FDA's conservative stance on kidney perfusion devices means that Xvivo's technological advantages may not translate into rapid regulatory or commercial success without multi-year, randomized controlled trials.

Section 6 – Services and Logistics: NOP vs. Partnership

Section summary
  1. TransMedics' National OCS Program provides a vertically integrated service that Xvivo is attempting to match through a more fragmented partnership model. Medium confidence: The service model is the primary driver of TransMedics' U.S. growth, and Xvivo's success depends on the scalability of its OPO hubs.

The TransMedics Concierge Model TransMedics has successfully shifted from a device manufacturer

to a "concierge" service provider through its National OCS Program (NOP). By owning its own planes and employing its own procurement surgeons, TransMedics handles the entire transplant process from donor site to recipient hospital, a model that is particularly attractive to centers with limited surgical staffing. This "one-call" solution has solved a major logistical pain point in the U.S., making it difficult for device-only competitors to gain equivalent traction. Contrary evidence: Some OPOs and health systems view TransMedics' control over the procurement process as a potential conflict of interest, as the organizations that govern the process also stand to profit from it.

Xvivo's Hub and Partnership Strategy Xvivo is pursuing a different route to market by partnering with

organizations like Perfusion Solution to create centralized "hubs" for organ evaluation. Under this model, an OPO can send lungs to a central facility to be perfused on an XPS device, after which they are distributed to transplant centers. While this is less capital-intensive than TransMedics' model, it relies on the execution of third-party partners and has yet to achieve the same national scale as the NOP. Other side of the story: The "back-to-base" model favored by Xvivo and OrganOx is gaining academic support as studies suggest that some organs do not require expensive continuous perfusion during transport to achieve good outcomes.

Section 8 – If the Answer Differs from the Common View

Section summary
  1. A failure of TransMedics' service model or a faster-than-expected shift to "back-to-base" perfusion would dramatically revalue Xvivo's portable device strategy.

The common view in the market is that TransMedics has won the U.S. organ preservation race through its vertically integrated service model, which creates a significant barrier to entry for device-only players. However, if hospitals and OPOs begin to push back against the high costs and perceived conflicts of interest of the National OCS Program, there could be a rapid shift back toward the "back-to-base" model that Xvivo champions.

In this scenario, Xvivo's earnings would benefit from a surge in hardware sales as transplant centers seek to bring perfusion expertise back in-house to capture the margins currently being taken by TransMedics. A major knock-on effect of such a shift would be seen among aviation and logistics partners. If the market moves toward Xvivo's portable, commercial-flight-friendly heart and kidney boxes, the demand for dedicated private medical jets used by the NOP would soften, potentially hurting TransMedics' asset-heavy cost structure.

Conversely, Xvivo's partnership model with Perfusion Solution and other regional providers would see significant volume growth, validating its "hub" strategy and potentially leading to higher-margin service revenue for Xvivo itself. United Therapeutics holds the power to push the outcome away from expectations by either accelerating its proprietary CLES rollout or, conversely, deciding that the regulatory burden of maintaining its own platform is too high.

If United Therapeutics remains a long-term Xvivo hardware customer, Xvivo's U.S. lung revenue would be significantly more stable than currently feared. An early sign to watch for is any regulatory delay or clinical setback for the CLES platform, which would immediately strengthen Xvivo's bargaining power in the lung segment. Finally, the FDA remains a wildcard that could favor Xvivo if it prioritizes portable, lower-cost solutions to address the national organ shortage.

If the FDA grants a broad label to Xvivo's Heart Assist system that includes both brain-dead and DCD donors, it would remove one of TransMedics' few remaining clinical advantages (functional assessment). This would likely lead to a faster-than-expected adoption of Xvivo technology among cost-sensitive community hospitals, a segment currently ignored by the "concierge" market leaders.
