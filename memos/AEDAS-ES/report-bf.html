EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF) 1 finding

AEDAS Homes operates a highly scalable, decentralized model across six autonomous regional branches in Spain, with the Centre branch representing 48% of the active land bank as of March 2025. Neinor DMP Bidco acquired 96.83% of AEDAS by March 2026 through a mandatory tender offer, creating Spain's largest residential developer while maintaining operational independence through strategic service agreements. The core Build-to-Sell segment generated EUR 1,028 million in delivery revenues during fiscal year 2024/25, supplemented by turnkey institutional Build-to-Rent projects and asset-light Real Estate Services that grew 40% year-onyear.

AEDAS Homes achieved total revenues of EUR 1,156 million for the fiscal year ended March 31, 2025, with adjusted EBITDA margin of 15.1%, demonstrating strong peer-relative profitability despite a shifting product mix toward lower-margin institutional sales. Net financial debt declined to EUR 266 million from EUR 310 million in the prior year, with net debt to EBITDA improving to 1.6x from 1.8x, reflecting disciplined deleveraging momentum and conservative capital structure management.

Real estate inventories reached EUR 1.48 billion, representing approximately 128% of annual sales, which structurally caps asset turnover and constrains return on capital employed despite healthy gross development margins of 22.94%. Deutsche Bank Research projected a 12% longterm ROCE for AEDAS compared to 12% for Metrovacesa and 14% for Neinor, positioning the company in the middle tier of peer return profiles due to extended inventory holding periods averaging over three years.

The Spanish residential market faces a structural deficit of approximately 750,000 homes since 2021, driven by rapid household formation outstripping new supply, creating exceptional pricing support for developers in strategic urban markets. The combined Neinor-AEDAS entity manages an active land bank of over 43,200 units following the 97.90% controlling stake acquisition completed by June 2026, establishing unprecedented platform scale in a historically fragmented sector where the top three players controlled less than 10% combined market share. Completed homes in 2025 represent an approximately 89% decline from the historical peak in 2006, while annual net household creation reached 242,200 units against only 141,100 new construction permits. Developers must navigate 17 highly autonomous regional planning systems with typical residential projects requiring 15-25% of total costs for land acquisition and 50-60% for construction, creating steep barriers to entry that protect scaled platforms with established municipal relationships from aggressive new competition.

The Spanish residential value chain runs from land sourcing to final delivery, with land acquisition and urban planning capturing significant development premiums of 29% to 64% on unpermitted soils, positioning AEDAS in the highest-margin stages while outsourcing low-margin construction to third parties. The construction execution stage exhibits high labor intensity with contractor profit margins typically hovering in low single digits due to escalating materials costs, enabling developers to capture substantially greater economic value through fixed-price turnkey contracts that transfer execution and inflation risk downstream.

Asset-light development management on behalf of institutional joint ventures delivers gross service margins of 32.5%, providing incremental fee streams without proportionate capital deployment. AEDAS completely outsources physical construction to third-party general contractors, with turnkey fixed-budget contracts protecting the company from direct building cost overruns, though this creates dependency on external capacity that could constrain delivery schedules during regional builder insolvencies. typical AEDAS retail buyer is a Spanish couple in their mid-40s with children, positioned in the upper-middle income bracket, with first-time buyers comprising 34% of the retail customer mix, demonstrating exceptional financial solvency among the target demographic.

Plausible problems to monitor

extreme fragmentation contrasts sharply with consolidated housing markets in other European jurisdictions and constrains the ability of individual developers to influence municipal planning processes or secure preferential supplier terms at a national level, as detailed in Section 3 – Industry Structure + Concentration + Value Chain Economics. AEDAS Homes' Build-to-Rent institutional segment generates significantly lower gross development margins of 15% to 17.7% compared to its core Build-to-Sell residential margins of approximately 23%, creating a material drag on overall profitability as the company scales its institutional partnerships.

This margin compression in the BTR format is clearly worse than the company's own BTS baseline and reflects the reduced pricing power inherent in large turnkey transactions with sophisticated institutional counterparties, as examined in Section 4 – Value Chain Map + Company Position + Margin Distribution. If institutional BTR deliveries increase in mix, average development margins face downward pressure, potentially eroding the company's peer-relative profitability advantage.

AEDAS Homes exhibits extreme exposure to land bank capital lock-up, with its inventory asset base of EUR 1.48 billion representing approximately 128% of annual sales, materially worse than the peer median and structurally constraining return on invested capital. This working capital intensity could significantly compress returns on capital if a persistent macroeconomic downturn freezes residential sales and prevents inventory liquidation, as analyzed in Section 2 – Financial Profile + Peer Benchmarking.

The company's Net Working Capital days of approximately 467 days substantially exceed Neinor Homes' estimated 400 days, highlighting a clear underperformance in asset turnover efficiency relative to its closest peer. AEDAS maintains complete dependence on external constructors by entirely outsourcing physical construction to third-party general contractors, leaving the company vulnerable to capacity bottlenecks and execution delays that lie outside direct management control.

This structural reliance leaves AEDAS vulnerable to capacity bottlenecks that delay deliveries if key general contractors fail, a risk factor explored in Section 4 – Value Chain Map + Company Position + Margin Distribution and Section 6 – Supplier + Distributor + Channel Analysis. While turnkey fixed-price contracts provide some cost protection, they cannot fully insulate the company from severe contractor insolvencies or regional labor shortages that could materially disrupt the delivery pipeline and damage revenue visibility.

Deutsche Bank Research projected a moderate long-term ROCE of 12% for AEDAS Homes, which reflects the structural constraints imposed by slow turnover of residential inventory, with return metrics structurally capped below peers if inventory rotation periods remain extended. This return profile is in line with Metrovacesa but meaningfully below Neinor Homes' estimated 14% ROCE, indicating that AEDAS faces a clear competitive disadvantage in capital efficiency despite operating a similar business model in the same geographic markets, as assessed in Section 2 – Financial Profile + Peer Benchmarking and Section 9 – Operator + Foundation Quality.

The company's average inventory holding period exceeding three years acts as a fundamental structural headwind to achieving sector-leading returns on capital employed.
