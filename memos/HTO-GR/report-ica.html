1. Bottom line

  • Richard was right on the direction of 2027: the 29-Oct memo's +1.0% is withdrawn, but a full 3% is not yet earned. On the 30-Oct call the chief executive said "The intention is to stay within these kind of growth levels moving into 2027" (about 3%), and that OTE aims to run System Solutions at about the same level in 2027, in revenue and margin. Putting System Solutions flat into the memo's own bridge gives about +1.8%, and that already includes an early-2027 price step that management has not committed to; without the step it is about zero. I weight management's signal against that bridge and settle at about +2.3% (model update 3 carries +2.4%), close to Barclays' +2.5% and the median broker's +2.7%. Why it matters: the February estimate cut the memo expected now looks unlikely, but growth still rests on price and cost, not volume, as the memo's "spine" said.
  • The revenue cuts are mostly low-margin revenue, not core revenue, but not "purely" transit. Brokers' 2027 revenue forecasts differ by 239 EURm and their EBITDA forecasts by only 56 EURm; in the third quarter revenue rose 1.0% (5.0% without transit) while EBITDAaL rose 3.1%. Transit explains perhaps half to two-thirds of the cut in consensus sales; most of the rest is System Solutions (about a 15% to 20% margin), a real cut with a small profit effect. The one normal-margin cut found is Barclays', and it is small. Why it matters: the Pool's sales-revision test fails on one cut out of eight brokers, so it says little about the core business.
  • The Sell is a valuation call made across the whole sector, with one Greek-specific cut. The downgrade was made in Barclays' sector note of 20-Oct-26 (completed 19-Oct, the date FactSet shows; we found no separate note). It cut terminal growth in every telecom valuation for artificial intelligence and satellite risk, without scoring Greece, and cut OTE's wholesale revenue for the PPC-Vodafone fibre venture; OTE's earnings per share fell only 4% (2026) and 1% (2027). Barclays' 2027 and 2028 EBITDAaL after the results (1,450 and 1,468 EURm) are almost the same as model update 3's; the disagreement is long-run growth, plus a lower payout (its 2027 dividend per share 0.76 EUR against the model's 1.03). The strongest near-term evidence against OTE is on the call: the chief executive agreed that traditional fixed-line revenue, without TV, is falling about 2.5% to 3%, which he said is "a result of pricing"; wired broadband lines fell about 23,600 in the quarter.
  • On free cash flow and EV/EBITDA, with multiples held, the return is about the cash yield: +8% to +11% to end-2027. The 29-Oct memo's +28.5% needed the P/E to re-rate to 12.5x, which equals about 6.1x EV/EBITDA and a 7.1% free cash flow yield. Against European incumbents OTE is about 15% cheaper on EV/EBITDAaL and about the same on equity free cash flow yield (8% against 7.9%) with far less debt, so it is modestly cheap on risk, not on yield.
  • Risk and reward here and now: roughly balanced, slightly positive. Six scenarios run from 13.00 EUR (Barclays' downside, -26%) to 23.44 EUR (+29%); the probability-weighted return is about +5% to end-2027 (about +4.6% a year), below the cash yield, because a de-rating and Morgan Stanley's bear case (15.20 EUR) are given about as much weight as a re-rating. The 10.8x two-year-forward P/E is already at the 69th percentile of five years and the 81st of two, after OTE re-rated while the telecoms group de-rated 12%.
  • The One Thing: topping out, not rolled over. Profit growth is holding at about 3%, but the engines under it are slowing (mobile service growth 5.5%, 2.3%, 2.0% in the three quarters of 2026; System Solutions +52% to +9%; the broadband base turning down). Sell-side momentum is drifting lower: four Holds and a Sell among 13 ratings, FY2028 EPS consensus -2.2% over six months (FactSet weekly mean to 23-Oct), Eurobank's target cut 11%. The price is below a falling 50-day average (19.53) and above a rising 200-day average (18.04). What would make it rolling over: a close below about 18 EUR with the 200-day average of its price relative to the Stoxx Europe 600 flattening, a 2027 guide below 2%, or no price step in early 2027.
  • The rules still say no to a Core Investment. Hellenic Telecom fails the Pool (Stock RS 59, Sector RS 31, sales revisions -12.5%) and is flagged on Sell Criteria 1 (Industry RS below 30) and 3 (Sector RS below 50 with Stock RS below 70). A Probing Bet is Richard's judgement, not a rule outcome; nothing in this memo is a recommendation to trade.

3. The 2027 growth path, and the model update

What management said about 2027 (the call transcript, 30-Oct-26)

  • Group growth: asked by JPMorgan about 2027, the chief executive said "The intention is to stay within these kind of growth levels moving into 2027", that there are challenges and opportunities, and that management feels confident it can keep growing at this level, as in 2026. This matches Richard's reading: "IMHO CEO was clear on conference call expectation of similar growth (3%) next year too". It is an intention, not guidance; the 2027 guidance comes in late February.
  • System Solutions: asked by Eurobank Equities about life after the Recovery and Resilience Facility, he said OTE is trying to be in a position to "run more or less along the same levels of this year, moving into 2027", in revenue and in margin, from public-sector work that continues, private-sector cloud, security and artificial intelligence work, and European institutions (the European Investment Bank, the European Parliament, NATO). The release is more cautious: the new work will "help offset" the end of the Recovery and Resilience Facility projects. This matches Richard: "CEO/CFO were clear on call re. they think it should be similar levels in 2027" (on the transcript, the chief executive said it).
  • Capital spending: the chief financial officer said the envelope of about 600 EURm is one "which we expect to continue also for the next year, more or less", with no visible reason for it to rise. This matches Richard's "not being higher than this year at 600m EUR"; the model keeps 600 EURm plus about 78 EURm for spectrum in 2027.
  • Price indexation: OTE changed its contract terms in July 2026 to allow a CPI-linked increase at the start of each year (Alpha Finance's analyst read a cap of about 2.80 EUR); on applying it in 2027 the chief executive said "what we are going to do, we'll see", depending on the market, customer value and OTE's commercial strategy. Greek inflation was 5.0% in September (the Earnings Review).
  • Competition: he said "the competitive intensity of the market has increased since the beginning of the year", especially visible in recent months ("It's not only PPC acting, it's also the market reacting"), and that holding the broadband base comes at the cost of some pressure on average revenue per customer. Fixed retail revenue stays positive only with Data Com included, as he confirmed to Goldman Sachs. TV, which keeps that line up, will grow at a more moderate pace (chief financial officer).
  • Wholesale: about 160 EURm of transit revenue rolls off during 2026, completing a rundown that began in 2025 (the release: completed by the end of 2026), with very minimal effect on profit; national wholesale is expected to fall about 15 EURm in 2026.

4. Greek competition: absorbed, or is the damage ahead?

"I am not clear enough on the imact on the case from PPC related competitive intensity #Q"; "is this being successfully absorbed…? Or is the risk ahead… Unclear to me. VERY IMPORTANT KEY DRIVER #J" (Richard, 2-Nov-26)

My judgement: not absorbed. It was held on volume until the third quarter and is not held on price; the larger damage sits in 2027 to 2028, through retail pricing first and wholesale second.

  • Volume held, until this quarter. The broadband base was about flat for a year, but the third quarter brought most of the year's loss (-8,164 of -10,093), despite 52,250 fibre additions. Fixed wireless customers (+15,461) are counted inside that base (the release lists them as a sub-line), so wired broadband fell about 23,600 in the quarter and about 82,350 in a year, partly through OTE moving its own copper customers to fixed wireless; retail fixed lines fell 14,783 in the quarter and 1.4% in a year. Satellite is being held: the chief executive said Starlink's growth in Greece slowed from early 2026 and has run at about last year's levels in recent months, after OTE added more than 100,000 fixed wireless customers in under 18 months. PPC itself had about 11,000 retail broadband customers at end-2025 (Deutsche Bank) and perhaps 20,000 later (the 29-Oct memo's estimate), under 1% of OTE's lines, so the pressure works through prices more than through PPC's own sales.
  • Not held on price. Retail fixed revenue without Data Com fell 0.6% in the quarter, and on the call JPMorgan's analyst worked out that, with TV growing about 9% inside the line, traditional fixed-line revenue is falling about 2.5% to 3%; the chief executive agreed and said it was "a result of pricing". OTE is keeping customers at a cost in average revenue per customer. PPC prices about 50% below the incumbents. Vodafone and Nova, the challengers, raised prices in line with inflation in early 2026, the second year running, but the chief executive now describes the whole market reacting to PPC, especially in recent months, so that cover is fading.
  • Why most of the damage is ahead. (1) The Vodafone-PPC fibre venture (a non-binding 50-50 term sheet of June 2026, about 1.5 to 1.85 million homes, about 130 EURm to PPC) targets closing in the first half of 2027 after competition review; Vodafone, and possibly Nova, could then move lines they rent from OTE onto it. (2) PPC keeps building, from about 1.8 million homes in early 2026 towards 2.7 million at end-2026 and 3.8 million by 2028. (3) The regulator has proposed cutting fibre wholesale prices by 10% to 13% while raising copper wholesale prices by 4% to 5% (decision expected early 2027).
  • Size. National wholesale is about 232 EURm of 2026 revenue at an 80% to 85% margin (the model's reconstruction). The model already has it falling 6% to 8% a year, about 40 EURm of EBITDA lost by 2029 and 51 EURm by 2030. A faster migration of Vodafone's lines (30% to 40% of national wholesale gone by 2029) would cost about 55 to 75 EURm in total, so only about 15 to 35 EURm (1% to 2% of EBITDA) more than the model. The larger bear risk is retail pricing spreading from fixed to the whole base, which no source sizes; together with no price step it would take 2027 growth to about zero.
  • Precedents cut against "stabilisation". In Italy and Ireland, pricing pressure lasted as long as the overbuilder was still building, which argues against the KQ report's own "may stabilise into 2027" headline; the challengers' price rises and a venture framed as co-investment argue for it.

Sources: the KQ report on Greek competitive intensity (2-Nov-26; medium confidence, several stale or misattributed citations, no euro figure for revenue at risk); OTE third-quarter figures; the 29-Oct memo's wholesale reconstruction.

5. Revenue cuts: transit or real?

"The company are clear about calling out that revenue cuts from zero-margin wholesale international transit revenues do not have any real profit impact. Risk is that it will keep cutting the SS revenue estimates…? And algos will pick up on that. A negative "sentiment" risk - one I am willing to take. Also, our sell criteria may be distorted by it - unclear to me #J" (Richard, 2-Nov-26)

My judgement: mostly low-margin revenue, not purely transit. Transit (zero margin) explains perhaps half to two-thirds of the cut in consensus sales; most of the rest is System Solutions (about a 15% to 20% margin), a real cut with a small profit effect. The one normal-margin cut found is Barclays', and it is small. The evidence:

  1. Across brokers (27-Oct-26 snapshot): 2027 revenue forecasts range from 3,383 to 3,622 EURm (a spread of 239) while EBITDA forecasts range from 1,453 to 1,509 (a spread of 56); a euro of revenue difference carries about 6 cents of EBITDA, against a group margin of about 42%. That fits a mix of zero-margin transit and low-margin System Solutions, not core telecom revenue.
  2. Over time: since September 2025, consensus FY2027 sales fell 5.4% (about 200 EURm) while FY2027 EBITDA rose 1.0% (FactSet by named year). Transit in 2027 is about 125 to 130 EURm.
  3. Not all transit: Eurobank Equities, which has the lowest 2027 revenue, forecasts revenue falling 97 EURm from 2027 to 2028, after transit has ended, while its EBITDA barely moves; its notes point to a System Solutions "growth cliff". That is a real cut, at a low margin.
  4. The quarter: revenue +1.0% (+5.0% without transit) with EBITDAaL +3.1%. Morgan Stanley then cut its fourth-quarter revenue and kept EBITDAaL (KQ report).
  5. Barclays' cut is normal-margin but small: 2027 revenue -0.7% and EBITDAaL -0.6% together; EPS -2.4% (1.70 to 1.66 EUR), mostly from depreciation and interest. Barclays has no revenue estimates in FactSet, so it does not move the Pool test.

The rules effect. The Pool's SS Sales Net Upgrade Breadth over 100 days is -12.5% because one broker of eight cut its next-year sales estimate and none raised (FactSet, calendar year plus one; the counts are dated 28-Oct-26, before the results). One clean-up is enough to fail the test. Which broker it was is not on file; the likeliest on the evidence is Eurobank (target cut to 17.50 EUR on 14-Sep-26, the lowest revenue, the System Solutions cliff), which would make it a System Solutions cut rather than transit. Why it matters: the failed test is reading low-margin revenue, not the core business; it is not wrong, but it is weak evidence. Whether to treat low-margin revenue cuts differently in the rules is Richard's decision; this memo only reports the distortion.

6. The Sell, investigated

"IA - A "sell" rating has emerged. Investigate in depth the logic for the recommendation. Take this seriously" (Richard, 2-Nov-26)

What the Sell is (Barclays' sector note "An UnaMUSEing Basket Case?", 20-Oct-26, now in hand)

  • The downgrade note is the sector note. The downgrade was made in the sector note, completed on 19-Oct-26 (the date FactSet shows) and released on 20-Oct; we found no separate Hellenic Telecom note and the Barclays portal shows none. Barclays moved OTE from Overweight (20 EUR) to Underweight (18 EUR) at 19.58 EUR, alongside Vodafone, Telefonica and Proximus to Underweight and KPN and Telia to Overweight; its sector view stayed Neutral.
  • The sector argument: artificial intelligence agents that shop and switch for customers could take about 5% of revenue from private customers over about five years, because operators discount to win new customers and charge more to old ones; satellite broadband could take about 2.5%. At stock level that is 4% to 7% of revenue and 8% to 13% of EBITDA over five years, about 1% of revenue a year. Barclays put it into valuations by cutting terminal growth by 0.25 to 0.75 percentage points, which cut price targets by 5% to 27% as Barclays states (10% on average; its own chart runs from -2% to -30%); it changed no earnings per share estimates for the thesis.
  • Where OTE sits in it: outside the scoring, inside the cut. Greece is not among the 11 markets Barclays scores for artificial intelligence and satellite risk, and OTE is not in its company exposure charts, but it took the sector-average 10% target cut. OTE's own paragraph gives two reasons: lower terminal growth (its valuation uses an 8.5% cost of capital and 0% terminal growth), and lower wholesale revenue from the PPC-Vodafone fibre venture. Earnings per share fell 4% for 2026 (1.92 to 1.84 EUR) and 1% for 2027 (1.72 to 1.70). The page's "Why Underweight?" box still carries the Overweight-era text ("The Greek competitive environment is relatively benign"), and the same note values Greece inside Deutsche Telekom at 0.5% terminal growth and a 9% cost of capital. The appendix's list of risks to the rating, still written for an Overweight, names only downside risks. The call is a terminal-value judgement applied across the sector, plus one Greek wholesale cut.
  • The Greek argument came after the results (Barclays, 2-Nov-26): PPC's aggressive pricing is spreading; retail fixed revenue is already declining as pricing weakens; the PPC-Vodafone venture points to lower wholesale revenue; these limit growth beyond cost efficiencies. On the call Barclays' analyst asked about satellites and whether PPC's pricing is raising churn; the chief executive gave no churn figures in reply.
  • Its numbers (2-Nov-26 note, after the results): EBITDAaL 1,450 EURm in 2027 (+2.5%) and 1,468 in 2028; revenue growth of about 0.5% a year to 2030; shareholder returns "well supported"; target 18 EUR; upside case 25 EUR (Greece growing 1% to 2% a year for three to five years), downside 13 EUR (more competition in mobile and fixed). The 20-Oct note gave the same values with other reasons (upside: stronger revenue and cost delivery; downside: the Greek macro environment and market risk premium). It models dividends plus buybacks of 440 to 535 EURm a year against its free cash flow of 573 to 601 EURm, the low end of the company's 70% to 100% policy (about 100% in 2026), which is why its 2027 dividend yield (4.4%) is below the incumbents' (6.3%).
  • What it implies: 18 EUR against 18.85 is a 4.5% price fall; with a dividend yield of about 5% the twelve-month total return is about flat, below the sector's. On Barclays' own 20-Oct table OTE is at 4.9x 2027 EV/EBITDAaL against 5.8x for European incumbents, with the same 8.3% equity free cash flow yield.

The other cautious brokers (13 ratings in FactSet: 7 Buy, 4 Hold, 1 Sell, 1 without a rating)

  • Eurobank Equities, Hold, 17.50 EUR (14-Sep-26; 19.60 EUR in July): little room after the 2026 rise; 5.7x 2026 EV/EBITDA, only modestly below the European average; a System Solutions "growth cliff" in 2027. Management's 2027 signal answers the last point, not the first two.
  • Goldman Sachs, Hold, 20.00 EUR and New Street Research, Hold, 21.20 EUR (both 29-Jul-26); a Restricted broker, Hold, 21.60 EUR. Their reasoning is not in hand.
  • Deutsche Bank is shown "Without" a rating since 23-Oct-26 (probably a restriction, not a view).
  • The positive brokers, for contrast: Morgan Stanley Overweight 23.80 EUR (unchanged after the results; base 6.8x EV/EBITDA; bear case 15.20 EUR at 4.5x); NBG Securities Outperform.

Judgement: how seriously to take it

Seriously on the long run; it is not a near-term earnings call, and Barclays applied its sector-wide artificial intelligence and satellite cut to OTE without assessing Greece's exposure. On almost the same 2027 and 2028 EBITDA as Barclays, model update 3 is worth 19.45 to 20.64 EUR at today's to modestly higher multiples, against Barclays' 18. The whole gap is long-run growth and the multiple: whether Greek telecoms grows revenue about 0.5% a year to 2030 and nothing thereafter (Barclays' 2-Nov forecasts and its 0% terminal growth) or 1% to 2% a year (its own 2-Nov upside case, and the model's 1.0% to 1.2% for 2029 to 2030), plus a payout at the low end of the policy. The best evidence for the Sell is in this quarter and on the call, not in Barclays' note: the first decline in retail fixed revenue without Data Com, traditional fixed-line revenue down about 2.5% to 3% from pricing, wired broadband down about 23,600 in the quarter, the broadband base turning down, and the chief executive's remark that competition has increased. The best evidence against is that EBITDAaL still grew 3.1% with the margin up 0.9 points, and that the revenue cuts are mostly low-margin. What would prove Barclays right within six months: a second quarter of falling retail fixed revenue (February 2027), no price step or a capped one, a binding venture with Nova as a tenant, and a 2027 guide below 2%.

10. Richard's other questions

  • Why FY2028 EBITDA consensus "fell through 1H-26 and ... recovered" (the chart Richard read: the PMS Per-stock Dashboard's consensus estimate chart, FactSet by named financial year): on that chart FY2028 EBITDA was flat at about 1,452 to 1,468 EURm through 2025 and January 2026, rose from 1,460 in January 2026 to 1,533 in June, fell to 1,510 in July and 1,500 in August, and partly recovered, to 1,514 by October and November (below the June peak). So the fall came in July and August 2026, around the second-quarter results (29-Jul-26: Morgan Stanley's "top-line weakness" from the transit phase-out; mobile growth halving; Eurobank's System Solutions cliff), after a rise of about 5% from February, when the FY2025 results (26-Feb-26) led brokers to roll their models forward. The pattern of a fall in the first half and a recovery is on the FY2028 earnings per share chart, but in 2025, not 2026: 1.93 EUR in March 2025, 1.61 in June and July 2025, back to 1.82 by November 2025 and 1.82 today. Only five brokers forecast FY2028 EBITDA today (Optima 1,489; Morgan Stanley 1,534; Eurobank 1,492; the Restricted broker 1,543; Goldman Sachs 1,512 EURm; six forecast earnings per share) and probably fewer in 2025, so a single broker joining, leaving or changing can move these lines by several percent; the dashboard does not store the history of the broker count, so the two causes cannot be separated.
  • "Barclays ... may move to neutral": Barclays had already moved further, from Overweight to Underweight in its 20-Oct sector note, and kept Underweight after the results. Richard's read of the analyst's preamble was right (he said he was a bit distant from Greece). A move back would need a lower price against its 18 EUR target, a change in its long-run (terminal growth) view, or the Greek upside the 20-Oct note names as the key risk to its rating (a stronger Greek economy).
  • The Theon-like pattern (a strong stock in a weak group): OTE's weekly moves are largely its own (the Stoxx Europe 600, telecoms and Greek stocks together explain about 10% of them), but the group's weakness has still taken about half of its relative fall since July.
  • Interest rates: OTE trades as a yield stock; the telecoms de-rating of 11-Sep to 9-Oct came with United States ten-year yields above 5%, and a fall below about 4.5% is one of the Sector Memo's triggers for the group.
