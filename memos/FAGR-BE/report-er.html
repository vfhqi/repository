Buying approved drugs from original manufacturers to repackage them increases raw material costs and compresses profit margins. Sourcing shifts away from raw materials will compress traditional compounding profitability unless Fagron optimizes its automated repackaging lines.

Weight-Loss Normalization and Generic Drug Expansion

9.

Excluding drug shortage contributions, the North American Brands and Essentials segment delivered a resilient organic growth rate of 6% in the first half of 2025. Lapping these historical weight-loss revenues creates challenging year-on-year comparisons that will persist until late 2026.

Volatile brand loyalty: Obesity patients frequently prioritized lower prices over brand loyalty by turning to cheaper compounded formulations during active drug shortages. Bernstein research from November 2025 reported that telehealth prescriptions plummeted once the Food and Drug Administration ended the shortage status of semaglutide. Patients began transitioning back to branded options as manufacturers adjusted cash prices through their own internal pharmacy programs. Falling compounding volumes restrict the near-term organic growth of large outsourcing providers that fail to diversify.
11.
Global generic competition: Compounding pharmacies face further disruption from the emergence of ex-United States generic semaglutide launches in markets like Canada and Brazil. Deutsche Bank research from November 2026 projected that generic semaglutide will expand market penetration due to a lower price point. Generic alternatives compete like complex synthetic drugs because semaglutide is a relatively small peptide that can be replicated chemically. Rising generic availability displaces the demand for compounded alternatives by offering highly standardized, affordable branded substitutes.
12.
Other side of the story: Fagron's investments in automated compounding and 3D printing could suffer from severe underutilization if regulatory restrictions on active ingredients intensify. Should the Food and Drug Administration enforce a rapid transition to sterile-to-sterile repackaging, the strategic advantage of raw material sourcing would be entirely lost. Higher procurement costs from buying brand-name drug products would permanently compress operating margins. Operational failure to adapt would severely depress future earnings per share development.

Section 15 – Value chain: supply side

1.
Significant geographic concentration: Fagron sources 60% of its procurement from Asia, exposing the company to regional trade volatility. Within this Asian supply chain, China accounts for 60% of the volume, while Indian manufacturers supply the remaining 40% of materials. Regional concentration means geopolitical tensions or regulatory shutdowns in these countries could disrupt the essential raw material supply globally.
2.
Successful direct procurement: Fagron historically relied on US wholesalers but transitioned to direct sourcing from Indian and Chinese manufacturers to optimize purchase conditions. Sourcing active pharmaceutical ingredients directly from originators helped the company capture additional gross margin points across its Essentials division. Direct import structures require intensive quality controls to ensure that imported batches comply with strict FDA standards.
3.
Heavy product concentration: Active pharmaceutical ingredients, excipients, and finished products represent approximately two-thirds of Fagron's total global procurement. The remaining procurement consists of specialized equipment, packaging materials, and other compounding supplies. Managing a vast catalog of over 25,000 stock-keeping units helps the company minimize single-product supply dependencies over time.
4.
Inefficient logistics structures: Fagron outsources most of its logistical operations, which introduces regional transport inefficiencies. Cold-chain shipping practices generate substantial waste and high packaging costs for sterile compounding deliveries. Regionalizing manufacturing hubs between Wichita and Boston could lower consolidated shipping expenses and improve turnaround times.
5.
Robust inventory safeguards: Fagron maintains a significant inventory position of three and a half to four months of stock to mitigate potential supply interruptions. Buffers of raw materials allow the company to resist sudden price spikes and logistical shocks from international shipping routes. Carrying elevated working capital represents a necessary compromise to maximize product availability for customers consistently.
6.
Efficient pricing pass-through: Fagron demonstrates strong commercial leverage by passing rising raw material and shipping costs to its customer base. Price adjustments can face temporary delays in Europe due to longterm government contracts and rigid reimbursement schedules. Active price adjustments remain vital to protect consolidated gross profit margins from inflationary pressures.
7.
Stringent supplier qualification: Fagron requires all raw material suppliers to maintain certified GMP or ISO 9001 quality compliance. Quality control teams audited over 150 global suppliers during 2025 to verify complete traceability of ingredients. Rigorous screening protocols are essential to prevent costly product contamination risks and regulatory license losses.
8.
Escalating peer cost pressures: Malaysian compounding peer Duopharma Biotech Berhad also imports key active ingredients from China and India, facing severe foreign exchange risks. Duopharma reported that a 5% increase in active ingredient prices would depress its annual earnings by up to 5%. Peer margin sensitivities illustrate that stable API pricing remains critical for compounding pharmacy profitability.
9.
Other side of the story: Fagron's heavy reliance on direct raw material sourcing from Asian manufacturers could expose the company to sudden import restrictions and tariff hikes. If political tensions disrupt trade routes or global regulators enforce stricter quality bans on specific facilities, the company's inventory buffer will deplete rapidly. Sourcing alternative Western-made active ingredients would inflate cost of goods sold and severely compress consolidated margins over the next eighteen months.

Section 16 – Conclusion

Overall conclusion. FY 2024 was a strong year, and it is the high-water mark against which the next 18 months to 3 years should be judged. Fagron reported revenue of €872.0 million, up 14.28% from €762.99 million. Gross margin rose 180 basis points to 62.35%.

Operating profit grew 20.31% to €130.70 million. Net profit reached €81.01 million and EPS climbed 14.39% to €1.10. My conclusion is that EPS will keep growing over the next 18 months to 3 years, but more slowly and more unevenly than FY 2024. Growth will come mainly from acquisitions, Brands and Essentials, and capacity build-out.

Organic compounding volumes and operating leverage will contribute less, and higher financing costs and the end of buybacks will offset part of the gain.

Semaglutide compounding then stopped on May 22, 2025.
