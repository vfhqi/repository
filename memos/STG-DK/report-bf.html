Section 1 – Business model and organisation

A three-division, Americas-weighted cigar and pipe tobacco group whose profitability varies sharply by division. The company operates three commercial divisions: Europe Branded, North America Branded and Rest of World, and North America Online and Retail. The Americas is the largest regional market, with a net sales share of about 50.2% in fiscal year 2025. Divisional EBITDA margins ranged from 31.1% in North America Branded to 12.6% in North America Online and Retail. Coverage is active: Deutsche Bank has six named analysts and DNB Carnegie two.

Revenue predictability is moderate, and delivery against guidance has been mixed. Machine-rolled cigars and smoking tobacco were 50% of net sales in fiscal 2025 and handmade cigars were 35%. High fixed manufacturing costs create negative operating leverage when volumes fall unless prices offset it. Fiscal 2025 net sales landed at the lower end of revised expectations. Guidance was then raised in November 2026, with adjusted EPS expectations lifted to DKK 9.50–11.50.

Section 3 and Section 4 – Industry structure and value chain

The industry is fragmented and shrinking, so profit depends on execution rather than market growth. Cigar and smoking tobacco volumes are in structural decline of at least 3% to 5% per year. The cigar market is highly fragmented, in contrast to the oligopolistic cigarette market. Altria's smokeable segment reached a 64.5% adjusted margin in the first half of 2025, a ceiling well above anything in cigars. Winners are separated from laggards by how well they execute the transition to next-generation products.

STG occupies the profitable stages of the value chain, but one external change threatens them. Leaf is bought from over 5,000 suppliers across more than 40 countries, which leaves growers with little bargaining power. Manufacturers capture the largest share of value-chain profits, and direct online and superstore operations earn about a 40% gross margin. Lifting the US embargo on Cuba is identified as the single most damaging potential change, since Cuban cigars hold 70–75% of the non-US international market but are barred from the US.

Sourcing and processing stages

1.
Fragmented sourcing: Raw leaf tobacco cultivation represents the starting stage of the global value chain where farming remains highly fragmented. Scandinavian Tobacco Group procures its raw leaf tobacco from a vast network of over 5,000 global suppliers rather than participating directly in farming.
