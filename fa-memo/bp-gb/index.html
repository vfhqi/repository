The reasoning behind the inputs

Part 4 is the second set's reasoning as written. Where a later set changed an input, that input's figure in the tables above is the changed one and its reasons are in the changed-inputs file named above; Part 4 is not rewritten.

Four conventions the model's user must know. Without them, three of the input lines will look wrong.

  1. The bridge from the company's own reported figures to the basis consensus uses

  • Profit.
  • Start from IFRS profit attributable to BP shareholders. Remove inventory holding gains and losses to get the RC basis, then add back adjusting items to get underlying RC profit attributable to BP shareholders. Adjusting items are impairments (Q3 2026: $4.0bn post-tax), disposal gains and losses, restructuring, environmental and legal items, and fair-value effects on LNG hedges (BP marks the hedges to market but not the underlying contracts).
  • Underlying RC profit is after hybrid coupons and minorities. That is the brokers' adjusted net income.
  • Gap examples: FY2025 IFRS $55m against underlying $7.49bn; Q2 2026 underlying above IFRS by $1.8bn.
  • EPS. Underlying RC profit divided by the weighted basic share count (15.56bn in Q3 2026; diluted 15.71bn). FactSet's "EPS, brokers' basis" (FY2025: $0.480) is on this basis. FactSet's reported EPS ($0.004) and adjusted diluted EPS ($0.166) are not.
  • EBITDA. Brokers' EBITDA is about underlying RC profit before interest and tax plus depreciation (FY2025 $36.6bn). FactSet's operating EBITDA ($31.3bn) is 17% lower and is not comparable. My margins are on the brokers' basis.

How to read these figures

  • Amounts are in millions of the reporting currency; per-share figures are in units of that currency. Negative figures are shown in brackets.
  • Historical figures use FactSet's standardised lines, which can differ from the company's own presentation.
  • Earnings per share appears on four bases, which must not be mixed: reported basic; FactSet's adjusted diluted, before unusual items; the Viewforth definition, which is the model's standing convention; and the brokers' adjusted basis, on which consensus is based.
  • Consensus is FactSet's median of the broker estimates it holds. Where FactSet's line-by-line consensus differs from the broker-by-broker detail, the line-by-line table takes precedence.
  • Viewforth's forecasts are the Excel model's base case. There are no bull or bear cases.
