Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The evaluation of Fevertree's strategic transition to a US licensing model is critical to determining its future earnings per share trajectory.

The Core Investment Question

1.
Primary question: "how plausible is the change now expected for this company, given its record of delivery and the strengths the business demonstrably has, rather than strengths it would need to acquire?"
2.
Accelerated growth: Fevertree targets a major recovery in earnings per share as it transitions its primary growth engine from the mature United Kingdom market to the expanding United States market. Financial analysts at Jefferies forecast that adjusted earnings per share could double from 28.01p in fiscal year 2024 to 53.3p by fiscal year 2028. This projected earnings recovery is highly dependent on resolving whether Fevertree can execute its strategic transition without incurring significant overheads.
3.
Strategic alliance: Fevertree entered into an exclusive long-term licensing agreement with Molson Coors starting on February 1, 2025, to run its United States operations. Under this capital-light model, Molson Coors assumes full responsibility for sales, marketing, distribution, and local production. This structure is designed to leverage Molson Coors' extensive network of 500,000 accounts to unlock substantial operating efficiencies.

Incentive alignment: Industry experts caution that the ultimate success of the United States partnership depends heavily on motivating local distributor sales representatives.

Deutsche Bank research shows that ready-to-drink volumes expanded by 11% annually between 2019 and 2025 while conventional spirits volumes flatlined. Value rotation: Macroeconomic uncertainty has forced a notable shift in United States consumer purchasing patterns toward affordable options. Jefferies research shows that de-premiumisation trends have intensified since mid-2024 as affordability constraints weigh on discretionary spending. These headwinds are causing retail buyers to select smaller pack sizes or migrate from premium spirits back to mainstream brands.
