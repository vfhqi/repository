Dynamics

US Royalty Structure and Margins

1.
Transformational change: Fevertree Drinks PLC finalized its strategic US partnership with Molson Coors Beverage Company on 1 February 2025. Under this arrangement, Molson Coors acquired the shares of Fevertree USA Inc. for a total cash consideration of $25.4 million to take over local working capital. This transaction shifts the operational financial statements directly into Molson Coors' consolidated accounts.
2.
Shared rewards: Fevertree Drinks PLC extracts its share of US partnership profits through a contractually defined royalty fee. This license agreement is structured to share profits broadly equally at the EBITDA level. Consequently, Fevertree avoids direct exposure to volatile local logistics costs and tariff impacts.
3.
Strong protection: Fevertree Drinks PLC benefits from guaranteed minimum royalty payments across the 2026 to 2030 period. These guaranteed profit levels materially de-risk the US trajectory by shielding the group from underlying market weakness. This structural floor provides high visibility for earnings over the medium term.
4.
Contrary evidence: Fevertree's short-term US profitability fell despite the partnership protections. The US segment delivered an Adjusted EBITDA of £4.1 million in H1 2026 compared to £5.0 million in H1 2025, with margins slipping from 8.1% to 6.2% because of upweighted marketing spend. This near-term margin pressure indicates the royalty floor does not fully prevent transition-related profitability dips.

This trajectory is expected to eventually reach a 16% margin over the long term as local sourcing efficiencies are realized. Consequently, localized US margins will improve from the initial low levels.
