APM Mini-Memo: Made Tech

Written by the Assistant Portfolio Manager, 23 October 2026. Ideas Bench rank 13 in the lists drawn from the Pool of Stocks Eligible for Middle Innings Portfolio Selection. Evidence: the Investment Analyst's memo of 10 October 2026; the research digest and Earnings Review digest of 9 October 2026; the ratings store and dashboard of 23 October 2026.

The one-line view. Bullish on an eighteen-month view and neutral on three years, as a working hypothesis: a debt-free public-sector digital specialist whose contracted backlog supplies the revenue and whose contractor-to-permanent mix repair supplies the margin, clearing the 20% bar at eighteen months by about one percentage point and not at three years.

What the company does

Made Tech sells cleared digital, data and artificial intelligence engineers by the day to the United Kingdom state, modernising legacy and mission-critical systems. Founded in 2008 and listed in 2021, it earned £58.9 million of revenue in the year to 31 May 2026, up 26.8%, with 482 permanent staff. Eight of the ten largest government bodies are clients; the top four accounts are 57% of revenue and Customer A alone 22%. The founder has led since 2008 and holds 28.53% of the equity.

A plausible investment thesis

Two engines, both already evidenced. Outside the company, the June 2025 Spending Review gave departments budget certainty to 2028-29 and the Procurement Act put a sovereign preference behind mid-market suppliers. Inside it, management unwound a contractor spike (19% of the billable base in FY25, 14% in the first half of FY26, a 10% target) and hired 108 permanent staff.

The mechanism to earnings per share runs from billable heads and utilisation through gross margin and a near-fixed overhead. FY26 adjusted earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation rose 69% to £5.89 million at a 10.0% margin, from 7.5%; adjusted earnings per share rose 72.5% to 2.38p. A £40 million four-year consortium award in August 2026 took the backlog to a record £115 million and lifted FY27 revenue guidance to £63 million to £66 million. The Investment Analyst's memo of 10 October has earnings per share compounding at 21.3% a year to 4.25p in FY29.

Drivers to track: the contractor ratio against 15% monthly, utilisation against 72% quarterly, backlog against £85 million semi-annually, debtor days against 50. The first dated test is the first-half FY27 results in February 2027.

The Target Setup

The Target Setup is 'Big Corporate Change-driven EPSU'. The Investment Analyst's memo of 10 October rated Setups' Fit/Crispness C: the mechanism is clear, a delivery-model reset dropping a demand recovery through at a higher margin, and the outputs have arrived along the whole chain. But only two of the twelve internal forces are strong and three moderate, with no chief-executive change, so the inputs are narrower than the pattern wants. The demand half looks like 'Demand-driven EPSU' at the end-market level, yet the company fails that pattern on structure: no dominant position, no one-to-many scalability, no oligopoly, modest fixed-cost leverage.

Case Riskiness and simplicity

The Investment Analyst's memo of 10 October rated Case Riskiness C, between a Simplicity element at B and a Riskiness element at D. Simple: one country, one segment, one customer type, three drivers. Not low-risk: a £74 million company on the Alternative Investment Market with about half a million dollars a day of trading, one payer, a falling 30% gross margin, bookings that swing 38% to 128% a year, a time-and-materials model facing artificial-intelligence deflation. The same four numbers can move together and against us on one fiscal event, with no cash return and a correlated multiple to cushion the fall.

Business quality

The Investment Analyst's memo of 10 October rated Foundations' Robustness C: adequate, not robust. The moat is procedural, not economic. Frameworks enforce transparent rate cards and global integrators discount for volume, so the gross margin fell from 34.2% in FY24 to 30.4% against a sector median of 37% and Kainos's near-40%. The improvement scope is the one B: contractor mix to 10% is worth about 1.6 points of gross profit and utilisation toward 80% to 85% about 2.0 points.

No re-rating is in the modal case: Kainos is 19.2 times and the memo places Made Tech about a third below. On 14.0 times, the memo's modal return is 20.9% a year over eighteen months and 15.2% over three, with no cash return; the ratings store re-bases these at 49.5p on 23 October to 21.5% and 15.4%, and the script's TSR/Valuation reading is B, where the memo's own element grade was C. The entry at which the three-year path also clears is about 44p.

Value chain and thematics: the pros and the cons

Pros: customers and policy-makers act for the company; every key client since the initial public offering was retained through FY26, and the Procurement Act, Procurement Policy Note 026 and G-Cloud 15 favour domestic mid-market suppliers. Cons: competitors do not; the industry is fragmented, Capita leading at 6.6%, and the attitude in slowdowns is to discount. The script's Thematic Fit/Momentum reading of 23 October is B, where the memo pinned C on 10 October. The script's Technical Momentum reading of 23 October is B, Stock Relative Strength 94. Sell-side Momentum is not in our files.

The strongest counter-case

This is a false friend: a record backlog won at tight public-sector rates, carried by a permanent workforce hired ahead of conversion, where one fiscal event moves all four big customers. The gross margin has fallen 380 basis points in two years while the backlog rose; FY26 bookings fell 38% to £50.7 million and were rescued by one award; growth halves to about 10% in FY27. If call-offs slip for two quarters after an Autumn Budget pledged to halve discretionary consultancy spend, 482 permanent heads become bench cost, utilisation returns to the FY23 level of 68% to 70%, the margin falls back to 5% to 7.5% and the multiple falls with the earnings to the 6 to 12 times seen within five years. That is the 22p bear case.
