Section 3 – Margin-Linked Formula and Revenue Base

Section summary
  1. Siemens Energy's brand license fee is determined by an escalating sliding scale linked directly to its reported adjusted EBITA margin.
  2. The brand license fee excludes Siemens Gamesa's revenues, protecting the wind segment's financial turnaround from parent royalties.
  3. Industrial precedents demonstrate that post-merger or spin-off brand licensing structures typically rely on fixed rates rather than sliding scales.

Mechanical Calculation of the License Fee

1.
Sliding scale royalty mechanics: Siemens Energy operates under a highly sensitive brand license framework where the royalty percentage escalates as the company's profitability rises. Under the original 2020 spin-off agreement, the sliding scale starts at a base rate of 0.3% of revenue when the company's adjusted EBITA margin is up to 6.5%. This structure was designed to provide financial relief to the company during its early, lowermargin standalone years. It ensured that the brand licensing overhead remained proportionate to operational cash flow during initial restructuring phases. Punitive margin-linked escalation: The royalty fee rate climbs steeply to a maximum rate of 1.2% of revenue once the adjusted EBITA margin reaches 9.0% or above. There is no contractually disclosed cap on the total absolute payments that can be collected under this arrangement. This means that during a strong power equipment demand upcycle, Siemens Energy is penalized with exponential royalty increases as its operational efficiency improves. Consequently, this sliding scale mechanism acts as a severe drag on net income margin expansion precisely when the company achieves its peak operational targets.
2.
Absolute cost calculations: The practical application of this sliding scale formula has resulted in ballooning cash outflows for Siemens Energy. At its lower historical margin levels in FY2024, the company paid a modest €51 million in brand fees under the lower end of the sliding scale. However, as underlying margins expanded toward the 9.0% threshold, the fee escalated to approximately €300 million in FY2025 and is projected to reach nearly €400 million in FY2026. This massive increase highlights how the formula captures a larger share of marginal revenue as the business cycle peaks.

Exact Revenue Base Exclusions

3.
Exclusion of wind power revenues: The brand licensing fee is applied exclusively to the revenue base generated under the core "Siemens Energy" brand, which strictly excludes Siemens Gamesa. This segment-level exclusion is a key structural element of the Trademark and Name Use (Sub-)License Agreement signed on March 31, 2020. Because Siemens Gamesa retains its own independent brand heritage and separate operational licensing terms, its revenues are exempt from the sliding scale calculations. This contractual exemption protects the group's wind turbine segment from contributing to legacy parent royalties during its post-merger integration.
4.
Applied revenue base segment mapping: The actual revenue base subject to the sliding scale fee comprises Gas Services, Grid Technologies, and the Transformation of Industry segment. In FY2025, these non-Gamesa businesses generated the vast majority of the group's €39,077 million in total revenue. By isolating the revenue base to these segments, the fee is directly tied to the legacy businesses that rely most heavily on the Siemens trademark. However, as these segments grow rapidly due to global grid electrification, the addressable revenue base for the fee expands dramatically. Accounting classification within group overhead: Siemens Energy accounts for the brand license fee centrally rather than allocating the cost down to individual operating segments. The corporate brand license expense is recorded under "Other central items" within the group's Selling, General, and Administrative (SG&A) expenses. This classification ensures that individual segment EBITA margins appear unaffected by the royalty. However, at the consolidated group level, the fee represents approximately 1% of non-Gamesa revenue, creating a midtriple-digit million euro headwind to total group profitability.
