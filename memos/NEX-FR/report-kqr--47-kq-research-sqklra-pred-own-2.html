EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The comprehensive analysis of Nexans' (NEX-FR) adjusted earnings per share bridge from adjusted EBITDA for 2026 to 2028 reveals that the Key Question can be partly answered with medium-to-high confidence, though several critical variables remain unresolved pending final accounting disclosures. The overall confidence in broker consensus models is medium due to outstanding purchase-price allocation variables and vessel depreciation curves, particularly concerning the Republic Wire acquisition completed in June 2026 and the newly commissioned Electra cable-laying vessel.

The evidence demonstrates that while the structural framework of the EBITDA-to-EPS conversion is well-established through broker research and company filings, significant modeling discrepancies persist across covering analysts, creating material uncertainty around the consensus earnings trajectory for this French electrification cable manufacturer.

The analysis establishes that Nexans finalized its transition to a pure electrification player by classifying its entire Industry and Solutions segment as discontinued operations under IFRS 5, which applied to both the 2025 results and the comparative 2024 periods to maintain absolute consistency. This strategic transformation provides a clean continuing-operations baseline for forward projections, with restated adjusted EBITDA for continuing operations at €728 million representing an all-time high margin of 11.9% of standard sales.

However, the bridge from this EBITDA base to adjusted EPS encounters substantial complexity at each conversion step, driven by capital-intensive expansion projects, acquisition accounting uncertainties, and international tax rate volatility that collectively challenge the precision of broker forecasts through 2028. 2 The depreciation and amortization assumptions represent the first major source of modeling divergence, as the Electra vessel was completed at a total cost of €380 million and entered operation in June 2026, yet the exact depreciation schedule over the vessel's useful life remains undisclosed by management.

This gap is compounded by the Republic Wire purchase-price allocation, where provisional goodwill was recognized at €474 million, leaving €279 million in acquired net attributable assets, creating uncertainty around the magnitude of amortizable intangible assets that will flow through adjusted EBIT. Broker estimates for total depreciation and amortization show material variance, with Hana Financial projecting Nexans' total D&A will rise from €156 million in 2025 to €188 million in 2026 and reach €247 million in 2027, while Deutsche Bank models significantly higher figures that suggest different treatment of acquired intangibles and capital project timing.

The net financial items and tax rate assumptions introduce additional complexity to the EBITDA-to-EPS bridge, though the fixed-rate refinancing provides some stability. Nexans announced on October 22, 2026, that it used a new bond issuance to repay the bridge facility for Republic Wire, with the bond having a five-year maturity and a 4.25% fixed coupon that eliminates Euribor exposure. However, the effective tax rate remains highly volatile, as Nexans' H1 2026 financial report showed an effective tax rate of 32.66% compared to 31.27% in H1 2025, substantially above the French statutory rate due to foreign tax rate differentials and unrecognized deferred assets.

This elevated tax burden creates meaningful drag on the EBT-to-EPS conversion that brokers model with varying degrees of conservatism, particularly as the US-based Republic Wire operations introduce new regional tax dynamics into the group structure.

The share count and minority interest assumptions represent the most transparent element of the bridge, with Nexans' share capital at December 31, 2025, consisting of 43,744,779 fully paid-up ordinary shares, each with a par value of 1 euro and carrying identical voting and profit-participation rights. The company maintains a simplified single-class equity structure with limited dilution risk, as there were 1,291,564 outstanding free and performance shares at June 30, 2026, representing approximately 2.95% of the total capital stock.

Minority interests are concentrated in the Nexans Continuous Copper Casting joint venture, where the 70% controlled subsidiary has the remaining 30% held by non-controlling partners, though this represents an immaterial drag on group net income. The primary modeling divergence centers on whether brokers use basic or diluted share counts, with some analysts building forecasts on 43.70 million shares while others incorporate dilutive instruments to reach 44.90 million shares.

The rebuilt 2025 continuing-operations base provides critical context for evaluating future growth rates, as the restated 2025 continuing net income stood at €219 million, whereas discontinued operations added another €138 million, resulting in basic continuing earnings per share of €4.90 alongside discontinued basic earnings per share of €3.18. Broker models exhibit significant discrepancies in their treatment of this baseline, with Bernstein Research projecting an adjusted EPS of €8.34 for 2025, which is much higher than Nexans' continuing EPS of €4.90, suggesting incomplete adjustments for the three divested businesses.

The discontinued portfolio comprised AmerCable sold to Mattr on January 2, 2025, for an enterprise value of 280 million US dollars, while the industrial cable division Lynxeo was divested on June 30, 2025, for an enterprise value of €525 million, and Autoelectric entered exclusive negotiations for sale to Motherson on December 22, 2025, with closing expected in mid-2026.

The critical information gaps that prevent full resolution of the Key Question center on three primary variables. First, the final Republic Wire purchase-price allocation will determine the magnitude of customer relationship amortization flowing through adjusted EBIT for 2027 and 2028, yet the company's interim financial report from July 30, 2026, confirmed that 474 million euros was recognized as provisional goodwill pending full-year audit completion.

Second, the Electra vessel depreciation curve remains undisclosed despite the vessel's substantial €380 million capitalized cost, creating uncertainty around the annual EBIT drag from this strategic asset. Third, the Charleroi plant expansion timing introduces variability in depreciation onset, as Deutsche Bank reports the new capacity is scheduled to come online in mid-2027, meaning full-year depreciation impact will only materialize in 2028.

The competitive and market implications of the EBITDA-to-EPS bridge uncertainties extend beyond Nexans' standalone valuation to affect relative positioning versus Prysmian and other capital goods peers. Jefferies highlights that Nexans trades at a 30% discount to peers, yet this valuation gap depends critically on whether analysts exclude amortization of acquired intangibles when calculating adjusted EBIT multiples.

The discrepancies in depreciation treatment particularly matter because Prysmian's D&A was €646 million in 2025 and is projected to reach €509 million in 2026, demonstrating that scale differences in asset intensity directly affect EBITDA-to-EBIT conversion efficiency. Any material revision to Nexans' D&A assumptions would alter the perceived operational leverage and margin expansion trajectory that underpins the investment thesis for this pureplay electrification transformation story.

The execution risks and alternative scenarios that could derail consensus EPS forecasts center primarily on adverse movements in the unresolved variables. If the final Republic Wire purchase-price allocation shifts more value from goodwill to amortizable customer relationships, incremental intangible charges will depress adjusted earnings per share through 2028 given the high premium paid for the Cincinnati asset at a 40% premium to Nexans' own multiple.

Similarly, sustained elevation in the effective tax rate would compress the EBT-to-EPS conversion, as foreign tax rate differentials will limit the profit flow unless offset by regional tax incentives. The most severe downside scenario involves prolonged subsea project delays that create fixed-cost underabsorption at key manufacturing facilities, while the most significant upside case rests on accelerating data center demand that drives volume leverage faster than incremental depreciation charges accumulate.
