EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF) 4 findings

1.
Materiality of CHANGE: Mapfre has executed a transformational USD 1.54 billion acquisition of Safety Insurance

Group that fundamentally reshapes its North American profile, while simultaneously concluding a multi-year bancassurance restructuring with CaixaBank that delivered EUR 570.8 million in capital. These strategic pivots coincide with orderly executive succession, including CEO Antonio Huertas' re-election to a new four-year mandate and the transition to CFO José Luis Jiménez, establishing governance continuity through 2030.

The cumulative effect represents the most significant structural transformation in a decade, positioning Mapfre to migrate from a Spain-centric insurer with fragmented international exposure toward a premium Northeast US platform projected to compete with Brazil as the second-largest profit contributor. However, the majority ownership by Fundacion Mapfre, which controls 67.6% of share capital, places the group under less shareholder pressure to maximize profitability compared to free-float peers, moderating the urgency for return optimization.

The strategic reorientation is further reinforced by upgraded financial targets for 2024-2026, including average ROE of 10% to 11%, combined ratio of 95% to 96%, premium growth above 6%, Solvency II ratio of 200%, and a payout ratio above 50%, signaling management's confidence in delivering sustainable value creation despite persistent emerging market currency headwinds that eroded reported growth by 420 basis points in 2025.

Appendix

Outliers worth noting

Mapfre's adjusted return on equity reached 13.3% in FY2025, placing the group in line with top-tier European composite insurance peers, yet this performance represents a genuine statistical outlier when contextualized against the company's own historical trajectory and strategic guidance framework. The group originally targeted an average ROE of 10% to 11% under its 2024-2026 Strategic Plan before subsequently upgrading the 2026 target to above 13%, suggesting that management's initial conservative stance may have systematically underestimated the group's structural earnings power or alternatively that recent outperformance stems from transient benign underwriting conditions rather than sustainable operational improvements.

Cross-reference to Section 10 reveals that this ROE beat occurred alongside a non-life combined ratio of 92.2%, which outperformed the strategic target range of 95% to 96% by a substantial 2.8 percentage points, raising questions about whether these margin expansions can persist once European claims frequency and catastrophe losses revert to historical base rates. Brazil's subsidiary dividend contribution included EUR 80 million in extraordinary dividends brought forward to avoid fiscal changes, representing an unexplained acceleration of capital repatriation that distorts the group's underlying cash generation profile.

This extraordinary remittance, when combined with total subsidiary dividend upstreaming of EUR 900 million in FY2025–a 28% increase year-on-year–with Iberia providing EUR 348 million, creates a statistical anomaly in parent company cash flow coverage that materially exceeds both the group's historical dividend payout patterns and European composite peer norms. Cross-reference to Section 4 confirms that this elevated remittance cycle enabled a dividend payout ratio of 51.4%, representing a total allocation of EUR 554 million in cash, yet the pull-forward nature of the Brazilian contribution suggests this coverage may prove non-repeatable in subsequent reporting periods, particularly if anticipated Latin American fiscal reforms materialize or currency depreciation accelerates.

Life insurance premiums surged by 11.6% to EUR 6,679 million, driven by high demand for savings products in Spain, constituting a top-decile growth rate that diverges sharply from the group's stagnant Non-Life premium trajectory and raises questions about the sustainability and profitability of this product mix shift. Crossreference to Section 9 highlights that Spain Life Protection premiums grew 6.6% in H1 2026 with a highly profitable combined ratio of 75.7%, while global Life Risk recorded an outstanding 64.8% combined ratio in Q1 2026, yet the 11.6% aggregate Life growth significantly exceeds the Protection segment pace, implying that the expansion is disproportionately concentrated in lower-margin Life Savings products that generate weaker risk-adjusted returns and consume higher capital intensity relative to the Protection book.

This compositional anomaly within the Life segment warrants deeper scrutiny, as European composite peers typically derive superior economics from Protection-led growth rather than Savings-led volume expansion. Italy's result was dragged by a EUR 14 million reserve increase for unprofitable motor portfolios, representing a material adverse development in a market where Mapfre has historically struggled to achieve technical profitability and where EMEA operations are returning to profitability following strict technical control plans and structural cost reductions in auto lines.

This EUR 14 million reserve strengthening in Italy during H1 2026 constitutes a genuine statistical outlier when benchmarked against the group's broader combined ratio
