EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Key Question examines whether competition for affluent and active Swedish customers is compressing platform pricing at Avanza Bank Holding AB (AZA-SE), Sweden's largest digital savings and investment platform with 2.39 million customers and SEK 1,277 billion of savings capital as of 30 September 2026. The evidence demonstrates that pricing pressure exists but is primarily cyclical rather than structurally destructive, driven by temporary customer mix shifts during periods of lower retail trading activity rather than permanent list price erosion.

This question matters critically because commission lines represent 65% of Avanza's operating income, meaning any sustained pricing compression would directly dilute earnings per share. launched aggressive marketing campaigns including one commission-free week per month on OMXS30 buy transactions for existing users and fully commission-free Nordic trading through June 2027 for new customers acquired in late 2026, which will directly compress overall brokerage margins across the Swedish digital platform landscape if Avanza is forced to respond with similar fee waivers.

However, Nordnet reduced its foreign exchange fee on non-Nordic transactions to fifteen basis points from twenty-five basis points for new customers, directly challenging Avanza's standard currency conversion markup of twenty-five basis points. Avanza has defended its high-value segments by offering a manual currency exchange account within endowment insurance for Private Banking and Pro customers at twelve and a half basis points instead of the standard twenty-five basis point rate.

Montrose has emerged as a specialized platform backed by Carnegie to target active and affluent Swedish savers, representing a direct threat to Avanza's lucrative Private Banking and Pro customer segments which account for over forty-five percent of total savings capital and generate half of Avanza's brokerage turnover. Nordnet announced a major private banking joint venture with ABG Sundal Collier on 15 September 2026 to launch in 2027, which will compete directly with Avanza's premium segments and intensify high-net-worth fee pressure because it combines digital scale with established investment banking advice. Deutsche Bank Research and DNB Carnegie both assume that Avanza's dominant domestic position and superior platform features will insulate it from immediate structural pricing deterioration, while Jefferies Research emphasizes that international discount brokers like Interactive Brokers will intensify pricing pressure on active traders. Avanza reported robust capital accumulation as net inflows reached SEK 23.1 billion in the third quarter of 2026, representing a sixty-one percent increase year-over-year, supported by record quarterly net fund purchases of SEK 18 billion which captured a thirty-two percent share of the entire Swedish fund market net inflows.

However, Nordnet captured net savings of SEK 25.6 billion during the third quarter of 2026, a twenty-three percent yearover-year increase driving total savings capital up twenty-eight percent to nearly SEK 1.5 trillion, meaning Nordnet is compounding assets faster than its domestic peer because of its broader geographic diversification. Avanza's Private Banking customer count grew twenty-eight percent year-over-year to 51,200 clients by the end of the third quarter of 2026, while Private Banking savings capital rose thirty-one percent to SEK 526 billion, outperforming the platform's overall standard retail asset growth because premium clients contribute disproportionate assets to the platform's high-margin services.

Avanza Sigma demonstrated robust early adoption by attracting SEK 710 million in assets under management within one month of its broad launch on August 31, 2026, after previously accumulating SEK 100 million during its quiet beta testing phase, showing that integrating automated quantitative factor investing can help Avanza penetrate the traditional discretionary market.
