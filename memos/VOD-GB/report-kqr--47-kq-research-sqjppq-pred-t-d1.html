Section 2 – Cost-Led Margin Expansion and Net Opex Reductions

Section summary
  1. Vodafone plans to expand its Adjusted EBITDAaL margin through a two billion euro gross opex reduction program.
  2. The European opex targets directly overlap with the newly upgraded one billion pound VodafoneThree synergies in the United Kingdom.
  3. Historical peer precedents indicate that gross savings are heavily diluted by wage inflation and integration costs.

European opex reduction targets

1.
Ambitious margin expansion: Vodafone is targeting mid-term operating leverage to expand its group adjusted EBITDAaL margin from 28.1% in fiscal year 2026. The group plans to achieve this through a cumulative €2.0bn gross efficiency potential. Management expects these initiatives to deliver a €1.0bn net European opex reduction between fiscal years 2027 and 2030. If successful, this net reduction will directly enhance consolidated earnings per share.
2.
Direct synergy overlap: The €1.0bn net European cost-out target explicitly includes committed UK cost synergies from the VodafoneThree joint venture. Vodafone expects that the majority of the UK synergies will be realized as opex savings. This opex contribution will support the broader European and Shared Operations cost-reduction targets. Consequently, any delay in UK integration will directly impact the European margin target.
3.
Slowing historical pace: Morgan Stanley notes that Vodafone has successfully removed €2.0bn of net opex from Europe since 2018. However, the pace of these cuts has slowed by roughly 45% over the last four years. The broker indicates that the group cut €1.3bn between 2018 and 2022 but only €0.7bn between 2022 and 2026. This historical deceleration raises questions about the execution of future targets.
4.
Contrary evidence: Morgan Stanley argues that the targeted net opex reductions represent only 5% of total European opex. While Vodafone has already completed 11,000 role reductions, the broker believes the current program is too small. Unless Vodafone expands its efficiency program to cover emerging markets, margin expansion will remain limited.

Rationalized network infrastructure: Deutsche Bank Research reports that network cost synergies will make up 30% of the VodafoneThree target. The integration plan involves reducing combined macro cell sites from 37,000 to 26,000 over time. The broker highlights that organisational and retail synergies are already running ahead of schedule. However, network savings will only begin materializing from the second year of the merger.

Heavy upfront investment: Vodafone expects group restructuring and integration costs to peak at €0.7bn in fiscal year 2027. This projection includes approximately €0.4bn of upfront integration costs specifically tied to the UK merger. Deutsche Bank Research notes that these peak integration expenses will initially dilute operating free cash flow. This dilution means that material opex benefits will only flow through from fiscal year 2028.

Centralized role reductions: Vodafone has already made rapid progress on headcount reduction as part of its simplicity agenda. The group has delivered over 1,200 role reductions across Europe and Shared Operations in a single quarter. Management claims these actions keep the company on track for material net opex reductions by the fiscal year end. This centralized control represents a key execution strength that peers often lack.
