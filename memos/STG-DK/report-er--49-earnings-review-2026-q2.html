Section 1 – Brief company description and history

Section summary
  1. Sell-side sentiment immediately prior to the Q2 2026 results was highly cautious due to persistent European volume declines and severe margin compression.
  2. The strategic divestment of the German fine-cut tobacco business for DKK 1.30 billion emerged as a major positive catalyst to reduce leverage.
  3. Market focus for Scandinavian Tobacco Group centered on traditional cigar stabilization, contrasting with global peers focused heavily on fast-growing smoke-free alternatives.
1.
Dominant global presence: Scandinavian Tobacco Group holds a premier position as a world-leading manufacturer of cigars and pipe tobacco. The company maintains the largest portfolio of handmade cigar brands in the United States while serving as the undisputed global leader in pipe tobacco globally. Its diverse operations span over 100 countries with key market-leading roles in European machine-rolled cigars and selective oral nicotine brands.

2.
Concentrated revenue stream: The core operations of Scandinavian Tobacco Group are categorized into three distinct product divisions that drive net sales. In the 2025 fiscal year, machine-rolled cigars and smoking tobacco represented 50% of net sales, whereas handmade cigars contributed 35% of group revenue. Nextgeneration oral nicotine pouches under the XQS brand and accessories accounted for the remaining 15% of consolidated net sales combined.
3.
Severe fundamental pressure: Scandinavian Tobacco Group has experienced fundamental headwinds and contracting profitability since its COVID-19 pandemic peak. In the 2025 fiscal year, reported net sales decreased by 1.8% to DKK 9,036 million from DKK 9,202 million in the 2024 fiscal year. This organic top-line deterioration reached negative 3.1% for the full year, illustrating an accelerated structural decline in demand across key European and North American markets.
4.
Contracting profitability metrics: Profitability metrics for Scandinavian Tobacco Group registered notable declines during its recent historical period. For the full year of 2025, EBITDA before special items dropped to DKK 1,791 million compared to DKK 2,079 million in 2024. This contraction represented an EBITDA margin of 19.8% in 2025, representing a drop of 2.80 percentage points from the 22.6% EBITDA margin delivered in 2024.
5.
Slashed shareholder returns: Underwhelming operational execution has heavily impaired shareholder returns for Scandinavian Tobacco Group. Adjusted earnings per share plummeted to DKK 10.80 in 2025 from DKK 13.70 in 2024 and DKK 14.40 in 2023. Consequently, the company was forced to reset its dividend payout to DKK 4.50 per share on 2025 earnings from DKK 8.50 in 2024, representing a 47% reduction.
6.
Lagging peer performance: While Scandinavian Tobacco Group struggled with margin compression, large global tobacco peers achieved stronger performance. Philip Morris International grew its 2025 adjusted net revenues by 7.3% to over $40.60 billion while expanding its adjusted operating margin. Similarly, British American Tobacco posted a 2.3% constant-currency increase in adjusted profit from operations for 2025. This divergence suggests that Scandinavian Tobacco Group faced firm-specific structural demand issues that did not affect consolidated cigarette giants.
7.
Persistent stock underperformance: The capital market severely penalized Scandinavian Tobacco Group's shares for its deteriorating financial performance over multiple years. In the 12 months leading up to the Q2 earnings release, the company's absolute share price performance fell by 17.69%. Over the preceding six months, the stock dropped by 27.70%, though it managed a minor recovery of 3.60% in the three months prior to August 2026.
8.
Severe relative discount: The equity valuation of Scandinavian Tobacco Group also trailed the broader Danish stock market indices on a relative basis. Relative to the OMX Copenhagen PI, the company's share price underperformed by 25.31% over the prior 12 months. This underperformance widened to a relative decline of 40.25% over the six months leading up to August 2026, showcasing weak investor sentiment.
9.
Downgraded analyst expectations: Broker analysis reflects the progressive loss of confidence in the company's turnaround initiatives leading up to 2026. Analyst Niklas Ekman at DNB Carnegie downgraded Scandinavian Tobacco Group to a Hold rating on 7 March 2025. Ekman steadily slashed his target price from DKK 115 on 7 March 2025 down to DKK 75 on 20 April 2026 as earnings visibility worsened.
10.
Pivotal rating upgrade: Broker consensus was deeply divided regarding the company's prospects before the Q2 results were reported. Analyst Damian McNeela of Deutsche Bank maintained a Hold rating and cut his target price from DKK 90 to DKK 77 on 5 March 2026. However, McNeela upgraded the shares to a Buy rating on 28 July 2026 with a target price of DKK 79 following positive portfolio modifications.
11.
Value-accretive brand divestment: The primary catalyst for the broker upgrade in July 2026 was the strategic divestment of the German fine-cut tobacco business. On 22 July 2026, Scandinavian Tobacco Group signed an agreement to sell its BREAK and Moro brands to Japan Tobacco for DKK 1.30 billion. This transaction valued at DKK 1 billion post-tax was praised for its attractive multiples of 12x EBITDA and 3.6x sales compared to the company's historical multiples.
12.
Elevated financial leverage: Elevated debt levels had severely constrained the financial flexibility of Scandinavian Tobacco Group leading up to the middle of 2026. The company's leverage ratio stood at 3.0x net debt to EBITDA at the end of the first half of 2026, which is identical to the leverage registered at the end of 2025. This leverage remained substantially above the target threshold of 2.5x set by the company's capital allocation framework.

Section 3 – Key moving parts and changes

Section summary
  1. Scandinavian Tobacco Group delivered Q2 2026 earnings that beat broker consensus, driven by resilient North American premium cigar demand and lower operating costs.
  2. Full-year guidance was fully maintained despite severe European disruptions caused by a rare raw tobacco quality issue.
  3. Balance sheet leverage remained elevated at 3.0x net debt to EBITDA, but upcoming divestment proceeds are expected to restore financial flexibility.
1.
Stable leadership: Executive management at Scandinavian Tobacco Group remains highly experienced under Chief Executive Officer Niels Frederiksen and Chief Financial Officer Marianne Rørslev Bock. Board of Directors Chairman Henrik Brandt continues to provide corporate continuity since his appointment in 2022. Operational execution is governed by these long-standing executives to guide the group's transition toward nextgeneration categories. Underwhelming historical execution has kept management under intense scrutiny to stabilize earnings under the new Focus2030 corporate strategy framework.
2.
Lucrative divestment: Scandinavian Tobacco Group signed an asset purchase agreement on July 22, 2026, to sell its non-core German fine-cut brands BREAK and Moro to Japan Tobacco. The pre-tax transaction value of DKK 1.3 billion represents an attractive valuation of approximately 12x EBITDA. Post-tax cash proceeds of DKK 1.0 billion will directly reduce corporate debt upon closing in late 2026. Such portfolio pruning represents a key milestone under the Focus2030 program to sharpen focus on core brands globally.
3.
Ongoing integration: Synergies from the acquisition of Mac Baren for DKK 535 million in 2024 are supporting the group's manufacturing efficiency. Scandinavian Tobacco Group expects to generate DKK 150 million in annual cost savings from this combination. Integration activities have progressed well despite past geopolitical uncertainties and supply chain disruptions. Cost-efficiency programs will offset traditional smoking tobacco headwinds in core European geographies.
4.
Positive insourcing: The group will reclaim the SAIL cigar trademark in Canada from its distributor in the second quarter of 2027. Reintegrating this premium brand under own distribution is expected to provide a meaningful revenue tailwind. Direct commercial control over the SAIL brand will protect margins from intermediary distribution fees. Strategic commercial repatriation projects will partially offset divestmentled revenue dilution in the outer years.
5.
Restructured distribution: Scandinavian Tobacco Group established its own sales organization in Norway in June 2026 to optimize regional operations. Sales from Norway will now be reported under Europe Branded instead of North America Branded and Rest of the World. Favorable geographic reporting changes reflect the group's policy to house direct-sales markets within Europe Branded. Realigned sales infrastructure will support European operating margin recovery despite the removal of high-margin fine-cut brands.

Structural volume contraction: Global cigar volumes are experiencing a structural decline of 3% to 5% annually, dragging down corporate net sales. Scandinavian Tobacco Group has struggled to fully mitigate lower volumes with pricing power. Mass-market cigars face persistent headwinds as consumers migrate to alternative nicotine products. Pricing strategies and SKU rationalization are essential to protect operating profits from structural volume erosion.

Resilient handmade demand: Handmade cigars in the United States delivered robust organic sales growth of 5% in the second quarter of 2026. Scandinavian Tobacco Group gained market share in its premium branded business-to-business division during 2026. Consumer demand for premium cigars has proven highly resilient despite broader macroeconomic pressure. Volume outperformance across retail superstores will support group top-line stabilization over the near term.

Production of the Signature brand PLC was severely impacted because a leaf defect prevented cigars from burning satisfactorily. Obsolete product write-downs and related expenses totaled DKK 35 million in the second quarter of 2026. Normalized supply chains will allow the group to regain European market share momentum in 2027.

Accelerated marketing investments will weigh on short-term cash flow during the early expansion.

High interest rates also raise wholesale inventory holding costs across global distribution networks.

Regulatory headwinds: The upcoming Tobacco Products Directive TPD3 in Europe presents potential regulatory hurdles around 2028 to 2030. Anticipated legislation is expected to focus heavily on marketing restrictions, age verification, and track-and-trace requirements. Tightening regulations on flavor bans could disrupt Next Generation Product growth and oral nicotine distribution. Extreme regulatory scrutiny will keep equity valuations depressed relative to non-tobacco consumer staples.

Delivering DKK 200 million in annual cost savings under Focus2030 would quickly stabilize margins and drive a significant stock re-rating. Successful cost rationalization would trigger a strong multi-year stock recovery from historic valuation lows.
