EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Section summary
  1. Subsea 7's fourth quarter and full year 2025 results delivered a substantial earnings beat, demonstrating exceptional margin expansion across both conventional and renewables divisions, while record backlog coverage and conservative 2026 guidance position the standalone entity for continued operational outperformance ahead of the transformational Saipem merger completion in late 2026. The core investment thesis has strengthened materially following the results. Subsea 7 operates as the leading global subsea contractor, leveraging its extensive fleet of high-specification pipelay vessels and installation assets to deliver integrated offshore engineering solutions across oil, gas, and offshore wind markets. The pending nil-premium merger with Saipem will create Saipem7, a top-three global energy services player with projected pro forma revenues exceeding $26 billion by 2027 and annual synergies of €300 million by 2029. The standalone business demonstrated robust cyclical momentum, with fourth quarter Adjusted EBITDA reaching a record $477 million at a 24.3% margin, beating consensus by 11%. Full year EBITDA expanded 37% year-on-year

to $1.49 billion, representing a 21% margin versus the prior year's 16%. This margin trajectory confirms that higher-pricing contracts awarded in 2024 are now flowing directly into reported profitability, while legacy lowmargin contracts continue to roll off the backlog. The most critical changes to the predictable 18-month to 3-year EPS trajectory center on consecutive margin guidance upgrades and exceptional cash conversion.

Management raised full-year 2026 Adjusted EBITDA margin guidance to approximately 24%, marking the second consecutive upgrade from the initial 22% target. This structural margin expansion is supported by $13.8 billion in backlog covering 96% of 2026 revenue guidance at the midpoint, compared to historical averages of only 78%. Furthermore, working capital released $420 million in the fourth quarter, transitioning the balance sheet to a net cash position of $21 million including lease liabilities, compared to net debt of $602 million at the end of 2024.

This unprecedented cash generation enables €555 million in special dividends to shareholders prior to merger closing, alongside an ordinary dividend of NOK 13 per share totaling approximately $400 million for 2026. The primary IRR drivers supporting the investment case include structural vessel capacity constraints, direct sole-source contract awards, and pro forma synergy delivery. Global high-specification subsea installation vessel utilization remains extremely tight at 90-95%, with all three major contractors fully booked into 2027-2028.

This capacity bottleneck preserves strong contractor pricing power and protects medium-term margins from competitive erosion. Additionally, offshore operators are increasingly bypassing traditional competitive tenders in favor of direct sole-source awards through early engineering collaboration, allowing Subsea 7 to lock in lucrative margins before formal bidding processes commence. The OneSubsea joint venture, in which Subsea 7 holds a 10% equity stake, is targeting cumulative bookings exceeding $9 billion, enabling the Subsea Integration Alliance to secure highly profitable integrated projects.

Post-merger, the combined Saipem7 entity will deliver €250 million in fleet optimization synergies by consolidating overlapping vessel assets and spool base infrastructure. Material deviations versus published sell-side consensus were predominantly positive. Fourth quarter Adjusted EBITDA of $477 million beat Bloomberg consensus expectations by 11%, driven by outstanding margin execution across both business segments.

However, net profit of $148 million missed consensus estimates of $174 million by $26 million due to a substantial $50 million foreign exchange loss and higher net finance costs, which represented non-cash bottom-line headwinds rather than underlying operational weakness. Full year revenue of $7.11 billion landed in line with consensus, representing a 4% increase compared to fiscal year 2024's $6.84 billion. The Subsea and Conventional segment delivered full-year Adjusted EBITDA of $1.31 billion, representing a massive 46% year-on-year increase and a segment margin of 22.6%.

The Renewables division delivered flat year-on-year revenue of $1.2 billion but experienced significant margin expansion, with Adjusted EBITDA rising 9.2% to $202 million and a full-year division margin of 16.6%, outperforming the prior year's 15% margin. Material deviations versus buy-side whisper expectations centered on fourth quarter order intake and working capital dynamics. Fourth quarter order intake of $1.9 billion exceeded buy-side models of $1.6 billion, resulting in a book-to-bill ratio of 1.0x and maintaining a record year-end backlog of $13.8 billion.

This backlog coverage provides unprecedented earnings predictability, with $3.9 billion scheduled for 2026 execution and $5.6 billion secured for 2027. However, the massive $420 million working capital release that boosted fourth-quarter cash flow significantly exceeded whisper expectations and raised concerns about near-term reversals. Sell-side analysts project that working capital outflows of $100-150 million will create a drag on free cash flow in the second half of 2026 as project execution accelerates. Despite this anticipated headwind, full-year free cash flow expanded significantly to $1.2 billion, up from $583 million in fiscal year 2024, driven by a 61% increase in operating cash flow to $1.5 billion and capital expenditure discipline. The standalone business sustainability remains a key investor focus ahead of merger completion. Management maintained conservative 2026 revenue guidance of $7.0-7.4 billion despite delivering at the top end of the narrowed range in 2025.

This unchanged outlook prompted questions because consensus estimates are already sitting at the absolute top end of that range. However, historical "book and turn" variation orders, which have averaged $1.20 billion annually over the past five years, are not yet fully captured in consensus models and provide a highly predictable cushion for earnings outperformance. Furthermore, the company's addressable subsea bidding pipeline has remained steady at approximately $20 billion, representing a flattish outlook compared to peer TechnipFMC, which has shown consecutive quarters of pipeline value growth.

This divergence suggests Subsea 7's standalone organic revenue visibility may be approaching its peak as fleet capacity utilization maxes out. The Renewables division turnaround represents a critical operational milestone. Fourth-quarter Renewables margins surged to 20%, supported by favorable project closeouts, contingency releases, and commercial settlements. This exceptional delivery exceeded the division's guided full-year run-rate of 14-16%, proving that selective bidding strategies focused on balanced risk allocation are successfully restoring profitability.

However, management maintained its conservative medium-term guidance for Renewables, warning that project-level closeouts can introduce near-term volatility. The Seaway7 wind business continues to execute a highly selective bidding approach, focusing strictly on balanced risk allocation to ensure the division delivers sustainable profitability. This discipline protects group returns from the poor historical returns in offshore wind that historically degraded overall portfolio margins.

The pending Saipem merger timeline and regulatory progress remain on track despite administrative appeals. The nil-premium cross-border merger of equals remains on track for completion in the second half of 2026. US regulatory approvals progressed as the Hart-Scott-Rodino Antitrust waiting period expired on July 31, 2026. Although Brazil's antitrust authority unconditionally cleared the merger, administrative appeals from Petrobras and the ABEP association have extended the regulatory review timeline by approximately 90 days.

Statutory resolution is expected by November 2026. Outside of Brazil, the transaction faces intensive Phase 2 merger reviews by the European Commission and the Australian Competition and Consumer Commission, with the European Commission's in-depth review carrying a statutory decision deadline of November 26, 2026. Any prolonged antitrust remedies or vessel divestiture requirements could compromise pro forma synergy timelines, allowing competitors like TechnipFMC to capture critical market share during the cyclical peak.
