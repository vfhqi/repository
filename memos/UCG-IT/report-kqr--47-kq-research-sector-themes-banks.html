Section 2 – Regulatory and Sovereign Policy Shifts 12 findings

Section summary
  1. Structural regulatory easing and sovereign policy shifts across Poland, Greece, and the United Kingdom are unlocking substantial trapped capital, driving unprecedented multi-year earnings trajectories for regional banking champions.
  2. While peripheral jurisdictions offer near-term capital distribution tailwinds, political intervention risks and Basel III output floor implementation remain key hurdles for core eurozone institutions.

Regional Capital Optimization and Polish Reforms

1.
Landmark capital opening: In September 2026, mBank completed the first euro-denominated Additional Tier 1 issuance by a Polish bank, raising EUR 250 million with coupon pricing tightened by 50 basis points due to peak demand exceeding EUR 2.2 billion, a transaction that opens the euro-denominated AT1 market to Polish banks
2.
Mitigated legal overhang: The Court of Justice of the European Union issued a landmark ruling in February 2026 concerning preliminary questions on the WIBOR index, providing banks with substantial legal relief on variable interest rates
3.
Rising capital requirements: Polish banks face elevated domestic regulatory hurdles as the countercyclical capital buffer is set to increase from 1% to 2% starting in September 2026
4.
Accelerating credit expansion: Morgan Stanley expects Polish loan growth to recover from late 2025 onwards, driven by EU recovery fund disbursements, allowing PKO to achieve 9% annual loan growth in 2027-28
5.
Complex model adjustments: In its Q1 2025 earnings call, mBank noted that CRR3 implementation and other regulatory changes drove a 4% increase in risk-weighted assets, which was comfortably below its internal planning limit of 6.5%
6.
Easing holiday headwinds: PKO reported that its mortgage-related legal provisions and credit holiday adjustments decreased substantially by mid-2026, enabling the bank to achieve PLN 5.29 billion in first-half net profit
