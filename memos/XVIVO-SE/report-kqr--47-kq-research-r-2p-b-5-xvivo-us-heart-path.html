Section 4 – US OPO Decertifications and Lung Perfusion Hubs

Section summary
  1. The impending Medicare OPO decertifications will fundamentally reshape the US organ procurement landscape.
  2. Xvivo is strategically expanding its collaborative OPO hub model to capture shifting transplant volumes.

Regulatory pressure on

1.
OPOs Strict quality metrics: Under the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services final rule, US organ procurement organizations are ranked in a three-tier system based on donation and transplantation rates. Organizations falling into the bottom tier are classified as Tier 3 and face the prospect of automatic decertification. This pressure forces underperforming operators to pursue marginal donor organs more aggressively to retain their geographic contracts. Consequently, these regulatory changes are driving institutional demand for machine perfusion systems.
2.
Upholding federal rules: The US District Court for the Middle District of Florida issued a landmark decision on September 28, 2026, upholding the legality of the new performance metrics. This legal victory paves the way for CMS to fully implement its national rankings and begin decertification actions. While some operators continue to challenge the rule in other jurisdictions, the Florida ruling secures a critical regulatory path. This regulatory momentum is poised to accelerate technology adoption across the nation.
3.
Imminent system transitions: The Association of Organ Procurement Organizations reports that nearly half of all US organ procurement organizations face potential decertification in late 2026. The affected service areas currently provide medical care to approximately 72% of the domestic population. This unprecedented administrative transition could trigger substantial operational friction in the organ matching network. These major transitions will heavily test transplant logistics infrastructure in the near term.
4.
Widespread industry consolidation: High-performing Tier 1 organizations are expected to bid on territories vacated by decertified operators to expand their geographic reach. Expert insights suggest that while consolidation will improve long-term organ utilization, the intermediate integration period will create temporary administrative chaos. This administrative disruption is likely to restrict immediate case volume expansion. Therefore, operational turbulence could temporarily slow thoracic sales growth for device manufacturers.
