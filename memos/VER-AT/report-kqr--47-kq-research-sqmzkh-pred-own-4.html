Allowed Returns and Methodology

1.
Differentiated returns: E-Control maintains a split regulatory rate that separates legacy assets from new capital expenditures. Bernstein Research reported on June 1, 2026, that pre-tax nominal WACC for assets commissioned up to 2022 is fixed at 4.16% until the end of the current regulatory period in 2028. Bernstein Research noted in the same report that investments from 2023 onward receive higher annually updated returns. A Council of European Energy Regulators report published on February 3, 2025, stated that this distinct treatment protects customers from unjustified historic financing cost burdens.
2.
Annual updates: The regulatory framework for new assets is adjusted each year to reflect volatile market conditions. A Council of European Energy Regulators report published on February 3, 2025, showed that E-Control calculates this annual adjustment using a twelve-month average of interest rates ending on August 31. Bernstein Research explained in a report on June 1, 2026, that this mechanism updates both the risk-free rate and the cost of debt. Bernstein Research stated in the same document that the allowed pre-tax nominal return on new investments reached 6.11% in 2026.
3.
Capital structures: The regulator enforces a strict capital structure that determines interest allowances regardless of actual gearing. Bernstein Research reported on June 1, 2026, that E-Control applies a standard ratio of 40% equity and 60% debt for transmission operators. Bernstein Research noted in the same report that if a network operator fails to maintain an equity ratio of at least 36%, the actual lower capital structure is used to calculate the return. Bernstein Research also showed in that June 2026 document that holding a higher equity level like 45% does not increase the allowed rate.
4.
Nominal framework: Austrian network operators receive allowed returns calculated on a nominal basis with no inflation indexation. Bernstein Research stated on June 1, 2026, that Austrian Power Grid does not benefit from direct inflation-linked uplifts on its assets. Bernstein Research argued in the same report that without indexation, the real purchasing power of the grid returns can deteriorate during high inflation periods. Bernstein Research concluded in that June 2026 report that this lack of protection contrasts with other European jurisdictions that integrate inflation adjustments.

Legal Status and Core Arguments

1.
Ongoing litigation: Austrian Power Grid is actively pursuing legal appeals against E-Control's regulatory decisions before the Federal Administrative Court. Verbund confirmed in its third-quarter report on November 24, 2026, that the proceedings against the tariff notices for 2022 to 2025 remain unresolved. The company is contesting these settings because they fail to reflect realistic market financing costs. This ongoing litigation remains a key regulatory catalyst for Verbund's medium-term grid earnings.
2.
Flawed calculations: Verbund argues that E-Control's WACC methodology relies too heavily on historical market averages. Bernstein Research reported on June 1, 2026, that the operator is challenging the lack of inflationlinked adjustments on legacy grid assets. In addition, Austrian Power Grid objects to individual efficiency targets that E-Control established using unscientific benchmarking. Resolving these calculations in court is necessary to secure sustainable asset remuneration for upcoming grid periods.
3.
Inappropriate benchmarks: The regulator's cost-of-debt calculation is another central point of contention for Austrian Power Grid. Bernstein Research noted on June 1, 2026, that E-Control used BBB-rated corporate bonds to calculate interest allowances. This benchmark is considered highly inappropriate because all rated Austrian energy peers actually maintain A ratings or better. Verbund argues that this benchmark artificially depresses its allowed returns during interest rate spikes, thereby undermining its capital access for network upgrades.
4.
Supportive frameworks: Other regional operators are facing similar regulatory planning challenges in Austria. Energie Steiermark reported on June 2, 2026, that regulatory risks regarding cost recognition have a direct impact on operating profits. The company noted that active negotiations with E-Control are necessary to achieve fair returns. This sector-wide pressure suggests that a positive court ruling for Verbund would benefit the entire Austrian network sector.
5.
Contrary evidence: Austrian Power Grid's historic appeals have faced prolonged administrative delays without yielding immediate tariff adjustments. Verbund's interim report published on July 30, 2026, indicated that appeals against the 2022 to 2025 notices have been pending for multiple quarters. This delay means that even if the court eventually rules in favor of the operator, any financial recovery will face a multi-year lag before translating into higher cash flows.
