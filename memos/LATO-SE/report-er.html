Section 1 – Brief company description and history

Section summary
  1. Strong private industrial performance anchors Latour's long-term compounding thesis, but near- term share price performance has lagged the rising benchmark due to a widening listed portfolio valuation gap.
1.
Company structure – mixed: Established in 1985 by the Douglas family who still controls 76% of the voting capital, Latour operates as a mixed investment company with its asset base split between wholly-owned industrial operations and ten listed holdings. Wholly-owned industrial businesses currently account for approximately 47% of the total net asset value, representing a significant increase from 35% recorded five years ago.

Industrial segment – diversified: The unlisted industrial portfolio generates pro forma annual sales of approximately SEK 28 billion and is divided into seven independent business areas. These business areas include Bemsiq Group, Caljan, Hultafors Group, Innovalift, Latour Industries, Nord-Lock Group, and Swegon. All these business areas hold leading positions in their respective niches developing proprietary products for growing international demand.
3.
Listed portfolio – concentrated: Latour maintains an investment portfolio of ten substantial listed holdings valued at SEK 74 billion as of June 30, 2026. The principal listed holdings where Latour acts as an active principal owner include world-leading industrial giants such as ASSA ABLOY, Securitas, TOMRA, and Sweco. Over 60% of the listed portfolio's total value is derived from holdings owned for more than twenty years.
4.
Historical performance – superior: Over the long term, Latour has delivered exceptional value creation, boasting a 20-year annualized Net Asset Value compound annual growth rate of 14%. This performance represents considerable outperformance versus its investment sector peers at 12%, the benchmark SIXRX index at 10%, and the MSCI World index at 8%.
5.
Fundamental decline – short-term: During the first six months of 2026, Latour's reported net asset value decreased from SEK 216 per share to SEK 203 per share. This represents a net asset value decline of 3.7%, adjusted for paid dividends, underperforming the broader market. In comparison, the benchmark Nasdaq OMX Stockholm SIXRX index rose by 8.1% during the exact same six-month period.
6.
Financial targets – ambitious: For its wholly-owned industrial operations, Latour enforces a long-term goal of achieving at least 10% annual growth over an economic cycle. Additionally, management targets an operating margin of over 15% and a return on operating capital of over 15% for each business area. During the last 12 months, the group achieved 8.7% growth, a 14% EBIT margin, and a 13.9% return on operating capital.
7.
Share price underperformance – negative: Latour's stock has faced severe downward pressure in recent periods, posting an absolute share price performance of -3.50% over the last three months. This negative trend escalated over longer horizons, with the share price falling by -15.56% over the last six months and declining by -23.94% over twelve months.

Section 3 – Key moving parts and change forces for an investment case

Section summary
  1. Capital reallocation through listed asset sales has unlocked SEK 5.0 billion in liquidity to fund higher-margin wholly-owned industrial acquisitions, successfully offsetting near-term margin pressure.
1.
CFO transition – executed: Mikael Johnsson Albrektsson officially assumed the role of Chief Financial Officer on May 1, 2025, succeeding the retiring Anders Mörck. Prior to this transition, Albrektsson spent six years successfully building Latour's fast-growing Bemsiq business area. This internal promotion ensured operational continuity and maintained the group's distinct decentralized corporate culture.
2.
Executive transition – background: The leadership change was highly anticipated by the market, as Anders Mörck had served as CFO for 17 years and presented 69 quarterly reports during his tenure. Johan Hjertonsson highlighted that Albrektsson brought deep experience in operational finance and corporate mergers and acquisitions. This transaction-heavy background aligns directly with Latour's strategy of accelerating its unlisted industrial expansion.
3.
Divisional leadership – updated: To fill the vacancy left by Albrektsson at Bemsiq, Anselmi Immonen was appointed as the acting chief executive officer to lead the segment. Under Immonen, Bemsiq has maintained strong operating momentum in building automation, particularly within its North American operations. This executive transition proves the depth of Latour's internal talent pool and succession planning.
4.
Strategic portfolio sale – executed: In May 2026, Latour completed a massive capital reallocation by selling SEK 5.0 billion of B-shares in its two largest listed associate companies. The accelerated book-building processes divested 7.6 million Class B shares of ASSA ABLOY and 16.4 million Class B shares of Securitas. Both transactions raised gross proceeds of SEK 2.5 billion each from institutional investors.
5.
Portfolio sale terms – discount: The ASSA ABLOY divestment was priced at SEK 330 per share, which represented a tight 1.6% discount to the market closing price on May 26, 2026. Meanwhile, the Securitas shares were sold at SEK 153 per share, translating to an unfavorable 3.5% discount. Latour agreed to customary 180-day lock-up undertakings on its remaining shares in both companies.
6.
Ownership retention – post-sale: Despite these partial divestments, Latour remains the principal owner and largest shareholder in both companies. Following the transactions, Latour holds 29.0% of the voting rights in ASSA ABLOY and 27.6% of the voting rights in Securitas. CEO Johan Hjertonsson emphasized that the parent company remains fully committed to their long-term strategies.
7.
Capital deployment – shift: Management's core objective for this divestment is to fund the wholly-owned industrial operations. Over the past decade, these unlisted businesses have expanded their annual turnover from SEK 7 billion to SEK 28 billion. By realizing a fraction of listed capital, Latour can self-fund higher-yielding private acquisitions.
8.
Swegon M&A – acceleration: Acquisition activity has remained intensely high, with Swegon expanding its European footprint through three strategic transactions in early 2026. Swegon acquired Western Airconditioning in the Netherlands, LaminAir AG in Switzerland, and Dantherm's Danish residential ventilation assets. LaminAir AG alone contributes SEK 100 m in pro forma annual sales at a profitability level well above targets.
9.
Swegon divestments – streamlining: Simultaneously, Swegon executed three strategic divestments between late 2025 and April 2026, including selling its underperforming UltimAir B.V. business. These divested operations generated combined revenues of SEK 400 m but had margins below targets. Swegon also divested its Norwegian third-party products business in Q2 2026 to focus on higher-margin proprietary offerings.
10.
Caljan US M&A – expansion: Through its Caljan business area, Latour completed the acquisition of 100% of WyCo Services LLC in June 2026. WyCo is a premium provider of maintenance and installation services in the US material handling sector. In 2025, WyCo generated USD 23 m in revenues with profitability exceeding Latour's corporate targets.
