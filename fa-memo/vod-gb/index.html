How the forecasts were built

The Investment Analyst's forecast inputs after Layer 2, the inputs that Layers 3 and 4 changed with the reasons for each change, and the notes exchanged between the Investment Analyst and the Financial Analyst.

Forecast inputs after Layer 2

Money in millions of EUR, except per-share figures; costs and outflows negative.

Basis of these inputs (read before entering). EBITDA is the company's adjusted EBITDAaL (after lease costs), the basis of company guidance and of FactSet's brokers'-basis consensus (FY26 EUR 11,351m, 28.1 percent), not FactSet's operating EBITDA (EUR 16,337m, before leases). Consistently: depreciation and amortisation is on owned assets only (right-of-use depreciation is inside EBITDAaL); lease payments are entered as zero because lease cash (principal and interest, about EUR 4.1bn in FY26) is already deducted inside EBITDAaL; and "net financial items" is a composite (net financing cost excluding lease interest, plus associates, less minority interests grossed up at the tax rate), because the form has no minorities or associates line and Vodafone's minorities are large. With these inputs, the model's net income equals adjusted net income attributable to Vodafone's shareholders on the brokers' basis. Part 4 sets out each component.

How to read these figures

  • Amounts are in millions of the reporting currency; per-share figures are in units of that currency. Negative figures are shown in brackets.
  • Historical figures use FactSet's standardised lines, which can differ from the company's own presentation.
  • Earnings per share appears on four bases, which must not be mixed: reported basic; FactSet's adjusted diluted, before unusual items; the Viewforth definition, which is the model's standing convention; and the brokers' adjusted basis, on which consensus is based.
  • Consensus is FactSet's median of the broker estimates it holds. Where FactSet's line-by-line consensus differs from the broker-by-broker detail, the line-by-line table takes precedence.
  • Viewforth's forecasts are the Excel model's base case. There are no bull or bear cases.
