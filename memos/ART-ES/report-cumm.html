Arteche (ART-ES): Case Update Mini-Memo, 1 October 2026

From the Investment Analyst. Baseline: the Stock-specific Investment Case A&J Memo of 10-Sep-26 (Triaging stage, price €29.5). Window: 1-Sep-26 to 1-Oct-26, plus anything important the memo did not cover. Valuation and upside are out of scope by Richard's instruction. Version 1: built from web sources, our own files and the dashboard's FactSet data; two AlphaSense research reports are pending and will be added as an update.

Bottom line

  • The month's big change is in perception, not in the fundamental inputs. The shares rose 28% from 10-Sep to 30-Sep (€29.5 to €37.7) with no company filing. Grid peers moved between −7% and +8%. The move came on a broker upgrade, a re-telling of Spanish grid money the memo had already counted, a conference appearance, and one day of very heavy volume.
  • Fundamentals: unchanged to slightly stronger. Consensus estimates edged up (earnings per share +4% in a month), and the Spanish grid plan became firmer and broader. But it is the same money, and most of it is spent in 2028 to 2030.
  • Case Riskiness/Simplicity: riskier on the share side, simplicity unchanged. The shares are technically stretched, covered by three analysts, above the June placement price, and leaning on one dated event.
  • Case Setup Profile fit/crispness: unchanged. 'Demand-driven EPSU' still fits. The month neither proved nor disproved the memo's open question: structural or cyclical.
  • Direction: neutral on the case, conviction medium. The business-side forces are slightly better; the share-side risks are higher by about as much.
  • Three forces matter most for adding or reducing over the next three to six months:
  • the Strategic Plan 2027-2029 presentation on 30-Nov-26
  • a further sale of shares by the large holders to lift the free float
  • how a price this stretched reacts to either.

1. What changed in the last month

Newsflow and narratives
- Spanish grid plan, 16-Sep-26. The Ministry for Ecological Transition published the post-consultation version of the 2025-2030 electricity grid plan.
- It sets investment above €17bn to 2030, 30% more than the €13.6bn proposed a year earlier.
- The split is distribution +€10.2bn and transmission +€7.7bn, steeply back-loaded: about €1.7bn in 2027, rising to about €6.4bn in 2030.
- Planned substations rose 17% to 193, and connection points for new demand rose 52% to 300.
- Final approval is targeted before the end of 2026, and a capacity-market order (the government's "anti-blackout" mechanism) was published the same week.
- Analysis: this is mostly not new money for the case. The memo already counted €17.9bn authorised on 28-Jul-26, which is the same €10.2bn plus €7.7bn. What is new:
- wider scope (more substations and demand connections)
- a firm approval timetable
- confirmation that the spending ramps in 2028 to 2030.
It strengthens visibility beyond 2027 and does little for 2026 or 2027.
- The narrative moved from "good business, sold off after the half-year results" to "Spanish grid winner". Santander's note (14-Sep), the grid headlines (16 and 17-Sep) and management at the CaixaBank Iberian Conference (17-Sep) carried most of the 28%, between 16 and 25-Sep.

Sell-side estimates, price targets and ratings
- Santander: Outperform; target raised from €31.50 to €42.34 on 14-Sep-26, with the stock at €29.70.
- Consensus (FactSet, three analysts), the last month:
- consensus target €35.5 to €39.1 (+10%); €24.8 six months ago
- earnings per share +4% (+18% over six months)
- EBITDA (operating profit before depreciation and amortisation) +4%
- sales +1%.
- All three analysts now rate it a buy, against two of three three months ago; one estimate raised and none cut in the last 45 days.
- Analysis: the upgrades come from margin, not sales. That matches the memo's point that the improvement rests on one lever. Coverage is still thin, so one broker moves the consensus.

Company guidance and communications
- No regulatory filing since the half-year results of 28-Jul-26, according to the Spanish market regulator's register. FY2026 guidance stands: revenue €565m to €590m, EBITDA €92.1m to €100.4m.
- The strategy update now has a date and a different name. The company's investor agenda lists the "Strategic Plan 2027-2029" presentation on 30-Nov-26. The memo expected a "Strategic Plan 2030" in October or November.
- Investor marketing is widening beyond Spanish brokers: ODDO (5 to 6-Oct), Berenberg in the US (18-Nov), Goldman Sachs (2-Dec).

Stock technicals
- Price: +28% from 10-Sep and +35% from 1-Sep, to €37.7 on 30-Sep. The low was €27.2 on 30-Jul; the shares are still 18% below the 12-May high of €46.0, and now above the €33 June placement price.
- Stretch: 23% above the 50-day average (€30.6) and 27% above the 200-day (€29.65), with the 200-day still rising. Relative strength is in the top 4% of the universe.
- Volume: 380,116 shares on 18-Sep, about 13 times the recent daily average of about 28,000. It was a quarterly expiry and index-rebalancing day; the IBEX committee made no change on 10-Sep, so it was not an IBEX inclusion. The cause is unconfirmed.
- Your stops: the probing bet's 50-day stop sits about 19% below the price, and the main tranche's 200-day stop about 21% below.

2. What it does to the case

Fundamentals (Investment Thesis Inputs/Outputs): unchanged to slightly stronger
- Inputs: the external force (policy-funded grid spending) is firmer and broader, and has a dated approval, but it is not larger than the memo assumed and it is back-loaded. Nothing in the month touches the internal forces.
- Outputs: estimates rose modestly, as the sell-side caught up with the July guidance raise; they do not reflect new company data.
- Judgement: the change forces behind the thesis are no weaker and slightly better evidenced. The memo's view of an ordinary earnings path from here (about 10% to 13% a year) is neither confirmed nor refuted. The test is still the strategy update on 30-Nov.

Case Riskiness/Simplicity: riskier on the share side; simplicity unchanged
- Share-side risk is up:
- a 28% move with no company news
- thin coverage
- a free float of about 12.5% against a stated target of 20% to 25%
- the price now above the June placement price, which makes a further sale by the large holders easier, not harder.
The shares are more exposed to a sharp reaction to the 30-Nov event in either direction.
- Business-side risk is unchanged: no estimate cuts, guidance intact, no new competitive or accounting evidence this month.
- Simplicity holds: one force, one mechanism, trackable through guidance. The case now leans more heavily on one dated event and on a policy narrative.

Case Setup Profile fit/crispness: unchanged
- 'Demand-driven EPSU' still fits. The evidence for its mandatory condition (sector-level, policy-funded demand with multi-year visibility) is firmer after the plan's consultation.
- The open question is still open: the memo treats the inflection as cyclical until the strategy update or a second year of orders after the new rules proves it structural.
- The secondary setup could strengthen on 30-Nov: that is 'Big Corporate Change-driven EPSU', if the plan brings new targets, acquisitions or a higher payout.

4. The strongest counter-case

  • The strongest counter-case: the market is right and this note is behind. The price may be anticipating a higher earnings path from the 30-Nov plan and wider coverage; consensus earnings per share is already up 18% in six months.
  • For it to win: the plan's targets clearly exceed consensus, and new brokers initiate with higher estimates. If both happen, Fundamentals moves from "unchanged" to "stronger".
