Section 3 – STG Management, Board, and Shareholder Views on Buybacks

Section summary
  1. Management and board members maintain a highly disciplined capital allocation framework focused on protecting the balance sheet before resuming buybacks. Major shareholders support the pro-rata distribution of excess capital once leverage drops below the target threshold. The completed fine-cut divestment provides the financial capacity to resume repurchases if operational earnings stabilize.

Executive Capital Framework

1.
Cautious stance: Management remains highly conservative regarding the timing of capital distributions following recent leverage increases. Chief Financial Officer Marianne Rørslev Bock confirmed in March 2025 that the company would pause all share buybacks to preserve financial flexibility. This temporary suspension was designed to allow the company to fund necessary investments and evaluate acquisitions without stretching the balance sheet. Consequently, the group did not initiate any new buyback programs throughout the remainder of the fiscal year.
2.
Balanced approach: The board continues to monitor the leverage ratio closely against its historical targets before committing to future distributions. Representative Anders Obel, who represents major shareholder C.W. Obel on the board, supports this balanced capital allocation approach. The group's net debt had risen to DKK 5.4 billion by the end of 2024 due to significant capital outlays and acquisitions. This rapid debt accumulation forced the board to balance capital allocation priorities to conserve cash.
3.
Disciplined execution: Major shareholders continue to support the company's long-term capital allocation framework while waiting for operational performance to stabilize. Chr. Augustinus Fabrikker, which holds a 28.5% stake in the group, has historically participated in the company's buyback programs on a pro-rata basis. This structured participation ensures that the major foundations maintain their proportional ownership while allowing the company to distribute excess capital efficiently. The board remains committed to this structured approach once the leverage ratio falls back below the target threshold of 2.5x.

Board and Shareholder Perspectives

Section 5 – Precedents of Deleveraging and First Buybacks at European Peers

Section summary
  1. European consumer peers show that reaching a leverage target of 2.0x to 2.5x consistently triggers substantial, multi-year share buyback programs. Peer precedents suggest that initial share repurchases typically range from 2% to 7% of market capitalization and drive meaningful earnings-per-share accretion. Strategic deleveraging at global tobacco and beverage firms consistently leads to valuation multiple expansion once buyback discipline is established.

Precedents in European Tobacco Deleveraging

1.
Imperial Brands Capital Return: Imperial Brands restarted its capital return program in October 2022 after successfully reducing leverage toward its target corridor. Morgan Stanley reported on October 3, 2024 that the initial program of £1.0 billion was launched once leverage reached the lower end of the company's 2.0x to 2.5x target range. This initial buyback represented approximately 7% of its market capitalization and helped drive high-single-digit earnings-per-share growth.
2.
Imperial Brands Program Expansion: Imperial Brands progressively increased its annual share repurchases as cash flows stabilized and leverage remained controlled. The company announced in its October 8, 2026 trading update that it completed a £1.45 billion buyback for fiscal year 2026 and initiated a £1.5 billion program for fiscal year 2027. Deutsche Bank Research noted on October 16, 2026 that this consistent execution keeps leverage at 2.1x and yields an attractive 15.5% total distribution. This multi-year program shows how sustained deleveraging supports expanding repurchases over time.
3.
British American Tobacco Strategy: British American Tobacco has structured its capital allocation to resume buybacks once its leverage falls back within target bounds. Deutsche Bank Research reported on October 2, 2026 that the company expects to reach its net debt to EBITDA target of 2.0x to 2.5x by the end of 2026. Management previously launched a £1.6 billion buyback program in March 2024 using proceeds from its ITC stake sale. This tactical capital return represents about one percent of its outstanding shares and demonstrates how asset sales accelerate buyback timelines.
4.
British American Tobacco Projections: British American Tobacco could expand its share repurchases significantly if operational cash flows remain robust. Jefferies Research estimated on September 18, 2026 that the company has the capacity to increase annual repurchases to £2.0 billion or £2.5 billion post-2026. This expanded capital return would keep leverage within the 2.0x to 2.2x corridor. These potential buybacks could drive constant-currency earnings-per-share growth of five percent to eight percent.

The company reported in its 2024 annual report that it repurchased DKK 20 billion of its own shares between 2019 and 2024. These annual repurchases ranged from DKK 2.0 billion to DKK 4.4 billion. This disciplined program allowed Carlsberg to maintain leverage well below its target of 2.0x.
