Section 3 – Co-Packer Reliance & Quantified Margin Drag 13 findings

Section summary
  1. Relying on external co-packers throughout 2026 creates a temporary gross margin drag on Pepsi volumes, holding local profit contribution near breakeven before internal plant commissioning unlocks historical 47.5-49.5% gross margins in 2027.

External Co-Packer Cost Drivers and Supply Overhead

1.
Substantial tolling margin: External tolling agreements require paying third-party processors a manufacturing markup that absorbs the majority of gross profit on Pepsi volume
2.
Extended transit corridors: Sourcing finished soft drinks from external production sites outside Kazakhstan incurs elevated freight charges across Central Asian transit routes
3.
Cross-border tariff overhead: Import tariffs and customs clearance procedures on cross-border co-packer shipments add transactional costs that cannot be fully passed onto retail prices
4.
Unabsorbed fixed capabilities: Pre-hiring local sales teams and placing retail coolers prior to plant opening creates unabsorbed fixed operating expenses in Almaty
5.
Contractual tolling rigidity: Fixed minimum-volume co-packer commitments limit supply chain flexibility during initial distribution rollouts
6.
Shattered container economics: Transporting heavy liquid cargo over long overland routes yields higher unit freight overhead than shipping concentrated syrup inputs

Margin Drag Quantifications and Post-Commissioning Inflection

7.
Loss-making first half: Sell-side consensus from Bernstein Research models 1H26 Pepsi volume as slightly loss-making and margin dilutive due to external co-packer purchase costs
8.
Breakeven full-year trajectory: Carlsberg management guidance confirms that Pepsi volumes will deliver zero net profit contribution in FY26 despite adding 1.5 percentage points to group volume growth
9.
Second-half breakeven transition: Gradual operational ramp and partial internal test batching allow 2H26 Pepsi sales to achieve marginal profitability while remaining group margin dilutive

Accretive medium-term inflection: Financial modeling by Bernstein Research indicates Pepsi Central Asia operations turn strongly margin accretive in FY27 and FY28 as local production eliminates co-packer fees
