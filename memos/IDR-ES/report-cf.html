EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

The investment thesis for Indra Sistemas centers on a profound structural transformation from a hybrid IT services provider into a sovereign European defense champion, driven by Spain's unprecedented defense modernization cycle and strategic space platform integration. The company has experienced rapid executive chairmanship turnover through three distinct leaders within 18 months, accompanied by active activist interventions from Third Point and Amber Capital demanding accelerated defense consolidation to unlock shareholder value.

Spain's defense equipment spending is projected to nearly double to EUR 21 billion by 2030, with Indra positioned as prime contractor or lead partner in 16 of 31 Spanish modernization programs. The company is delivering exceptional revenue growth of 27% year-on-year in FY26 with organic growth reaching 16% in H1 2026, significantly outpacing the IT services peer average of low-single digits, while group EBIT margins are guided to exceed 10% in FY26, up from 9.5% in FY25.

However, this transformation carries material execution risks. While the balance sheet shows record net cash of EUR 1,003 million in June 2026, this position is artificially inflated by massive customer downpayments, with underlying free cash flow only EUR 15 million without prepayments, while capital expenditure is expanding to

4.3% of revenue to fund industrial footprint scale-up. The aggressive pivot toward capital-intensive land vehicle manufacturing and complex satellite platform integration introduces significant operational bottlenecks that could derail margin expansion and cash conversion if labor shortages or supply chain disruptions materialize. The substantial valuation disconnect between Indra's hybrid structure and pure-play defense peers reflects market skepticism about execution credibility, despite management's historically conservative guidance track record.

Materiality of Change represents the most significant corporate transformation in Indra's history, shifting from a domestically-focused IT services operator into a vertically-integrated aerospace and defense platform with sovereign space capabilities. The governance reset included three executive chairmen in 18 months, culminating in Angel Simon Grimaldos becoming non-executive Chairman in April 2026 and Josep Maria Recasens appointed CEO in June 2026, bringing extensive industrial strategy expertise from Renault Group.

Strategically, Indra is transforming into a sovereign European defense player through massive public backlog conversion, consolidating reporting into five distinct business areas with end-to-end P&L accountability, while group headcount decreased 5% driven by a 12% reduction in Minsait personnel as traditional outsourcing slows. Portfolio rotation is actively underway, with the completed BPO divestment generating EUR 70 million in proceeds in H1 2026 and the Minsait Business Consulting sale to Waterland Private Equity transferring 1,700 professionals to crystallize value and redirect resources to advanced defense systems.

Key Drivers of Forward EPS demonstrate a portfolio of five to eight high-conviction structural tailwinds with varying plausibility profiles and interdependencies. The Spain PEM defense budget cycle represents the highest-impact driver, with equipment spend projected to reach EUR 21 billion by 2030, positioning Indra as prime contractor in 16 of 31 programs and driving Spanish defense market share to 17% from 7% in 2025, supporting a 30% revenue CAGR for the Defense division through 2030 with high plausibility and confidence, though exposed to sovereign budget execution risks.

The Space division platform integration targets EUR 1 billion revenue scale by 2030, transitioning Indra into a vertically integrated space operator spanning payload, platforms, and ground systems, heavily dependent on the EUR 10 billion IRIS2 sovereign communications program with medium plausibility but carrying substantial operational and cultural integration complexity from Hispasat and Hisdesat consolidation.

The Air Traffic Management expansion operates as a highly visible infrastructure-like asset with low double-digit organic growth, earning a valuation multiple upgrade to 15x EV/EBIT on improving backlog visibility, delivering sustained low-teens operating margins and 15% revenue growth in Q2 2026, with high plausibility and strong technical adjacencies with Defense secure communications. The Minsait IT services transformation targets 7% EBIT margins by FY26 through capital recycling out of lowmargin outsourcing, establishing a 6% EBIT margin floor as a sustainable segment baseline, with Digital and Solutions revenue growing 25% in Q2 2026 offsetting declining legacy revenues, though valuation remains anchored to legacy IT services multiples until the transformation is finalized, representing medium plausibility and confidence.

The armored vehicles and TESS integration driver reflects Indra's upstream shift to armored vehicle platform orchestrator, contributing EUR 180 million in Q2 2026 but imposing margin dilution to 17.0% from 18.9% due to consolidation costs, with medium plausibility but low confidence given potential temporary inefficiencies from ramping manufacturing footprint and specialist engineer hiring. The sovereign AI IndraMind platform targets EUR 700 million by 2028 from EUR 300 million in 2025, positioning Indra as a sovereign AI operator rather than labor-arbitrage provider, with AI software increasingly embedded in European command-and-control architectures, but represents low plausibility and confidence given uncertain commercial delivery timelines.

This places Indra at the extreme positive tail of leverage distributions across European aerospace and defense peers, most of whom operate with net debt-to-EBITDA ratios between 1.0x and 2.5x to fund R&D and manufacturing capital expenditure. The company's negative leverage position while simultaneously guiding capex to 4.3% of revenue to quadruple industrial footprint creates a structural contradiction that can only be explained by the temporary inflation of working capital liabilities from customer downpayments, representing a non-recurring balance sheet tailwind that distorts conventional peer credit quality comparisons. This single-country concentration creating over 60% of group revenues significantly exceeds the domestic exposure of Thales (approximately 25% France), Leonardo (approximately 45% Italy), and Saab (approximately 35% Sweden), placing Indra in the bottom 5% of geographic diversification among European defense contractors.

The simultaneous contraction of European revenues, with revenues in Europe declining 9% in H1 2026 to EUR 475 million, showing underperformance vs global tech markets amplifies this concentration risk and creates an unexplained structural dependency on Spanish Ministry of Defense procurement that materially distinguishes Indra's risk profile from more geographically balanced peers.
