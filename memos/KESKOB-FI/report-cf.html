EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Kesko Oyj presents a mixed forward profile anchored by orderly executive leadership transitions, resilient grocery operations, and aggressive Nordic consolidation, but faces headwinds from prolonged construction cyclicality and intense domestic pricing competition. The company passed its cyclical earnings trough in 2024 with comparable EBIT bottoming at EUR 580 million, and is now executing a dual-strategy combining self-help cost automation with leverage to Nordic residential recovery.

The landmark Dahl acquisition positions Kesko to triple technical trade procurement volumes, but introduces near-term equity dilution risks and demands successful integration across fragmented Scandinavian markets. Recent earnings delivery in Q2 2026 met consensus expectations, with net sales growing 5.8% and comparable EBIT reaching EUR 194 million, driven by strong technical wholesale outperformance offsetting subdued grocery profitability and weak foodservice trends.

Management demonstrates high short-term guidance credibility as an honest tracker, having narrowed 2026 comparable EBIT guidance to EUR 670-730 million from an initial broader range, supported by positive monthly sales tracking and structural grocery market share gains. However, mid-term return targets face credibility pressure with ROCE tracking at 10.2% versus the over 14.5% target, contingent on delayed Nordic construction recovery and successful margin restoration in building trade operations.

Leadership and Strategic Architecture Jorma Rauhala became President and CEO on 1 February 2024,

ensuring strategic continuity and leadership stability, bringing extensive experience leading multiple core business divisions since joining Kesko in 1992, including grocery and building trade. Planned CFO transition saw Anu Hamalainen appointed as Chief Financial Officer effective 1 June 2024, replacing departing CFO Jukka Erlund, with Hamalainen previously serving as Vice President of Group Finance and Treasury, joining Kesko in 2020 from Wartsila.

Employee turnover decreased to 16.4% in 2025, showing improved retention and stable staffing versus 18.5% in 2024, while the K Voices wellbeing index rose to 85 in 2025, demonstrating higher workplace satisfaction and engagement versus 83 in 2024. Kesko maintains three focused core business divisions of grocery trade, building and technical trade, and car trade, with solvency and liquidity targets managed at the Group level to secure funding flexibility, targeting an interest-bearing net debt to EBITDA ratio of at maximum 2.5 times, and mandating distributing a steadily growing dividend of 60% to 100% of comparable EPS.

Danish consolidation established market shares near 20% following strategic regional bolt-on acquisitions, with Kesko completing the landmark acquisition of Davidsen A/S on 31 January 2024 to enter Denmark, integrating three local builders merchants of Roslev, CF Petersen, and Tommergaarden in 2025, with these 2025 bolt-ons doubling Kesko's Danish market share to nearly 20% nationwide. The massive Dahl acquisition signed with Saint-Gobain involves a debt-free transaction price of €1,200 million, reflecting an EV/EBITDA multiple of 10.4 times, with financing utilizing bridge loans to be refinanced with debt and a €500-700 million equity issue.

CEO Jorma Rauhala stated on 15 June 2026 that Dahl is the largest acquisition in Kesko's history, projecting Group net sales of €20 billion by the early 2030s and division net sales of €10 billion in building and technical trade.

External Value-Chain Dynamics Finnish grocery sales grew by 4.3% in H1 2026, showing increasing

retail volume and customer traffic over the period, while B2B foodservice sales at Kespro decreased by 0.6% in H1 2026, indicating a subdued restaurant and hospitality demand cycle, though demand for premium food products has stabilized and turned upwards recently. S Group leads Finnish grocery with 49% market share in 2025, while Kesko maintains a second-place 34% share, with S Group, K Group, and Lidl collectively controlling 92% of the grocery market, creating high entry barriers for newcomers.

S-Group and Lidl drive aggressive price campaigns in Finland, forcing Kesko to invest €50 million in price cuts, while Kesko leverages AI-based logistics route planning to drive significant fuel savings and reduce carbon emissions, and the newly launched K Retail Media platform leverages customer transaction data to generate high-margin advertising revenue. The EU Deforestation Regulation forces Kesko to trace origin data across all primary commodities after 2025, implementing the new CSRD reporting frameworks escalates compliance costs and audit complexity for Kesko's administration, and the PPWR regulation requires 20% of takeaway food to be sold in reusable packaging by 2030.

Onninen's Onnela logistics centre is fully operational with robotics-based sorting, delivering next-day fulfillment on 12 million rows, though Kesko relies on direct suppliers in amfori risk countries like China and India, presenting human rights compliance exposures, and mandates social responsibility audits for direct risk-country factories representing 0.9% of total procurement volume. Finnish housing construction is declining with starts remaining at historic lows through 2026, with the Finnish construction turnaround waiting for more stable geopolitical conditions, though technical trade sales rose 10.7% in comparable terms in July 2026, outperforming the weak residential building segment.

Finland's real GDP is expected to grow by 1.0% in 2026 and 1.5% in 2027, with stabilizing consumer prices helping restore private consumption growth of 1% annually, and Finnish variable- rate mortgages accelerating household purchasing power recovery as interest rates fall.

Outliers worth noting

The comprehensive financial analysis of Kesko Oyj reveals several statistically anomalous metrics and unexplained performance outliers when benchmarked against Nordic retail peers and the company's own historical norms. Onninen secures an estimated 44% share of the domestic technical wholesale market in Finland, representing an exceptionally high concentration ratio that places the division in the top 5% of European wholesale distributors by market dominance.

This structural positioning contrasts sharply with the more fragmented competitive landscapes observed in peer markets across Sweden, Norway, and Denmark, where technical wholesalers typically capture 15% to 25% market shares. Kesko's net sales rose to EUR 12,406 million in 2025 with projections reaching EUR 12,785 million in 2026 and EUR 13,229 million in 2027, demonstrating a consistent low-single-digit organic growth trajectory that materially underperforms the aggressive expansion rates exhibited by comparable Nordic building materials distributors.

Private labels represent 24% of Finnish grocery sales, constituting an exceptionally low market share compared to other European retail environments where private label penetration routinely exceeds 35% to 45% in mature grocery markets. This structural anomaly positions Kesko's grocery division in the bottom 5% of Western European food retailers by own-brand penetration, creating unexplained margin compression risks relative to peers who leverage higher-margin private label assortments to offset supplier inflation and competitive pricing
