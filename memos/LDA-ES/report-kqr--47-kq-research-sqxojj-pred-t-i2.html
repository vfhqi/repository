Pricing Lag and Earned Margins

1.
Pricing lag: Motor pricing at Linea Directa increased by approximately 2% during Q1 2026, creating a substantial pricing gap against a broader Spanish market growing at 6%. This lower rate of tariff adjustment raises concerns that premium growth will fail to keep pace with persistent claims inflation over the next year. Consequently, this pricing deficit will likely compress earned margins in fiscal year 2027 unless underwriting volumes increase significantly to offset the lower rates.
2.
Controlled tariffs: Spanish motor underwriting remains highly competitive as major insurers struggle to match premium increases with persistent claims inflation. Mapfre Iberia reported auto premium growth of 1.1% in the first nine months of 2026, supported by selective profile-based tariff adjustments. This controlled growth indicates that raising rates too aggressively risks severe customer volume losses across Spain.
3.
Market comparisons: Pricing pressure in the UK motor market shows that market premiums often lag claims inflation by several quarters before stabilizing. Sabre Insurance reported that material market-wide price increases are required to cover claims inflation running at 6% to 7% in 2026. This lag suggests that Linea Directa's low pricing will create prolonged margin pressure before Spanish industry rates adjust to claims realities.
4.
EPS risks: Industry projections suggest that pricing strategies implemented in 2026 will heavily dictate profitability trends for personal-lines insurers in 2027. Linea Directa's decision to price below market averages could trigger a structural decline in its earnings per share if claim frequencies rise. This risk is exacerbated by rising vehicle repair costs that continue to erode standard direct-to-consumer profit margins.

Section 3 – Home, Weather, and Health Diversification

Section summary
  1. The home segment combined ratio of 86.6% in H1 2026 was driven by exceptionally benign weather conditions that are unlikely to persist over the long term.
  2. The transition to the HNA health network in Q1 2027 and the restructured Munich Re contract are key to reaching segment breakeven.

Home Performance and Weather Volatility

1.
Strong profitability: Linea Directa achieved an excellent home combined ratio of 86.6% in the first half of 2026, representing an improvement of 2.3 percentage points compared with the previous year. This technical performance was supported by a standalone second-quarter combined ratio of 83.9% due to favorable claims frequency. Management has stated that the careful subscription strategy helped secure these high margins, but this performance is substantially better than the CFO-described normal range of 90% to 91%. Consequently, investors should view this margin expansion as primarily driven by temporary weather benefits rather than permanent structural improvements.
2.
Seasonal sensitivity: Personal lines underwriting remains highly exposed to atmospheric events that can rapidly deteriorate the technical result of the home segment. During the severe weather seasons of 2023, heavy storms added 5.0 percentage points to the home combined ratio. These atmospheric and water damage claims forced the segment's combined ratio up to 97.7% in the first nine months of 2023. Therefore, any increase in storm frequency will quickly push the combined ratio back to normalized historical levels of over 90%.
3.
Atmospheric impact: In past periods of extreme weather, water damage and localized windstorms have been the primary drivers of loss ratio spikes in Spain. Linea Directa's management reported that water damage and localized atmospheric events accounted for over half of the home segment's total claims incurred during seasonal downturns. Because these localized weather events are inherently unpredictable, their return to longterm historical frequencies threatens to erode the home underwriting margin over the next 18 months.
4.
Severe climate: Linea Directa has identified physical climate changes as a major long-term risk that could permanently raise the frequency and severity of claims. High-emission climate scenarios suggest that extreme weather events will increase claims costs, elevate operating expenses, and trigger higher reinsurance rates. The company has responded by designing specific reinsurance programs to mitigate these physical underwriting exposures. However, persistent atmospheric volatility means that home segment earnings will remain highly volatile over the next three years.
5.
Moderate premium: Linea Directa's home insurance premiums grew by 2.6% to €128.9 million in the first nine months of 2026, while the customer base expanded to over 796,000 policyholders. This growth represents a moderate deceleration compared with the 7.4% home premium expansion reported at year-end 2025. Despite gaining scale, the segment's growth remains sensitive to the economic cycle and competitive market pressure. This slower top-line expansion makes the division highly dependent on benign weather conditions to support its contribution to group EPS.

Moving to this extensive network is intended to strengthen the company's health value proposition and improve customer retention.

Baremo Indexation and Claims Inflation

1.
Baremo indexing: The Spanish personal injury compensation scale, known as the Baremo, is legally indexed to state pension adjustments and broader macroeconomic inflation. For the upcoming 2027 update, these regulatory adjustments are determined by the average Consumer Price Index recorded between December 2025 and November 2026. This calculation currently yields an estimated revaluation of 3.33% that will apply across the Spanish insurance sector. Consequently, Linea Directa faces elevated average cost per claim because bodily injury payouts will increase in line with pension indexation.
2.
Lagged impacts: The delayed indexation of Spanish public spending highlights how inflation trends continue to filter into the broader economy with a visible time lag. Morgan Stanley reported that wage catch-up and pension indexation will add approximately 40 basis points of Gross Domestic Product to state spending in fiscal year 2027. For motor underwriters, this delayed fiscal effect means that bodily injury claims inflation will peak well after general consumer prices begin to stabilize. Under these conditions, Linea Directa's historical claims reserves may face significant upward restatement pressures in 2027.
3.
Inflationary steps: Spanish consumer price pressures have remained stubbornly elevated throughout the final quarters of fiscal year 2026. CaixaBank Research reported that general inflation in Spain reached 4.9% in October 2026, while underlying subjacente inflation stood at 3.1%. This persistent high-inflation environment directly increases the costs of vehicle parts, mechanical labor, and medical services for domestic insurers. Unless Linea Directa implements aggressive tariff adjustments, this structural inflation step will severely compress 2027 underwriting profits.
