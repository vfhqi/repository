EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Section summary
  1. The July 2026 EU parcel levy has triggered a significant reduction in consumer spend and traffic on Chinese e-commerce platforms, while physical discount variety chains are experiencing accelerating store footfall and robust like-for-like sales growth. However, medium confidence surrounds this shift due to major information gaps concerning the long-term pace of online marketplace logistics shifts and local warehousing execution, as industry experts argue that warehousing workarounds will ultimately allow Chinese marketplaces to maintain low consumer pricing structures.

The abolition of the de minimis exemption on July 1, 2026, introduced a €3 fee on low-value imports, fundamentally altering the competitive dynamics between cross-border e-commerce platforms and European physical retailers. German online sales for Shein, Temu, and AliExpress dropped 35.5% in the third quarter of 2026, slashing their combined market share to 3.1%, while Sweden's customs agency recorded a 70% year-on-year decline in direct parcel shipments from China.

This regulatory shock created immediate friction in the directto-consumer fulfillment model that Chinese marketplaces had relied upon to undercut traditional European retailers. discount variety chains rapidly captured displaced consumer demand across multiple geographies. Pepco generated strong Q4 like-for-like sales growth of 9.5% excluding FMCG, boosted by strategic clearance activity, while Europris expanded its non-food sales by 10% under its multi-year store remodeling program, and Norwegian toy sales grew 16.4% year-on-year in July 2026.

The evidence demonstrates particularly strong momentum in high-overlap categories where online marketplaces previously competed aggressively on price. Jumbo home products reached €234.17m in the first half of 2026, representing 45.10% of total group sales, while the toy category generated €99.76m comprising 19.21% of overall revenue, and seasonal sales reached €96.04m.

Yet the durability of this competitive advantage remains highly uncertain because Chinese marketplaces are executing aggressive logistics mitigation strategies. Temu plans to fulfill over 80% of European orders from local warehouses by late 2026, while Shein is actively expanding its European local warehouse network to mitigate rising import duties. In April 2025, Temu partnered with DHL to support local merchant fulfillment across Europe and is expanding its Local Seller Program across 35 European countries to bypass direct-to-consumer tariffs. These infrastructure investments suggest that the pricing differential which drove initial consumer switching could erode within twelve to eighteen months as local fulfillment restores marketplace unit economics. Association Geographic positioning and sourcing exposure create meaningful differentiation among European discount chains in their ability to defend market share gains. Rusta remains vulnerable to geopolitical freight and sourcing inflation as 40% of its products are Asian-sourced, while Nordic retailers face different local currency headwind pressures when purchasing Asian inventory in US dollars. The strategic response of each chain to these structural challenges will determine whether temporary traffic gains translate into sustained earnings growth or merely represent a brief reprieve before Chinese platforms reclaim lost volume through localized operations.

The investment thesis confronts critical information gaps that upcoming financial disclosures and regulatory developments will illuminate. It remains unclear whether retailers can successfully shift sourcing to alternative regions like North Africa or Egypt to completely bypass Asian supply chain disruptions, while logistics experts express deep skepticism regarding whether Temu can actually surpass a 40% to 45% local-to-local sales share without experiencing severe merchant integration challenges. It is uncertain how subsequent regulatory fees like the additional handling fee of €2 slated for late 2026 will affect overall marketplace competitiveness. Furthermore, loophole exploitation and rapid shifting of marketplace inventory to local European Union warehouses could quickly erase discounters' market share gains. The window for physical retailers to capitalize on regulatory disruption may prove narrower than initial evidence suggests, particularly if Chinese platforms demonstrate the operational agility to localize inventory at scale while maintaining their structural cost advantages through merchant partnerships and bulk consolidation strategies.
