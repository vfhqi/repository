The one-line view. Neutral, medium conviction: a strong and improving high-voltage cable maker riding a policy-driven grid build-out, whose growth the share price already pays for.

What the company does

Cenergy is a Belgian holding company, listed in Brussels and Athens, that owns two manufacturers based in Greece. Hellenic Cables makes land and subsea power cables, up to the ultra-high-voltage systems that link islands, countries and offshore wind farms to the grid. Corinth Pipeworks makes steel pipe for gas, hydrogen and carbon-capture networks. Revenue was 2,061 million euros in 2025, about 70% from cables, and the parent, Viohalco, owns 66.7%.

A plausible investment thesis

Demand is the engine. European and United States grid operators are contracting high-voltage cable years ahead at prices that have doubled, because regulated transmission spending is growing faster than qualified capacity. Cenergy doubled its subsea and land capacity between 2023 and early 2025, qualified for 525-kilovolt high-voltage direct current cable in July and August 2026, which opens the largest interconnector tenders, and is building a plant in Maryland. The backlog rose from 1.0 billion euros in 2021 to 3.9 billion at 30 June 2026. The Investment Analyst models earnings per share compounding about 14% a year, from 1.21 euros in 2026 to 1.79 in 2029, a little above consensus at about 12%.

Fit with the Target Setup Profiles

The memo judged 'Demand-driven EPSU' (demand-led earnings per share step-up) the primary fit at B, with 'Delivering - Serial Acquirer - Mechanical EPS Upgrader' a weaker secondary at C. The three cause-for-concern setups do not fit. I agree, with a timing caveat. The pair is clean: 'Demand-driven EPSU' combines freely with any other. But this is the pattern's fourth year: consensus 2027 earnings per share rose 21.8% in twelve months and the shares doubled, so the large upgrades the setup promises have mostly happened.

Case Riskiness and simplicity

Simple on fundamentals, risky on price. The memo rated the Investment Case C (the dashboard carries it as Case Riskiness C), with Simplicity B and mark-to-market risk F. With earnings unchanged, the memo's arithmetic at 23.10 euros had the shares falling 23% at the two-year median multiple and 41% at the ten-year tenth percentile. Concentrations add to it: no owned cable-laying vessels and Greece's Independent Power Transmission Operator at 68% of backlog.

Business quality

Good, held back by what it lacks. The memo rated Foundations B: an A industry, a B business, B momentum. Initial guidance was beaten by 7 to 13% in each of 2023 to 2025, and adjusted return on capital employed of 27.8% leads the sector. Against that, revenue is about a tenth of Prysmian's, cash conversion was minus 18% in 2025, free cash flow turns positive only in 2027 on consensus, and governance carries a discount.

Plausible returns and valuation

Below Richard's D anchor. At 23.10 euros the memo's modal return was about 10% a year over eighteen months and 7% over three, against about 12% for a D; the memo's valuation Headline Rating is F, and the dashboard's TSR/Valuation reading of 30 September is F. The price is now 24.24 euros (30 September), or 16.4 times 24-month forward earnings per share of 1.48 euros, at the 84th percentile of ten years, so the return from here is lower still. Even a held multiple gave only 16.6% a year, a C. The memo would re-open at about 20 euros, 17% lower.

Value chain and thematics: the pros and the cons

The pros are real. High-voltage cable is a qualified oligopoly: the top three hold 74 to 80% of European interconnections, and pricing per kilometre is 2.3 times its 2019-2020 level. Local-content rules and 50% United States tariffs protect local producers. The memo rated thematic momentum B (grid electrification and data-centre power strengthening, offshore wind fading), and Portfolio Fitness for Fighting holds grid capital goods at Overweight.

The cons are dated and visible. A capacity wave from NKT, Prysmian, Nexans and Asian entrants lands in 2027-2028, inside three years, while offshore wind falters. Portfolio Fitness for Fighting stops short of a stronger grid call because the programmes are debt-funded in a rates-led late cycle, and it prefers large, proven names, which is why the fitness reading penalises Cenergy's 4.8-billion-euro size. The script's Thematic Fit/Momentum reading of 30 September is C.

The strongest counter-case

A tier-two subcontractor riding a tier-one cycle. Record margins come from a temporary capacity shortage the leaders are spending to close by 2028, and the parent sold stock at 24.20 euros in June 2026, where the shares sit today. The risks report's bear case is 6.80 euros. For it to win, subsea prices and utilisation must fall as new capacity arrives from 2027, or a sector de-rating must pull the multiple towards 11 times regardless of delivery. From the other side, a first 525-kilovolt interconnector award and a full Maryland order book within eighteen months could lift earnings per share growth towards 20% a year and clear the bar at today's price. Neither is contracted.
