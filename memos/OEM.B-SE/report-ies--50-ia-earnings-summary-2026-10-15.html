OEM International (OEM.B-SE): what the Q3 2026 report does to the case

16 October 2026

On what is in front of us these results leave the case where it was, and not because the quarter was quiet: the Earnings Review carries the bar the sell-side set for 15 October 2026 and no figure the company reported against it, so the one thing this piece exists to test cannot yet be tested, while the three things in the review that are new, a Chair leaving, an eighth deal of GBP 2.5m of sales, and German orders down 10.6% in a month, press modestly on legs the case already carries and move no rating.

What changed

The reported quarter is absent. The review states that the Q3 2026 figures scheduled for 15 October 2026 are not available to it and that no broker has published on or after that date, so there is no sell-side response to carry. What it carries is the bar and the backdrop.

The bar:

  • DNB Carnegie (30 September 2026) expected Q3 2026 net sales of SEK 1,484m, up 16% year on year (7% organic, 9% structural, currency neutral), adjusted EBITA of SEK 250m, up 19%, a margin of 16.9% (up 40 basis points), and reported EPS of SEK 1.30. It raised 2027e and 2028e adjusted EPS by 1% to SEK 6.03 and SEK 6.47 and held Buy with a SEK 200 target (14 July 2026); the review also carries SEK 205 as the level reached before the report.
  • RedEye expected net sales of SEK 1,504m, up 18%, adjusted EBITA of SEK 270m at about an 18% margin, and net income of SEK 200m.
  • The base: Q3 2025 net sales SEK 1,280m, adjusted EBITA SEK 211m, a 16.5% margin, organic growth 6%; Q2 2026 net sales a record SEK 1,559m, up 17%.
  • The pre-results multiple: 25.9x 2026e EV/EBITA against Addtech 24.1x, Lagercrantz 24.2x, Lifco 21.8x and a peer median of 18.8x; 36.0x 2026e P/E against a peer median of 31.8x. RedEye's warning is that flat organic order growth in Q3 could take the multiple toward its historical mean of 22x.

The backdrop, which is where the new facts are:

  • Chair Christopher Norbye announced on 21 September 2026 that he intends to step down, and an Extraordinary General Meeting on 23 October 2026 is called to elect a new Chair. Chief executive Jörgen Zahlin leaves the role during 2026; the CFO changed on 1 August 2026; a Deputy CEO arrived in April 2026.
  • OEM bought 100% of ABSSAC Ltd, a UK motion-control distributor with annual sales of GBP 2.5m, on 1 October 2026, with a "marginally positive" earnings effect this financial year.
  • German industrial orders fell 10.6% in August 2026; Rothschild & Co Redburn has pushed the European construction recovery to 2027; Morgan Stanley warns that short-cycle indicators may be overstated by pre-buying ahead of tariffs; and DNB Carnegie attributed OEM's 15% organic order growth in Q2 2026 to "a strong pre-buying industrial mentality". Rest of Europe organic sales were -2% in Q2 2026, and construction segments in Sweden and Finland remained weak.
  • The price: SEK 176.8 on 14 October 2026, the day before the print, from the case's SEK 187.2 of 23 September; three-month return -3.1%, twelve-month +32.3%; P/E 28.2x at the 72nd percentile of the stock's own ten-year history; consensus 24-month-forward EPS SEK 6.2726; six-month EPS revisions +8% and target revisions +24%; buy share 75%; consensus target SEK 196.0.

The challenge

Three readings the case does not hold, each stated as strongly as the evidence allows.

The top of the company is being rebuilt twice, not once. The case's comfort on succession was that the layer above the subsidiaries had already changed: a chairman since April 2025, six of seven directors new, a Deputy CEO and a CFO in place, so the one pending move was the chief executive, and the case's modal is a successor named by 31 December 2026 who keeps the cadence. That layer is now changing again. The Chair who brought the Beijer Ref playbook leaves eighteen months in, eight days after the print, and the board that elects his replacement on 23 October 2026 is the board that then names or confirms the chief executive. The strongest form of the reading: the sequencing pushes the successor decision past the case's own alarm date of 31 December 2026, or hands it to a new Chair whose brief we do not know; the caretaker red flag the case says is absent becomes live; and the one hard datum of strain, employee turnover of 13% in 2025 against a target below 10%, is the leading edge of what the case's risks research called a false friend. What would have to be true: no successor by year-end, or a successor whose mandate is set by the new Chair with a platform-deal remit; the departure of two division heads within a year would be the case's own invalidation. What runs against it: the stated reason is other commitments, EQT holds 73.68% of the votes on the case's figures so the controller and therefore the mandate do not change, and the CFO and Deputy CEO are in post. We think the reading is plausible rather than probable, and the case does not carry it at all.

The order book is borrowed. The case reads the Q2 2026 order surge as visibility into the first half of 2027 and gives organic growth of 5% or better through 2027 about 60% odds; it weighed Finnish restocking and set it aside. It did not weigh pre-buying ahead of tariffs as the source of the whole swing. The review does: DNB Carnegie's own description of the 15% organic order growth is pre-buying, Morgan Stanley says short-cycle indicators are being overstated by it, German orders fell 10.6% in a single month, and construction is now a 2027 story. The strongest form: the Q3 bar of 7% organic is met out of the backlog while orders fall below sales, Q4 2026 and Q1 2027 pay it back, and the case's alarm, organic growth below 5% for two quarters, trips in the spring of 2027 on a stock still at 28.2x. What would have to be true: a Q3 book-to-bill below 1.0x beyond the seasonal softness the case allows for, organic order growth well below Q2's 15%, and German orders not rebounding in September and October. What runs against it: Finland's demand improvement is described as a clear recovery after a multi-year downturn rather than a pre-buy, and nearshoring is a slow but real tailwind. Plausible, and sharper than the case's version of the same risk, because the case's risk is construction spreading, not the order book itself being front-loaded.

The cadence is being kept by count, not by size. The case's path has eight deals a year of SEK 30m to 250m of sales, about SEK 60m each, adding about 8% of the base a year, and its alarm is fewer than four deals in a year. The 2026 deals in the review are Cre8 at SEK 160m of annual sales, Weingrill at EUR 7.7m, Kokkolan Sähkö ja Automaatio at EUR 3.6m, Motion29 at SEK 13m and now ABSSAC at GBP 2.5m. One, Cre8, is comfortably inside the case's range; Motion29 is below its floor; the other three, in euros and sterling, are small single-digit millions that sit at the low end of the range rather than at the SEK 60m the path needs on average. A count alarm cannot see a size shortfall. The strongest form: acquired growth runs at perhaps half the case's 8% a year, the 'Compounder' secondary's upgrade by construction shrinks to rounding, the growth leg of the return, which the case already puts a little below its 10% path, falls further, and the balance sheet at -0.5x net debt to EBITDA for 2026e on DNB Carnegie's numbers says the capacity the leverage branch needs is not being drawn. DNB Carnegie's own model needs acquisitions to add 11% to EBITA every year from 2026 to 2033 to justify the valuation. What would have to be true: the second-half 2026 deals adding well under the SEK 476m of annual sales the case counts for the twelve months to July 2026, and no deal above about SEK 100m of sales by year-end. What runs against it: Cre8 shows the range is reachable, management describes a solid pipeline for upcoming quarters, and one GBP 2.5m deal is a data point, not a trend. Possible, and worth a line on the monitoring table that counts sales acquired rather than deals.

The reading we cannot put up is the one Richard would most want: that the quarter beat or missed. Nothing in the review says either, and we do not guess. The reading on the other side, which the case already holds, is that the de-rating leg has started without a miss: the multiple went from the case's 30.4x at the 100th percentile to 28.2x at the 72nd in three weeks while 24-month-forward consensus EPS rose from the case's SEK 6.1645 to SEK 6.2726, which is the case's 60% branch beginning, and a print that merely meets DNB Carnegie's bar on a stock that has rested for three months is likely to read as relief rather than disappointment.

We re-rate nothing. The pressure runs against the case on succession, the cycle and deal size, and for it, slightly, on price.

What would settle it

  • The reported quarter itself: organic growth against DNB Carnegie's 7%, the adjusted EBITA margin against 16.9%, order intake and book-to-bill against the 1.08x of Q2, Rest of Europe against -2%. The company was due to report on 15 October 2026, and the second Earnings Review, four working days on, is the one that carries those figures and the post-results estimates and targets, which this one cannot. That review settles the beat-or-miss question and the first half of the order-book reading. It does not settle the other half, which needs Q4 2026 orders in February 2027, and it is unlikely to carry the new Chair, because the EGM on 23 October 2026 falls after it.
  • The successor: a name and a brief by 31 December 2026, which is contractual in the sense that the incumbent's contract ends then; the new Chair's identity on 23 October 2026 comes first and is the earlier test.
  • Deal size: every acquisition announced through the fourth quarter with its sales. The company discloses sales and not the multiples paid, and has not committed to disclosing them, so the case's alarm of any deal above 7x EBITA is a question nothing in the company's own disclosure answers unless the practice changes.
  • The integration evidence the review itself asks for, subsidiary-manager retention after acquisition and the return on capital of acquired units three to five years on, is not a disclosure the company has committed to; that is an open question of the other kind, and it stays open.
  • German industrial orders for September and October 2026, monthly, which read directly on the Rest of Europe segment and on the pre-buy reading.
