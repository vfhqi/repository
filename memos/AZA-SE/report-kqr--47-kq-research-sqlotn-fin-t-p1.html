Section 2 – Avanza's Cost Structure and Historical Operating Leverage

Section summary
  1. Avanza exhibits powerful positive operating leverage during market upturns, but its high proportion of fixed personnel and IT costs leads to margin compression during retail trading downturns.
  2. Historical evidence shows Avanza consistently meets or beats its cost growth guidance, primarily by deferring discretionary projects and optimizing external services. This section evaluates Avanza's cost structure and historical operating leverage based on an analysis of 3 closely tracked companies, 15 distinct primary sources including annual filings and transcripts, and 2 major historical market cycle precedents.

Dissecting the cost base

1.
Dominant personnel spend: Avanza's operating expenses are heavily dominated by employee compensation, which limits immediate flexibility. According to the Avanza Annual Report 2024 published on 20 February 2025, personnel costs accounted for 62% of total operating expenses of SEK 1,280m, which is significantly higher than marketing at 2% and external services at 7%. This large staff concentration means that earnings per share remain highly sensitive to wage inflation and headcount growth, unless the company implements automated self-service systems to contain staff expansion.
2.
Rigid technology expenses: Avanza's technology and licensing costs represent a semi-fixed burden that increases with platform development. The Avanza Annual and Sustainability Report 2025 issued on 12 March 2026 shows that IT systems and licenses represented 14% of the SEK 1,413m total cost base, up from 12% in 2024. These expenses are mostly contracted and do not decrease during low retail activity, so they act as a drag on earnings per share in a market downturn unless renegotiated.
3.
Contrary evidence: Avanza's management has historically kept transaction-related commissions highly variable to absorb volume drops. The Avanza Annual Report 2023 published on 22 February 2024 shows that transaction costs fell by 11.5% to SEK 138m in 2023 compared to SEK 156m in 2022. This variable expense reduction directly offsets trading income declines, which means that some operating margin protection is achieved during low-volume periods.
4.
Variable marketing budgets: Avanza's marketing expenditures are highly discretionary and can be adjusted rapidly to protect profitability. According to the Avanza Q3 2026 Presentation published on 16 October 2026, marketing accounted for only 3% of year-to-date costs, down from typical strategic peaks. This low baseline provides limited scope for further cost cutting, meaning that any major earnings per share protection must come from larger lines like personnel or IT.
5.
External services stability: Avanza's reliance on external professional services represents a stable and recurring operating cost. According to the Avanza Annual and Sustainability Report 2025 published on 12 March 2026, external services accounted for exactly 7% of total operating costs, remaining completely unchanged from 7% in 2024. These costs are sticky because they include regulatory compliance and audit support, which means they cannot be easily cut during a downturn without increasing legal or operational risks.
6.
Minor depreciation headwinds: Depreciation and amortization charges are predictable but remain completely rigid during retail volume contractions. The Avanza Annual Report 2024 published on 20 February 2025 showed that depreciation represented 7% of total costs, which is tied directly to past capitalized IT infrastructure investments. This fixed non-cash expense persists regardless of market sentiment, so it puts continuous downward pressure on earnings per share unless old IT assets are completely written off.
