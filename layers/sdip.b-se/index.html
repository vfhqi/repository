To what extent, and in which direction, is competitive intensity changing?

C-4821st of 56

Layer 1 · External change forces

In the product niches, competitive intensity is unchanged and benign: units hold top-three positions in micro-verticals with few direct competitors, prices did not fall when volumes fell in 2024 and 2025 (the Water & Bioeconomy margin loss was cost-driven, not price-driven), and Energy & Electrification held about a 25% margin through component inflation. The one exception is EV charging, where more than 60 UK AC-charger suppliers and Chinese hardware at USD 25 to 40 a unit already cost Rolec (about 6% of group EBITA) its early lead. That shock has happened; Rolec reported a strong third quarter on UK commercial fleet demand, and a competitor (Pod Point) exited non-core business-to-business depot charging.

The competitive change that matters more to this case is in the market for acquisitions, because the acquisition engine is the largest single EPS lever. It is tightening, and it is tightening exactly where Sdiptech is now buying. Two of the four 2026 deals were British (A.U.T. Wheels and Castors, VALE Engineering), and in October 2026 Addtech agreed to buy 82% of Stoke-on-Trent's Mechanical Power Transmission (GBP 10m of sales) while the Nordic compounders broadly push abroad (acquisitions outside the Nordics rose from 28% of the largest compounders' deals in 2013 to 61% in 2023). New entrants (Tången, Auroora, Röko, InstallatørGruppen) and private equity add bidders. The offset, which the market under-notices: the weaker listed acquirers have lost their acquisition currency (Volati's engine is stopped by leverage, Indus has paused, Storskogen is restarting slowly at 7x or below), which removes some competing bidders in the near term.

Net judgement: product competition is stable; competition for targets is rising slowly. The evidence so far is that pricing discipline has held (the second-quarter deals were at about 6x EBITA; the third-quarter deals add SEK 40m of run-rate profit for about GBP 18.5m of initial consideration, roughly 6x before earn-outs, my calculation from the disclosed figures). The risk is that the total price including earn-outs drifts toward the historical 9x.

How are input costs, input availability and supplier concentration changing, and is the net effect a headwind or a tailwind?

❌D4429th of 56

The net effect is a mild headwind for the next six months, turning neutral in 2027, and the reason is contract structure rather than supply.
