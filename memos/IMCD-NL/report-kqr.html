Working capital expansion: Rising chemical prices inflate the balance sheet values of inventory and receivables for specialty chemical distributors. This cash absorption occurs because distributors must purchase higher-priced inventory before collecting receivables from customers, directly reducing cash conversion efficiency. If raw material inflation persists, these capital requirements can significantly depress free cash flow generation and raise net debt. Leverage constraint: Programmatic consolidators experience a strict leverage-based cap on their acquisition engine when working capital expands. Morgan Stanley reports on September 8, 2026, that elevated leverage ratios restrict the ability of specialty distributors to execute bolt-on mergers. This occurs because rating agencies impose strict leverage thresholds that companies must respect to safeguard their credit ratings. Consequently, raw material inflation can trigger a balancing loop where higher prices block programmatic M&A compounding, ultimately threatening long-term earnings per share
