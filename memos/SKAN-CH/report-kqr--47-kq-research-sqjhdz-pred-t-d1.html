Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. The turnaround at SKAN Group AG is plausible but not yet proven. The strongest evidence is a record order book and structural demand tailwinds. The weakest evidence is the company's recent delivery record, with both segment margins still well below 2024 levels and an unproven new pay-per-use service. The report's Key Question asks how plausible the change now expected is for this company, given its record of delivery and the strengths the business demonstrably has, rather than strengths it would need to acquire. New CEO Jonas Greutert, who took over on January 1, 2026, previously managed the product inspection division at Mettler-Toledo. Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. SKAN Group AG faces a critical period as it attempts to restore profitability and execute on its record order backlog of CHF 442.3 million.
  2. Delays in project conversion and margin pressure in both segments represent major risks to future earnings per share and valuation.

Primary key question

1.
Plausible change: "how plausible is the change now expected for this company, given its record of delivery and the strengths the business demonstrably has, rather than strengths it would need to acquire?"

Earnings and risk implications

2.
Operational execution: SKAN Group AG must successfully convert its CHF 442.3 million order backlog to secure its high-teens sales growth target. Any further project delays or customer cancellations would severely depress earnings per share and trigger a downward valuation re-rating. Investors face heightened risks because past execution failures led to missed revenue guidance in 2024 and withdrawn guidance in 2025. Consequently, on-time project delivery remains critical for restoring investor confidence over the next three years.
3.
Severe margin: SKAN Group AG saw its Equipment & Solutions EBITDA margin collapse from 11.2% in 2024 to a mere 2.3% in the first half of 2026. The dramatic margin contraction reflects the non-linear nature of project execution and a lack of high-value-added phases during the period. If the segment fails to rebound, corporate earnings per share will remain depressed despite high revenue growth. Thus, the recovery of this core segment is essential to justify the company's premium mid-term growth valuation.
4.
Declining profitability: SKAN Group AG recorded a sharp decline in its Services & Consumables EBITDA margin from 29.5% in 2024 to 20.1% in the first half of 2026. Segment margins were negatively impacted by a shifting product mix and a high baseline of comparison. Because this segment is expected to be a highly profitable recurring revenue stream, a swift margin recovery is vital to lift corporate margins. Structural profitability declines would impair overall cash flows and compound execution risks in the equipment division.
5.
Unproven business: SKAN Group AG faces significant execution uncertainty regarding its new Pre-Approved Services pay-per-use sterile filling model. Swissmedic regulatory approval has repeatedly slipped, moving from late 2025 to within 2026, which delays the projected CHF 50 million revenue ramp. While management targets an exceptionally high EBITDA margin of up to 50% for this business, commercial viability remains entirely unproven under real operating conditions. Regulatory or commercial setbacks would result in asset write-downs rather than the expected earnings per share accretion.

Related capital expenditure questions

6.
Accelerating regulatory: Jefferies Research reported that the EU GMP Annex 1 revision has raised regulatory expectations around contamination control and quality risk management. To comply with these new standards, manufacturers are increasingly replacing older clean rooms with specialized isolator systems. Consequently, West Pharmaceutical Services generated significant high-value product organic growth in 2026, supported by 211 new upgrade projects. The ongoing technological transition means Annex 1 upgrades support long-term demand for SKAN's advanced sterile filling solutions.
7.
Expanding demand: Morgan Stanley Research highlighted that Annex 1 regulatory tailwinds are still in their early innings and offer a multi-year growth runway for packaging players. Furthermore, the US FDA published draft container closure guidance in August 2026 that could spark a similar regulatory-driven upgrade cycle in the United States. Market analysts conclude that sterile packaging demand remains highly durable over the medium term. Ultimately, evolving regulatory mandates force pharmaceutical companies to continuously invest in state-of-the-art sterile manufacturing equipment.
8.
Surging peptide: Bernstein Research expects global peptide manufacturing demand to increase dramatically from 202 metric tons in 2026 to approximately 1.4k metric tons by 2033. Rising peptide consumption is driven not only by continued GLP-1 adoption but also by a shift toward higher-dosage therapies. Consequently, leading biologics CDMOs are heavily expanding their sterile fill-finish facilities to accommodate escalating commercial volumes. Thus, the unprecedented scale of peptide manufacturing represents a highly favorable structural tailwind for aseptic equipment makers.
9.
Substantial capital: Nuvama Institutional Equities reported that the multibillion-dollar GLP-1 market is experiencing massive investment in manufacturing capacity due to unprecedented demand. Dedicated sterile fill-finish lines are required because self-injection pens must be assembled under strictly controlled environmental conditions. For instance, Eli Lilly is executing a massive capital expenditure program to construct robotic and automated aseptic filling capabilities. Therefore, GLP-1 capacity expansion is fueling a sustained equipment procurement cycle across the pharmaceutical industry.

Opposing evidence and perspectives

10.
Contrary evidence: Deutsche Bank reported that pharmaceutical visual inspection and assembly equipment sales declined by 12% at Stevanato Group due to slower pipeline conversion and customer order delays. Persistent capital equipment underperformance suggests that large-pharma capital spending may be cooling or becoming increasingly selective after recent capacity expansions. Slower decision-making among biopharma clients poses a direct threat to the execution schedules of equipment manufacturers worldwide.
11.
Other side of the story: SKAN Group AG might overcome its recent execution hurdles because its record backlog and the non-recurring nature of its 2025 project postponements provide a highly visible path to high-teens sales growth. If customer sites are validated on time, the rapid conversion of projects into high-value-added phases would trigger a swift margin recovery. Thus, historical execution failures may not dictate the company's future performance under its restructured segment accountability and new leadership.

Section 5 – Pre-Approved Services and Pay-Per-Use Viability

Section summary
  1. SKAN Group AG has established Maeven AG to pioneer a unique pre-approved pay-per-use clinical filling service.
  2. Repeated regulatory approval delays highlight the execution complexities of transitioning from an equipment manufacturer to a licensed service operator.

Maeven AG and Service Concept

1.
Strategic launch: SKAN Group AG established its wholly owned subsidiary Maeven AG on 6 February 2025 to manage the Pre-Approved Services segment. This specialized entity provides pre-approved filling services directly to biopharma clients to support technology transfer and validation. By utilizing Maeven AG, customers can significantly shorten drug time-to-market by performing early-stage stability testing on precertified systems. This strategic setup shifts SKAN from a pure hardware provider to an integrated service partner.
2.
Facility setup: SKAN Group AG designed its Maeven launch facility to incorporate two advanced aseptic filling lines. The first line handles medium production volumes in various container formats such as vials, syringes, and cartridges. The second dedicated line focuses on manufacturing cell and gene therapies under advanced therapy medicinal product standards. This flexible hardware configuration allows the company to target diverse biopharmaceutical clients from early clinical phases through commercialization.

Swissmedic Regulatory Approval Delay

3.
