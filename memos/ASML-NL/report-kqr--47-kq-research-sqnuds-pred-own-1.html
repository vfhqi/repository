Annual Revenue Expansion and Performance

1.
Accelerating segment growth: ASML generated substantial service and upgrade sales as its global fleet expanded and utilization normalized. According to ASML's annual report published on February 24, 2026, the company reported Installed Base Management revenue of €6.5 billion in 2024 and €8.2 billion in 2025. This rapid growth occurred because customers ran existing systems at high utilization rates to meet AI chip demand, which ultimately bolstered Installed Base Management recurring sales across both EUV and DUV platforms.
2.
Unprecedented upgrade demand: ASML experienced a dramatic surge in segment sales during 2026 as customers turned to performance modifications instead of waiting for long-lead new machines. CCB International reported in its broker research note of October 15, 2026, that Installed Base Management revenue was guided to grow by around 50% YoY in 2026. This massive acceleration means segment revenue will reach approximately €12.25 billion (calculated) in 2026, so ASML successfully generated highly resilient midcycle cash flows even as macroeconomic concerns made customers cautious about starting new fab construction projects.
3.
Predictable recurring streams: ASML secures a highly durable long-term cash flow foundation from its service and maintenance contracts because they scale automatically with tool shipments. Bernstein Research calculated in a note of October 19, 2026, that system backlog historically represents 85% of remaining performance obligations, meaning that service and upgrade commitments account for the remaining 15% of performance obligations representing approximately €8.2 billion at the start of 2026. These recurring contracts provide a predictable revenue floor unless fab utilization drops sharply and leads customers to defer non-discretionary maintenance.

Upgrade Composition vs Recurring Service

4.

An expert call with a managing director at an IT services firm on September 5, 2026, revealed that leading-edge logic customers typically allocate 70% to 75% of capital to new tools and 25% to 30% to upgrades across mature fabs. This balanced mix ensures that ASML benefits from both greenfield expansions and brownfield optimizations, which means that earnings per share remain protected even if new tool installations face temporary delivery delays.
5.
Evolving segment mix: ASML's memory customers adjusted their upgrade allocations more aggressively than logic foundries during the recent memory cycle recovery. An expert call with a procurement leader at a memory device manufacturer on September 3, 2026, indicated that memory fabs allocated 40% of lithography capital to upgrading existing tools to expand high-bandwidth memory capacity. This high upgrade allocation helped the customer bypass long tool lead times, so ASML was able to capture near-term revenue gains without stretching its own system assembly resources.
6.
Contrary evidence: A former semiconductor company vice president suggested in an expert call on June 30, 2026, that customer expectations of service upgrade revenues only accounted for 5% to 10% of ASML's total business. This lower estimate contradicts the broader consensus because it understates the massive highmargin software upgrades deployed in 2026, which means that actual segment revenue contribution was far higher than this single expert anticipated.
