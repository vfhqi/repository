Section 2 – Regulatory mechanics of cost and traffic deviations

Section summary
  1. The approved DORA III settlement enforces a highly asymmetric risk-sharing framework where Aena remains fully exposed to operating cost overruns while retaining significant financial upside from

outperforming conservative regulated passenger traffic forecasts.

Treatment of operating cost deviations

1.
Complete cost exposure: Under the basic tenets of Act 18/2014, Aena bears 100% of the risk for any operating expenditures that exceed the regulator's approved allowances, with no direct mechanism to pass these overruns onto airport users through tariff adjustments.
2.
Inefficiency penalties: Because the regulatory framework is built upon an efficient operator cost-recovery model, any operational cost overruns incurred by Aena directly dilute its operating margins and EPS trajectory over the 18-month to 3-year horizon.
3.
Exceptional opex carve-out: The sole exception to this risk allocation occurs under Article 27 of Act 18/2014, which permits upward tariff adjustments exclusively for unpredictable, non-imputable "exceptional operating costs" that directly threaten the financial viability of the airport network.
4.
Rigid efficiency benchmarking: To enforce cost discipline, the Directorate General of Civil Aviation benchmarks Aena's allowable operational expenses against the average of the five largest listed airport operators in Europe.
5.
Diverging opex expectations: Bernstein Research highlights that while Aena proposed a dynamic opex growth rate of 3.8% annually, Spain's competition authority, the Comisión Nacional de los Mercados y la Competencia, pushed back heavily by recommending an opex growth rate of only 1.9% p.a.

Regulatory treatment of passenger traffic deviations

6.
Traffic volume retention: The DORA III regulatory architecture does not feature a standard retrospective clawback mechanism, meaning that Aena fully retains the financial windfall when actual passenger volumes outperform the regulator's baseline projections.
7.
Asymmetric volume risk: While Aena benefits from traffic outperformance, it is similarly exposed to the downside of traffic volume shortfalls, as tariffs cannot be adjusted mid-period to compensate for lower-thanexpected passenger numbers unless specific legal thresholds are crossed.
8.
Exceptional traffic threshold: Under Act 18/2014, a formal DORA modification and extraordinary tariff revision can only be triggered if unpredictable, non-imputable events (such as natural disasters, terrorist acts, or war) cause a network-wide annual passenger drop exceeding 10%.
9.
Cabinet approval rules: If exceptional traffic or opex deviations exceed 50% of the maximum cumulative deviation allowed under the DORA, any emergency tariff remedy must be formally authorized by the Spanish Council of Ministers following a report from the Government Delegate Commission for Economic Affairs.
10.
Conservative traffic baselines: Deutsche Bank Research notes that the approved DORA III settlement models a conservative traffic CAGR of 1.3% from 2025 to 2031, which is substantially below Aena's actual passenger growth and sets a very low bar for traffic outperformance.
11.
Structural outperformance potential: Morgan Stanley emphasizes that Aena's high exposure to narrowbody fleet growth and low-cost carrier penetration makes it the primary beneficiary of structural traffic upside, which is not captured in the conservative regulatory forecast.

Annual IMAP to IMAAJ adjustment formula

12.
Annual price cap transition: The National Markets and Competition Commission adjusts the baseline Annual Maximum Revenue per Passenger (IMAP) each year to establish the Adjusted Annual Maximum Revenue per Passenger (IMAAJ), which represents the actual cap on airport charges. Inflation indexing (P-factor): The first adjustment variable is the P-index, approved annually by the regulator to capture changes in input costs outside Aena's control, utilizing a specific cost-profile formula developed under Royal Decree 162/2019. Quality incentive adjustments (B-factor): The baseline tariff is adjusted by a quality incentive factor (B) ranging from +2% to -2%, which penalizes or rewards Aena based on its annual performance across 11 key service quality indicators. Investment compliance factor (D-factor): Tariffs are adjusted downward via the D-factor to penalize Aena for delays in executing its approved strategic capex program, with the maximum annual penalty capped at 2% of the year's scheduled investment. Two-year recovery lag (K-factor): Under the K-factor adjustment, any historical over- or under-earnings per passenger relative to the IMAAJ cap from two years prior are fully reconciled and adjusted in the current year's tariff.
13.
Other side of the story: Deutsche Bank Research warns that the regulator remains highly sensitive to Aena's historical over-earnings under DORA II, which could lead to more aggressive regulatory clawbacks or lower opex allowances in future periods if actual traffic continues to outpace the baseline forecasts.
