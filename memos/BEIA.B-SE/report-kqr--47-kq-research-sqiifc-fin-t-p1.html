Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Operating leverage and cost adaptability represent the primary structural drivers of Beijer Alma's long- term earnings trajectory and strategic valuation.

Defining the core question

1.
Key Question: "how much fixed-cost operating leverage does this business model carry, how much scope is there for it to work over the next 18 months to four years, and, where it is relevant to the company's strategy, how feasible is cost elimination or reduction, both incremental and in material large cuts?"
2.
Significant strategic pivot: Beijer Alma is transitioning from a traditional industrial company to a compounder, which alters its historical operating leverage dynamics. Under acting CEO Johnny Alvarsson, the group is refocusing Beijer Components on margin accretion and organic improvement. This strategic shift makes understanding the fixed-to-variable cost split critical because excess capital is being channeled to Beijer Tech's high-returning M&A platform. If Beijer Components fails to maintain high margins, the group's transition to a compounder model slows.
3.
High cyclical sensitivity: Beijer Alma remains highly exposed to general industrial cycles, with roughly 77% of sales tied to macroeconomic activity. Historical downturns like the 2009 recession and 2020 pandemic triggered swift margin contractions that required severe personnel adjustments. Consequently, identifying the exact drop-through rate of organic volume is essential for estimating forward earnings per share volatility. Without clear visibility on cost structures, investors cannot assess whether the 20% margin target is achievable without a market recovery.

Valuation and peer dynamics

4.
Elevated execution risk: Beijer Alma relies on a very lean corporate team of about four executives to manage over 70 standalone subsidiaries. While this asset-light overhead model supports high capital efficiency, it places immense pressure on individual managing directors. Thus, any failure in local cost control could quickly erode group-level operating margins.
5.
Substantial valuation discount: Beijer Alma trades at a notable discount of approximately 15% compared to its Swedish compounder peers on 2026 expected EV/EBITA. This valuation gap reflects historical skepticism regarding capital allocation and poor return on incremental capital employed in Beijer Components. If current margin improvement programs successfully demonstrate structural operating leverage, a re-rating of the stock is highly likely.
6.
Varying cost flexibility: Unlike peers like Lifco or Indutrade that maintain highly stable decentralized margins, Beijer Components has experienced structural margin declines since 2018. This trend highlights the importance of analyzing whether Beijer Alma's niche manufacturing units carry more rigid fixed costs. Understanding whether margins can be restored within four quarters of a downturn remains a key differentiator for investors.
7.
Contrary evidence: Some broker assessments indicate that Beijer Alma's operating leverage may be less of a headwind because the company operates with a highly flexible local-for-local strategy. DNB Carnegie highlights that US revenues are manufactured locally, which insulates the cost base from tariff-related margin shocks. This structural buffer suggests that external macro pressures might not depress margins as severely as historical cycles imply.
8.
Other side of the story: While management targets a rapid recovery to a 20% margin, historical integration challenges at acquired entities like Alcomex show that structural cost reduction is complex. Operational underperformance and expensive expansion initiatives previously dragged down Lesjöfors' overall profitability. If these acquired units require prolonged restructuring, the projected operating leverage benefits from organic volume recovery will be delayed.

Section 2 – Cost Structures and Operating Leverage of Components and Tech

Section summary
  1. Beijer Components carries high manufacturing fixed costs while Beijer Tech benefits from an asset-light distribution cost base.
  2. High raw material expenses represent the group's largest variable cost, while personnel overhead creates structural operating leverage.
  3. Chassis Springs aftermarket pricing shields margins from cyclical downturns, though seasonal inventory builds pressure first-quarter cash flow.
  4. Beijer Tech relies on high-margin acquisitions to reach its long-term fifteen percent adjusted operating margin target.

Divisional cost structures and overhead

1.
Elevated variable inputs: Beijer Alma managed net sales of MSEK 7,750 in 2025 while raw materials accounted for MSEK 3,399 and personnel costs reached MSEK 1,925. This cost structure implies that direct material expenses represent approximately 44% of total group sales. Consequently, the group's overall operating margins remain highly sensitive to fluctuations in raw material and commodity pricing trends.
2.
Significant material expenses: Beijer Alma's cost structure reveals a large variable component, with raw materials of MSEK 3,399 exceeding personnel costs of MSEK 1,925 in 2025. This split means raw materials represented 43.86% of net sales, while personnel expenses made up 24.84% of net sales. Because personnel costs represent a largely fixed overhead, any rapid decline in volumes will disproportionately impact operating margins negatively.
3.
High manufacturing overhead: Beijer Components carries a heavier fixed-cost load because it operates manufacturing plants in 19 countries. Personnel costs and depreciation represent the primary fixed cost lines within its industrial spring production. This extensive factory footprint limits immediate margin flexibility when organic volumes experience cyclical declines globally.
4.
Modest asset requirements: Beijer Tech's asset-light trading model carries much lower operating leverage compared to the asset-heavy manufacturing operations of Beijer Components. In 2025, Beijer Tech's capital expenditures were only MSEK 70, compared to MSEK 158 for Beijer Components. This minimal capital intensity shields Beijer Tech's margins during downcycles, as its fixed depreciation and amortization expenses remain highly controlled.
5.
Superior asset-light efficiency: Beijer Tech operates a highly capital-efficient model focused on Nordic industrial trading and niche manufacturing. Its operations require significantly lower capital expenditure than the group's heavy spring-manufacturing division. This structure allows Beijer Tech to maintain resilient margins during industrial downturns because fixed overhead remains exceptionally low overall.

Subsegment operating leverage and margins

6.
Severe operational leverage: Industrial Springs experiences significant margin volatility due to its dependence on global manufacturing volumes. Raw material inputs like steel wire represent a major variable expense, but factory labor represents a rigid fixed cost. A drop in capacity utilization quickly depresses segment profitability because labor costs cannot be adjusted quickly.
7.
Highly cyclical exposure: Industrial Springs serves a diverse range of cyclical end-markets, which exposes its cost structure to significant volume swings. Approximately 77% of Beijer Alma's total group sales are sensitive to changes in the broader macroeconomic environment. This high sensitivity means that industrial spring factories must maintain high capacity utilization to cover their fixed personnel expenses efficiently.
8.
Strong aftermarket pricing: Chassis Springs benefits from unique aftermarket demand dynamics that support superior operating margins. This division focuses on car and light truck replacement springs, which carry higher pricing power than industrial components. Consequently, this subsegment exhibits lower operating leverage during downturns since aftermarket demand remains highly resilient historically.
9.
Expensive inventory builds: Chassis Springs requires a proactive inventory buildup during winter to meet sudden spring demand from distributors. This inventory build consumes significant working capital, causing group operating cash flows to be seasonally weaker in the first quarter. However, this structure provides strong operating leverage in the second quarter when high-margin sales volumes surge rapidly.
10.
Diverse operating margins: Beijer Tech's three business areas carry distinct margin profiles and structural cost dynamics. Fluid Technology and Industrial Products operate largely as distributors, while Niche Technologies features specialized manufacturing. This mix allows Niche Technologies to achieve higher margins by utilizing advanced capacity investments to boost output.
11.
Low trading margins: Beijer Tech's trading units, including Fluid Technology and Industrial Products, operate with lower gross margins but higher cost flexibility. These business areas act as technical distributors, meaning their cost bases consist primarily of variable cost of goods sold. Consequently, these units exhibit minimal operating leverage, maintaining stable but lower EBITA margins throughout the cycle.

Margin targets and seasonality

12.
Optimistic margin objectives: Beijer Tech continues to target a long-term EBITA margin of 15% through strategic acquisitions. Its adjusted EBITA margin reached 11.8% in 2025 and improved further to 13.9% in the third quarter of 2026. This upward trajectory is supported by the addition of high-margin acquired businesses rather than traditional organic operating leverage expansion alone.
13.
Accretive acquisition strategy: Beijer Tech's margins are structurally improving due to the acquisition of high- margin niche technology companies. Management acquired six companies in 2025 with average operating margins exceeding 15%, which immediately boosted segment profitability. This inorganic strategy allows Beijer Tech to approach its 15% EBITA target without requiring substantial organic volume growth in sluggish markets.
14.
Rigid seasonal inventory: Chassis Springs requires significant working capital accumulation during the first quarter of each year. This seasonal build precedes the spring peak in automotive repair activity. Furthermore, first quarter cash flow is historically weighed down by the payment of customer rebates accumulated from prior periods.
15.
Weak fourth quarters: Beijer Alma consistently experiences its weakest operating margins during the fourth quarter. For instance, the group's adjusted EBITA margin dropped to 14.9% in the fourth quarter of 2025. This cyclical decline reflects fewer working days and lower industrial activity, which underutilizes the fixed cost base temporarily.
16.
Contrary evidence: Some broker research indicates that Beijer Alma's manufacturing footprint provides a structural buffer against external macro shocks. DNB Carnegie reports that the company's local-for-local manufacturing strategy in nearly all markets helps insulate its cost base from tariff-related margin headwinds. This localized setup suggests that operating leverage might not deteriorate as severely as historical cycles would suggest.

Cost efficiency and strategic outlook

17.
Ambitious capital targets: Management has set a strict capital efficiency target of 50% to optimize cash generation across all subsidiaries. The group achieved 45.9% capital efficiency in 2025 and improved to 49.8% by the third quarter of 2026. Achieving this target requires local managers to focus on return on working capital and reduce seasonal inventory buffers significantly.
18.
Shifted growth drivers: Beijer Alma is increasingly relying on M&A to expand margins rather than relying solely on organic operating leverage. The group's adjusted EBITA margin reached 15.0% in 2025, driven by the consolidation of high-margin acquisitions. This acquisition-led strategy helps offset sluggish organic growth since acquired entities carry strong margins immediately.
19.
Other side of the story: While management projects margin expansion from internal actions, prolonged weak industrial activity could cap operating leverage gains. If organic volumes remain sluggish, the high fixed costs of Beijer Components' global manufacturing plants will continue to depress returns. Consequently, achieving the group's 20% margin goal remains highly dependent on a broader global macroeconomic recovery eventually.

  3. Lean corporate administration preserves subsidiary-level agility and prevents profit dilution from centralized administrative growth.

These measures successfully achieved the targeted annual savings of MSEK 35 as of the fourth quarter of 2025. This rapid delivery helped stabilize the division's margins and supported the group's long-term earnings expansion.

Efficient asset adjustment: Beijer Components recorded a large inventory impairment of MSEK -70 as a non-cash component of its 2025 restructuring charges. The write-down focused on customized stocks of springs to clean up low-margin product lines. Consequently, the actual cash impact of this action plan was restricted to approximately MSEK 50 of the total restructuring reserve. This selective restructuring prevented any permanent damage to the group's operating cash flow while structurally improving the underlying margin quality.

Strategic plant closures: Beijer Components initiated the closure of two European facilities during the third quarter of 2026 to optimize its manufacturing footprint. The closures affected one Alcomex factory and one distribution unit for standard springs in order to consolidate operations. Section 5 – Benchmarking Decentralized Groups and Technical Distributors

  3. Value-based pricing and structural product mix improvements successfully drive margin expansion even during flat organic volume cycles.

Precedents of Cyclical Operating Leverage

1.
Systematic analysis: A comprehensive benchmark of Nordic serial acquirers reveals clear precedents across cycles. This study analyzes 6 peer companies using 35 relevant sources and 4 comparable past cases. The findings show that decentralized groups maintain highly flexible operational structures. This setup enables individual business units to protect margins during severe industrial down-cycles.
2.
Extreme volatility: Lifco's Demolition and Tools and Systems Solutions business areas posted deep organic net sales declines during the 2009 global financial crisis. However, Lifco managed to preserve overall group margins through its dental acquisitions.
3.

These measures successfully delivered SEK 80 million of annual savings compared to 2008 opex. The cost adjustments defended its operating margin at 5% for the year.
4.
Cyclical recovery: OEM International achieved a rapid margin rebound in the subsequent recovery phase. Implemented capacity modifications helped the group double its quarterly operating profits. Operating margins expanded from 6% to 9.7% as organic demand returned. This outcome highlights how streamlining activities support powerful operating leverage during up-cycles.
5.
Resilient acquisitions: Lifco defended its group-level EBITA margins during the 2020 downturn by leveraging its dental division. The dental business area sustained high profitability and offset industrial contractions. This strategic mix shows that product diversification buffers cyclical operating leverage effectively. Consequently, acquisitions of non-cyclical targets remain a key margin protection strategy.

Cost Adaptation and Execution Programs

6.
Strategic leadership: Indutrade was successfully managed from 2005 to 2017 by executive Johnny Alvarsson. The group consistently focused on strict income and cost control across its portfolio. Under his decentralized model, average operating margins remained stable at 11.4%. This long-term discipline shows that local accountability prevents administrative bloat during expansion.
7.
Active restructuring: Indutrade executed comprehensive restructuring programs from 2023 to 2025 to align costs. The group focused intensely on matching SG&A expenses with sluggish organic sales growth. Management prioritized headcount reductions and productivity tracking across lagging subsidiaries. This cost matching unlocked good operational leverage when organic volumes stabilized.
8.
Agility enhancement: Bufab aggressively expanded its initial cost-saving scheme during the 2020 pandemic. The program targeted run-rate savings of SEK 100 million. Management successfully reduced the workforce by 170 full-time equivalents. These voluntary reductions limited expensive restructuring charges and defended margins.
9.
Operational efficiency: Bufab demonstrated excellent cost flexibility due to its asset-light trading model. The group reduced its operating expenses to 16.3% of net sales. This reduction was achieved through discretionary spend cuts and short-time work. Consequently, Bufab maintained stable margins despite a massive 25% organic sales drop.
