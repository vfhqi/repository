Section 1 – Key question and brief

Section summary
  1. German undergrounding priority changes create long-term pipeline uncertainty but are highly resolvable and do not threaten NKT's near-term earnings trajectory.

Verbatim key question and overview

1.
Critical inquiry: The investigation addresses what has changed in 2026 in German law and grid planning on underground cabling against overhead lines for new high-voltage direct-current corridors, and what it means for NKT and underground HVDC demand, utilizing four distinct company, expert, and analyst sources.

Materiality to EPS trajectory

2.
Elevated materiality: This regulatory shift is highly material because NKT’s near-term earnings trajectory depends on high-margin onshore underground HVDC projects executed through the next 18 months to 3 years.
3.
Earnings trajectory: Jefferies estimated on 11 October 2026 that NKT's EPS will rise from €4.15 in 2026E to €5.49 in 2027E and €7.58 in 2028E as the factory expansions begin to commercialize.
4.

Boundaries and adjacent questions

5.
Defined boundaries: The geographic scope of this brief is restricted to German onshore transmission corridors, while adjacent questions examine emerging competition from Chinese suppliers targeting European subsea cable tenders.
6.
Capacity utilization: A critical adjacent question concerns whether the Cologne high-voltage factory expansion will face underutilized capacity after 2027 if German underground corridor demand structurally shrinks.

Sceptical counter-view

7.

Section 2 – German legislative and grid planning changes

Section summary
  1. Germany's legislative pivot back to overhead lines in mid-2026 removes long-term underground HVDC cabling priority but fully insulates current contracted backlogs.

Legislative milestones and Cabinet draft

1.
Cabinet approval: The German Federal Cabinet adopted the draft bill amending the Federal Requirements Plan Act on 29 April 2026.
2.
Underground priority shift: This draft legislation mandates that new long-distance DC transmission lines will in principle be built overhead from the early to mid-2030s onwards.
3.
Economic driver: Jefferies Research reported on 10 June 2026 that switching to overhead lines is estimated to save approximately €35bn by 2045.
4.
Bundestag reading: The draft bill was debated in its first reading in the German Bundestag on 11 June 2026.
5.
Bundestag approval: Jefferies Research reported on 14 July 2026 that the Bundestag approved the bill, removing the previous preference for underground cabling for new HVDC projects.
6.
Netzanschlusspaket passage: The Federal Cabinet subsequently approved the comprehensive grid connection package and Renewable Energy Sources Act amendment on 29 July 2026.

Exclusions and industry feedback

7.
Existing plan protection: Amprion's half-year report on 14 August 2026 clarified that the move away from undergrounding applies only to new projects and does not affect existing plans.
8.
Specific corridor impacts: Jefferies Research noted on 14 July 2026 that the statutory preference removal specifically affects the DC42 and DC42plus transmission corridors.
9.
Onshore project protection: Jefferies Research confirmed on 10 June 2026 that the draft law has no impact on ongoing projects like A-Nord and SuedLink.
10.
Permitting exceptions: Underground cable construction remains permissible under the draft law if overhead lines are not technically feasible or demonstrated to be more cost-effective.
11.
Grid expansion delays: A Friedrich Vorwerk presentation on 12 May 2026 highlighted that the overhead shift requires new planning approval procedures that cause supply chain delays.
12.
Other side of the story: State-level political pushback remains intense, as Schleswig-Holstein on 30 April 2026 and Lower Saxony on 4 May 2026 protested against the overhead mandate, which could trigger widespread local legal action and delay construction.

2.
Affected mileage: Jefferies Research highlighted on 10 June 2026 that the legislative preference for overhead lines directly impacts approximately 4,100 kilometres of power lines currently in early-stage planning.
3.
Specific corridor savings: TransnetBW reported in its August 2024 policy update that converting just three corridors–NordOstLink, NordWestLink 7, and SuedWestLink 8–to overhead lines reduces capital outlay by €20bn to €23bn.
4.
Grid fee reduction: TransnetBW's August 2024 calculations show that these three corridor conversions alone will lower annual consumer grid fees by €1.0 billion to €1.5 billion starting in 2030.
5.
Tender window contraction: Because high-voltage underground DC cable systems carry an estimated supply value of €3 million to €5 million per route kilometre, replacing 4,100 route kilometres removes €12bn to €20bn in orders from the 2027–2032 tendering landscape.

Section 6 – Competitive landscape and European policy shifts

Section summary
  1. European power cable leaders enjoy insulated backlogs, but upcoming European preference procurement rules and Chinese subsea targeting will reshape the competitive landscape.

Peer exposure to German grid changes

1.
Massive backlog protection: Prysmian maintains robust structural insulation against German regulatory shifts because its massive transmission segment order backlog reached €17 billion as of 30 June 2026, heavily supported by multi-billion euro long-term framework agreements with Amprion and 50Hertz.
2.
Onshore corridor dominance: Prysmian's specialized HVDC backlog of approximately €3.8 billion is heavily anchored in executing active German onshore corridor contracts, which are legally protected from the new 2026 overhead pylon transition rules.
3.
Localized production strategy: Sumitomo Electric strategically mitigated local political risks in Germany by acquiring a 90% stake in Mannheim-based Südkabel and investing €90 million to expand local 525 kV HVDC cable manufacturing facilities.
4.
Secured anchor contracts: Sumitomo Electric's Südkabel facility is fully utilized through 2032 executing more than €3 billion in contracted Amprion projects, including the Korridor B V49 route and the Rhein-Main-Link DC35 underground system.
5.
Korean market entry: South Korea's LS Cable has successfully established its presence in the German grid, manufacturing 525 kV DC cables for TenneT's BalWin4 and LanWin1 projects under a major May 2023 consortium agreement.
6.
Balanced portfolio execution: Morgan Stanley noted in January 2025 that while NKT and Prysmian still faced major capital outlays to reach full run-rate production by 2027, LS Cable and Sumitomo could collectively add 35% to total 2024 high-voltage capacity.
7.
Segmented market positioning: Jefferies analysts highlighted in September 2026 that NKT and Prysmian enjoy a strong leadership position in subsea HVDC, whereas Nexans is positioned to benefit more from medium-voltage renewable integration.

Emerging European procurement policy shifts

8.
Unified regulatory framework: The European Commission proposed a landmark Public Procurement Act on 9 September 2026 to consolidate three existing directives into a single directly applicable regulation, eliminating divergent national procurement rules across the bloc.
9.
Quality over price: This proposed procurement overhaul mandates that quality, sustainability, security, and resilience criteria must represent at least 30% of contract scoring, shifting tenders away from lowestprice competition.
10.
Explicit local preference: The draft regulation introduces a horizontal "European preference" framework that allows public buyers to reject bids with less than 50% EU-origin content or restrict participation to European operators.
11.
Exclusion of non-reciprocal bidders: Jefferies argued in September 2026 that because China is not a party to the WTO Government Procurement Agreement, Chinese manufacturers will face severe origin screens and potential exclusion from strategic tenders under the new rules.
12.
Mandatory sustainability requirements: The Dutch government is accelerating this policy shift by proposing mandatory environmental performance requirements for public infrastructure procurement, forcing suppliers to calculate and minimize carbon footprints.

Chinese competition and subsea tenders

13.
Increasing standard accessibility: A NKT research and development executive stated in September 2026 that Chinese and Korean players will increasingly compete in high-voltage markets as standards and qualification processes become more globally standardized.
14.
Subsea targeting constraints: A global energy utility expert observed in September 2026 that while Chinese manufacturers are securing market share in medium voltage, they remain excluded from major highvoltage transmission because of system-level qualification barriers.
15.
Inherent technology barriers: A Prysmian strategy director noted in August 2026 that Chinese competition is essentially absent from European overhead high-voltage lines above 100 kV, preserving a highly profitable niche for European players.
16.
Escalating trade tensions: Jefferies observed in June 2026 that Chinese manufacturers are gaining European market share at a record pace, prompting the European Union to accelerate anti-dumping and anti-subsidy investigations to protect local industrial supply chains.
17.
Other side of the story: If the European Parliament waters down the September 2026 Public Procurement Act during 2027 negotiations, low-cost Chinese suppliers may successfully bypass local origin content rules and disrupt pricing across the European subsea market.
