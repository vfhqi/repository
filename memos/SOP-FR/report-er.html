Section summary

Sopra Steria operates as a major European digital services group, maintaining a workforce of 51,275 employees across nearly 30 countries as of late 2025. The group's geographic footprint is anchored in France, which accounts for 43% of group revenue, followed by the United Kingdom at 16%, Scandinavia at 10%, Benelux at 9%, Germany at 7%, and other European territories making up the remaining balance. Through this diversified footprint, the company establishes itself as a credible alternative to global tech giants across key European digital transformation markets

The company's operational model is structured around five primary service lines, led by Systems Integration which commands 60% of total group revenue. Digital Platform Services contributes 13% of sales, while Business Process Services represents 12%, high-value Consulting through Sopra Steria Next accounts for 9%, and specialized Solutions make up 6% of the mix. This structural allocation enables the group to deliver comprehensive, end-to-end technological capabilities while shifting towards high-value digital advisory services over time

Sopra Steria targets critical industries, with public and highly regulated markets representing over 70% of its business exposure. Specifically, the Public Sector is the single largest vertical at 26% of revenue, followed by Financial Services at 21%, and Defence, Space, Security & Aeronautics at 23% combined. This defensive, highly critical vertical mix provides fundamental demand stability during periods of broader macroeconomic uncertainty, although it exposes the group to local policy shifts and public procurement cycles
4.
Historical top-line performance has followed a highly cyclical trajectory, starting with a strong FY23 where revenue grew 13.8% to €5.805 billion, representing 6.6% organic growth. However, the spending environment softened in FY24, causing revenue to contract slightly by 0.5% on both a reported and organic basis to €5.776.8 million. This top-line pressure intensified into FY25, where revenue decreased 2.2% to €5,648.0 million, though a late organic growth rebound of 1.8% in the fourth quarter pointed to stabilizing demand conditions
5.
Despite revenue fluctuations, the group successfully maintained robust profitability, with its operating margin on business activity holding close to mid-term targets. The operating margin on business activity expanded from 9.4% in FY23 to 9.8% in FY24, driven by defensive portfolio management and structural costout programs. Although margins slipped slightly to 9.5% in FY25, the delivery landed within the company's guided range of 9.3% to 9.8%, proving the resilience of the group's delivery model in a challenging environment
6.

In FY25, free cash flow normalized to €340.9 million, representing a healthy 6.0% of revenue and aligning with the guided range of 5% to 7%. This strong cash conversion supported aggressive balance sheet deleveraging, allowing the group to reduce its net financial debt by 35.4% year-on-year to €246.7 million
