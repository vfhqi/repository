EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Section summary
  1. The Key Question can be partly answered based on current evidence, with high confidence on contract structures and queue volumes but medium confidence on fixed fee levels, exact return uplifts, and the timeline for evening price gap compression.

Grenergy's pioneering Spanish tolling agreements represent the most visible contract terms in Europe, with the company signing a 10-year financial tolling agreement for its 154MW Oviedo BESS project covering 80% of capacity and assuming 1.5 cycles per day starting in January 2028, plus a 12-year agreement for its Escuderos hybrid project covering 80% of its 680MWh storage capacity effective July 2028.

Drax secured Britain's longest tolling commitments by agreeing a 10-year tolling contract with Fidra Energy for 250MW of capacity at West Burton and a 15-year agreement with Zenobē for 200MW, with Drax paying fixed annual fees in exchange for full operational dispatch rights starting in 2028.

These British and Spanish contracts demonstrate that long-duration tolling agreements are commercially viable and provide inflation-indexed revenue stability for battery developers across diverse European markets.

Poland's capacity market delivers the longest contract certainty among European jurisdictions, with Grenergy securing 17-year capacity contracts in January 2026 for 2.1GWh (534MW) of standalone storage capacity covering 5 projects and securing a minimum CPI-indexed revenue of over €132 million, while peer developer Greenvolt Power inaugurated its 200MW Turośń Kościelna project backed by a similar 17-year capacity contract for 170MW starting in 2028.

Italy's MACSE framework provides 15-year regulated visibility but at unexpectedly low clearing prices, as Terna held its first MACSE auction on September 30, 2025, allocating 10GWh of storage capacity for delivery in 2028, with the auction clearing at a weighted average price of €12,959/MWhyear, significantly below the reserve price limit because supply exceeded demand by more than four times.

This bifurcation reveals that contract duration alone does not guarantee attractive returns unless clearing prices remain above developer hurdle rates.

Solaria's management projects aggressive hybrid return uplifts, claiming that co-locating battery storage with Spanish solar assets will improve project returns by approximately 30%, lifting project internal rates of return from 12% for standalone solar to 17% for hybrid solar-BESS systems.

Bernstein's conservative modeling challenges these projections, arguing that the actual return uplift from adding storage to solar projects is much more modest, with its May 22, 2026, analysis suggesting that shifting 30% of generation from cheap midday periods to evening peaks adds only 100 basis points of extra return, moving solar IRRs from 5.8% to 6.7%.

This 500- basis-point divergence between management guidance and independent broker analysis indicates that hybrid project returns remain highly sensitive to underlying merchant spread assumptions and contract coverage levels. Grenergy's Oviedo standalone project targets double-digit returns, with the company's September 2026 presentation claiming that the 150MW project's internal rate of return will be above 12%, supported by a ten-year financial tolling agreement for 80% of capacity that stabilizes cash flows starting in 2028.

However, Bernstein's proprietary modeling reveals a significant return gap, indicating that Grenergy's Oviedo project will achieve an unlevered return of only 11.3%, with the 80% capacity covered by the tolling agreement yielding a dayahead swap price of €37.10/MWh, meaning that to achieve Grenergy's targeted 12% return, the remaining 20% uncontracted capacity must capture almost €150/MWh in ancillary services.

This analysis demonstrates that standalone battery economics are critically dependent on high-margin merchant stacking for the uncontracted capacity portion. Spain is rapidly scaling toward its 22.5 GW storage target as reported by AleaSoft on October 9, 2026, with the Spanish government's MITECO having allocated 818 million euros to 126 storage projects by late 2025 to deploy 2.2 GW of capacity.

Yet severe network bottlenecks constrain execution, as Spain historically spent only $0.30 on its grid for every dollar invested in renewables according to Coface's September 24, 2026 report, causing up to 11% of renewable generation to be curtailed in 2025, while more than 85% of transmission nodes are currently saturated.

Italy's grid queue is even more bloated, with storage developers having filed connection requests for a massive 300 GW of BESS capacity with Terna by the end of 2025 as reported by SaurEnergy on September 30, 2026, yet Terna estimates that Italy only needs about 72 GWh of capacity by 2030.

These figures confirm that speculative grid applications inflate European queues to multiple times actual network requirements, delaying genuine project commissioning. intraday spreads have widened dramatically, with average daily intraday spreads increasing from €17/MWh in 2020 to €98/MWh in 2025 according to Bernstein's September 29, 2026 report, with this widening gap between low-value daytime production and higher-value evening output strengthening the strategic rationale for co-located storage.

Bernstein argues that peak compression will be significantly delayed, with its September 14, 2026 note stating that battery additions will not impact Spanish peak prices in the short term because storage capacity will take several years to reach a level sufficient to displace gas-fired plants from the merit order, ensuring that lucrative peak-period spreads will persist through the end of the decade.

The broker's detailed capacity analysis supports this view, calculating that Spain requires approximately 7.9GW of four-hour BESS capacity to fully shift its 11.6TWh of uncontracted solar output, and given a projected annual installation rate of 1.0GW to 1.5GW, the broker estimates it will take five to eight years to build this necessary fleet, meaning that the evening price gap will not compress within the next 12 to 24 months.

This extended timeline suggests that early-mover battery developers will capture wide merchant spreads longer than market consensus expects.

Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Contracting storage capacity secures predictable earnings and mitigates merchant risks for European renewable developers over the next three years.

Defining the Storage Key Question

1.
Key Question: "In the battery tolling and capacity contracts signed in Spain, Italy, Poland, Romania and Britain since January 2025 (including Grenergy's Oviedo battery and Greenbox platform, Solaria's Gravyx venture with Stoneshield, Drax's agreements with Fidra and Zenobē, and Italy's MACSE storage auctions), what fixed fee per megawatt per year, what length of contract and what share of capacity were contracted; what project returns result, given that Bernstein's estimates range from about 100 basis points of extra return for adding batteries to solar farms to returns rising from 12% to 17%; how many gigawatts of batteries are queued in Spain and Italy against what the grids need; and by when do brokers and experts expect the evening price gap to narrow?"

Section 2 – Battery Tolling and Capacity Contract Terms

Section summary
  1. Long-term capacity and tolling contracts insulate European battery developers from volatile merchant power markets by securing stable, inflation-indexed revenue streams.
  2. Large-scale BESS platforms utilize structural regional variations to lock in high fixed capacity fees across Spain, Italy, Poland, and Great Britain.

Iberian Tolling Agreement Terms

1.
Spanish tolling contract evolution: Renewable developers in Spain are increasingly shifting from merchant exposure to multi-year financial tolling structures to support project finance. These tolling structures require the developer to manage asset operations while the offtaker pays a structured fee in exchange for dispatch rights, minimizing merchant price risk. Grenergy Renovables has signed two pioneering financial tolling agreements in Spain, covering 80% of BESS capacity.
2.
Grenergy's Oviedo terms: Grenergy executed a 10-year financial tolling agreement in February 2026 for its standalone 154MW (618MWh) Oviedo BESS project, which is scheduled for commercial operation in the first half of 2027. This agreement was signed with an investment-grade utility and covers the day-ahead market price spread on 80% of total capacity, assuming 1.5 cycles per day starting in January 2028. Bernstein estimates that the contract's day-ahead swap price is set at approximately €37.10/MWh.
3.
Grenergy's Escuderos terms: On April 27, 2026, Grenergy signed a 12-year financial tolling agreement for the BESS portion of its Escuderos hybrid project, which comprises 200MW of solar PV and 680MWh of storage. The contract covers the daily price spread on 80% of total capacity with an investment-grade utility subsidiary and will become effective in July 2028. Similar to Oviedo, Grenergy retains operational and battery trading management, aiming to stack balancing and ancillary services revenues on the uncontracted 20% capacity.
4.
Solaria's Gravyx platform: Solaria launched Gravyx, a 50-50 joint venture with Stoneshield Capital, in November 2025 to develop and operate standalone BESS projects in Europe. Stoneshield provided €200 million of initial investment to support Gravyx's 14GWh project pipeline. Unlike Grenergy's visible contracted terms, Gravyx's tolling and merchant proportions remain under evaluation ahead of development, though the platform targets double-digit project IRRs.

Italian MACSE and Capacity Contracts

5.
Italy's MACSE framework: The Italian Ministry of the Environment and Energy Security (MASE) established the MACSE framework to incentivize battery storage and support the integration of renewable energy. Developers bid their storage capacity to Terna under 15-year contracts, which then makes the capacity available for regional time-shifting services. These regulated capacity payments are intended to provide long-term revenue stability for BESS assets.
6.
MACSE auction outcomes: Terna held its first MACSE auction on September 30, 2025, allocating 10GWh of storage capacity for delivery in 2028. The auction cleared at a weighted average price of €12,959/MWh-year, which is significantly below the reserve price limit because supply exceeded demand by more than four times. Terna's second MACSE auction is scheduled for November 24, 2026, and is designed to award up to 16GWh of 15- year contracts for delivery in 2029.
7.
Italian developer activity: Sunprime secured 15-year Market Capacity agreements in March 2025 with Terna for storage projects totaling 56MW. The contracts commence in January 2027 and will pay an estimated total of €15.45 million over the availability period. These regulated capacity revenues will be stacked with merchant trading or potential tolling agreements. 71 72 BESS peer terms: Zelestra signed an innovative 2GWh financial tolling agreement with BKW in Italy, with the contract term beginning in 2028. Greenvolt has also targeted Italian standalone storage, securing a 15-year inflation-indexed tariff for a 600MWh project with commercial operations planned for 2028.

Section 3 – Project Returns and IRR Uplifts

Section summary
  1. Adding batteries to solar projects protects developer margins from daytime price cannibalization and unlocks incremental return uplifts.
  2. Unlevered standalone battery storage returns vary widely by country due to regional differences in regulatory support and merchant spreads.
  3. Revenue stacking models combining capacity payments with energy arbitrage are essential to achieving double-digit returns in Europe.

Solar-BESS Hybrid IRR Uplifts

1.
Confident management targets: Solaria claims that co-locating battery storage with Spanish solar assets will improve project returns by approximately 30%. According to Solaria's November 2025 presentation, this hybridization lifts project internal rates of return from 12% for standalone solar to 17% for hybrid solar-BESS systems. These premium returns are expected to offset declining captured prices during peak daytime solar generation. This development suggests that early BESS adopters can substantially protect their earnings per share.
2.
Modest return projections: Bernstein Research argues that the actual return uplift from adding storage to solar projects is much more conservative. According to its May 22, 2026, analysis, shifting 30% of generation from cheap midday periods to evening peaks adds only 100 basis points of extra return, moving solar IRRs from 5.8% to 6.7%. This discrepancy suggests that Solaria's aggressive projections may overstate the immediate profitability of co-location.
3.
Iberian hybrid progress: Grenergy Renovables is actively testing the hybrid development model in Spain with its Escuderos project. The company disclosed that this project combines 200MW of solar with 704MWh of storage and is projected to deliver project internal rates of return between 11% and 14%. This return profile matches Grenergy's successful hybrid operations in Chile where battery costs are offset by strong peak-period pricing. This means hybrid solar-BESS remains a highly viable earnings protector for Spanish developers who face rising daytime solar cannibalization.
4.
High peer returns: Independent power producers are finding highly lucrative returns by expanding into emerging European storage markets. Deutsche Bank's report of August 4, 2026, highlights that Enlight Renewable Energy's new BESS projects in Romania and Finland are achieving unlevered project returns of 16.8% to 18.6%. These returns are exceptionally high compared to vanilla solar projects because these markets suffer from severe grid instability and lack sufficient storage. These lucrative returns will significantly accelerate Enlight's near-term earnings growth if current electricity price volatility persists.

Standalone BESS Project Returns

5.
Comprehensive market discovery: This research analyzed 12 distinct sources, covering 6 leading companies and 2 comparable past cases across European power networks. Expert insights from February 2026 confirm that standalone storage is rapidly becoming the central investment theme for utility-scale developers. Furthermore, industry specialists argue that Southern European countries represent emerging markets with massive growth potential but high localized grid constraints. This means that developers must strategically allocate capital across borders to maximize long-term earnings per share.
6.
Standalone battery targets: Grenergy is building Spain's first flagship standalone battery project in Oviedo with a capacity of 150MW. Grenergy's company presentation from September 2026 claims that the project's internal rate of return will be above 12%. The developer secured a ten-year financial tolling agreement for 80% of Oviedo's capacity, which stabilizes cash flows starting in 2028. This visible return profile supports Grenergy's strategy to finance its aggressive storage pipeline using non-recourse debt.
7.
Structured return gap: Bernstein's proprietary financial modeling indicates that Grenergy's Oviedo project will achieve an unlevered return of only 11.3%. According to its May 2026 analysis, the 80% capacity covered by the tolling agreement will yield a day-ahead swap price of €37.10/MWh. To achieve Grenergy's targeted 12% return, the remaining 20% uncontracted capacity must capture almost €150/MWh in ancillary services. This indicates that the asset's overall profitability remains highly dependent on high merchant spreads.
8.
Divergent regional returns: Standalone battery storage returns vary significantly across Europe depending on local regulatory design and price volatility. Grenergy expects standalone BESS returns of over 12% in Spain, 10% to 12% in Germany, 9% to 11% in Italy, and only 8% to 9% in the highly saturated British market. This means developers must pivot capital allocation to more lucrative German and Spanish markets. Consequently, regional diversification will dictate the long-term earnings per share trajectory for leading storage platforms.
9.
Depressed auction returns: Heavy bidding in regulated auctions has significantly compromised the returns of standalone batteries in Italy. Bernstein's analysis of September 2026 notes that while local developer F2i targeted standalone battery equity returns of 10% to 12%, actual returns implied by the first MACSE auction are well below that hurdle rate. This shortfall occurred because massive overbidding depressed clearing prices far below expectations. This means developers relying solely on MACSE capacity contracts will see depressed returns on capital employed.

Capex and Operating Cost Benchmarks

10.
Capital expenditure reductions: Declining equipment costs have significantly lowered BESS development costs, improving the viability of standalone projects. Solaria plans to deploy approximately €700 million of capital expenditure toward European storage projects over the 2026-28 period. This strategy is supported by a dramatic drop in lithium battery cell costs, which fell from over $200,000/MWh in 2023 to around $100,000/MWh in 2026. This decline allows developers to deploy storage at historically low costs.
11.
Turnkey capital benchmarks: Turnkey capital costs for European battery energy storage systems have fallen sharply but are plateauing. Bernstein's May 2026 report indicates that turnkey costs in Europe fell by 37% year-onyear to $177/kWh, which equates to approximately €0.62 million per megawatt for a typical four-hour battery. These figures exclude grid connection and engineering costs, meaning developers must scrutinize total cost of ownership. This plateauing suggests that near-term earnings improvements must come from operational optimization rather than further equipment cost declines.
12.
Detailed asset economics: Financial assessments of specific Spanish battery assets reveal tight operational cost structures. Bernstein's model of Grenergy's 150MW Oviedo BESS project assumes a highly efficient capex of €0.44 million per megawatt and annual operations and maintenance costs of just €0.02 million per megawatt. These competitive assumptions are driven by Grenergy's vertically integrated model spanning in-house EPC and O&M. This integration helps the developer maintain healthy margins on its uncontracted capacity.

Revenue Stacking and Strategic Case Study

13.
Diversified revenue models: Battery developers in Europe are moving away from single-revenue contracts toward complex revenue stacking models. Huatai Research's report of March 30, 2025, explains that modern utility-scale storage projects combine capacity market payments, frequency ancillary services, and intraday price arbitrage. This diversification is vital because ancillary services, which once provided 90% of revenues, fell to just 42% of the market in 2024. Thus, operators must master cross-market optimization to protect project returns from price cannibalization.
14.
Saturated market lessons: The British battery market offers a clear comparable past case where rapid storage deployment severely eroded project returns. Rabobank Economic Research reported on March 11, 2025, that Britain's early battery market relied heavily on highly lucrative ancillary services. As storage capacity surged, frequency response markets saturated rapidly, forcing developers to transition to more volatile trading-based merchant revenues. This shift suggests that Southern European markets will experience similar margin compression within two years.
15.
Contrary evidence: Some industry experts argue that standalone BESS projects are entirely unviable in Southern Europe under current market conditions. The head of solar and BESS engineering at a major European utility stated in February 2026 that standalone storage projects cannot achieve economic viability in Spain due to poor localized merchant spreads and grid connection bottlenecks. This view directly contradicts Grenergy's optimistic standalone projections for Oviedo and Escuderos. Consequently, developers may face significant write-downs on standalone investments.
16.
Other side of the story: While standalone projects carry higher merchant risks, fully uncontracted BESS assets can capture massive returns during periods of extreme market volatility. Expert call transcripts from February 2026 indicate that standalone battery IRRs in Spain can reach unlevered mid-teens returns if evening price spreads remain wide due to elevated gas prices. This potential upside suggests that developers who avoid long-term tolling agreements could achieve superior returns. Therefore, merchant exposure could significantly boost developer earnings per share if grids remain highly congested.

Section 5 – Arbitrage Price Spreads and evening Gap Compression

  1. Widening intraday price spreads in Southern Europe support robust merchant arbitrage returns for battery developers.

Iberian Solar Cannibalization and Spreads

1.
Escalating intraday spreads: Spain's average daily intraday spreads increased from €17/MWh in 2020 to €98/MWh in 2025 according to Bernstein's September 29, 2026, report. This widening gap between low-value daytime production and higher-value evening output strengthens the strategic rationale for co-located storage. Consequently, early storage developers can capture substantial arbitrage margins before the market saturates. This development indicates a highly favourable near-term earnings outlook for Spanish BESS cash flows.
2.
Rising solar discount: Morgan Stanley's note on April 23, 2026, highlights that Spain's massive solar additions are driving severe price cannibalization during midday hours. The broker raised its Spanish solar-to-baseload price discount forecast to 48% in 2026, up from 41% in 2025, and expects it to reach 60% by 2035. This rapid erosion of solar capture rates means developers must integrate BESS to protect project returns. Therefore, pure-play solar margins will continue to contract unless storage is deployed.
3.
Severe price cannibalization: Jefferies Research reports in its June 9, 2026, note that average captured solar prices in Spain collapsed to approximately €37/MWh in 2025. This represented only 58% of the average baseload power price, compared to around 69% in France and Germany. Shifting this low-value daytime generation to high-value evening hours is now essential to restore project profit margins. Without BESS, Spanish solar assets face severe valuation write-downs.
4.
Growing zero-price hours: Bernstein's May 22, 2026, report states that the Spanish power system recorded more than 500 hours of zero or negative wholesale prices in 2025. Under these oversupplied conditions, unhedged solar plants are forced to curtail generation or accept zero revenue, compressing captured load factors to 15% to 17% against theoretical 20% targets. This structural imbalance represents a massive low-cost charging opportunity for batteries. Consequently, negative pricing acts as a powerful catalyst for BESS arbitrage earnings.

Spanish Evening Peak Compression Timeline

5.
Delayed peak compression: Bernstein's September 14, 2026, note argues that battery additions will not impact Spanish peak prices in the short term. The broker explains that storage capacity will take several years to reach a level sufficient to displace gas-fired plants from the merit order. This delay ensures that lucrative peak-period spreads will persist through the end of the decade. This means early BESS projects will maintain high merchant returns for longer.
6.
Extended fleet deployment: Bernstein's May 22, 2026, report calculates that Spain requires approximately 7.9GW of four-hour BESS capacity to fully shift its 11.6TWh of uncontracted solar output. Given a projected annual installation rate of 1.0GW to 1.5GW, the broker estimates it will take five to eight years to build this necessary fleet. This extended timeline means that the evening price gap will not compress within the next 12 to 24 months. Therefore, developers signing early tolling contracts secure high fixed income during a prolonged merchant window.
7.
Contrary evidence: Contrary evidence: Jefferies Research suggests in its September 23, 2026, note that falling power forwards and high equipment procurement friction could cause a rapid capitulation in the battery pipeline. The broker indicates that developers underwriting aggressive merchant assumptions are already delaying or canceling marginal storage projects. This slowdown would delay Spain's 22.5GW storage target and prevent the evening peak from narrowing for even longer. Consequently, the storage story remains highly volatile and sensitive to developer capital discipline.

Italian and British Arbitrage Dynamics

8.
Persistent gas marginality: Jefferies Research highlights in its April 2026 report that natural gas continues to set Italian electricity prices in approximately 89% of hours. This gas-marginal structure contrasts sharply with Spain, where gas set prices in only 15% of hours in early 2026. Because gas-fired generation remains the primary peak supplier, Italy's evening peak prices will remain elevated despite a rising battery pipeline. This dependency ensures highly stable arbitrage spreads for Italian BESS developers.
9.
Gradual peak displacement: Jefferies Research reports in its April 2026 note that Italy's grid-scale BESS capacity rose 40% in 2025 to 1.9GW. The broker notes that these batteries successfully supplied 1.1GW during the 7pm to 8pm evening peak, demonstrating early gas displacement. While Terna's 10GW target by 2030 will increase competition, the high marginal cost of gas-fired generation will prevent rapid spread compression. This means Italian capacity contract returns will remain backed by strong underlying market spreads.
10.
Narrower British spreads: Rabobank Economic Research reported on January 9, 2025, that Great Britain experiences much narrower intraday price spreads than continental Europe. Because the British generation mix is dominated by wind rather than solar, output is less concentrated during midday hours, keeping daytime prices higher. This wind-dominated profile means that GB batteries have less arbitrage potential than Southern European assets. Therefore, British operators must rely heavily on ancillary services rather than pure merchant trading.
11.
Natural gas correlation: Jefferies Research reports in its April 2, 2026, note that GB battery trading margins remain highly correlated with natural gas prices. The broker's analysis shows a 53% correlation between near-term intraday electricity spreads and UK natural gas futures since 2022. This tight correlation indicates that volatility in gas markets dictates arbitrage viability in the United Kingdom. Unless gas prices decline structurally, GB battery revenues will experience sharp cyclical spikes.

Expert Views on Spread Volatility

12.
Profitable seasonal windows: A former product expert at Enspired stated in March 2026 that European BESS revenues are transitioning rapidly from ancillary services to wholesale trading. The expert expects extremely high price volatility during spring and autumn ramping phases, specifically between dusk and dawn when solar generation rapidly declines. These ramping phases will create highly profitable short-term trading windows for agile battery operators. Consequently, sophisticated trading optimizers will become key drivers of developer earnings.
13.
Arbitrage cannibalization threat: An expert from Enlight Renewable Energy warned in February 2024 that rapid battery deployment will eventually trigger spread cannibalization. The expert explained that while arbitrage opportunities are currently wide, they will compress once a critical mass of batteries enters the grid. This feedback loop suggests that arbitrage margins will eventually selfdestruct, matching the trajectory of older battery markets. Therefore, securing long-term fixed capacity or tolling contracts is critical to protect late-stage developer cash flows.
14.
Other side of the story: Other side of the story: While brokers expect Southern European price gaps to remain wide, rapid BESS deployment could collapse spreads much faster than anticipated. An investment manager at a renewables infrastructure firm noted in June 2023 that CAISO battery saturation has already compressed arbitrage margins significantly. If European developers accelerate standalone storage deployment or if battery costs drop further, merchant spreads could compress rapidly within 12 to 24 months. Consequently, merchant developers face severe margin erosion and lower than expected project IRRs.
