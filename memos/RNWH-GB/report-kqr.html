Section 2 – Rail and Water Infrastructure Cycles

Section summary

Weather reactive rail growth

1.
Weather-driven rail maintenance: The group's rail segment benefited from elevated reactive maintenance spend during H1 FY26 because Network Rail prioritized emergency works over major renewals. As reported in Renew's H1 FY26 interim report on 12 May 2026, these weather-driven interventions allowed subsidiaries AmcoGiffen and QTS to deliver record levels of emergency response. This operational shift helped the group achieve H1 FY26 organic growth of 2.2% but left high-volume renewal backlogs deferred, which will ultimately dictate the mix of engineering segment margins and EPS. Challenging comparative setup: The volatile nature of weather-related rail works creates a demanding comparison for H1 FY27 organic growth. Deutsche Bank's research note of 12 May 2026 highlights that the exceptional winter reactive spend in H1 FY26 is unlikely to repeat with the same intensity. Consequently, unless severe weather events occur, Renew's rail division could see organic growth moderate in H1 FY27, which means analysts must re-evaluate short-term EPS growth trajectories to reflect normalized maintenance demand. Contrary evidence: Ongoing delay and deferment of rail renewals under CP7 have persistently offset maintenance gains, as reported by Deutsche Bank on 24 January 2025. This shows that structural challenges in Network Rail's procurement are constraining overall segment volumes regardless of temporary weather boosts. Unless these funding bottlenecks are resolved, the rail business is unlikely to sustain the group's historical organic growth of 9% over the medium term.

Competitive peer landscape: The competitive environment in the water maintenance market remains intense, with peers expanding their technical capabilities through strategic acquisitions. Sector-wide construction transition: The transition from early design work to asset construction is a sector-wide driver of earnings acceleration. As reported in Costain's H1 FY26 interim results on 13 August 2026, the ramp-up of AMP8 water and road infrastructure projects into construction will support sustained medium-term revenue growth from FY27. This industry momentum suggests that the back-loaded construction phase is a highly predictable driver of infrastructure EPS across the entire UK contracting sector.
