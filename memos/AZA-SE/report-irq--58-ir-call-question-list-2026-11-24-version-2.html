Terms used throughout. EPS: earnings per share. FY2026, FY2027 and FY2028: the financial years to 31 December. Basis point: one hundredth of a percentage point. What the call should settle, and why

The case in one line: a 40%-return-on-equity franchise whose asset engine is at its strongest since 2021 but whose earnings conversion has reset lower, so that 2027 is a squeeze year before the rate rise, a loosening leverage ratio and Denmark can turn the skew from the spring of 2027. Six judgements decide whether to add, hold or cut.

  1. Judgement 1, the fourth quarter against the market. The market expects fourth-quarter operating income of SEK 1,313m (Jefferies, 16-Nov-2026); we have about SEK 1,290m, costs about SEK 430m and EPS about SEK 4.55 against an implied consensus near SEK 4.80. October was soft (customers 14,100, net savings down 22% on September). If November is no better, the cut the memo expects at the January compilation (2% to 4% on FY2027) arrives on time; if trading recovered, the January print is a non-event and the spring upgrade comes without a preceding cut.
  2. Judgement 2, how much of the yield compression is structural. Brokerage yield 10.3 basis points (from 11.0) with Private Banking and Pro at 30% of trading; fund margin 22.2 (from 22.9); income per customer SEK 2,170 (down 6% in a quarter). Cyclical: FY2027 EPS about SEK 21.2 (+4.8%); fully structural at the 2023 to 2025 pace: about SEK 19.4 ((4.2%)), DNB Carnegie's number.
  3. Judgement 3, trading velocity. Brokerage plus currency is SEK 2,160m in our 2027 base, 39% of revenue. First-half 2026 velocity back: +SEK 1.5 of EPS (+7.5%; DNB Carnegie says +17%); a 2022-type year: EPS about SEK 16.2 ((20%)) and a share price near SEK 300.
  4. Judgement 4, deposits after the rise to 2.00%. Our base: 60% to 70% pass-through, about SEK 160m a year; Avanza's cumulative beta in the 2022 to 2023 rises was 36%; deposits are 8.9% of savings capital. A lower pass-through or a second rise by February 2027 is +SEK 100m (+2.7% of EPS) or more; deposits falling to 8% costs SEK 50m to 100m.
  5. Judgement 5, the 2027 cost guide on 22-Jan-2027. Our central case is 7% to 8% for Sweden plus Denmark stepping from SEK 40m to about SEK 130m; group costs SEK 1,780m against DNB Carnegie's SEK 1,772m. Each point of Swedish growth is SEK 16m (SEK 0.09 of EPS); the signal matters more than the money: above 8.5% supports the platform-capacity reading.
  6. Judgement 6, the payout and capital. Leverage ratio 4.8% against 3.5%; payout policy 70% or 75% (our files differ); a 10% buyback authorisation unused. A payout above 78% or a first buyback is upside the estimates do not carry; a payout at 70% would put our SEK 14.3 and the pre-results consensus of SEK 14.52 too high.
