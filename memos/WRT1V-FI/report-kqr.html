Section 2 – Market structure and data center power demand 13 findings

Section summary
  1. Extreme grid connection queues of three to seven years are driving hyperscalers toward behind- the-meter reciprocating engines.
  2. Capacity is severely constrained across all major suppliers with backlogs extending well into 2028 and 2030.
  3. Competitors like Caterpillar and Cummins are rapidly expanding capacity, introducing mid-term pricing and market share risks for Wartsila.

Grid queue bypass and BTM demand

1.
Severe grid delays: On-site gas-fueled reciprocating engines are bypassing utility three to seven year grid queues in key North American and European data center markets
2.
Expanding BTM footprint: The total available capacity for behind-the-meter data center solutions is projected to reach 69GW of global capacity by 2030, highlighting the scale of off-grid deployment
3.
Hyperscale prime preference: Large-scale AI data center builders are deploying medium-speed reciprocating engines to secure immediate baseload and prime power supply rather than wait for traditional grid reinforcement
4.
Water efficiency advantages: Closed-loop engine cooling systems provide Wartsila with a negligible water consumption edge over gas turbines in arid regions like Texas
5.
Temporary bridge perception: Some hyperscalers evaluate on-site reciprocating engines as temporary bridge power solutions until permanent grid connections are established
6.
Contradictory turbine preference: While reciprocating engines offer faster time-to-power, some developers prefer larger aeroderivative gas turbines as individual data center facility scale exceeds 80MW
