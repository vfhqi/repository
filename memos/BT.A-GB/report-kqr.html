Pension Deficit Restructuring

1.
Deficit reduction: BT Group successfully concluded its triennial funding valuation as of June 2026 showing an improved deficit of £2.7bn, according to BT Group's press release on 5 November 2026. This reduction is expected to improve longterm earnings per share, as estimated by Morgan Stanley on 11 November 2026, by greater corporate and dividend flexibility.
2.
Scheduled maturities: BT Group outlines five zero-coupon bonds with £590m maturing annually in April of 2027, 2028, and 2029, per BT Group's investor presentation on 5 November 2026. A subsequent maturity of £490m falls in April 2030, followed by a final par value maturity of £420m in September 2033, as described in the same presentation. These maturities closely match the previous contribution timetable to protect the pension scheme's funding trajectory, according to BT Group's press release on 5 November 2026. This structure means that the ultimate cash requirements on BT Group remain unchanged, as noted by Deutsche Bank Research on 5 November 2026.

Openreach Line Impact

TalkTalk Deal Structure

Inorganic transaction: BT Group acquired TalkTalk Telecommunications and PlatformX Communications out of administration on October 5, 2026, to protect continuity of service. The company executed the deal on a debtfree basis to avoid systemic risks. This transaction eliminates over £1.0bn of debt from the struggling broadband operator's balance sheet.
2.
Cash outflow: BT Group expects a cash flow impact of approximately £400m during fiscal year 2027 to stabilize the acquired businesses. This total contains a £60m operating loss and £100m of unpaid wholesale fees owed to Openreach. Consequently, the group was forced to exclude these acquisition costs from its normalized free cash flow guidance of £2.0bn.
3.
Earnings progression: BT Group anticipates that the TalkTalk acquisition will eventually become value accretive after initial integration costs are digested. Sell-side analysis from Morgan Stanley on 11 November 2026 notes that the transaction eliminates bad debt risks. This support should help stabilize long-term earnings per share by realizing substantial overhead synergies over time.
4.
Contract cancellations: BT Group's rapid absorption of TalkTalk's infrastructure has directly disrupted legacy industry suppliers. For example, Netgem suffered a non-recurring depreciation of EUR 0.65m due to the sudden contract terminations. This supplier disruption highlights how BT Group's in-housing of wholesale services negatively impacts smaller market players.

CMA Review and PlatformX

The competitor argued in its letter to the Secretary of State that separate ownership of the retail and wholesale units was workable. If accepted, a forced retail sale would prevent BT from expanding its share of the consumer market.

Divestment debate: BT Group face demands from independent competitors to separate and divest the wholesale unit PlatformX. Sell-side commentary suggests that retaining both divisions under common ownership is highly anti-competitive. This structural debate means the company may be forced to sell PlatformX to clear regulatory hurdles.
