Executive Summary -- We are looking for a clear, conviction-carrying statement of the investment thesis, key ratings, and actionable conclusion; avoiding vague or overly hedged language.
Judgement summary
  • Overall verdict. C OEM International is a genuinely high-quality distributor whose change thesis has been confirmed in three consecutive prints, and whose share price has already captured it
  • Valuation upside is the binding objection. D At SEK 167.6 the shares sit above RedEye's own base case of SEK 165, and a scenario-weighted fair value lands slightly below the live price
  • The multiple is now the sector's highest. RedEye puts OEM on 25.9x 2026 enterprise-value-to-EBITA, above Addtech at 24.1x and Lagercrantz at 24.2x, and 21% to 47% above its own averages
  • Industry structure is the weakest building block. D The top five European technical traders hold less than 15% combined share, so OEM is neither first nor second in a structurally unconcentrated market
  • Governance alignment is thin. Six of seven directors own zero direct Class B shares, variable pay was lifted to 200% of fixed salary, and no clawback provision is disclosed
  • The operator is leaving mid-thesis. Jörgen Zahlin, chief executive since 2000, retires during 2026 at age 62, and his successor carries no public-company chief-executive record
  • Working capital is a persistent drag. Net working capital ran at 25.1% of sales in 2025 and 92 days, against a peer band of 65 to 78 days in the Business Foundations report
  • Earnings base quality carries three amber flags. Earn-out liabilities collapsed to SEK 9.8m from SEK 46.5m, receivables over 181 days rose to SEK 1.9m, and inventory rebuilt to SEK 1,211m
  • Foreign exchange is unhedged by policy. OEM holds zero financial derivative instruments, imports 80% of goods in foreign currency, and covers only 68% of sales with currency clauses
  • The good news is narrow but real. Organic growth moved 1% to 5% to 8% across three quarters, and Q2 2026 set all-time highs in orders, sales and operating profit
  • Change forces remain top-decile. A No peer in the comparison set carries an ownership discontinuity of EQT's magnitude, and the acquisition engine tripled its contribution to 9%

  • Recommendation. Progress to Deep Dive at medium conviction, explicitly price-gated, with the permanent chief executive and the acquired-growth run-rate as the two gating reads
Business Description (B.1) 22 findings
Business Description -- A plain-English description of what the company does, how it makes money, and its primary market.
1.
What the business is. OEM International is a Swedish technology trading and distribution group acting as the local sales and logistics arm for specialised industrial component manufacturers
2.
How it earns. It buys components from manufacturers, adds technical selection advice, customisation, local stock and next-day delivery, then resells to industrial customers at a gross margin
3.
Gross margin level. Q1 2026 gross margin strengthened to 37.1%, calculated in source, which is the level at which this value-add distribution model operates
4.
Product breadth. The group carries more than 60,000 products from over 400 manufacturers, spanning automation, electrification, installation components and mechanical power transmission
5.
Customer base. More than 30,000 industrial customers are served, with named accounts including ABB, Sandvik, Komatsu, Epiroc and Volvo across mining, energy, healthcare, automotive and forestry
6.
Revenue mix by product. Automation is approximately 51% of sales, installation components 18%, other products 22% and standard industrial components 9%, per RedEye via Business Foundations
7.
Revenue mix by type. Standard product trading is 70% to 80% of group sales, modified and semi-assembled products 10% to 15%, and proprietary or private-label products the remaining 10% to 15%
8.
Geography. Sweden is approximately 58% of net sales and 65% of group EBITA; Finland, the Baltics and China are 19%; the rest of Europe is 23%
9.
Nordic concentration. Approximately 81% of sales are generated in the Nordics, with China contributing about 1% of total group revenue
10.
Organisational shape. The group runs 38 business units across 16 countries through roughly 35 autonomous subsidiary chief executives, five business-area heads and a 26-person head office in Tranås
11.
Revenue scale. Full-year 2025 net sales were SEK 5,316.9m, up 1% on SEK 5,238.9m in 2024, with organic growth of 3%, acquisitions 1% and a 3% currency drag
12.
Most recent quarter. Q2 2026 net sales reached a record SEK 1,559m, up 17% year on year, with organic growth of 8%, acquisitions 9% and no currency effect
13.
Margin profile. The EBITA margin was 15.0% in 2025, 16.2% in Q1 2026 and 15.4% in Q2 2026, against a stated through-cycle floor of 12%
14.
Earnings. Basic earnings per share were SEK 4.27 in 2025 against SEK 4.37 in 2024, and Q2 2026 earnings per share rose 31% to SEK 1.26
15.
Balance sheet. The group held net cash of SEK 603m at end-2025, net debt to EBITDA of negative 0.8x, and an equity-to-assets ratio of 70% at 30 June 2026
16.
Liquidity. Cash and unutilised credit facilities totalled SEK 897m at 30 June 2026, against a capital-structure target of gross debt to equity below 100%
17.
Returns. Return on capital employed was 30.7% in 2025, down from 35.3% in 2024 and 40.2% in 2023, while return on invested capital reached 28% in 2025
18.
Ownership. EQT acquired 73.68% of votes and 26.19% of capital in February 2025, ending more than 50 years of founding-family principal ownership
19.
Leadership in transition. Chief executive Jörgen Zahlin leaves during 2026, Henrik Forsberg Schoultz joined as deputy chief executive in April 2026, and Daniel Warnholtz became chief financial officer on 1 August 2026
20.
Capital returns. The board proposed an unchanged dividend of SEK 1.75 per share for 2025, a payout ratio of 41% of net income, with no buybacks executed since 2011
21.
Investment style implication. This is a capital-light serial-acquirer compounder in an accelerated acquisition phase under new private-equity control, so the case is change-driven rather than steady-state
22.
Cyclical character. Roughly half the demand base tracks Nordic and European industrial production, and construction-exposed subsidiaries remain depressed, giving the compounder a genuine cyclical overlay
