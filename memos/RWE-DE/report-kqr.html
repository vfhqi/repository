According to a regulatory report on 8 October 2026, the updated rules for the storage capacity mechanism with 2029 delivery were approved by ministerial decree in early 2026. This regulatory framework establishes a structured annual remuneration that ensures high revenue predictability, which means secured cash flows will support long-term utility investments.
2.
Auction adjustments: Italian energy regulators are dynamically adjusting pricing limits to encourage battery investments while protecting consumer interests. Bernstein Research reported on 29 September 2026 that Italy established a revised cap of 27,000 euros per megawatt annually for the 2029 MACSE battery storage auction. This cap is designed to support the development of 16 gigawatt-hours of new battery capacity under 15-year contracts, which will help underpin the long-term earnings of flexible power providers.
3.
Generation remuneration: Italy is maintaining separate support systems for both new electricity storage and traditional generation capacity to secure dispatchable power. A regulatory report on 8 October 2026 confirmed that ministerial decree No. 259 approved Terna's proposed rules for the generation capacity remuneration auction with 2028 delivery. This separate remuneration framework provides traditional power plants with stable cash flows, which means utilities can maintain base earnings while transitioning to cleaner technologies.
4.
Price contraction: Early competitive auctions have seen aggressive bidding that drove clearing prices down significantly compared to the initial regulatory ceilings. Bernstein Research reported on 24 September 2026 that intense competition in Italy's first MACSE auction lowered prices from 37,000 euros per megawatt-hour annually to just under 13,000 euros. Section summary

Historical auction performance

1.
Depressed clearing prices: Great Britain's capacity market has transitioned through significant price volatility over recent delivery cycles. The 2029-30 T-4 capacity auction concluded on 11 March 2026 with a clearing price of 27.10 pounds per kilowatt, which is a marked reduction from the 60 to 65 pounds range seen in the previous three T-4 auctions. This substantial price drop means flexible generators will capture materially lower contracted revenues in 2029 unless subsequent auctions clear near the cap.
2.
Cheapest top-up clearing: Recent short-term auctions have delivered record low prices due to high bidder participation. The T-1 auction for the 2026-27 delivery year concluded on 4 March 2026 at a clearing price of only 5 pounds per kilowatt, representing the lowest level in five years. This low clearing price suggests that near-term top-up capacity remains highly oversupplied, which means marginal operators will struggle to cover fixed costs.
3.
Escalating delivery costs: Historical analysis reveals that high clearing prices in earlier auctions have created substantial funding burdens for the system. According to a regulatory report published on 10 September 2026, the 2027-28 delivery year represents the most expensive year since 2018-19, costing approximately 3.20 billion pounds. This peak cost was driven by high T-4 clearing prices of 65 pounds per kilowatt, whereas the 2029-30 delivery year is projected to cost only 1.90 billion pounds due to lower clearing rates.
4.
Declining reserve margins: The gradual retirement of legacy baseload stations continues to put structural pressure on the UK electricity grid. Morgan Stanley reported on 29 September 2024, which is older than twelve months, that the phaseout of coal and nuclear power in Great Britain has significantly tightened reserve margins. This structural tightness will likely escalate long-term capacity payments to flexible generators over the next decade as grid stability concerns grow.

Grid connection queue reforms

5.
Stricter gate criteria: Grid operators are introducing aggressive reforms to flush out speculative projects and accelerate viable developments. The UK government announced its transition to a "first ready and needed, first connected" approach on 29 September 2026 to resolve grid delays. This policy shift means that unviable projects will lose their queue positions, which will help tighten the future capacity auction supply.
6.
Substantial commitment fees: Regulators are proposing significant financial barriers to deter developers who lack committed end-users. An Ofgem consultation on 29 July 2026 proposed a returnable Data Centre Commitment Fee ranging from 237,500 to 712,500 pounds per megawatt. This high commitment fee will discourage speculative applications and ensure that only mature projects secure grid access.
7.
Battery storage levies: Rising connection bottlenecks for storage technologies have prompted targeted regulatory penalties. Bernstein Research reported on 21 September 2026 that Ofgem proposed the Oversubscribed Technologies Commitment Fee of up to 25,000 pounds per megawatt for batteries. This levy will likely moderate the battery pipeline from its current 90 gigawatts back toward the projected 29 gigawatts system requirement.
8.
Long de-rating curves: Regulators are adjusting technology de-rating assumptions to ensure that battery fleets deliver power during periods of extreme system stress. A regulatory update from 16 May 2025, which is older than twelve months, indicated that the system operator implemented de-rating changes specifically for limited duration storage assets. These tighter de-rating factors mean battery storage operators must install longer duration capacity to capture the same level of capacity contract remuneration.
