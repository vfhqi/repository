Section 2 – US Building-Wire and Data-Centre Cable Market Dynamics

Section summary
  1. The introduction of the 2026 Section 232 copper tariffs has widened the COMEX premium over the London Metal Exchange.
  2. Rapid expansion of US data centers is driving unprecedented demand for high-specification building wires and grid accessories.
  3. Worsening utility equipment shortages and local pushback represent severe bottlenecks to energized capacity delivery across North America.

Tariff Impacts and Metal Premiums

1.
Tariff-driven pricing mechanics: The implementation of Section 232 tariffs on refined copper has widened the COMEX premium over the London Metal Exchange. Jefferies Research argues in its note of 6 October 2026 that the scheduled 15% tariff on refined copper starting in January 2027 and 30% in January 2028 will widen this premium because it raises the cost of imported inputs. This tariffinduced price umbrella will support the domestic pricing power of US cable producers over the next three years, thereby boosting their operating margins.
2.
Copper recycling integration: Domestic cable manufacturers are rapidly accelerating their use of recycled copper to mitigate raw material price volatility under the new tariff regime. Morgan Stanley reports in its document of 6 February 2026 that Prysmian has increased its use of recycled copper to 60% in its United States operations compared to just 20.7% globally. This aggressive recycling strategy helps insulate domestic manufacturing margins from tariff-driven raw material cost inflation, which protects regional earnings per share from downside pressure.
3.
Favorable volume comparison: The physical volume of copper orders in the United States has experienced extreme fluctuations due to tariff-induced customer stockpiling behavior. Nexans reported in its press release of 22 October 2026 that its metallurgy sales grew by 24.7% organically in the third quarter of 2026 compared to a steep decline in the prior year period. This unusual performance arose because customers aggressively brought forward copper orders in early 2025 ahead of anticipated tariffs, meaning that future low-voltage cable volumes remain highly sensitive to regulatory timelines.

Data Center Capacity Demands

4.
Accelerating capacity build: The rapid scaling of generative artificial intelligence is driving an unprecedented expansion of data center infrastructure across North America. Morgan Stanley forecasts in its report of 26 October 2026 that global data center capacity will grow at a 23% compound annual rate to reach 316 gigawatts by 2030, with the United States adding a market-leading 88 gigawatts of capacity. This massive infrastructure investment creates sustained demand for high-specification lowvoltage building wires, which underwrites long-term organic volume growth for local cable producers.
5.
AI-driven power bottlenecks: The extreme power density of modern artificial intelligence clusters is generating a severe electricity supply shortfall that threatens data center deployment schedules. Morgan Stanley's thematic research of 26 October 2026 projects a massive 70 gigawatt US power shortfall facing data center developers through 2028 before factoring in off-grid solutions. This structural bottleneck means that cable producers with guaranteed access to utility grid-reinforcement projects will capture higher market share, thereby protecting their regional earnings trajectories over the next three years.
6.
High-density connectivity demand: The technological transition from legacy cloud architectures to high-power-density artificial intelligence clusters has structurally altered cable specifications. Nuvama Institutional Equities notes in its report of 15 October 2026 that artificial intelligence-enabled facilities require 70% higher optical fiber density compared to traditional platforms. While optical connectivity dominates near-term big-tech capital expenditure, heavy electrical power distribution cabling accounts for 8% to 10% of total data center cost of goods sold, which supports sustained high-margin backlogs.

Supply Chain and Grid Constraints

7.
Transformer lead-time constraints: Severe equipment shortages and lengthy grid interconnection queues represent the primary physical barriers to bringing new data center capacity online. An expert interview with a head of energy procurement at a global data center operator on 19 October 2026 revealed that transformer shortages are the most severe bottleneck, with wait times worsening by six to eight months and reaching up to seven years. Consequently, cable installation volumes are often deferred, which means that near-term low-voltage wire margins may suffer from project delays.
8.
Local regulatory pushback: Evolving political and community opposition to large-scale data center builds is introducing substantial site-selection and timing uncertainty across key US states. Deutsche Bank Research reported on 22 October 2026 that over 300 local data center moratoriums have been adopted nationwide, although only 2.3 gigawatts of the 38 gigawatts planned for 2027 have faced actual delays. If local environmental opposition intensifies, developers will be forced to shift projects to secondary markets, which would disrupt the geographical delivery of regional cable volumes.
9.
Contrary evidence: Doubts regarding the timely implementation of the refined copper tariffs could undermine the expected pricing protection for domestic manufacturers. Deutsche Bank's report of 25 October 2026 highlights that uncertainty over the scheduled January 2027 tariffs has kept the COMEX premium narrower than anticipated, which is further complicated by elevated domestic stockpiles of over 600 kt. If these tariffs are delayed or canceled, cheap foreign imports of lowvoltage building wire will flood the US market, which would compress domestic margins.

Impact on Nexans Business Segments

10.
Segment organic acceleration: The robust demand for US data center power connectivity has directly accelerated the financial performance of Nexans' low-voltage PWR- Connect segment. Nexans reported in its 9M 2026 financial release of 22 October 2026 that standard sales in PWR-Connect grew by 21.9% to EUR 2,124.7 million, supported by an 8.3% organic volume expansion and a 14.5% acquisition scope effect. Grid infrastructure expansion: Rising utility capital expenditure on grid reinforcement and distribution modernization has sustained exceptionally high momentum within Nexans' PWR-Grid segment. This investment will expand the existing Republic Wire facility to capture accelerating demand from utility customers, which will drive double-digit earnings accretion for the grid division.
12.
Other side of the story: While the structural tailwind from data centers remains exceptionally strong, the severe cyclical weakness in traditional US commercial and residential construction poses a substantial threat to low-voltage volumes. Morgan Stanley's report of 27 October 2026 indicates that while data center construction spending has more than doubled, traditional residential and commercial construction declined -8% over the past two years. If residential construction remains depressed, the cyclical volume decline will offset data center gains, which would ultimately weigh heavily on group earnings unless public spending recovers.
