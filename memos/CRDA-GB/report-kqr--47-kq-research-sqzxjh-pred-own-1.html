Unpacking the baseline calculation

1.
Historical benchmarking: Croda International Plc utilizes a pre- pandemic baseline index of 100 from late 2019 to track absolute volume recovery. This baseline index is calculated using sales volumes from 11 multi-purpose manufacturing sites. The company selected 2019 because it represents a clean operational period before subsequent pandemic volatility. These 11 sites are critical assets that account for seventy percent of group volumes and sixty percent of total sales.
2.
Capacity mismatch: Croda International Plc historically operated its 11 shared manufacturing sites at absolute utilisation rates of eighty to eighty-five percent before the pandemic. Chief Executive Steve Foots confirmed that normal pre-pandemic utilisation was typically around eighty percent or slightly higher. Since 2019, the group has added substantial new physical footprint across several regions. This expansion implies that returning to one hundred percent of the 2019 baseline volume would correspond to a much lower absolute utilisation rate today.
3.
Diluted metrics: Croda International Plc reported that rolling shared-site sales volumes rebounded to ninety-three percent of the baseline in the first half of 2026. This expansion means that ninety-three percent baseline volume translates to lower operational run-rates than in 2019. Therefore, the company faces ongoing negative operational leverage despite the top-line recovery.
4.
Incremental overheads: Croda International Plc has introduced a significant layer of annual fixed depreciation costs from its completed capital expenditure program. These added assets mean that the asset-turnover ratio has deteriorated relative to 2019. Consequently, the group requires much higher absolute volumes to cover its elevated fixed overheads. Failing to load these expanded assets directly dilutes operating margins and returns.
5.
Shifted utilisation: Croda International Plc saw absolute capacity utilisation at its shared sites trough at fifty to fifty-five percent in late 2023 during severe destocking.
