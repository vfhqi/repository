EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Recordati's transformation from a family-controlled Italian specialty pharma into a rare disease powerhouse is accelerating under the strategic direction of first non-family CEO Robert Koremans, yet the pending voluntary tender offer by CVC Fund IX and GBL via Respighi BidCo S.p.A. at EUR 51.29 per share threatens to remove public market oversight at a critical inflection point. The EUR 10.7 billion delisting transaction, valued at approximately EUR 51.29 per share, represents a massive strategic pivot that could reshape governance and operational flexibility. The Board approved the offer with a six-to-four split vote based on conflicting financial fairness opinions, with six directors supporting the Lazard fairness opinion while four rejected it citing a Rothschild report deeming it inadequate. This division signals profound uncertainty about intrinsic valuation at a moment when the rare disease franchise is delivering extraordinary momentum, creating a complex risk-return dynamic for remaining public shareholders.

The core strategic changes driving the investment thesis center on three transformational elements. First, the 2025-2027 Three-Year Plan targets Net Revenue of EUR 3,000 million to EUR 3,200 million by fiscal year 2027, with EBITDA guided to reach EUR 1,140 million to EUR 1,225 million and Adjusted Net Income of EUR 770-820 million. Second, growth is driven by Rare Diseases which is guided to represent 50% of revenue by fiscal year 2026.

Third, because the group does not engage in early discovery, M&A acts as its core search and development engine, with management targeting approximately one major rare disease or specialty care transaction per year to support growth. These strategic pivots institutionalize a deliberate shift from mature primary care cash generation toward high-margin orphan drug franchises, though execution risk remains elevated given the reliance on inorganic growth and external licensing partnerships.

The five to eight key drivers ranked by impact and plausibility reveal a portfolio concentrated in endocrinology and hematology with meaningful pipeline optionality. Isturisa sales reached EUR 178.8 million in H1 2026, representing accelerating growth of 58.0% year-on-year, with management doubling peak year sales targets to greater than EUR 1.2 billion. However, unlocking the non-overt Cushing's population requires EUR 40-50 million in annual strategic operational investments.

Enjaymo generated EUR 91.0 million in H1 2026 sales, representing robust momentum of 31.1%, while sutimlimab is advancing into a pivotal registrational Phase 3 trial for chronic ITP in early 2027. On the pipeline front, Recordati licensed global rights to mRNA-3927 for propionic acidemia, paying USD 50 million upfront, with target enrollment for the registrational study complete and initial data expected in late 2026.

Additionally, Recordati secured ex-US rights to Ionis' zilganersen, paying USD 30 million upfront, with zilganersen under FDA Priority Review with a PDUFA action date of September 22, 2026. Pasireotide Phase 2 trial in post-bariatric hypoglycemia met its primary endpoint with significant glucose increases, with the group expecting to finalize the Phase 3 clinical development plan by the end of 2026. Finally, mature specialty care portfolios generate steady margins to fund global clinical developments in rare diseases, with Recordati achieving a high cash conversion rate of approximately 90% from adjusted net income.

The concentration risk in Isturisa and Enjaymo is partially offset by stable specialty primary care cash flows, though therapeutic competition from Corcept's relacorilant and Crinetics' atumelnant could compress future market share.

The fundamental thesis rests on three pillars: first, Isturisa and Enjaymo are delivering hyper-growth that structurally upgrades the margin profile; second, the pipeline collaboration with Moderna and Ionis offers meaningful near-term catalysts; third, mature specialty primary care provides stable cash generation to self-fund rare disease M&A. However, the delisting introduces governance uncertainty at a moment when strategic execution is paramount, while therapeutic competition from Corcept and Crinetics, regulatory delays in Qarziba, and severe Turkish Lira devaluations create material downside risk.

The Board's six-to-four approval split underscores deep valuation disagreement, suggesting that minority shareholders tendering at EUR 51.29 may forgo substantial upside if the rare disease engine continues to exceed expectations. For long-term investors, the private equity transition offers operational flexibility but removes public market discipline, creating a binary outcome where successful rare disease scaling could deliver returns well above the tender price, while execution failures or prolonged pipeline delays could compress intrinsic value below public market levels.

The investment case is therefore conditional on conviction in management's ability to execute the Three-Year Plan targets, navigate therapeutic competition, and integrate pipeline collaborations without material delays or cost overruns.
