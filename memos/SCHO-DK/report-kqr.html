Section 2 – Tax Rate Normalization and Country Mix

Section summary
  1. Schouw & Co's effective tax rate normalizes around 24% to 26% as temporary impairments subside.

Schouw historical tax anomalies

1.
Elevated tax anomaly: Schouw & Co's reported tax rate rose significantly to 30.8% in 2024 and 40.4% in 2025. This performance represents a major departure from the 22% to 24% levels observed historically during 2019 to 2021. Management has initiated strong efforts to reduce this rate because elevated tax rates directly depress attributable EPS going forward.
2.
Goodwill impairment impacts: The primary driver of the 40.4% tax rate in 2025 was a large non-deductible impairment of goodwill. This impairment, primarily related to Borg Automotive, added a full 6.0% to the group's effective tax rate. Because goodwill write-downs are typically non-deductible, they inflate the reported tax rate without affecting the underlying cash payments.
3.
Unrecognized tax losses: Unrecognized tax losses have historically penalized the group's bottom-line performance. In 2024, unrecognized losses, particularly at Borg Automotive or US units, added 4.8% to the effective tax rate before dropping to 3.0% in 2025. These losses are not capitalized as deferred tax assets because management remains cautious about local profitability timelines.
4.
Withholding tax friction: Withholding tax on intercompany dividends and service fees created additional friction for the holding company. This input added 1.1% to the effective tax rate in 2024 and expanded to 2.1% in 2025. Such withholding friction occurs because international operations require profit repatriation, which dilutes the consolidated EPS conversion unless offset by bilateral tax treaties.
5.
Danish corporate baseline: The underlying baseline for the holding company is the Danish corporate tax rate of 22.0%. Adjustments for foreign subsidiaries relative to this rate added 2.6% in 2024 and 1.9% in 2025, bringing the weighted consolidated tax rates to 24.6% and 23.9% respectively. These weighted baselines indicate that international operations carry structurally higher rates than Denmark.
