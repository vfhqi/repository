EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This report evaluates the leadership quality, governance structures, and strategic credibility of PGE Polska Grupa Energetyczna S.A. under President Dariusz Lubera, who assumed office in January 2026 following the sudden departure of Dariusz Marzec in December 2025.

The executive board experienced a major reshuffle in late 2025 when Dariusz Marzec and Maciej Górski both departed, with Supervisory Board member Dariusz Lubera assuming the acting presidency before his formal appointment as board president on January 15, 2026.

The analysis draws on comprehensive company filings, sell-side broker research from Bernstein Research, ING Wholesale Banking, Morgan Stanley, and BGZ BNP Paribas, as well as expert calls from former industry executives at Ørsted, RWE AG, and Invenergy LLC.

The investigation covers sixteen substantive sections examining leadership track records, governance independence, capital allocation discipline, cultural indicators, and the critical risks threatening PGE's ambitious PLN 210 billion energy transition program through 2035.

The overarching conclusion is that PGE operates under a fundamentally state-dominated governance model that limits true independent oversight and exposes minority shareholders to persistent political interference despite nominally strong board independence rates.

The Polish State Treasury holds a sixty point eighty-six percent shareholding in PGE and exerts dominant influence over board decisions, with direct statutory authority to appoint and dismiss one member of the Supervisory Board directly.

While management demonstrates partial qualities of disciplined strategic planning and has secured advantageous capacity market contracts relative to domestic peers, the complete absence of executive share ownership, rigid salary caps, and frequent board turnover weaken long-term alignment with shareholder value creation.

The single largest threat to management credibility remains the unresolved separation of legacy coal assets, which could force PGE to absorb mounting carbon compliance costs and conventional generation losses that would severely erode projected earnings per share over the next three years.
