Section 1 – Brief company description and history 18 findings

Section summary
  1. Avanza's dominant domestic position provides a highly lucrative, capital-light earnings flywheel, but escalating international expansion costs and competitive campaigns threaten historical valuation premiums.
1.
Dominant digital savings platform: Avanza operates as the leading digital savings and investment platform in Sweden, providing online access to equities, funds, and pension products. The company commands an 8.7% market share of the Swedish savings market as of September 30, 2026. It services 2.39 million customers with SEK 1.28 trillion in savings capital. This represents a major expansion from its historical base. Overall, Avanza focuses on offering cheaper, better and simpler offerings to retail investors

Section 2 – Sell-side and market focus prior to these recent earnings 17 findings

Section summary
  1. Sell-side focus ahead of Q3 2026 was heavily dominated by the balance between cyclical interest rate cuts and the multi-year investments required for Denmark.
  2. Analysts were highly focused on whether Avanza could successfully internalize Sparkonto+ deposits to offset the negative net interest income impact of falling Swedish policy rates.
  3. While some brokers prioritized the near-term valuation premium risk relative to Nordnet, others highlighted the long-term compounding power of Avanza's domestic wallet-share strategy.

Macroeconomic variables and interest rate headwind

1.
Anticipated rate cuts tailwind: DNB Carnegie analyst Keric pointed out that while the Riksbank's interest rate cuts pose a challenge, they also act as a tailwind supporting Swedish consumers by reducing Value Added Tax (VAT) on food and mortgage costs in late 2026. The broker expected these lower mortgage costs to ultimately improve retail savers' capacity to invest on the platform
2.
91 Net interest margin compression: Jefferies analyst Roberts highlighted that the Riksbank's aggressive cuts from 4% to 1.75% compressed net interest margins across Swedish savings platforms. The broker remained cautious about the pace at which these cuts would flow through to deposit yields
3.
93 Depressed Net Interest Income (NII) expectations: Morgan Stanley analyst Ellinas reduced Earnings Per Share (EPS) estimates for Avanza to reflect a faster-than-expected pace of interest rate declines. The broker noted that competitive pressures would likely hinder the rapid repricing of interest rates on deposits
4.
Resilient non-interest income: Deutsche Bank analyst Killian expected the broader revenue mix to protect the group's top line from rapid rate normalization. The broker forecasted net interest income of SEK 524 million for Q3, representing 32% year-on-year growth driven by credit book expansion

Regulatory tailwinds and product innovations

5.
Supportive tax reform: DNB Carnegie analyst Keric emphasized that the Swedish budget proposal to double the tax-free Individual Savings Account (ISK) cap to SEK 300,000 in 2026 from SEK 150,000 in 2025 would be highly supportive. The broker expected this regulatory shift to stimulate retail trading and asset accumulation
6.
Premium wealth capture: Deutsche Bank analyst Killian highlighted that the launch of Avanza Sigma in August 2026 would help Avanza capture greater Swedish private banking share of wallet, compared to its modest 8.3% Swedish savings market share in FY25. This new digital discretionary service targets affluent customers who traditionally use active advisory wealth managers
7.
100 Gradual discretionary monetization: Jefferies analyst Roberts noted that the newly launched Avanza Sigma discretionary service charges fees of 0.55% to 0.85% of assets under management. The broker adjusted its model's net brokerage margin down by 0.5 basis points and fund commissions up by 1 basis point to reflect shifting customer asset mixes

Discontinuation drag and cash internalization

8.
Lucrative deposit internalization: DNB Carnegie analyst Keric reported that Avanza's final wind-down of its external Sparkonto+ deposit partnership in May 2026 would provide significant net interest income tailwinds as cash moved onto the balance sheet. The broker noted that Avanza only earned a 5 basis point commission on Sparkonto+ but could generate a much wider 50 basis point treasury spread on its own deposits
9.
Regulatory capital consequences: Jefferies analyst Roberts explained that the termination of Sparkonto+ forced Avanza to issue SEK 1.3 billion of AT1 debt across 2025 and 2026, which is comparable to Nordnet's historical SEK 1.4 billion Additional Tier 1 (AT1) issuance program. This issuance was required to bolster leverage ratios as massive client deposit volumes migrated directly onto Avanza's own balance sheet
10.
106 Reassuring cash retention: DNB Carnegie analyst Keric pointed out that Avanza's internalization of migrated Sparkonto+ deposits reached approximately 55% during late 2025, up from the broker's initial estimate of 50% in early 2025. The broker anticipated that the company would successfully maintain this high retention level for the remaining Sparkonto+ assets

International expansion and valuation debate

11.
Unfavorable dual-platform strategy: DNB Carnegie analyst Liljeberg trimmed near-term EPS estimates by 3% to reflect the guided Denmark pre-launch costs of SEK 120 million to SEK 150 million. The broker criticized the decision to run dual IT platforms as suboptimal for long-term scalability
12.
110 Costly geographic expansion: Jefferies analyst Roberts argued that Avanza's Danish expansion would be far more expensive, costing SEK 340 million to SEK 380 million over three years, compared to just SEK 200 million to SEK 230 million for Nordnet's German launch. The broker warned that Denmark is a smaller and highly penetrated market compared to Nordnet's German expansion vector
13.
112 Non-essential growth optionality: Deutsche Bank analyst Killian initiated coverage with a Buy rating, viewing the Denmark expansion as attractive medium-term growth optionality rather than a core requirement. The broker highlighted that Denmark offers twice the median household wealth of Sweden, providing substantial asset upside
14.
114 Positive consensus divergence: Deutsche Bank analyst Killian published net profit estimates for FY26 to FY28 that were 9% ahead of street consensus. The broker expected resilient brokerage yields and deeper Swedish wallet share to serve as strong, stock-specific earnings catalysts
15.
Demanding valuation premium: Jefferies analyst Roberts maintained an Underperform rating, citing Avanza's excessive PEG ratio of approximately 2, compared to Nordnet's more attractive Price-to-Earnings growth (PEG) ratio of 1.5. The broker argued that a forward Price-to-Earnings (P/E) multiple of 21.2x was demanding given a low forecast earnings growth rate
16.
Deep historical discount: DNB Carnegie analyst Keric noted that Avanza was trading at a 20% discount to its historical forward P/E of 23x to 25x. The broker viewed this discount as unjustified given the platform's high domestic market share and sticky customer base
17.
120 Other side of the story: While sell-side bulls argued that Avanza's domestic depth and Sparkonto+ internalization would defend NII, the rapid succession of Riksbank interest cuts presented a structural margin challenge that simple volume growth could not offset. Jefferies analyst Roberts warned that entering a highly mature Danish market with a custom-built, artificial intelligence-driven platform introduces massive execution and cost risks that could severely dilute Return on Equity (ROE) over the next five years

Section summary
  1. Avanza's multi-channel strategic shifts are heavily geared toward accelerating international expansion and discretionary assets, but near-term execution and platform costs present a high hurdle for earnings growth.

7.

9.
New discretionary product launch: In August 2026, Avanza officially launched its algorithm-driven discretionary equity service, Avanza Sigma, which is designed to capture Sweden's premium wealth segment
10.
Discretionary asset progression: The initial pilot phase of Avanza Sigma yielded positive results, accumulating SEK 100 million under management from Private Banking clients prior to its broad commercial launch
11.
Planned deposit internalization: The mandatory phase-out of the third-party Sparkonto+ product was completed in May 2026, successfully retaining 60% of client deposits on Avanza's own balance sheet
12.
Financial profile shifting: As central banks lower policy rates, Avanza's revenue mix is expected to transition from net interest income dominance back toward high-margin commission revenues
13.
Elevating capital requirements: The influx of migrated deposits from Sparkonto+ required Avanza to bolster its capital base, leading to the issuance of SEK 1.3 billion in AT1 debt to support its leverage ratio
14.
Strategic financial targets: Management reiterated its 2030 goals, which target 15% annual savings capital growth and 8% average cost growth, alongside a 35% return on equity target
15.
Tightened cost guidance: Excluding Danish expansion costs, Avanza's cost growth for the Swedish business is expected to decelerate to 9% in 2026, beating initial buy-side projections
16.
Intensifying domestic competition: The Swedish digital savings landscape is facing escalating competition as low-cost foreign players like Interactive Brokers launch highly competitive Swedish ISK accounts
17.
Other side of the story: While Avanza's custom-built, AI-driven platform for Denmark is designed to optimize local savings culture, the decision to run dual IT infrastructures may substantially increase long-term technology overhead and execution risks compared to regional platforms
