Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The timing of wood cost declines is critical for 2027 earnings estimates because timber represents half of operating costs, meaning delayed cost relief directly threatens projected profitability.

Defining the Inquiry

1.
Primary inquiry: "Pointed follow-up question for a stock picker's sector note, Sector M.7. Forestry, paper and packaging, with Metsa Board (METSB-FI) as one worked example only. Our first draft cannot yet judge this: Finnish standing-sale pulpwood prices have fallen about a third from their June 2025 peak, and Swedish buyers expect falls through 2026, yet Metsa Board's Finnish wood costs still rose 3% in the second quarter of 2026. How long is the lag between the market price of standing timber and the delivered wood cost at the mills of Metsa Board, UPM-Kymmene, Stora Enso and Billerud, and what fall in delivered wood cost per cubic metre or per tonne do brokers and the companies expect for 2027? How much operating profit does that add for each company, and how much of it do brokers expect board and pulp customers to claim back through lower prices at the next contract round? Are any new buyers, such as biomaterials or energy plants, expected to push pulpwood demand back up?."
2.
Financial significance: Raw wood costs represent approximately 50% of the total operating expenses for Nordic paperboard and pulp producers. A substantial decrease in standing timber prices directly enhances operating profit and earnings per share. However, the structural lag in delivered costs determines whether these gains materialize in 2026 or are delayed into 2027. 20, this timing decides the near-term earnings trajectory for the entire sector.
3.
Varying sensitivities: Nordic packaging peers exhibit highly divergent sensitivities and wood self-sufficiency levels. For instance, Billerud possesses low self-sufficiency, rendering its EBITDA highly sensitive to Scandinavian wood cost changes. Conversely, backward-integrated peer Holmen enjoys high self-sufficiency, which shields it from inflation but reduces its relative benefit from falling market timber prices. This disparity determines how each company's earnings recover as costs fall.

  1. Structural delays in timber procurement mean that falling market prices for standing timber require four to six months to flow through to delivered mill gate costs across the Nordic packaging sector.
  2. Persistent supply deficits in Finland contrast with storm-related excess wood volumes in Sweden, creating divergent regional timber pricing dynamics that favor Swedish mills over Finnish competitors heading into 2027.

Section 3 – Operating Profit Impacts across Nordic Peers

Section summary
  1. Lower delivered wood costs generate substantial but highly divergent operating profit increases across Nordic forest peers, depending directly on their respective fiber sensitivities and wood self-sufficiency levels. Net read: Billerud represents the purest industrial exposure to falling timber costs due to its low self-sufficiency, whereas heavily integrated forest owners like SCA and Holmen face offsetting headwinds from lower forest earnings.

Fiber Cost Sensitivity

1.
Significant sensitivity: Billerud's operating profit is highly sensitive to shifts in northern wood markets because of its low self-sufficiency. Jefferies Research estimated on October 23, 2026, that every 10% decline in Nordic pulpwood costs adds SEK 0.9 billion to the company's EBITDA. This significant sensitivity represents approximately 22% of Billerud's projected 2027 EBITDA. This leverage makes Billerud a primary beneficiary of raw material deflation.
2.
Substantial benefits: Metsä Board benefits directly from falling timber costs across its extensive Finnish and Swedish production network. Metsä Board's investor presentation on October 29, 2026, indicated that a 10% decline in wood costs delivered to Finnish mills improves annual operating profit by EUR 40 million. The company's Swedish operations at the Husum mill add another EUR 20 million of operating profit for every 10% wood cost drop. These combined impacts total an estimated EUR 60 million annual EBIT tailwind.
3.

Jefferies Research estimated on September 22, 2026, that a 10% reduction in Nordic pulpwood prices boosts Stora Enso's EBIT by approximately EUR 100 million. This relief is crucial for the company's target of achieving a 10% EBIT margin within two to four years. Consequently, lower fiber costs will support the company's broader corporate transformation goals.
4.
Moderate gains: UPM-Kymmene captures meaningful but more moderated profit improvements from regional pulpwood price declines. Jefferies Research projected on July 19, 2026, that a 10% lower Nordic pulpwood price adds EUR 50 million to EUR 60 million to UPM's earnings before taxes. This moderate relative gain reflects the company's extensive backward integration into low-cost Uruguayan eucalyptus plantations. Thus, regional European wood deflation plays a smaller role for UPM compared to its Nordic competitors.
5.
Contrary evidence: some analysts argue that the financial benefit of falling timber costs will be entirely erased by rising secondary expenses. Jefferies Research warned on October 23, 2026, that elevated logistics and diesel costs are actively offsetting stumpage price relief across Sweden and Finland. Furthermore, Stora Enso reported in its third-quarter 2026 results that geopolitical tensions continue to drive energy and chemical costs higher. These inflationary pressures could limit the net operating profit expansion for most Nordic mills.

Integration and Exposure

6.
Diluted returns: Svenska Cellulosa AB (SCA) experiences a diluted financial impact from falling timber costs due to its high integration. SCA noted in its third-quarter 2026 report on October 23, 2026, that its 60% wood selfsufficiency rate acts as a natural buffer against external market volatility. Jefferies Research noted on July 22, 2026, that lower wood costs benefit SCA's industrial mills but simultaneously depress its forest division's earnings. Therefore, the net group earnings benefit is substantially lower than that of non-integrated peers.
7.
Protected positions: Holmen AB faces a similar earnings dilution because of its extensive forest holdings and internal wood supply structure. DNB Carnegie reported on October 2, 2026, that Holmen has a 50% wood selfsufficiency rate which protects its long-term competitive position. However, Jefferies Research reported on August 20, 2026, that falling timber prices reduce the profitability of Holmen's forest business area. Consequently, the group's net benefit from falling raw material costs remains limited.
8.
Divergent profiles: Nordic packaging producers display highly divergent earnings profiles based on their position in the supply chain. Jefferies Research argued on August 20, 2026, that investors should favor downstream packaging and pulp mills over forest owners when entering 2027. Pure industrial players capture the full upside of falling fiber costs without absorbing the negative valuation effects of declining timberland returns. This strategic preference highlights Billerud as the cleanest corporate vehicle to play the wood cost recovery story.
9.
Clean exposure: Billerud represents the purest corporate exposure to Nordic timber deflation due to its structural reliance on open-market sourcing. DNB Carnegie confirmed on October 1, 2026, that Billerud possesses low self-sufficiency in wood raw materials, which increases its exposure to market price fluctuations. This structural deficit allows Billerud to capture the maximum margin benefit during periods of falling timber prices. Therefore, the company's operating profit will expand more rapidly than heavily integrated peers.
10.
Other side of the story: highly integrated forest owners like SCA and Holmen offer superior downside protection if regional timber deficits persist. DNB Carnegie argued on October 1, 2026, that high wood raw material costs are likely to remain elevated in the long term due to structural supply constraints. Under these conditions, SCA and Holmen's high self-sufficiency levels represent a critical competitive advantage that shields their industrial mills from future price shocks. Thus, Billerud's open-market sourcing strategy carries significantly higher long-term operating risk if wood prices rebound.

Section 4 – Customer Price Clawbacks and Contract Rounds

Section summary
  1. European packaging and pulp producers face significant margin erosion as customers seek to claw back raw material cost savings during upcoming annual contract negotiations.
  2. High energy costs and low inventory levels provide temporary support for containerboard pricing, but structural overcapacity in Europe makes long-term pricing power highly vulnerable.

Cartonboard Contract Negotiations

1.
Gradual improvement: Metsä Board is entering annual negotiations for European folding boxboard with improved supply balance. CEO Esa Kaikkonen stated in the third-quarter 2026 earnings call on October 29, 2026, that improved paperboard market balance should continue to support the pricing environment in early 2027. However, Carnegie analysts noted on June 17, 2026, that the company faces persistent overcapacity in European folding boxboard which limits pricing leverage. Consequently, some of the anticipated wood cost savings could be eroded as customers demand lower prices.
2.
Delayed realization: International Paper expects European packaging contract resets to occur primarily in December and January as customer negotiations commence. Jefferies Research reported on October 29, 2026, that a typical six-month lag applies to box price contract resets in Europe. Management indicated that every €100 per tonne of pricing represents a $300 million EBITDA benefit once fully implemented by the second quarter of 2027. Because the company is short on paper, they will face higher open-market costs before realization.

Vulnerable pricing: Billerud expects packaging customers to manage their working capital carefully by bringing inventories down towards the end of 2026. Pulp Pricing and Past Cases

7.
Past precedent: Historical cycles in the European paper sector show that falling raw material costs almost immediately trigger customer price clawbacks. Jefferies Research observed on July 20, 2026, that in oversupplied packaging markets, finished product prices follow wood costs lower with a lag. In previous downturns, such as the 2023-2025 cycle, falling fiber costs did not expand margins because packaging customers successfully demanded equivalent price reductions. This pattern suggests that the 2027 profit recovery could be much smaller than anticipated.

Section 5 – Emerging Demand Drivers and Biomaterial Players

Section summary
  1. Emerging biochemical plants and biomass energy operations create persistent competing demand for Northern European pulpwood, keeping raw material costs structurally higher.
  2. Commercial biorefineries like UPM Leuna and Borregaard debottlenecking expand alternative markets for wood fiber but do not provide immediate relief to Nordic paperboard mills.

Biochemical Capacity Expansions

1.
Accelerated development: UPM-Kymmene is expanding its biochemical capacities by constructing a next- generation biorefinery in Leuna, Germany, with an estimated investment of €1,370 million. The plant has a planned capacity of 220,000 tonnes and has reached production readiness for renewable functional fillers. Management expects integrated production to start in the second quarter of 2027, with nominal capacity and positive EBIT reached in early 2028. This commercial expansion represents a major shift toward woodbased biochemical alternatives that will permanently absorb regional beechwood feedstocks.

Robust demand: UPM-Kymmene has secured strong customer interest for Leuna's products, with a contract pipeline exceeding annual capacity multiple times. The biorefinery relies on regional Central European beechwood sourced from private forest owners, avoiding direct competition with Nordic softwood mills. This regional sourcing strategy supports the long-term margin profile of the new business. Consequently, the mill gate costs of beechwood raw material in Central Europe are expected to remain elevated.
3.
Strong demand: Industrial technology suppliers are experiencing strong demand for bio-based and coal-to- biomass conversion projects across Europe. Deutsche Bank Research reported that Andritz holds a strong second position in the €960 million power boiler market, which is expected to reach €1.1 billion by 2030. This structural growth is driven primarily by coal-to-biomass conversions and waste-diversion projects. This trend confirms that alternative industrial energy consumers are investing heavily to secure wood-based fuel sources.
4.
Efficient biorefining: Borregaard operates an advanced integrated biorefinery in Sarpsborg, Norway, that utilizes 94% of its wood feedstock. The company is executing low-risk debottlenecking projects to increase total capacity by 5% to 10% gradually from the second quarter of 2027. These investments will add 15,000 tonnes of lignin-based biopolymers and 10,000 tonnes of speciality cellulose ICE Bear. The company sources 75% to 85% of its wood, mainly Norwegian spruce, from domestic forests under annual and mid-year contracts. Norwegian wood prices doubled post the Russian sanctions, which removed significant pulpwood and heating volumes from Europe. Consequently, this tight supply is expected to keep spruce wood input costs elevated for nearby industrial mills.

Biomass and Energy Competition

6.
Persistent competition: Industrial wood consumers face intense competition from the bioenergy sector as heating plants increase their use of alternative biofuels. Jefferies Research reported that European pulpwood prices doubled compared to 2021 levels due to the Russian import ban and competition for biomass from energy and heating. This structural deficit in the Nordics cannot be resolved through higher harvesting, as energy plants bid up wood costs. This competing demand is expected to prevent pulpwood prices from returning to historical baselines.
7.
Significant transition: Metsä Board's home market in Finland has experienced structural shifts as energy peat consumption fell to one-third of 2019 levels. Neova Group reports that the rapid electrification of heat production is permanently changing the fuel market. However, energy peat still plays a crucial role in balancing the regional availability of wood biomass. This transition forces heating plants to consume greater volumes of forest biomass, pushing pulpwood costs upward.
8.
Extensive alternative: Norske Skog operates massive biomass boilers across its European assets to secure self- sufficient steam and energy. The Saugbrugs mill in Norway runs a 70 MW biomass boiler, which is the largest in the country. The Bruck mill in Austria utilizes a 50 MW waste-to-energy boiler alongside a dedicated biomass boiler. These bioenergy assets represent substantial alternative consumption of wood residues that would otherwise supply pulp mills.
9.
Constrained supplies: Lenzing AG reported that European timber markets were disrupted in 2026 by geopolitical conflicts that curtailed sawmill cutting. This curtailment reduced the availability of sawmill chips and constrained broader wood harvesting. Management warned that should energy prices remain elevated, increased wood use for biomass energy will limit pulpwood availability. This threat of fuel substitution poses significant upside risks to fiber costs across Central Europe.

Biofuel Regulations and Mandates

10.
Profitable expansion: Svenska Cellulosa AB is expanding its presence in the liquid biofuels market to maximize value from wood residue. The company has commissioned a joint-venture biorefinery in Gothenburg with a capacity of 200,000 tonnes to produce HVO diesel and jet fuel. This facility relies on tall oil and forest biomass feedstock provided by SCA's own operations. This downstream integration enables SCA to capture higher margins from forest resources than raw timber sales.
11.
Stricter regulation: Strict European environmental regulations, specifically the Renewable Energy Directive RED III, introduce new sustainability criteria that could limit wood availability. ENCE Energia y Celulosa warns that these new requirements may restrict the use of forest biomass for power generation, particularly stump-fired plants. This regulatory pressure is forcing energy plants to seek alternative biomass sources or bid more aggressively for industrial wood chips. Consequently, these regulatory restrictions will likely intensify competition for clean pulpwood resources in coming years.
12.
Fluctuating mandates: Morgan Stanley reported that Sweden's biofuel blending mandates have fluctuated, initially dropping to 6% and driving down domestic prices. However, the Swedish government recently announced a mandate increase back to 10% for both diesel and gasoline. This policy reversal is expected to support the Swedish biofuel market and encourage further industrial residue utilization. Consequently, these regulatory mandates will sustain high demand for local bioenergy feedstocks through 2027.
13.
Contrary evidence: some forestry associations and private forest owners expect biomass energy wood demand to remain modest due to warmer winter cycles. Neova Group reported that warmer-than-usual winters in the Nordics during early 2025 created a temporary oversupply of energy wood. Additionally, lower industrial operating rates have reduced overall wood demand and stabilized some regional stumpage prices. These weatherdriven dynamics suggest that biomass demand may periodically soften, preventing runaway fiber costs.
14.
Other side of the story: although biomaterial and energy plants are expanding, their regional wood footprint is too small to disrupt the massive pulpwood oversupply created by European paper mill closures. Jefferies Research highlights that high wood costs have already triggered several permanent and temporary mill closures across Finland and Sweden. These industrial shutdowns, such as Stora Enso closing its Sunila pulp mill, release millions of cubic meters of pulpwood back into the market. Consequently, the demand from new biorefineries will be fully offset by industrial downscaling, allowing mill gate wood costs to fall through 2027.
