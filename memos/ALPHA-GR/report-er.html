EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Alpha Bank's H1 2026 earnings delivery represents a decisive inflection point in the group's multi-year transformation from a restructuring story into a capital-light, fee-driven universal business bank. The €316 million net attributable profit for Q2 2026 delivered a material 34% beat against company-collected consensus, driven by resilient net interest income expansion of 7.3% year-over-year to €853 million and a better-thanexpected 42 basis point cost of risk that came in well below the bank's conservative 45 basis point full-year guidance.

Management upgraded its normalized 2026 EPS guidance to €0.41 from €0.40, representing a 13% year-over-year growth rate and beating consensus expectations of €0.39, while simultaneously raising its net credit expansion target to €3 billion from €2.3 billion following exceptional corporate lending momentum that delivered €7 billion in new disbursements, up 33% year-over-year. The most critical change to the 18-month to 3-year EPS trajectory stems from three structural vectors working in tandem: first, the accelerated integration of the UniCredit partnership has unlocked substantial capital-light fee income, with onemarkets Fund assets under management surging to €1.7 billion by June 2026 from just €242 million at end-2024; second, the completion of the reverse merger on June 27, 2025 successfully unlocked €245 million in deferred tax assets and established a cleaner corporate structure; and third, the accelerated amortization of Deferred Tax Credits has cleared regulatory approval for higher shareholder distributions, with the bank accruing €273 million for H1 2026 payouts and securing ECB approval for up to €407 million in total distributions including a planned €259.4 million share buyback.

These structural improvements position Alpha Bank to deliver a 2025-2028 normalized EPS compound annual growth rate of approximately 18%, significantly ahead of the European banking sector average and substantially closing the historical profitability gap with domestic peers. Material deviations from consensus and sell-side expectations were concentrated in three areas. On the positive side, the bank's performing loan book expanded by 5% quarter-over-quarter to €39.9 billion, substantially exceeding volume forecasts and prompting management to upgrade credit expansion guidance by 30%.

Fee income delivered a robust 24% year-over-year expansion to €242 million excluding the €39.6 million Prodea dividend, though core retail transaction fees missed consensus by 3% in Q2. The most significant negative deviation came from the fully loaded Common Equity Tier 1 ratio, which declined to 14.3% versus consensus expectations of 14.7%, driven by a 64 basis point quarter-over-quarter reduction caused by accelerated risk-weighted asset growth from the corporate lending surge, a 15 basis point impact from the

Alpha Trust acquisition, and a 10 basis point prospective employee share program accrual. Despite this capital compression, management maintained its reported net profit guidance of €950 million as stronger underlying operational trends successfully offset higher-than-expected one-off expenses including Katseli law impacts and customer hedge derecognition. The sell-side response to the earnings update was uniformly constructive, with all major covering brokers raising price targets and earnings estimates.

Deutsche Bank's Alfredo Alonso lifted his target price to €5.10 from €4.80 and upgraded net attributable profit estimates for 2026-2028 by 7.2%, 3.9%, and 5.3% respectively, emphasizing that significantly stronger core banking volume growth and positive net interest margin dynamics will offset rate cuts. Morgan Stanley's Noemi Peruch raised her price target to €5.50 from €5 and fine-tuned EPS forecasts upward by 2% for 2026-2027 and 1% for 2028, highlighting the upcoming November 2026 Investor Day as a potential catalyst for further consensus upgrades of 4% to 5% as new medium-term targets are unveiled.

Jefferies' Alexander Demetriou and Joseph Dickerson increased their target price to €5.25 from €5 and upgraded mid-term EPS forecasts for 2027 and 2028 by 1% to 2%, arguing that the stock trades at an attractive valuation of 7x 2028 price-to-earnings, representing a 20% discount to peers despite one of the strongest earnings growth profiles among European financial institutions. Alpha Bank's competitive positioning has been fundamentally reshaped by its strategic partnership with UniCredit, which holds a 29.8% direct equity stake and provides access to cross-border corporate banking platforms and co-branded investment products that structurally distinguish the franchise from purely domestic rivals.

The bank's universal business banking pivot has driven transaction banking revenues up by 38% over the past 18 months, reducing overall sensitivity to the ECB rate cycle. The completion of targeted regional acquisitions including AstroBank in Cyprus and the pending merger of Altius Insurance with Universal Life to create Cyprus's third-largest insurance platform reinforces management's strategy of capital-light, fee-generating bolt-on transactions that must meet a strict return on investment hurdle of over 15%.

The Greek banking sector remains highly concentrated with the top four systemic banks controlling approximately 90% of the loan market, helping insulate Alpha Bank from aggressive challenger bank erosion despite rapid expansion by players like Optima Bank and digital platforms such as Viva Wallet and Piraeus's Snappi neobank. Macroeconomic tailwinds remain robust despite the European Central Bank's hawkish reset that lifted the deposit rate to 2.50% in September 2026 in response to persistent energy-driven inflation accelerating to 3.8%. The Bank of Greece forecasts domestic economic expansion of circa 2% annually across 2026-2028, consistently outpacing the Eurozone average by more than 1 percentage point, driven by a structural transition from consumption-led recovery to investment spending with fixed investment expanding by 7% annually. The Recovery and Resilience Facility deployment provides critical support, with Greece having received €24.6 billion representing 68.5% of total allotted €35.95 billion, though less than 60% of received loans have actually been spent, mitigating concerns of an investment cliff-effect when the formal program concludes in December 2026. Corporate credit demand remains exceptionally robust, with Greek corporate lending growth at 11% in July 2026 compared to only 4% for the European Union average, while retail mortgage demand has turned marginally positive for the first time in fifteen years though remains structurally constrained by acute domestic housing supply shortages.

The most significant regulatory development centers on the accelerated amortization of Deferred Tax Credits, which comprised 45.3% of Alpha Bank's Common Equity Tier 1 capital as of December 2025. Under the voluntary framework, Greek banks deduct an additional 29% of annual shareholder payouts from regulatory capital, allowing most systemic lenders to fully utilize their deferred tax credits by 2032-2033 and exit the restrictive Article 27A regime that imposes a 29% tax rate instead of the standard 22%.

This accelerated path has successfully cleared Single Supervisory Mechanism approval for higher dividend payout ratios, with peers like Eurobank targeting a 70% ordinary payout and National Bank of Greece securing approval for €464 million in total cash distribution plus a €300 million share buyback. The completion of the Hellenic Financial Stability Fund's multi-year divestment strategy, which previously held 40% of National Bank of Greece and 9% of Alpha Bank, has increased free float and liquidity, attracting deeper international institutional investment.

Supply-side cost pressures remain elevated, with domestic wage inflation running at 6-7% and the bank's blended funding cost rising to 98 basis points in Q2 2026 from 96 basis points in Q1 due to higher wholesale funding volumes and incremental MREL debt issuance including €750 million senior preferred bonds in February, €600 million green bonds in April, and €700 million senior preferred notes in September at a 4.3888% coupon.

Despite these headwinds, Alpha Bank achieved its cost-to-income ratio target of 38% in H1 2026, supported by strict cost discipline that kept total operating costs at €470 million even as personnel costs rose to €225 million driven by €13 million from new subsidiaries and €14 million from variable remuneration. Management's €47 million Greek voluntary separation scheme recorded in Q1 2026 is showing a clear payback path of approximately three years, while ongoing digital transformation through the Digital Factory employing over 300 professionals is expected to drive the cost-to-income ratio to a very competitive 34% by 2028.

Leadership changes were surgical and strategically focused, with the appointment of Iossif Kiouroukoglou as Chief of Wholesale Banking effective September 15, 2026 intended to strengthen client-centric advisory services and support cross-border corporate activity. The realignment elevated Georgios Michalopoulos, the Chief Wealth Management Officer, to report directly to Group CEO Vassilios Psaltis, reflecting the strategic priority of wealth management following the €2.2 billion assets under management added through the Alpha Trust acquisition completed on June 24, 2026. The departure of independent non-executive board member

Elanor Hardwick effective October 1, 2026 created a near-term governance vacancy, though board committees were swiftly reconstituted. CEO Vassilios Psaltis was ranked as the top chief executive officer in Greece and EMEA by Extel, validating management's strategic execution ahead of the critical November 5, 2026 Investor Day. The investment case for Alpha Bank over the 18-month to 3-year horizon rests on four pillars: first, the structural pivot to capital-light fee income through wealth management and bancassurance that is diversifying revenues away from pure net interest income dependency; second, the UniCredit partnership optionality that provides both commercial synergies and potential long-term M&A catalysts despite full takeover remaining highly unlikely given UniCredit's primary strategic focus on Germany and Italy; third, the accelerated deleveraging of Deferred Tax Credits that will unlock a 22% normalized tax rate and structurally boost earnings per share by approximately 15% to 20% once the Article 27A regime is exited; and fourth, the progressive dividend framework that targets a 60% payout ratio by 2027-2028 combined with the pending €259.4 million share buyback, positioning the bank to distribute approximately 30% of its market capitalization to shareholders over the next three years.

The stock trades at a 17% price-to-earnings discount to Greek banking peers adjusted for excess capital, which appears unwarranted given the upgraded earnings trajectory and strategic transformation progress. The strongest counter-hypothesis to the positive case centers on capital consumption risk driven by the bank's aggressive corporate loan growth ambitions. The 64 basis point sequential decline in the CET1 ratio to 14.3% demonstrates that rapid credit expansion is materially consuming regulatory capital faster than organic generation, leaving smaller buffers to absorb unexpected negative surprises relative to better-capitalized peers like National Bank of Greece at 18.8% CET1.

If corporate disbursements continue at the current €3.5 billion quarterly run-rate without corresponding deposit inflows or synthetic risk transfer relief, the bank may struggle to simultaneously fund loan growth, maintain Basel IV compliance, and increase dividend payouts above the current 55% target without triggering dilutive equity raises or M&A complications with UniCredit. Additionally, intensified deposit competition could drive deposit betas beyond the current 6% level toward the 23% back-book loan beta, compressing net interest margins as Eurozone rates potentially remain elevated in the low 3% range through 2027, eroding the volume-driven earnings momentum that underpins the upgraded guidance.
