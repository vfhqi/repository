Lagercrantz (LAGR.B-SE): what the Q2 2026/27 results do to the existing case

These results strengthen the existing case, modestly: they settle the organic-growth leg in the case's favour and restart the beat-and-raise record, but they leave the margin leg, on which the case's own entry condition rests, unproven for another quarter, and the share price has not paid for the beat.

1. What changed

Reported on 23 October 2026, for July to September 2026 (Q2 2026/27).

  • Organic growth was 12 percent, against 1 percent a year earlier, 6 percent in each of the two preceding quarters and a Bloomberg consensus of 5 percent. Electrify grew 18 percent organically, Control 16 percent, TecSec 13 percent, Niche Products 7 percent and International 5 percent. The case's modal path assumes 4 to 5 percent a year.
  • Net revenue was MSEK 2,990, up 22 percent and 6 percent above the FactSet consensus of MSEK 2,825. EBITA was MSEK 531, up 21 percent, 6 percent above consensus of MSEK 502 and level with Redeye's MSEK 533. The case's last data point was a 5 percent EBITA miss.
  • The EBITA margin was 17.8 percent, against 17.9 percent a year earlier: level with consensus, one point below Redeye's 18.8 percent, and better than the 40 basis point fall DNB Carnegie had forecast. The rolling twelve-month margin is 18.0 percent. The case's entry condition asks for a margin back above 18 percent; its alarm level is a margin below 17.5 percent with Control still at 14 percent.
  • The divisions split. Niche Products made 23.6 percent (19.6 percent a year earlier). Control made 12.9 percent (15.1 percent), carrying restructuring and acquisition costs. International made 17.3 percent (19.2 percent). Three or four underperforming subsidiaries in Control and TecSec are being restructured, and further non-recurring costs are possible over the next one or two quarters. DNB Carnegie puts the lag in passing on raw-material and freight costs at about 40 basis points of the quarter's margin.
  • Diluted EPS was SEK 1.65, up 31 percent; SEK 6.45 on a rolling twelve months. The case expected about 16 percent growth for this half.
  • Cash lagged profit. Operating cash flow was MSEK 270, up 2 percent and below management's own expectation. Return on working capital was 72 percent (73 percent). Net debt including leases and pensions was SEK 5,190 mm at 30 September 2026, 2.1x EBITDA on Redeye's basis, from SEK 4,489 mm at 31 March 2026.
  • The deal engine ran. Eight acquisitions in the half, adding about SEK 660 mm of annual sales, against five in the same half a year earlier. No paid multiple is given for any of them.
  • Order intake was strong in early summer, softer in July and August and stronger again in September, when five to ten group companies reported better project orders. The review gives no ratio of organic order intake to invoiced sales for the quarter itself; the only ratio it carries is the first quarter's 10 percent.
  • The sell-side response, two houses only. Redeye raised 2027/28 EPS by 9.2 percent to SEK 8.72 and 2028/29 EPS by 15.6 percent to SEK 10.75, on a rebuilt model that now carries 10 to 13 acquisitions a year, trimmed its near-term margins, and set fair value at SEK 251. DNB Carnegie raised EPS by 2 to 3 percent (2026/27 to SEK 8.00), kept Hold, and cut its target to SEK 242 from SEK 248 on a peer de-rating it ties to higher European interest rates.
  • The price was 230.0 SEK on 9 November 2026, up 0.1 percent over three months and 1.8 percent above its 200-day average. The case's entry range of about 195 to 207 SEK has not been reached, and the price is further from it than the 221.0 SEK at which the case was written.

2. The challenge

We see four readings the case does not hold. The first two are mirror images and the same evidence settles both.

Reading one: this is a demand turn, and the case's growth rate is too low. The case rejected the 'Demand Inflection' pattern because organic growth was mid-single digit and confined to one division. This quarter three divisions grew 13 to 18 percent organically, management says no single large project distorted it, September order intake strengthened, and the review cites euro-area manufacturing output at a fifty-five-month high, with DNB Carnegie's rule of thumb that such readings lead organic growth by two to five quarters. If that is right, the case's 4 to 5 percent organic assumption and its 14 percent EPS path are both too low, and the debate moves from "is the wobble noise" to "how long does the upswing run". For this to be right, organic growth has to stay near double digits for at least two more quarters against comparisons that get harder, and order intake has to run above sales while it does. One quarter against a 1 percent comparison does not establish it.

Reading two: this is a backlog flush, and the organic leg is weaker than it looks. The review's own explanation of the 12 percent is the invoicing of order backlogs built in early summer. Orders softened for two of the quarter's three months. A quarter in which invoicing jumps while orders soften is one in which order intake can plausibly have fallen to, or below, invoiced sales. That is precisely the level at which the case says its organic Key Driver is invalidated, and it is the second leg of the case's entry condition. The review does not give the number. For this reading to be right, the quarter's organic order intake has to have been below sales and Q3 organic growth has to fall back toward mid-single digits. The September pick-up argues against it; the missing ratio means we cannot dismiss it.

Reading three: growth without drop-through, so the margin problem is mix and a weak tail, not timing. The case treats the margin shortfall as transient: prices lagging costs in Control, seasonality in newly bought International units. But the strongest organic quarter in five years produced no margin expansion at all. The explanations offered now include a third item the case counted only in passing: a dilutive mix, because the units growing fastest earn less than the group. Control grew 16 percent organically and its margin fell to 12.9 percent, below the 14 percent the case already treated as the trough. Restructuring is described as non-recurring, yet it is forecast to recur for one or two more quarters. If the growth sits in the 13 to 15 percent margin units, more of it lowers the group margin, and the 20 percent target recedes as organic growth improves. That would make the case's path of about 18.0 percent this year and about 19 percent by 2028/29 too high, and it would turn the case's two good things, organic growth and margin recovery, into a trade-off. For this to be right, the group margin has to stay at or below 18 percent in Q3 even as the 40 basis points of pricing lag closes, and Control has to stay below 15 percent after restructuring. Against it: Niche Products at 23.6 percent, and a margin that would have been about 18.2 percent without the pricing lag.

Reading four: the sell-side is closing the mis-modelled line itself, and the result is small. The case's 'Compounder' fit rests on out-year estimates that omit unannounced deals. One house has now put the deals in. Its 2028/29 EPS is 22.8 percent above consensus, and its fair value is SEK 251, 9 percent above the price at the time. DNB Carnegie's acquisition scenario gives SEK 265 and its blended target SEK 242. So the two houses that do value the unannounced deals find little upside in them. The case conceded that the mis-modelling is known; what is new is evidence of what it is worth when modelled. For this to matter, other houses have to follow, taking the mechanical upgrades in one step, with the price unmoved. The price's response to this quarter is consistent with that.

3. What it does to the case

  • 'Organic order intake' (Key Driver) and 'Revenue and demand cycle': less pressure on growth, more uncertainty on orders. The false-friend reading of organic decay is refuted more firmly on reported growth. The order-intake leg is unverified for this quarter.
  • 'EBITA margin' (Key Driver), 'Margin', and 'Clearing evidence' under 'OSHQC CfC Clearing': same to slightly more pressure. The entry condition was not met at this report. The group alarm level was not breached, but Control is below the level the alarm names. The case's 60 percent odds of clearing by Q3 now rest on one report, and the second half has to run well above 18 percent for the case's full-year figure to hold.
  • 'Upgrade record', 'Proven upgrader' and 'Revision skew': less pressure. The beat-and-raise record resumed after one miss, and both houses in hand raised EPS. The 'A second quarter of margin shortfall' risk did not turn into estimate cuts.
  • 'Mis-modelled line' under 'Compounder': more pressure, on reading four.
  • 'Demand Inflection': upward pressure on its mandatory condition, on reading one; one quarter is not enough.
  • 'Acquisition pace' (Key Driver): same; on target. 'Price of acquisition targets': same and untested, since no multiple is disclosed.
  • 'Cash conversion' and 'Net debt': more pressure. Cash flow grew 2 percent against EBITA growth of 21 percent, and net debt rose.
  • 'The multiple' (Key Risk), 'Limited multiple risk', 'Peer trend': same to more pressure. A 6 percent beat was met with a target cut on peer de-rating, which is the case's claim that the multiple is not the company's to earn, confirmed. Higher rates also bear on 'Below-EBITA drag' through all-floating debt.
  • 'Modal return' and the Building Blocks: the earnings leg is under upward pressure, the price is higher and the multiple unchanged. We see pressure in both directions and no Building Block pushed out of its grade.

4. What would settle it

  • The second review, four working days on. It should carry the estimates and targets of the houses not yet in hand, the consensus revision, and whether other houses follow Redeye in modelling unannounced deals, which tests reading four. It is not expected to carry anything new from the company: no order-intake ratio, no margin evidence, no restructuring figure. It cannot settle readings one to three.
  • The Q3 2026/27 report. The review dates it to January 2027; the case has it at about February 2027, and the date needs confirming. It tests readings one and two on the same numbers (organic growth and order intake against invoiced sales) and reading three on the group margin against 18 percent and Control against the 15 percent management has indicated within two quarters. The Q4 report in May 2027 is the second observation for all three.
  • Disclosures the company has not committed to. A quarterly ratio of organic order intake to sales, the size of the restructuring costs, the margins of acquired units, and the multiples paid on the eight deals. Reading two turns on the first and the paid-multiple risk on the last, so both may stay open even after Q3.
  • Two inconsistencies in the evidence to clear. Redeye's fair value appears as SEK 290 in July 2026 and as SEK 242 immediately before this upgrade, with the step between unexplained. The 10 percent order-intake figure is attributed to the first quarter in most places and to the half in one.
