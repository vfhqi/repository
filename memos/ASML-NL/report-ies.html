ASML (ASML-NL): what the Q3 2026 results, and the fortnight's response to them, do to the case

With eleven brokers' responses now in hand, these results strengthen a business side of the case that was already rated at the top of the universe and press hard, upward, on the one judgement that sets the recommendation, the return, so the net effect is positive and on our reading material: the evidence the case said it would wait for until January 2027 has arrived in part through the balance sheet and the capacity statement, while the sell-side has done exactly what the case's return arithmetic assumed it would, raising earnings and cutting the multiple in the same fortnight.

The challenge

Three readings were put up on the morning of the results. We test each against the evidence now in hand, keep what holds, drop what does not, and add the one reading the sell-side's response has opened.

The first reading, that the proof the case was waiting for has started to arrive early, gained force and remains the strongest. The case holds the inflection cyclical until proven structural, names the proof as 2028 order coverage observable from January 2027, and says that datum alone would move its FY2029 from 68 EUR toward 85 EUR and the thesis rating from C to B. The fortnight has added to the balance-sheet evidence rather than subtracted from it: deposits for deliveries beyond a year of 9.301 billion EUR against 3.366 billion EUR a year earlier; a backlog inferred at about 56 billion EUR; about 60 percent of 2028 EUV revenue read as covered on Bernstein's model. Management's statement that the 2028 capacity the case's bull branch required is not enough is the demand side of the same fact. The case's plateau rests on the mechanism that "the capacity built through 2028 is absorbed" in 2029; a company being asked for 2029 and 2030 tools, with its next campus arriving in 2029, has no absorption year in prospect. What the fortnight has also done is sharpen the two conditions the reading needs. The deposits must be for tools, not options: Morgan Stanley's warning that the balance is not completed orders and may include Terafab, and the company's own admission that it has not accepted every 2029 to 2030 request, keep the pull-forward alternative alive, and nothing printed tests it. And the capacity must be buildable: 2027 is fixed at about 85 by lead times, Low-NA lead times lengthened in the quarter, and the constraints the supply-side evidence names (optics, precision stages, module build in Wilton and Taiwan, field installation, a plan for roughly 20,000 hires against Dutch housing) are not the kind a quarter resolves. The arithmetic at stake is worth stating in the case's own terms. If FY2029 moves to the 85 EUR the case says this datum would move it to, then on the case's own convention (three-quarters of FY2029 and a quarter of FY2030, FY2030 carried at the case's growth rate from there, at the case's 22.4 times) the value at March 2028 is about 1,950 EUR before dividends, about 31 percent above today's 1,489.6 EUR over eighteen months, which is the case's 20 percent bar met from the current price at the case's own multiple, with no re-rating. That is the whole of what this reading is worth.

The second reading, that the gross-margin driver is mis-sized rather than merely early, gained force for 2027 and split for 2028, and the split is the new information. The one published 2027 gross margin, Jefferies' 58.3 percent, sits above the 57 percent the case puts at the top of its path in FY2028, before pricing has landed in full. For 2027 the reading holds. For 2028 the sell-side has come out on both sides: Jefferies flat at 58.2 percent because supply tightness eases and the upgrade contribution reverses; Deutsche Bank at 60 percent by the fourth quarter; Bernstein toward 61 percent by 2030. Two facts make the case's margin line more fragile in both directions. Jefferies now sizes the productivity-upgrade contribution at nearly 400 basis points of 2026 gross-margin expansion, double the 200 basis points the case treats as cyclical, so the case has both under-counted the margin now and under-sized what could be given back; and management guided Q4 down to 57 percent on higher revenue, naming ramp costs, supplier investment and High-NA mix (at about 20 percent gross margin on Morgan Stanley's estimate) as the offsets, and would not give a 2027 figure. For the reading to be right in its strong form, three things must hold: the pricing reset must be broad across the NXE and immersion platforms (the company says critical high-value products, upgrades and services, and will not say by how much); the upgrade run-rate must hold rather than normalise, on which Morgan Stanley (the 3800E cycle is early) and Jefferies (growth slows from an exceptional rate) disagree; and High-NA mix must stay small through 2028, which Jefferies' eight systems supports. The size is still bounded: on the case's FY2028 revenue of 63,000 EURm, three points of gross margin is about 1.9 billion EUR of gross profit, roughly 4 EUR a share after tax on the case's share count, so margin alone does not carry the case from 62 EUR to the 68 to 84 EUR the houses now model for FY2028. Units and price do, which is the first reading.

The third reading, from the bear side, that the Street has extrapolated past the company's frame and management declined to endorse it, lost force as a timing claim and kept it as a valuation claim. The timing claim was that a 2028 unit number of 115 to 125 in January 2027 would meet estimates built on more. The published numbers do not now carry more: Jefferies models 109 Low-NA systems in 2028, Bernstein 110, Deutsche Bank about 110 under improvement; only Redburn's 30 to 35 percent sits above the plan. The gap Morgan Stanley names is between the company and the buy side's 120 to 125, not between the company and published consensus, so a January number near 115 would be a beat against what is printed and a miss against what is whispered. The valuation claim survived and strengthened: the houses raised earnings 8 to 19 percent and four of seven left their targets unchanged, Morgan Stanley cut its multiple to 25 times and Jefferies to 22, the shares sit 4 percent below the equipment peers on two-year-forward EV to operating income against a 40 to 100 percent historical premium, and the dashboard shows EPS up 7 percent in a month against targets up 1 percent. The case's re-rating leg, rated D on the view that a decelerating grower holds its 35th percentile rather than its median, has been enacted by the sell-side a year early. For this reading to carry the whole case, the AI capital-expenditure deceleration Morgan Stanley models (infrastructure spending growth from about 60 percent in 2027 to 14 percent in 2028) and Jefferies' cycle peak around Q4 2026 must reach the order book before the 2028 capacity ships, and the memory engine (46 percent of system sales; 2026 memory system sales up about 75 percent against logic about 10 percent) must turn as DRAM cycles do when supply arrives. The case holds memory-cycle softening as a risk low near term and rising beyond two years; the quarter has made memory the faster engine, which brings that risk closer than the case has it.

The case's own reading, the cyclical beat, was the weakest on the morning of the results and the fortnight has knocked it down, so we drop it. An in-line quarter whose beat came from full-margin upgrades bought because cleanrooms are full, with a tenth of net income from a revaluation and system sales about 5 percent below consensus, is a true description of the quarter and a false description of the fortnight: deposits for tools beyond a year do not nearly triple on a cyclical patch, and the houses that raised 2028 did so on units and pricing, not on upgrades.

The reading the sell-side's response opens, which the case does not hold: this may be a value cycle rather than a unit cycle, and the case's plateau mechanism does not apply to a value cycle. The case transmits the thesis as units times price plus an annuity growing at about 11 percent a year; its plateau is a unit plateau. The quarter and the response describe a company selling wafers per hour rather than scanners: Installed Base Management guided up about 50 percent this year and modelled by Bernstein at a 23 percent compound rate to about 23 billion EUR by 2030; the NXE:3800F at 260 wafers an hour against 230; next-generation immersion tools at 330; a pricing reset framed as payment for productivity; Redburn's equipment-sales growth of 35 to 40 percent on capacity growth of 30 to 35 percent. If revenue per installed tool is the lever, 2029 is not capped by the capacity built through 2028, and the case's plateau has no mechanism even if the unit count stalls at 110. For this reading to be right, three things must hold: customers must keep paying for throughput rather than deferring scanners because upgrades gave them the wafers, which is the cannibalisation the evidence itself raises; the pricing must flow to margin rather than be consumed by the capacity programme, which management qualified; and the upgrade pool must be as deep as management says and Morgan Stanley half-believes. It carries a bear mirror: a company whose incremental earnings come from software and price is more exposed when those normalise than one whose earnings come from a sold-out factory. Either way the case's plateau needs a second mechanism or none, and that is a reading the case has not considered.

The questions left open on the morning of the results, what the fortnight has done to each, and what settles those still open.

  • The target-price response: settled, and it is the part that matters for the return. Targets moved 1 percent while earnings moved 7 percent; four of seven houses held their targets; the mid-cycle multiples named are 22 to 26 times, with Bernstein at 30. The market has accepted the earnings and withheld the multiple, which is the case's own re-rating leg arriving early.
  • What the regional split says about China: partly settled. Q3 China at 23 percent of system sales is the re-acceleration the case's guidance arithmetic needed; whether it reaches the case's Q4 2026 threshold of 2.5 billion EUR prints in January 2027, and the September import data says the quarter's strength is probably shipment timing rather than a trend.
  • The 2028 unit number: not settled, and the evidence now in hand was never going to carry it. The January 2027 results are the first place it can appear, beside the annual backlog and FY2027 guidance; the company has said the number exceeds the prior plan and has deferred the plan itself, so January may bring a 2027 ceiling and a 2028 direction rather than a 2028 count, and the 10 June 2027 Capital Markets Day is where the company has placed the long-range figures. A January count of 120 or more with the backlog up is the case's proof delivered; 110 to 115 with the backlog flat is the third reading; no count at all is the case's own alarm level and leaves the first reading resting on deposits alone until June.
  • The pricing uplift by platform and by quarter: not settled, and nothing scheduled settles it. The company has declined to give a percentage, a product schedule or a margin contribution, and says it does not intend to repeat the negotiation yearly, so the only evidence is the printed gross margin through 2027 and 2028, read against the case's own tests: a quarter below the 54 percent floor with no one-off named invalidates the margin driver, and Installed Base Management falling more than 20 percent sequentially in Q1 2027 confirms the cyclical reading of the upgrade component.

Non-current contract liabilities flat or rising through the 2027 delivery year say orders; a sequential fall of more than 20 percent says timing; a company-disclosed backlog in January 2027 above Bernstein's inferred 56 billion EUR would convert the inference into a datum.
