Section 2 – Historical FX Translation and Transaction Effects on Sonova

Section summary
  1. Historical foreign exchange movements have repeatedly diluted Sonova's reported performance despite robust organic expansion.
  2. Operational adjustments and production regionalization have only partially insulated reported operating margins from Swiss franc volatility.

Early Decade Currency Pressures

1.
Severe dilution: Sonova experienced considerable currency headwinds in FY2010/11 that masked strong operational expansion in its core markets. Reported sales reached CHF 1,616.70 million which represented a local currency growth of 13.30% but was reduced by 5.60% due to currency translation. Unfavorable currency developments also reduced operating profit EBITA by approximately CHF 35 million during the year. This volatility illustrates how exchange rate movements can directly degrade bottom-line cash earnings per share.
2.
Significant headwind: Sonova faced even greater currency pressures during FY2011/12 as the Swiss franc appreciated dramatically against key trading partners. Reported sales grew by only 0.20% in Swiss francs to CHF 1,619.80 million despite achieving a solid 11.60% growth in local currencies. The negative currency translation effect reduced reported sales by CHF 184 million and operating profit by CHF 82 million. These results fell far below the initial budget assumptions of EUR 1.28 and USD 0.89 set at the start of the year.
3.
Moderate volatility: Sonova managed to stabilize operational performance in FY2012/13 despite ongoing volatility in global currency pairs. The company achieved local currency sales growth of 7.40% but continued to face transactional and translation impacts from the euro and US dollar. Operating profit EBITA reached CHF 363.70 million on a normalized basis, representing an operating margin of 20.90%. This performance was heavily exposed to exchange rates which averaged EUR/CHF 1.21 and USD/CHF 0.94 over the period.
4.
Operational leverage: Sonova achieved strong financial results in FY2013/14 as top-line momentum accelerated across all geographic regions. Reported sales reached CHF 1,951.30 million, representing an increase of 11.70% in local currencies and 8.60% in reported Swiss francs. Operating profit EBITA rose to CHF 430.10 million which reflected solid operational leverage despite a negative currency impact of CHF 4.50 million. These outcomes were supported by strong growth in the Asia Pacific region where sales increased by 14% in local currencies.

Impact of the SNB Peg Removal

5.
Unfavorable shifts: Sonova delivered record sales of CHF 2,035.10 million in FY2014/15, representing a growth of 6.20% in local currencies. The reported operating profit EBITA rose by 5.90% in Swiss francs to CHF 455.60 million, but exchange rate developments reduced reported EBITA by CHF 16.50 million. This headwind occurred during a highly volatile year that culminated in the Swiss National Bank breaking the exchange rate peg in January 2015. The resulting currency realignment immediately pressured the company's unhedged international cost structure.
6.
Abrupt realignment: Sonova saw its reported operating profit severely impacted in FY2015/16 following the full- year effect of the Swiss National Bank peg removal. Reported sales rose 5.80% in local currencies to CHF 2,071.90 million but grew only 1.80% in reported Swiss francs. The strong Swiss franc reduced reported EBITA by CHF 31.50 million, dragging the reported operating margin down to 20.80%. These headwinds highlights the vulnerability of the company's concentrated Swiss head office cost base relative to its global sales footprint.
7.
Contrary evidence: Sonova experienced a rare positive currency translation effect during FY2016/17 that supported its reported sales growth. Reported sales rose by 15.60% in Swiss francs to CHF 2,395.70 million, which actually exceeded the local currency growth rate of 15.30%. Operating profit EBITA rose by 11.80% in Swiss francs to CHF 481.40 million on a normalized basis, while currency developments had a minimal impact on the reported operating margin. This positive deviation shows that exchange rate trends can occasionally reverse and provide temporary tailwinds for reported earnings.

Deutsche Bank Research calculated that during this period, every 10% appreciation of the Swiss franc against the Euro would reduce Sonova's sales by CHF 90 million. Section 3 – Comparative Analysis of Corporate FX Hedging Strategies

Section summary
  1. Diverse corporate hedging programs create stark variations in operational predictability and cash flow protection across hearing-care and medical technology peers.
  2. While Sonova and Straumann rely on short-term horizons, Danish peers employ extended layered programs, and Ypsomed utilizes structural contract pricing.

Sonova Transactional Hedging Model

1.
Short-term protection: Sonova mitigates its foreign currency transaction risks by entering into forward currency contracts that have a short duration of between one and six months. The treasury department focuses its efforts strictly on transactional exposures because net assets are left completely exposed to foreign translation risk. This short-term horizon means that Sonova is highly vulnerable to persistent Swiss franc appreciation compared to international peers that maintain longer-term protection.
2.
Declining hedge volume: Sonova engaged in forward currency contracts amounting to CHF 277.7 million as of March 31, 2026,.

which represents a substantial reduction from CHF 391.3 million in the prior fiscal year. All of these open derivative contracts at the end of the reporting period were scheduled to mature within one year. This

3.

4.
Shorter duration baseline: Sonova's tactical hedging window of one to six months is substantially shorter than the rolling horizons maintained by other European medical technology peers. While Sonova seeks short-term flexibility, Danish competitor Demant implements forward exchange contracts spanning up to 18 months rolling forward to secure margin stability. This shorter window prevents Sonova from locking in favorable exchange rates during brief periods of Swiss franc depreciation.
5.
Contrary evidence: Sonova can successfully mitigate transactional currency risks over the long term by shifting its operational cost base away from the strong Swiss franc. Financial analyst reports from Jefferies indicate that the company aims to reduce its global cost exposure to the Swiss franc from approximately 15% to under 10% over the medium term. Shifting procurement and manufacturing to regional hubs provides a highly effective natural hedge that reduces the need for financial derivative contracts.

Danish Peer Layered Programs

6.
Extended protection horizon: Demant secures greater operational predictability for its international business by utilizing forward exchange contracts with a rolling horizon of up to 18 months. The corporate treasury policy dictates that Demant maintains an active hedging level between 70% and 100% of its total currency exposure. This systematic layered program smooths out sudden currency fluctuations over multiple reporting periods and provides management time to adjust pricing.
7.
Severe transaction losses: Demant faced a significant loss on its currency hedging contracts of DKK 158 million during the turbulent currency movements of fiscal year 2015. The group also experienced an additional transaction loss of DKK 34 million on unhedged currencies like the Brazilian real during the same period. These severe impacts prove that long-term hedging carries material risks if exchange rates shift permanently and derivative contracts lock in unfavorable rates.
8.
Rolling security window: GN Store Nord manages its foreign currency risk by hedging its expected net EBITA in material trading currencies 12 months rolling forward. The Danish peer enforces strict treasury guidelines requiring each business division to hedge between 75% and 100% of its net currency exposure. This policy ensures that the group postpones EBITA currency impacts by approximately one full fiscal year to allow operational adjustments.
9.
Successful derivative realization: Demant realized a gain of DKK 108 million on forward exchange contracts in 2025 compared to a gain of DKK 5 million in 2024. These gains were recognized in the income statement directly together with the revenue in foreign currencies. This outcome demonstrates how a rolling 18-month hedging program can enhance reported revenue during periods of favorable currency realignment.
10.
Shared translation exclusion: Danish peers Demant and GN Store Nord both explicitly exclude translation risks from their corporate financial hedging operations. Both companies choose to focus financial resources entirely on safeguarding cash flows and commercial transactions rather than paper accounting adjustments. This shared strategy means that both peers accept unhedged balance sheet translation volatility when consolidating local currency results into Danish kroner.

Swiss Peer Natural Hedges

11.
Brief transactional cover: Straumann manages its transactional exchange rate risk by anticipating its intercompany exposures over a maximum horizon of 12 months. The group executes forward contracts to ensure that these anticipated intercompany transactions are 100% hedged over the next six months. This selective coverage matches Sonova's short-term model and provides immediate operating margin defense without committing to long-term speculative derivative costs.
12.
Scalable manufacturing offsets: Straumann actively deploys a local-for-local manufacturing strategy to naturally hedge its global operating profit against Swiss franc volatility. This operational footprint reduces logistics costs and increases the natural foreign currency hedge across emerging markets like China. Broker reports from Bernstein indicate that currency headwinds still impact Straumann's top-line but localization mitigates bottom-line margins.
13.
Solid currency isolation: Ypsomed minimizes transaction and translation risks by invoicing the vast majority of its international commercial contracts in Swiss francs. This protective pricing strategy ensures that international pharmaceutical clients bear the exchange rate risk instead of Ypsomed's treasury. This structural commercial setup creates a robust natural FX hedge that isolates its reported operating profit from global currency swings.
14.
Localized pricing matching: Ypsomed actively adapts its invoicing currency in key growth regions where local market dynamics prevent Swiss franc contracting. The company negotiates contracts with Chinese clients directly in renminbi to match its localized China-to-China operating expenses. This selective operational matching ensures that translational FX effects are mitigated without relying on complex and expensive financial derivatives.

Alcon Specialized Treasury Policy

15.
Dominant dollar reporting: Alcon eliminates a large portion of Swiss franc translation risk by utilizing the US dollar as its functional and reporting currency. The company is still exposed to fluctuations across various foreign currencies, including the Euro, Japanese Yen, and Swiss franc. Alcon manages this risk by engaging in forward contracts and swaps where management deems appropriate to preserve asset values.
16.
Active net investment: Alcon manages translation risk on its foreign operations by utilizing sophisticated net investment hedge accounting. The group designated Euro-denominated Series 2033 Notes as a hedging instrument to offset the foreign exchange exposure of Eurozone subsidiaries. This active treasury program safeguards consolidated equity from translation developments that other medical technology peers leave completely unhedged.
17.
Sharp currency disruption: The Swiss National Bank removal of the EUR/CHF peg in 2015 caused severe immediate translation and transaction shocks across the medical technology sector. Ypsomed was taken entirely by surprise by the unpegging and faced immense challenges because its sales were concentrated in the eurozone. This precedent proves that short-term hedging offers little protection when structural exchange rate policies are terminated.
18.
Other side of the story: While Sonova's short-term transactional hedging strategy provides limited coverage, its low execution cost prevents the massive speculative derivative losses occasionally suffered by peers with longer-term programs. Demant's experience in 2015 demonstrated that locking in long-term hedges can backfire and exacerbate financial losses during persistent currency trends. Sonova's lean model provides superior flexibility to adapt pricing and relocate operations, which ultimately offers a more sustainable long-term defense than financial derivatives.

This omission prevents analysts from seeking to calculate precise margin sensitivities when individual currency pairs like the Euro or US dollar diverge from historical averages. Reviewing upcoming half-year filings, examining regional segment revenue breakdowns, and conducting expert calls with the treasury department would close this information gap by revealing precise regional cost allocations.

Accessing internal treasury spreadsheets, auditing derivative maturity disclosures, or organizing expert treasury calls would resolve this modeling limitation by defining the exact contract execution parameters.

Shifting terms: Sonova remains quiet on how frequently it renegotiates commercial contract terms, pricing structures, and billing currencies with major global retail accounts. Reviewing future wholesale distributor agreements, monitoring contract renegotiation announcements, and auditing contract templates would clarify how much exchange risk is systematically shifted to global buyers.
