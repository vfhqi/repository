Section summary

Hybrid Coupon Dilution and

1.
Accounting Dilutive pricing: Rémy Cointreau priced its inaugural 250 million euros hybrid bond issue on September 30, 2026, which carries a high fixed coupon of 6.75% and is classified entirely as equity under current accounting standards. Although this classification helps lower the reported net debt to EBITDA leverage ratio, the high interest coupon represents a significant cash outlay. This strategic financing choice improves balance sheet metrics but shifts dilution risks to the EPS level where the cash coupon must still be serviced.
2.
Inconsistent treatment: Morgan Stanley updated its financial model on October 8, 2026, to account for the hybrid coupon deduction at the earnings per share level, which is a method consistent with other European consumer staples peers. This modeling convention results in a direct reduction of two to three percent to Rémy Cointreau's projected earnings per share for fiscal years 2028 and 2029. This negative impact is often ignored by generalist investors who focus solely on the company's highly publicized accounting leverage improvements without adjusting for coupon payments.
3.
Leveraged adjustments: Jefferies incorporated the 250 million euros hybrid bond into its research model on October 28, 2026, by allocating fifty percent of the bond to equity and fifty percent to debt. This analytical adjustment successfully reduced the company's estimated fiscal year 2027 leverage ratio from 3.3x to 2.6x. This divergence in broker treatment highlights how different analytical approaches can obscure the true dilutive impact on minority shareholders who are focused on earnings per share development.

The dual-tranche issue carries initial coupons of 5.625% and 6.125%, which are lower than Rémy Cointreau's 6.75% coupon due to Pernod's larger scale and stronger credit rating.
