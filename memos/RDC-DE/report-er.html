Pharmacy reform – finalized clarity: The German Bundesrat's adoption of the pharmacy reform has provided a highly stable regulatory framework for e-commerce pharmacy operators. This legislation enacted a phased increase in the fixed pharmacy dispensing fee, or Fixum, from €8.35 to €9 on July 1, 2026, with a subsequent rise to €9.50 scheduled for January 1, 2027. While these fee adjustments improve baseline dispensing economics, they are accompanied by a statutory discount increase from €1.77 to €2.07 on January 1, 2027. This clear regulatory schedule removes a multi-year policy overhang for online pharmacies.

COURT RULINGS AND MARKET ACCESS

6.
BGH landmark ruling – pricing advantage secured: In a monumental decision on July 17, 2025, the German Federal Court of Justice confirmed that foreign mail-order pharmacies are not bound by Germany's fixed drug pricing regulations. The highest court ruled that applying national price-fixing laws to EU-based mailorder pharmacies violates the free movement of goods under Article 34 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. This ruling permanently cements a structural pricing advantage for Dutch-based operators.

457 liability – precedent established: Following the landmark pricing ruling, the Federal Court of Justice determined that local pharmacy chambers could be held liable for historic damages. Specifically, the court ruled that the North Rhine Chamber of Pharmacists may be liable to pay damages to DocMorris for unjustly blocking past advertising campaigns. Deutsche Bank analyst Jan Koch noted that while substantial financial recovery is unlikely, this decision sends a strong jurisprudential signal of legal protection for cross-border e-pharmacies.
8.
Karlsruhe court ruling – marketing limits enforced: While the legality of direct cash bonuses is secured, courts continue to place strict limits on promotional voucher advertising. On December 9, 2025, the Higher Regional Court of Karlsruhe ruled that a Dutch pharmacy's campaign offering €25 vouchers for first eprescription redemptions violated Section 7(1) of the German Medicinal Products Advertising Act. The court argued that allowing these vouchers to be redeemed on non-prescription medicines in the same order promotes inappropriate use of OTC drugs.
