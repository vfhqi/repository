Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Vitrolife's rising working capital and expanded credit loss allowances pose material risks to cash generation and valuation multiples.
1.
Key Question: "Vitrolife AB (VITR-SE), a Gothenburg-based supplier to IVF clinics reporting three segments: Consumables (culture media and disposable devices such as pipettes and EmbryoCath transfer catheters), Technologies (EmbryoScope time-lapse incubators and their slides, eWitness witnessing and clinic workflow software, iDAScore AI, service) and Genetics (Igenomix preimplantation genetic testing services and genomic kits), sold across EMEA, the Americas and Asia-Pacific. For Vitrolife AB (VITR-SE), what explains the rise in net working capital from about 24% of sales at the end of 2024 to about 30% in Q3 2026 and the increase in the credit loss allowance from SEK 35m (2024) to SEK 51m (2025): how much is inventory and safety stock, how much is receivables from distributors in the Middle East, Asia-Pacific and Latin America, and how much is longer payment terms on capital equipment sold to US clinic chains? How have working capital, receivables and bad debts behaved at the year-end of past years, what has management or the new chief financial officer said about a release, and how do Vitrolife's receivable days and credit losses compare with CooperSurgical, Coloplast, Ambu and Xvivo Perfusion?"

2.

Section 2 – Inventory Dynamics and Safety Stock Build-up

Section summary
  1. Vitrolife's inventory levels remained highly stable through fiscal year 2025, meaning that inventories were not the primary driver behind the rapid net working capital expansion to thirty percent of sales.
  2. Stricter raw material stocking policies and expanded safety margins were implemented across Consumables and Technologies to prevent supply chain disruptions, structurally locking up cash within the manufacturing segments.
  3. Seasonal stocking ahead of the summer vacation period creates temporary inventory peaks in the second quarter of each fiscal year that typically resolve through normalized shipments during the second half.

Segment inventory and safety stocking

1.
Systematic buffers: Vitrolife implemented stricter raw material inventory buffers to protect against global supply chain disruptions. The company expanded its predefined safety margins in production and proactively qualified alternative component suppliers. This risk management strategy was designed to ensure uninterrupted clinic deliveries but permanently elevated the base level of raw material inventory. Consequently, these systematic buffers will likely restrict near-term working capital releases unless supply chains stabilize.
2.
Perishable media: Vitrolife Consumables segment manages culture media solutions that require highly frequent replenishment due to strict shelf-life constraints. Clinics typically maintain low on-site safety stock of these items and place recurring, just-in-time orders to avoid expiration losses. This purchasing pattern forces Vitrolife to maintain rapid distribution capabilities and buffer stocks of finished media close to major clinical markets. Maintaining these regional buffers prevents sales disruptions but increases total holding costs and limits absolute inventory optimization.
3.
Technology fluctuations: Vitrolife Technologies segment experiences volatile inventory cycles due to the production of high-value EmbryoScope time-lapse incubators. Capital equipment sales do not follow a steady pattern and fluctuate based on individual clinic expansion schedules. To meet sudden demand spikes from consolidated clinic chains, Vitrolife must carry completed hardware systems in finished goods inventory. Carrying these expensive systems increases overall inventory values during periods of slow capital sales.
4.
Genetics transitions: Vitrolife Genetics segment faced major inventory swings as customers transitioned to newer technology platforms. The genomics kits business was severely impacted by customer stocking of older kits in early 2023 prior to adopting the EmbryoMap technology. This stocking effect led to subsequent customer destocking that depressed sales and left the company with excess kit components. Although this kitted inventory build finally bottomed out in late 2024, it illustrated how technology transitions can create sudden working capital drags.

Delayed switches and supply buffers

5.
Delayed conversions: Vitrolife faced challenges converting customers back to its global assortment after historical supply issues were resolved. Regulatory introductions in specific markets like China required local clinics to wait for official approvals. This transition lag forced the company to maintain double stocking of parallel product lines. Such delays in switching prevented immediate inventory liquidation and weighed on operational cash flow.
6.
Manufacturing complexities: Vitrolife has previously struggled with regulatory transitions that required running parallel manufacturing lines. The company had to produce both legacy and newly developed disposable devices simultaneously within the same plant to maintain customer supply. This dual manufacturing requirement was driven by delayed regulatory approvals for new product lines in international markets. Running parallel lines significantly increases critical work-in-progress inventory and limits manufacturing efficiency.
7.
Inventory growth comparison: Vitrolife historical inventory growth trends show relatively stable asset efficiency compared to the broader sales expansion. Inventories only rose by SEK 9m to SEK 422m at the end of 2024 despite notable sales expansion. This stable performance reflected effective supply chain coordination across the three operating divisions. Maintaining this tight coordination is essential to prevent finished goods accumulation during periods of shifting clinic demand.

11.
Working capital composition: Vitrolife net working capital rose as a percentage of sales, but broker data shows this was led by trade receivables. Operating cash flow decreased to SEK 224m from SEK 255m in the prior year's corresponding period. This decrease was primarily driven by a lower contribution from working capital and higher tax payments. Consequently, persistent working capital demands continue to hamper overall cash generation despite stable operating profits.
14.
Product line exits: Vitrolife decided to systematically exit low-profit customer accounts within its South American Genetics business. This strategic rationalization was executed during 2026 to improve regional market contributions. Exiting these low-margin accounts reduces the complexity of regional distribution networks and local product holding requirements. Over time, these exits should help lower overall genetics inventory and streamline the global supply chain.
15.
Other side of the story: Although absolute inventory levels remained stable through 2025, the combination of enhanced safety stocks and complex global regulatory approvals makes structural inventory reduction highly unlikely. The company is committed to maintaining high product availability for critical IVF procedures where supply failures are unacceptable to clinics. Consequently, the predefined safety margins and parallel manufacturing lines will permanently lock up cash in inventories, preventing a significant working capital release.
