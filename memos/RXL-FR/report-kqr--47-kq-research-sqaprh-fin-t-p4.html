French Surcharge Parameters

1.
Persistent fiscal headwind: Large French companies face prolonged profit tax surcharges under successive government budgets. French tax policy changes are analyzed across 12 distinct regulatory and broker documents, benchmarking Rexel against two key peers. Although initially described as temporary, the 2026 Finance Act extended these surcharges through 2026. This ongoing fiscal pressure means that Rexel must continue to operate under a higher statutory tax regime than originally anticipated.
2.
Surcharge revenue thresholds: Surcharge rates are calculated based on French revenue tiers which determine the ultimate cash tax impact. For companies with French turnover between €1 billion and €3 billion, the surcharge is 20.6%, while those above €3 billion face a 41.2% rate. Groupe BPCE's filing of September 22, 2026 confirms these rates result in effective corporate income tax rates of up to 36.13% for the largest entities. Consequently, any expansion of Rexel's French operations could inadvertently push it into a higher surcharge tax bracket, depressing net earnings.
3.
Invoicing and CVAE extensions: Additional corporate tax reforms in France have further delayed planned tax relief measures for domestic businesses. The French government postponed the planned abolition of the CVAE valueadded contribution tax to 2030, according to a tax regulatory report of March 18, 2026. Furthermore, France plans to mandate electronic invoicing starting September 1, 2026, which will increase compliance costs. Therefore, these cumulative tax burdens are stretching domestic operational cash flows, limiting Rexel's ability to offset rising interest expenses.
