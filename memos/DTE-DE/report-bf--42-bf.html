EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Deutsche Telekom operates as a leading global integrated telecommunications provider anchoring a resilient transatlantic model where the hyper-growth US mobile segment cross-funds and structurally anchors European digital infrastructure deployment. The group generated €119.10 billion in net revenue across fiscal year 2025, serving approximately 198,079 employees through five operating segments: Germany, United States (T-Mobile US), Europe, Systems Solutions (T-Systems), and Group Development.

The United States operating segment represents the overwhelming engine of top-line performance, contributing 66.10% of total Group net revenues in the first quarter of 2026, while the international revenue mix is heavily weighted with 78% of net revenues generated outside Germany. This executive summary synthesizes the key findings from comprehensive analysis of Deutsche Telekom's business model, financial profile, industry structure, value chain position, customer dynamics, supplier relationships, competitive landscape, cross-value-chain signals, operator quality, and long-term disruption forces.

Business Model Strength and Structural Revenue Resilience

1.
Deutsche Telekom possesses a highly resilient integrated telecom model where US mobile cross-funds European fiber deployment, with 66.10% of group revenues sourced from T-Mobile US versus 22% from Germany.
2.
Contractual revenue predictability is secured by multi-year service contracts shielding cash flow from cyclical macro volatility, with FMC penetration reaching 62% among high-value segments versus industry average 40%.
3.
German broadband repricing demonstrated strong retail pricing power via a €2 to €3 monthly backbook price increase in April 2026, applied to approximately 50% of the active broadband base.
4.
Systems Solutions pivots toward high-margin digital and sovereign public sector clouds alongside defense, generating €1.03 billion in Q1 2026 revenue with 5.90% order entry growth.
5.
Fiber-to-the-home demand accelerates with 21 million FTTH lines by March 2026, while unified production platforms like OneApp and OneTV compress structural opex across nine European subsidiaries.

Appendix

Outliers worth noting

The GD Towers passive infrastructure asset exhibits extraordinary margin performance, capturing EBITDA margins exceeding 80% compared to the telecom sector average of 35%. Deutsche Telekom retained a 49% associate stake in this high-margin tower business following the 2023 divestment, creating a striking structural disconnect between the company's integrated telecom operations and its passive infrastructure economics.

While consolidated telecom operations deliver adjusted EBITDA AL margins of 37.10%, and the German segment achieves a best-in-class 40.50%, the tower layer generates returns that are approximately double the core business profitability. This exceptional margin differential reflects the highly specialized economics of passive infrastructure ownership, where multi-tenant site sharing and inflation-linked Master Service Agreements generate cash flows with minimal incremental capital or operating costs.

The strategic decision to partially monetize rather than fully exit this asset class reveals management's recognition of the structural scarcity value embedded in physical tower sites, particularly as network densification requirements intensify across both Europe and the United States. The 45 percentage point margin gap between passive towers and integrated telco operations represents the single largest value-chain margin discontinuity documented across Deutsche Telekom's entire operating footprint. [Cross-ref: Section 4] T-Mobile US delivered an anomalous 3.90% year-on-year increase in postpaid account ARPA during Q1 fiscal year 2026, representing an unexplained pricing power acceleration that significantly diverges from the rational, consolidated three-player US wireless market structure.

The US mobile market maintains a tightly balanced oligopoly, with AT&T, T-Mobile, and Verizon each holding approximately 20% to 22% market share in a structure designed to support healthy industry growth and moderate promotional intensity. Yet while AT&T added only 84,000 consumer postpaid accounts and Verizon experienced an 80,000 account decline in Q1 2026, T-Mobile simultaneously expanded both subscriber volumes and achieved superior pricing realization.

This dual performance–capturing volume growth while extracting near-4% ARPU expansion –contradicts the expected trade-offs inherent in competitive telecom markets where share gains typically require promotional pricing sacrifices. The magnitude of this pricing acceleration is particularly striking given that competitors like Verizon and AT&T launched aggressive value-focused plans, including Verizon's $30 Simplicity offering and AT&T's $15 Build-A-Plan.

The 390 basis points of annual ARPA growth achieved amid intensifying lowprice competitive activity suggests either superior customer mix migration, unmatched plan tier upselling execution, or structural advantages in customer retention that peers cannot replicate. This performance anomaly raises critical questions about the sustainability of US wireless pricing discipline and whether T-Mobile's differentiated execution represents a temporary competitive dislocation or a permanent strategic advantage that will compress rival returns over time. [Cross-ref: Section 7] Deutsche Telekom's passive equity stake accumulation in T-Mobile US, which reached 52.60% in fiscal year 2025 and is projected to reach 55.90% by end-2026 and potentially exceed 60% by 2030, represents an extraordinarily accretive capital allocation mechanism that fundamentally decouples from traditional buyback economics.

Management raised the fiscal year 2025 dividend to €1 per share while simultaneously benefiting from highly accretive passive ownership creep as T-Mobile US aggressively repurchases its own shares. This structural arrangement enables Deutsche Telekom to capture the full economic value of T-Mobile's $14 billion annual buyback program without deploying any incremental capital, creating a unique value
