B.1 Business Description 11 findings
Business Description -- A plain-English description of what the company does, how it makes money, and its primary market.
1.
What Ambu does. Ambu A/S, founded in 1937 and listed in Copenhagen, designs and manufactures single-use endoscopes and airway devices, plus a legacy anaesthesia and patient-monitoring consumables range
2.
Segment mix. Endoscopy Solutions is 63% of group revenue and the growth engine, with legacy Anaesthesia and Patient Monitoring at 37% and managed for cash to fund endoscopy research
3.
Clinical breadth. The endoscopy range spans four specialties, Respiratory, Urology, Ear-Nose-Throat and Gastroenterology, all running on one software platform called EndoIntelligence
4.
Revenue model. Discounted hardware monitors are placed to build an installed base, and 90% of endoscopy revenue is recurring single-use scope consumption, a razor-and-blade structure
5.
Financial scale. FY2024/25 revenue reached DKK 6,037m from DKK 5,391m, gross margin 60.2%, and earnings before interest and tax before special items rose to DKK 784m from DKK 645m
6.
Balance sheet character. Ambu is debt-free, with a net cash position of DKK 319m in FY2024/25 against peak leverage of 4.0 times net debt to earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation in FY2021/22
7.
Geographic mix. North America is the largest region at 49% of group revenue, Europe 42% and Rest of World 9%, with direct sales representatives in 20 key markets
8.
Manufacturing footprint. Four sites operate, Penang in Malaysia at roughly 55% of global endoscopy output, Ciudad Juarez in Mexico ramping, Xiamen in China for legacy, and Noblesville in the United States
9.
Market position. Ambu is the largest pure-play single-use endoscopy maker with a 60% volume share of single-use, but only a 5% share of the whole flexible-endoscopy market
10.
Ownership. The Hesse family holds 29.1% of capital and N.P. Louis-Hansen 14.0%, under a two-class share structure in which Class A shares carry ten votes each
11.
Investment-style implication. The shape is a high-growth structural-conversion compounder mid-way through a tariff-and-currency margin trough, closer to a growth-at-a-reasonable-price inflection than a defensive compounder
