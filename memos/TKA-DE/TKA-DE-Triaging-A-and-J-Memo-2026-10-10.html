[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = C

Thesis. thyssenkrupp is a self-help and break-up case in a weak business, not a demand inflection and not a compounder: a chief executive with a mandate to May 2031 is dismantling a loss-making conglomerate into listed parts and cutting the cost base of its weakest part by a third under a signed labour agreement, while EU trade policy lifts the price that weakest part receives. At EUR 13.695 the shares have already paid for the two parts sold and the agreement signed; the third part, a standalone Steel Europe, has no date and no structure, and without it our modal return is about 15 percent a year over eighteen months and about 11 percent a year over three years, below the 20 percent bar.

  • The change is internal, broad and dated, and it has a policy tailwind outside it.
    • Six of the twelve internal forces sit at B or better, which is twice the screen's three, and the chief executive change is combined with guidance narrowed upward, so the input side of the case is the strongest feature of it.
    • Marine Systems was listed on 20 October 2025 with 51 percent retained
      • 49 percent of the materials-distribution business, tk accelis, was approved for distribution to shareholders on 7 August 2026 by 99.99 percent of votes, with a late-October 2026 listing target
      • The 50 percent HKM stake went to Salzgitter in July 2026
      • A binding agreement with IG Metall of December 2025 covers 11,000 positions by September 2030 and had been blocked for years under previous leadership.
    • Outside the company, safeguards from 1 July 2026 cut tariff-free flat-steel imports by about half, CBAM has been fully operational since 1 January 2026, and northern European hot-rolled coil has tested EUR 800 per tonne.
      • The brokers call the policy change structural
      • We call it a price made by policy, which can be unmade by policy.
    • Against the tailwind, automotive demand at about 50 percent of Steel Europe's volumes is weak and not turning, energy and hydrogen inputs are against the company, and German GDP is projected at 0.5 percent in 2026.
      • The external forces net to a tailwind this year and to a C as an attribute.
  • The change has travelled five of the six layers of the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain; the cash line is the layer it has not reached.
    • New people (a chief executive since June 2023, a new steel team), new choices (ACES 2030, Transform 30+), completed actions (two listings done or approved, the HKM exit, 4,000 of 11,000 cuts), key operating metrics (Steel Europe shipments up 9 percent in Q3 FY2026, gross margin up 1.0 point to 12.5 percent), the financial statements (Q3 adjusted EBIT EUR 183m against EUR 155m a year earlier; revenue up 7.8 percent) and the sell-side's estimates (FY2027 consensus earnings per share up 12.7 percent in six months) have all moved.
    • Free cash flow before M&A was EUR -1,940m in 9M FY2026 and is guided to EUR -600m to -300m for the year
      • Net cash fell from EUR 4,862m at the end of FY2025 to EUR 2,607m at 30 June 2026.
      • The story and the cash are moving in opposite directions, which is why 'Financial Outputs' are C and not B.
  • What we think it earns: earnings per share on the brokers' adjusted basis of about EUR -0.80 in FY2026 (a charge year), EUR 1.05 in FY2027, EUR 1.40 in FY2028, rising to about EUR 1.85 by FY2031, after the 49 percent minority leakage the tk accelis spin-off creates.
    • From the FY2025 base of EUR 0.750 that is 23 percent a year to FY2028, above the framework's 12 to 20 percent band because it starts in a trough
      • On the forward base the market prices, EUR 1.457 now to EUR 1.85 at the three-year horizon, it is 8.3 percent a year, below the band.
      • The honest statement is a trough-to-recovery rate above the band, then growth below it.
    • We sit below the FY2027 consensus of EUR 1.18 because no file states whether consensus deducts the tk accelis minority and we do
      • In line with consensus from FY2028 when the HKM savings arrive.
  • What it costs: EUR 13.695, or 9.4 times the 24-month-forward earnings consensus implies, the 88th percentile of the stock's own five-year range (median 6.58x) and the ten-year median band of 9.30x
    • The shares are up 65 percent year to date and 41.9 percent over twelve months.
    • The contracted catalysts have already moved the price.
      • Morgan Stanley says the shares trade in line with its sum-of-the-parts.
      • The re-rating leg has been earned by whoever owned the shares before us, so the modal case carries none.
  • The return against the bar fails at three years.
    • On the framework's valuation convention, a multiple on the earnings two years beyond each horizon, we apply 9.0x (a small de-rating from today's 9.4x for the mix shift toward steel) to EUR 1.68 at eighteen months and EUR 1.85 at three years, add EUR 1.80 and EUR 1.95 of cash returns (of which EUR 1.50 is the tk accelis distribution in kind), and reach EUR 16.92 (15.1 percent a year) and EUR 18.60 (10.7 percent a year).
    • The eighteen-month figure is a C and is carried by a one-off distribution
      • The three-year figure is a D and is the one the case must clear.
      • The price below which the case clears the bar on the modal path is EUR 10.76 at three years, near the 200-day average of EUR 11.27.
    • The conceded floor has an anchor: a completed break-up (tk accelis listed well, Steel Europe separated with its pensions allocated) moves the fair multiple to the ten-year 75th band of 11.5x, which gives 33.5 percent a year at eighteen months and 19.3 percent at three years.
      • We give that branch odds of 0.30, because the second condition has failed twice and has no date.
    • The drop-through sensitivity runs the other way: if EBITDA converts to net income at 32 percent rather than the 39.6 percent consensus mechanics imply, the three-year value including cash is EUR 13.56, a loss, and the 'Modal return' falls to F.
      • The chain below EBITDA is the least-guided part of this company, which is why the sensitivity is sized at a third of earnings.
  • The pattern it fits is 'Major Corporate Change', and the fit is crisp.
    • The mechanism is management and the regulator, not the business: a holding-company strategy executed through listings converts a conglomerate discount into separately priced parts, a labour agreement and a joint-venture exit convert a bottom-quartile cost base into a mid-cycle one, and a policy-made price lifts the revenue that cost base serves.
    • The 'Foundations' do not supply the mechanism, which is why the fit is crisp and the robustness is not.
  • Where the chain breaks: at the Steel Europe separation, which carries EUR 2.7bn of pensions and has no structure
    • At the drop-through from EBITDA to earnings per share, which no one guides
    • And at the cash line, which is moving the opposite way to the story.

  • What the case therefore is: real, dated and compelling as a story

    • Not compelling as an investment at EUR 13.695, because the price already carries consensus' 78 percent rise in EBITDA to FY2028 and the two contracted legs, and what is left to earn is the leg with the longest odds.

  • Rating = C, because the change is material in its inputs ('Change Forces' B) and has reached the sell-side's estimates, the sixth layer of the chain, but the outputs beyond the recovery year are modest ('Financial Outputs' C) and the return at EUR 13.695 is below the bar ('Modal return' D) because the price has already moved 65 percent this year on the contracted catalysts

    • The parts bind: the strongest Element cannot carry a return that fails the bar at three years.
    • Not B because the return fails
    • Not D because the change forces and the staircase of dated steps are real.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.

    • The strongest case against our neutral stance is the bull case in its strongest form.

    • The case against, stated at its strongest: the street under-models Steel Europe and the July 2026 safeguards are a structural change the market has not capitalised.

      • Deutsche Bank sits 2 to 23 percent above consensus adjusted EBIT for FY2026 to FY2028 and models Steel Europe EBITDA of EUR 1.5bn against the company's own EUR 1.2bn target and Morgan Stanley's EUR 1.13bn
        • The Capital Markets Day target itself is 27 percent above the Visible Alpha medium-term consensus of EUR 0.94bn.
      • Hot-rolled coil at EUR 800 per tonne with Europe short 10 to 15 million tonnes would, on Morgan Stanley's arithmetic, drive significant consensus EBITDA upgrades if the price holds.
      • The tk accelis listing crystallises EUR 1 to 3 per share above street expectations, the pension liability halves on a Steel Europe deconsolidation (Jefferies), and Morgan Stanley's bull-case sum-of-the-parts is EUR 24.70, 80 percent above the price.
    • What we concede: the policy change is the largest in European steel's competitive field in a decade and is not fully in the FY2027 estimates
      • More than half of the EUR 800m of Steel Europe self-help is contractual
      • The listing is dated and approved
      • And the fair multiple on a completed break-up is 11.5x, not 9.0x.
      • We have shown that return: 33.5 percent a year at eighteen months and 19.3 percent at three years.
    • Why we still hold our view: the two legs of the bull case that are contractual, the listing and the labour agreement, are already in a price that has risen 65 percent this year to the 88th percentile of its five-year multiple range.
      • The leg that would move the multiple to 11.5x, a separated Steel Europe with its pensions allocated away from the parent, has failed twice (EP Group handed back its 20 percent in late 2025; Jindal paused in May 2026 on valuation) and has no date, so we give it 0.40 even after a successful listing, and the branch 0.30 in all.
      • The chain from the brokers' EBITDA to earnings per share is unguided, and a third of the earnings can leak from it: the reported tax rate was 55.6 percent in FY2025, minorities take 49 percent of two listed subsidiaries and 49.8 percent of nucera, and special items ran at EUR 544m in nine months.
      • The cash line is moving the opposite way to the story: net cash down EUR 2.3bn in nine months.
    • The bear's case we also do not take: the risks research's "NO profile", a EUR 11.36bn structural burden exceeding the market capitalisation, sets gross liabilities against equity while ignoring EUR 2.6bn of net cash and the operating value of the listed parts, and its two hard inconsistencies, that the listing and the signed agreement would not happen, are exactly the things that have happened.
    • Where the case against would win: a stated ownership target, external funding and pension allocation for Steel Europe.
      • That is the single event that moves the multiple anchor to 11.5x and the 'Foundations' to C, and it is why our park is a park and not an invalidation.

Summary: Written Summary of the Investment Case (not rated) (E 3, BB 4)
The case in 8 to 10 sentences (the thesis as tested), kept in full at both ends of the record (Part 1, E 3, BB 4)
  • This is a self-help and break-up case in a weak business, a 'Major Corporate Change', not a demand inflection and not a compounder.
  • A chief executive in his fourth year, with a mandate to May 2031, is turning a loss-making conglomerate into a financial holding of listed parts: Marine Systems was listed in October 2025, 49 percent of the materials-distribution business is to be distributed to shareholders in late October 2026, and the flat-steel business is being cut by a third in headcount under a signed labour agreement, has shed its loss-making HKM joint venture, and is to be made standalone on a date and in a structure nobody has stated.

    • At EUR 13.70 the market has paid for the two parts already sold, and the third has no date.
