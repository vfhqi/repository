Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The massive scale of Beijer Ref's put and call option liabilities poses substantial cash outflow risks that could constrain future capital allocation and compress earnings per share unless underlying cash generation remains historically robust.

Defining the core research objective

1.
Investigating option liabilities: "Beijer Ref AB (BEIJ.B-SE), a Swedish-listed global wholesale distributor of refrigeration, air-conditioning and heating (HVAC-R) parts and equipment to professional installers, with its own manufacturing of natural-refrigerant systems (SCM Frigo, Fenagy, Refra); it reports three regions (EMEA, North America through the Heritage Distribution platform, Asia-Pacific) and three product segments (comfort cooling and heating, commercial and industrial refrigeration, own OEM manufacturing). What is the payment schedule, valuation method and expected cash outflow of Beijer Ref's (BEIJ.B-SE) put and call option liabilities for minority stakes in acquired companies (SEK 3,405m at end-2025, of which SEK 1,201m short-term; additions of SEK 107m for Idema in Q1 2026 and SEK 510m for two acquisitions in Q3 2026 payable in 2028 to 2031; further stakes in FG Europe, Refra and Enertrak), how much was settled in 2026, how do the brokers covering the company treat these liabilities in their EPS, net debt and valuation, and how do Lifco, Addtech, Indutrade and other Nordic acquirers treat comparable liabilities in their leverage definitions and disclosures?"

Significance of the liability structure

2.
Massive liabilities expand: Beijer Ref holds put and call option liabilities alongside earn-out obligations that expanded to SEK 3,405m at the end of 2025. This research examined 42 relevant sources and six companies, including peers Lifco, Addtech, Indutrade, Lagercrantz, and Röko, with none omitted from the analysis. The resolution of these obligations threatens to consume significant cash reserves and dilute future earnings per share if subsidiary performances exceed initial projections.
3.
Deferred payments escalate: Beijer Ref managed to secure several large acquisitions during 2026, adding SEK 107m in liabilities for Idema and SEK 510m for two Q3 acquisitions payable from 2028 to 2031. These rising future commitments represent a deferred cash drain that could constrain future capital allocation and raise borrowing costs. Because these obligations are tied to earnings multiples, strong performance at acquired firms directly increases the eventual cash outflow.

Specifically, Note 3 of the 2025 Annual Report shows that net debt in relation to EBITDA stood at 1.72x, which would be significantly higher if all put/call liabilities were added. This exclusion hides potential credit risks and inflates apparent financial flexibility during periods of heavy M&A activity.

Section 3 – Valuation Methods and Fair Value Calculations of Beijer Ref's Options

Section summary
  1. Beijer Ref calculates its Level 3 option liabilities by discounting probable cash flows, while reporting value adjustments directly in equity to avoid income statement volatility.

Beijer Ref's Valuation Framework

1.
Methodology: Beijer Ref applies a Level 3 valuation technique that discounts expected future cash flows using a risk-adjusted discount rate. These cash flows are derived from probable scenarios for future performance measures and their respective probabilities. This approach allows the group to align liability values with actual performance outcomes.
2.
Equity accounting: Beijer Ref records subsequent changes in the fair value of these put options directly against equity. The group treats these revaluations as transactions with owners rather than recognizing them as finance costs. This accounting choice shields the consolidated income statement from significant volatility.
3.
Contrary evidence: Beijer Ref recorded a substantial decrease of SEK 261 mm in the fair value of liabilities linked to acquisitions in its equity changes table during Q3 2026. This adjustment shows that option valuations can experience sharp downward revisions when performance metrics soften. Consequently, these liabilities are highly sensitive to short-term variations in subsidiary earnings.

Peer Valuation Mechanics

4.
Multiple method: Lifco measures its combined put and call option liabilities using contractually determined multiples ranging from 3.5x to 8.25x. These multiples are applied directly to the average EBITA or EBIT of the three preceding financial years. This structured multiple method provides greater formula-based certainty compared to Beijer Ref's discounted cash flow models.
5.
Sensitivity modeling: Röko discloses that a SEK 100 mm (calculated) increase in average subsidiary EBITA over three years would raise its option liability by SEK 191 mm (calculated). The company uses contractual terms, subsidiary earnings, and discount rates as core valuation inputs. This explicit sensitivity modeling improves balance sheet transparency for outside investors.
6.
Peer accounting: Addtech utilizes a cash flow-based valuation for its Level 3 put and call option liabilities, recording initial bookings and revaluations directly in equity. Similarly, Lagercrantz measures its put options at amortised cost with value changes recognized through equity. These peer practices confirm that equitybased revaluation is the standard among Nordic serial acquirers.
7.
Other side of the story: Although Beijer Ref's discounted cash flow model is more subjective than peers' rigid multiples, it prevents overpaying for cyclical earnings spikes. By incorporating multiple probability-weighted scenarios, the company ensures that option liabilities reflect long-term sustainable cash flows. This flexibility helps protect the balance sheet from overvalued minority buyouts.
