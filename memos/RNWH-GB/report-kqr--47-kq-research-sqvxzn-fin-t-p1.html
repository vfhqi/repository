EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Section summary
  1. The evidence shows that the Key Question on fixed-cost operating leverage and cost elimination feasibility can be partly answered now, with medium overall confidence supported by detailed company disclosures and peer precedents, though critical gaps remain around subcontractor margin splits and localized wage pass-through lags that prevent a complete assessment of Renew's earnings resilience during regulatory transitions.

The Key Question asks "how much fixed-cost operating leverage does this business model carry, how much scope is there for it to work over the next 18 months to four years, and, where it is relevant to the company's strategy, how feasible is cost elimination or reduction, both incremental and in material large cuts?" This question is critical to investors because Renew's earnings per share development is heavily influenced by how its operating margin responds to organic revenue growth fluctuations over the next 18 months to four years, particularly as the group navigates the slow start to Network Rail's CP7 renewals programme and the ramp-up of water sector AMP8 frameworks. Renew operates a directly employed workforce model with total staff costs of £304.44m in FY25 supporting 4,583 average monthly employees, including 2,689 operational delivery staff and 1,789 administrative employees, with approximately 5,000 accredited staff forming the core of essential maintenance and renewals delivery.

This high proportion of direct labor creates significant fixed-cost exposure during periods when clients defer capital spending.

Adjusted operating margins reduced 20 basis points to 6.5% in FY25 as organic revenues declined by 2.5% during the CP7 transition, a margin level lower than Balfour Beatty's 8% margin in Support Services, demonstrating how revenue deferrals directly erode profitability when directly employed crews become under-utilized. natural hedge against pure capital cycle volatility.

Half-year margins show significant seasonality with H1 FY26 at 5.7% compared to H2 FY25 at 7.3%, driven by winter weather events that generate record emergency response services, although lower-margin work patterns depress H1 margins. Peer precedents during regulatory transitions show mixed margin trajectories depending on contract terms and portfolio mix.

Galliford Try delivered a combined divisional operating margin of 3.2% in H1 FY26, up from 2.7% in H1 FY25, supported by strong commercial terms on new frameworks as the group began executing early AMP8 design works.

Similarly, Costain recorded a substantial operating margin of 7.9% within its Natural Resources segment in FY25 compared to 5.9% in FY24, driven by positive contract completions as AMP7 concluded alongside an improved mix of consultancy services.

In power transmission, Balfour Beatty Support Services achieved a strong profit from operations margin of 8.5% in FY25, up from 8.0% in FY24, benefiting from increased volumes in power transmission as national infrastructure investments under Ofgem frameworks accelerated. Major UK contractors have demonstrated meaningful cost reduction capabilities when restructuring operations.

Balfour Beatty's Build to Last strategy reduced operating expenses by almost 40%, while Kier Group's headcount reductions under Andrew Davies delivered £115 million of annualised savings and Costain identified over £320 million of cost savings across water projects.

However, these programmes typically required multi-year execution periods and were concentrated in simplifying corporate structures rather than cutting frontline operational capacity.

Rising employer costs pose near-term headwinds, as increased National Insurance contributions shave approximately 20 basis points from UK housebuilder and contractor profit margins, though modern public infrastructure procurement positions long-term frameworks as the default route and Balfour Beatty expanded its low-risk, target-cost or cost-reimbursable agreements to 83%, providing inflation protection mechanisms that mitigate margin erosion over time.

The most significant information gap concerns the exact ratio of subcontract to direct labor across individual Renew subsidiaries, which remains undisclosed in public company accounts, and the localized framework wage pass-through lag times for recovering construction wage inflation are not explicitly quantified in broker literature.

These gaps prevent precise quantification of how much operating leverage is fixed versus variable and how quickly cost inflation can be recovered through framework indexation mechanisms.

If operating leverage proves materially higher than the market assumes, budget delays by Network Rail would trigger disproportionately large downward revisions to Renew's consensus EBIT of £77.7m, as direct-delivery crews under-utilized due to spending pauses cannot be cut quickly due to the group's decentralized structure.

The competitive landscape is evolving rapidly, with the completed acquisition of Telent by CVC-backed private equity competitor M Group Services creating an infrastructure giant with pro forma revenues exceeding £3 billion, directly threatening Renew's market share in transport and energy where clients increasingly demand integrated digital solutions.

Unexpected changes in direct delivery headcount serve as an early indicator that operating margins are diverging from the common market view, making workforce disclosures in upcoming trading updates critical early-warning indicators of whether the business model's fixed-cost base is being actively managed or whether under-utilization is persisting longer than anticipated. summary, Renew's operating leverage is demonstrably higher than a pure subcontractor model due to its 5,000- strong directly employed workforce, creating vulnerability during regulatory transition periods when client spending is deferred, but the decentralized structure and entrepreneurial subsidiary model make rapid, material cost cuts structurally difficult without damaging the specialized capabilities and client relationships that underpin long-term framework awards.

The path to answering the Key Question more completely requires granular disclosure of subcontractor ratios and close monitoring of direct employment trends over the next two to four reporting periods as CP7 and AMP8 spending patterns normalize.

Section 3 – Decentralized Overhead and Acquired Assets Restructuring

Section summary
  1. Renew's decentralized multi-brand model delivers high operational autonomy through independent subsidiaries, making central overhead highly lean but rendering subsidiary-level costs structurally un-cuttable without damaging delivery capabilities.
  2. The group has rapidly expanded its higher-margin Energy and Water capabilities through six major acquisitions since 2024, deploying capital at attractive entry multiples to build an integrated grid and marine engineering services offering.
  3. Restructuring efforts within Full Circle's underperforming French wind services subsidiary highlight the integration and execution risks of international expansion, acting as a near-term drag on consolidated margins.
  4. Peer performance during regulatory transitions is heavily driven by framework terms and bidding discipline.
  5. Contractors like Costain and Galliford Try experienced temporary margin expansion or flattening revenues as they navigated water AMP transitions.
  6. In the rail and power segments, major players maintained robust margins through selective bidding and cost-reimbursable contract structures.

Decentralized Structure and Operational Autonomy

1.
Autonomous operating model: Renew operates as a decentralized holding company that grants high operational autonomy to its ten or more independently branded subsidiaries, including AmcoGiffen, Carnell, and Clarke Telecom.
2.
Central holding role: The central holding company focuses strictly on providing strategic oversight, financial support, and overall standards, rather than managing day-to-day delivery.
3.
Entrepreneurial risk mitigation: This decentralized model empowers individual subsidiary management teams to respond rapidly to local market disruptions, which serves as a core mechanism for mitigating operational and regulatory risks.
4.
Corporate overhead efficiency: Because the administrative burden is pushed down to the operating brands, the central corporate overhead remains extremely lean relative to the group's scale.
5.
Under-utilization cost trap: While this structure keeps central corporate costs low, it means that subsidiary- level overheads cannot be easily cut or consolidated without disrupting the specialized brand identity and customer relationships that underpin the framework agreements.
6.
Collaboration over consolidation: Rather than pursuing cost-cutting centralizations, Renew focuses on driving organic growth by encouraging inter-subsidiary collaboration to secure larger, multi-disciplinary frameworks.
