Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Understanding the reconciliation bridge between reported metrics and statutory cash flows is critical for evaluating Zegona's deleveraging speed and dividend capacity.

Background and Context

1.
Investigating the cash bridge: "Zegona Communications plc (ZEG-GB), a London-listed holding company whose only operating asset is Vodafone Spain, Spain's third-largest integrated mobile and fixed-line operator, with consumer (Vodafone and Lowi brands), business and wholesale (including Finetwork and Onivia) segments, renting its fixed network from the PremiumFiber and FiberPass joint ventures and its towers mainly from Vantage Towers, Cellnex and American Tower. For Zegona Communications (ZEG-GB), how does the bridge from reported EBITDAaL to statutory earnings per share and to equity free cash flow work, item by item?
2.
Specifically: (a) of the €452.6m of intangible amortisation in FY26 (company accounts), how much relates to the intangibles recognised in the May 2024 purchase-price allocation (customer relationships, brands, spectrum and any acquired software) and how much to software and capitalised customer-acquisition costs; what useful life applies to each acquired intangible; and what is the resulting acquired-amortisation schedule for FY27-FY31; (b) in which income-statement line is the release of the €2,012m of PremiumFiber and FiberPass contract liabilities recognised (€60m in FY26), is it included in reported EBITDAaL and operating free cash flow, and how large is the release expected to be each year; (c) what items (lease principal and interest, restructuring, spectrum payments, working capital, the contract-liability reversal, cash tax) made up the difference between operating free cash flow (€763m) and equity free cash flow in FY26 and in the half-year to September 2026; and (d) how do Morgan Stanley, Deutsche Bank, Santander, Goldman Sachs, Barclays, CaixaBank BPI, Canaccord Genuity, JB Capital, Bestinver and New Street each treat these items in their EBITDA, depreciation and amortisation, earnings-pershare and free cash flow forecasts for FY27-FY30?"
3.
Strategic acquisition structure: Zegona completed the purchase of Vodafone Spain in May 2024 for an enterprise value of €5 billion to pursue its classic buy-fix-sell strategy. The acquisition was funded with €4.1 billion in cash and €0.9 billion in redeemable preference shares issued to Vodafone Group. This asset remains the sole operating segment of the company, making its standalone performance and cash flow the sole driver of investor returns going forward.
4.
Equity payout policy: Zegona's updated capital allocation policy announced on 24 September 2026 commits to return all free cash flow to shareholders. This makes understanding the exact bridge between EBITDAaL and cash flow critical, since distributions will be highly sensitive to working capital shifts and lease liabilities. Consequently, any variation in the cash bridge items will directly impact dividend yields and share buybacks.

Transmission Channels and Cash Reconciliation

5.
Non-cash accounting drags: The massive non-cash purchase price allocation amortisation heavily depresses Zegona's statutory earnings per share while leaving cash flows unaffected. In its FY25 accounts, Zegona reported a consolidated net loss of €439 million primarily driven by €755 million of total depreciation and amortisation. Thus, statutory EPS remains a poor indicator of dividend capability, making the reconciliation to equity free cash flow essential for valuation.

Zegona pays approximately €130 million to €140 million annually to Vantage Towers under a long-term agreement. Because these payments are deducted to calculate EBITDAaL, lease renegotiations represent the primary operational lever for de-leveraging and growing free cash flow.

Therefore, Zegona's threat to leave Vantage Towers might be tactical leverage rather than operational reality, risking expected savings.

This accounting treatment means reported profitability may diverge from cash generation, demanding close analytical scrutiny.
9.
Other side of the story: While returning all cash flow to shareholders is Zegona's primary target, deleveraging could take precedence if competitive pressures from rivals like Digi Spain intensify. In its capital distribution plan of September 2026, Zegona set a leverage target of 2.25x to 2.75x net debt to EBITDAaL. If revenues soften under pricing pressure, cash flow will be diverted to debt reduction, limiting the actual distributed cash and undermining the high-yield bull thesis.
