EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Materiality of Change Ambitious Project Horizon targets €50 to €60 million annual gross savings against the 2025 baseline, with €17 million achieved by June 2026, demonstrating operational momentum despite persistent construction market weakness across Europe and North America. The portfolio rotation through paper asset divestment to Flacks Group triggered a €301.4 million non-cash translation loss in 2024, though the transaction strategically exits cyclical low-margin exposures. French lithium ambitions advanced through €50 million state equity from Banque des Territoires in April 2026 for the EMILI project, while the UK British Lithium project was mothballed with €28.5 million impairment, reflecting capital reallocation toward the more advanced French asset. Board downsizing to eight members in May 2026 concentrated GBL influence on governance, introducing capital allocation risk despite maintaining 62.5% independent director representation.

Key Forward Drivers Ranked by Impact

European residential construction recovery represents the highest-impact driver at approximately 15% EPS sensitivity, as construction and infrastructure account for 38% of group revenues, though recovery timing remains delayed until late-2026 or 2027 per sell-side commentary. Project Horizon cost run-rate delivers 10% EPS impact with €50 to €60 million annual savings expected at full run-rate in 2027, offering self-help margin protection independent of external demand cycles.

TQC high-purity quartz demand contributes 8% EPS sensitivity, rebounding to 50.6% EBITDA margins in H1 2026 after solar value chain normalization, with dividend restart prospects supporting cash generation. Conductive additives and EV sales drive 6% EPS impact through Graphite and Carbon segment growth of 13.6% in H1 2026, anchored by robust Chinese and European battery demand. USD/EUR currency fluctuations represent 5% EPS sensitivity, with negative 2.7% H1 2026 sales impact from US dollar depreciation, creating translation headwinds on positive net USD positions.

EMILI lithium project milestones carry 5% EPS optionality at 34,000 tons annual capacity, backed by €50 million state equity but requiring €1 billion total construction capex, introducing execution and financing risks.

Recent Earnings Delivery and Quality H1 2026 reported revenue of €1,740 million declined 0.9% while constant currency sales grew 1.8%, with adjusted EBITDA expanding 10% to €290 million, reflecting pricing discipline and cost-out momentum offsetting volume pressure. Organic volume increased 0.5% supported by 1.0% pricing, with Project Horizon delivering €17 million savings against €30 million restructuring charges, demonstrating execution credibility.

Net current free operating cash flow surged to €109 million from €44 million, though inflated by €45 million definition change replacing ROU additions with lease repayments, yielding €63 million under prior methodology. Net financial debt rose to €1,468 million with leverage stable at 2.6x adjusted EBITDA, maintaining covenant headroom but reflecting lithium development capital intensity. FY 2025 GAAP losses of €409 million included €469 million goodwill impairment, indicating portfolio cleaning costs obscure underlying operational resilience.

Forward Guidance Credibility Management acts as honest tracker, delivering FY 2025 EBITDA of €546 million within €540 to €580 million guidance after meeting FY 2024 and FY 2023 ranges with variance under 3% from midpoints. FY 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance of €550 to €580 million remains confirmed, with H1 EBITDA of €290 million tracking toward €565 million midpoint supported by Horizon savings acceleration. Outliers worth noting

The deep-dive research into Imerys revealed several statistical outliers and unexplained oddities that warrant attention. The Quartz Corporation joint venture achieved an exceptionally high adjusted EBITDA margin of 50.6% in H1 2026, a figure that stands dramatically above typical specialty minerals benchmarks and even exceeds the group's own historical performance in this asset. This margin level, while attributed to favorable one-off sales, represents a significant deviation from the prior year when TQC's full-year 2025 revenue fell 50% to €167 million (100% basis) due to high solar inventories in Asia. The swing from severe demand disruption to exceptional profitability within a single year raises questions about the sustainability and underlying drivers of this outlier performance. The disposal of paper market assets triggered an unusually large negative €301.4 million non-cash loss in 2024, stemming from the recycling of historical Brazilian Real translation reserves. This accounting treatment created a substantial one-time GAAP loss that materially distorted reported earnings, yet the transaction itself was valued at only €146.5 million with payments spread over time based on performance.

The magnitude of the accounting charge relative to the transaction value represents an atypical outcome that complicates historical earnings trajectory analysis and peer comparisons.

The Board of Directors reduced its size to eight members in May 2026, with controlling shareholder GBL adjusting its representation from three to two board seats. Despite this reduction, the board maintains 62.5% independent directors, exceeding the AFEP-MEDEF recommended minimum of 33% for controlled companies. The combination of an unusually small board size for a company of Imerys' scale alongside an exceptionally high independence ratio creates a governance structure that deviates from typical controlled-company configurations, where larger boards with proportionally lower independence are more common.

The change in cash flow reporting methodology in 2026 to replace right-of-use additions with lease repayments inflated reported H1 cash generation by €45 million, meaning that under the previous method, net current free operating cash was €63 million rather than the reported €109 million. This definitional shift introduced a significant year-over-year comparability distortion at a critical juncture when the company is emphasizing improved cash generation as evidence of operational recovery.

The timing and magnitude of this accounting policy change warrants scrutiny, as it obscures the underlying trend in operating cash performance during a period of intensive restructuring spending. Citations INTRM
