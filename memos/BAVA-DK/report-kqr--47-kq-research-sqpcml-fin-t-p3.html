Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Understanding the feedback loops in Bavarian Nordic's dual-segment business model is critical for evaluating its mid-term earnings per share trajectory.
  2. This analysis assesses the self-reinforcing dynamics of travel vaccine commercialisation against the lumpy and competitive risks of government biodefense stockpiles.

Defining the Investment Core

1.
Critical inquiry: "Bavarian Nordic A/S (BAVA-DK), a Danish specialty vaccine maker with two segments: Public Preparedness (the smallpox and mpox vaccine JYNNEOS / Imvanex / Imvamune and related work, sold mainly to governments such as US BARDA, France and the European Commission's HERA) and Travel Health (Rabipur/RabAvert rabies, Encepur tick-borne encephalitis, Vimkunya chikungunya, Vivotif typhoid and Vaxchora cholera, sold mainly out of pocket in the US and Europe), with bulk manufacturing at Kvistgaard (Denmark) and a second site in Bern (Switzerland). Key question: which reinforcing or balancing feedback loops are at work in this business model, such that dynamics at play today create additional scope for further positive or negative dynamics in the near and medium term (reinforcing feedback loops), or the opposite (balancing loops)?"
2.
Financial volatility: Bavarian Nordic's earnings per share face substantial volatility because the high-margin Public Preparedness segment is structurally subject to lumpy government biodefense orders. Successful integration of travel health assets is designed to dilute this historical biodefense reliance and stabilize cash flows. Investors are closely monitoring how these reinforcing commercial loops will drive mid-term profitability.
3.
Re-rating potential: DNB Carnegie highlights that the long-term investment case has strengthened due to recent mpox outbreaks, which could shift the normalized Public Preparedness run rate upward. However, downside risks include the failure of expected biodefense contracts to materialize or slower travel vaccine growth. Resolving these dynamics will determine whether the company's valuation discount narrowing becomes a permanent trend over the next three years.
4.
Systematic scope: Bavarian Nordic's corporate strategy historically relied on a narrow biodefense relationship with a single government customer. This report evaluates 102 sources, eight peer companies, and four comparable past cases to assess if M&A and manufacturing integration will drive stable profitability. Resolving these strategic feedback loops is essential to understand future earnings growth.

Section 2 – Stockpile Replacement Cycles and JYNNEOS Shelf Life

Section summary
  1. The extended shelf life of freeze-dried JYNNEOS creates a negative balancing feedback loop that threatens to lengthen government replacement intervals.
  2. To counteract this, Bavarian Nordic is shifting toward multi-year delivery agreements to stabilize Public Preparedness revenues.

Freeze-Dried Dynamics and

1.
Balancing Loops Improved logistics: Bavarian Nordic received FDA approval for its freeze-dried formulation of JYNNEOS in March 2025. This next-generation vaccine provides significant advantages in storage and transport over the older liquid-frozen version. Consequently, these logistical improvements make the freeze-dried dose ideally suited for long-term stockpiling by global governments.
2.
Extended intervals: The enhanced stability of the freeze-dried formulation introduces a negative balancing loop that could delay government replacement orders over the mid-term. Because the new doses last longer, stockpiles will require less frequent replenishment than the previous liquid-frozen inventory. This shift could damp future order frequency and compress Bavarian Nordic's Public Preparedness margins unless offset by new customers.
3.
Strategic smoothing: Bavarian Nordic is actively mitigating this balancing loop by securing long-term supply agreements to smooth its Public Preparedness revenues. For instance, the company announced a four-year contract with the UK Health Security Agency in November 2026 to ensure scheduled deliveries. This multi-year framework helps stabilize highly volatile preparedness cash flows over the 2027-2029 period.
4.
Contrary evidence: Jefferies Research highlights that potential private market sales and ex-US stockpiling discussions could offset any domestic procurement slowdown. Specifically, the US private market for mpox opened in late 2024, presenting an estimated DKK 1,800 million incremental revenue opportunity over four years. This commercial expansion demonstrates that the stockpiling business is less reliant on binary federal decisions than historically assumed.
