Section 3 – Buying-Group Dynamics and Consolidation Threats

Section summary
  1. VetFamily's rapid membership expansion drives stronger purchasing scale but faces headwinds from corporate consolidation in mature veterinary markets.
  2. Alternative platforms like Covetrus and Chewy are building integrated e-commerce and clinic networks that could bypass traditional buying groups.
  3. Independent clinic availability remains a critical constraint as corporate consolidation shrinks the long-term addressable market.

Membership Growth and

1.
Scale Benefits Steady expansion: Vimian's buying group VetFamily has demonstrated consistent membership growth to strengthen its global purchasing scale. The platform expanded its member base from 10,900 clinics at the end of 2025 to 12,400 clinics by the end of the third quarter of 2026. This membership expansion was heavily supported by officially entering the Portugal and Japan markets during 2026. Management expects this geographic broadening to sustain organic revenue growth and expand commercial scale.
2.
Purchasing scale: Large veterinary buying groups leverage their collective membership to negotiate better commercial terms directly with pharmaceutical suppliers. Industry experts observe that corporate groups and GPOs improve profitability primarily by securing improved supplier terms on medicines. This bulk purchasing power allows smaller independent practices to access competitive pricing that they could not negotiate on their own. Consequently, these rebates help independent clinics maintain their profit margins against larger corporate competitors.
3.
Member retention: Independent clinics increasingly join buying groups like VetFamily to professionalize their operations and lower overhead costs. VetFamily provides member clinics with critical services including procurement, marketing, and digitalization tools. These services help independent clinics remain competitive in a rapidly consolidating market. This comprehensive service platform strengthens member clinic retention over time.

Consolidation Threats in

4.
Mature Markets Shrinking pool: The rapid consolidation of independent clinics by private equity and large corporate groups poses a direct structural threat to VetFamily. DNB Carnegie notes that continued consolidation of freestanding clinics could have a significant negative impact on Vimian's long-term growth. When corporate groups acquire independent practices, those practices typically exit independent buying groups. This consolidation dynamic permanently shrinks the pool of available independent clinics in mature regions.
5.
Consolidation precedents: Mature markets demonstrate how corporate consolidators can absorb the vast majority of independent veterinary clinics. In the United Kingdom, corporate groups have consolidated approximately 60% of the veterinary practice market. CVS Group represents a major consolidator in this space, holding approximately an 8% to 9% market share by number of clinics. This high level of corporate ownership significantly limits the target addressable market for independent GPOs.
6.
Scale comparison: Corporate groups leverage internal scale and vertically integrated services to bypass traditional buying networks entirely. CVS Group utilizes its internal Vet Direct purchasing division alongside its own diagnostic laboratories to supply its clinics. Similarly, IVC Evidensia operates over 2,500 clinics across 20 countries, leveraging massive corporate scale to optimize procurement. These corporate networks do not rely on GPOs, which highlights the risk of independent clinic pool erosion.

Contrary Evidence and

7.

Industry consultants report that pet owners are deliberately seeking out independent practices due to rising dissatisfaction with corporate clinic upselling. Additionally, the UK Competition and Markets Authority concluded its veterinary market investigation in March 2026, forcing corporate groups to implement strict transparency remedies. These regulatory interventions and shifting consumer preferences are helping local independent practices defend their market share.
8.
Independent demand: Many veterinary clinic owners actively resist corporate buyouts in order to preserve clinical autonomy and local community trust. Jefferies highlights that many clinic owners refuse to sell to corporate chains but still want to benefit from belonging to a professional community. This professional need drives steady demand for GPO partnerships. Consequently, buying groups like VetFamily are able to recruit members who prioritize clinical independence.

Platform Bypass and

9.
E-Commerce Threats Software integration: Digital veterinary platforms are successfully routing clinic purchasing away from traditional buying groups by integrating procurement software directly with online pharmacies. The Covetrus VetSuite program has surpassed 4,000 member practices and delivered $150m in cumulative benefits to independent clinics. VetSuite achieves this bypass by combining practice management software with automated online pharmacy fulfillment. This tight technological integration allows clinics to optimize inventory and secure supplier rebates without traditional GPOs.
10.
Distribution consolidation: Strategic mergers among major veterinary distributors are creating dominant logistics networks that limit GPO purchasing flexibility. Covetrus and MWI Animal Health completed a major merger in late 2026. This transaction consolidated a massive portion of the veterinary supply chain. Veterinarians expect this consolidation to reduce purchasing flexibility and increase prices as distributor options shrink. This consolidated distributor power threatens to squeeze GPO negotiating margins.
11.
Retailer expansion: Large consumer e-commerce platforms are establishing physical veterinary networks to route prescription and supply purchasing into their own digital ecosystems. Chewy completed the acquisition of Modern Animal for $400m in May 2026 to accelerate its healthcare footprint. 110 111 114 10Q This acquisition positions Chewy to scale its clinic footprint to approximately 60 clinics exiting 2026. Chewy integrates these clinics directly with its proprietary online pharmacy and Autoship platforms. This integrated digital model completely bypasses traditional distributor and buying group networks.

Direct procurement: Pharmaceutical manufacturers are increasingly selling directly to major e-commerce platforms, undermining the pricing advantages of buying groups. Major manufacturers have established direct supply relationships with online retailers like Chewy. This direct channel allows online pharmacies to capture approximately half of online prescription customers. Because veterinarians are increasingly writing portable prescriptions for online fulfillment, clinics are losing high-margin product sales. This volume loss reduces the overall purchasing scale that GPOs can aggregate.
13.
Other side of the story: While VetFamily has successfully expanded its global membership, the direct commercial benefits of this scale may be diluted by aggressive platform bypass from tech-enabled distributors and online pharmacies. Jefferies observes that independent clinic margins are under structural pressure from e-commerce erosion of pharmaceutical sales. If platforms like Covetrus and Chewy capture a greater share of clinic procurement and prescription fulfillment, VetFamily's GPO scale advantages will diminish. Under these conditions, VetFamily's ability to drive high-margin partner agreements and support Vimian's overall EPS growth will be severely constrained.
