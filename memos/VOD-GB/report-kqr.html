Section 4 – Working capital dynamics and reverse factoring 17 findings

Section summary
  1. Working capital represents a manageable seasonal swing factor rather than a structural drag, supported by tightly controlled reverse factoring and manageable spectrum schedules.

Reverse factoring transparency and extended supplier terms

1.
Controlled supply chain financing footprint: Vodafone operates an extensive supply chain financing scheme with €1,983m of invoices registered and €1,312m early drawn by suppliers as of March 2026, down from €2,198m registered and €1,443m drawn in FY25
2.
Limited extended payment terms: The group does not utilize supply chain financing to extend payment terms beyond customary industry norms, except for a modest €128m of trade payables extended from 30 to 90 days via reverse factoring
3.
Orderly reduction in extended terms: Extended reverse factoring payables declined by €20m year-on-year from €148m in FY25 to €128m in FY26, demonstrating that Vodafone is not expanding off-balance-sheet supplier financing to artificially bolster operating cash flow
4.
Strict IFRS trade payable classification: Independent auditor reviews confirm that none of Vodafone's supply chain financing arrangements meet the criteria for reclassification as bank borrowings, as contractual payment terms remain strictly below the 180-day accounting threshold
5.
Underlying supplier settlement profile: The majority of non-supply-chain-financing invoices are paid within 60 days, while supply-chain-financing invoices are cash-settled under standard 90-day terms, preserving healthy working relationship parameters across the vendor base

Spectrum license liabilities and scheduled cash outlays

6.
Predictable annual spectrum outflow schedule: Spectrum license payables entail a steady cash drain of €215m annually for the next four years (FY27 through FY30), before stepping down sharply to €89m in year five and €60m thereafter

Manageable spectrum cash drain relative to peers: Total discrete spectrum cash payments amortized to €404m in FY26 (versus €421m in FY25), representing a predictable c.3.5% hit to reported operating cash generation
8.
Emerging market spectrum installment phasing: Specific market auctions have been structured into multi-year installment plans, such as Türkiye's 5G auction where the €539m total cost is split into three equal €180m installments paid across 2026, 2027, and 2028

B2B digital mix shift and group cash conversion drag
