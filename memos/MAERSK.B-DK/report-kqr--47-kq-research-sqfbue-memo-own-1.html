Section 2 – Cycle Turns and Lead-Lag Dynamics (2015-2016, 2022-2023, 2024-2026)

Section summary
  1. Historical shipping cycles demonstrate that Maersk's share price typically acts as a leading indicator of freight rates and earnings turns.
  2. Structural disruptions such as the Red Sea crisis have extended peak freight levels and shifted traditional lead-lag dynamics in the 2024-2026 cycle.
  3. Maersk's price-to-book multiple historically floors at 0.40x during deep shipping downturns.
  4. Peer carriers display highly divergent valuation ceilings, with Hapag-Lloyd maintaining a structural multiple premium over Asian pure-plays.
  5. Massive fleet growth in the current cycle limits the likelihood of multiples returning to pre-pandemic historical averages.

Timing dynamics in the 2015-2016 downturn

1.
Early peak: Maersk experienced a substantial valuation contraction during the 2015-2016 shipping downturn as global capacity expanded rapidly. The company B share price reached its peak of DKK 16,410 on 30 March 2015 before falling to its trough of DKK 7,410 on 11 February 2016. This share price peak led the reported quarterly earnings trough by nearly two years as the group reported deep underlying losses in late 2016. Thus, the stock market quickly anticipated the severe structural oversupply before the financial downturn fully materialized.
2.
Delayed recovery: Maersk average freight rates demonstrated a delayed recovery following the 2015-2016 turn due to a high exposure to weaker trade lanes. During late 2016, the spot-weighted Shanghai Containerized Freight Index rose sequentially but the company reported weaker overall pricing due to loss-making long-term contract rates. These long-term contracts represented about 40% to 60% of total volume during the cycle turn. Consequently, the container liner experienced a one month revenue recognition lag after the physical voyages concluded.
3.
Contrary evidence: Maersk's average realized freight rates decoupled from the rising China Containerized Freight Index during the latter half of 2016 because of weak pricing across North-South trade lanes. While the main Chinese spot indices continued to rise sequentially, the group reported softer freight rates due to the cascading of excess vessel capacity into regional trades. This regional divergence meant that the broader spot shipping indices failed to serve as accurate proxies for the group's actual revenue performance. This decoupling suggests that index-based lead times can be highly misleading during localized fleet reallocations.
4.
Normalizing cycle: Maersk experienced a prolonged destocking drag that extended into the second half of 2023 and depressed underlying profitability. The Shanghai Containerized Freight Index declined about 81% from its record peak to November 2023, while the China Containerized Freight Index fell 76%. This severe rate erosion put massive pressure on the group's forward earnings estimates. Consequently, sell-side analysts were forced to slash corporate earnings expectations repeatedly through the end of 2023.

Turning points in the 2022-2023 normalization

5.
Accelerated peak: Maersk entered the post-pandemic normalization phase after experiencing record-high shipping rates and equity valuations in early 2022. The group B share price peaked at DKK 21,500 in January 2022, aligning closely with the China Containerized Freight Index peak of 3,588 points. The peak in the share price occurred almost concurrently with the peak in freight rates as global supply chains remained severely congested. This alignment indicates that equity markets treated the shipping peak as a coincident freight market indicator rather than a leading signal.
6.
Pricing correction: Maersk experienced a massive revenue contraction throughout 2023 as pandemic-era port congestion resolved and freight rates corrected. The company B share price troughed at approximately DKK 10,000 in November 2023, while the China Containerized Freight Index reached its bottom of 811 points in October 2023. Reported earnings and cash flows reached their quarterly trough in early 2024 when the company reported highly conservative outlooks. This timing showed the share price bottom lagged the contract freight index by only one month.
7.
Recognition delay: Maersk reported that its quarterly earnings typically lag changes in spot freight indices by approximately one month. The company recognizes shipping revenues only after a specific voyage has ended, which delays the financial impact of sudden rate spikes. This structural delay explains why reported quarterly earnings troughs often lag share price turning points by several months during rapid cycle transitions. Therefore, investors must look past reported accounting figures to gauge real-time market turns accurately.
8.
Index volatility: Maersk valuations are heavily influenced by global freight benchmark movements including the Drewry World Container Index. The Drewry composite index has historically shown sharp gains during peak shipping seasons as liners implement general rate increases. However, these spot indices tend to be highly volatile and can drop rapidly when frontloading activity ceases. As a result, the share price often experiences sharp valuation adjustments when these spot benchmarks begin to turn.

Dynamic shifts in the 2024-2026 disruption cycle

9.
Rapid escalation: Maersk benefited from another massive freight rate spike in 2026 as intense port congestion and early peak-season demand restricted effective vessel capacity. The Shanghai Containerized Freight Index reached its 2026 peak of 3,590 points in early September 2026, which was nearly three times its February 2026 trough. This sudden capacity squeeze drove freight rates back to the elevated levels seen during the historical Red Sea crisis. Thus, spot market metrics surged well ahead of long-term contract negotiations.
10.
Valuation peak: Maersk experienced a significant share price rally in late 2026 as spot freight indices surged to multi-year highs. The company B share price peaked near DKK 24,900 in late 2026, representing a substantial gain from its 52-week trough of DKK 12,255. This peak was closely aligned with the seasonal high points of the Shanghai Containerized Freight Index in September 2026. Therefore, stock market movements continued to track short-term spot freight volatility closely.
11.
Forecast revisions: Maersk witnessed positive revisions to sell-side consensus estimates during the latter half of 2026 as sustained shipping congestion supported elevated spot rates. Analysts upgraded the company's fullyear earnings expectations as the downturn was pushed out into subsequent years. These upgraded projections aligned with the company's own guidance updates that raised expected underlying EBITDA to a range of $14 bn to $14.5 bn. Consequently, the sell-side consensus peak coincided with the physical rate peak in September 2026.
12.
Temporary benefit: Maersk experienced a peak in its mark-to-market earnings during the third quarter of 2026 as disruption-led rates began to soften on major trade lanes. Although spot rates remained elevated compared to pre-conflict averages, transpacific and Middle East routes carried the majority of the headline rate increases while Asia-Europe routes softened. This geographical divergence indicates that the company faced highly volatile operational conditions. As a result, equity valuations remained highly vulnerable to inevitable freight rate normalization in 2027.
13.
Concurrent bottoming: Maersk experienced its lowest quarterly earnings of the recent cycle during the first quarter of 2026 when underlying profit fell to $171 mm. This financial bottom was closely mirrored by the stock market as the B share price touched its lowest levels of the year during the same quarter. This concurrent bottoming indicates that there was no lead time between equity market pricing and reported corporate financials. Consequently, investors reacted to immediate spot rate declines rather than projecting subsequent operational recoveries.
14.
Guidance momentum: Maersk repeatedly adjusted its full-year 2026 financial outlook upward on the back of resilient container demand and prolonged port congestion. The group raised its underlying EBITDA guidance from an initial range of $4.5 bn to $7 bn in May 2026 to a final range of $14 bn to $14.5 bn in November 2026. Fleet bottlenecks: Maersk faced severe fleet constraints during the peak of the 2026 shipping cycle due to high utilization rates. The company reported an average ocean utilization rate of 96% during the third quarter of 2026, which limited its operational flexibility. These capacity bottlenecks prevented the carrier from fully capturing the financial benefits of surging spot rates. Consequently, the ocean segment missed consensus estimates and highlighted why capacity limits can cap earnings during cyclical peaks.
16.
Other side of the story: Maersk's historical lead-lag relationships may be permanently disrupted by its strategic transformation into an integrated container logistics provider. While historical share price movements closely tracked spot freight indices with a clear leading relationship, the expansion of the Logistics & Services and Terminals segments is designed to buffer group earnings from ocean freight volatility. If these land-based business segments achieve stable contract margins, the share price could decouple from volatile shipping indices in future cycles.

Container shipping peer dynamics

High correlation: Maersk's stock price continues to trade in close correlation with listed container peers like Hapag-Lloyd and COSCO Shipping. DNB Carnegie reported in October 2026 that major container lines share core industry drivers such as global supply and demand. These factors mean that even with Maersk's integrated logistics push, its trading beta remains heavily tied to the broader container shipping industry cycles and freight rates. Consequently, its near-term earnings per share are still dictated by ocean freight market conditions.
2.
Peer consolidation: Hapag-Lloyd's proposed acquisition of ZIM at USD 35 per share highlights the tight strategic alignment and consolidated trading beta within the container shipping sector. Jefferies reported in November 2025 that this transaction implies a high valuation multiple of 5.9x EV/EBITDA for ZIM. This potential deal demonstrates that container carriers remain focused on scaling their core maritime networks rather than fully diversifying. Thus, the trading correlations among container peers remain high as they cooperatively manage global vessel capacity and alliances.

Quantitative gap: Statistical calculations for the precise rolling six-month correlation and beta of Maersk to COSCO, Evergreen, and HMM are not explicitly published in current broker databases. Logistics and forwarding correlations

4.
Strategic shift: Maersk is actively attempting to reshape its market beta by expanding its Logistics & Services segment to resemble asset-light forwarding giants like DSV. DNB Carnegie reported in October 2026 that growing land-based logistics exposure seeks to partly insulate the group from shipping cyclicality. However, because forwarding and solutions segments command much higher trading multiples, Maersk's transition is highly complex. As a result, its trading profile continues to align more with volatile ocean liners than with stable logistics operators.
5.
Asset-light stability: Forwarding giants such as Kuehne + Nagel and DSV trade on completely different capital- efficiency metrics compared to integrated container liners. Morgan Stanley noted in October 2026 that Kuehne + Nagel maintains a highly resilient gross profit model that is less sensitive to asset overcapacity. This resilience indicates that pure-play logistics companies possess a structurally lower trading beta relative to macro-driven ocean carriers. Consequently, Maersk's land-based earnings are unlikely to dominate its stock price movement in the near term.
6.
Forwarder valuation: DSV's focus on capital allocation and cash distribution through share buybacks supports a premium valuation that Maersk has struggled to achieve. DNB Carnegie outlined in October 2026 that DSV's asset-light model yields exceptionally strong cash flows from ocean and air volumes. This financial performance allows the forwarder to trade at a premium to integrated carriers. Therefore, Maersk's stock price remains highly idiosyncratic as it attempts to bridge the massive valuation gap with forwarding peers.
