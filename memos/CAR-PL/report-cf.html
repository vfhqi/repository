EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Inter Cars S.A. presents a compelling regional consolidation thesis anchored by best-in-class organic growth of 12% in Euro terms during Q1 2026, substantially outperforming legacy Western European automotive aftermarket distributors who face structural headwinds from ERP migration failures and chronic market share erosion. The distributor achieved 12% organic revenue expansion in Euro terms during Q1 2026, outperforming its closest European sector peers.

The company operates across 21 European markets and has pivoted strategically from domestic Polish concentration toward higher-margin Central and Eastern European territories, where foreign subsidiaries now represent 57.1% of consolidated sales as of Q1 2026. Inter Cars actively operates across 21 European markets, and foreign distribution subsidiaries increased their sales share to 57.1% in Q1 2026. The investment case rests on the intersection of structural European fleet aging dynamics, independent workshop channel expansion, aggressive warehouse automation investments totaling PLN 600 million for the Stęszew facility, and the return of spare parts price inflation following multi-year deflationary pressures.

The European light vehicle fleet expanded to 294 million vehicles by 2024, with the average age reaching 12.9 years in 2025; construction of the PLN 600 million Stęszew warehouse is scheduled for completion in Q2 2027; and spare parts prices increased by 1.5% quarter-on-quarter in the second quarter of 2026.

However, the thesis confronts material execution risks including persistent domestic Polish price wars, foreign exchange translation volatility that compressed reported margins in 2025, elevated net working capital of PLN 5.76 billion tying up substantial liquidity, and a historical pattern of narrow misses on ambitious revenue guidance despite achieving 99.2% of EBITDA thresholds under the 2025-2027 management incentive scheme.

A fierce three-year local price war persists between the top Polish players seeking market dominance; a strengthening Polish złoty compressed reported net margins, offsetting localized European revenue gains; elevated inventory of PLN 5.76 billion ties up substantial operating liquidity and exposes the group to holding costs if demand contracts; and the group achieved 99.2% of the EBITDA threshold, satisfactorily meeting its initial 2025 targets.

Management operates with a conservative sandbagging archetype, consistently setting internal budgets at 7- 10% growth while delivering 20-25% actual expansion, which creates forward credibility challenges when evaluating the ambitious PLN 1,633.9 million EBITDA target for 2026 tied to executive share option vesting. The scheme mandates achieving EBITDA of PLN 1,633.9 million as part of key targets defined for 2026.

The sellside pool remains concentrated among Polish brokers with Noble Securities analyst Mateusz Chrzanowski recently upgrading his target price to PLN 1,117 while paradoxically downgrading the rating from Buy to Accumulate following the stock's significant run-up, reflecting tension between fundamental momentum and valuation discipline.
