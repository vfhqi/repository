EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF) 3 findings

Computacenter delivered exceptional H1 2026 results with Group revenue reaching £6,845.2m representing 71.6% growth and adjusted PBT expanding to £152.4m, up 87.0% reported from £81.5m in the prior year, driven by unprecedented North American hyperscaler demand for AI-related digital infrastructure. The committed product backlog reached £9.3bn at June 30, 2026, up 323.2% year-on-year, providing extraordinary forward visibility into structural revenue conversion through FY27 and beyond.

However, this hyper-growth in Technology Sourcing volumes diluted the gross margin percentage to 9.6% in the half, exposing the fundamental tension between market share expansion and pricing power in high-volume hyperscaler contracts.

The materiality of change centers on a strategic pivot from traditional European IT reselling toward North American AI infrastructure deployment, with Computacenter occupying the top percentile for top-line revenue momentum in H1 2026 driven by its unique exposure to North American AI data center builds. This transformation is structurally reshaping the Group's geographic profit mix, as the US segment now represents 62% of operating profit compared to historically domestic-heavy European peer profiles. The scale of this shift is unprecedented in the Value-Added Reseller landscape, creating both substantial growth optionality and concentrated execution risk around hyperscaler capital expenditure cycles. Forward EPS expansion through FY27 is anchored on five to eight ranked operational drivers that collectively underpin management's upgraded profit guidance. US hyperscaler demand represents the dominant driver with an estimated forty percent impact on incremental group profit expansion, underpinned by high plausibility and high confidence given hardware vendor GPU allocations.

UK Sourcing momentum contributes an estimated fifteen percent impact with high plausibility and medium confidence, driven by high-performance AI cluster deployment, while German public sector recovery is expected to add ten percent contingent on infrastructure fund budget releases under the incoming Merz government fiscal stimulus. The critical fourth driver involves improving EBITto-gross-profit conversion toward the 30% medium-term target from the current 24%, requiring substantial administrative cost discipline and successful completion of global CRM and ERP system rollouts.

North American M&A integration, particularly AgreeYa and GAI acquisitions, provides an estimated five percent immediate EPS accretion by expanding high-margin professional services and securing direct access to US federal agencies. Additional secondary drivers include UK services margin recovery from contract rationalization, working capital efficiency improvements to narrow the gap versus peer benchmarks like CDW's 16-day cash conversion cycle, and the strategic automation of integration facilities including the £32m Atlanta center investment to support liquid-cooled GPU configurations. & Co Redburn Management raised its FY26 adjusted PBT guidance to no less than £380m following blowout results, and the company has a consistent track record of guiding conservatively and over-delivering on its expectations, firmly categorizing leadership as disciplined sandbaggers rather than aggressive honest-trackers.

Actual adjusted PBT in FY25 reached £272m beating the guided floor of no less than £270m, driven by a material acceleration in North American Technology Sourcing, extending a pattern of conservative floor-setting followed by upward revision as visibility improves. This delivery archetype creates a structural margin of safety for forward estimates, particularly given the unprecedented order backlog transparency. However, the guidance framework remains highly sensitive to North American dollar fluctuations and Technology Sourcing margin realizations, with a 50bps change in EBIT margin representing the most powerful operational lever for net earnings, while North America's 62% profit contribution makes earnings highly sensitive to dollar fluctuations.

Sell-side consensus positioning has shifted dramatically constructive following the H1 2026 results delivery, with Jefferies upgrading FY26/27 EPS by 18%/16% and raising its price target to 6,500p with a Buy rating. The active coverage pool comprises three primary investment banks with a rating mix reflecting forty-two percent Overweight and fifty-eight percent Equal-weight positions, though Morgan Stanley discontinued active coverage in April 2026 at a final target of 3,400p.

Target price dispersion remains extreme, ranging from Morgan Stanley's discontinued 3,400p to Jefferies' street-high 6,500p, representing an 87% escalation in Jefferies' valuation over the nine-month period through September 2026. The core bull thesis centers on Computacenter representing the best European picks-and-shovels play on AI hardware capex, with Jefferies justifying premium peer-parity multiples of 23x calendarized P/E in line with Softcat's software-heavy valuation.

Conversely, the bear case emphasizes structural gross margin compression from hyperscaler pricing power, near-term IT spending caution outside AI categories, and service commoditization risks as competitors shift up the value chain.

Peer-relative positioning reveals extreme outperformance on top-line momentum but material underperformance on profitability metrics and working capital efficiency. Computacenter's total revenue surged by 71.6% (73.3% in constant currency) in H1 2026, far outstripping the mid-teens growth rates of European peers Bechtle and Softcat, securing the number-one momentum ranking in a six-company comparative framework. However, rapid highvolume Technology Sourcing expansion diluted its gross margin percentage to 9.6% in H1 2026, while in contrast Bechtle achieved stable gross margins of 18.5% in Q2 2026 highlighting a substantially higher-margin product configuration mix.

Profitability efficiency gaps remain pronounced, as Softcat maintained an operating margin of 36.4% on gross profit in FY25, proving the superior scalability of software reselling versus Computacenter's physical integration model. Working capital management also lags best-in-class benchmarks, with Redburn Atlantic highlighting that Computacenter carries the largest portion of unbilled DSO among its coverage followed by Softcat, Bytes, and Bechtle, while Softcat delivered cash conversion of 147.6% in H1 2026 setting the sector benchmark for high-quality cash-backed earnings.

The peer analysis confirms that Computacenter trades volume for margin, occupying a structurally different business model compared to premium domestic software resellers.

No active activist presence was detected in the AlphaSense library, confirming a stable shareholder registry profile consistent with domestic peer Softcat's governance structure. The absence of activist intervention reflects management's disciplined capital allocation framework, including targets for adjusted return on capital employed above 45% significantly outperforming the European IT reseller median of 22%, and a track record of returning over one billion pounds to shareholders since 2013 through dividends and special capital returns.

The clean activist screen removes a layer of near-term governance risk but also eliminates a potential catalyst for accelerated margin improvement or portfolio rationalization that could unlock latent value in underperforming segments.

Plausible problems to monitor

Computacenter's explosive growth in low-margin Technology Sourcing volumes has created severe gross margin compression to 9.6% in H1 2026, substantially underperforming Bechtle's stable 18.5% gross margin achieved in Q2 2026 and exposing a structural pricing power deficit in hyperscale procurement contracts (Section 4, Section 6). The company carries the largest portion of unbilled DSO among European IT resellers, indicating unfavorable client payment terms that contrast sharply with Softcat's exceptional 147.6% cash conversion in H1 2026, revealing persistent working capital inefficiency despite record revenue growth (Section 4, Section 7).

Service margins faced pressure from increased contract provisions on an underperforming UK Managed Services contract, highlighting execution friction in the services portfolio that undermines the strategic pivot away from pure hardware reselling (Section 6). Germany's adjusted operating profit declined 13.3% in constant currency due to earlier-thanexpected efficiency cost recognition, demonstrating premature cost phasing that contrasts with the measured expense discipline exhibited by domestic peers and suggesting potential operational control weaknesses (Section 6).

Computacenter's adjusted operating profit reached only 24% of gross profit in FY25, lagging its medium-term operational efficiency target of 30% and falling significantly short of Softcat's 36.4% operating margin on gross profit, exposing persistent scalability constraints in the business model relative to software-centric competitors (Section 4, Section 7). The broader reseller peer group has experienced Microsoft commission changes that pressured gross profit growth to historical lows, while Computacenter's own variable compensation expenses rose 19.6%, indicating dual margin headwinds from both external software ecosystem changes and internal incentive structure inflation (Section 4, Section 7).

The company's capital intensity is escalating, with annual capex expected to reach £70-75m in FY26 driven by core operating system replacements, temporarily depressing free cash flow at a time when working capital outflows reached £245.1m in H1 2026 to support massive H2 project deliveries, creating a cash generation profile that lags the sector's premium operators (Section 4).

Outliers worth noting

Computacenter's financial profile reveals several statistical anomalies that position the company at the extreme tails of peer distributions across multiple dimensions. The most pronounced outlier is the company's revenue acceleration, with H1 2026 total revenue surging by 71.6% (73.3% in constant currency), far outstripping the midteens growth rates of European peers Bechtle and Softcat. This extraordinary top-line expansion places Computacenter in the top percentile for revenue momentum within the European IT reseller landscape, fundamentally driven by unique exposure to North American AI data center builds rather than traditional client refresh cycles.

On the margin side, Computacenter's rapid high-volume Technology Sourcing expansion diluted its gross margin percentage to 9.6% in H1 2026, while in contrast, Bechtle achieved stable gross margins of 18.5% in Q2 2026. This margin compression places Computacenter at the bottom ~3% of the peer distribution, highlighting that the company's business model has pivoted dramatically toward commoditized hardware reselling at the expense of premium software and services pricing power.

The 9.6% gross margin is a record low for the company and represents a structural outlier when benchmarked against domestic-focused resellers like Softcat or Bytes. Geographic profit concentration presents another extreme outlier, with North America representing 62% of Group operating profit in H1 2026, compared to the predominantly domestic profit profiles of UK-listed peers. This geographic skew exposes Computacenter to disproportionate currency translation risk and hyperscaler capital expenditure volatility that simply does not exist for peers operating primarily within their home markets.

The 62% profit contribution from a single region places Computacenter at the top ~2% of geographic concentration risk among European technology distributors. Working capital efficiency reveals a concerning negative outlier, as Redburn Atlantic highlights that Computacenter carries the largest portion of unbilled DSO among its coverage, followed by Softcat, Bytes, and Bechtle. This metric positions the company at the bottom ~5% for working capital quality within the peer set, suggesting that customer payment terms are structurally unfavorable relative to competitors.

In stark contrast, Softcat delivered cash conversion of 147.6% in H1 2026, setting the sector benchmark for high-quality, cashbacked earnings, while Computacenter struggles with large seasonal working capital outflows. From a valuation perspective, Jefferies upgraded Computacenter's FY26/27 EPS estimates by 18%/16% in September 2026, leading the peer group's revision cycle. This estimate revision magnitude places Computacenter at the top ~5% of positive momentum within the coverage universe, reflecting the structural inflection in earnings trajectory that sell-side analysts are racing to capture.

However, the target price dispersion between brokers is equally extreme, ranging from 3,400p to 6,500p, representing a 91% differential that highlights profound disagreement about the sustainability of current growth rates and the appropriate valuation framework. The final statistical anomaly concerns capital intensity, with annual capital expenditure expected to rise to £70- 75m in FY26, driven by core operating system replacement.

This capex-to-revenue ratio positions Computacenter at the top ~10% of capital intensity among asset-light resellers, primarily due to simultaneous SAP ERP migrations and physical integration center automation investments. Parallel peer investment is visible at Bechtle, which expects operating margins to be compressed by internal SAP S/4HANA migrations across 90-100 subsidiaries, suggesting this is a sector-wide transition cost rather than company-specific execution failure.

Nonetheless, the elevated capex burden represents a temporary outlier that depresses near-term free cash flow conversion relative to software-pure resellers operating with minimal infrastructure investment requirements.
