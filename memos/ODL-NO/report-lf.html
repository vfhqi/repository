EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This comprehensive report examines the leadership and governance of Odfjell Drilling (ODL-NO), focusing on Chief Executive Officer Kjetil Gjersdal's operational track record, management quality, board oversight, and insider alignment. The analysis synthesizes evidence from company filings, broker research, expert interviews, and market data to assess whether management possesses the capabilities to sustain the company's recent exceptional performance in the harsh-environment drilling sector. The findings reveal a CEO with deep operational expertise and a strong delivery record since his March 2022 appointment, supported by industry-leading fleet utilization and progressive shareholder returns. However, governance exhibits moderate weaknesses due to concentrated family control, and the organization faces significant key-person dependencies and geographic concentration risks that could undermine long-term credibility.

Chief Executive Quality and Delivery Record

Gjersdal brings over 25 years of drilling experience accumulated entirely within Odfjell Drilling, having advanced steadily through operational roles including Rig Manager, Operations Manager, and Senior Vice President MODU International. His tenure as CEO has delivered exceptional operational performance, achieving a 98% average financial utilization for the owned fleet in 2025 and expanding annual operating revenue by 16% to USD 901 million.

The own fleet segment achieved an EBITDA margin of 59% in Q3 2025, while Odfjell Drilling delivered a 358% share price increase since FY19, significantly outperforming global deepwater peers. Gjersdal completed the strategically accretive USD 480 million Bollsta acquisition in December 2025, adding the Deepsea Bergen rig which contributed approximately USD 100 million in annual EBITDA with minimal capital expenditures.

He demonstrated strong capital discipline by refinancing the balance sheet with a USD 650 million bond at 7.25% coupon, reducing leverage to 1.5x net debt to EBITDA by Q2 2026, and raising quarterly dividends to USD 0.25 per share totaling USD 60 million.

Management Style and Strategic Decisiveness

Gjersdal maintains highly transparent and plain-spoken public dialogue, openly describing his strategic approach as boring while emphasizing consistent execution. He displays strong capital discipline by avoiding speculative transactions, stating he won't do anything to speculate on deals, and executing the USD 480 million rig acquisition only when pricing became immediately accretive to stakeholders. Broker sentiment remains overwhelmingly positive, with DNB Carnegie's Karlsen upgrading Odfjell to BUY in May 2026 following an unwarranted market reaction to the Deepsea Atlantic incident, subsequently raising the target price to NOK 120 in September 2026, and praising the company's industry-leading contract backlog coverage in Norway.

Management resolved the Deepsea Atlantic equipment incident within 106 off-hire days in 2026 and recognized USD 82 million in insurance proceeds to offset repair costs, demonstrating effective crisis response. However, much of Gjersdal's record-breaking operational success was inherited from constructive market cycles and high-spec assets already under contract.

Governance Structure and Board Independence

Odfjell Drilling's board consists of only four non-executive directors, including Chair Simen Lieungh, Helene Odfjell, Knut Hatleskog, and Alasdair Shiach, with recent board refreshment through Shiach's appointment in April 2025. The company deviates from corporate governance codes by lacking separate nomination and remuneration committees, which represents a material governance weakness. Ownership remains highly concentrated with Director Helene Odfjell holding 49.85% of common shares through Odfjell Partners Holding Ltd, while other board members hold very limited equity stakes, with Chair Simen Lieungh holding only 20,000 shares. The board determines annual variable executive pay based on holistic qualitative assessments rather than a structured quantitative KPI system, creating flexibility but reducing transparency. Executive contracts and remuneration policies contain no explicit clawback provisions for variable pay, showing weak risk mitigation, though a 100% cap on variable pay protects shareholders from runaway compensation.

Executive Alignment and Insider Trading Signals

CEO Gjersdal's total remuneration grew 13% to USD 1,030,000 in 2025, including USD 1,267,000 in cash-settled options, while CFO Ørjan Lunde received USD 356,000 in total pay following his May appointment. Gjersdal holds 47,450 shares representing only 0.04% of freely traded shares, indicating limited direct equity alignment despite his long tenure. Open-market purchases by senior executives indicate growing internal conviction, with Gjersdal and Lunde acquiring shares on 15 May 2026 and expanding their holdings to 47,450 and 6,000 shares respectively by June 2026.

The company elected to settle exercised options in cash during February and July 2026, with Lunde exercising 100,000 options on 3 July 2026 through non-dilutive cash settlement. However, the heavy reliance on cash-settled options means that insiders are not building long-term direct equity stakes, potentially limiting permanent alignment.

External Validation and Expert Concerns

Expert calls confirm Odfjell Drilling's exceptional performance and regional dominance in the Norwegian harshenvironment sector, with a competitor CFO noting Odfjell delivers the best performance in the sector through tight geographical focus, and a former Valaris director highlighting the company's specialization in operating only ultraharsh semisubmersibles globally. A former Valaris VP notes Odfjell maintains strong offshore rig engineering power unlike many global peers, while a former Valaris director states Odfjell is much better reputed than Transocean in the Norwegian market. Experts report Odfjell securing leading commercial dayrates in the USD 445k to 500k range, though a competitor CFO observes the company's corporate funding costs have risen significantly alongside peers recently. The single largest risk to management credibility is Odfjell's extreme geographic concentration in Norway, making it highly vulnerable to domestic regulatory changes or localized market downturns, with a competitor CFO noting the business would not make sense if spread across multiple global continents.

Critical Dependencies and Long-Term Risks

No public successor is identified for CEO Kjetil Gjersdal, raising key-person risk if he departs suddenly, though the immediate executive tier features strong operational and commercial depth with key executives averaging over a decade of experience within Odfjell Drilling's corporate structure. Failing to recruit specialized external talent represents a highly persistent operational risk for North Sea drilling firms, with the flat Norwegian organizational structure creating cultural integration challenges for foreign executive recruits, and replicating CFO Lunde's maritime finance expertise presenting an extremely demanding recruitment challenge due to niche harshenvironment demands. Helene Odfjell's 49.85% controlling stake ensures high protection against hostile takeover or activist-forced turnover, effectively insulating management from external pressure. The company's governance framework shows moderate weaknesses through concentrated founder control, absent committee structures, and limited pre-emption rights for minority shareholders, though clean audit opinions and active whistleblower systems provide some offsetting protection.
