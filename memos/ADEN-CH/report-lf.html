EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

CEO QUALITY AND BACKGROUND Denis Machuel possesses a strong engineering background and digital transformation track record spanning Altran and Sodexo, with his dual expertise in technology and workforce services matching Adecco's immediate strategic need to integrate Akkodis. His tenure as Sodexo Group CEO ended abruptly on September 30, 2021, with Morgan Stanley reporting the departure was driven by slow decision velocity and insufficiently bold postpandemic operational changes. He was appointed Adecco Group CEO effective July 1, 2022, with the board conducting a thorough one-year selection process to ensure readiness for the next strategic cycle. His technical credentialing from ENSIMAG and 16-year Altran tenure provide relevant engineering services expertise, but his Sodexo dismissal for execution speed concerns raises questions about his ability to accelerate underperforming US operations. #J

FINANCIAL DELIVERY AND OPERATIONAL EXECUTION

Adecco delivered 5.6% organic revenue growth in Q2 2026, representing five consecutive quarters of growth under Machuel, while gaining 160 basis points of market share against direct peers. However, the tenure table reveals persistent TSR underperformance across all years from FY22 through HY26, with gross margin reaching 18.6% in Q2 2026 as client mix headwinds impacted profitability sequentially. EBITA margin excluding one-offs reached 2.8% in Q2 2026, driven by strong operating leverage and productivity, yet relative share performance lagged Randstad because leverage remained elevated relative to peers.

The EUR 150 million savings target was met in FY24, but margin expansion remains constrained by adverse client mix and structural automotive headwinds in Germany. #A

PRIOR COMPANY TRACK RECORD

Sodexo revenues shrank from €20,698 million in FY17 to €17,428 million in FY21 under Machuel's executive leadership, with operating margin falling from 6.5% at start to 3.3% at exit, reflecting a severe 320 basis points contraction. Universities in North America suffered unmitigated contract losses under his tenure before stabilizing slightly in FY19, while Sodexo lost €200 million of annualized healthcare revenues in Q4 2019 due to historic operational execution deficits.

Sodexo's organic sales growth lagged behind peers like Compass Group who delivered superior organic top-line momentum. The Sodexo board unanimously ended his mandate in 2021 due to transformation execution shortfalls, establishing a clear pattern of margin compression and relative underperformance that demands scrutiny at Adecco. #J

Morgan Stanley observed Machuel was surprised by the cautious outlook of peers Randstad and Manpower, suggesting potential optimism bias in his projections. CAPITAL ALLOCATION AND BALANCE SHEET PRIORITIES

Net debt fell by EUR 235 million year-on-year in Q2 2026, bringing leverage down to 2.7x, progressing toward the CFO's target leverage at or below 1.5x by end-2027 compared to a peer median of 1.2x.

Management divested a dilutive US tech staffing asset, redeploying proceeds into aerospace bolt-ons, demonstrating pragmatic portfolio optimization. The disciplined CapEx run rate is over EUR 50 million lower than in 2022, supporting free cash flow generation, but the scrip dividend mechanism creates equity dilution concerns highlighted by Morgan Stanley's Underweight rating. #A

INSIDER CONVICTION AND TRANSACTION PATTERNS

Board members received one-third of fixed fees in shares subject to a three-year blocking period, with Chairman Deslarzes increasing his holding by 21,106 shares reaching 104,014 total shares in 2025, while non-executive directors expanded personal equity ownership by a combined 56,565 shares during the 2025 financial period. CEO Denis Machuel held 52,709 shares plus 186,806 unvested performance units as of late 2025, with the group raising the shareholding guideline to 300% of base salary for the CEO compared to 150% for other executives.

However, former CFO Coram Williams forfeited 84,302 unvested share awards upon his resignation in late 2025, reducing aggregate executive ownership. The complete absence of open-market purchases by executives suggests insiders rely solely on compensatory equity rather than personal cash conviction, representing a weak insider signal despite structural share accumulation through remuneration programs. #J

CULTURE AND ORGANIZATIONAL CAPACITY Adecco's frontline personnel experience 20% annual attrition, which provides a natural cost buffer, compared to typical recruiter attrition averaging 30% annually across the broader staffing sector. Management maintains 10% of recruiter headcount on flexible placement contract structures to manage cyclical demand drops, with frontline capacity adjusted rapidly through attrition rather than immediate structural corporate layoffs. Adecco's client Net Promoter Score rose to 48 in 2025, up 7 points year-on-year, with candidate satisfaction reaching 44 in 2025, indicating a positive long-term trajectory. Adecco delivered EUR 174 million G&A savings, with G&A overhead dropping 10% year-on-year in 2026, reflecting a high-performance cost-discipline archetype. The culture demonstrates superior retention versus sector averages and strong cost discipline, but aggressive frontline headcount reductions could leave branches severely under-resourced to capture high-margin placements during demand rebounds. #A

EXPERT NETWORK AND COMPETITIVE POSITIONING A global aerospace recruiter confirmed Adecco is the primary partner for high-volume temporary staffing requirements, though the same customer noted Randstad is gaining share in specialized mid-senior engineering roles. Client reviews show Adecco's single point of contact provides strong responsiveness but limits proactive candidate engagement, while heightened local competition has left onsite teams struggling to close critical specialized labor placements. Adecco has successfully migrated approximately 70% of global revenues onto a unified front-office Salesforce instance, with early trials of automated pre-screening tools improving placement quality and generating positive enterprise client feedback. However, operational leaders caution that disconnects between global systems and localized middle-office platforms slow deployment, as middle-office software remains localized because regulatory compliance requirements differ across sixty operating countries. Expert testimony reveals strong volume staffing capabilities but rising specialized recruitment vulnerability, with agentic AI showing early productivity gains offset by middle-office integration friction. #A

The sell-side remains deeply polarized, with bulls citing volume momentum and valuation troughs while bears emphasize structural AI threats, leverage concerns, and dividend sustainability risks. #A

GOVERNANCE RED FLAGS AND LEGAL EXPOSURES

Each registered share represents one vote providing a clean 1-to-1 voting structure with no dual-class shares or participation certificates, PwC serves as statutory auditor since its appointment in 2024 with the engagement renegotiated on an annual basis, and management assessed internal financial controls using COSO framework finding zero material weaknesses, confirming highly effective reporting integrity. However, French authorities continue a competition investigation into acquired unit AKKA alleging historical anti-competitive corporate practices, though management has fully co-operated with investigators and adequately accrued for potential penalty outcomes in France. Zero cases of bribery or corruption were classified as material in FY25, verifying robust ethical compliance oversight. The governance structure is clean with strong audit oversight, but the historical AKKA antitrust liability represents a latent cash reserve risk that could severely impact deleveraging timelines if finalized with material damages. #A

The aggregation of management and governance evidence across this report reveals five material vulnerabilities that cluster below peer median benchmarks and directly threaten the investment thesis. These cross-cutting weaknesses span executive track record credibility, capital structure constraints, operational execution friction, compensation misalignment risks, and persistent margin compression dynamics that collectively undermine confidence in management's ability to deliver sustainable shareholder value creation.

1.
CEO Prior-Company Execution Failure: Denis Machuel's abrupt termination from Sodexo in 2021 following board pressure over slow decision velocity and insufficient post-pandemic operational changes represents a significant red flag that directly contradicts the narrative of operational competence. This dismissal occurred after severe margin compression of 320 basis points and revenue contraction from €20,698 million to €17,428 million
2.
Elevated Financial Leverage Overhang: Adecco's current leverage position of 2.7x Net Debt/EBITDA in Q2 2026

substantially exceeds the peer median of 1.2x, creating a material constraint on strategic flexibility and capital allocation optionality that directly threatens the group's competitive positioning. The board's decision to rebase the dividend to CHF 1 in FY25 and implement a scrip dividend option that saved EUR 97 million in cash through a 53.01% take-up rate demonstrates the severity of balance sheet pressure and suggests management prioritizes deleveraging over shareholder distributions.

This elevated leverage profile triggered both Moody's negative outlook revision in September 2026 citing weak gross margin performance and S&P's rating downgrade to BBB in November 2025 reflecting persistently high financial leverage, materially increasing borrowing costs and constraining merger-and-acquisition capacity relative to better-capitalized peers. The persistent overhang from this leverage disadvantage limits management's ability to pursue value-accretive bolt-on acquisitions or defend market share through counter-cyclical pricing strategies. headcount reductions affecting 450 consultants with restructuring liabilities reaching EUR 30 million in first-half 2026, representing a material deviation from the clean operational execution demonstrated by peer engineering services providers. The group's cumulative exceptional restructuring costs reached EUR 82 million for Akkodis Germany since program inception in fiscal 2025, demonstrating persistent underperformance that management has failed to remediate despite multiple quarters of targeted intervention.

This chronic restructuring dependency contrasts sharply with Randstad's ability to execute automotive exposure diversification without comparable multi-year margin drag, suggesting fundamental operational deficits in Adecco's German automotive footprint that threaten the broader Akkodis integration thesis. The risk that German automotive weakness continues indefinitely and depresses margins below target levels represents a material execution hazard that could derail the medium-term profitability recovery pathway outlined at the November 2025 Capital Markets Day. [#A Section 2, Section 10] Diluted Compensation Performance Metrics: The group's 2025 LTIP introduced 15% combined weightings for carbon emissions, cyber security, and employee engagement alongside traditional financial targets, creating a material misalignment between executive incentives and pure shareholder value creation that diverges from peer compensation best practices.

Furthermore, reported earnings growth lags adjusted incentive metrics due to continued restructuring charges that persistently bypass short-term bonus calculations, allowing management to earn variable compensation on adjusted performance that excludes recurring operational deficits. This compensation structure creates perverse incentives where executives benefit from adjusted metrics while shareholders absorb the full economic impact of persistent one-off charges, fundamentally undermining the alignment principle that should govern executive pay design.

The structural risk that management focuses excessively on adjusted metrics while statutory EPS and returns remain depressed below peer benchmarks represents a governance vulnerability that could perpetuate value destruction. [#J Section 8]

4.
Persistent Gross Margin Compression: Adecco's Q2 2026 gross margin of 18.6% faces sustained pressure from

adverse client mix dynamics, with top-line outperformance remaining skewed to large enterprise accounts that operate at structurally lower margins. This margin dilution pattern diverges materially from ManpowerGroup's sequential gross profit margin expansion to 16.1% in Q2 2026, demonstrating that peers successfully protected pricing discipline while Adecco sacrificed profitability for volume growth. Jefferies explicitly warned that top players in France have shown flat long-cycle growth compared to small agile competitors, suggesting Adecco's market share gains may prove unsustainable without structural margin sacrifice.

The fundamental tension between management's aggressive volume growth strategy and margin protection requirements creates a critical vulnerability where continued large-client dependency perpetuates gross margin erosion and threatens the group's ability to achieve its 6% EBITA margin target articulated at the Capital Markets Day.
