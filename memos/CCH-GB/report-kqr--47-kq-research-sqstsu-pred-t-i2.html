Section 3 – Equity Issuance, Lock-Up Agreements, and Option Structures

Section summary
  1. The issuance of twenty-one million consideration shares to Gutsche Family Investments expands Coca- Cola HBC's capital base with no active discussion of lock-up restrictions in public markets.
  2. The option structure over the remaining twenty-five percent stake of the African business is accounted for as a balance sheet liability, avoiding profit leakage.
  3. Historic beverage sector precedents highlight how put and call options manage joint venture exits but can trigger valuation disputes.

Equity Issuance and Capital Base Changes

1.
Significant adjustments: Capital structure changes are set to take place as Coca-Cola HBC issues new shares to complete the acquisition of the African bottling operations. The group has agreed to issue 21,027,676 new ordinary shares to Gutsche Family Investments to fund the transaction. This equity issuance represents approximately 5.45% of the company's enlarged issued and outstanding share capital. The newly issued shares will expand the share denominator and directly impact intermediate-term earnings per share calculations unless offset by immediate operational synergies.
2.
Orderly admissions: Market listing preparations are complete with multiple exchange listings confirmed to support the newly issued equity. The secondary listing on the Main Board of the Johannesburg Stock Exchange takes effect on November 23, 2026. Admission to trading for the consideration shares on the London Stock Exchange and Euronext Athens is set for November 30, 2026. This multi-exchange admission ensures liquidity for the major selling family but creates near-term equity overhang concerns among institutional investors.
3.
Undiscussed terms: Lock-up narrative analysis reveals a complete absence of public discussion regarding selling restrictions for the Gutsche family holdings. Sell-side analysts covering the transaction focus entirely on the operational merits of the African consolidation. No major broker notes or company regulatory filings discuss specific lock-up terms or duration for the newly issued consideration shares. This structural gap in the market narrative means investors cannot easily evaluate when or if this major shareholder block might execute market sales.

Put and Call Option Mechanics

4.
Structured pathways: Unique option arrangements establish the pathway for Coca-Cola HBC to acquire complete ownership of the African operations. The group holds a call option over the remaining 25% equity interest held by The Coca-Cola Company that is exercisable between three and five years post-completion. Conversely, the parent company holds a put option to sell its residual stake back to the group exercisable between three and a half and six years post-completion. This structured timeline gives the group three years of financial breathing room before facing further cash requirements.
5.
Compounding valuations: Precise valuation formulas govern the option pricing to protect both parties from short- term share price volatility. The exercise price matches the initial share purchase price paid to the sellers plus an applicable compounded coupon. The contracted coupon is set at 2.75% per annum for the first year and rises to 3% per annum until the third anniversary. This predictable compounding rate allows analysts to model future cash outflows accurately based on the initial 3.4 billion dollar equity valuation.
6.
Favorable accounting: Favorable accounting treatment prevents standard minority interest deductions from diluting the group's consolidated net income. Because risks and rewards of ownership pass on day one, the 25% residual stake is not recognized as a minority interest in the profit and loss statement. Instead, the outstanding minority stake is modeled as a redemption liability on the balance sheet against goodwill. This non-dilutive accounting setup is expected to support immediate accretion in the group's reported earnings per share.

Precedents in the Beverage Sector

7.
Strategic protections: Historical emerging market transactions demonstrate how multinational beverage groups utilize option structures to manage local partnerships. Coca-Cola FEMSA acquired a majority stake in Coca- Cola Beverages Philippines in 2013 with a call option to buy the remaining stake and a put option to sell it back. Following severe local sugar taxes and profit pressures, the firm exercised its put option in 2018 to divest the asset. This precedent shows that put options serve as vital shields for bottlers operating in volatile regulatory environments.
8.
Hostile disputes: Hostile valuation conflicts in international partnerships highlight the risk of relying on long-term option agreements. Carlsberg faced a protracted legal dispute with its partner in Carlsberg South Asia when the partner exercised its put option in 2023. The valuation was referred to arbitration due to disagreements over whether unreasonable assumptions were applied to the independent valuations. This dispute warns that option pricing formulas can break down under operational stress, delaying strategic resolutions.
9.
Systemic applications: Turkish bottling peer Coca-Cola Icecek utilizes similar put and call options to consolidate its regional holdings. The firm carries a put option liability of 115.5 million liras for the purchase of a 12.5% stake in Turkmenistan Coca-Cola. It also consolidated its ownership of Al Waha by acquiring a 19.97% stake following the partner's put option exercise. This systemic use of options across the global system confirms that the structure is a standardized tool for anchor bottlers.

Section 4 – Russian Cash, Leverage Corridors, and Profit Exposure

Section summary
  1. The potential state-seizure of Coca-Cola HBC's Russian operations, Multon, would trigger significant balance sheet restructuring and valuation multiple re-ratings.
  2. Excluding the trapped one billion euros of Russian cash from the net leverage corridor would elevate pro-forma leverage toward the ceiling of management's target.
  3. Peer precedents from Heineken and Carlsberg show that exiting Russia involves massive non-cash accounting write-downs but has limited direct operational impact on the wider group.

Russian Cash and Leverage Calculations

1.
Trapped balances: Coca-Cola HBC maintains a massive cash position in Russia that remains highly restricted due to local capital controls. The group reported eight hundred and fifty million euros of Russian cash at the end of fiscal year 2025, which expanded to one billion euros by mid-2026. If this cash is lost, the pro-forma net debt to EBITDA ratio for fiscal year 2027 would rise from 1.6x to 2x, touching the ceiling of management's target range. This shift would significantly reduce the group's balance sheet flexibility during the integration of its African bottling business.
2.
Elevated leverage: Extreme balance sheet deterioration could occur if both the Russian operating profit and the trapped cash are removed simultaneously. If Coca-Cola HBC faces a total loss of Russian operating earnings, the pro-forma leverage ratio for fiscal year 2027 would elevate to 2.2x net debt to EBITDA. Furthermore, losing both the operations and the one billion euros of trapped cash pushes pro-forma leverage to 2.7x, far exceeding the standard target corridor. Such a severe leverage spike would test the group's investment-grade rating unless offset by rapid debt paydown.
3.
Widespread restrictions: Capital repatriation constraints are an industry-wide challenge that limits the cash utility of multinational corporations operating in Russia. Small marketing and consulting peers like Ebiquity report holding restricted cash balances of over one million pounds within their local Russian subsidiaries. These funds are legally unavailable for remittance to parent companies and can only meet local obligations as they fall due. This restriction highlights how Coca-Cola HBC's massive cash pile remains isolated from the wider corporate treasury, preventing its use for debt service.
4.
Standalone structures: Structural separation provides a critical buffer that protects Coca-Cola HBC's global business from direct operational contagion. The Russian unit, Multon, has operated as a self-sufficient local business since losing the main Coca-Cola franchise in 2022. Multon produces local brands and is financed entirely through local credit facilities, meaning that day-to-day operations would not be directly affected by global supply disruptions. This independent operational design ensures that the consolidated entity retains its core earning power even under extreme regional stress.

Profit Exposure and Interest Income

5.
Substantial exposure: Consolidated earnings remain heavily dependent on the highly profitable Russian business despite the company's diversification efforts. The Russian operation generates approximately 27% of Coca- Cola HBC's total consolidated operating profit. Following the integration of the newly acquired African bottling business, Russia's contribution will dilute to about 21% of the enlarged group's operating income. This dilution helps reduce the company's geographical risk concentration, though Russia remains the largest single country profit contributor.
6.
High margins: Exceptional profitability characterized the Russian and Ukrainian operations prior to the recent regional restructuring. Russia and Ukraine together accounted for approximately 32% of Coca-Cola HBC's total operating profit in fiscal year 2025, up from 30% in fiscal year 2024. These joint markets delivered a stellar 22% operating profit margin in fiscal year 2025. This margin is more than double the modest 9.5% operating margin achieved by the rest of the group's global bottling network.
7.
Severe dilution: Absolute operating income would drop sharply if regional geopolitical conflicts force a complete deconsolidation of the eastern European territories. Stripping out the combined contributions of Russia and Ukraine would cause Coca-Cola HBC's fiscal year 2025 operating profit to fall from 1,356 million euros down to 918 million euros. Under this scenario, group free cash flow would contract from 628 million euros to 480 million euros. This cash contraction would limit capital expenditure and delay any potential shareholder distribution programs.
8.
Negative impact: Understated interest income losses will further compound the bottom-line drag if the group is forced to exit its Russian operations entirely. Pro-forma models indicate that losing Russia results in 26% lower earnings per share for fiscal year 2027, including lost interest on the cash. If Coca-Cola HBC retains its trapped cash, the fiscal year 2027 earnings per share is estimated at 2.43 euros. A total loss of this cash reduces the projected earnings per share to 2.35 euros.

This request has reportedly received partial support from relevant deputy prime ministers, representing a clear threat of state intervention. This escalating administrative pressure has forced analysts to actively model immediate expropriation scenarios.

Valuation models from Bernstein demonstrate that Coca-Cola HBC trades at a consolidated enterprise value to EBIT multiple of 12.4x for the next twelve months. Excluding the high-margin Russian business entirely reveals an implied multiple of 16.3x for the core operations. This wide multiple gap indicates that the market heavily discounts the Russian earnings stream due to the constant threat of state intervention.

Under these constrained balance sheet conditions, the group will struggle to resume its share buyback program, depressing near-term shareholder yields.
