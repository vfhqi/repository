Section summary
  1. Mercedes faces intense cyclical and structural headwinds as a severe contraction in China margins and premium segment volumes has forced the sell-side to slash estimates and lower price targets over the past six months.
1.
Strategic spin-off – completed: Mercedes operates as a leading manufacturer of premium passenger cars and commercial vans following the December 2021 spin-off of its truck and bus division. The company continues to maintain a 30% direct equity stake in Daimler Truck. This legacy structure provides a key source of financial flexibility through potential open-market stake divestments
2.
16 Divisional alignment – integrated: The group manages its global operations through three core divisions comprising Mercedes-Benz Cars, Mercedes-Benz Vans, and Mercedes-Benz Financial Services. To optimize its go-to-market execution and lower administrative overhead, the company fully integrated its mobility operations into the main automotive segment as of January 2026. This structural change seeks to establish a unified customer interface across both sales and financial services

17 18 19 Revenue performance – down-trending: The group's absolute financial performance has deteriorated over the last three years amid intensifying competition. Group revenue contracted to €132.2bn in FY25, marking a 9.2% decrease year-on-year from €145.6bn in FY24. This decline was a direct reversal from FY23, when revenues peaked at €152.4bn before macro headwinds in China took hold

Adjusted EBIT plunged by 39.9% to €8.2bn in FY25 compared to €13.7bn in FY24. Section 16 – Conclusion

The Q2 2026 earnings release on July 28, 2026 fundamentally altered the Mercedes-Benz investment case by confirming that the Chinese market deterioration is structural rather than cyclical, as evidenced by a €704 million non-cash impairment of Beijing Benz Automotive Co. joint venture assets alongside a 30% unit sales contraction in the region. The single most important change is that the write-down of the core Chinese joint venture reflects a permanent reduction in long-term profitability and dividend expectations, with pro-rata earnings from Beijing Benz Automotive Co. plunging 63% year-on-year in the first half of 2026.

This impairment signals that management no longer expects the Chinese premium segment to recover to historical margin levels, forcing investors to completely re-underwrite the group's long-term cash generation profile and dividend sustainability. While aggressive pricing from Chinese OEMs in both the Entry and Core luxury segments has severely eroded Mercedes-Benz's market share since 2023, the company is aggressively localizing its software stack through the proprietary MB.OS platform to counter domestic rivals, though software integration delays and execution setbacks threaten its premium positioning. The strongest counter-hypothesis in its most robust form argues that the prevailing negative view that intensifying trade tariffs and local content rules will permanently damage Mercedes-Benz's global operating margins could be incorrect if European protectionism acts as a defensive shield, as the local-content rules under the Industrial Accelerator Act could successfully create a durable demand pool for European-built electric vehicles, thereby stabilizing long-term European earnings expectations and supporting stock market sentiment.

Furthermore, the phased sell-down of the non-core Daimler Truck holding, which generated €417 million in Q2 2026 with an additional €600 million sold in July, provides funding firepower for capital returns without disrupting organic industrial investments. This tactical financial flexibility, combined with the completed €1.0 billion expansion at the Kecskemét plant in Hungary that doubled manufacturing capacity to 400,000 units annually and delivers factor costs 70% lower than in Germany could offset Chinese margin erosion faster than the market anticipates.
