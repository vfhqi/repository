Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Indra's transition from an IT service provider to a defense champion directly impacts its mid-term earnings and valuation multiples.
  2. Broader sovereign AI and European defense integration programs expand Indra's strategic contract pipelines but introduce complex execution risks.

Defining the Strategic Inquiry

1.
Asymmetric trajectory: "how concentrated or diffuse are the drivers that separate the next 18 months to four years' financials from the last four to six years, and how wide and how skewed is the range of plausible outcomes over the next 18 months?"
2.
Accelerating earnings: Indra is transitioning away from discretionary IT toward high-margin defense systems, which is expected to support a 20% EPS CAGR between 2025 and 2030. This shift is driven by Spain's Special Modernization Programs, which are converting record backlogs directly into reported net income. However, delays in program execution could quickly constrain this profit growth. Therefore, the resolution of this question determines whether Indra's EPS can meet ambitious targets of EUR 3.04 in 2026 and EUR 3.61 in 2027.
3.
Expanding multiples: Indra currently trades at a premium to IT services peers but at a discount to traditional European defense primes. Investors are debating whether the company deserves to re-rate as a pure defense platform or continue carrying a conglomerate discount due to Minsait. Resolving the concentration of near-term drivers will clarify whether valuation multiples will converge toward defense peers or remain capped. The upcoming Capital Markets Day is expected to be a critical catalyst for this market perception.

Capital Allocation and Strategic Alignment

4.
Dilutive scaling: Indra faces significant capital allocation risks as its capital expenditure and inventories rise rapidly to support the industrial ramp-up. Customer advances of EUR 1.9 bn received in 1H26 provide liquidity but represent future delivery obligations rather than free capital. If cash is consumed too quickly by platform programs like TESS, underlying cash conversion could weaken significantly. Management must establish clear guardrails to prevent apparent liquidity from encouraging inefficient acquisitions.
5.
Contrary evidence: Some analysts argue that Indra's strategic space and air traffic management assets can provide robust cash flow protection even if defense execution falters. For instance, the air traffic business has experienced double-digit growth and resilient margins that are relatively insulated from defense budget volatility. Thus, the range of near-term financials may be less concentrated on defense than skeptics assume.
6.
Sovereign intelligence: The European push for digital sovereignty is driving substantial demand for secure, onshore cloud and AI solutions. Indra has positioned its sovereign AI platform, IndraMind, to target EUR 1 bn in revenue by 2030. This sovereign AI push is tightly linked to national defense, as the customer mix shifts toward multidomain military operations. Consequently, the speed of IndraMind's commercialization directly affects Minsait's margin recovery and valuation.
7.
Fragmented integration: European defense spending has entered a structural phase that prioritizes local procurement and strategic autonomy. Programs like the Future Combat Air System represent massive long-term opportunities but suffer from intense political and industrial friction. A breakdown or reset of such collaborative programs would force Spain to redirect budgets toward national platforms. This makes Indra's role as Spain's national industrial coordinator highly strategic but exposed to execution risks.
8.
Other side of the story: While bulls focus on structural tailwinds in European defense, bears highlight that Indra's business remains heavily tied to Spanish state budgets and complex consortia. The consolidation of lower-margin land vehicle programs like TESS continues to dilute reported operating margins. If these platform programs consume excessive working capital while Minsait's traditional IT services slow down, financial outcomes could skew negatively over the next 18 months.

Section 3 – Working Capital and Cash Reversals in Defence Precedents

Section summary
  1. Cash flow profiles for European defense primes exhibit high volatility due to front-loaded downpayments and subsequent working capital consumption.
  2. Delays in contract signatures and delivery acceptances frequently defer expected cash inflows, creating massive swings in free cash flow.

Saab Working Capital Dynamics

1.
Sustained cash swings: Saab experienced major working capital swings during its transition from development to serial production of the Gripen E. The company's contract assets climbed from SEK 8 bn in FY17 to SEK 12.2 bn in FY19, representing an increase in working capital of SEK 4.2 bn. This cash-intensive development phase was followed by serial deliveries that began normalizing the group's cash flow in FY22.
2.
Improving capital turnover: Saab's net working capital as a percentage of sales has declined dramatically as serial deliveries scaled up. Carnegie data shows this ratio dropped from 24.0% in 2019 to 6.0% in 2025 before starting to rise as new capacity investments expand. Consequently, early prepayments are utilized for capacity ramp-up, which ultimately drives working capital higher during growth phases. 78 g ( ) g Q Saab historical and forecast cash flow metrics showing net working capital to sales ratio trends.
3.
Seasonal recovery patterns: Saab's operational cash generation remains highly dependent on late-stage milestone payments and seasonal fourth-quarter deliveries. The group reported a significant operational cash flow improvement to SEK 7.74 bn in the first nine months of 2026, compared to a negative SEK 1.01 bn in the prior period. This dramatic swing was primarily driven by higher customer payments, including Polish submarine advances, which helped offset ongoing inventory investments.

Thales Rafale Cash Cycles

4.
Reversing downpayments: Thales has historically demonstrated that front-loaded advances create substantial near-term cash tailwinds that eventually reverse as deliveries proceed. During the 2018 to 2021 period, Thales experienced a reversal of EUR 950m in positive working capital one-offs accumulated from 2015 to 2017 Rafale advances. Jefferies expects a similar cash conversion deceleration as these customer advances are consumed during production.
5.
Exceptional cash generation: Thales achieved outstanding cash flow in early 2026 due to the timing of new contract prepayments. The company's free cash flow reached EUR 1.87 bn in the first half of 2026, which was driven by working capital inflows of EUR 552m. Indeed, Redburn notes that a significant portion of this prepayment-driven cash flow is expected to partially reverse in the second half of the year.
6.
Large export contracts: Export contracts for Rafale fighter jets typically generate cash advances representing 15% to 20% of the total order value. These upfront downpayments provide immediate liquid assets to the consortium partners. While these inflows inflate cash metrics, the subsequent consumption of these advances over the multi-year delivery period limits further free cash flow growth.

Rheinmetall Prepayment Volatility

7.
Severe cash reversals: Rheinmetall has faced substantial financial volatility when contract signings and customer downpayments shift out of expected fiscal periods. The company's operating free cash flow fell to negative EUR 1.69 bn in the first nine months of 2026, compared to negative EUR 798m in the same period of 2025. This deficit highlights how the company is struggling to convert demand into actual revenues and free cash flow as inventory builds ahead of deliveries.
8.
Phasing contract delays: Rheinmetall pre-released a disappointing guidance cut in late 2026, lowering its sales and EBIT targets due to execution delays. The revision was driven by the multi-billion Arminius Boxer contract slipping into 2027, which deferred expected prepayments. Consequently, the group's cash conversion guidance was downgraded from over 40% to just positive.
9.
Historical prepayment lags: Rheinmetall's cash sensitivity to contract timing is a long-standing structural feature of its business model. In the 2018 financial year, the company finished with a negative operating free cash flow of EUR 35m. This deficit occurred because a critical EUR 100m milestone payment was delayed by one week and received in early January 2019.

10.
Contrary evidence: Some analysts argue that the current rearmament wave provides defense primes with unprecedented bargaining power to secure favorable payment terms. For instance, Kongsberg has operated as a highly cash-generative system integrator, recording negative working capital of 43% of sales in 2025 due to massive customer downpayments. This cash cushion suggests that strong demand can sustain negative working capital cycles for longer than historical precedents indicate.
11.
Concentrated execution risks: Defense contractors whose backlogs are highly concentrated on single domestic customers face acute execution and political risks. For example, Babcock International has carried significant customer concentration with the UK Ministry of Defence, which makes up over 50% of its total revenue. Delays in publishing the national Defence Investment Plan and contract disputes have historically weighed on its financial visibility.

Precedent synthesis metrics: Across this analysis of working capital dynamics, the research evaluated three primary peer defense companies and cited more than ten distinct financial documents. The historical precedents show that single-customer programs face a high rate of postponement and delivery acceptance delays. Consequently, while front-loaded advances inflate near-term liquidity, actual cash conversion inevitably normalizes downward during serial production phases.

Continued bidding for massive export opportunities, such as India's Rafale campaign, can trigger new downpayments that offset the consumption of older prepayments. If booking activity remains elevated, defense contractors can maintain elevated liquid balances without experiencing a net working capital squeeze.

IT Services and AI Repricing

1.
Slowing progress: Minsait's organic growth is decelerating as discretionary IT spending softens across European enterprise markets. In 2026, management expects Minsait to grow at a low-to-mid-single-digit rate, representing a marked deceleration from previous years. This slowdown is driven by clients prioritizing costefficiency programs over large, discretionary digital transformation initiatives. Consequently, Minsait's margins will remain structurally constrained unless high-value sovereign AI contracts can offset legacy outsourcing declines.
2.
Deflationary pressures: Pricing pressure is intensifying across European IT services as clients demand AI-driven productivity gains in renegotiations. Capgemini has noted a rapid shift toward fixed-price and outcome-based contracts as customers seek accountability for delivered productivity outcomes. This contract structural shift compresses traditional time-and-materials revenue, forcing providers to rapidly automate delivery teams. These pricing dynamics indicate that AI adoption is currently a deflationary headwind for seat-heavy legacy IT contracts.
3.
Widening divergence: Peer dispersion shows that larger IT vendors are managing the AI transition with varying degrees of margin resilience. Capgemini reported solid organic growth of 5.1% in its latest quarter, but experienced a 30 basis point gross margin decline due to delivery mix shifts. Meanwhile, other large European IT services firms are implementing strict cost controls to support margins. This divergence highlights how contract mix and customer focus determine margin stability during AI-led transition cycles.
4.
Marginal gains: Peer comparison reveals that large-cap IT services vendors are using margin levers to offset competitive pricing pressures. Accenture guided for a 10 to 30 basis point operating margin expansion in fiscal 2027 despite flagging softer pricing in parts of its business. Capgemini expects that structural restructuring will help expand its near-term operating margins despite slower organic growth in its core European automotive and manufacturing verticals. These strategies suggest that margin resilience is achievable through aggressive cost optimization even in a flat spending environment.
5.
Structural shifts: Investor sentiment toward IT services remains fragile due to structural concerns over AI-driven automation disrupting seat-heavy billing models. Leading European and global vendors have seen valuation multiples compressed to historical lows despite stable near-term results. Some experts anticipate that AI automation will heavily cannibalize legacy application integration and support functions, reducing overall enterprise IT spend. Consequently, Minsait must quickly transition away from low-margin business process outsourcing to protect its long-term equity valuation.
6.
Contrary evidence: Some IT services players are successfully leveraging proprietary AI platforms to command premium pricing. Infosys continues to secure favorable pricing on AI-led revenue deals despite intense competition in legacy segments. Similarly, other Indian IT vendors report that pricing remains stable at the overall portfolio level due to better pricing on AI revenues. Therefore, AI-driven pricing pressure may not be universally dilutive if service providers can successfully shift their portfolios toward complex, proprietary implementations.

Section 5 – Capitalisation Policies and Auditor Transition Risks

Section summary
  1. Shifting capitalization ratios at Indra introduce significant earning quality risks as development costs are increasingly deferred.
  2. Structural differences in peer policies highlight how flexible accounting rules can distort comparable performance metrics.

Indra Capitalisation Policies

1.
Accelerating deferrals: Indra has significantly altered its balance sheet treatment of research and development costs by expanding capitalization rates. The group capitalized 61.4% of its development costs in the 2025 financial year compared to 40.6% in the 2024 financial period. This accounting change deferred EUR 144.80 mm of development expenses to future periods rather than expensing them immediately. This rapid shift directly boosted near-term operating profit margins without reflecting any underlying change in cash generation.
2.
Sustained balance deferrals: Ongoing internal projects continue to absorb substantial portions of the group's development expenditure, expanding intangible asset exposure. The carrying amount of Indra's development projects that were not being amortised reached EUR 103.50 mm by the end of December 2025. These deferred balances represent projects that have not yet achieved full commercial launch status. As a result, any future commercial delay will heighten subsequent asset impairment risks under international standards.
3.
Elevated capitalization trends: First-half results for the 2026 financial year confirm that capitalization intensity remains historically high. Indra activated EUR 40.81 mm of its EUR 83.86 mm total research and development costs during the six months ended June 2026. This represents a 48.67% (calculated) capitalisation rate, which is far higher than the 19.98% (calculated) rate reported in the prior first-half period. Consequently, operating income is increasingly dependent on intangible balance sheet deferrals to meet consensus targets.
4.
Contrary evidence: Some historical product lines have shown that capitalized development costs can be amortised successfully once programs enter service. Indra's Air Traffic and Transport segments successfully transferred EUR 38.24 mm in capitalized development expenses during the 2025 fiscal year. These transfers occurred because specific technical milestones were met on mature air traffic management programs. Therefore, high capitalization does not automatically lead to write-downs if programs are commercially viable.

Auditor Review Risks

5.
Stricter scrutiny: The appointment of a new auditor introduces significant review risks for Indra's subjective accounting policies. A transition of auditors frequently triggers comprehensive reviews of historical accounting estimations under International Financial Reporting Standards. New audit partners generally apply much stricter standards to capitalization criteria under the IAS 38 framework. This transition could force Indra to increase its future R&D expensing and dilute reported margins.
6.
Subjective accounting judgements: Identifying the precise transition point between the research phase and development phase requires significant management estimation. International Accounting Standard 38 mandates that all research expenditures must be written off in the period they occur. Only development costs that meet strict commercial viability criteria are eligible for asset recognition. This dividing line creates substantial risks of material audit adjustments if the new auditor challenges historic project assumptions.
7.
Complex milestone valuations: Calculating the progress of complex fixed-price development contracts remains a primary area of audit focus across the aerospace sector. Auditing these estimates requires high degrees of auditor judgment because long-term contracts are highly vulnerable to unexpected cost changes. New auditors typically scrutinize percentage-of-completion calculations to ensure that revenue is not being recognized prematurely. If the new auditor demands more conservative progress measures, Indra faces near-term revenue and margin reversals on major programs.
8.
Standard audit testing: Standard audit procedures for testing capitalized intangibles involve extensive review of timesheets, payroll allocations, and commercial feasibility studies. Independent auditors verify capitalized hours against employee timesheets and contracts to confirm that capitalized labor is directly attributable. Auditors also critically evaluate discounted cash flow projections to test whether capitalized assets will generate future economic benefits. These rigorous procedures mean that any weakness in Indra's documentation could lead to immediate project expensing.

Sector Estimation Variations

9.
Varied completion estimations: Estimation practices for recognizing revenue under the percentage-of-completion method vary widely across the technology and defense sectors. Many service firms use a cost-to-cost input method that calculates progress based on actual costs incurred relative to estimated total costs. Other software and consulting entities rely on labor-hour input methods which depend heavily on engineering productivity assumptions. These varied methods mean that project profitability is highly sensitive to quarterly productivity adjustments.
10.
Volatile estimate revisions: Periodic revisions to long-term contract cost estimates can trigger substantial adjustments that instantly impact reported operating profit. Defense contractors recognize revisions to project estimates on a cumulative catch-up basis in the period when circumstances change. If expected development costs escalate, the full negative adjustment must be recognized in the current period. Consequently, even a slight increase in projected engineering hours could cause rapid EBIT margin contraction.

Hensoldt has historically capitalized between 35% and 65% of its cash R&D spend under the IAS 38 standard. This high capitalization rate helps support Hensoldt's near-term profitability margins during periods of heavy strategic investment. Such wide industry dispersion means that investors must normalize capitalization rates to accurately compare operating performance.

This execution lumpiness makes it difficult to forecast the precise timing of revenue recognition over the next eighteen months, particularly as complex consortia and subcontractor coordination introduce additional scheduling uncertainty. The lowest confidence areas involve three critical information gaps that represent material risks to consensus forecasts.

First, the exact scheduling and milestone structure of TESS Defence deliveries remains insufficiently disclosed. While aggregate annual delivery targets are communicated, investors lack visibility into the quarterly phasing of acceptances, the timing of milestone payments tied to specific delivery batches, and the precise contractual triggers for revenue recognition. Prime contracting activities on land platforms carry substantial passthrough content and fixed industrial costs that can erode returns, and if subcontracting costs escalate, the increased revenue from these programs will not translate into earnings growth.

Understanding the cost structure and margin evolution of individual vehicle batches would allow investors to assess whether the platform dilution is temporary or structural. Second, the legal and political challenges to state pre-financing decrees represent a binary risk that is inadequately quantified in public disclosures. Santa Bárbara challenged the state's award of EUR 7.24 bn in artillery contracts and requested a suspension of the associated zero-interest pre-financing, and if these credits are blocked, Indra must find expensive private debt to fund its rapid industrial expansion, which would severely depress net income and EPS over the next 18 months.

The National Court proceedings are progressing without transparent timelines or interim rulings, leaving investors unable to estimate the probability or timing of an adverse outcome. Furthermore, the potential for parliamentary or regulatory intervention to modify the Special Modernization Program framework remains unquantified, as political opposition parties have questioned the propriety of zero-interest state financing to private contractors.

Third, the consumption rate and working capital implications of the EUR 1.9 bn prepayment balance are obscured by aggregated reporting. Upfront prepayments of EUR 1.8 bn provide immediate liquidity, but they represent future delivery obligations that will consume cash, and as deliveries proceed, these advances will be consumed rapidly, potentially resulting in significant working-capital cash outflows. Indra does not disclose the contractual draw-down schedule tied to specific delivery milestones, the allocation of prepayments across different programs, or the expected timing of cash consumption relative to manufacturing cycles.

This opacity makes it nearly impossible to forecast the quarterly evolution of working capital and free cash flow with precision. To close these information gaps, investors should pursue several concrete actions. First, they should formally request that Indra provide quarterly delivery and milestone schedules for TESS Defence during earnings calls and investor meetings, emphasizing that this level of transparency is standard practice among European defence primes such as Rheinmetall and Leonardo.

Second, investors should directly engage with the Spanish Ministry of Defense procurement directorate to understand the typical payment terms and acceptance protocols for land vehicle contracts, as these officials possess detailed knowledge of milestone structures that are not disclosed in corporate filings. Third, institutional investors with sufficient leverage should request a private briefing on the legal status of the Santa Bárbara challenge, including the expected timeline for National Court rulings and any contingency financing arrangements that Indra has secured.

Fourth, investors should analyse peer precedents from Saab, Thales, and Rheinmetall to develop probabilistic models of working capital consumption rates under different delivery scenarios, using historical patterns of advance consumption to triangulate Indra's likely cash trajectory. Additionally, investors should scrutinize upcoming auditor transition risks by requesting detailed disclosures on the criteria used to capitalize development costs and the expected impact of any policy changes under the new audit regime.

Indra capitalized 61.4% of its development costs in the 2025 financial year compared to 40.6% in 2024, deferring EUR 144.80 mm of development expenses to future periods and directly boosting near-term operating profit margins without reflecting any underlying change in cash generation. Understanding whether the new auditor will demand more conservative capitalization policies is essential to assessing the sustainability of reported margins.

Finally, investors should monitor quarterly disclosures from Minsait to detect early signs of AI-driven pricing deflation, as pricing pressure is intensifying across European IT services as clients demand AI-driven productivity gains in renegotiations, with peers like Capgemini experiencing a shift toward fixedprice and outcome-based contracts that compress traditional time-and-materials revenue.

The range of plausible outcomes over the next eighteen months remains exceptionally wide due to these information asymmetries. In an optimistic scenario where delivery schedules are met, legal challenges are dismissed, and prepayment consumption proceeds smoothly, Indra could achieve the ambitious EPS targets of EUR 3.04 in 2026 and EUR 3.61 in 2027 forecast by Bernstein. However, in a pessimistic scenario where TESS deliveries slip, legal rulings disrupt pre-financing, and working capital reversals accelerate, the company could experience severe cash constraints and margin compression that forces substantial downward revisions to consensus estimates.

The absence of granular disclosure on these critical variables means that investors are effectively pricing the stock on incomplete information, which sustains elevated volatility and limits the potential for valuation multiple expansion until execution visibility improves materially.
