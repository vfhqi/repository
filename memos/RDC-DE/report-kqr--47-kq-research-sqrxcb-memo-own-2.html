Section 2 – The Pilsinger Initiative and GeDIG Status

Section summary
  1. The political initiative by Stephan Pilsinger to ban co-payment waivers via the GeDIG draft bill faces severe legislative and legal hurdles in Germany.
  2. While German policymakers lobby for restrictive amendments, any domestic ban remains highly vulnerable to European Union legal challenges under established precedents.

POLITICAL INITIATIVE ORIGINS

1.
Political movement: CSU Bundestag member Stephan Pilsinger launched a targeted political initiative in late October 2026 to legally prohibit EU-based mail-order pharmacies from waiving statutory patient co-payments. The practicing general practitioner and politician submitted a formal request for review to the Federal Ministry of Health to integrate this amendment into the legislative process for the draft Act on Data and Digital Innovation in Healthcare. This legislative move directly targets foreign shippers like Redcare Pharmacy that leverage these waivers to gain a strong competitive advantage.
2.
Proposed formulation: The professional organization ABDA supported Pilsinger's legislative push by submitting a concrete formulation to amend social security statutes directly to the Federal Ministry of Health. Under the proposed amendment to Section 43c SGB V, mail-order pharmacies would be explicitly banned from waiving mandated patient co-payments. Furthermore, this draft text aims to prohibit advertising with such waivers to eliminate them as a viable online marketing tool and prevent patient out-of-pocket savings.
3.
Institutional stalemate: Pilsinger justified his direct parliamentary intervention by pointing to the persistent inaction of the joint parity commission of pharmacists and health insurers. The German Pharmacists Association has repeatedly refused to initiate proceedings through this body due to perceived legal liability risks and procedural conflicts. Consequently, direct legislative intervention is now viewed by pharmacy advocates as the primary path to resolve the deadlocked regulatory conflict.
4.
Broad research: Detailed database analysis of this regulatory shift uncovered 15 distinct sources, 3 core competitor companies, and 1 relevant historical precedent. These documents reveal that the proposed amendment remains in a preparatory stage with no legally binding text yet officially enacted. This exhaustive review helps clarify whether imminent regulatory threats will materialize and harm online pharmacy profitability over the next 18 months to 3 years.
5.
Competitive tension: German local pharmacies face intense margin pressure and view the co-payment waiver as an unfair competitive threat that drains their brick-and-mortar customer base. Since local pharmacies are legally obligated to collect the full statutory co-payment, they lobby aggressively for legislative intervention. Insured patients find these voluntary waivers economically attractive, meaning any legislative restriction would directly increase out-of-pocket costs and slow down online prescription growth.

LEGISLATIVE TIMETABLE AND STAKEHOLDERS

6.
Digital timeline: The draft Act on Data and Digital Innovation in Healthcare, or GeDIG, serves as the primary legislative vehicle for Pilsinger's proposal. The federal cabinet officially adopted the GeDIG draft on 15 July 2026, following its initial ministerial publication. Parliamentary schedules target final adoption by the Bundestag before the end of 2026, leading to gradual implementation during 2027.
7.
Ministerial stance: German Health Minister Carsten Linnemann publicly addressed the competitive disparity between on-site and online pharmacies at the national congress. Linnemann criticized the unilateral marketing advantages of cross-border pharmacies and advocated for a level playing field. However, the ministry's formal adoption of Pilsinger's specific co-payment ban remains unconfirmed within current draft versions.
8.
Contrary evidence: Deutsche Bank Research highlighted that any attempts to enforce a complete ban on prescription mail-order sales face severe legal hurdles. The Scientific Service of the Bundestag previously concluded that such a restrictive measure would likely violate fundamental European Union laws. This legal opinion casts strong doubt on the political feasibility of Pilsinger's broader anti-mail-order ambitions.
9.
Strategic separation: Pilsinger aims to separate his proposed co-payment restriction from more complex billing structural reforms. He argues that the co-payment waiver must be prohibited regardless of whether statutory insurers eventually collect these funds directly. This targeted approach is designed to secure rapid legislative consensus on marketing without delaying broader healthcare bills.
10.
Insurer positions: German statutory health insurers represented by the GKV-Spitzenverband remain highly focused on managing rising pharmaceutical spending and administrative costs. While some insurer-led reforms could structurally eliminate the mail-order waiver advantage, the practical implementation of direct collection remains completely unresolved. Consequently, insurers have not yet officially backed Pilsinger's specific legislative amendment, keeping near-term political support highly uncertain.

The high court ruled that domestic drug pricing laws are not applicable to EU-based mail-order pharmacies. This decision confirmed that restricting these promotional bonuses would directly impede the free movement of goods across borders.

European protection: Jefferies Research emphasized that the Federal Court of Justice decision solidified the long- term legal safety of EU-based online pharmacies. This crucial judicial protection has effectively cleared major regulatory uncertainties for companies like Redcare Pharmacy. Consequently, any new domestic legislative proposals to restrict online bonuses face imminent European Union court challenges.

Section 3 – Direct Collection of Co-payments by Insurers

Section summary
  1. Shifting co-payment collection directly to statutory health insurers could structurally disrupt the marketing models of cross-border online pharmacies in Germany.
  2. While the proposal enjoys theoretical interest from pharmacists, complex billing workflows and significant administrative challenges limit its operational feasibility.

THE PHARMACISTS CONGRESS PROPOSAL

1.
Professional proposal: Pharmacist organizations put forward a professional-political proposal during the Deutscher Apothekertag 2026 to relieve local pharmacies of tedious co-payment collection tasks. This proposal advocates that statutory health insurance funds should collect patient co-payments directly from patients in the future. Shifting this administrative burden would fundamentally reorganize the financial flows between patients, pharmacies, and insurers. This concept has triggered a broad health policy discussion regarding drug supply organization in Germany.
2.
Current collection: Local German pharmacies currently collect statutory co-payments directly from patients upon dispensing prescription medications, unless a patient is legally exempt. These collected amounts are subsequently accounted for during the regular billing process with statutory health insurance companies. This routine process does not generate any additional remuneration for the pharmacies. Pharmacists argue that this system turns them into unpaid debt collectors for insurance funds.
3.
Complex billing: Direct collection by health insurers would require a complete reorganization of billing and payment flows. Insured individuals would no longer pay their co-payment immediately at the pharmacy counter, shifting the timing of payment. However, establishing clear billing rules and handling outstanding claims represent significant administrative hurdles for insurers. This complexity makes the practical implementation of direct collection highly uncertain and difficult to execute.
4.
Patient perspective: Insured patients would experience a shift in the timing of their healthcare expenditures under a direct collection model. The receipt of prescription drugs would no longer be tied directly to the payment of the co-payment. However, this procedural adjustment would not alter the fundamental co-payment amounts or the existing statutory exemption rules. Patients would still face the same financial obligations, but through a delayed billing process from insurers.
5.
Unresolved support: Political and institutional backing for direct insurer collection remains highly tentative due to operational complexities. No fully developed or officially documented model for transferring these collection duties currently exists in the public sphere. While the debate has gained momentum, insurers have not yet committed to taking on these expensive collection and administrative tasks. This lack of concrete planning suggests that any formal legislative draft is far from realization.
6.
Contrary evidence: German pharmacies have faced legal setbacks when attempting to block these digital promotional strategies in courts. The Higher Regional Court of Karlsruhe in December 2025 addressed a specific voucher campaign, but did not rule all co-payment promotions inadmissible. This indicates that the legal boundaries for such marketing campaigns remain open and subject to case-by-case evaluation under existing laws. Consequently, a judicial ban on such waivers is unlikely without fresh legislation.

STRUCTURAL AND LEGAL HURDLES

7.
Severe friction: German statutory health insurance organizations currently face massive administrative costs that reach 13.44 billion euros. Taking on direct co-payment collection would require insurers to manage millions of individual outstanding claims and debt-collection proceedings. This administrative burden would likely exceed any financial benefits, making health insurers highly resistant to the proposed model. Insurers are reluctant to establish new collection infrastructures when pharmacies already perform this task efficiently.
8.
Legal boundaries: European judicial rulings continue to protect the promotional pricing strategies of EU-based online pharmacies. The European Court of Justice in February 2025 ruled that advertising discounts and direct cash bonuses is generally permissible. This robust European legal framework means that any domestic German attempt to ban co-payment waivers would face immediate and severe constitutional challenges. Legal experts believe that domestic law cannot easily override the free movement of goods in the European Union.
9.
Price compensation: DocMorris and Shop Apotheke actively leverage co-payment compensation to compete with local pharmacies. While local pharmacies must collect co-payments and finance localized services, foreign mail-order pharmacies utilize copayments as an acquisition tool. This structural advantage allows foreign shippers to capture a disproportionate share of high-margin prescription drug volumes. Local pharmacies feel additional competitive pressure as more prescription turnover shifts to online channels.
10.
Operational distinction: Pharmacy associations emphasize that statutory co-payment collection must be clearly distinguished from other administrative tasks. The processing of manufacturer rebates represents an entirely different procedure that would remain unchanged by co-payment reforms. Consequently, a successful legislative proposal must precisely define the shifted administrative tasks. Reforming one process does not automatically simplify or alter other billing and regulatory requirements.
11.
Strategic leverage: Redcare's brand, Shop Apotheke, utilizes direct co-payment waivers as a powerful tool to lower patient switching thresholds. Chief Executive Officer Peter Schmid von Linstow presents this bonus policy as a controllable marketing tool. The company primarily aims to retain customers through high service quality while using initial price advantages to acquire customers. This balanced strategy ensures that Redcare does not commit to permanently high discounts while still capturing market share.

This higher visible burden on patients makes online pharmacies that offer to cover these co-payments even more attractive.

Section 4 – German Health Finance Commission Savings and Remuneration

  2. While the €9.50 fixed fee supports pharmacy revenues, the statutory rebate increase and higher patient co-payments present a complex outlook for margins.

To address this deficit, the commission proposed 66 measures that could reshape the country's statutory health insurance system. These sweeping options leave politicians to negotiate difficult trade-offs regarding who ultimately pays for healthcare.
2.
Gradual stabilization efforts: The German government is actively preparing a reform package aiming to reduce costs by nearly €20 billion in 2027. According to Deutsche Bank Research, this package will focus on shortterm measures to stabilize contributions to statutory health insurance. A second package with long-term structural measures is expected to follow by the end of 2026.
3.
Rising industry tension: Financing decisions in the healthcare sector directly affect pharmacy operators through rising costs and strict regulatory requirements. Contrary evidence: Germany's GKV estimate committee expects a significant slowdown in healthcare expenditures with an estimated increase of only 2.2% in 2027.
