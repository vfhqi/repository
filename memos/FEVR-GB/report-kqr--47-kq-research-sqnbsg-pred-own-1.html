Section 2 – Sugar Content Profile of Fevertree's Main UK Products

Section summary
  1. Fevertree faces direct product exposure under the revised sugar tax thresholds.
  2. Distinct sugar profiles across the product range create divergent reformulation risks.

Sugar Levels in Core Mixer Portfolios

1.
Elevated sweetness: Fevertree's core Premium Indian Tonic Water contains 8.0g of sugar per 100ml, which puts it at the highest tier of the current and future levy systems. This sugar density means the flagship product is subject to the highest rate of the UK sugar tax. Unless the company decides to reformulate this recipe, the product will remain in the higher tax band when the new regulations take effect in 2028.
2.
Varying levels: Fevertree's broader portfolio of ginger beverages and adult sodas features diverse sugar profiles across individual product lines. While the Refreshingly Light Tonic Water contains lower sugar levels to target wellness trends, the popular Premium Ginger Beer carries a heavier sugar load to maintain its distinct taste profile. This variation creates divergent tax risks across the portfolio because the standard mixers will face higher tax pressure under the upcoming levy system.
3.
Lower density: Fevertree's light portfolio has seen accelerating volume share because consumers are actively moving away from high-sugar mixers. The Refreshingly Light Tonic represents a substantial portion of the UK sales mix and sits safely below the proposed 4.5g per 100ml regulatory threshold. This low-sugar positioning shields the light portfolio from future tax penalties while capturing the growing consumer preference for low-calorie options.

Comparative Analysis of Premium and Mainstream Peers

4.
Stronger protection: Competitor AG Barr is well-positioned for the upcoming regulatory shift because the vast majority of its soft drink portfolio sits below the new sugar limits. Specifically, 96% of its soft drinks currently contain less than 5g of sugar per 100ml, while its Nichols portfolio is entirely below the 4.5g threshold. This extensive compliance reduces the financial pressure on AG Barr relative to premium competitors who rely on sugar-sweetened mixers.
5.
Contrary evidence: Some industry analysts suggest that Fevertree's high-sugar profile does not represent a critical threat because premium consumers are highly price-inelastic. For example, a typical price increase under the sugar tax represents only a small percentage of the total retail price of a premium pack. Consequently, the company may comfortably pass the tax on to consumers without experiencing a significant drop in sales volume.
6.
Mainstream rivalry: Mainstream competitor Schweppes, owned by Coca-Cola Europacific Partners, continues to challenge Fevertree's market share by offering cheaper and more diverse low-sugar alternatives. Schweppes has historically captured market share from Fevertree during periods of consumer belt-tightening by leveraging its affordable Slimline range and aggressive promotional pricing. This competitive pressure limits Fevertree's ability to raise prices on its sugar-taxed premium lines without risking consumer downtrading.
7.
Different positioning: Premium competitor London Essence, owned by Britvic, has expanded its range of low- calorie crafted sodas to directly compete with Fevertree's adult soft drinks. London Essence has maintained strong sales momentum in the UK off-premise channel by targeting the premium health-and-wellness segment with lighter options. This premium positioning allows Britvic to avoid the heavy sugar tax burdens that face Fevertree's standard product ranges.

Dynamic Elasticity and Pricing Risks

1.
Severe volume erosion: Fevertree Drinks PLC could face unexpectedly high price elasticity of demand if it attempts to pass the full cost of the Soft Drinks Industry Levy to retail shoppers. While some market commentators assume premium consumers are price-insensitive, a high-elasticity scenario where price elasticity of demand reaches negative two would trigger a massive volume drop. This volume contraction would significantly compress domestic gross profits and directly drag down group EPS over the next three years.
2.
Intense retail pushback: UK grocery giants like Tesco and Sainsbury's possess the power to block Fevertree's planned price increases to protect their own commercial margins. Expert insights from retail category managers show that supermarket merchants act as the absolute CEOs of their shelves and tightly control final shelf prices. If these grocery partners refuse to accept cost increases, Fevertree will be forced to absorb the tax burden internally. This unpassed tax absorption would contract operating profit and dilute domestic margins by over 50 basis points.
3.
Favorable legal resolution: Fevertree's ongoing legal challenge against UK environmental packaging regulations could provide an unexpected earnings buffer that offsets sugar tax headwinds. Broker analysis from Jefferies indicates that a successful resolution of the EPR dispute represents an immediate five percent upside to company EBIT. This positive regulatory wildcard would neutralize potential gross margin compression and strengthen underlying earnings per share if domestic mixer volumes remain under pressure.
4.
Adverse consumer backlash: Fevertree faces significant brand equity risks if it reformulates its premium mixers using artificial or natural sugar substitutes like stevia. Expert insights from food scientists reveal that up to 27% of global consumers actively avoid products containing alternative sweeteners due to metallic aftertastes. Rushing these reformulations to avoid the tax could trigger immediate customer rejection and damage the brand's premium credentials relative to mainstream competitors.

Competitive and Channel Distortions

5.
Accelerated private substitution: Cash-constrained UK consumers are increasingly trading down to high-quality private label alternatives, which threatens Fevertree's off-premise market share. Expert insights indicate that major supermarkets are aggressively expanding their own-brand carbonated mixers to capture value-seeking shoppers during persistent inflation. This structural shift towards private label mixers would severely restrict Fevertree's volume recovery and limit its long-term pricing power in the grocery channel.
6.
Divergent peer resilience: Mainstream competitors like AG Barr and Nichols remain highly insulated from these alternative scenarios because of their extensive low-sugar portfolios. Broker research from Deutsche Bank shows that Nichols' entire UK packaged portfolio sits safely below the new 4.5g sugar threshold, while 96% of AG Barr's drinks are tax-compliant. This superior structural positioning means that any sudden tax rate increases will disadvantage Fevertree's regular variants while protecting peer operating margins.
7.
Severe hospitality contraction: Fevertree's highly profitable on-trade channel faces severe downside risks from persistent cost inflation and potential regulatory tightening across the UK hospitality sector. Reports from UKHospitality in April 2026 warn that 15% of venues face permanent closure, which would severely restrict mixer volumes. If on-trade demand collapses further, the resulting negative mix shift would severely dilute group profitability and suppress future domestic sales.

Mitigation and Cost Realities

8.
Accelerated portfolio diversification: Fevertree could mitigate these non-consensus pricing pressures by accelerating its strategic entry into the rapidly growing adult soft drinks and cocktail mixer categories. Company reports highlight that these non-tonic categories are expanding quickly to target younger, health-conscious consumers who are actively moderating their alcohol consumption. Successfully scaling these alternative categories would reduce the brand's dependence on traditional high-sugar mixers and unlock high-margin growth occasions across global retail channels.
9.
Significant margin sensitivity: Fevertree's financial model remains highly vulnerable to any sudden inflation in domestic packaging and distribution costs that coincides with the tax implementation. Industry reports from peer companies like Varun Beverages indicate that packaging material cost spikes can contract gross margins by over 300 basis points. If these input cost pressures persist and EBITDA margins remain flat, the company would face significant earnings downgrades across the street.
10.
Contrary evidence: Some industry experts suggest that Fevertree's premium brand equity is strong enough to completely bypass the negative impacts of retailer pushback and consumer substitution. Expert insights from former competitors indicate that Fevertree functions as the undisputed leader of premium mixers with unmatched brand credentials. Consequently, the company may successfully implement price-pack downs and fully pass on tax costs without experiencing any material drop in sales volumes.
11.
Other side of the story: While high price elasticity and retailer resistance represent credible threats, Fevertree's ongoing shift toward low-sugar mixers and non-tonic formats provides a highly effective buffer. The brand's Refreshingly Light range and flavored sodas already account for over 40% of its global sales, protecting core revenues from the 2028 threshold drop. Therefore, the company's diversified portfolio and strong brand equity will insulate it from non-consensus risks and maintain stable mid-term earnings.
