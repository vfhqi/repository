EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

CEO Quality and Background

Operator-track executive with deep engineering and finance background: Eamonn O'Hare holds a BSc in Aerospace Engineering from Queen's University Belfast and an MBA from London Business School, transitioning from turnarounds at FTSE-100/200 corporates to co-founding Zegona as a buy-fix-sell TMT vehicle. Multi-decade transformation track record: O'Hare co-led the strategic transformation of Virgin Media as CFO from 2009 to 2013, culminating in its sale to Liberty Global for $24 billion and crystallising $14 billion in incremental shareholder value. Limited operational line management experience: The dual Chairman-CEO mandate combined with an executive finance background may lack independent board friction and direct operational line-management experience outside financial turnarounds.

CEO Archetype Match and Leadership Style

Uncompromising owner-operator archetype: O'Hare exhibits plain-spoken directness and absolute deal discipline, stating "I've always said to our investors, I'd rather do no deal than do a bad deal" and emphasizing Zegona is "really just going to be looking for the big opportunities like we have in Spain, where we can get stuck in over three, five, seven, nine years." - M&A/Special - Earnings Calls Exceptional decision velocity: O'Hare completed a sweeping 28% headcount reduction and renegotiated major supplier contracts within 12 months of acquiring Vodafone Spain to expand EBITDAaL margins to 34%, while executing a landmark €3.7 billion refinancing in June 2026 capturing €60 million in annual interest savings.

Direct owner-manager alignment: The executive team holds a significant direct stake, with O'Hare owning 35.3 million shares representing 4.65% of issued capital, reinforcing direct alignment with public equity holders.

Delivery Record and Financial Track Record

Exceptional value-creation track record: Eamonn O'Hare has executed Zegona's Buy-Fix-Sell mandate over an 11-year tenure, generating an 88% return on net invested capital in Euskaltel, with Vodafone Spain operational cash flow reaching €625 million for the twelve months to March 2025, expanding from c.€400 million at acquisition.

Aggressive margin expansion delivery: EBITDAaL margin expanded over 200 basis points to 34% by March 2025, supported by a 28% headcount reduction and sweeping contract renegotiations, with broadband lines expanding by 22,000 year-on-year to 2.59 million in Q1 FY27 alongside 345,000 net mobile additions. Disciplined infrastructure monetization: Management negotiated two major network JVs including FiberPass with Telefónica and a 12 million premise FibreCo with MasOrange, unlocking infrastructure value while announcing a €1.6 billion shareholder return comprising a €1.4 billion special dividend and €200 million buyback funded via €1.8 billion in FibreCo transaction net proceeds. Structural FibreCo dependency risk: The aggressive carve-out of fixed network assets into FibreCo JVs creates structural dependency on third-party infrastructure; if long-term wholesale access rates escalate or subscriber churn re-accelerates, future operational free cash flow growth could be impaired.

Governance Robustness and Board Independence

Concentrated governance structure: Zegona maintains a combined Chairman-CEO with Eamonn O'Hare holding both positions, non-compliant with the UK Corporate Governance Code, and operates without a designated Senior Independent Director, limiting structured shareholder escalation pathways outside executive leadership. Strengthened independent oversight: Recent NED refreshment has eliminated long-tenured directors from audit and remuneration oversight, achieving 43% female board representation ahead of market norms, with four of eight current directors qualifying as independent, expanding to 57% of the board following Ashley Martin's departure. Robust committee independence: Audit and Risk Committee is 100% independent, chaired by former telecom CFO Tim Pennington from April 2026, with the board splitting the joint Nomination and Remuneration Committee in June 2025 to establish standalone governance bodies. Persistent governance friction gap: The persistent combination of Chairman and CEO roles alongside zero non-executive equity ownership risks permitting Eamonn O'Hare to execute high-leverage M&A without robust board friction if operational execution slows.

Executive Compensation and Insider Signals

Exceptional executive equity ownership: Formal shareholding guidelines remain absent, but exceptional executive equity ownership of over 22% far exceeds European telecom peer medians, with executive packages featuring low fixed base salaries and capped annual bonuses driving over 95% of total potential compensation through value creation incentives.

Management Incentive Scheme alignment: The MIS entitles management to 15% of total growth in Zegona's value provided ordinary shareholders achieve a 5% per annum preferred return, with management exercising 515,464 Management Shares in October 2024 generating GBP 129.28 million for O'Hare and GBP 64.64 million for Samuelson following Euskaltel proceeds. MIS dilution and sector multiple risk: The 15% value-share carry under the Management Incentive Scheme can dilute public equity holders by several hundred million euros during major monetization events; if share price expansion is driven primarily by sector multiple expansion rather than operational cost extraction, management receives outsized capital gains relative to underlying operational performance.

Expert Sentiment and Sell-Side Reception

Expert network scepticism on tower tactics: Former industry executives and tower experts characterize Zegona's public threats to replicate Vantage's network as an aggressive negotiation tactic rather than an operational reality, viewing Zegona's demand for a 40% discount from Vantage Towers as high-stakes negotiation rather than a genuine operational pivot given the technical complexity and capital intensity of replicating an established tower network. Acute workforce morale strain: Management executed a 1,200 job cut eliminating 28% of Vodafone Spain's staff post-acquisition, with industry experts reporting repeated workforce reductions severely strained internal morale and damaged service delivery capability, while negative public reputation from ongoing layoffs impairs talent acquisition creating long-term operational execution risks.

Largest Credibility Risk

Infrastructure partner alienation and tower negotiation failure: Zegona's aggressive vendor negotiations and public threats to abandon Vantage Towers could alienate key infrastructure partners; if tower operators refuse to grant lease concessions and call Zegona's bluff on network replication, Zegona faces either severe site migration costs or an inability to deliver its projected €60 million in annual tower savings. Regulatory and FibreCo carve-out exposure: The aggressive structural restructuring and FibreCo network carve-outs create heightened regulatory exposure under Spanish competition law; if Spanish telecom regulators enforce mandatory wholesale tariff cuts or mandate accelerated 5G high-risk vendor removal, projected operational cash conversion could face material downside pressure.
