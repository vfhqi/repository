Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. TKH Group stands at a historic crossroads as it separates its Electrification business to focus on asset-light Automation.
  2. The separation will transform continuing EPS but exposes the transition to execution and stranded cost risks.

The Key Question and Scope

1.
Key Question: "how concentrated or diffuse are the drivers that separate the next 18 months to four years' financials from the last four to six years, and how wide and how skewed is the range of plausible outcomes over the next 18 months?"
2.
Extensive research base: Our comprehensive analysis reviewed more than 100 relevant sources to address this key question, including detailed broker reports, official company filings, and expert interview transcripts spanning from 2023 to 2026. This robust empirical base evaluates seven named peer companies and six historical corporate precedents such as Smiths Group and Sandvik. Although this extensive dataset provides high confidence regarding the strategic drivers, it highlights thin evidence on post-separation central cost allocation.

Earnings Drivers and Divestment Risks

3.
Material separation impact: The carve-out of Electrification will fundamentally reshape continuing earnings per share because this segment represents approximately 30% of total group turnover. While the transaction aims to unlock value, it introduces significant near-term volatility and changes the risk profile of the parent. Jefferies highlights that the Electrification segment is currently supported by a substantial medium-term recovery, with EBITDA margins expected to exceed 19% compared to only 12.6% in the first half of 2026.
4.
Capital expenditure dynamics: Normalizing capital intensity represents another critical driver for continuing financials because the historical period was heavily burdened by a massive 200 million Euro strategic investment program. With the subsea cable plant in Eemshaven now fully operational, future capital expenditures are expected to align with depreciation. This shift from capital-intensive cable manufacturing to asset-light automation should substantially improve cash conversion.
5.
Executive transition risks: The simultaneous leadership transition complicates the upcoming operational execution because Guido Grandi takes over as group chief executive on 1 January 2027. He succeeds Alexander van der Lof, who moves to lead the separated Electrification business. While Grandi brings extensive industrial experience from Norma Group, managing a new reporting perimeter alongside a new chief executive increases execution risk.

Related Questions and Counter-Views

6.
Contrary evidence: The proposed restructuring may face severe delays from hostile financing conditions and offshore wind developer stress. Jefferies warned that changing market circumstances, such as high interest rates, could delay the divestment program and impact valuations. Furthermore, the Eemshaven facility has faced persistent start-up complexities and output issues in past quarters, suggesting that a smooth separation is not guaranteed.
7.
Unresolved related questions: Key questions remain regarding the exact scale of the share buyback and the treatment of remaining central costs. Management indicates that excess cash will be returned to shareholders, but this is subject to keeping debt leverage below 2.0. Additionally, the establishment of separate corporate entities will inevitably create duplicate IT, legal, and rebranding costs that could dilute continuing EPS.
8.
Other side of the story: Supporter views argue that the separation will immediately eliminate the conglomerate discount which has historically exceeded 45% to sum-of-the-parts value. By separating the capitalintensive cable business, the parent company will become a pure-play automation leader with higher returns. However, this optimistic scenario assumes that VMI tyre-machinery orders recover rapidly and that stranded central costs do not overwhelm the smaller continuing perimeter.

Segment transition complexity: Continuing operations will require significant reorganization as TKH transitions into a pure-play, asset-light automation business. Section 3 – Parent Company Performance Post-Divestment

Section summary
  1. Historical precedents show that mid-cap parent companies separating major divisions face severe continuing earnings dispersion due to transaction leakages and stranded corporate overhead.
  2. Cash proceeds from divestments are frequently returned through buybacks, but operational execution in the remaining perimeters often determines long-term EPS trajectory.

Precedents of Parent Performance Post-Divestment

1.
Smiths Group medical separation: The historical separation of Smiths Medical by Smiths Group provides a prime example of the earnings and margin adjustments that follow a major carve-out. Smiths Group completed the sale of Smiths Medical to ICU Medical on 6 January 2022 for an enterprise value of $2.7 billion. Following this divestment, Smiths Group reclassified Smiths Medical as discontinued operations, which significantly altered the continuing perimeter's financials. Jefferies highlights that while the sale of Medical focused the group on its industrial technology core, continuing operations initially faced growth challenges as the group's through-cycle organic revenue growth struggled to immediately accelerate. Continuing operations margin was re-based, with the group targeting an operating profit margin of 18% to 20% post-separation.
2.
Smiths Group multi-asset divestment: In a subsequent strategic phase, Smiths Group executed further portfolio high-grading by divesting two more segments. During its fiscal year 2026, Smiths Group completed the sales of Smiths Interconnect to Molex for £1.3 billion and Smiths Detection to CVC Capital Partners for £2.0 billion, crystallizing £3.3 billion in combined enterprise value. These transactions left the continuing parent company, "Smiths", as a focused premium industrial engineering company specializing in flow management and thermal solutions. Following the divestments, continuing operations reported fiscal year 2026 revenue of £1,937 million, representing a modest 1.2% organic growth, while the headline operating margin stood at 20.6%. Jefferies notes that although these disposals successfully built a high-margin premium business, the transition year of 2026 was marked by near-term trading bumps and downward revisions to short-term consensus estimates.
3.
Sandvik Alleima spin-off: Sandvik's distribution of its Materials Technology business (rebranded as Alleima) on 31 August 2022 represents a clean structural separation of a capital-intensive, lower-margin segment. The spin-off was completed through a master separation agreement, transferring all SMT assets and liabilities to Alleima. Deutsche Bank notes that the spin-off of Alleima was a transformational move for Sandvik, making the parent group structurally more profitable and less cyclical. the listing of Alleima, Sandvik's remaining core became heavily geared toward highly profitable mining aftermarket and short-cycle machining, with through-cycle EBITA margins consistently targeted at 20% to 22%. Capital Return and Share-Count Outcomes

5.
Smiths Group capital returns: Mid-cap parents that divest significant portions of their revenue often use structured buybacks to mitigate earnings dilution. Following the early completion of its Smiths Medical sale, Smiths Group launched a £750 million share buyback program, which was completed in July 2023 and achieved a 12% reduction in its total share count. Upon completing the Interconnect and Detection sales in 2026, the board immediately launched a massive £1.5 billion share buyback program to return the majority of the transaction proceeds. Rothschild & Co Redburn notes that these massive capital returns were essential to support group EPS growth, with continuing headline EPS rising to 86.8 pence in FY2026, up from 81.9 pence in the prior year.
6.
Aalberts capital allocation: Rather than returning the entirety of divestment proceeds, some parents balance capital returns with strategic acquisitions. Following its €400 million divestment program, Aalberts announced a €75 million share buyback program in February 2026, representing approximately 2% of its market capitalization. However, Aalberts primarily directed its cash toward high-margin acquisitions, such as the purchase of Grand Venture Technology (GVT) in Southeast Asia. Jefferies highlights that this balanced capital allocation approach was highly successful, as the GVT integration performed ahead of expectations, generating 30% annualized revenue growth and supporting a rapid EBITA margin recovery. Ramsay Health Care buyback analysis: Parent buybacks funded by divestments face strict financial hurdles in high interest rate regimes. In an analysis of potential UK divestments for Ramsay Health Care, Jefferies calculated that divesting UK assets would be structurally EPS dilutive by 6% because the after-tax yield on cash proceeds is lower than the operating yield of the assets. Utilizing surplus cash to buy back shares at an earnings yield of 4.3% to 4.4% narrows the EPS dilution but cannot completely offset it unless the assets are sold at a massive premium. Jefferies argues that transaction leakage, including taxes, separation fees, and stranded overhead, dramatically raises the break-even sale multiple required to keep the transaction earningsneutral.

Section 4 – Automated Machinery Cycles and Backlog Recoveries

Section summary
  1. VMI's severe order backlog contraction highlights the vulnerability of TKH's continuing perimeter to tyre-maker spending cycles.
  2. Competitors like Mesnac and HF Group are aggressively expanding as tyre manufacturers maintain strict capital freezes.
  3. Historical precedents indicate that capital equipment troughs often persist longer than sell-side expectations assume.

Cyclical Downturns and Backlog Contractions

1.
Severe backlog contraction: TKH's Automated Machinery segment has experienced a severe contraction in its order book due to delayed procurement. Jefferies' report on 12 August 2026 notes that VMI's backlog declined by 38% as major tyre manufacturers postponed key investment decisions. This significant drop reflects a broader industry-wide reluctance to commit capital amid macroeconomic uncertainties. Consequently, nearterm continuing earnings per share will remain under pressure until these backlogs begin to rebuild.
2.
Delayed order placements: Persistent geopolitical friction and elevated inflation continue to delay automated machinery order placements globally. Across our research of 12 sources, 4 companies, and 3 comparable past cases, high energy costs are cited as primary drivers of client hesitation. While the long-term structural drivers for advanced automation remain intact, short-term demand remains severely suppressed. Unless these macro headwinds subside, VMI will struggle to restore its previous peak production rates.
3.
Working capital pressure: The reduction in incoming orders has strained TKH's operational cash conversion cycles. TKH's AGM minutes from 13 August 2026 highlight that lower order intake reduces the advance payments received from customers, driving working capital to 17% of turnover. This is significantly above the company's long-term target range of 12% to 15%. This capital tie-up directly reduces the funds available for immediate share buybacks or debt reduction.

Tyre Maker Capex Dynamics and Competitors

4.
Industry capital freeze: Major tyre manufacturers are maintaining a strict capital expenditure freeze with an undated end. Nokian Tyres' earnings call on 6 May 2025 indicated a shift back to basic maintenance capital expenditures, limiting future investments. Michelin's Q3 2026 earnings release also confirmed volumes declined 1.5% due to weaker original equipment markets. This widespread capital discipline among tyre makers delays the cyclical recovery for equipment providers.
5.
Competitive market dynamics: VMI faces intense competition from reorganized global equipment suppliers like Mesnac and HF Group. Possehl's 2025 annual report reveals that HF Mixing Group and HF TireTech merged in 2024 to create a unified technology player. Meanwhile, news reports on 18 December 2024 show Mesnac investing $20 million in a new Guanajuato plant to capture nearshoring demand. This competitive push threatens VMI's dominant 70% share of the outsourced market.
6.
Competitor technology initiatives: Key rivals are introducing innovative sustainable manufacturing solutions to attract capital-constrained tyre builders. HF Group's 2025 report highlights their new electric curing press and "Smart Final Mix" digital services that reduce emissions. These product developments directly target the sustainability and efficiency demands of global tyre makers. VMI must continuously innovate to defend its technology leadership against these high-end competitive threats.

VMI Order Targets and UNIXX Platform

7.
Quarterly order target: Sell-side expectations for VMI's recovery rely on achieving eighty million Euros in quarterly order intake. Historical data from TKH's 2011 annual results release shows H2 order intake previously bottomed at €80 million. Current consensus models expect a similar baseline to signal an operational turnaround. However, VMI's current run rate remains depressed, and achieving this target consistently requires a synchronized re-engagement of Tier 1 buyers.
8.
UNIXX technology commercialization: VMI's revolutionary UNIXX platform represents a critical driver for future higher-margin growth. Jefferies' coverage initiation on 24 May 2024 reports that UNIXX systems are priced above €10 million each, combining component preparation and assembly. VMI successfully delivered its first full platform in 2025 and secured initial repeat orders. While UNIXX expands VMI's addressable market, its long installation periods delay near-term revenue recognition.
9.
Contrary evidence: The transition to next-generation systems can be severely delayed by lengthy homologation processes. Jefferies' report on 24 May 2024 notes that Tier 1 tyre manufacturers require extensive testing for new machinery, and VMI must compete with customers' own in-house engineering departments. This complex approval process means VMI's revolutionary UNIXX platform cannot drive an immediate, sharp recovery in earnings per share.
10.
Premium brand recognition: VMI's technological superiority has been validated by prestigious industry accolades from leading global brands. TKH Group's press release on 23 October 2024 announced VMI winning the Pirelli Supplier of the Year Award. Pirelli recognized VMI's UNIXX technology for improving process flexibility and reducing material waste. This strong commercial validation positions VMI as the preferred partner once capital expenditure freezes are lifted.

Historical Recovery Precedents and Dispersion

11.
Trough duration dispersion: Historical machinery cycles indicate that order troughs can persist much longer than initial forecasts suggest. Morgan Stanley's factory automation report on 13 August 2026 notes the current machine tool recovery cycle has extended to 39 months. This is significantly longer than the rapid 13-to-14- month recoveries seen after the 2008 and 2020 crises. This suggests that VMI's current order slump could linger well into 2027 before showing a meaningful inflection.
12.
Replacement wave skepticism: Investor forecasts that rely on an imminent replacement wave often face disappointment due to extended machine lifespans. Morgan Stanley's mining equipment report on 19 October 2026 notes that fleet operators frequently choose servicing and rebuilds over new purchases. This extends the life of existing assets and defers capital expenditure. Consequently, VMI's replacement cycle, which covers 40% of the non-TKH installed base, may develop as a gradual plateau rather than a sharp peak.
13.
Post-backlog sales lag: The historical relationship between new orders and reported sales exhibits a multi-year lag during recoveries. Bernstein's capital goods report on 15 October 2026 explains that post-pandemic backlogs took two years longer than normal to reconnect with underlying demand. Companies continued to report revenue growth from old backlogs even as new orders collapsed. This means VMI's reported revenues will lag any eventual order recovery by several quarters.
14.
Other side of the story: Optimistic sell-side analysts argue that VMI will experience a powerful recovery driven by unavoidable nearshoring and sustainability investments. Jefferies' report on 25 September 2025 asserts that plant modernisations and regional supply chain reconfigurations will force Tier 1 manufacturers to resume spending. However, TKH Group's own H1 2026 report cautions that geopolitical tensions and high energy costs can prolong order delays indefinitely. This persistent headwind would leave VMI's order book flat and continue diluting parent earnings.
