Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Regulators across Europe are under pressure to raise allowed returns to prevent stranded asset risks.
  2. If other countries do not follow Spain's supportive template, the European regulated utility investment thesis will restrict.

Defining the critical regulatory linkages

1.
Investigating regulatory harmonization: "Spain's regulator made its gas rules for 2027 to 2032 final in September 2026, with a 6.46% allowed financial return and stronger incentives to extend asset lives; do brokers and regulatory experts expect the gas resets still ahead (Portugal's gas review for 2028, Belgium's 2028 to 2031 tariffs for Fluxys, Italy's gas transport return from 2028, and Romania's next period for Transgaz) to follow the same pattern of higher allowed returns, life-extension payments or faster depreciation, and what do they say each would add to earnings at REN, Fluxys Belgium, Snam and Transgaz?"
2.
Elevated return requirements: Regulated network operators face structural earnings risks unless allowed returns rise to offset higher refinancing costs. The September 2026 Spanish decision setting a 6.46% return serves as a benchmark for capital recovery. If other regulators follow Spain's template of higher WACC and lifeextension incentives, it will safeguard long-term EPS. Conversely, stable or lower returns will compress return spreads. Equity analysts recognize that higher allowed financial returns are crucial for maintaining the viability of these capital-intensive gas grids.
3.
Mitigating asset stranding: Declining gas demand across Europe means operators must recover their investments much faster before grids become underutilized. Regulators can address this by shortening depreciation periods or introducing life-extension payments. Bernstein's report of 8 October 2026 highlights that Spain and France are leading with proactive depreciation policies. Unless peer regulators adopt similar accelerated depreciation measures, companies face severe asset impairment risks that will suppress future earnings per share. Proactive regulatory templates are essential to support shareholder returns.
4.
Interconnected infrastructure questions: Closely related questions concern how electricity network expansions and green hydrogen investments will compete for capital. For example, Enagás has proposed a large-scale hydrogen capex plan of €3.3 billion to transition away from fossil gas. Investors are questioning whether funding these projects will dilute current EPS unless supported by public grants. Thus, WACC resets are linked to broader energy transition investment programs.

Market re-ratings and peer exposure

5.
Inconsistent equity re-ratings: Enagás and Transgaz have experienced substantial stock re-ratings as regulatory clarity and financial metrics improved. Morgan Stanley notes that Enagás' consensus EPS estimates rose 11% following positive revisions to Spain's gas transport framework. Meanwhile, Transgaz reached an all-time high of 95 lei on 30 June 2026, supported by an upgraded credit rating and strong cash generation. These developments indicate that supportive regulatory changes can quickly drive valuation multiples higher across the sector.
6.
Vulnerable return spreads: Pure-play network operators like Snam and Redes Energéticas Nacionais remain highly sensitive to upcoming regulatory reviews. Bernstein estimates that a 30bp WACC reduction from ARERA would lower Snam's EBITDA by €89 million. Meanwhile, Redes Energéticas Nacionais faces a gas reset in 2028 that will dictate its capital recovery. If returns do not rise, REN's EPS will face downward pressure under rising bond yields. Therefore, these peers carry the story's remaining valuation gain only if they match Spain's positive precedent.
7.
Compounding regulatory pressure: Fluxys Belgium is grappling with the declining role of natural gas in the energy mix, which threatens to leave assets stranded. Although the company's net profit rose to €45.3 million in the first half of 2026, long-term asset value is highly dependent on tariff revisions. Investors are watching whether the Belgian regulator will adopt shorter depreciation lives or nominal WACC remuneration. Without such structural updates, Fluxys faces unrecovered investments as demand declines.
8.
Other side of the story: Some analysts caution that the Spanish regulatory template is idiosyncratic and unlikely to be replicated in other jurisdictions. Deutsche Bank emphasizes that regulatory proposals can change materially before being finalized, meaning Spain's high 6.46% return could remain an isolated event. If other regulators maintain lower allowed returns, Snam and REN will de-rate as their EPS forecasts are cut. This would restrict the bullish network thesis and limit the sector's remaining upside.

Section 2 – Spain's 2027-2032 Gas Rules as the Regulatory Benchmark

Section summary
  1. Spain's final gas framework provides a supportive benchmark with a 6.46% return.
  2. New asset life incentives protect cash flows but fail to address stranded asset risks.

Spanish allowed return mechanics

1.
Uplifted regulatory returns: Spain's CNMC finalized the pre-tax nominal WACC at 6.46% for the 2027 to 2032 period. This represents a significant increase from the 5.44% return allowed in the current cycle. The upgrade aligns the framework with higher financing costs to improve core gas network viability.
2.
WACC parameter calibration: The 6.46% allowed transport return is built on a 3.27% risk-free rate and 0.733 beta. This WACC compares with a 6.58% return set for Spanish electricity distribution. The lower gas return reflects the regulator's lower risk perception, which means Enagás earns a lower spread than electricity peers.

Asset life extension mechanisms

3.
Upgraded REVU incentives: The REVU mechanism guarantees the availability of assets that have exceeded their regulatory lives. Under the final rules, Enagás will receive around €63 million per year in REVU payments. This is up from the €19 million average received previously, providing substantial cash flow support to offset structural RAB erosion.
4.
Asset valorization incentives: The final methodology introduces a new IFVA incentive to promote useful life extensions. This mechanism applies when 75% of an asset class is depreciated. Operators can earn a WACC return on 10% of replacement value, establishing clear economic signals for operators.

Structural risks and valuation impacts

5.
Contrary evidence: Spain's framework does not incorporate any specific mechanism to mitigate long-term stranded asset risk. Unlike French networks that benefit from shortened depreciation, Spain keeps straight-line depreciation unchanged. This omission suggests the Spanish model is idiosyncratic and fragile because it fails to protect against declining gas volumes.
6.
Market valuation re-rating: Enagás shares re-rated to trade at a 30% premium to its natural gas RAB. This valuation is up from a 15% premium before the draft regulatory rules were published. The re-rating indicates that supportive incentive parameters have successfully driven equity multiples higher.
7.
Refinancing and growth headwind: Morgan Stanley downgraded Enagás to Underweight, setting a price target of €16. The broker notes that rising bond yields create pressure because Spain's allowed return is fixed with no indexation. High gas exposure and delayed hydrogen investments could trigger a de-rating, which means upward revisions are completed.
8.
Other side of the story: Some experts believe Spain's new framework offers sufficient long-term stability for grid operators. They argue the 6.46% return and higher operational opex margins guarantee reasonable profitability. However, without annual adjustments for inflation, Enagás must absorb rising costs through efficiency programs, creating a vulnerable position for equity returns.

Section 3 – Portugal's 2028 Gas Reset for Redes Energéticas Nacionais

Section summary
  1. Portugal's upcoming 2028 gas reset is highly expected to mimic the country's recent electricity WACC uplift.
  2. Dynamic WACC indexation shields Redes Energéticas Nacionais from interest rate fluctuations better than Spanish peers.

Portuguese regulatory return parameters

1.
Depressed current returns: The current Portuguese gas transmission framework is relatively unfavorable for operators. Bernstein's note of 2 September 2026 shows that the allowed pre-tax nominal return is set at 5.34% for the 2024 to 2027 regulatory period. This return represents a severe discount to electricity peers despite the higher structural risks.
2.
Favorable electricity precedent: Portugal's regulator ERSE has demonstrated a willingness to support network operators in adjacent sectors. According to Bernstein's report of 2 September 2026, the pre-tax nominal WACC for Portuguese electricity transmission was raised to 6.19% for the 2026 to 2029 period. This regulatory decision establishes a strong precedent that supports a constructive future gas reset in 2028.

Anticipating the 2028 gas reset

3.
Reverting historical premiums: Historically, Portuguese gas network returns have carried a premium over electricity to compensate for asset stranding. Bernstein's analysis of 2 September 2026 explains that gas networks carry greater perceived risk than electricity infrastructure. If ERSE restores this historical premium, the 2028 gas WACC could exceed the current electricity rate of 6.19% and meaningfully boost regulatory earnings.

Indexation protection mechanisms: Unlike Spanish networks with fixed returns, Portugal incorporates an annual indexation mechanism to mitigate macro volatility. Bernstein's report of 2 September 2026 highlights that a 100 basis point shift in Portuguese government bond yields translates into a 30 basis point adjustment in allowed returns. This dynamic framework protects REN from detrimental refinancing cost pressures during inflationary periods.

Asset life and incentive programs

5.
Limited incentive packages: The ongoing Portuguese gas regulatory period offers very limited operational upside. Bernstein's report of 2 September 2026 indicates that the 2024 to 2027 framework features only a 2% annual opex efficiency factor and lacks profit-sharing mechanisms. Consequently, REN cannot easily generate operational regulatory outperformance without structural changes in 2028.
6.
Contrary evidence: Bernstein's report of 8 October 2026 notes that Portugal's current framework has no explicit mechanisms to address stranded-asset risks. Unlike Spain's innovative REVU and IFVA systems, Portugal has failed to introduce life-extension payments for gas, which exposes operators to asset stranding.

Financial and valuation impacts

7.
Projected earnings trajectory: REN's near-term growth is driven primarily by electricity assets rather than gas. Bernstein's note of 2 September 2026 projects that REN's adjusted EPS will grow at a 6.8% compound annual rate through 2029, aided by fiscal tailwinds. However, the structurally flat gas transmission business will weigh on reported net income unless the 2028 reset provides an uplift.
8.
Upgraded price targets: Supportive electricity returns and positive fiscal tailwinds have already triggered upward valuation adjustments. Bernstein's report of 2 September 2026 upgraded REN's price target to 4.35 euros per share. This upgrade reflects strong operational cash flows and the potential recovery of tax claims, even as the gas business remains temporarily depressed.
9.
Other side of the story: ERSE may decide to break with historical precedents and refuse to grant a premium for the 2028 gas reset. Bernstein's report of 2 September 2026 shows that gas transmission only accounts for 20% of REN's EBITDA. If the regulator maintains depressed gas returns to protect consumers, the negative impact will be contained, but REN will lose a critical earnings catalyst.

Section 4 – Belgium's 2028-2031 Tariffs for Fluxys Belgium

Section summary
  1. Belgium's CREG will continue to apply a nominal cost-plus methodology for Fluxys Belgium's 2028-2031 tariff period.
  2. Rising interest rates will support higher regulatory returns while accelerated depreciation rules mitigate long-term asset stranding risks.

The Belgian regulatory framework and CREG parameters

1.
Stable regulatory architecture: The Belgian energy regulator CREG has established a highly stable cost-plus framework for the domestic utility networks. According to Fluxys Belgium's annual report published on 16 April 2026, all justified operating expenses, financing costs, and regulatory returns are fully covered under this system. This structural arrangement protects the network from volume fluctuations, which means that longterm earnings remain highly predictable for investors. This framework establishes a reliable foundation for corporate planning during the transition.
2.
Nominal equity remuneration: The allowed return on equity is calculated using a formula based on the Belgian ten- year linear bond yields. Under the current 2024 to 2027 period rules, the risk-free rate was set at a baseline of 1.68%. Bernstein's report of 15 June 2026 confirms that CREG applies an annual indexation mechanism to the return on equity. This dynamic alignment ensures that returns rise with bond yields, which will expand allowed ROE spreads over the next cycle.
3.
Sovereign yield indexation: The dynamic return mechanism automatically recalculates the allowed cost of equity on an annual basis. Bernstein's report of 1 July 2026 points out that Belgium is one of the few European countries with annual return adjustments. This annual true-up shields Fluxys Belgium from persistent interest rate inflation, which means that rising debt refinancing costs are offset. Unless sovereign bond yields plunge dramatically, these adjustments will sustain robust regulated returns for the company.
4.
Leverage and capital structuring: The Belgian regulator enforces a strict capitalization cap to optimize the funding structures of utility networks. According to Fluxys Belgium's disclosures on 22 April 2022, the reference equity base is capped at 40% of the total Regulatory Asset Base. Any equity exceeding this 40% threshold is remunerated at a lower risk-free rate plus 70 basis points. This capital constraint pressures the operator to maintain efficient debt-to-equity gearing ratios of around 60%.

Financial baseline and tariff implications

5.
Expanding revenue baseline: Fluxys Belgium has demonstrated consistent top-line growth driven by higher tariff allocations. The company reported 2025 revenues of €650.5 million, marking an increase of 6.8% compared to €608.8 million in 2024. This revenue expansion reflects the updated components covered under the regulated tariff methodology. Higher revenues directly support robust operational cash flow margins before regulatory settlements are finalized.
6.
Underlying net income trajectories: The operator's bottom-line profitability remains tightly coupled with regulatory parameters and transition spending. Net profit for 2025 fell to €74.9 million from €82.1 million in 2024 due to upfront hydrogen development costs. This temporary earnings pressure will ease once the regulator establishes a formal hydrogen recovery framework. Consequently, the long-term earnings per share outlook depends on the 2028 tariff parameters.
7.
Regulated asset expansion: Capital investments continue to enlarge the infrastructure footprint of the Belgian gas transmission system. Fluxys reported a natural gas transmission Regulatory Asset Base of €2.674 billion in 2025, up from €2.575 billion in 2024. This solid asset base provides a wider foundation for capital remuneration during the upcoming regulatory cycle. Sustained capital expenditures will expand the network's asset valuation and support future EPS growth.
8.
Comparable power sector benchmarks: The regulatory outcomes of the electricity transmission sector provide a clear direction for gas tariff expectations. Elia Group reported a net profit of €348.7 million in the first half of 2026, supported by asset growth and higher returns. Morgan Stanley's note of 31 July 2026 notes that Elia's earnings projections rose after CREG approved its 2028-2031 tariff methodology. This positive precedent suggests that gas networks can similarly achieve improved remuneration parameters, which would drive valuation upgrades across utilities.

Stranded asset mitigation and depreciation policies

9.
Addressing asset stranding risks: The transition away from fossil fuels poses structural risks of stranded infrastructure for natural gas grids. To address this, the company actively promotes the repurposing of existing transport networks into multi-molecule pipelines. In its annual report on 16 April 2026, Fluxys highlighted that over 85% of its ten-year investment plan is focused on transition infrastructure. These proactive measures will mitigate capital loss exposures as natural gas demand declines.
10.
Accelerated depreciation mechanics: Adjusting the useful lives of assets is a primary regulatory tool used to protect capital recovery. Deutsche Bank's report on 29 July 2026 notes that discussions are ongoing with CREG about shortening depreciation periods to support utility cash flows. Front-loading capital recovery reduces the risk of unrecovered write-downs when pipelines are decommissioned. Consequently, implementing these accelerated depreciation policies will secure the long-term asset value for equity investors.
11.
Diminishing balance remuneration: Specific gas transmission assets already benefit from alternative depreciation methodologies to accelerate investment payback. Fluxys Belgium's annual report on 16 April 2026 states that regulated investments for European security of supply are depreciated under a diminishing balance method. This approach allows the company to recover investment costs much faster than traditional straight-line methods. Expanding this treatment to the broader grid will enhance near-term capital recovery velocity.
12.
Regulatory transition pipelines: The creation of dedicated subsidiaries allows the operator to partition high-risk transition assets from the core grid. Fluxys created Fluxys hydrogen and Fluxys c-grid in 2023 to develop hydrogen and carbon dioxide transmission infrastructure. In its report on 24 September 2026, the company confirmed that these units carry latent regulatory assets of €15.7 million and €8.7 million. Once finalized, these assets will convert into regulated base value and generate predictable regulated returns.

Counter opinions and sovereign actions

13.
Contrary evidence: In contrast to the highly supportive Spanish regulatory benchmark, Belgian utility regulation faces distinct operational and political resistance. Fluxys Belgium disclosed on 31 March 2026 that the federal government intends to withdraw €300 million from the positive balances of its regulatory accounts. This significant withdrawal threatens to weaken the stabilization mechanisms built into the CREG framework. This intervention demonstrates that the Belgian regime is subject to unexpected political interference, unlike the independent Spanish cycle.

Section 5 – Italy's Gas Transport Return and ROSS Framework for Snam and Italgas

Section summary
  1. ARERA is likely to trigger a 30bp WACC reduction for Italian gas networks in 2027.
  2. The rollout of the Simplified ROSS framework poses a profitability risk for Italgas.
  3. Rising bond yields and tax modifications could support a WACC recovery from 2028.
  4. Italian networks retain some of the most attractive regulated yields in Europe.

This activation implies a 30-40bp reduction in allowed returns across Italian gas networks starting in 2027. Unless bond yields shift dramatically, Snam and Italgas face immediate downward pressure on their regulated earnings per share.

Sovereign spread pressures: The reduction in the Country Risk Premium is the primary driver behind the expected near-term return cuts. Earnings sensitivity profiles: The financial impact of the 2027 WACC recalculation varies significantly between the two gas operators.
