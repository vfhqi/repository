Plausible problems to monitor

Kambi Group PLC exhibits several material weaknesses relative to its peer cohort and sector base rates, concentrated primarily in revenue growth momentum, operating margin compression, and structural customer concentration risk. The company's Q2 2026 revenue growth of 13.5% year-on-year significantly trails the sector median of 11.75% and, more critically, lags far behind fast-growing data peers such as Genius Sports at 65.0% and Betsson's organic volume expansion of 36.0%.

This growth underperformance reflects the severe impact of turnkey customer migrations, particularly the impending loss of Kindred (completing by end-2026) and LeoVegas (by end-2027), which together represent an estimated €15 million annual revenue headwind. While management has attempted to pivot toward modular Odds Feed+ products, the commercial traction remains nascent, contributing only an estimated 10-15% of revenue in the near term, raising concerns about whether modular contract wins can adequately offset the loss of high-margin turnkey relationships.

Operating profitability presents an even more concerning picture, with Kambi's actual EBIT margin of 12.7% trailing the peer median of 19.25% by a substantial 650 basis points and sitting dramatically below premium operators like Evolution's 55.0% EBIT margin. This margin compression stems from multiple compounding factors: rising non-discretionary data supplier costs (which constitute virtually all cost of sales), elevated capitalized development expenses of €14.4 million in the first half of 2026, and persistent fixed personnel costs that fail to scale proportionally with revenue.

The FY25 operating profit margin of just 5.0% represented a severe contraction from 10.6% in FY24, underscoring the business model's vulnerability to revenue volatility and its limited ability to flex costs downward during customer transition periods. Furthermore, the company's FY25 revenue contraction of -8.2% places it in the bottom quartile of its peer group, starkly contrasting with Sportradar's projected 17.2% growth for FY26E and highlighting the structural headwinds facing turnkey-centric business models.

The governance structure introduces additional concerns, as co-founder dominance within the board limits independent oversight capacity, with Anders Ström holding 6,078,188 shares as the largest individual shareholder while simultaneously serving as Chair. This concentration of power means that if strategic disagreements arise, independent directors possess minimal power to intervene, creating potential alignment risks during periods of strategic pivot.

The company's historical delivery track record further validates these concerns, with repeated guidance cuts including a severe -24% reduction to FY25 EBITA targets triggered by external delays in Brazil regulation and Ontario lottery integration, classifying management as an honest tracker facing high external volatility rather than a conservative sandbagger. Finally, Kambi faces intensifying competitive threats from prediction markets, with survey data showing that 60% of active NFL bettors may trade event contracts, directly competing for the high-value younger demographic cohorts that traditional sportsbooks depend upon for longterm customer lifetime value growth.

These compounding structural weaknesses–spanning growth trajectory, margin sustainability, governance independence, execution consistency, and emerging competitive disruption– position Kambi materially below peer median performance across multiple critical dimensions of fundamental health.
