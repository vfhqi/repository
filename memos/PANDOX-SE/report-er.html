This rising cost trajectory fundamentally alters the investment thesis because it compresses the company's interest coverage ratio and demonstrates that refinancing headwinds are actively eroding the substantial operational gains from the landmark transaction. The market now clearly understands that while the Dalata portfolio generates strong rental income, the complex ownership structure and elevated interim financing costs create a meaningful near-term cash earnings drag that was not fully anticipated prior to the results.

From an operational perspective, the underlying hotel demand dynamics remained remarkably resilient across Pandox's core Northern European markets. Sweden hotel RevPAR expanded by an exceptional 11.6 percent yearover-year in July 2026, while Denmark continued its robust path with a 7.4 percent RevPAR expansion supported by healthy 8.9 percent year-to-date growth. This strong demand environment validates Pandox's turnover-based lease model with guaranteed minimum rent levels and a weighted average unexpired lease term of 14.4 years, which provides highly predictable earnings even during market downturns.

The company's specialized focus on upscale and upper midscale hotels allows management to leverage deep hospitality expertise and maintain a 6.4 percent property yield requirement that offers a healthy spread over funding costs despite elevated base rates. However, the strongest counter-hypothesis to the bullish investment case centers on Pandox's elevated financial leverage and the risk that prolonged high interest rates will permanently compress equity returns.
