Section 16 – Conclusion

The Q2/H1 2026 earnings release on August 21, 2026, delivered a bifurcated outcome that fundamentally altered the near-term investment thesis for Københavns Lufthavne. Total revenue reached DKK 2,771 million YTD, representing solid 9% year-on-year growth, while management unexpectedly downgraded its full-year passenger guidance to approximately 34.5 million passengers from the prior target of 35.5 million. Simultaneously, profit before tax guidance was upgraded to DKK 1.80-1.95 billion from DKK 1.75-1.90 billion, aided by the Smarter Airports divestment and a favorable passenger mix shift.

The single most important thing that changed is the explicit confirmation that Copenhagen Airport's passenger composition has undergone a structural shift toward lower-spending transit travelers, with departing transfer passengers surging by 31% to 2.1 million while local departing passengers grew by just 2% to 5.9 million, raising the transfer share to 27% from 22% in H1 2025. This mix shift represents a permanent alteration to the commercial economics of the airport because transfer passengers exhibit lower retail spend per head due to reduced dwell times and weaker Scandinavian exchange rates, diluting average spend per passenger despite the 7% growth in concession revenues to DKK 471 million.

The earnings print revealed that management's decision to lower volume guidance was driven by physical capacity bottlenecks rather than demand weakness, while the profit upgrade was supported by the DKK 50 million gain from divesting the Smarter Airports joint venture to Netcompany on May 1, 2026.

The specific observable datapoints that would change the investment view include monitoring whether the transfer passenger share stabilizes below 30% of total departing passengers in Q3 and Q4 2026, as any further mix deterioration toward 35% would signal a permanent retail yield compression that cannot be offset by aeronautical tariff indexation. A second critical datapoint is tracking whether CPH's contractual commitments to build and maintain facilities, which grew by 25.55% to reach DKK 909 million by June 30, 2026, from DKK 724 million at the end of 2025, accelerate beyond DKK 1.2 billion by year-end 2026, as this would indicate severe cost overruns on the Terminal 3 expansion that threaten the guided DKK 3.0 billion annual capex budget.

Additionally, investors should monitor whether cargo operations, which decreased by 18% in H1 2026 compared to the prior year due to global trade cooling and weaker industrial export demand, stabilize above a 10% year-on-year decline in H2 2026, as persistent cargo weakness would signal structural logistics routing away from Copenhagen's hub. The outlier call-out is that despite the passenger volume downgrade and retail spend dilution, SAS has accelerated its strategic retreat from its historical three-hub Nordic model and refocused its long-haul traffic heavily on Copenhagen as its primary global gateway, with Copenhagen's long-haul capacity rising 4% versus 2019 while Stockholm Arlanda fell 62% and Oslo Gardermoen dropped 34%.

This competitive moat widening is further reinforced by SAS's integration into the SkyTeam alliance on September 1, 2024, providing seamless connectivity to 1,060 destinations globally, and Air France-KLM's formal process to acquire a majority stake and increase its ownership to 60.5% by the second half of 2026. This strategic consolidation creates a structural revenue floor for CPH's aeronautical segment that is far more durable than the current market perception suggests, as long as SAS remains financially stable and committed to its Copenhagen hub strategy.
