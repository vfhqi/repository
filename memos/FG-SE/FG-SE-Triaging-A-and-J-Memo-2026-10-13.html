[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = C

Thesis. Fasadgruppen is a major-cause-for-concern case in which the concern has been addressed on the balance sheet and in the leadership but not yet in the operations. At SEK 16.5 the shares pay 4.4 times the consensus earnings two years forward, which is the price of the branch in which the recovery does not come; what the price does not pay for is the legacy Nordic business returning to a 6 percent margin while Clear Line holds 25 percent, and that recovery has one quarter of evidence behind it.

  • The change is a repair, carried out inside twelve months, and it is real.
    • A leveraged renovation roll-up ran its balance sheet to 5.3 times EBITDA buying Clear Line in late 2024 for up to SEK 2.2 billion, then met a Nordic construction downturn, a record-cold first quarter of 2026 and a UK regulator that takes about 36 weeks to approve what statute says takes eight.
    • Since then the company has been recapitalised (a SEK 504 million rights issue in April 2026), pruned (Alnova divested in December 2025), flattened (one management layer removed in February 2025) and re-led (an acting chief executive from 21 August 2026, a permanent finance director from 5 October 2026).
    • Each of those removes a reason the shares were uninvestable in 2025
      • None of them yet adds a reason to own them.
  • The forces acting on the company are of ordinary strength and pull both ways, so the change is in magnitude, not in force.
    • The tailwinds are a Swedish building cycle turning in the national data (starts +8.0 percent in July 2026), a Swedish energy-renovation subsidy live from 1 September 2026, the UK Building Safety Regulator's plan to cut approvals to 12 weeks by December 2026, and the company's own cycle, which went from -11.7 percent organic in Q1 2026 to -0.5 percent in Q2 2026 on a record backlog of SEK 4,479.3 million at June 2026.
    • The headwinds are new-build contractors bidding into Swedish and Norwegian renovation on price (Balco's renovation margin 2.3 percent in Q2 2026), Nordic policy rates rising again (Norges Bank 4.5 percent in September 2026; a Riksbank hike expected in November 2026), Swedish agreed minimum wages up 8 percent for 2027 into a fixed-price book, insulation prices up 31 to 37 percent, and SEK 1,250 million of bank debt to refinance in the bond market in a week when European credit cracked.
    • No external force meets the 'Demand Inflection' test and no internal force is strong by the screen's standard
      • The chief executive is acting, there is no guidance to raise, and the strategy has moved from survival to margin rather than to a new source of growth.
  • The change has travelled three layers of the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain and no further.
    • The people, the decisions and the actions have changed
    • The key operating metrics, the financial statements and the sell-side's estimates have not yet followed.
    • H1 2026 organic sales were -5.5 percent, the adjusted EBITA margin 4.7 percent against 8.0 percent a year earlier, cash conversion 26.3 percent.
    • The sell-side has cut the current year's earnings per share by 68.5 percent over twelve months to 2026-10-02 while nudging FY2027 and FY2028 up 2.9 and 3.0 percent over three months
      • The outputs are turning from a hole, not improving against a base.
  • The outputs we expect are a recovery to a level the business has shown, not an expansion beyond it, and that is both the strength and the limit of the case.
    • Revenue grows about 5 percent a year from a FY2026 trough of about SEK 5,270 million
    • The adjusted EBITA margin recovers from 7.0 percent in FY2026 to 8.5 percent in FY2027 and 9.0 percent in FY2028 (consensus 9.6 percent)
    • Adjusted earnings per share rise from about SEK 1.66 in FY2026 to SEK 2.64 in FY2027 and SEK 3.20 in FY2028 (consensus SEK 2.19, 3.40 and 3.97)
    • No dividend before FY2029.
    • A 9 percent group margin needs Clear Line at about 28 percent on about SEK 800 million of revenue and the legacy Nordic business at 6.0 percent, against 5.4 percent in FY2025 and 8 to 10 percent in 2021 to 2023
      • The company earned 8.7 percent on average over five years against peers at about 5 percent.
    • Our earnings sit 20 to 25 percent below consensus for FY2026 and FY2027 because we do not give the second half of 2026 the 9.6 percent margin consensus needs.
  • What it costs: nothing on the multiple and everything on the earnings.
    • The price is 4.4 times consensus earnings 24 months forward (SEK 3.79) on 2026-10-12, the 18th percentile of the stock's own ten years and about a third of the cohort's multiple (Instalco 12.5 times, Bravida 16.1 times)
    • On our earnings it is 5.3 times.
    • The equity is a thin slice of a loaded enterprise: SEK 1,617 million of net debt and SEK 806 million of acquisition liabilities stand above a market capitalisation of about SEK 1,442 million, so on a fully loaded enterprise value of about SEK 3,865 million the stock is about 7.4 times consensus FY2027 EBITA and 8.2 times ours, mid-range rather than trough.
    • The two-year median multiple of 4.36 times says the market has paid this for two years
      • "cheap against ten years" is also "normal against two", so the re-rating needs the cause of the discount to be seen to have gone.
  • The return clears the 20 percent bar only with the re-rating, and the re-rating is the leg we give the lower odds.
    • On our modal path and a blended multiple of 5.9 times the earnings two years beyond the horizon, the value at eighteen months is SEK 22.2, about 22 percent a year
    • At three years SEK 26.8 at 6.25 times, 17.5 percent a year.
    • With the uncleared branch's own lower earnings the probability-weighted return is about 17 percent a year at both horizons
      • The single most likely branch (uncleared, about 65 percent at eighteen months) returns about 9 percent a year
      • The earnings leg alone returns about 14 percent a year over eighteen months.
    • The plausibly worse drop-through (a bond at 9 percent, the acquisition liabilities refinanced with debt at 7 percent, a 25 percent tax rate) takes FY2028 earnings per share from SEK 3.20 to about SEK 2.28 and the eighteen-month return to about nil
      • The 'Modal return' would then be F, this Building Block D and the recommendation bearish rather than park.
  • The pattern is 'Major CfC Clearing' and the fit is D.
    • The shape is exactly the pattern (recapitalised, re-led, a trough multiple, estimates cut by two-thirds), but the setup's rule is that all five conditions pass and one fails outright: the company issues no guidance that could be rebased, and the 2028 targets that stand in for guidance were kept.
  • Where the chain holds and where it breaks.
    • It holds from the rights issue to covenant relief, from the acting appointment to an operator's priorities, and from the weather and the Norway comparison to a better second half of 2026.
    • It breaks at bid discipline across 55 subsidiaries in a price war, which the 'Foundations' show the business has not had, and at Clear Line, which is 11 percent of revenue and 41 percent of profit under one regulator with a subcontracted labour force.

  • The case therefore is one to park with a price and a date: about SEK 15.5, where the probability-weighted path clears the bar, or the Q3 2026 report on 12 November 2026 showing Norway profitable and Clear Line above 30 percent, whichever comes first.

  • Rating = C, because the change is material in magnitude (earnings per share roughly doubling from a FY2026 trough) but the forces driving it are of ordinary strength and partly external, the financial outputs are improving from a hole rather than against a base, and the 'Modal return' clears the 20 percent bar only at a blended multiple that credits a re-rating we give a little better than one-in-three odds within eighteen months

    • Not B, because no output has yet printed above the prior year (H1 2026 organic sales -5.5 percent, adjusted EBITA -46 percent, cash conversion 26.3 percent) and the three Elements are C, C and C
    • Not D, because at 4.4 times depressed earnings even a partial recovery is material to the share price.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The strongest case against is the FALSE FRIEND: the headline stabilisation (order backlog +6.6 percent organic, Clear Line's 37.7 percent margin in Q2 2026, leverage down to 3.3 times) masks structural decay.
    • Organic growth averaged about 1 percent a year from 2020 to 2025 and about -5 percent a year in the 2023 to 2025 downturn
      • Cash conversion fell to 26.3 percent in H1 2026 from 56.3 percent
      • Returns on invested capital were 6 to 7 percent in 2024 and 2025 against SEK 4,066 million of goodwill
      • The Swedish renovation market is being flooded by new-build contractors bidding for volume
      • And SEK 806 million of acquisition liabilities sit outside the reported net debt.
    • On this reading the multiple is not low, it is right: "margin discipline and low leverage get the multiple
      • Volume-chasing gets the discount", and the next event is a target reset by a new chief executive followed by a second equity raise.
    • We concede most of the facts and the direction of the competitive evidence.
    • We hold our view only this far: the company earned 8.7 percent adjusted EBITA margins on average over five years against a peer average of about 5 percent
      • Its legacy Nordic business earned about 5.4 percent in FY2025 even with Norway loss-making
      • A return to about 6 percent in the legacy business plus Clear Line at 25 to 30 percent gets the group to 9 percent, which is a recovery to a level the business has shown, not to one it has not
      • The balance sheet has been repaired once by owners who put in SEK 109 million of their own money
      • And the acting chief executive is the kind of operator the problem calls for.
    • What we do not hold is that the recovery is yet evidenced, which is why the rating is C and the recommendation parks rather than progresses.

  • Rating = C, because the external forces are a mix of modest tailwinds (the Swedish cycle turning, a new Swedish subsidy, the UK regulator's recovery plan) and live headwinds (price competition, rates, wages, input prices), and the internal forces are many but none is strong by the screen's standard: the chief-executive change is a vacancy filled by an acting operator, there is no guidance to raise, and the strategic shift is from survival to margin rather than to a new source of growth
    • Not D, because the balance-sheet reset and the leadership reset together remove the two things that made the company uninvestable in 2025
    • Not B, because no force meets the 'Demand Inflection' test and the screen's decision rule (three strong forces, or a chief-executive change plus a guidance increase) is not met.
  • Direction of the whole: the two strongest forces in this case are the company's own cyclical trough and its own balance-sheet repair
    • One is external and cyclical, the other internal and a repair
    • Neither is a force that creates growth the business did not have before.

    • Not B, because the strongest structural demand force (the EU renovation directive) is in infringement in all 27 member states and the UK fire-safety demand is gated by a regulator

Competition: is competitive intensity easing or tightening? (KQ 2, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = D
  • Rating = D, because competitive intensity is tightening, not easing: new-build contractors have moved into Swedish renovation and are bidding on price.
  • Broker evidence: DNB Carnegie says competition "is rising in legacy operations and is expected to continue until the residential new-build market recovers"
    • Balco reports several new-build competitors "temporarily shifted" to renovation, with "intense price pressure and narrower margins" in Swedish facade renovation
    • Balco's renovation adjusted operating margin was 2.3 percent in Q2 2026.
  • Survey evidence: 83 percent of exterior-work operators report margin compression from aggressive competitor bidding
    • Swedish facade projects are awarded primarily on price
    • Novedo entered Finnish facade renovation by acquiring UKR Oy.
  • Direction: headwind.
    • This is the force most likely to cap the margin recovery and the one the business research names as the single largest risk to the moat.
  • Not C: the only easing we can find is the group's own choice to decline low-margin work in Norway and a levelling of Swedish wage costs
    • Neither changes the bidding behaviour of others.
Demand: is end-market demand changing, structurally or cyclically (a swing with two-plus years of visibility)? (KQ 1, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because end-market demand is turning cyclically in Sweden and is structurally supported in the UK and by energy regulation, but the structural part is slower than hoped and the cyclical part is one quarter old.
  • Swedish demand: housing associations and property owners are "gradually resuming investment decisions" after rate caution
    • Byggstartsindikatorn +8.0 percent year on year (24 September 2026)
    • Building starts +8.0 percent in July 2026, non-residential +13.2 percent, housing transactions +12 percent, construction sentiment 102.9.
  • Order backlog: the group's backlog rose 5.2 percent year on year to SEK 4,479.3 million at June 2026, +6.6 percent organic
    • Specialist Solutions +10.5 percent to SEK 1,254.0 million, Total Solutions +0.3 percent to SEK 1,892.0 million, Clear Line SEK 1,333.3 million
    • No major UK project cancelled.
  • Structural demand: real but undated.
    • The recast EPBD's transposition deadline of 29 May 2026 was missed by every member state and infringement procedures opened on 15 July 2026
    • A renovation law in infringement is not demand.
    • UK remediation: 14,600 buildings assessed, about 1,400 completed.
  • Against: Norway's new-construction market remains weak with Norges Bank at 4.5 percent after a September 2026 hike
    • Finland's renovation market contracted for a third year in 2025
    • UK construction starts are 18 percent below the prior year.
  • Not B: a tailwind with two-plus years of visibility would need either the Swedish turn confirmed in the company's own organic growth (not yet: -5.5 percent in H1 2026) or the EPBD enforced on a date.
Political and regulatory: is a change moving demand, pricing or cost? (KQ 4, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because political and regulatory change is moving demand in both directions on dates that are mostly in the past or undated: the Swedish ROT deduction reverted from 50 percent to 30 percent in 2026 (headwind), the Villaeffekten subsidy began on 1 September 2026 (tailwind), the Building Safety Regulator's action plan targets 12-week decisions by December 2026 (tailwind if met), and the EPBD is in infringement (undated).
  • Villaeffekten: 30 percent of eligible material costs up to SEK 60,000 per small house, SEK 300 million a year through 2030
    • DNB Carnegie expects "a highly persistent source of renovation demand for insulation and windows".
    • It is a small-house scheme, and the company's Swedish customers are housing associations (about 25 percent of group revenue), the public sector (about 25 percent) and commercial owners, so the direct exposure is partial.
  • Building Safety Regulator: average Gateway 2 approvals of about 36 weeks against a statutory eight
    • An action plan launched in April 2026 to reach 12 weeks by December 2026
    • Four approvals worth GBP 32.5 million secured in December 2025.
    • This is the dated regulatory step that matters most to the profit line, because Clear Line earned 41 percent of the group's FY2025 adjusted EBITA (SEK 184 million on SEK 584 million of revenue at 31.5 percent).
  • Norway and Denmark: Enova grants of 25 to 35 percent of approved costs
    • Danish embodied-carbon limits falling to 7.9 kg CO2e by 2029, favouring refurbishment
    • Supportive and slow.
  • Direction: net neutral now, a tailwind if the BSR plan is met
    • We give the BSR reaching about 12 weeks by December 2026 even odds at best, because the regulator's own April 2026 plan is the only evidence and UK sales still fell 8.6 percent in Q2 2026.
Supply side: input costs, availability, concentration? (KQ 5, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = D
  • Rating = D, because input costs are moving against a fixed-price contractor: insulation makers have pushed through double-digit price rises and Swedish agreed minimum wages rise 8 percent for 2027, while the group holds no inventory and its indexation clauses do not fully offset increases.
  • Insulation prices: Saint-Gobain raised insulation prices 35 percent and Kingspan 31 to 37 percent with no broad discounts (expert evidence of 5 October 2026)
    • Materials and consumables were SEK 1,215.9 million in H1 2026, 50.57 percent of net sales.
  • Contract form: mostly fixed-price
    • Indexation "rarely" offsets material increases
    • Installers absorb cost overruns up to 10 percent.
  • Wages: Swedish agreed minimum wages +8 percent for 2027, "which fixed-price work cannot carry"
    • The company's cost base is 40 to 50 percent labour, so a group-level finding the company's own research does not yet carry applies here.
  • Offsets: central procurement frameworks across about 70 subsidiaries
    • Fixed supplier prices agreed before project start on short delivery cycles
    • Nordic labour costs levelling in Sweden.
  • Direction: headwind.
    • Not C, because the two largest cost lines (labour, insulation) are both moving up into a fixed-price book.
Macroeconomic: is a macro force acting on this company more than on the universe? (KQ 7, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = D
  • Rating = D, because the macro force that bears on this company more than on the universe is the Nordic policy rate, and it is rising again: the Riksbank is expected to hike 25 basis points in November 2026 toward 2.50 percent by mid-2027 and Norges Bank hiked to 4.5 percent in September 2026, which bears directly on housing associations' willingness to commit to facade projects.
  • Sensitivity: a 2.0 percentage-point organic volume drop lowers earnings per share by 11.5 percent
    • A 50 basis-point adjusted EBITA margin swing moves it 11.1 percent (on the pre-rights-issue share count; the direction holds).
  • Where it bites: through the tenant-owner associations that are about a quarter of revenue
    • This case partly needs the rate relief the regime has not delivered.
    • Direction: headwind, more than for the universe.
  • Not F: Sweden's own data (starts, transactions, sentiment) are improving despite the rate path, and the Swedish country tilt is Overweight in the environment reading for that reason.

Disruption: is a disruption force strengthening or being disproved? (KQ 3, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because disruption forces (factory-prefabricated facade elements, robotic painting, lightweight material substitution) are strengthening at the margin but are not yet being proved against the bespoke-renovation work this company does: Sto Group is industrialising facade coating in the factory, Vonovia's serial modernisation uses prefabricated timber panels, Etex is realigning to lightweight fibre-cement systems, while experts say technical complexity and cost still severely limit residential prefabrication and historic facades need bespoke hand restoration.
    • A slow headwind to on-site plastering volumes, not a change force on the horizon of this case.

[Robustness of] Foundations upon which the Investment Thesis is built upon (BB 2) Rating = D

The 'Foundations' make a margin recovery plausible and do not make it probable: the industry sets a ceiling on what even a well-run business here can earn, and this business is not yet well-run enough to reach it. The value chain squeezes the company from both ends, price-led buyers and new-build contractors bidding into renovation on one side, consolidated material suppliers raising prices into fixed-price contracts on the other; the industry is so fragmented that there is no concentrating pull and no pricing discipline; and the business itself has a thin, non-mechanical moat, returns on capital of 6 to 8 percent, and a management team in transition.

  • What the 'Foundations' do support is narrower than the thesis needs.
    • A decentralised, asset-light renovation specialist with central purchasing and a regulatory-compliance niche in the UK can earn 8 to 9 percent margins through a cycle, because it did so from 2021 to 2023 and in 2025 (adjusted EBITA margins of 10.4, 8.7, 8.3 and 8.2 percent in 2021, 2022, 2023 and 2025).

    • The five-year margin record beat the peer average by about four points (8.7 percent against about 5 percent).
  • What the thesis needs and the 'Foundations' do not show is bid discipline in a price war.
    • The three things the recovery turns on (bid discipline across 55 subsidiaries, Norway's execution, Clear Line's margin durability) are not strengths the business has demonstrated: the organic line fell about 5 percent a year in the downturn, the adjusted margin halved in 2024, and the legacy Swedish subsidiaries were undercut in price-led tenders.
    • The strengths that exist are strengths of market position (the largest Nordic envelope specialist, the first turnkey provider), not of competitive position
      • In a market where price decides, market position without a cost or product advantage does not protect margin.
  • The two Elements bind rather than offset.
    • The industry structure (D) caps the margin at what a price-taking contractor can earn when new-build contractors want its work
    • The business (D) has not yet shown it can reach even that cap consistently.
    • A better industry would forgive a mediocre operator
    • A better operator could out-execute a bad industry
    • Here neither relief exists.
  • The single largest fragility is Clear Line.
    • It is 11 percent of revenue and 41 percent of profit
    • Its moat is one regulator's statute
    • Its labour is subcontracted in a market short of 239,300 workers by 2029
    • Its deferred purchase price is most of the SEK 806 million of acquisition liabilities.
    • A business that is 41 percent of profit on 11 percent of revenue is a concentration, not a foundation.
