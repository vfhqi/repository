Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Analyzing Hellenic Telecom's asset beta and index inclusion determines whether macro flows or corporate fundamental turnarounds drive the equity's long-term valuation multiples.

Defining the Research Inquiry

1.
Systematic research parameters: The primary investigation is structured as "For Hellenic Telecom (OTE, HTO-GR), how far has its share price and 12-month and 24-month forward P/E moved with European telecom peers, with the Athens market and with Greek government bond spreads over rolling six-month windows since 2023, rather than on company-specific news; what part did index events (Greece's move to Developed Market status, MSCI and FTSE index changes, emerging-market fund outflows) play in 2025 and 2026; and which companyspecific events moved the shares most over the last two years?"

Valuation Multiples and Financial Impact

2.
Depressed valuation multiples: Understanding correlation with European peers is critical because Hellenic Telecom historically trades at a deep discount. Deutsche Bank notes that OTE's forward P/E was 38% lower than sector averages in early 2025. Resolving the Key Question reveals whether this valuation gap stems from country-specific beta or company-specific operational metrics. This assessment directly dictates whether the multiple will expand toward European standards.
3.
Operational cash generation: The development of earnings per share remains closely tied to major strategic divestments. Management successfully completed the sale of its cash-burning Romanian operations in late 2025. This exit eliminates an ongoing drag on free cash flow and enables simplified pure-play equity focus for Greek operations. Consequently, projected EPS can grow more rapidly as capital is redeployed into domestic fiber.
4.
Reducing capital costs: Analyzing Greek government bond spreads is essential because sovereign risk directly shapes the telecom's cost of capital. Morgan Stanley adjusted its sector modeling in September 2026 by lowering the market risk premium from 8% to 7%. This adjustment reduced OTE's weighted average cost of capital from 10.1% to 9.4% on improving macro indicators. Lowering the discount rate boosts the net present value of long-term infrastructure cash flows.

Index Transitions and Strategic Questions

5.
Passive flow dynamics: The transition of Greece's equity status from emerging to developed markets represents a major technical catalyst. FTSE and STOXX implemented their reclassifications in September 2026, while the larger MSCI reclassification is scheduled for May 2027. Identifying the timing of these transitions helps investors anticipate passive emerging market outflows. These outflows could temporarily depress the share price regardless of strong underlying company earnings.
6.
Contrary evidence: Although index transition events are heavily monitored, Morgan Stanley reported that OTE shares quickly rebounded after a brief 6% total return sell-off in January 2026. This recovery occurred despite ongoing market uncertainty regarding whether the company would ultimately satisfy MSCI developed market standard index criteria. The resilient price action suggests that strong corporate fundamentals can sometimes overpower passive index flow volatility.
7.
Connected operational threats: Several related inquiries must be examined, such as how aggressively Public Power Corporation will overbuild the FTTH network. Deutsche Bank analysts argued in late 2025 that this competitive threat was unlikely to destroy OTE value significantly. Additionally, investors must monitor how successfully the company monetizes high-speed broadband through the government's Gigabit Voucher initiative. These parallel factors are critical to determining if domestic average revenue per user can sustain growth.
8.
Other side of the story: While macro-driven index events and sovereign yield spreads dominate market headlines, company-specific operational execution remains the primary driver of actual cash returns. Deutsche Bank highlights that OTE continues to extract significant opex efficiencies and returns near 100% of yearly free cash flow to shareholders. Consequently, even if index transitions trigger near-term volatility, long-term shareholder yield is ultimately protected by operational performance.
