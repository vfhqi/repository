Turkish Hyperinflation and IAS 29 Application

1.
Specific accounting: Vodafone applies IAS 29 to its Turkish operations because the Turkish economy was designated as hyperinflationary from 30 June 2022. The company's interim report on 10 November 2026 stated that Turkish lira results and non-monetary assets are revalued using consumer price indexes. This revaluation process ensures that Turkish financial metrics reflect current purchasing power before translation into euros.
2.
Excluded adjustments: Vodafone's definition of adjusted profit attributable to owners of the parent explicitly excludes the impact of Turkish hyperinflation. According to the H1 FY27 interim results on 10 November 2026, the revaluation of net monetary assets is recorded in other income, which is fully adjusted out of core performance measures. This exclusion prevents volatile hyperinflationary gains from distorting the group's adjusted basic earnings per share metric.
3.
Contrary evidence: Deutsche Bank Research reported on 10 November 2026 that Turkey's service revenue grew 33% organically during the first half, demonstrating that the segment remains highly operational. This shows that while IAS 29 accounting is stripped from adjusted results, the underlying Turkish business continues to deliver strong organic growth. Consequently, the complete exclusion of Turkey's hyperinflation adjustments might lead investors to undervalue the region's actual commercial contributions.
4.
Operating adjustments: Vodafone adjusts its core organic growth metrics to entirely remove the impact of the Turkish hyperinflation adjustments and foreign exchange translation. The group's preliminary report on 12 May 2026 confirmed that organic EBITDAaL growth calculations exclude these volatile non-cash accounting adjustments. This approach allows analysts to evaluate core operational trends without hyperinflationary distortions.

This shift illustrates how hyperinflation adjustments fluctuate significantly between reporting periods.

Depressed operating profit: Vodafone reported that Turkey's hyperinflation adjustments reduced its consolidated operating profit by 146 million euros for the six months ended 30 September 2026. This reported drag, which included a 118 million euro gain on net monetary assets, compared to a 159 million euro reduction in the prior half-year, as disclosed in the interim report on 10 November 2026. This substantial impact indicates how severely hyperinflation compresses reported operating metrics.
7.
Drastic net drag: Vodafone recorded a total net profit reduction of 197 million euros for the half-year ended 30 September 2026 stemming from the Turkish hyperinflation adjustments. The group's interim report on 10 November 2026 showed that this net decrease was slightly lower than the 200 million euro drag reported for the prior half-year. This persistent net drag highlights that hyperinflation remains a significant headwind for reported earnings.

This difference demonstrates how IAS 29 application inflates nominal growth figures compared to clean operational performance.
