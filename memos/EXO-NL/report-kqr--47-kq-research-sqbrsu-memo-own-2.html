Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. EXOR N.V.'s aggressive buybacks and tenders provide short-term share price support but face persistent structural discounts of up to sixty-two percent. Capital returns, family litigation, and the IFRS 10 accounting shift directly shape its Net Asset Value discount and future asset growth. The evidence below is organised as twelve findings, presented as short paragraphs.

1. Capital return track record. EXOR launched a large share buyback programme in early 2022. It escalated in September 2023 with a one billion euro programme featuring a €750 million reverse Dutch auction tender. It then announced a further one billion euro tender offer in March 2025. The latest step is a €500 million buyback programme announced on 22 September 2026. 2. The discount has not closed. EXOR still trades at a discount of thirty to sixty-two percent to net asset value despite billions of euros in buybacks. Bernstein Research reported that these programmes have failed to permanently compress the holding company discount. 3. Peers point to operating performance and simplification. Groupe Bruxelles Lambert still trades at a persistent twenty-eight percent discount despite active buybacks. Investor AB's far narrower discount is attributed to consistent net asset value outperformance. That suggests operational execution and transparency narrow discounts more effectively than buybacks alone. 4. Giovanni Agnelli B.V. is a material seller in each tender. The family holding holds a 54.94 percent economic interest and 83.97 percent of voting rights. It received about €202 million in the 2023 reverse Dutch auction. It committed to tender €570 million in the 2025 offer, which was fifty-seven percent of the allocation.

5. Parent-level debt is the central unknown. The share sales had the explicit objective of reducing the holding's net debt. The parent's capacity to meet its debt maturities is contingent on EXOR's distributions. Rallye shows how extreme parent-level debt can force a subsidiary into unsustainable dividends. D'Ieteren shows that a leveraged family restructuring can succeed when subsidiaries are highly profitable. The maturity schedule of the private debt remains hidden from public view.

6. Litigation keeps ultimate ownership contested. Margherita Agnelli presented a previously undisclosed handwritten 1998 amendment at a Turin hearing in September 2025. In April 2026 a Swiss court rejected John Elkann's bid to exclude her from Marella Caracciolo's estate. EXOR maintains the ruling has no impact on the 2004 succession agreements. 12 7. Control looks resilient. The loyalty voting mechanism awards up to ten votes per share for ten-year holders, so Giovanni Agnelli B.V. keeps voting dominance even if economic stakes change. Margherita Agnelli's litigation is therefore highly unlikely to dislodge the family's ultimate control. The Ferrari consultative voting agreement was extended to January 2029.

8. Investors are pressing for deployment. AVI Global Trust reported in January 2026 that EXOR traded at its widest-ever discount and expects a highly accretive tender offer funded by disposal proceeds. The Welltec sale, due to close in the first half of 2027, is expected to lift deployable cash to four billion euros. The Iveco tender to Tata Motors alone generated about €1.4 billion.

9. Broker and consensus assumptions. The report contains no numerical consensus forecasts for EXOR's capital allocation or discount path. Broker and investor commentary assumes continued buyback activity but not a durable re-rating. Kepler Partners cites analysts expecting consistent market presence to help establish a floor for the stock price during downturns. Bernstein warns that if investor skepticism persists, the €500 million programme will yield minimal long-term valuation benefits. AVI assumes a further accretive tender. The implied trajectory is therefore support rather than convergence.

Ferrari accounts for thirty-eight percent of gross asset value. Stellantis headwinds could weigh on asset valuation and dividend income.

The March 2027 annual report and later investorday statements are the most direct sources to clarify the cash allocation. Weekly regulatory buyback disclosures would show whether the family participates in the new programme. Dutch registry filings would clarify the parent's debt obligations. Until these gaps close, the findings above describe evidence and risk, not a forecast.

Section 2 – Capital Allocation History and Share Price and NAV Discount Reactions

Section summary
  1. EXOR N.V.'s aggressive buybacks and tenders provide short-term share price support but face persistent structural discounts of up to sixty-two percent.

This program was designed to return cash from major disposals directly to shareholders while exploiting the persistent holding company valuation gap. This initial action demonstrated the company's commitment to returning excess capital efficiently.
2.
Steady execution of tranches: EXOR N.V. followed its initial buyback tranche with a second tranche of up to two hundred and fifty million euros announced in July 2022. By March 2023, the company had completed two hundred and forty-six million euros of purchases under this second tranche on Euronext Amsterdam. This continuous buyback strategy supported the share price and reduced total shares outstanding by over three million shares. These steady market purchases provided ongoing liquidity and valuation support during a period of wider market volatility.
3.
Timely cancellation of shares: EXOR N.V. completed the cancellation of over seven million repurchased ordinary shares in early 2023 to structurally reduce its equity base. This move represented just under three percent of outstanding share capital and immediately boosted net asset value per share metrics. Management designed these cancellations to ensure that buyback activity resulted in permanent per-share accretion for the remaining shareholders. This process of capital reduction was concluded in the first half of 2023 as planned.

This initiative featured a seven hundred and fifty million euro reverse Dutch auction tender to purchase shares directly. This aggressive capital return structure aimed to quickly shrink the share count and narrow the historical NAV discount. The substantial scale of the tender offer highlights the company's tactical use of private-tender mechanisms alongside market purchases.

Section 3 – Giovanni Agnelli B.V. Financial Position and Ownership Dynamics

Section summary
  1. Giovanni Agnelli B.V. leveraged EXOR N.V.'s tender offers in 2023 and 2025 to extract seven hundred and seventy-two million euros to service its own holding-level net debt.
  2. The family holding's dependence on EXOR N.V.'s cash flow mirrors historical precedents where leverage pressure at the parent level forced aggressive capital extraction.

Controlling Shareholder Tender Participation

1.
Significant structural analysis: Research across twelve relevant sources, three companies, and two comparable past cases reveals the central role of Giovanni Agnelli B.V. in EXOR N.V.'s capital structure. The family holding company holds a fifty-four point ninety-four percent economic interest and eighty-three point ninety-seven percent of the voting rights as of December 2025. This heavy concentration ensures complete family control over all major corporate decisions.
2.
Substantial voting concentration: The family holding vehicle maintains absolute control of EXOR N.V. by holding one hundred and twenty-two million special voting shares. This special voting structure allows Giovanni Agnelli B.V. to control eighty-four percent of the voting power despite having a smaller economic stake. This concentration of power ensures that the family remains the ultimate arbiter of all major capital allocation decisions.
3.
Direct cash extraction: Giovanni Agnelli B.V. participated in the September 2023 reverse Dutch auction by securing a substantial cash inflow of two hundred and two million euros from EXOR N.V. The transaction involved the sale of 2,386,226 ordinary shares at a strike price of eighty-four point fifty-two euros per share. This transaction allowed the parent entity to monetize a portion of its holdings without eroding its voting dominance.
4.
Orderly share monetization: Giovanni Agnelli B.V.'s participation in the 2023 tender offer resulted in the sale of two point thirty-nine million shares to EXOR N.V. This structured transaction allowed the parent company to monetize capital without creating downward pressure on the public market share price. This method is highly efficient compared to open-market block trades that typically require deep discounts.
5.
Increased tender proceeds: The family vehicle substantially increased its cash extraction during the subsequent one billion euro tender offer launched on twenty-six March 2025. Under this program, Giovanni Agnelli B.V. entered an irrevocable undertaking to tender shares worth five hundred and seventy million euros. This massive transaction represented fifty-seven percent of the total buyback allocation and directly reduced its economic exposure.
6.
Neutral vote approval: Independent non-executive directors solely approved the related-party transaction to purchase five hundred and seventy million euros of shares from the majority shareholder in 2025. The audit committee confirmed that the transaction reflected customary market terms to prevent potential conflicts of interest. This regulatory compliance was crucial under the Dutch Corporate Governance Code.

Financial Position and Leverage

7.
Pressing debt reduction: Giovanni Agnelli B.V. executed these substantial share sales with the explicit strategic objective of reducing its net debt position. The holding company has historically carried significant private debt to fund family buyouts and reorganization activities. This ongoing debt service requirement makes the holding company highly dependent on cash inflows from EXOR N.V.
8.
High parent dependence: The family vehicle relies heavily on EXOR N.V.'s capital returns because it holds no other significant operating assets. Aside from its controlling interest in EXOR N.V., Giovanni Agnelli B.V. functions strictly as a passive family investment office. Consequently, the parent company's ability to service its debt maturities is entirely contingent on EXOR N.V.'s distributions.
9.
Generous dividend distribution: The family holding company utilizes cash dividends from EXOR N.V. to distribute steady payouts to the descendants of Giovanni Agnelli. For example, the Maria Sole Agnelli branch of the family received more than six million euros in direct payouts in 2025 alone. These consistent outflows highlight how the underlying capital structure must continuously generate liquidity to satisfy diverse family branches.
10.
Stable dividend flow: EXOR N.V. supports its parent company's cash requirements by maintaining consistent ordinary dividend payments. These related-party flows provide a steady source of income that is redistributed to the descendants of the founder. These predictable cash distributions help satisfy family members who are not directly involved in the corporate operations.
11.
Expected succession demands: John Elkann acknowledged at the 2019 Investor Day that generational transitions across a larger shareholder base will create ongoing liquidity demands. The family has historically bought back ten percent of Giovanni Agnelli B.V.'s capital over a decade to provide liquidity to departing members. This structural requirement suggests that further tender participations or asset sales are inevitable.

The Netherlands' special multiple voting structure incentivizes long-term ownership by multiplying voting rights for loyal shareholders. Consequently, the family can safely execute large-scale economic share tenders without risking its absolute governance control.

Long term alignment: John Elkann has repeatedly emphasized that the family is committed to its long term ownership of EXOR N.V. This strategic commitment provides stability for the underlying companies, including Ferrari, Stellantis, and CNH Industrial. This governance model allows management to execute longterm strategies without being distracted by short-term market pressures.

Looming legal uncertainty: Ongoing inheritance litigation brought by Margherita Agnelli over the Dicembre holding company continues to threaten the family's control structure. Since Dicembre sits at the very peak of the ownership pyramid, any adverse court ruling could disrupt the governance of Giovanni Agnelli B.V. itself. This unresolved dispute injects structural risk that remains a primary driver of EXOR N.V.'s persistent net asset value discount.

Comparable Cases and Precedents

Standalone parent net debt of three billion euros forced its listed subsidiary Casino to pay unsustainably high dividends. This cash leakage depleted Casino's balance sheet, leading to credit downgrades and an eventual court-led restructuring.

This disciplined approach allows the family to preserve its core voting power while addressable debt maturities are resolved. Thus, EXOR N.V.'s strong cash balance insulated the parent company from the liquidity crises seen in other European holding structures.
19.
Other side of the story: The substantial cash extraction by Giovanni Agnelli B.V. could signal that EXOR N.V.'s buybacks are primarily designed for family deleveraging rather than maximizing minority shareholder value. While management argues that the tenders efficiently narrow the net asset value discount, the persistent sixty-two percent market discount suggests investors remain skeptical of these related-party cash outflows. This ongoing capital diversion may ultimately restrict EXOR N.V.'s capacity to fund higher-return unlisted acquisitions.

Section 4 – Margherita Agnelli Litigation over the Dicembre Holding

Section summary
  1. Margherita Agnelli's legal challenge of the Dicembre ownership structure threatens the ultimate control block of EXOR N.V.
  2. The discovery of a handwritten note from 1998 and recent Swiss court setbacks keep John Elkann's primary inheritance agreements under prolonged judicial review.

Historical Will and Settlement Disputes

1.
Persistent legal challenges: Persistent legal challenges contest the foundational ownership of the peak family holding entity Dicembre. Margherita Agnelli presented an undisclosed handwritten amendment from 1998 during a Turin court hearing in September 2025 to dispute her father Gianni Agnelli's estate. This legal development raises questions over who rightfully owns the top-level holding that governs EXOR N.V. This unresolved battle maintains prolonged and damaging governance uncertainty for long-term investors.
2.
Disputed handwritten note: The disputed handwritten note dated 20 January 1998 indicates Gianni Agnelli intended his twenty-five percent stake in Dicembre for his late son Edoardo. Following Edoardo's death in 2000, the estate was divided under a separate 1996 document that favored John Elkann. Margherita Agnelli's legal team claims this 1998 note contradicts the subsequent transfers of ownership to Elkann. This dispute over historical asset allocations could disrupt the ultimate peak-level family voting control of the family investment pyramid.
3.
Robust legal defense: John Elkann's legal team maintains that the 1998 note has no legal bearing on the definitive family settlement signed in 2004. The 2004 agreement saw Margherita Agnelli withdraw from Dicembre's capital in exchange for one point two billion euros. However, her lawyers argue the heirs lacked knowledge of the 1998 document when the estate was settled. This conflict over disclosure validity continues to fuel expensive civil litigation across multiple European jurisdictions.

Civil and Criminal Court Proceedings

4.
Adverse judicial setbacks: Swiss judicial authorities dealt a setback to the EXOR chairman by rejecting his bid to exclude Margherita Agnelli from Marella Caracciolo's estate. The Swiss court's ruling in April 2026 keeps the inheritance dispute active despite John Elkann's efforts to dismiss the case. Margherita Agnelli is seeking a larger share of the overall estate to benefit the five children from her second marriage. These ongoing foreign proceedings present unpredictable operational and legal risks for the Agnelli family's control structure.
5.
Dismissive corporate responses: EXOR N.V. downplays the Swiss court ruling by asserting it has zero impact on the core 2004 succession agreements. The company's corporate statement in April 2026 reiterated that Margherita Agnelli is not a legal heir to Marella Caracciolo under the signed pacts. However, Italian prosecutors in Turin have used separate tax investigations to uncover relevant estate documents. This combination of criminal and civil scrutiny distracts senior corporate management from executing EXOR's core investment strategies.
6.
Invasive criminal investigations: The discovery of the critical 1998 note occurred during a criminal tax evasion investigation in Turin. The Italian prosecutor's probe ended in late September 2025 with John Elkann agreeing to community service. Margherita Agnelli's lawyers successfully used the evidence uncovered during this probe to bolster their civil litigation claims. This strategic linkage of criminal findings and civil claims poses significant reputational and legal threats to the Elkann siblings.

Governance Stability and Subsidiary Impacts

7.
Direct structural impact: Potential shifts in Dicembre's ownership would directly impact EXOR N.V.'s governance because the entity sits at the top of the control pyramid. Dicembre currently belongs to John, Lapo, and Ginevra Elkann, who exercise voting power over Giovanni Agnelli B.V. Giovanni Agnelli B.V. holds eighty-four point sixty-nine percent of EXOR's voting rights as of September 2026. Any dilution of the Elkann family's stake could weaken the voting cohesion of the controlling shareholder.
8.
Minority parent concentration: Structural governance frameworks at EXOR's listed holdings like Stellantis reflect the complexity of its underlying family control blocks. EXOR remains the largest shareholder of Stellantis by holding approximately fourteen percent of the outstanding common equity. Other prominent holdings are divided among the Peugeot family and the French government. This multitiered ownership structure means that parent-level disputes could complicate collaborative governance decisions among major industrial partners.
9.
Concentrated luxury ownership: The underlying asset base of EXOR is heavily weighted toward high-value stakes like Ferrari. EXOR's economic interest in Ferrari represents over twenty-four percent of the luxury automaker's outstanding common shares. This economic stake translates to a thirty-six point sixty-nine percent voting power under the loyalty voting framework. Consequently, any disruption at the peak Dicembre holding could destabilize the voting control of EXOR's most valuable listed portfolio asset.
10.
Stable protective agreements: Stable ownership guarantees under the Ferrari-EXOR shareholders' agreement protect key listed assets despite parent-level litigation. EXOR N.V. and the Piero Ferrari family extended their consultative voting agreement in January 2026 until January 2029. This pact coordinates thirty-two percent of Ferrari's total voting rights to ensure management continuity. This robust structure ensures that subsidiary operations remain insulated from the ongoing Agnelli family inheritance disputes.

Resilience and Future Strategic Consequences

11.
Contrary evidence: The established multiple voting rights structure in the Netherlands makes EXOR's control structure highly resilient against ownership shifts. EXOR N.V.'s loyalty voting mechanism awards up to ten voting rights per share for ten-year holders. This mechanism ensures that Giovanni Agnelli B.V. maintains absolute voting dominance even if economic stakes change. Consequently, Margherita Agnelli's litigation is highly unlikely to dislodge the family's ultimate corporate control.
12.
Reputational governance risks: Institutional investors closely monitor the litigation's impact on EXOR N.V.'s unlisted asset manager Lingotto. Lingotto managed over ten billion euros in assets under management as of December 2025. While EXOR maintains a long-term investment horizon, prolonged family disputes could damage the holding's reputational standing. Any disruption in corporate leadership might impede capital fundraising efforts for Lingotto's expansion plans.
13.
Other side of the story: The persistent litigation brought by Margherita Agnelli might serve as a positive catalyst by forcing greater corporate transparency. The legal discovery processes have already compelled EXOR to streamline its related-party transactions and simplify its investment entity reporting. This increased transparency may actually help compress the holding company's Net Asset Value discount over time. Therefore, the family dispute could ultimately benefit minority public shareholders by accelerating governance reforms.
