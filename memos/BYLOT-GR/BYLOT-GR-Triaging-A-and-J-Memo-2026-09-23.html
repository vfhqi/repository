[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = D

Thesis. Bally's Intralot is a 'Major Corporate Change' case that is not compelling at EUR 1.077. The change is real at every input, a new owner, a new chief executive, a new strategy and two acquisitions, but the UK gaming duty and the debt taken on to build the group have so far cancelled it in the outputs, so the estimates are being cut rather than raised. The return needs a re-rating that only deleveraging can earn, and the company keeps spending the deleveraging on the next deal.

  • The change inside the company is broad and real, and it is the case.
    • In October 2025 a Greek lottery-technology supplier bought Bally's International Interactive for EUR 2.7 billion, tripled its revenue, took the acquirer's chief executive and its 58 percent parent, and became a UK-centred online casino operator (B2C 76.4 percent of H1 2026 revenue) with a long-contract lottery business as ballast.
    • Seven of the twelve internal forces are strong: chief executive change, other leadership change, a strategy changed in kind, execution standards, new geographies, new products and a shareholder-return policy started from zero.
      • That is well above the screen's threshold of three.
    • The actions are resourced and dated: a EUR 41 million cost-synergy programme, a EUR 30.0 million dividend paid July 2026, EUR 20.8 million of buybacks in H1 2026, and, eight months after the first deal, a GBP 243.1 million offer for Evoke plc approved by Evoke shareholders on 17 August 2026.
    • Three of the screen's red flags sit beside the forces: dangerous leverage (adjusted net leverage 4.05x at 30 June 2026, heading to about 4.5x pro forma on Evoke), a buyback executed at that leverage, and a second acquisition before the first has deleveraged.
      • They do not remove the change
      • they set its cost.
  • The external force is a headwind of the first order, and it arrived at the same time.
    • The UK Remote Gaming Duty rose from 21 percent to 40 percent on 1 April 2026, a EUR 34.0 million hit in Q2 2026 alone
    • management offset 65 percent of it inside the quarter, and the remaining 35 percent rests on marketing and "generosity" cuts scheduled for H2 2026.
    • A second step is enacted: General Betting Duty from 15 percent to 25 percent in April 2027.
      • Management has said the duty delays the business plan by one year.
    • The same duty is the consolidation opening the strategy is built on: sub-scale UK operators are expected to exit, and the group would be number two in UK online by gross gaming revenue with Evoke.
  • The change has reached the operating metrics and stopped there.
    • It shows in the people, the choices and the actions, and in the key operating metrics: UK online revenue grew 11.6 percent in constant currency in Q2 2026, up from 10.5 percent in Q1, through the doubling of the duty, and UK net gaming revenue reached an all-time high.
    • It has not reached the financial statements as improvement: pro-forma twelve-month adjusted EBITDA of EUR 399.9 million and a 37.2 percent margin sit below the FY2025 baseline of EUR 430.8 million and 39.7 percent
      • adjusted net leverage rose to 4.05x from 3.50x
      • reported net income was a loss of EUR 14.3 million in H1 2026 beneath the adjusted profit.
    • It has not reached the sell-side estimates as upgrades: FactSet's mean earnings per share for FY2026 to FY2028 was cut 8 to 10 percent over the six months to 2026-09-11, while targets rose and the buy share went to 100 percent.
      • Brokers like the story and have been lowering the numbers, which is the profile of a stock waiting for proof.
    • So the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain holds through four layers and breaks at the fifth and sixth.
      • The break is not the operations
      • it is the duty and the leverage.
  • What we think it earns.
    • On our modal path adjusted earnings per share compound at about 12.5 percent a year, from about EUR 0.070 in FY2026 to about EUR 0.100 in FY2029
    • consensus is EUR 0.072, 0.086 and 0.097 for FY2026 to FY2028.
    • About 5 percent a year of that is revenue growth, about 1.6 percent is margin recovery to about 39 percent as the BII synergies and the remaining mitigation land, and about 5.5 percent is the below-EBITDA line as debt is repaid and interest falls.
      • Nearly half the growth is the leg investors do not extrapolate, which is why a 12.5 percent compound rate is only a C on the framework's growth test.
  • What it costs.
    • EUR 1.077, or 11.5x consensus adjusted earnings twenty-four months forward (EUR 0.0937), the 17th percentile of a five-year own history that is not a usable anchor and the 26th percentile of a usable two-year one
    • mid-peer against Allwyn 10.5x, FDJ United 8.1x, Lottomatica 11.3x and ZEAL 14.9x (2026-09-22).
    • The price is not demanding.
      • The multiple, though, sits on adjusted earnings that reported earnings do not yet confirm
      • on the reported line there is no multiple to apply.
    • The market has paid nothing for the catalysts: twelve months -9.5 percent and three months -8.7 percent against a 100 percent buy share and targets of EUR 1.35 to 1.41.
      • The catalysts are known and the market is discounting the leverage that funds them.
  • The return does not clear the bar on the modal path.
    • With 11.5x held on the earnings two years beyond each horizon, the three-year return is about 8.8 percent a year and the eighteen-month return about 7.4 percent a year, against the 20 percent bar: a D on the framework's anchors, and F on a strict reading of the arithmetic.
    • The cash return is probable and small (about 2 percent a year)
      • the earnings growth is plausible and carries the return
      • the re-rating is the swing factor and we do not put it in the modal case.
    • At the cleared branch's 13.5x the three-year return is 14.4 percent a year, a C
      • at the uncleared branch's 10.5x it is 5.7 percent a year, an F.
      • The cleared branch, leverage at or below 3.5x at end FY2027 with Evoke inside, we put at about 35 percent.
    • The plausibly worse drop-through from EBITDA to earnings per share (interest that does not fall because Evoke debt replaces repaid debt, and EUR 15 million more depreciation and amortisation) takes about 15 percent off the earnings path from FY2027 and the return to about 3 percent a year over three years, an F.
      • That is the case Deutsche Bank's 2 percent EBITDA growth rate to 2028 already describes.
  • The pattern is 'Major Corporate Change', crisply on one half of it.
    • The inputs fit the pattern in every respect
    • the "major earnings-per-share trajectory upgrades" the pattern is named for have not begun, and on the evidence they are eighteen months away at the earliest (EUR 25 million of BII synergies in 2027; Evoke year two).

  • The case therefore is a park, not a rejection.

    • The operating delivery is real, the price is not demanding, and a cleared branch is a C on our own arithmetic.
    • But the modal return is a D, the adverse case an F, and the thing that would make it a B, deleveraging, is the thing management has deferred three times in the files while doing two deals.
    • The case cleared the bar at EUR 0.90 in March 2026
    • on the modal path it clears it below about EUR 0.80 to 0.91.

  • Rating = D, because the inputs are strong (a B) but the outputs they have driven are not yet improving (a D) and the modal return sits at about 9 to 12 percent a year, below the framework's C band

    • not C, because materiality is judged on the whole chain from forces to outputs to return, and the chain breaks at the outputs, however broad the input breadth and however good the staircase of dated events.

Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.

  • The case against, at its strongest: the debt-funded consolidation is a rational, value-creating response to a once-in-a-generation dislocation, not an overreach.
    • The 40 percent duty removes the UK online tail and hands share to the scaled: Tier Two and below took 7 percent of share since 2021 and are expected to give it back.
    • Evoke at 52 pence, with GBP 180 million of targeted synergies against GBP 25 million of cash cost and 99.63 percent shareholder approval, is an irreplaceable database bought at a distressed price.
      • Lottomatica has just shown that a levered online consolidator can deliver synergies 50 percent above plan and trade at the 90th percentile of its own history.
    • The Vitruvian and PlayerX platforms give real marketing efficiency: spend 10 points below peers, and growth without dilutive promotion.
    • The lottery book is a sixteen-year contracted floor
      • free cash flow of EUR 89.0 million in H1 2026 after EUR 117.4 million of capital expenditure shows the cash engine works
      • the chief executive took Gamesys from 3.8x to 1.1x leverage in two years.
    • At 11.5x adjusted earnings and about 7x EV/EBITDA the price already discounts the leverage, so a return to 3x by FY2028 re-rates the shares to 13 to 14x and, with 13 percent earnings growth and a 2 percent yield, gives 20 percent a year.
      • Jefferies' EUR 1.35 target and the 100 percent buy share are this hypothesis in the market.
  • What we concede.
    • The operating evidence is genuinely strong: the mitigation was delivered, not promised
    • UK growth accelerated through the duty
    • the 2026 contract wins are real.
    • The price is not demanding against peers.
    • If the cleared branch happens, the return is a C on our arithmetic (14.4 percent a year at 13.5x) and higher on consensus earnings.
    • The reported loss is largely amortisation of acquired intangibles and deal costs, which the market is right to look through in part.
  • Why we still hold our view.
    • The return in the competing hypothesis is the product of three things going right, growth held through the cuts, synergies delivered and no further deal, and its odds are the product of ours: 0.60 times 0.35 times at most 0.65 on restraint is about 14 percent, before the contract cycle.
    • The modal path with the multiple held is a D and the drop-through adverse case an F.
    • The deleveraging promise has been deferred three times in the files (the 2.5x target moved from "within two years" in October 2025 to FY2028 to FY2029) while two deals were done.
    • The reported earnings do not yet exist, so the multiple sits on a line the company defines.
    • Management is a D on the framework's own red flags (dangerous leverage, a debt-funded buyback, empire-building), which parks the case whatever the return.
  • The bounded-downside claim, tested, because the competing hypothesis rests on one.
    • The claim that the contracted lottery business covers the enterprise value does not survive like-for-like arithmetic.
    • Legacy B2B adjusted EBITDA was EUR 52.0 million in H1 2026, about EUR 105 million a year before the Ohio loss
    • at the lowest EV/EBITDA in the comparison set (Everi's 4.1x) that covers EUR 430 million, and at the highest multiple for contracted lottery assets (Lottomatica's 9.4x FY2027) EUR 990 million, against an enterprise value of EUR 3,559 million: 12 to 28 percent cover.
    • The floor under the equity is the UK online business, and that is the business the duty is aimed at.
