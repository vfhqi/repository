Section 1 – Brief company description and history

Section summary
  1. Telia entered the 22 October 2026 results with a stronger Swedish and Baltic operating profile, improving cash generation and lower leverage, but with Finland and Norway still preventing the market from treating the recovery as broad-based or fully structural.
1.
Leading regional operator: Telia is a telecommunications infrastructure company serving the Nordic and Baltic markets through mobile, broadband, television, business solutions, towers and related connectivity services, with approximately 25 million mobile, broadband and television subscriptions reported for 2025. Its strategic position is strongest where network quality, security and converged household services support customer retention, while its principal earnings exposure is Sweden, followed by Norway, Finland and the Baltic operations.
2.
Simplified portfolio: Telia has materially narrowed its portfolio toward core connectivity and adjacencies, following the disposal of Denmark and the television and media business, while retaining towers, fibre, Telia Finance, Latvia and selected technology activities within Other operations. The disposal of TV and Media to Schibsted closed on 1 July 2025, making current comparisons more dependent on continuing telecom operations rather than the broader media portfolio.
3.
Geographic earnings concentration: Deutsche Bank estimated that Sweden would represent 46% of Telia’s 2027 adjusted EBITDA, Norway 19%, Finland 14%, Lithuania 7%, Estonia 5% and Other operations 9%. This concentration makes Swedish execution the main determinant of group earnings, but also means that persistent weakness in Finland or competitive pressure in Norway can materially dilute group earnings quality even when reported revenue beats expectations.
4.
Three-year revenue base: Telia’s reported revenue increased from SEK 88.6 billion in 2023 to SEK 89.1 billion in 2024, before falling to SEK 81.0 billion in 2025 as disposals changed the continuing portfolio. On the more relevant like-for-like basis, service revenue increased 1.8% in 2024 and 1.5% in 2025, while the first nine months of 2026 delivered 2.8% growth, showing a gradual improvement rather than a high-growth reset.
5.
EBITDA recovery: Adjusted EBITDA rose from SEK 30.3 billion in 2023 to SEK 31.3 billion in 2024 and SEK 31.9 billion in 2025, with the 2025 result benefiting from the major simplification and cost programme launched during 2024. Telia’s 2025-2027 ambition was approximately 4% adjusted EBITDA compound annual growth, so the historical improvement supported the target but did not by itself prove that the cost savings could be repeated after the initial workforce reduction.
6.
Margin progression: Group adjusted EBITDA margin increased from 34.2% in 2023 to 35.2% in 2024, while the 2025 annual-report figures show a continuing operations margin of approximately 39.5% after portfolio changes and restatement effects. The first nine months of 2026 produced a 40.6% margin, unchanged from the comparable period, indicating that revenue growth was being converted into EBITDA without further group-wide margin expansion before the Q3 mix effects were considered.
7.
Q3 operating trajectory: Telia reported Q3 2026 revenue of SEK 21.3 billion and adjusted EBITDA of SEK 8.9 billion, compared with SEK 19.9 billion and SEK 8.5 billion respectively in Q3 2025. Like-for-like service revenue growth accelerated to 3.3% from 2.8% in Q2, but adjusted EBITDA growth slowed to 2.9% from 3.4%, showing that the incremental revenue contained a less profitable mix. Sweden as the recovery engine: Sweden delivered Q3 service revenue growth of 4.7% and adjusted EBITDA growth of 2.1%, with mobile service revenue returning to 0.9% growth after declining 1.0% in Q2. Sweden as the recovery engine (cont.): Telia said the Swedish result included elevated business solutions and other non-connectivity revenue, some of which had been pulled forward from Q4, so the headline improvement exceeded the sustainable underlying growth rate investors were seeking.
8.
Convergence supports retention: Telia’s Swedish consumer strategy is built around combining mobile, broadband and television services, and the Q3 presentation showed converged customers at approximately 55% of the base, or nearly 60% excluding former Halebop customers. Management stated that each additional service reduces household churn by about 50%, making convergence a potentially more durable earnings driver than isolated price increases. - Earnings Calls Finland remains the principal weak spot: Finland’s Q3 service revenue declined 0.2% like for like, with mobile service revenue down 3.9% and fixed service revenue up 5.4% through business solutions and broadband. Morgan Stanley described the EBITDA result as 0.9% growth from lower operating expenses, but the combination of falling mobile revenue and cost-led EBITDA growth left Finland less supportive of a durable EPS recovery than Sweden or Lithuania.
9.
Norway is improving, but competitively exposed: Norway delivered Q3 service revenue growth of 1.8% and adjusted EBITDA growth of 0.9%, while mobile postpaid subscribers improved by approximately 10,000 during the quarter. Norway is improving, but competitively exposed (cont.): The improvement was encouraging relative to the prior quarter, but Telia still faced ICE and Lyse competition, and the proposed radio access network-sharing arrangement is intended to reduce costs rather than remove the underlying market-share threat. Baltics provide the cleanest growth: Lithuania delivered Q3 revenue growth of 9.7% and adjusted EBITDA growth of 9.5%, while its nine-month adjusted EBITDA margin increased to 41.0% from 40.3%. Estonia and Lithuania therefore provided a stronger growth and margin profile than the mature Nordic markets, although Lithuania’s nine-month free cash flow declined 10.1% because capital expenditure increased 44.8%, limiting the direct cash contribution of that growth.
10.
Cash flow and leverage strengthened: Telia generated SEK 9.3 billion of free cash flow in 2025, compared with SEK 3.4 billion in 2024, and reported SEK 7.8 billion in the first nine months of 2026 versus SEK 6.9 billion a year earlier. Leverage declined to 2x at September 2026 from 2.06x at June and 2.28x at the end of 2024, supporting the expectation of continued dividend growth and possible additional distributions if cash generation remains durable. EPS and shareholder returns improved: Total EPS increased from SEK 0.60 to SEK 0.69 in Q3 year on year, while nine-month EPS increased to SEK 1.68 from SEK 1.23. Telia paid a quarterly dividend of SEK 0.51 in Q3, and rolling twelve-month free cash flow per share reached SEK 2.61 against paid dividend per share of SEK 2.05, which strengthened the case for progressive distributions but did not yet establish the scale of any future buyback.
11.
Peer positioning: Deutsche Bank’s pre-results comparison rated Telia more favourably than Telenor, Elisa and Tele2 because it saw stronger earnings momentum, lower exposure to agentic artificial-intelligence-driven customer savings, and greater cost-cutting scope. The comparison was not uniformly positive, however, because Telenor had stronger forecast cash returns while Telia’s advantage depended on executing its proposed headcount reduction and converting operational momentum into higher medium-term free cash flow. Cost base remains the valuation swing factor: Telia announced a proposed reduction of approximately 900 positions and targeted SEK 1 billion of annual gross savings, with full run-rate benefit expected from 2028 and approximately SEK 600 million of restructuring charges in Q4 2026. Deutsche Bank’s pre-results thesis was that consensus assumed less domestic operating-cost reduction for Telia than for Telenor or Tele2, creating upside if the programme succeeded, whereas DNB Carnegie warned that Telia’s historical record on net cost reduction was not strong enough to treat the target as certain. Share-price performance was strong over 12 months but weaker recently: Bernstein reported that Telia’s share price had increased 28.3% over the preceding 12 months but declined 0.3% over six months and 1.1% over one month through 21 October 2026, underperforming the relevant European developed-market index over the six-month period. Share-price performance was strong over 12 months but weaker recently (cont.): DNB Carnegie separately reported three-month performance of negative 2.92%, six-month performance of negative 7.62% and 12- month performance of positive 24.59%, versus relative performance of negative 3.98%, negative 16.05% and positive 12.23%, respectively, with the discrepancy reflecting different measurement dates or benchmarks. Broker expectations diverged before Q3: DNB Carnegie entered the results with a Hold rating and SEK 51 target price, expecting Q3 like-for-like service revenue growth of 1.7% and adjusted EBITDA growth of 1.5%, followed by 5.3% EBITDA growth in Q4. Broker expectations diverged before Q3 (cont.): Bernstein entered with an Underperform rating and SEK 39.20 target price, forecasting 2026 adjusted EPS of SEK 2.13 and 2027 adjusted EPS of SEK 2.42, while Deutsche Bank upgraded Telia to Buy and SEK 50 after raising its 2027-2030 free-cash-flow forecasts by 10%. 63 Telia Company (Hold): Q3 weakness well rehearsed, Q4 looks better – Q3 preview Broker revisions to Telia’s 2026–2028 EPS estimates.

Market bar was moderate, not transformational: The company’s Q3 delivery exceeded consensus on service revenue by 1.2% and adjusted EBITDA by approximately 1%, but Bernstein judged that the revenue beat was partly pulled forward from Q4, the EBITDA beat came largely from Other operations, and Finland and Norway each missed consensus by roughly 1%. The results therefore improved the near-term EPS and cash-flow outlook, but the contrasting broker valuations showed that the market still required evidence of sustainable Swedish growth, a Finnish recovery and repeatable cost savings before assigning Telia a durable premium to Nordic peers. Other side of the story: Telia could be closer to a structural recovery than the cautious interpretation implies if Swedish convergence, Baltic growth, Norwegian network savings and the SEK 1 billion cost programme reinforce one another, because Deutsche Bank forecast 2028 free cash flow 4% above consensus and 2027 free cash flow of SEK 10.7 billion against Telia’s minimum ambition of SEK 10 billion. Section 3 – Key moving parts and changes

Section summary
  1. The main earnings-relevant change is a shift from recovery through the existing country-led model toward a second, deeper simplification programme, with the near-term EPS effect limited but the 2028 free-cashflow potential materially higher if Telia converts gross savings into net recurring cost reduction.
1.
Leadership continuity, stronger accountability: Patrik Hofbauer remains President and Chief Executive Officer and Eric Hageman remains Chief Financial Officer, but the operating model is changing responsibility rather than executive leadership, with country units receiving more decision-making authority and Group Functions retaining strategy, governance and performance follow-up.
2.
Investor-relations continuity: No material change to Telia Company’s central investor-relations leadership was disclosed in the Q3 reporting materials, while Telia Lietuva continued to identify Darius Džiaugys as Head of Investor Relations, so the earnings-relevant change is organisational execution rather than a new investorrelations narrative.
3.
Second restructuring programme: Telia proposed removing approximately 900 employee and consultant positions and reducing other operating costs, targeting SEK 1 billion of annual gross savings with full run-rate benefit from 2028, after the December 2024 programme had already reduced headcount and central resources materially. 22 Oct Telia Company – 2026 Q3 Presentation Telia headcount decline and projected workforce reduction through January 2027.
4.
Workforce burden concentrated in weaker markets: External reporting indicates that approximately 280 positions are expected to be removed in Finland, 260 in Sweden, 150 in Norway, 110 in Lithuania and 100 in Estonia, making Finland the largest proportional restructuring exposure while also highlighting that the weakest operating market is receiving the deepest intervention.
5.
Restructuring cash drag: Telia expects approximately SEK 600 million of restructuring charges in Q4 2026, with the cash-flow effect mainly occurring in 2027, so the programme should not be treated as an immediate 2026 EPS or free-cash-flow uplift.
6.
Gross-to-net uncertainty: The SEK 1 billion target includes position reductions and additional operating-cost cuts, but Telia has not disclosed how much comes from employees, consultants or other expenditure, leaving the eventual EPS benefit below the gross headline until implementation evidence appears.
7.
Localization versus central scale: Approximately 220 positions are proposed to move primarily from the common Technology unit into country organisations, while central technology resources remain in areas such as security and television where Telia believes scale benefits persist, creating a trade-off between faster local execution and possible duplication of capabilities.
8.
Bredband2 now part of the operating base: Telia completed the SEK 3.1 billion Bredband2 acquisition and consolidated the business from February 2026, adding a value-oriented Swedish broadband brand and crossselling potential, while approximately SEK 280 million of annual internal wholesale revenue is expected to disappear on consolidation without an adjusted EBITDA effect.
9.
Bredband2 integration is a medium-term growth lever: Telia’s Q3 materials describe the integration as progressing well and identify opportunities to broaden the fixed-broadband offer and cross-sell services, so the acquisition can support revenue mix and customer retention over the next three years but does not remove the need for organic Swedish growth.
10.
