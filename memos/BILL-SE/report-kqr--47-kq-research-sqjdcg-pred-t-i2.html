Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Billerud's upcoming earnings per share performance depends heavily on the resolution of critical cost, pricing, and operational dynamics across its European and North American assets.

Central investment debate

1.
Central inquiry: "how comprehensively are these dynamics articulated in the current narrative of sell-side commentary and of investor commentary: the end of Swedish storm-salvage wood and the meaning of management's 2027 wood guidance; the move of liquid packaging board to annual price setting and the 2027 round; the timing of the UK recycled capacity and its effect on the virgin premium; the roll-off of Billerud's currency hedges during 2027 and Riksbank-driven krona strength; the loss of free emission allowances, Swedish ETS1 reentry and ETS2; the Gävle recovery boiler decision and its effect on dividends and buybacks; a possible European capacity closure by Billerud (Karlsborg and Pietarsaari have been named by Jefferies as candidates) or by peers; North American Evolution volumes (Tribute and Voyager) against the 300kt target and US solid bleached board oversupply; the gap between named-broker forecasts and FactSet consensus; the difference between FY2027 EPS revisions and EBITDA and sales revisions?"
2.
Upward price momentum: Billerud implemented sequential price hikes in both Europe and North America to counteract elevated raw material costs and inflation during the third quarter of 2026. The company relies on these pricing and mix actions to offset significant variable cost pressures and protect packaging margins. Resolving whether these price adjustments can permanently cover structurally higher Nordic fiber costs will decide if the group can sustain its target EBITDA.
3.
Severe margin compression: Nordic packaging producers are heavily exposed to structurally elevated wood fiber costs following the permanent loss of historical supply sources. Billerud faces high volatility in its cost structure because Central Sweden pulpwood costs have increased by approximately fifty percent. Every ten percent change in Nordic wood costs represents an estimated impact of nine hundred million Swedish kronor on group EBITDA.
4.
Technical valuation reset: Jefferies downgraded Billerud to Hold on September 29, 2026, while raising its target price to SEK 90. The broker noted that further valuation expansion requires active capacity rationalization or specific strategic actions to balance market oversupply. While North American operational targets appear highly achievable, Europe requires substantial cost relief to justify a higher trading multiple.

5.

This rating shift reflects investor concern that earnings improvement may hold at stable 2026 levels rather than accelerating.

Rapid equity expansion: Billerud recorded a sharp share price appreciation of approximately 43% over a six-month period, reflecting a significant relief rally in the paper sector. This valuation run-up was driven by better-thanexpected quarterly operating earnings and sequential margin recovery within the European business. Section summary
  1. Billerud faces structural margin headwinds in Europe as domestic wood salvage benefits end, although management expects stable sequential raw material costs.
  2. Forest ownership models provide strategic self-sufficiency for peers like SCA and Holmen, highlighting Billerud's high exposure to volatile open-market pulpwood prices.

Domestic fiber market dynamics

1.
Stable sequential outlook: Billerud expects sequential stability in its Nordic pulpwood costs heading into the 2027 operating period. Cole Hathorn of Jefferies reported on October 23, 2026, that management sees flat pulpwood costs quarter-on-quarter in late 2026, with average 2027 costs remaining stable versus 2026 levels. This stable cost outlook is critical because it indicates that recent cost deflation benefits will persist and support near-term European operating margins.

Diminishing storm salvage: Billerud anticipates a shift in wood availability as the temporary supply of cheap Swedish storm-salvage pulpwood concludes in the fourth quarter of 2026. This depletion of cheap domestic wood means that Billerud may experience unfavourable fiber cost inflections unless demand remains muted.

Tomi Railo of DNB Carnegie stated on October 22, 2026, that the company's wood self-sufficiency in Scandinavia is below five percent. This structural deficit makes Billerud's European pulp and board margins exceptionally sensitive to Nordic pricing compared to backward-integrated peers.

Section 3 – Liquid Packaging Board annual Pricing and the 2027 Round

Section summary
  1. Billerud's transition from multi-year to annual pricing for liquid packaging board stabilizes margins but transfers volume volatility risk directly to converting buyers.
  2. Major converters Elopak and SIG employ different pricing structures to manage paperboard cost headwinds, with SIG relying on defensive long-term contracts and Elopak utilizing raw material surcharges.

Transition to annual pricing structures

1.
Structured pricing: Billerud transitioned its historical multi-year liquid packaging board contracts toward annual negotiations to prevent severe margin compression. Cole Hathorn of Jefferies explained on September 19, 2023, that Sweden wood costs surged over fifty percent, making it necessary to reprice these long-term agreements. This contractual shift helps Billerud build in inflation escalation clauses for protection against raw material volatility.

Significant sales exposure: Billerud relies on liquid packaging board as a primary contributor to its European division sales. Cole Hathorn of Jefferies highlighted on October 23, 2026, that liquid packaging board represents approximately twenty-three percent of Billerud's total sales. Company interim reports from December 2025 confirmed that Region Europe liquid packaging board sales reached one thousand eight hundred eighty-seven million Swedish kronor.

High margin sensitivity: Billerud's operating earnings are highly responsive to price adjustments within its liquid packaging board segment. Cole Hathorn of Jefferies calculated on September 19, 2023, that a five to ten percent price improvement in liquid packaging board increases European division EBITDA by four hundred to eight hundred million Swedish kronor. This price sensitivity underscores why Billerud prioritized repricing its customer agreements following historical cost inflation.

Billerud reported in January 2026 that the company secured liquid packaging board volumes for 2026 in line with previous levels. This volume security suggests that Billerud's annual contract negotiations focused on value preservation over volume growth.

Contrary evidence: Billerud faces pricing pressure on liquid packaging board when European consumer packaging demand softens. Section 10 – If the Answer Differs from the Common View

Section summary
  1. The prevailing narrative treats 2027 as a holding pattern for 2026 gains. Management expects flat pulpwood costs quarter-on-quarter in late 2026, with average 2027 costs stable versus 2026 levels. Brokers have become more cautious after a strong run: Billerud's share price rose approximately 43% over six months, and the DNB Carnegie downgrade reflects investor concern that earnings improvement may hold at stable 2026 levels rather than accelerating. The alternative reality is that several headwinds coincide in 2027 and turn a pause into severe margin compression. The evidence does not prove that outcome. It does show that the report's separate risk channels, which the market models one at a time, could compound each other. EPS transmission. The largest channel is wood. Cheap Swedish storm-salvage pulpwood is expected to run out in the fourth quarter of 2026, and Jefferies warns that the end of excess storm-salvage felling will likely increase raw material competition. Wood represents approximately one-third of total operating costs, and every 10% change in Nordic wood costs impacts group EBITDA by 900 million Swedish kronor. Billerud has little protection, because its wood self-sufficiency in Scandinavia is below five percent. A guide for stable average costs is not the same as stable exit-rate costs. If spot competition re-emerges after salvage ends, the full-year average could hide a deteriorating run-rate. A second channel is currency. The euro hedged share falls from eighty percent in late 2026 to seventy-three percent in the first quarter of 2027, before falling to zero by the fourth quarter of 2027, and the dollar and sterling books follow a similar path. A ten percent change in the krona impacts pretax profit by six hundred forty million kronor before hedges. Wood and currency effects would therefore accumulate on the same earnings base, and they would reach EPS with a lag as hedges expire.

Regulatory and capital layers. The carbon effect is a further structural drag. The loss of free allowances represents a structural loss of approximately five hundred million Swedish kronor of historical annual EBITDA benefits. Management's view that the move into ETS1 on January 1, 2027 will be "pretty neutral" year over year depends on carbon prices not rising materially.

The Gävle boiler faces a final investment decision in 2027, is estimated to require more than three billion Swedish kronor, and Jefferies models a five hundred million kronor annual capital outflow starting in 2027. If earnings weaken while carbon costs rise, the cushion that supports a dividend payout of over fifty percent of net profit narrows.
