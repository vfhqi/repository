Section 16 – Conclusion

Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. Storskogen's Q2 2026 results marked a pivotal inflection point from defensive deleveraging into organic profit growth resumption and disciplined M&A acceleration, delivering a 2% beat on both sales and adjusted EBITA versus consensus while maintaining leverage comfortably within target at 2.4x. The quarter validated management's strategic pivot toward quality-tilted acquisitions at 7x EBITA or below, though persistent cash conversion softness to 74% LTM and ongoing construction-exposed margin pressure in Services temper the nearterm re-rating case relative to proven Nordic peers trading at substantial valuation premiums.

Financial delivery exceeded expectations across headline metrics. Q2 2026 net sales rose 5% year-over-year to SEK 8,861 million, representing a 2% beat against DNB Carnegie's forecast of SEK 8,702 million, while adjusted EBITA increased 2% to SEK 864 million versus the SEK 847 million consensus estimate. This marked the first quarter of EBITA growth since Q4 2024, signaling a structural turn in the earnings trajectory. Organic sales growth reached a robust 5% in Q2 2026, driven primarily by the Industry segment's 6% organic expansion supported by strong global automation demand. Profit for the period surged 46% to SEK 379 million, benefiting from successful debt refinancing that reduced net financial costs to SEK 146 million from SEK 297 million in the prior year. Leverage remained stable at 2.4x, sitting comfortably within the group's 2.0-3.0x target range and providing meaningful headroom for resumed acquisitions.

M&A acceleration delivered ahead of sell-side expectations. Storskogen completed two platform acquisitions in Q2 2026–Safi Equipment in Services and Darlington EMS in Industry–reaccelerating its deal engine faster than the market anticipated. These transactions were executed at disciplined valuation multiples of 7x EBITA or below, addressing historical concerns about the elevated pricing paid during the aggressive 2021- 2022 expansion phase. Following the Q2 outperformance, DNB Carnegie raised adjusted EPS estimates by 2% across 2026e, 2027e, and 2028e, projecting EPS of SEK 1.12, SEK 1.28, and SEK 1.39 respectively. The broker upgraded its target price to SEK 16.0 from SEK 15.5, citing cautiously positive momentum heading into the second half of 2026. 5

Segment performance revealed sharp operational divergence. The Industry business area drove the Q2 beat with strong organic sales growth of 6%, powered by robust global automation demand and expanding order books. The adjusted EBITA margin landed at 9.7%, slightly below the prior year's 10.0%, though structurally flat after adjusting for a SEK 22 million one-off LTIP credit in Q2 2025. In contrast, Trade adjusted EBITA declined 6% to SEK 212 million, pressured by subdued Scandinavian retail demand as customers optimized beauty product inventory.

Services segment profitability remained impacted by weak Nordic construction activity, with organic profit recovery slower than expected despite 9% sales growth from the Safi acquisition. The unexpected departure of Services Head Jesper Kronstrand in May 2026 forced interim leadership by Head of M&A Johan Ekström, temporarily disrupting operational oversight in the underperforming segment.

Cash generation undershot buy-side forecasts. Operating cash flow fell to SEK 465 million in Q2 2026 from

SEK 527 million in Q2 2025, undershooting expectations as late-quarter sales activity temporarily tied up receivables. Operating cash conversion LTM declined to 74% compared to 80% in the prior-year period, reflecting working capital pressure that management attributed to strong June activity. While successful bond refinancing reduced net interest expenses to SEK 145 million from SEK 248 million, delivering a structural quarterly saving of SEK 20 million, the persistent deterioration in cash conversion raises questions about the sustainability of self-funded M&A if receivables fail to liquidate rapidly in the second half of 2026.

Strategic execution validated the quality-tilted M&A framework. Management tightened investment focus around five core themes, targeting less cyclical industries outside Sweden to drive geographic diversification. The group is actively concentrating Trade segment exposure on B2B-oriented distributors by divesting lowermargin B2C retail operations, exemplified by the Q1 2026 divestment of Swiss-based Perfect Hair, eliminating SEK 278 million in low-margin annual sales. CEO Christer Hansson confirmed that after years of strengthening the financial position, the group is shifting its focus back to growth, maintaining the long-term goal of average annual EBITA growth of 15% supported by both organic and acquired expansion.

Macroeconomic and regulatory headwinds persist across the portfolio. The European Central Bank is priced for 72bp of additional tightening through September 2027, fundamentally shifting corporate capital allocation strategies away from aggressive debt-fueled expansion. European swap rates rose 70bp over August and September 2026, dragging mortgage rates toward 3.6% and delaying any meaningful recovery in residential construction starts. Storskogen released its first annual statement under the EU Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive on 9 April 2026, substantially increasing administrative burdens, while the Swedish Screening of Foreign Direct Investments Act can impose fines up to SEK 100 million for non-compliance, adding transaction complexity to the resumed acquisition program. Raw materials and consumables rose 8% year-over-year to SEK 4,884 million in Q2 2026, outpacing the 5% net sales growth and representing clear margin dilution to 55.1% of sales.

Valuation discount to peers remains steep but justified by execution risks. Storskogen has historically traded at a steep 50% discount on EV/EBITA compared to proven Swedish serial acquirers, justified by its shorter track record, lower margins, lower return on capital, and higher historical leverage. DNB Carnegie maintained its BUY rating, arguing that Storskogen is attractively valued at 10x 2027e EV/EBITA at the SEK 16.0 target price. However, achieving the long-term annual EBITA growth target of 15% will be challenging, and operational slip-ups or delayed deals could cause Storskogen to fall short of this ambitious target.

The rapid M&A ramp-up introduces severe execution and integration risks, as losing pricing discipline or acquiring complex businesses requiring substantial restructuring could trigger operational bottlenecks and halt the re-rating.

Key counter-thesis centers on structural integration complexity. Managing a decentralized, highly internationalized portfolio of over 100 business units introduces significant operational and integration bottlenecks, and if new acquisitions fail to deliver expected collaboration synergies, the group's progress toward its structural 10% EBITA margin could be severely delayed. The rapid proliferation of programmatic acquirers could permanently elevate baseline valuations of high-quality niches, and if Storskogen rigidly adheres to its 7x EBITA multiple ceiling, it may find itself consistently outbid by peer compounders or forced to acquire lowerquality businesses.

While management expressed high confidence in accelerating M&A quarter by quarter within strict pricing limits, reaccelerating acquisitions too aggressively could lead to a loss of valuation discipline, and if the group fails to execute on the challenging 15% EBITA CAGR target, the anticipated equity re-rating will be delayed.
