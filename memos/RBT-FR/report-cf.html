EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Robertet SA has undergone material corporate transformation through the appointment of its first external CEO Jérôme Bruhat in July 2022, the complete exit of competitors Givaudan and DSM-Firmenich from the shareholder register, and the introduction of strategic institutional partners Peugeot Invest and Fonds Stratégique de Participations. This governance shift initiates operational modernization while preserving family control at 54.5% voting rights and raises the 2030 revenue ambition to €1.1-1.2 billion with a target EBITDA margin exceeding 20%.

The company delivered exceptional recent financial performance with 7.60% organic revenue growth in FY25 reaching €843.90M and an expanded EBITDA margin of 20.60%, substantially outpacing large-cap peer guidance of 2-4% and demonstrating robust execution across the historical L3Y trajectory.

Five critical structural drivers will dictate forward earnings trajectory: fine fragrance premiumization driving an estimated 2-3% EPS CAGR with high plausibility given luxury brand in-house perfumery trends; raw material costprice lag creating an anticipated 100-150 basis point EBITDA margin headwind with high plausibility due to suppliers demanding 20-40% price hikes; emerging markets capacity expansion targeting 1.5-2.5% sales CAGR with medium plausibility contingent on APAC client wins; bolt-on M&A execution projected to contribute 1-2% sales CAGR with high plausibility supported by conservative 0.50x EBITDA leverage; and Health & Beauty scaling offering 50-80 basis points margin uplift with medium plausibility constrained by current sub-scale operations at

3% of revenues. Management adopts a conservative sandbagger archetype by guiding FY26 organic growth to approximately 5% while projecting EBITDA margin contraction back to the historical average of 19.20%, yet the company has consistently outperformed initial targets with FY24 delivering 10.30% organic growth against a 7% guided trajectory. The sell-side consensus remains structurally divided with a small covering pool of two primary brokers: Jefferies maintains a Buy rating with a €950 price target emphasizing structural fine fragrance overweights and boutique positioning, while Bernstein initiated at Market-Perform with an €888 target citing near-term purchasing conditions, divisional mix headwinds, and currency pressure that will weigh on FY26E profitability.

Peer-relative positioning shows Robertet ranks first among F&F comparables on organic growth momentum at 7.60% versus sector median of 1.90%, though it operates with a persistent 200 basis point EBITDA margin discount to large-cap leaders like Givaudan at 24.20% and maintains elevated working capital intensity at 37% of sales versus peer best-practice of 25%. No active activist presence has been identified in the shareholder base following the complete competitor exit, establishing full strategic independence for the Maubert family.

Three material plausible problem to monitor emerge from cross-sectional analysis: severe foreign exchange headwinds subtracted €28M or 3.50% from FY25 reported revenue with a calculated 5% USD fluctuation translating to a €5.81 per share EPS impact representing 12.86% earnings volatility; suppliers demanding 20-40% synthetic input price hikes amid Middle East geopolitical tension that will compress margins through the typical 12-18 month pricing lag; and structurally high working capital requirements at 37% of sales creating persistent cash conversion drag compared to peer median of 30%, with Robertet's 38.30% FCF-to-EBITDA conversion significantly trailing Givaudan's 63% benchmark.

Additional stress-test concerns include apparent FY25 margin expansion being heavily reliant on temporary raw material cost deflation from cheap Chinese and Indian synthetic imports that will reverse in 2H26, extreme agricultural volatility and climate-induced crop failures that could force expensive synthetic alternative purchases wiping out gross margin improvements, and the deceptive predictability of guidance where a simultaneous 40% synthetic cost spike and sharp USD depreciation would cause structural EBITDA margin collapse below 18% through the historical pricing lag mechanism.

1.
Investment verdict: Robertet presents a high-quality boutique F&F compounder executing above-peer organic growth through natural ingredient leadership and fine fragrance exposure, yet near-term earnings face material downside asymmetry from the convergence of synthetic input inflation, adverse currency translation, and elevated working capital intensity that will likely compress FY26 EBITDA margins toward the 19% guided floor, creating a compressed risk-reward profile at current valuations until cost-price lag dynamics normalize over the subsequent 12-18 months and emerging market capacity investments demonstrate sustained commercial traction.

Plausible problems to monitor

Robertet's working capital intensity at 37% of sales represents a material structural weakness relative to its peer group, creating a persistent drag on free cash flow generation that undermines shareholder returns. This metric sits substantially above Symrise at 35% of sales and dramatically exceeds Givaudan's highly efficient 25% of sales, reflecting the capital-intensive natural sourcing model that requires holding two to three months of inventory across 1,600 raw materials sourced from sixty countries.

The cash conversion penalty is severe, with Robertet achieving only 38.3% free cash flow conversion of EBITDA in 2025 compared to Givaudan's 63% conversion rate, representing a 650 basis point disadvantage that directly limits the group's capacity to fund growth investments and return capital to shareholders. The working capital burden intensified during fiscal year 2025 when seasonal activity in May and June drove €36.5 million in working capital outflows during the first half, temporarily compressing cash generation despite robust operating performance. 311 - Earnings Calls The company operates at a persistent 200 basis point EBITDA margin discount to large-cap flavors and fragrances peers, a structural profitability gap that reflects both the lower-margin Raw Materials division and the absence of scale economies available to multinational competitors.

While Robertet's underlying EBITDA margin reached 20.6% in fiscal year 2025, representing a 120 basis point year-on-year improvement, this performance still trails Givaudan's robust 23.3% forecasted EBITDA margin for fiscal year 2026, highlighting the boutique operator's continuing challenge in translating natural ingredients premiumization into peer-level profitability. Management's forward guidance compounds this concern, as the company anticipates EBITDA margins contracting back to the historical average of 19.2%, representing a sharp decline from the record 20.6% achieved in 2025, driven by anticipated supplier inflation and cost-price lag dynamics.

Fonds Stratégique de Participations and Peugeot Invest Assets following their capital entry provides enhanced corporate governance oversight, the Maubert family group consolidated its voting rights to 54.5% as of December 2025, ensuring that strategic decisions remain firmly under family control. This concentrated ownership structure limits the influence of institutional investors and reduces the likelihood of external pressure to optimize capital allocation or pursue transformational mergers that might accelerate profitability improvements.

The Fragrance division recorded a modest 2.1% organic sales growth in fiscal year 2025, severely held back by customer destocking in North America, exposing the company's vulnerability to inventory cycle volatility among key clients. North American like-for-like growth was limited to 2.5% due to the fragrance destocking, creating a significant geographic concentration risk that undermines the group's otherwise robust emerging market expansion narrative.

This temporary cyclical headwind was compounded by organic sales growth moderating from 9.2% in the first half of 2025 to 5.9% in the second half of 2025, suggesting that the deceleration extended beyond North America and reflected broader demand normalization following postpandemic consumption peaks.

The most recent second half 2025 recurring EBITDA of €74 million came in 2% below consensus expectations of €75 million, representing a rare miss that highlights execution risks associated with the anticipated margin normalization cycle. More concerning, profitability faces severe headwinds from suppliers demanding +20% to +40% price hikes on synthetics, with margins temporarily compressed by a typical 12 to 18-month pricing lag before customer contracts can be renegotiated.

Bernstein initiated coverage forecasting an 8% decline in fiscal year 2026 net profit, reflecting analyst concerns that the current cost inflation wave will overwhelm management's ability to protect margins through operational efficiencies and selective pricing actions. The active sell-side pool consists of only two primary brokers delivering detailed equity coverage updates, with coverage led by Bernstein and Jefferies, creating a material information asymmetry risk for investors.

Political instability in Haiti forced the suspension of Fair for Life certification for vetiver sourcing, exposing the company's supply chain to geopolitical disruption risks that threaten the strategic sustainability credentials underpinning its premium positioning. Any further geopolitical or climate-related disruptions to natural ingredient sourcing regions could force the company to substitute lower-quality or synthetic alternatives, undermining brand differentiation and eroding the natural ingredients premium that drives abovemarket pricing power.

The company's working capital intensity of 37% of sales positions Robertet in the bottom 5% of capital efficiency relative to flavors and fragrances peers, creating a structural cash conversion headwind that is materially worse than Symrise at 35% and dramatically inferior to Givaudan's highly efficient 25% of sales.
