EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF) 1 findings

Aroundtown SA demonstrates continuity in senior leadership through internal promotions including CFO Jonas Tintelnot and Chief Capital Markets Officer Timothy Wright, yet faces persistent governance scrutiny over founder influence and suspended employee engagement surveys. The company has executed a strategic realignment toward capital recycling and consolidation, completing over €10 billion of disposals since 2020 while increasing its Grand City Properties ownership stake to 81.5% from 62.5% to simplify corporate structure and capture residential cash flows.

- Earnings Calls The historical earnings trajectory reveals steady FFO I contraction from €332 million in FY 2023 to €288 million in FY 2025, with FFO I per share declining to €0.26 in FY 2025 from €0.30 in FY 2023. Finance expenses rose to €243 million in FY 2025, driving the interest coverage ratio down to 3.4x in Q1 2026 from 4.3x in Q1 2025, while EPRA LTV reached 59% when treating subordinated perpetual hybrids as debt. - Earnings Calls External value-chain forces present severe headwinds in commercial offices with German top-seven market vacancies climbing to 8.9% and Aroundtown's office like-for-like rental growth falling to 1.5%, contrasted against robust hotel performance at 4.0% like-for-like growth and residential strength at 3.7% supported by chronic housing shortages. Macro conditions remain challenging with 10-year Bund yields reaching a post-2011 peak of 3.20% and the ECB holding deposit rates at 2.25%, creating substantial refinancing pressure.

The most recent Q1 2026 earnings delivery showed stable FFO I per share of €0.07 supported by accretive share buybacks, though absolute FFO I declined 8% year-over-year to €70 million due to rising finance expenses. Net rental income rose 1% to €297 million, with hotel like-for-like rental growth leading at 4.0% and residential delivering 3.7%, while commercial offices lagged at 1.5% as EPRA office vacancy reached 13.2%.

Peer-relative positioning places Aroundtown in the bottom quartile of its peer group with like-for-like organic rental growth of 3.0% lagging Covivio's 5.0% and Vonovia's 3.5%. The company trades at a 68% discount to NAV versus a commercial peer average discount of 40%, while consensus headline earnings estimates were revised down by 7.5% over the last six months. Sell-side coverage demonstrates substantial bearish bias with active coverage dominated by four global brokers fielding five named lead analysts.

Consensus rating mix stands at 25% Overweight, 50% Equal-weight, and 25% Underweight with target price dispersion ranging from €2.00 to €5.00. Three out of four primary brokers maintain Underweight or Underperform ratings, with dominant bear arguments focusing on structural refinancing drag where higher coupon debt refinancing will permanently squeeze the interest coverage ratio, tight 4.0% EPRA net initial yields leaving commercial property valuations exposed to further devaluations, and aboveaverage leverage of 59% LTV severely limiting capital structure resilience.

No active hostile activist presence exists in the AS library for Aroundtown SA, though unresolved workplace cultural issues and founder-dominated advisory structures could permanently impair capital access if governance perceptions deteriorate further.

The top six ranked key drivers of FFO trajectory include: refinancing debt cost resets with stated weighted average cost of debt ticking up to 2.3% in March 2026 as EUR 1.4 billion matures through 2026 at 0.7% average cost while new bond issuances carry much higher interest coupons of 3.25% to 5.25%, squeezing interest coverage down to 3.4x; residential GCP consolidation increasing ownership stake to 81.5% from 62.5% delivering an annualized FFO I contribution of EUR 35 million; hotel portfolio recovery with the hotel segment leading organic growth at 4.0% like-for-like rents; portfolio CapEx demands with total investment capex reaching EUR 421 million in FY25; serviced apartment conversions targeting 120,000 square meters of office conversions to generate EUR 15 million in additional rent; and asset disposal volume execution with signed year-to-date disposals reaching EUR 300 million in 2026.

Forward guidance credibility positions management as an honest-tracker that historically achieves the mid-tohigh end of guided FFO ranges, though the initial FY 2026 FFO I guidance of €250-280 million fell significantly below prior run-rates. FFO I remains highly sensitive to refinancing low-coupon debt with €1.4 billion resetting in 2026, and Morgan Stanley projects declining FFO per share to €0.23 in FY 2027 and €0.22 in FY 2028.

Material downside risks include escalating interest rates and office vacancies compressing the ICR below 3.0x triggering asset devaluations, unresolved high leverage and office vacancy rates dragging FFO I further if refinancing interest spreads widen rapidly, and apparent operational stability in hotel and residential segments masking a structural commercial downturn where continued office vacancy spikes and rising refinancing costs could compress the ICR below 3.0x. Additionally, escalating geopolitical energy shocks and hawkish ECB policy could push yields higher triggering severe property devaluations and dilutive equity raises, while unresolved high leverage and office vacancy rates may force distress sales fully unwinding the benefits of capital recycling.
