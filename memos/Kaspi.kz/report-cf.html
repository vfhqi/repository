EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Structural Changes and Strategic Positioning

Kaspi.kz completed the acquisition of Hepsiburada in Türkiye for USD 1,127 million, with its equity ownership stake reaching 86.74% by mid-2026, fundamentally transforming the company from a Kazakhstan-focused fintech into a regional super-app spanning over 100 million consumers across two markets. The acquisition of Rabobank A.Ş., subsequently rebranded as Hepsi Bank A.Ş., secured a commercial banking license backed by a USD 300 million capital commitment, enabling direct embedded fintech monetization through merchant and consumer credit products that were previously unavailable in the Turkish market. The rollout of the Kasper AI assistant introduced natural-language conversational discovery, with one in five consumers utilizing the feature within less than one month post-launch, materially accelerating the consumer purchase journey and demonstrating the company's commitment to technology-led differentiation. However, activist short-seller Culper Research released a highly critical report alleging undisclosed Russian sanctions exposure, causing a sharp 19% valuation drop over two consecutive trading sessions, though Kazakhstan regulatory agencies formally defended Kaspi Bank as fully compliant with international sanctions. #I

At the governance level, the corporate board appointed four independent non-executive directors and approved three-year vesting stock options to align board members with substantial long-term equity growth, representing a significant corporate governance milestone that reduces historical key-person risk concentrated around founder-CEO Mikheil Lomtadze. Lomtadze maintained a stable leadership team and increased his personal equity ownership stake during favourable secondary market transactions, reinforcing alignment with minority shareholders despite tightly centralized decision-making processes that potentially create key-person risk during unanticipated executive transitions.

Being a foreign private issuer permits Kaspi.kz to follow Kazakh corporate governance rules, which offers weaker minority protections compared to US domestic standards, creating an ongoing tension between operational flexibility and investor safeguards. #A

Key Drivers of EPS Growth Through FY2028

The dominant forward EPS driver remains domestic e-commerce frequency expansion, with annualized purchases per consumer surging 36% year-over-year to 15.8, establishing a robust transaction-driven volume foundation that compounds through the closed-loop ecosystem. Value-added services constant-currency revenue accelerated by 49% year-over-year, driving the 3P e-commerce take rate up 160 bps to a record 16.1%, demonstrating that payments monetization via advertising and delivery services represents a high-margin, highplausibility pathway to sustained profitability expansion.

The Turkish expansion, anchored by the control-phase acquisition of Hepsiburada and the strategic relaunch of Hepsi Bank to deliver embedded financial services, integrates the newly launched Hepsicredit shopping loan product which reached 0.4% of total marketplace GMV, though near-term execution remains medium-plausibility due to integration complexity and currency depreciation headwinds. #I Elevated central bank interest rates pushed average deposit funding costs up by 150 bps to 14.5%, but Kaspi's strategic 100 bps cut on 3-month deposits signals immediate net interest margin relief, creating a highplausibility, medium-confidence driver contingent on National Bank of Kazakhstan base rate policy trajectory.

Scaling Kaspi Alaqan pay-by-palm biometric technology drives transaction speed and checkout conversion, while the Kasper AI personal assistant accelerated basket pathway journeys by 30% for 20% of users, representing a high-confidence operational cost-control and conversion driver with minimal external dependencies. Ecommerce purchase frequency and Payments VAS monetization co-occur as mutually reinforcing drivers of marketplace margin expansion, with an estimated moderate 45% joint probability for simultaneous domestic rate cuts and Turkish breakeven, establishing the forward EPS growth framework as structurally robust but dependent on successful execution across multiple geographies and macroeconomic tailwinds. #J

Plausible problems to monitor

Elevated deposit funding costs represent the most immediate margin pressure, with average funding costs rising 220 basis points to 14.3% during the first half of 2026. This compression drove consolidated EBITDA margins down to 36% from historically higher platform levels exceeding 60% in prior years, placing Kaspi's nearterm profitability below the peer median EBITDA margin of approximately 30% for regional financial technology platforms.

The Fintech segment has proven particularly vulnerable, with net margins compressing to an estimated 23% in 2026 from historical peaks of 26%, and sensitivity analysis indicating that each 100 basis point increase in cost of risk could drag total net income down by an additional 4%. While management initiated a 100 basis point deposit rate cut in August 2026 to alleviate pressure, the benefits remain delayed due to the maturity profile of the existing deposit base.

Turkish marketplace consolidation has introduced structural EBITDA dilution as Hepsiburada operates near breakeven while requiring substantial marketing and infrastructure investments. Morgan Stanley estimates that Turkish net losses peaked at USD 250 million in 2026, materially dragging down consolidated group profitability during what management frames as a strategic investment year. Severe Turkish Lira depreciation of 21% year-over-year against the Kazakh Tenge further compounds this headwind by reducing the reported contribution of Turkish operations to consolidated revenue growth.

This combination of operational losses and currency translation effects represents a negative divergence from the peer benchmark, as comparable regional e-commerce platforms like Allegro and MercadoLibre have maintained positive EBITDA contributions from international expansion initiatives.

Flat Payments EBITDA growth in recent quarters signals maturing monetization dynamics in the core Kazakhstan market, with quarterly Payments EBITDA remaining flat year-over-year at KZT 98 billion despite continued transaction volume expansion. This stagnation reflects heavy technology investment in biometric pay-by-palm infrastructure and competitive pressure from aggressive cashback campaigns by rivals like Freedom Holding.

The Payments take rate declined to 1% from 1.07%, indicating that transaction growth alone is insufficient to offset margin compression from strategic initiatives and competitive dynamics. This trend underperforms the peer median expectation of stable-to-expanding payment monetization rates among leading digital wallet platforms. Interoperable QR payment standard adoption represents a longer-term structural risk to Kaspi's historically closed-loop transaction moat, as the unified national standard allows competing banks to accept payments at Kaspi merchant QR codes.

While the full competitive impact remains uncertain, early indicators suggest potential transaction leakage to rival banking ecosystems as consumers gain optionality in payment instrument selection. This regulatory-mandated openness contrasts unfavorably with peer ecosystems in markets like Brazil and Argentina, where dominant payment platforms have successfully defended proprietary clearing networks against similar interoperability mandates.

Supply chain volatility in high-velocity categories like smartphones demonstrated the platform's exposure to external disruptions, with device import delays dragging late-2025 GMV growth down to 10-12% year-over-year. While volumes subsequently recovered to 15% growth as supply chains normalized, the episode revealed concentration risk in category-specific merchant inventories, particularly around cross-border import channels from China.

This dependency on external logistics infrastructure represents a negative divergence from vertically integrated peers like MercadoLibre, which have invested heavily in proprietary fulfillment networks to insulate marketplace velocity from third-party supply shocks. Working capital outflows from accelerated Turkish marketing campaigns and regional expansion investments have created near-term cash generation pressure during the first half of 2026, contrasting with the historically exceptional cash conversion profile that Kaspi maintained in prior periods.

While these outflows are strategic and temporary in nature, they represent a weaker cash generation profile relative to pure-play domestic peers like Halyk Bank, which benefit from mature, capital-light fee income streams without the drag of international marketentry investments.
