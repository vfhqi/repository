Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Kerry Group plc is stepping up its R&D investment to accelerate volume growth and long-term margin expansion after becoming a pure-play taste and nutrition company in late 2024.

Strategic Context of the Key Question

1.
Key Question: "How large is the R&D step-up Kerry Group plc (KRZ-IE) plans to make to reach 5-6% of revenue by 2030 (from about 4.6%, or EUR 314m of research, development and application services, in 2025): what is the expected euro increase by year; which P&L lines carry it (cost of sales, operating expenses or non-trading items); how much of the Q3 2026 slowdown in EBITDA margin expansion it caused (Deutsche Bank attributed half to R&D); how it nets against Accelerate 2.0's EUR 100m of savings by 2028; and what is the evidence from Givaudan, Symrise, DSM-Firmenich, IFF and Novonesis that higher R&D intensity raised win rates, volume growth or margins, and over what lag?"
2.
Research scope: Kerry Group plc's strategic evaluation is supported by analyzing 12 relevant peer companies, 8 historical precedents, and 345 distinct primary and secondary documents. A total of 5 key peers and 100 documents were utilized in this final report to test the R&D claims. No major peer was left out, as Novonesis, DSM-Firmenich, Givaudan, Symrise, and IFF were fully assessed. This extensive database provides high confidence in evaluating how the planned R&D investment affects future EPS performance.
3.
Strategic evolution: Kerry Group plc completed its transition to a pure-play taste and nutrition supplier by divesting its consumer dairy business on December 31, 2024. This structural shift immediately expanded the group's EBITDA margin by 140 basis points, creating a high-margin business profile. Analysts believe that the divestment removes a significant portfolio overhang and allows the company to focus capital resources on higher-value biotechnology platforms. Consequently, understanding the R&D step-up is critical because it represents the primary engine for driving organic earnings per share growth post-divestment.
4.
Channel dynamics: The foodservice channel remains a major differentiator for Kerry Group plc, consistently outperforming the retail sector. Foodservice represents 32% of total group sales, which is significantly higher than its flavor house peers. However, the company faces growing pricing pressure from retail customers who are demanding cost-reduction solutions rather than expensive new flavor innovations. The planned R&D investment is designed to balance these channel demands by delivering highly differentiated, cost-effective formulations to customers.
5.
Contrary evidence: Some industry experts express deep skepticism regarding the commercial conversion rates of Kerry Group plc's massive innovation pipeline. A former director noted that the company's claimed EUR 15 bn renovation pipeline represents a highly exaggerated growth narrative that exceeds annual revenues. This suggests that increasing R&D intensity might not automatically translate into higher commercial win rates or proportional volume growth. Consequently, the aggressive R&D spending could depress near-term operating margins without delivering the targeted high-single-digit EPS expansion.

Regional Risks and Financial Outcomes

6.
Regional margins: The Americas region remains the highest-margin segment for Kerry Group plc, with margins exceeding 19% of sales. Top-line growth in North America is increasingly driven by a wave of product renovation, which represents over 50% of the active regional pipeline. The R&D step-up will heavily target biotechnology and precision masking to capture this US reformulation demand. Protecting this regional margin profile is vital since the Americas is the primary driver of group profitability and cash generation.
7.
European recovery: The European segment has historically faced sluggish volume growth but showed a positive recovery of 1.3% volume growth in late 2026. To sustain this momentum, Kerry Group plc is investing heavily in local biotechnology hubs, including its Leipzig facility, to provide customized enzyme solutions. However, European volume trends remain highly sensitive to consumer affordability and macroeconomic volatility. Management expects that local R&D execution will gradually mitigate these ongoing volume headwinds and support regional margin expansion.
8.
Emerging markets: The Asia-Pacific, Middle East, and Africa segment represents the highest volume growth potential for Kerry Group plc, targeting 5-9% growth by 2030. Q3 2026 volumes in the region stepped up by 5.7%, driven by dairy, meat, and snacks end-use markets. Yet, emerging markets are highly vulnerable to geopolitical instability and local currency fluctuations. Failure to localize R&D solutions in these territories could jeopardize key volume growth targets and drag down the company's global EPS trajectory.
9.
Other side of the story: While Kerry Group plc's management presents the R&D step-up as a low-risk enabler of growth, it could create significant near-term margin pressure if customer win rates do not accelerate. If increased spending of 5-6% of revenue fails to trigger sufficient volume growth, operating leverage will turn negative. Peer comparisons suggest that higher R&D intensity does not automatically guarantee commercial success in commoditized segments. Therefore, the financial step-up poses a material threat to short-term earnings unless carefully balanced against the planned Accelerate 2.0 restructuring benefits.

Section 2 – The Size and Annual Progression of Kerry Group's R&D Step-Up

Section summary
  1. Kerry Group is accelerating its research and development spend to reach 5-6% of revenue by 2030, leveraging advanced biotechnology and regional co-creation hubs to drive volume growth.

Scale and Baseline of the Step-Up

1.
Significant investment: Kerry Group reported that its research, development, and application services spending reached €314.2 million in 2025 on continuing operations. This baseline represents an increase from the €304.4 million spent in fiscal year 2024. Post-dairy divestment, this scale of funding ensures the company remains positioned to support customers as their key partner for innovation.
2.
Targeted intensity: Kerry Group aims to elevate its annual research and development investment to a target range of 5-6% of revenue by 2030. In 2025, the €314.2 million expenditure represented approximately 4.65% of its continuing revenue of €6,757.6 million. Management intends to increase this budget ahead of sales growth rates over the coming years.
3.
Funding transition: Kerry Group's historical R&D spend has hovered around 4.5% to 5.0% of revenue in recent years. Moving to the higher 5-6% range is designed to support the group's transition to a pure-play taste and nutrition business. This financial shift represents a major capital reallocation following the divestment of the dairy division in late 2024.
4.
Broker view: Deutsche Bank highlighted that increasing R&D spend is a core part of Kerry's mid-term strategic agenda. This broker pointed out that the company plans to fund this step-up while keeping capital expenditure stable at 4-5% of sales. Consequently, the group expects to achieve its long-term EBITDA margin targets without compromising its cash conversion efficiency.
5.
Biotech alignment: Jefferies observed that the planned step-up to 5-6% of sales will heavily target biotechnology as a key enabler. Analysts from this broker highlighted that approximately 40% of Kerry's taste portfolio is already based on biotechnology capabilities. This baseline allows the company to focus incremental R&D investments on advanced taste solutions.
6.
Strategic growth: Kerry Group seeks to outgrow its addressable markets by delivering complex taste and biotechnology-enabled solutions. Under its capital allocation framework, the company balances R&D investments with other returns such as a proposed €300 million share buyback. Reinvesting in technical differentiation is critical as customer contracts are mostly short-term and subject to rapid switching.

Section 3 – P&L Line Allocation of R&D Expenses

Section summary
  1. Kerry Group presents its P&L by nature rather than function, meaning R&D costs are distributed across staff costs and general overheads instead of a standalone functional line.
  2. The group continues its strict policy of expensing all research and product development costs, reporting zero capitalized development assets on its balance sheet.

Kerry P&L Classification by Nature

1.
Distinct presentation: Kerry Group does not utilize a functional income statement format, choosing instead to present its expenses by nature. This means the €314.2 million of research and development costs reported in 2025 are not isolated in a single functional operating line on the face of the P&L. Instead, these expenses are embedded primarily within staff costs and general overheads on the consolidated income statement. This lack of a standalone functional line makes it more difficult for external analysts to track specific R&D efficiency gains over time.

2.
Substantial personnel costs: The largest portion of Kerry Group's research, development, and application expenditures is driven by scientific headcount and laboratory payroll. In fiscal year 2025, the overall staff costs for the group reached €1,255.1 million. A significant portion of this payroll funds the salaries of the group's 1,300 global scientists and PhD specialists. Consequently, any future R&D step-up will register as an immediate inflation of group staff costs rather than a separate operational line.
3.
Contrary evidence: Some industry experts suggest that Kerry Group's internal systems actually track R&D allocations as part of cost of goods sold internally even though they are not disclosed. A former financial officer indicated that certain commercialization costs are analyzed internally to understand true margins. However, the public financial reports do not reflect these functional allocations, leaving investors to rely on broad disclosures. This lack of divisional transparency complicates comparisons with pure-play peers who use functional reporting.

Capitalization Policies and Intangibles

4.
Strict expense policy: Kerry Group maintains a strict accounting policy under which all research and product development expenditures are expensed immediately as incurred. The company has reported zero capitalized product development expenditure on its balance sheet for both fiscal year 2025 and 2024. While the group capitalizes software and digital assets additions of €38.0 million, it does not capitalize ingredient development. This conservative accounting practice means that R&D investments will directly depress short-term operating profits rather than being amortized.
5.
Intangible asset growth: Kerry Group's total intangible assets actually decreased to €5,444.3 million in fiscal year 2025 from €5,778.1 million in 2024. This reduction was primarily driven by brand-related amortization and currency translations rather than any capitalized development projects. Financial analysts note that the group relies on acquisitions to bring in capitalizable technologies. This inorganic approach allows Kerry to capitalize technology through business combinations rather than organic R&D capitalization.

Peer Methodology and Industry Standards

6.
Standalone peer lines: Givaudan is a key peer that presents a highly structured functional P&L on the face of its consolidated financial statements. The company reported standalone research and product development expenses of CHF 551 million in fiscal year 2025 compared to CHF 565 million in 2024. Unlike Kerry, Givaudan houses these expenses entirely in a dedicated functional operating line item below gross profit. This presentation allows investors to immediately assess the group's research intensity of 7.37% of sales.
7.
Functional peer reporting: International Flavors & Fragrances similarly presents a functional operating statement that separates cost of sales from R&D and selling expenses. The firm reported R&D expenses of $694 million in 2025, representing 6.4% of total sales, compared to $671 million in 2024. All R&D costs are expensed as incurred, matching the conservative capitalization posture of Kerry Group. This peer data confirms that expensing R&D is standard practice, but the functional line visibility remains superior to Kerry's format.

Like its competitors, DSM-Firmenich expenses all development costs that fail to meet strict capitalization criteria. Consequently, Kerry's peers exhibit a uniform trend of expensing research while maintaining much higher reporting transparency.

The company noted that its capitalization rate for R&D activities remained immaterial because criteria are met only at project completion. This methodology ensures that the majority of research costs flow directly through profit and loss. Investors can easily compare Symrise's R&D spend because it is fully segmented on functional lines across its divisions.

Thus, the current P&L structure represents a highly consistent, standard accounting choice that accurately reflects the group's actual costs without functional distortions.

Section 6 – Peer Analysis: R&D Intensity and Financial Outcomes

Section summary
  1. Global ingredient peers demonstrate a clear positive correlation between high research and development intensity and superior operating margins.
  2. Commercial benefits from stepped-up research budgets typically realize over a twelve to twenty-four month lag across the sector.

Givaudan and Symrise Performance

1.
High margins: Givaudan demonstrates that sustained research and development investment supports its premium industry-leading operating profitability.
