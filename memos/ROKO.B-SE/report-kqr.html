Performance of Serial Acquirers Following High-Leverage Acquisition Phases

Roko (ROKO.B-SE). Question: R\u00f6ko AB (ROKO.B-SE), a Swedish decentralised serial acquirer and perpetual owner of about 35 European niche businesses, listed on Nasdaq Stockholm since 11 March 2025, reporting two segments: Business to Business (about two-thirds of sales:

niche manufacturers and distributors such as Fri-Jado, ETB Technologies, Lambda, ATEMAG, Oppold, Pureoptics, Renovotec, Access Building Products) and Business to Consumer (about one-third: brands and retailers such as AJAT, Golfshopen, Golf Experten, Snowminds, Topa/Brauer, CyTech, Dan-Form). What happened to the earnings estimates, valuation multiples and share prices of listed serial acquirers in the 12 to 36 months after a year in which acquisitions financed mainly with debt lifted earnings strongly and took leverage close to the company's own ceiling, as at R\u00f6ko AB (ROKO.B-SE) in 2026 (five platform acquisitions adding SEK 1,543m of annual sales in nine months; financial net debt including option liabilities at 2.6x adjusted EBITDA against a stated ceiling of 3.0x; Q3 2026 interim report)?

Multiple Expanders vs. Decompressors: Broker Consensus

Brokers (DNB Carnegie, RedEye, Jefferies, Deutsche Bank) identify four critical criteria that determine whether an acquirer's valuation multiple expands or decompresses following a balance sheet leverage spike:

Balance Sheet Headroom & Financing Self-Sufficiency Expanders: Preserve organic free cash flow conversion (FCF/EBITA >75%–85%), enabling rapid deleveraging (0.5x–0.8x EBITDA per year) and self-funded deal-making without tapping equity markets (e.g., Lifco, Addtech, Diploma).

1.
Decompressors: Reach peak leverage (>3.0x Net Debt/EBITDA, or stretched preference share/debt structures >4.0x) near cyclical tops, triggering investor anxiety over dilutive rights issues and forced M&A freezes (e.g., AddLife in 2022–2023, Storskogen in 2022, Volati post-spin in 2026). Autonomous Organic Growth Engine Expanders: Complement acquired growth with steady positive organic volume/pricing growth (typically targeting 3%–6%), demonstrating that acquired businesses are not deteriorating post-acquisition (e.g., Lagercrantz at +12%, Diploma at +11%–15%).
2.
Decompressors: Experience negative organic sales and margins (-3% to -5%), revealing that acquisitions were merely compensating for baseline operational attrition or customer destocking. Return on Capital Employed (ROCE) vs. Goodwill Accumulation Expanders: Maintain group ROCE/ROIC >15%–20% by purchasing companies with high asset turnover and pricing power, maintaining strict entry valuation discipline (paying <=6x–8x EBITA).
3.
Decompressors: Experience ROIC erosion down to 6%–8% under the weight of accumulated goodwill, high earn-outs, and inflated purchase multiples. Decentralized Sourcing vs. Centralized Bottlenecks Expanders: Delegate deal origination and operational governance to divisional/subsidiary heads, allowing 15 to 25 deals annually without administrative strain.
4.
Decompressors: Rely on centralized management teams that become operational bottlenecks or drift into unfamiliar geographies and non-core sectors ("di-worsification").
