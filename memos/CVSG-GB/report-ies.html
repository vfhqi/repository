CVS Group (CVSG-GB) — what the FY26 results do to the existing case

These results weaken the existing case, moderately: the headline numbers were already known and were met, but two of the series the case leans on to hold Foundations at C rather than D have crossed the case's own alarm lines in a single print, and the shares fell 6.2 percent on an in-line day to within about 4 percent of the case's re-look level without the evidence that level was meant to buy.

What changed

The audited FY26 numbers (year to 30 June 2026, reported 24 September 2026) matched the July pre-release and the case: revenue £712.8m (+5.9 percent), adjusted EBITDA £141.5m (+5.1 percent) against a compiled consensus of £141.6m, margin 19.9 percent, adjusted EPS 85.6p (+6.9 percent), like-for-like sales +2.1 percent slowing from +2.7 percent in H1 to c.+1.5 percent in H2. None of that is new to the case. What is new:

  • Operating cash conversion fell to 70.6 percent from 76.9 percent, with free cash flow down to £69.2m from £72.2m, attributed by the company to working-capital movements. The internal target of over 70 percent was met by 0.6 points. The case's modal case was at or above 75 percent, its alarm below 75 percent, its invalidate below 70 percent.
  • Healthy Pet Club membership fell 2 percent to 508k from 519k; subscription revenue nonetheless rose 3.5 percent to £95.5m, which is roughly 5.7 percent more revenue per member. Jefferies attributes the decline to the transition of legacy schemes. The case's modal case was flat to slightly up from 516,000, with invalidate below 516,000 on a sustained basis.
  • The Veterinary Practices division's like-for-like was +1.0 percent, so organic volumes were flat to slightly negative. Deutsche Bank reports that July was also weather-affected and August improved, and that evidence of sustained volume recovery remains limited. Employment costs rose to 52.3 percent of revenue from 52.2 percent; the underlying EBITDA margin excluding R&D tax credits compressed to 17.6 percent from 17.8 percent (Deutsche Bank).
  • UK acquisitions have restarted: contracts exchanged post year-end on a two-site, nine-vet-FTE practice for £15.0m initial consideration, three days after the CMA published its final order on 22 September 2026 (prescription cap £21 inclusive of VAT plus £12.50 per further drug; transparency remedies only; £5.1m of exceptional implementation cost; over 80 percent compliant). Australia stood at 57 sites at June 2026 and 61 by the results, contributing c.£20.0m of pre-overhead EBITDA, with acquisitions at c.6x EBITDA and a stated ambition of £135.0m+ divisional EBITDA. Management guides c.£50.0m a year of acquisitions and c.£30.0m of capex, and restated its willingness to exceed the 2.0x leverage ceiling temporarily for attractive deals. Leverage was 1.63x at June 2026.
  • The chief executive is still in post with no successor named; a succession sub-committee was formed on 22 July 2026 and MWM Consulting engaged. Base salaries were raised from 1 July 2026: the retiring chief executive +16 percent to £570,000, the CFO +14 percent to £385,000, the Chief Veterinary Officer +19 percent to £345,000, with further phased increases of similar scale flagged as possible.
  • Final dividend 9.0p from 8.5p. Laboratories EBITDA grew 25.6 percent to £11.3m (Jefferies).
  • Sell-side: Deutsche Bank reiterated Buy, 1,700p, on c.7.6x EV/EBITDA and expects FY27 consensus broadly unchanged; Jefferies Hold, 1,400p, noting its DCF carries no M&A; Shore Capital initiated Hold, 1,400p, on 14 September. Company-cited FY27 consensus adjusted EBITDA is £149.0m to £151.7m and adjusted EPS 94.9p. Jefferies names downward FY27 revisions as the risk if volumes do not recover.
  • The shares closed at 1,221p on 24 September from 1,302p, now 2.9 percent below the 200-day average of 1,258p, with the twelve-month return at minus 0.7 percent and the relative-strength percentile at 40. The case's re-look level is about 1,170p; the price is about 4.4 percent above it, against about 10 percent when the case was written at 1,295p.

The challenge

The case reads the softness as at least partly model-specific but treats the balance sheet and cash generation as the floor that keeps the verdict neutral rather than bearish. These results admit three readings the case does not hold, and one that it already answers.

First: cash conversion is stepping down, not dipping. The case's C on Foundations, its A on dividend sustainability and its "near-certain 9-to-10-percent earnings path" all rest on 75-to-77-percent conversion. The strongest form of this reading is that FY26 is the first year in which the CMA regime became an operating cost rather than a one-off: itemised bills, written estimates over £500, written prescriptions within two working days, real-time price lists and dual branding all add working capital and administration that does not reverse, while a clinical wage base at 52.3 percent of revenue absorbs the pricing that used to fall through to cash. On that reading 70.6 percent is the new run-rate, the company's own over-70-percent target becomes the binding constraint, and the £50.0m of acquisitions, £30.0m of capex and the £38.3m of buyback still to run cannot all be funded from £69.2m of free cash flow without leverage passing the case's 2.0x invalidate level, which management has now said in terms it is prepared to do. For it to be right, H1 FY27 conversion would have to stay below 75 percent when the working-capital movement is given a chance to unwind. The review offers nothing beyond "working capital movements" as the explanation, so we cannot knock this reading down on the evidence; the case's benign version, a one-year timing effect, is equally unproven.

Second: the corporate model is losing clients, not just visits. The case's second re-look trigger needs Healthy Pet Club membership stable or rising alongside a half-year of UK companion like-for-like at or above 4 percent. Membership fell for the year and practices like-for-like was 1.0 percent. The strongest form: the mandated CVS Vets dual branding and the mandated disclosure of online prescription savings are doing exactly what Jefferies and the expert calls warned, pushing price-sensitive owners to independents and chronic medicine online, and the subscription is holding revenue only by charging the remaining members about 5.7 percent more. That would make the volume concern structural rather than transient, which is the one concern the case itself said may not be transient, and it would remove the OSHQC clearing counter-case entirely, since a concern that is not clearing cannot be cleared. For it to be right, membership would have to fall again at the November four-month update or the January half-year, and UK companion like-for-like would have to sit below the case's 1 percent alarm for a half. The benign alternative, that the decline is legacy-scheme migration and the Healthy Pet Club Advanced launch reverses it, is plausible and is Jefferies' own reading; it is not yet evidenced either.

Third, and against the grain of the first two: the price has done the case's work. The case's modal path clears the 20 percent bar at about 1,170p. At 1,221p the shares are about 4 percent from it, having fallen 6.2 percent on a print that met consensus, with Deutsche Bank expecting FY27 consensus broadly unchanged. The strongest form: this is the capitulation the case said had never happened, arriving on top of the two dated clearing events the case wanted (the CMA final order published; UK M&A restarted at £15.0m), with Australia at 61 sites and c.£20.0m of pre-overhead EBITDA, and the buyback now retiring shares at c.6 percent lower prices. If the earnings path is as probable as the case says, the return arithmetic is nearly there without any re-rating. For it to be right, the first reading must be wrong: the cash and leverage floor must hold. It is also only one of the three conditions the case set for a Major Concern Clearing pattern; estimates were not cut and guidance was not rebased, so it is a partial fit at best, and the market's own explanation for the fall is more likely the cash and membership lines than fatigue.

The reading we set aside is that Australia is bigger than the case credits. The £135.0m+ ambition, the 61 sites and Deutsche Bank's £105m-to-£130m projection are all in the print, but the case already weighs the same numbers in its counter-case and already values Australia at its 6x entry multiple; nothing in these results changed the deployment pace (c.£50.0m a year) or the entry multiple (c.6x), only the tone. That reading is not new, and the case answers it.

What would settle it

The second Earnings Review, four working days on, will carry the broker response to this print: whether FY27 consensus EBITDA (£149.0m to £151.7m) and EPS (94.9p) hold as Deutsche Bank expects or trim as Jefferies warns, and whether any target moves. That settles the sell-side momentum question and whether the market reads the 6.2 percent fall as a re-rating event or a reset. It will carry no new company data: no cash conversion, no membership figure, no like-for-like. It cannot settle either of the two readings that matter.

Those are settled by company disclosures on a known calendar. The four-month trading update in November 2026 is likely to give July-to-October like-for-like, which tests the second reading against the case's 1 percent alarm, with the weather distortion of July already flagged; it is not committed to give a membership figure or cash conversion. The first-half update in January 2027 and the interim results in February 2027 give H1 cash conversion, which is the test of whether the working-capital movement reverses (the first reading), a membership figure (the second), and leverage against the 2.0x ceiling with the buyback complete by November 2026. The chief executive's name has no date: the search firm was engaged in July and the case thought an appointment plausible within six months. Healthy Pet Club Advanced adoption and the Australian pipeline detail (practices under negotiation, entry multiples) are disclosures the company has not committed to, so the question of whether the subscription decline can be reversed by product, rather than by price, is one nothing scheduled will answer. The price against about 1,170p is continuous and needs no disclosure at all.
