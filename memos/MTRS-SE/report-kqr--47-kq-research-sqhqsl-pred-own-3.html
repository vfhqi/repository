Section 3 – Impact of Section 232 Tariffs and Price Timing

Section summary
  1. Munters Group manages temporary tariff headwinds through rapid U.S. manufacturing localization.

  3. Confidence is high about this tariff timing impact because of explicit regulatory timeline filings and detailed company transition disclosures.

Section 232 tariff timeline

1.
Restored import duties: Shifting national trade policies reinstated strict limits on foreign metal imports to protect domestic producers. The Trump administration issued an executive order on February 10, 2025, which restored Section 232 tariffs of 25% on steel imports and removed countryspecific exemptions starting March 12, 2025. These structural adjustments meant that foreign-made industrial components faced immediate import surcharges at the U.S. border. Ultimately, these protective trade barriers began to reshape the economics of imported climate equipment components by driving domestic price spikes.
2.
Elevated metal tariffs: Government actions further intensified the tax burden on imported industrial metals to encourage domestic manufacturing. President Trump signed a proclamation doubling Section 232 steel and aluminum duties to 50% effective June 4, 2025. This escalation closed previously negotiated quotas, halted product exclusions, and eliminated reciprocal tariff stacking exceptions. This aggressive policy change threatened to compress margins for manufacturers relying on foreign supply chains, unless they transitioned rapidly to domestic sourcing alternatives.
3.
Value calculation shift: Regulatory authorities revised collection practices to increase the effective tax rate on complex manufactured imports. Jefferies reported on April 15, 2026, that an April 6, 2026 amendment applied Section 232 tariffs to the full customs value of derivative metal products rather than just their raw metal content. Under the old regime, tariffs were calculated solely on the metal content value, but the new rule imposed a 25% duty on the total imported product value. This shift meant that the landed cost of imported subassemblies surged significantly for complex machinery.
4.
Tiered tariff structure: Modified trade guidelines established distinct tax brackets depending on the origin and composition of imported metal goods. First Solar reported in its SEC filing on July 30, 2026, that the updated Section 232 framework applied a 50% tariff to primary metal articles and a 25% tariff to derivative articles. This tiered approach meant that manufacturers importing complex subassemblies faced massive duty increases on full value. Consequently, unhedged companies faced immediate risk of material operating margin dilution upon shipment.

Because the contract lacked updated pricing assumptions, Munters had to absorb prolonged tariff headwinds during early delivery phases, which negatively impacted intermediate divisional profitability.

Because of this timing, management had the opportunity to incorporate tariff escalation clauses into quotes to protect its earnings.
