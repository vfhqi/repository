Kitron (KIT-NO) — what the Q3 2026 results do to the existing case

These results weaken the existing case modestly on its fundamentals, because the driver the case called its fulcrum paused while the drivers it treated as secondary carried the quarter, and they weaken it materially on case momentum, because the share price has since fallen through the average the momentum rating rested on.

What changed

The company reported on 22 October 2026 for the quarter to 30 September 2026.

  • Delivery beat. Group revenue was EUR 266.8m, up 59.0% year-on-year, against Bloomberg consensus of EUR 259.0m. Operating income was EUR 25.2m, a 9.5% margin, against consensus of EUR 24.0m. The case asked whether the margin above 9% is a floor or a cycle high; this quarter held it.
  • Defence paused. Defence and Aerospace revenue was EUR 123.8m, 46% of Q3 2026 group revenue. The case was written with defence at 52% of Q2 2026 revenue and 60% of backlog. The defence book-to-bill fell to 0.81, and defence order intake was 30% below consensus. Group intake less non-defence intake puts Q3 defence orders at about EUR 100.7m (calculated).
  • Non-defence surged. Non-defence order intake was EUR 215.1m, 30% above consensus. Electrification booked EUR 84.0m at a book-to-bill of 1.86, Connectivity 1.48, Industry 1.27. Industry revenue grew 16.8% organically; the case recorded it at minus 4% organically in Q2 2026.
  • Backlog at a record. EUR 843.3m at 30 September 2026, up 6.17% on the quarter; organic backlog EUR 778.1m, up 30.0% year-on-year.
  • Cash was weak. Operating cash flow was EUR 3.1m, on an inventory build for deliveries in Q4 2026 and into 2027. Customer deposits were EUR 110.5m at Q3 2026, against EUR 120m at the end of Q2 2026 on the same review's figures. Net debt to EBITDA stayed at 0.3x.
  • Supply did not ease. Guidance, raised in late August 2026 to EUR 1,050m to 1,150m of revenue and EUR 97m to 112m of operating income, was reiterated. Management rejected any assumption of supply easing and expects constraints to persist through 2027.
  • Sell-side response. DNB Carnegie cut adjusted EPS 2% to 3% for 2026 to 2028 (2026 to EUR 0.38 from EUR 0.39), cut its target to NOK 94.00 from NOK 103.00 on a lower defence share of the mix, and upgraded to Buy from Hold on price. Norne Securities left estimates and its NOK 135.00 target unchanged. Both read the defence pause as timing.

One conflict inside the evidence matters and we cannot resolve it. Non-defence order intake is given as up 63% on the quarter in the company's presentation and as up 14% on the quarter by DNB Carnegie. Against Q2 2026 group intake of EUR 284.1m, the first implies defence orders fell by roughly a third in the quarter, from about EUR 152m to about EUR 101m (calculated); the second implies defence orders barely moved and the non-defence recovery is far smaller than the headline. The two readings below divide on exactly this.

The challenge

The case holds one adverse reading already: that defence growth masks commercial decay. These results run against it, because the non-defence book-to-bill is about 1.5 (calculated: EUR 215.1m of orders on EUR 143.0m of revenue), far above the parity level the case named as the trigger. That reading is under less pressure. Three readings the case does not hold take its place.

1. The defence pause is the short-cycle half of the book running out, not a gap between product generations. The company and both brokers say established primes are ordering "in line with ongoing deliveries". Taken literally, that is a book-to-bill of about one from the primes: steady, not growing. All of the growth in defence then rests on the newer defence technology customers, which the case's own open question put at roughly 50% of 2026 defence sales on a sell-side estimate, and where DNB Carnegie says visibility beyond announced orders is limited. Defence revenue grew 126.0% organically while orders ran at 0.81 of revenue, so the defence backlog shrank in the quarter. For this reading to be right, the next-generation designs would have to be several quarters from serial production, and the primes would have to stay at replacement-rate ordering. It is plausible, not probable: the funding backdrop the case relies on is intact, and one quarter does not make a trend. But the case treats defence transmission as "proven, not hoped for", and this reading says the proof covered a restocking burst from customers whose reorder timing nobody outside them can see.

2. The non-defence surge is customers queuing for scarce capacity, not end demand recovering. The company says customers must commit firm timelines to secure production slots, and that board and substrate capacity is being reallocated toward artificial intelligence infrastructure through 2027. DNB Carnegie said before the results that customer purchases were recovering faster than underlying end sales, while the review also records soft European industrial investment and high financing costs. Orders rising well ahead of end demand, during an allocation squeeze, is what precautionary ordering looks like, whatever the backlog definition says. For this reading to be right, non-defence book-to-bill would fall back toward one within two or three quarters and non-defence revenue would not accelerate, because supply caps conversion either way. If it is right, the record backlog measures lengthening lead times, and the case's supply gate becomes the whole story rather than one element of it.

3. The favourable reading: a second trough-turn is starting underneath the defence story. The case judged the cyclical turn as already past and flagged the entry point as close to a cyclical peak trap. If destocking has ended in Electrification, Connectivity and Industry, the non-defence half of the business is near a trough rather than a peak, and group earnings are less peak-like than the case assumed. For this to be right, supply has to permit conversion, which is the same hinge as the second reading.

One obvious alternative fails on the evidence: that a lower defence share compresses the margin. Defence fell from 52% of Q2 2026 revenue, on the case's record, to 46% of Q3 2026 revenue, and the operating margin held at 9.5%. The mix effect that does bite is on valuation. The case took a split-multiple frame, 28.9x on defence earnings and 16.0x on non-defence, as its starting point for pricing. Under that frame, success outside defence lowers the blended multiple, which is why a broker can upgrade the shares and cut the target in the same note. The case never weighed the possibility that the business broadens and is worth less per unit of earnings for doing so.

What would settle it

  • The second review, four working days on. It is likely to carry the settled consensus after the print and any further broker changes, and it should resolve whether non-defence orders rose 63% or 14% on the quarter. The dashboard already shows consensus EPS down 1% and the consensus target up 1% over one month, so a large reset looks unlikely. It carries no new company disclosure, so it cannot settle either of the first two readings.
  • The Q4 2026 results on 11 February 2027. The first real test. Defence book-to-bill back above one argues for timing; the review's own benchmark for structural delay is a defence book-to-bill below 0.9x for three consecutive quarters, which could not be confirmed before the Q1 2027 results. Non-defence book-to-bill and non-defence revenue together test the second and third readings.
  • Q4 2026 revenue against guidance. Adding Q3 to the case's record of the first half gives EUR 835.2m for nine months (calculated), so the guided range implies Q4 revenue of roughly EUR 215m to 315m. A result near the floor would say supply is capping conversion.
  • The cash conversion cycle and customer deposits at year end. These settle the working-capital question, if the company reports the cycle as it did at Q2 2026.
  • What nothing scheduled answers. The split of defence backlog and orders between established primes and newer defence technology customers. The review records calls for a timeline to serial production but no commitment from the company to give one or to disclose the split. Until it does, the first reading can only be tested indirectly, one quarter's book-to-bill at a time.
