Customer Concentration and Contract Durability

1.
Extreme concentration: Volex's customer concentration has spiked dramatically because its largest customer, identified as Amazon, accounted for 18.7% of total revenue in FY2026 compared with only 8.0% in FY2025. Jefferies confirmed on 19 October 2026 that this single account resides within Volex's Complex Industrial Technology division. This intense concentration means any allocation shift by Amazon will severely impact group earnings per share over the next 18 months.
2.
Limited visibility: Volex typically operates without long-term structural revenue protection because it does not sign long-term five-year commitments with its major customers. An expert from TT Electronics stated on 5 March 2025 that Volex wins business first and then resolves operational details over the following 18 months. This short contract duration increases the risk of sudden volume losses if hyperscalers modify their procurement strategies.
3.
Elevated windfall: Volex's historic data-centre performance was bolstered by a significant temporary capacity windfall in the second half of FY2026. Jefferies estimated on 19 October 2026 that this one-off capacity contribution amounted to between $20 million and $30 million. This windfall occurred because Volex secured key components early and fulfilled near-term capacity needs when other suppliers were constrained.
4.
Strategic vulnerability: Hyperscale customers utilise strict multi-sourcing procurement models to ensure supply chain resilience and avoid vendor lock-in. An expert from Google noted on 24 October 2026 that hyperscalers typically restrict any single supplier to a maximum of 60% to 70% of total spend. Volex's high initial share with Amazon represents a prime target for multi-sourcing diversification that will dilute future earnings.

However, subsequent financial results show that data-centre revenues doubled in FY2026 to account for 63.0% of CIT segment revenue, rendering management's earlier concentration assurances obsolete. This rapid growth elevates the customer concentration risk beyond historical baselines.

Non-Data-Centre Segment Trajectories

1.
Positive momentum: Volex's Off-Highway division delivered exceptional organic revenue growth of 20% in FY2026, reaching $279.3 million. This strong performance was driven by new programme wins in Europe and North America. Although H1 FY2027 faced a tough comparator from a one-off military vehicle project, the segment continued to grow well due to material handling and agricultural demand. Replicating this high-quality European manufacturing offering in the North American market provides Volex with an attractive medium-term growth runway.
2.
Encouraging recovery: Volex's Consumer Electricals segment returned to organic growth in H1 FY2027 after experiencing an organic decline of 7.8% in FY2026. The prior-year decline occurred because European domestic appliance customers experienced intensified low-cost Chinese competition amid tariff shifts. Growth returned as Volex ramped up new domestic appliance harness programmes with major global OEMs. Expanding these complex internal harnesses represents a higher-value opportunity that should support sustainable organic expansion.
3.
Robust progression: Volex's EV & Electrification segment achieved organic constant currency growth of 4.8% during FY2026, totaling $181.8 million. This expansion accelerated very strongly into H1 FY2027, with revenues landing well ahead of prior-year levels. Volex secured a greater share of customer requirements from Tesla by leveraging its substantial investments in vertical integration. This deep relationship with its key EV customer is facilitating entry into adjacent high-growth markets like energy storage and solar.
4.
Gradual stabilization: Volex's Medical segment experienced a 10.5% organic revenue decline in FY2026 due to inventory destocking. This headwind began to moderate in H1 FY2027, delivering modest organic growth from surgical and diagnostic applications. Volex also completed the acquisition of Kepler SignalTek in July 2026, which contributed $11 million in revenue. This low-risk transaction enhances margins and accelerates Volex's move up the healthcare technology value chain.

Macro Sensitivities and Inflation Protection

5.

This hedging mechanism is particularly active in protecting margins within the Consumer Electricals segment. While copper price volatility creates short-term gross margin percentage fluctuations, the absolute gross profit remains protected. These contractual pass-through clauses ensure that raw material reversals do not structurally impair group profitability over the next 18 months.
6.
Contrary evidence: Volex's profitability remains exposed to regulatory changes, as a December 2025 Turkish tax law modification cancelled crucial inflation adjustments. This regulatory pivot exposes Volex's Turkish operations to adverse tax effects from lira devaluation against the Euro. Volex reported that this tax regime change will remain a persistent headwind for the group's effective tax rate in FY2027 and beyond. This tax drag offset Volex's operational Turkish lira benefits.
7.
Stable operations: Volex's manufacturing footprint in Turkey navigated severe local labor inflation without destroying operating margins. Ongoing wage increases were broadly offset by the depreciation of the Turkish lira, maintaining a stable labor cost base in Euro terms. To protect margins further, Volex accelerated productivity programs and closed two smaller Turkish sites during FY2026. Volex also retains contract rights to pass through inflationary costs to domestic Turkish customers.
8.
Persistent headwinds: Volex must actively manage currency volatility across its global manufacturing footprint to protect its underlying operating profit. While the majority of revenues are denominated in US dollars, other critical operational costs like rents and utilities are paid in local currencies. A 10% depreciation of the US dollar against the British pound sterling is estimated to create a $5 million impact on group profit before tax. These currency fluctuations represent a major source of earnings volatility.
9.
Flexible manufacturing: Volex's diversified manufacturing footprint helps the group mitigate severe trade disruptions and US import tariff changes. Tariff uncertainty caused short-term medical customer destocking, but Volex's 23-site global presence allowed customer product relocations. The group's extensive low-cost base in Mexico and Batam serves as a critical competitive advantage over Chinese competitors. Volex's physical agility helps shield non-data-centre segments from trade shocks.
