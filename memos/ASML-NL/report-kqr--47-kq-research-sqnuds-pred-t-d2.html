Section 5 – Geopolitical Risks, MATCH Act Exposure and China Substitution

Section summary
  1. Geopolitical escalation and the proposed US MATCH Act pose a severe threat to ASML's extensive installed base servicing revenue in China.
  2. Chinese domestic lithography alternatives are accelerating under Huawei's coordination but remain years behind ASML in advanced yield and throughput metrics.

MATCH Act and China Revenue Impact

1.
Geopolitical revenue exposure: Geopolitical developments represent the most significant near-term threat to ASML's long-term revenue targets. While the company's Chinese sales normalized to approximately 20% in 2026, previous years saw abnormal peaks of 41% in 2024 due to intense front-loading of deep ultraviolet tools. Although management anticipates stable mainland demand, further unilateral trade restrictions threaten this baseline. A total restriction would severely impair ASML's mid-term growth unless offset by rapid capacity expansion in other regions.
2.
Threatened servicing revenue: The proposed United States MATCH Act represents a direct threat to ASML's Installed Base Management business in China. Under the bipartisan bill drafted in April 2026, export restrictions would extend to the entire immersion deep ultraviolet range and critically prohibit servicing of already installed systems. Experts warn that Chinese fabs cannot operate their existing scanners without ongoing manufacturer servicing. This policy shift would jeopardize a highly lucrative revenue stream that otherwise provides strong cyclical cushion.
3.
Targeted facility constraints: Draft legislation designates critical Chinese chipmakers as covered facilities to systematically isolate their manufacturing capabilities. The proposed MATCH Act specifically targets leading Chinese entities including SMIC, Hua Hong, CXMT, YMTC, and Huawei. Analysts at Jefferies calculated on 28 July 2026 that the passing of this act could impose a fifteen percent revenue headwind on ASML. Forcing these restrictions on allied nations would severely depress ASML's valuation multiple even if the final legislation undergoes diplomatic revisions.

Consequences of DUV Servicing Ban

4.
Customer operational crisis: A statutory ban on servicing would trigger an immediate operational crisis for mainland Chinese semiconductor fabrication plants. Without ASML's technical support, SMIC's existing immersion tools would suffer rapid utilization declines and see production yields crater significantly. Meanwhile, structurally fragile players like Hua Hong would face immediate net losses. This severe disruption would effectively freeze China's domestic logic expansion unless they can independently duplicate advanced repair protocols.
5.
Displaced capacity redirection: The broader financial impact of a Chinese immersion ban depends heavily on whether global capacity can absorb displaced tool shipments. ASML Chief Financial Officer Roger Dassen stated on 22 April 2026 that global undersupply would prompt other regions to expand capacity if China is excluded. However, non-Chinese customers are facing cleanroom construction bottlenecks that prevent immediate pullforwards. This means ASML would face sluggish near-term system sales before global fab expansions materialize.

Contrary evidence: Chinese lithography import data reveals persistent near-term demand resilience despite escalating regulatory overhangs. This recovery suggests Chinese fabs are aggressively stockpiling spare parts and systems. Consequently, the immediate financial hit to ASML has been deferred by persistent transactional momentum.

Streamlined asset restructuring: Restructuring efforts at established domestic champion SMEE highlight China's strategic reorganization of its lithography supply chain. Domestic performance constraints: Despite notable engineering breakthroughs, Chinese domestic scanners face severe performance gaps that limit high-volume manufacturing viability. Thus, domestic progress might secure ASML's Chinese terminal value rather than completely eliminating demand.
