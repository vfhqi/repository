Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. The investment thesis for 1&1 AG hinges on evaluating a 16% compound probability of standalone success against a highly accretive 60% consensus probability of strategic consolidation or wholesale pivot, with the core question centered on the sourced basis for the 80%/50%/40% probability inputs (roaming, spectrum, rollout) used to derive this thesis-success rate.

The landscape reveals profound asymmetric risk-reward dynamics where Deutsche Bank Research models a 40% probability of continued standalone rollout valued at EUR 13.5 per share against a 60% probability of strategic pivot yielding EUR 34.5 to EUR 46.7 per share, positioning the investment case as fundamentally a question of strategic optionality rather than operational execution alone. The national roaming transition from Telefónica to Vodafone exposes 1&1 to fixed-capacity cost volatility and elevated customer churn, though completing core migration establishes a foundational base for standalone operational independence.

The Vodafone agreement calculates wholesale fees based on the exact percentage share of total network data traffic consumed by 1&1 subscribers, and because total data traffic growth across Vodafone's macro network slowed significantly relative to historical baseline trends, 1&1's relative traffic share rose faster than modeled, triggering unplanned wholesale cost spikes.

This mechanism directly contributed to management cutting fiscal 2025 EBITDA guidance by 5% from EUR 571 million to EUR 545 million in June 2025, underscoring the structural vulnerability embedded in capacity-based roaming economics.

However, full migration of 1&1's 12.48 million mobile customer base off Telefónica's core network was successfully completed in November 2025, meeting the Federal Network Agency deadline for competitive independence, demonstrating operational capability despite commercial headwinds. Securing low-band sub-1 GHz spectrum remains 1&1's most critical operational bottleneck, as regulatory mandates for shared access face total commercial resistance from incumbent operators.

The regulatory extension decision explicitly obligated incumbent operators Deutsche Telekom, Vodafone, and Telefónica to offer 1&1 shared access to sub-1 GHz spectrum, with BNetzA specifying that low-band sharing must grant 1&1 access to a minimum of 2x5 MHz paired spectrum in sub-1 GHz bands.

Yet direct negotiations between 1&1 and incumbent MNOs yielded no market-aligned commercial agreement throughout late 2025, with 1&1 confirming that incumbent operators failed to present acceptable low-band leasing offers, forcing reliance on pending regulatory intervention.

The spectrum deadlock creates a compounding operational disadvantage as lacking low-band indoor penetration capabilities forces 1&1 to purchase expensive national roaming capacity from Vodafone, with absence of sub-1 GHz spectrum directly inflating operating expenses by driving higher data offload volumes onto third-party host networks.

Critically, failing to obtain dedicated low-band spectrum greatly increases the likelihood of a value-accretive strategic pivot toward M&A, transforming spectrum regulatory failure into a potential consolidation catalyst. Open RAN rollout execution faces structural vendor friction and tower delays, but exceeding 34% household coverage and integrating proprietary fiber backhaul validates long-term operational viability.

Operational deployment reached 27% German household coverage by December 2025 and surpassed 34% coverage in mid- 2026, beating the Federal Network Agency mandate of 25% ahead of the 50% target for 2030, demonstrating that the network build trajectory remains ahead of regulatory minimums despite documented execution challenges.

Acquiring 1&1 Versatel provided over 68,500 kilometers of proprietary fiber-optic infrastructure directly connecting gigabit antennas to regional edge nodes, eliminating third-party backhaul lease costs, which represents a structural competitive advantage unavailable to peer greenfield entrants.

However, deployment pace suffered severe friction as primary infrastructure partner Vantage Towers defaulted on delivering its contractually promised 3,800 antenna sites, with the German Federal Cartel Office issuing a Statement of Objections in April 2025 provisionally finding that Vodafone and Vantage Towers engaged in abusive obstruction of 1&1 network entry.

The valuation framework embedded in this analysis operates within strict limitations requiring acknowledgment.

Material reporting omissions around spectrum rules, site-level ARPU flow-through, and migration churn prevent full quantitative resolution of the 16% standalone thesis success rate, constraining the precision with which probability-weighted outcomes can be modeled.

Future analytical refinement depends on monitoring regulatory developments from BNetzA regarding forced spectrum access enforcement, tracking quarterly Access segment contract net additions to validate churn stabilization post-migration, and conducting field verification of site-level profitability economics across the expanding Open RAN footprint.

The investment thesis ultimately pivots on whether regulatory intervention successfully compels low-band spectrum access by late 2026, whether parent company United Internet exercises its growing ownership position to force strategic optionality, and whether incumbent operators maintain their commercial resistance to voluntary spectrum sharing or pivot toward rational consolidation negotiations under pressure from deteriorating standalone economics for all market participants.
