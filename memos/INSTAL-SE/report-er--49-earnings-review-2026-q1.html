Section 1 – Brief company description and history

Section summary
  1. Instalco entered its Q1 2026 earnings with depressed valuation multiples and a severe share price underperformance relative to European installation peers.
1.
Niche technical services model: Instalco operates as a leading technical installation group across Sweden, Norway, and Finland, offering heating, plumbing, electricity, ventilation, industrial solutions, and technical consulting services.
2.
Mid-sized project concentration: The group specifically targets mid-sized projects with values ranging from SEK 1 million to SEK 75 million, which helps reduce exposure to complex macro risks associated with larger construction contracts.
3.
Aggressive historical acquisition trajectory: Since its establishment in 2014 by chairperson Per Sjöstrand, the group has successfully acquired more than 135 subsidiaries to compound its net sales.
4.
Multiple arbitrage strategy: DNB Carnegie's Söderblom calculated that Instalco historically acquired companies at low valuation multiples of 3x to 6x EV/EBITA, enabling profitable multiple arbitrage compounding when rolled into the listed parent entity.
5.
Decentralized governance structure: Each acquired subsidiary retains its original brand and local leadership, with local managers maintaining operational autonomy while accessing group-wide joint purchasing agreements and shared administrative support.
6.
Return of the founder: Following a period of severe margin pressure, the board appointed founder Per Sjöstrand back as CEO in July 2025, tasked with restoring group EBITA margins to the long-term target of 8%.
7.
Drag from Swedish operations: Pre-earnings, the market focused heavily on margin dilution within Sweden, where competitive pricing pressure had dragged the group's trailing 12-month EBITA margin down to only 6.4 percent by early 2026.
8.
Massive valuation discount: Prior to the earnings release, DNB Carnegie's Sundén highlighted that Instalco was trading at a 30 to 35 percent discount to European installation peers like SPIE and Burkhalter.
9.
Severe stock price underperformance: Since January 2025, Instalco's share price plummeted by 20% due to Swedish margin erosion and leverage concerns, whereas peer Bravida's stock price gained between 10% and 15%.
10.

11.
Leading peer relative multiples: According to valuation comparisons compiled by Bernstein's Peterc, listed European peer pure-plays traded at an average of 10x EV/EBITDA in 2024, placing Instalco's depressed pre-earnings valuation in sharp relief.
12.
Pre-earnings broker targets: Going into the Q1 results, DNB Carnegie's Lönnqvist Sundén reiterated a BUY rating with a target price of SEK 42.5, expecting a flat seasonal EBITA margin Y/Y.

Section 3 – Key moving parts and changes

Section summary
  1. The launch of Instalco 2.0 and the return of founder Per Sjöstrand as permanent CEO represent the critical pivots to restore group profitability over the next three years.
1.
Decisive CEO transition: In March 2026, the board resolved to appoint founder Per Sjöstrand as the permanent President and CEO, cementing his return to operational leadership. Sjöstrand had previously assumed the role of acting CEO on August 1, 2025, following the departure of former CEO Robin Boheman. The board believes his deep operational understanding of the business model will create stability across the organisation during this margin restoration phase.
2.
Stable investor relations team: The senior leadership layer has stabilized with Christina Kassberg continuing as CFO, Mathilda Eriksson as Head of IR, and Fredrik Trahn as Head of Communications and Sustainability. This stability is viewed as critical to managing market expectations and executing on the ongoing improvement initiatives across the group subsidiaries.
3.
Phased German expansion: Instalco is expanding its European footprint through a multi-step acquisition of German platform Fabri AG, agreeing in July 2026 to increase its stake from 24% to 51%. The transaction represents the second step of a structured option plan that will result in the full consolidation of Fabri's numbers starting in the third quarter of 2026.
4.
Selective domestic acquisitions: Domestic M&A remains a core growth driver, highlighted by the April 2026 agreement to acquire 70% of Sweden's Defensio Invest AB, which operates under the TSM Taksäkerhetsmontörerna brand. This transaction follows the strategic addition of Alf Näslunds Eltjänst in late 2025 to achieve multidisciplinary technical capacity in its local Swedish region.
5.
Restructuring for margin recovery: Under Sjöstrand's direction, the company launched the "Instalco 2.0" initiative to combat domestic margin erosion and drive EBITA margins back toward the long-term 8.0% target. The program introduces country-specific operating models and clearer expectations across subsidiaries to restore historical profitability levels.
6.
Segment reporting restructuring: To support clearer accountability, the group transitioned to distinct geographic segment reporting for Sweden, Norway, and Finland starting January 1, 2026. This structural shift replaces the historical "Sweden" and "Rest of Nordics" segments and was accompanied by restated quarterly historical data to facilitate year-on-year analysis for investors.
7.
Conservative dividend proposal: In line with its capital discipline focus, the board proposed a lower dividend of SEK 0.50 per share for the 2025 fiscal year, compared to SEK 0.68 paid out in the previous year. This payout represents approximately 39% of net profit, which is above the company's formal 30% dividend payout target.
8.
Elevated leverage ratio: Net debt to EBITDA peaked at 3.3x in Q3 2025 before declining to 2.8x at the end of 2025, which remains above the long-term target of 2.5x. Management is relying on cash conversion improvements and working capital discipline to bring the leverage back within targets.
9.
No share buybacks: Although the board maintains a mandate to buy back up to 5% of its outstanding treasury shares, the company executed zero treasury share buybacks during 2025. This reflects a strategic pivot toward preserving capital and reducing debt load rather than returning cash through share repurchases.
10.
Stable credit facility: To ensure financial flexibility, the group extended its credit facility agreement for a total of SEK 3.4 billion with its banking consortium in late 2025. The facility provides a robust financial foundation to support ongoing selective M&A and operating commitments over the next two years.
11.
Shifting demand drivers: Demand has shifted away from the weak private residential sector toward highly resilient public and industrial end-markets. Electrification, critical infrastructure, defense, and healthcare projects are providing structural support to the order backlog, offsetting the cyclical residential downturn.
12.
Rising sustainability targets: Instalco has stepped up its climate ambitions, targeting net-zero value chain emissions by 2045 and a 50% reduction in Scope 1 and Scope 2 greenhouse gas intensity by 2030. These formal targets were established in response to rising demands for ESG compliance from customers, lenders, and institutional investors.

This intense competitive environment has historically depressed contract pricing and directly diluted domestic margins for major players like Instalco.

High interest rates have historically pressured residential newbuilds, although renovation and technical service projects tend to be more resilient over the business cycle.
