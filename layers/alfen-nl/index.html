Layer 1 · External change forces

Written last by the Investment Analyst for its group. The rating above stands for it, so it carries no number of its own.

Alfen sits in a structurally growing end-market whose growth it cannot fully convert. The grid tailwind is the best-evidenced external force in the file (record Dutch distribution budgets, a dated legal path to faster permitting, a 70% share, substation volumes +27% in Q2 2026), but it is rationed by the operators' technician shortage and lands in the segment with the lowest gross margin per euro (22-25%). For the model: external forces justify grid growth of 8-12% a year, flat-to-modest charging, volatile storage, and no external support for gross-margin expansion; the six-month external read is negative (H2 2026 revenue down year on year), the 12-36 month read modestly positive, dated to mid-2027 Dutch permitting.

To what extent, and in which direction, is competitive intensity changing?

Layer 1 · External change forces

The Investment Analyst's heading Competitive intensity: to what extent and in which direction is it changing?

Judgement: competitive intensity is rising in the two segments that still produce about 63% of gross profit and is stable and benign only in Dutch grid. This is the main reason why the margin recovery the case needs is fragile.

  • Charging: intensifying, with a price-down-for-volume dynamic already under way. Over 200-300 European hardware suppliers; Chinese and European entry-level rivals have pushed entry-level wallbox prices from about €1,000 to under €500; Alfen's implied ASP fell 8% year on year in Q1 2026 (€0.9k, -7% sequentially) and 4% in Q2 (€968, flat sequentially); Alfen itself ran "defensive" price campaigns, which took EVC gross margin from 44.1% to 39.5% in H1 2026. The ASP stabilising sequentially in Q2 is the only sign the pressure may be plateauing, and it came on a low base. Consolidation (Wallbox bought ABL) and software-led players are winning.
  • Storage: intensifying sharply. Cell makers (BYD, CATL, Samsung) sell integrated turnkey systems and utilities buy packs directly; tenders draw up to 50 bidders; integrator gross margins are now 10-20% (expert view) against Alfen's guided 15-25% range and 22.8% in H1 2026 (27.4% a year earlier); the frozen US market diverts cheap supply into Europe; CATL's 3Q26 miss and a "new battery price war" (Bernstein, 23 Oct 2026; Jefferies downgrade of CATL, 26 Oct 2026) point to more price pressure into 2027.
  • Grid: stable and benign. Deutsche Bank describes benign competitor attitudes and pricing discipline; Q2 2026 substation prices were stable; local rivals exist (Liander sourced elsewhere during the 2024 Pacto stop), so the 70% share is defended but not unassailable.
  • Direction over the next six months against the last 12-18 months: charging from severe to severe-but-plateauing; storage from intense to more intense (new cell price war, more bidders); grid unchanged.
  • Modelling implication: do not model EVC gross margin above 39-40% or ESS gross margin above 22% in FY27; the margin risk is to the downside in both.
