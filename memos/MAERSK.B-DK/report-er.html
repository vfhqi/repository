Section 1 – Brief company description and history

Section summary
  1. Maersk's strategic transformation into an integrated container logistics leader faces severe mid-term earnings headwinds as massive industry-wide vessel deliveries threaten to overwhelm near-term disruption benefits.
1.
Integrated transport leader: Maersk operates as an integrated container logistics company connecting and simplifying global supply chains across its three core divisions of Ocean, Logistics and Services, and Terminals. This structural pivot represents a strategic transition from a diversified energy and shipping conglomerate into a focused global leader in container logistics.
2.
Dominant ocean footprint: The Ocean segment remains Maersk's primary revenue driver, operating a vast fleet of approximately 700 vessels to control a 14% global market share in container shipping. This segment focuses on key East-West and North-South trade lanes, transporting 12.9m FFE in 2025 across more than 500 ports.
3.
Refined logistics segment: To improve operational transparency for investors, Maersk refined its Logistics and Services segment into three distinct units of Forwarding, Solutions, and Landside. This division has expanded rapidly via more than USD 8bn in targeted acquisitions since 2019.
4.
Resilient terminals business: APM Terminals represents Maersk's most structurally attractive business, operating 53 facilities across 29 countries and generating high, resilient returns. The division plays a critical dual role in supporting Ocean's transshipment hubs and serving external carriers.

Eroding post-pandemic base: Reflecting a sharp normalization from pandemic-era peaks, Maersk's group revenue declined from USD 81.5bn in 2022 to USD 51.1bn in 2023, before stabilizing at USD 54.0bn in fiscal 2025. This drop was led by steep declines in freight rates in the core Ocean segment.
6.
Compressing operating profits: Group underlying EBIT fell significantly from USD 30.9bn in 2022 to USD 3.9bn in 2023, illustrating the high operational leverage of container shipping. Although EBIT recovered to USD 6.5bn in 2024, it compressed again to USD 3.5bn in fiscal 2025.

Parallel peer decline: Comparable container liner Hapag-Lloyd experienced a similar post-pandemic contraction, with its group EBITDA falling from EUR 4.6bn in 2024 to EUR 3.2bn in fiscal 2025. This underperformance reflected the same volatile developments in demand and average freight rates.

This highlight underscores how asset-light peers shield returns from shipping cyclicality.

Section 7 – Changes in leadership

Section summary
  1. Recent high-level executive turnover and sweeping corporate layoffs have intensified organizational instability at Maersk.

Smooth CFO transition: Swiss national Robert Erni officially took over as Chief Financial Officer and Executive Board Member in February 2026, succeeding Patrick Jany who oversaw the 2025 annual report before departing. Erni brings more than 30 years of global logistics experience, including senior roles at Kuehne+Nagel, Panalpina, and Dachser, which directly aligns with Maersk's ongoing integrator strategy. This transition is viewed by investors as a critical step to strengthen financial discipline and cost control during a projected cyclical normalization.
2.
Strategic analyst focus: Ahead of the leadership change, Morgan Stanley's Ekblom highlighted that the outside appointment of Robert Erni would refocus the market on key strategic and financial debates surrounding Maersk. Ekblom noted that Erni's extensive contract logistics background would be highly tested as the company tries to improve its underperforming landside margins. This external hire signals that management prioritizes deep logistics operating expertise over traditional maritime shipping backgrounds.
3.
Executive leadership shift: Long-serving Chief People Officer Susana Elvira departed Maersk in mid-October 2026 after a notable 24-year career to pursue outside opportunities. To fill the vacancy, Maersk appointed Roberta Duarte as Chief People Officer, effective November 1, 2026, who joins the Executive Leadership Team after a brief tenure at Werfen. Duarte previously spent a decade at Maersk in senior HR roles, making her well-positioned to manage global workforce culture amidst structural changes.
4.
Logistics leadership restructuring: Bruno Plantaz was appointed as the new Global Head of Solutions and member of the Executive Leadership Team, effective November 1, 2026, bringing over two decades of contract logistics experience from Kuehne+Nagel and CEVA Logistics. Plantaz succeeds Narin Phol, who officially left the company at the end of September 2026 after spearheading the early expansion of the Logistics and Services business. Dunwoodie took over prior to the March 2026 Annual General Meeting, coordinating directly with senior leadership to manage investor expectations regarding highly volatile ocean freight rate cycles.
