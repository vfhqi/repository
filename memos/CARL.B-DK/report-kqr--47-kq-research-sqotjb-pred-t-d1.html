Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Carlsberg's transition to a multi-beverage player is expected to drive robust earnings growth and re- rate its discounted valuation over the next three years.

Defining the Key Question

1.
Investigating strategic delivery: "how plausible is the change now expected for this company, given its record of delivery and the strengths the business demonstrably has, rather than strengths it would need to acquire?"

According to Bernstein's note on 30 October 2026, the company's adjusted earnings per share are forecast to rise from DKK 59.39 in 2025 to DKK 65.29 in 2026 and reach DKK 75.79 by 2027. This growth indicates a strong double-digit compound annual expansion rate that should support the brewer's overall valuation unless consumer demand in key regions weakens further.

Deutsche Bank Research noted on 29 October 2026 that the shares trade on a calendar year 2026 estimated price-to-earnings multiple of 14.6x, representing a 17% discount to European beverage peers. Achieving the medium-term targets could trigger a significant market rerating because the current valuation does not fully reflect the resilience of the diversified portfolio.

Developing these multi-beverage capabilities helps insulate the group from structural declines in standard beer categories by capturing faster soft drinks growth.

PepsiCo partnership upside: Expanding the soft drinks bottling partnership provides a low-risk avenue for accelerating earnings growth across central and eastern regions. DNB Carnegie noted on 15 October 2026 that Carlsberg's soft drinks business has been strengthened by new bottling deals in Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan.   2. Competitors like Heineken, Diageo, and Molson Coors increasingly rely on structured cost- saving initiatives, but low drop-through rates and high reinvestment needs present persistent margin risks.

Transition of Profit Drivers at Carlsberg

1.
Transitioning from cost control: Carlsberg successfully transitioned from its intense cost-cutting era into a growth-led framework by reinvesting efficiency gains. According to Carlsberg's annual report published on 4 February 2020, the three-year Funding the Journey programme concluded in 2018 after delivering DKK 3 billion in organic profit benefits. These massive savings allowed the brewer to reinvest over DKK 1 billion directly into its SAIL'22 commercial growth priorities. This transition implies that future earnings per share became highly dependent on top-line execution rather than structural overhead reductions.
2.
Sustaining top-line momentum: Commercial priorities successfully replaced finished cost programmes as the primary drivers of profit growth during the transition period. In the fourth-quarter earnings call on 4 February 2020, Carlsberg's management reported that 2019 organic operating profit grew by 10.5% on the back of 3.2% organic revenue growth. This top-line expansion was driven by a favorable 3% price/mix and strong volume growth across Asia. Consequently, Carlsberg proved that reinvesting cost savings into premium brands can offset the loss of direct cost-out tailwinds.
3.
Leveraging regional engines: Regional volume growth, particularly in emerging Asian markets, acted as the vital engine that replaced maturemarket cost savings. DNB Carnegie's research note from 20 January 2020 highlighted that China's significant premiumisation supported double-digit operating profit growth for the Asian segment. This persistent efficiency mindset supported continuous operating cost management and margin progression. This ongoing discipline suggests that profit growth can be sustained at mid-single-digit rates if a cost-conscious culture is permanently preserved.

Section 3 – Feasibility of Organic Revenue Growth Targets

Section summary
  1. Carlsberg's organic revenue growth target remains highly ambitious as current growth confronts structural volume slowdowns in core markets.
  2. Historic peer data from Heineken and AB InBev indicates that top-line acceleration is heavily dependent on consistent price/mix gains and premiumisation.
  3. Extreme weather and distributor destocking in China present severe volume headwinds that require strong performance in other Asian and European markets to offset.

Evaluating Carlsberg's Growth Algorithm

1.
Ambitious sales targets: Carlsberg has targeted an ambitious 4-6% organic revenue growth CAGR since February 2024 under its Accelerate SAIL strategy. According to Jefferies Research on 13 September 2026, the company's top-line growth came in at only 2.4% in 2024 and was negative 0.6% in 2025. This performance means the mid-term target remains highly demanding to achieve because the H1 2026 organic revenue growth of 2.7% and Q3 2026 growth of 3.0% both continue to trail this guidance.
2.
Deconstructing the algorithm: Management's organic revenue growth target requires a precise operational mix of volume and price. According to Jefferies Research on 13 September 2026, the 4-6% target is roughly 1/3 volume and 2/3 price/mix. This breakdown implies a 1.3-2.0% volume component and a 2.7-4.0% price/mix component. Meeting this target will be difficult unless the company can accelerate price and mix significantly because the average price/mix in recent years has run at only 1-2%.
3.
Non-beer growth engines: Diversifying into soft drinks provides Carlsberg with a faster-growing structural engine that beer alone cannot match. DNB Carnegie reported on 15 October 2026 that soft drinks now represent 33% of Carlsberg's group volumes. This soft drinks exposure is structurally growing, which helps to offset mature market beer declines as Western European consumers moderate their alcohol intake. This structural shift means that the group's long-term top line should become more resilient, provided that the newly acquired Britvic assets are integrated successfully.
4.
Organic growth distortion: One-off bottling volume consolidations create a temporary boost that masks Carlsberg's underlying top-line run-rate. Jefferies Research reported on 13 September 2026 that taking on the Kazakhstan Pepsi franchise adds 1.8m hl or roughly 1% to organic group volumes. This integration distorts the organic performance, meaning that group organic sales growth would actually be below 2% in fiscal 2026 when excluding this contribution. Consequently, the market remains skeptical of the run-rate unless the group can sustain organic volume growth after these initial M&A tailwinds are fully lapped.

According to a Bernstein Research note dated 9 September 2026, the company's stated pricing policy is to price in line with local consumer price inflation and cover over 80% of input inflation through active revenue management. This proactive policy supports earnings per share by protecting gross margins, unless extreme volume elasticity triggers a severe consumer backlash.

Utilizing these analytical insights allows the group to execute precise pricing decisions that protect profitability without sacrificing core market share.
3.
Extensive source research: Evaluating the effectiveness of these pricing policies requires analyzing diverse industry viewpoints and historic precedents. Across our research of 14 unique documents, covering 5 peer companies and 3 distinct historical cases, we assess how pricing actions trade off against volume progression. This baseline suggests that consistent revenue management execution remains the primary determinant of whether a brewer can protect its operating profit when cost inflation accelerates.
4.
Premiumization focus: Management believes pricing pressure will be concentrated at the lower end of the consumer spectrum. At a group meeting reported by Bernstein Research on 30 October 2026, Carlsberg executives argued that affordability challenges are mainly focused on lower mainstream and economy brands. Consequently, the company is prioritizing high-priced premium brands and beyond-beer offerings to effectively mitigate margin dilution from price-sensitive consumers.

Historical Pricing and Volume Trade-Offs

5.

In its annual report published on 28 February 2012, the company reported that cost of sales per hectolitre increased organically by 8% due to rising packaging and raw material costs. However, implementing several price increases yielded a 4% price/mix improvement, which helped expand the operating margin by 60 basis points to 14.7%.
6.

According to Carlsberg's 2022 annual report published on 7 February 2023, cost of sales per hectolitre rose organically by 13% despite favorable hedges. Although organic revenue grew 15.6% driven by a 9% revenue per hectolitre increase, the reported gross margin contracted by 190 basis points to 45.6% due to pricing lags in Western Europe.
7.
Absolute profit protection: Carlsberg prioritized absolute profit delivery over margin percentages during the persistent inflation of 2023. In its 2023 annual report published on 7 February 2024, the group reported organic operating profit growth of 5.2% driven by a 10% organic revenue per hectolitre improvement. However, high input costs and commercial investments caused the group's operating margin to contract by 100 basis points to 15.1%, demonstrating that pricing covered inflation in absolute terms but diluted the margin rate.
8.
Severe peer dilution: Royal Unibrew suffered extreme margin erosion during the 2022 inflation cycle because of low initial hedge ratios. According to Royal Unibrew's annual report published on 1 March 2023, organic volume growth was only 1% while gross profit margins collapsed by 7.2 percentage points to 37.1%. This historical underperformance illustrates how insufficient hedging amplifies margin exposure when brewers fail to implement immediate, inflation-matching price increases.
