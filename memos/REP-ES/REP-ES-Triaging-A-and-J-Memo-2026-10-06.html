[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = D

Thesis. Repsol is a cyclical-peak case dressed as a cash-return case. A geopolitical spike in European refining margins has doubled its earnings and its share price inside a year, and at EUR 29.18 the market is paying a top-decile multiple for a year of earnings that every broker, the company and we all expect to fade. We take the other side of the market's thesis from this price.

  • The change is external, extreme and at its peak.
    • Middle East and Russian supply disruption lifted the Spanish refining margin indicator from USD 5.60 a barrel in H1 2025 to USD 12.40 in H1 2026 and a record USD 38 in Q3 2026, about five standard deviations above the USD 6.50 mid-cycle the company plans on.
    • Because Repsol is the European major most levered to refining (54 percent of EBITDA), the effect on its outputs was larger than on any peer's.
    • The cause is a supply shock whose kind is temporary: 0.8 to 0.9 million barrels a day of exports lost through the Strait of Hormuz and up to 1.0 million barrels a day of Russian middle distillates.
      • Dated Brent has already spiked to USD 144 and de-escalated once.
    • The structural part is smaller but real: 35 European refineries closed since 2009, 20 percent of regional capacity, and Morgan Stanley expects mid-cycle margins to settle 30 percent above history.
      • That is the one durable external tailwind.
  • The internal change is genuine, incremental and already in the numbers.
    • Organic capex was cut from EUR 14.3 billion for 2023 to 2025 to EUR 8.5 to 10.0 billion for 2026 to 2028, the upstream perimeter fell from 18 countries to 10, eight renewable stakes were rotated, and 30 to 40 percent of operating cash flow is returned with buybacks of EUR 1.2 to 2.0 billion a year.
    • Three of the screen's forces are strong and dated, which is a real 'Major Corporate Change' input.
      • But the chief executive has been in post since 2014, the strategy is "written in stone", and the internal change on its own is worth perhaps EUR 0.5 to 0.8 of mid-cycle earnings per share.
      • It did not double the earnings
      • The crack spread did.
  • 'Financial Outputs' have improved enormously and are forecast to give half of it back.
    • Adjusted net income was EUR 2,711 million in H1 2026 against EUR 1,155 million a year earlier, gearing fell to 11.3 percent, and consensus FY2026 earnings per share rose 133 percent in twelve months.
    • The same consensus has earnings per share falling 25 percent in FY2027 and a further 11 percent in FY2028 (EUR 5.40 to 4.05 to 3.59), and EBITDA down 31 percent from FY2026 to FY2028.
      • The upgrade cycle is real and late: estimates are rising on a peak quarter while the buy-rating share has fallen 13 points in three months.
  • The change has travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain and has nowhere left to go but back.
    • The crack spread has passed through the company's choices (inventory built, turnarounds deferred), its actions (buybacks raised), its key operating metrics, its financial statements and the sell-side's estimates.
    • What remains is the reversal travelling back down the same chain.
  • What the price assumes, and what it does not.
    • At EUR 29.18 the shares are 7.52x the EUR 3.88 of 24-month-forward earnings the current multiple implies, the 96th percentile of the stock's five-year range (median 5.35x) and between the ten-year 'prudent' 6.50x and 'defendable' 7.80x bands.
    • The price carries the 2026 cash, the October buyback and the Q3 margin
    • It does not carry the FY2028 that every broker publishes.
  • The return does not reach the bar on any path we call probable.
    • Valuing the shares at 6.50x the earnings two years beyond each horizon, with earnings falling from EUR 3.88 to about EUR 3.50 to 3.60 as margins normalise, the 'Modal return' is about −11 percent a year over eighteen months and about −2.5 percent a year over three years against the 20 percent bar.
    • The cash leg is sound (a 3.6 to 4.3 percent dividend plus buybacks)
    • The earnings leg and the re-rating leg are both negative.
    • Even at the conceded 7.80x anchor the three-year return is +2.8 percent a year.
      • The price below which the modal path clears the bar is about EUR 18.6 at eighteen months.
  • The pattern it fits is 'Major Corporate Change', at C and not crisply.
    • The inputs pass on their face and the output link does not run through them
    • Three other setups describe this stock's mirror image.

  • The case is therefore a park on the sell side.

    • A holder from here earns the dividend and watches the earnings and the multiple fall together.
    • The case re-opens below about EUR 19, or on evidence that the European margin has structurally re-based.

  • Rating = D, because the expected change is material in size but not in direction for a holder from EUR 29.18: the input forces are strong but peaking ('Change Forces' C), the outputs have improved enormously and are forecast to revert ('Financial Outputs' C), and the 'Modal return' is below zero at both horizons (F).

    • The three Elements combine as a chain whose first two links are real and whose last link breaks on the price, so the Building Block sits below its first two parts and above its last.
    • Not C, because a C needs a modal return in the 13 to 17 percent band and we cannot find one on any path we call probable
    • Not F, because the near-term upgrade momentum is real, the balance sheet is the strongest in the comparison set, and a sustained-margin branch exists that would make the case.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The bull says European refining tightness is structural (closures, Hormuz, Russia), Repsol is the most levered European major to it, the 2026 free cash flow yield is 27 percent, net cash arrives in 2027, and 6 to 7 percent a year of buybacks on an 8x multiple compounds to 20 percent without any re-rating.
    • We concede the 2026 cash, the balance sheet and a probable H2 2026 beat.
    • We answer that the 27 percent yield is a one-year figure that the same bull house has at 8.6 percent in 2027
      • That consensus already has EBITDA down 31 percent by FY2028
      • That the company itself plans on USD 6.50, guides USD 20 against a USD 38 spot, and tests impairments at USD 50 Brent
      • And that a 96th-percentile multiple on peak earnings is not an 8x multiple in any sense that protects a buyer.
    • Where the bull would win: if the indicator holds above USD 15 a barrel through Q4 2027 with the company's premium above USD 8, the mid-cycle will have moved and we would re-anchor.
    • We put that branch at about one in three.

    • But the chief executive has been in post since 2014, the forces are incremental rather than transformational, and none of them is the reason earnings doubled.
  • Red-flag check: no caretaker chief executive
    • The transformation is in the cash-flow statement, not in a presentation (net capex EUR 2.7 billion in 2025, down 46 percent; capex EUR 3,746 million in FY2025 against EUR 5,106 million in FY2024)
    • The guidance rises are buyback tranches, not one-off
    • No crown jewel sold (control of upstream "completely out", the chief executive at the capital markets day of 10-Mar-26)
    • No empire-building (M&A ruled out)
    • No dangerous leverage (gearing 11.3 percent)
    • The buyback is cash-funded.
  • Not A, because there is no leadership change and no new strategy, and the internal change on its own is worth perhaps EUR 0.5 to 0.8 of mid-cycle earnings per share (our estimate from the FY2025 brokers'-basis EPS of EUR 2.32 to the FY2028 consensus of EUR 3.59 after stripping the margin assumption), not a re-rating of the business.
