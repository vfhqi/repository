Systems FY26 included the £23.3m NATO Support and Procurement Agency communications programme that does not repeat; its recurring base was about £39m. FY27 Systems is that base growing 4% to 6%, plus Project CAIN's £8.0m initial order (delivered in the quarter to June 2026), the DSTL cabinet contract and a probability-weighted follow-on tranche of about £5m in the second half (55% of about £9m). From FY28 Systems adds £15m to £20m a year of CAIN follow-ons, NATO framework follow-ons and Tier 2 sub-system work as the Ashchurch facility loads toward its £15m of headroom, which takes its margin to the top of the 12.5% to 15.0% target by FY29. Power grows 15% in FY27 on the over $20m of autonomy orders taken in the last four months of FY26 and $7.4m of Tier 1 wins, then 7% to 11% a year, reaching the bottom of its 10% to 14% margin target by FY30. Components grows 4.5% in FY27 (about one point of it pass-through inflation) on the industrial restock, AI data-centre contactors and own-brand lines, then 2% to 5.5%, with its margin rising on mix but staying below the 7% to 9% target. Gross margin by division: Systems about 38.5% to 39%, Power 36% rising to 37%, Components 27% rising to 27.5%; group 33.6% FY27 rising 20 basis points a year to 34.2% FY31 (mix and the exit of low-value work, net of memory inflation passed through at a lag).

The operating drivers behind the inputs

Revenue growth: a recurring base of about £131m (FY26 reported £154.1m less the £23.3m programme) growing 5% to 6% a year, plus a programme block of £13m in FY27 (CAIN initial order plus a weighted follow-on) rising to £20m to £30m a year from FY28 as UK and NATO defence budgets (rising 8% to 9% a year on Berenberg's December 2025 figures) convert into tranches once the Defence Investment Plan is published. The order book was £102.4m at 31 May 2026, £18m higher underlying after removing the £19m NSPA order from the prior-year figure, with most of it due to ship within eighteen months. The FY27 half-year phasing reflects the absence of the £23.3m programme from H1 (H1 FY26 revenue £85.7m), so H1 FY27 is modelled at about £75.5m (down 12%) and H2 FY27 at about £77.5m (up 13% on £68.5m).

EBITDA margin: adjusted operating profit plus depreciation of owned and right-of-use assets and amortisation of capitalised development (the brokers' adjusted basis; FY26 actual 9.0% on that basis). The margin path is the output of a largely fixed adjusted overhead (£42.0m in FY26, of which about £36m to £38m is fixed within a year) growing 4% to 5% a year (£42.2m FY27 including the £0.5m Autumn Budget employer National Insurance and wage effect, £44.4m FY28, £46.4m FY29, £48.7m FY30, £51.0m FY31) against revenue growing 6% to 8% from FY28, giving a net drop-through of 21% to 25% of incremental revenue to operating profit. The H1 FY27 EBITDA margin of 8.6% corresponds to an adjusted operating margin of about 5.6% (the thesis-at-risk level the first set set is 5.5%); H2 FY27 9.4% corresponds to about 6.4%; H1 FY28 9.7% to about 6.6%.

Depreciation and amortisation: FY26 total of £6.1m comprised £1.71m of depreciation of property, plant and equipment, £1.47m of right-of-use asset depreciation, and £2.90m of amortisation of which £1.76m was acquired intangibles (excluded here) and about £1.1m capitalised development and software; the £4.3m excluding acquired intangibles rises with the capital-spending step (over £7.5m across FY27 and FY28 per the company) to £4.6m, £5.0m, £5.3m, £5.5m, £5.7m.

Net financial items: £1.0m on the adjusted basis in FY26 (bank interest at 2.3% to 2.6% over SONIA, EURIBOR or SOFR on about £8m of average drawings, lease interest of about £0.3m, fees); falling as net debt of £4.2m (excluding £5.9m of leases) reaches about £3.7m at March 2027 and about nil at March 2028, then net cash of about £5.6m, £12m and £19m; lease interest and facility fees set a floor of £0.2m to £0.3m. Imputed interest on deferred consideration (£0.09m in FY26) is excluded as the brokers exclude it.

Tax rate: 24.1% adjusted effective rate in FY26 (management's "underlying" 26%; UK rate 25%; United States losses recognised); 25% assumed throughout.

Dividend per share: 2.75p for FY26 (raised 10%, covered 4.0x); 3.0p, 3.4p, 3.9p, 4.4p, 4.9p, a payout of 28% in FY27 falling to about 22% as earnings grow faster than the dividend, in line with the FY24 payout of about 21.5% at the last earnings peak. Consensus 2.9p, 3.3p, 4.0p.

Share count: 57.1m issued, 282,008 in treasury, FactSet diluted 57.6m; 1.27m options outstanding and up to 730,000 granted in May 2026 vesting between FY28 adjusted EPS of 11.5p and 16.0p; option exercises (158,500 in September 2026) add about 0.2m to 0.3m shares a year. No buyback is modelled; treasury purchases of about £0.2m a year for employee schemes are entered as the buyback line. No acquisitions are modelled; the balance sheet (£15.0m facility plus £10.0m accordion, net cash from FY28) could fund a bolt-on antenna acquisition or a buyback, which management has not signalled and which would be upside to EPS from FY29.

Capital spending: the company's "over £7.5m across FY27 and FY28" (the finance director, 30 June 2026, confirmed the figure is cumulative), for the Tewkesbury integrated systems facility, the near-field RF testing chamber, United States antenna production and Power's test rooms, including about £1.0m to £1.2m a year of capitalised development and software; then about £3.5m a year. Consensus carries £2.8m, £2.8m and £1.7m on the fixed-asset line only, which is light.

Working capital: net working capital (receivables plus inventories less payables) was £30.6m, 19.8% of revenue, at March 2026, within the 19% to 24% range of FY23 to FY26; FY27 rises to 21.5% on strategic memory and semiconductor stock (inventories £30.5m at March 2026 with more promised; work in progress £10.96m ahead of CAIN), easing to 20.5% in FY28 as CAIN work in progress ships and to 20% thereafter. Contract liabilities (£6.3m) and milestone billing moderate the swings; half-year balances move £4m to £7m around the year-end figure.

Lease payments: right-of-use depreciation of £1.47m plus lease interest of about £0.3m in FY26, so about £1.8m a year rising with the new sites.

The company's guidance, and how your inputs compare with it

The company issues no numeric guidance; it defines market expectations as the average of its covering brokers (Cavendish, Berenberg and Zeus Capital) and speaks to that average. How to read these figures

  • Amounts are in millions of the reporting currency; per-share figures are in units of that currency. Negative figures are shown in brackets.
  • Historical figures use FactSet's standardised lines, which can differ from the company's own presentation.
  • Earnings per share appears on four bases, which must not be mixed: reported basic; FactSet's adjusted diluted, before unusual items; the Viewforth definition, which is the model's standing convention; and the brokers' adjusted basis, on which consensus is based.
  • Consensus is FactSet's median of the broker estimates it holds. Where FactSet's line-by-line consensus differs from the broker-by-broker detail, the line-by-line table takes precedence.
  • Viewforth's forecasts are the Excel model's base case. There are no bull or bear cases.
