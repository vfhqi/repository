[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = C

Thesis. Standard Chartered is a self-help case in its late stage with a structural wealth engine attached: ten years of clearing credit, perimeter and clients under one chief executive have shown through in a 17.6 percent return on tangible equity in H1 2026, and the fee engine is now growing at double digits. At 2,151.0 pence the shares already pay for the self-help and for most of the engine, so the 'Modal return' is 17.0 percent a year over eighteen months and 12.8 percent a year over three years, short of the 20 percent bar at both horizons; the case clears the bar only on a pullback toward 2,070 pence or on earnings above consensus.

  • The change is internal, ten years old, and has travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain.
    • Six of the twelve internal forces are strong and dated (strategy, guidance, standards, perimeter, new products, shareholder returns), and their outputs are already in the statements, the sell-side's estimates and the price.
    • Underlying RoTE went from 11.7 percent in 2024 to 14.7 percent in 2025, delivering the 2026 target a year early, and to 17.6 percent in H1 2026.
    • The May 2026 plan (income growth of 5 to 7 percent a year to 2028, RoTE above 15 percent by 2028 and about 18 percent by 2030, a cost-to-income ratio of about 57 percent, a high-teens EPS CAGR, 68 percent of earnings distributed) is a second, smaller wave of the same change.
    • Consensus FY2026 EPS has risen 8.9 percent in twelve months and the price 45.4 percent, which is the market pricing the move.
  • The external forces pull in two directions, and the headwind acts on the larger profit pool.
    • Asian wealth demand is a structural tailwind and regulatory capital relief a modest one
    • The rate cycle and geopolitics are headwinds, and net interest income is 49 percent of H1 2026 income.
    • A parallel 100 basis point rate cut costs 630 USDm of net interest income, about 6 percent of it
      • The structural hedge has cut the down-rate sensitivity to 620 USDm a year from 1.5 USDbn.
    • Competition in Asian wealth and in deposits is tightening, and the company pays 70 basis points more for deposits than HSBC and DBS have over 25 years.
  • 'Financial Outputs' are improving on every horizon, carried by the share count and the cost line rather than by revenue.
    • Returns, jaws, distributions and estimate revisions are all up against the last three years
    • Revenue growth is steady at 6 to 7 percent, not rising.
    • Cost-to-income fell 270 basis points to 54.6 percent in H1 2026 with 5 percent positive jaws
      • The share count fell 6.7 percent in FY2025 and 3.2 percent in the first nine months of 2026.
    • The one deteriorating output is the net interest margin, 2.03 percent in Q2 2026, down 2 basis points in the quarter
      • It is small so far and hedged.
  • What we think it earns: underlying EPS of 2.22 USD in FY2025 rising to 3.23 USD in FY2028, 13.3 percent a year, at consensus for FY2026 and FY2027 and 2.4 percent below it for FY2028, because we let credit cost normalise to the company's own 30 to 35 basis point range.
    • The parts reconcile: revenue +6.5 percent, margin +3.1 percent, below-pre-provision lines -0.9 percent, share count +4.2 percent, which multiply to +13.3 percent a year.
    • The company's own target is a high-teens EPS CAGR
      • Our path sits a growth band below it, which is the room left for upgrades.
  • What it costs: 8.93x twenty-four-month-forward earnings on 2026-10-02, the 94th percentile of the stock's own five-year range (4.18x to 9.68x, median 5.62x), and about 8.3x on consensus FY2028 earnings after the 4.5 percent fall on 2026-10-07
    • The valuation pin is D.
    • Against the European set the stock is mid-pack (HSBC 9.8x, UBS 10.4x, Lloyds 7.8x, NatWest 7.5x), and two-thirds of that set sits above the 60th percentile of its own history, so the "expensive" reading is partly the sector's.
    • The re-rating is therefore not ours to earn: we apply 8.5x, a shade under the ten-year 75th percentile band of 8.80x, and the mix argument for a higher multiple is worth about half a turn, not the two to three turns the DBS comparison suggests.
  • The return against the bar: 17.0 percent a year over eighteen months and 12.8 percent a year over three years on the modal path at 8.5x, with a cash return of about 3 percent a year from dividends on top of a share count falling 4 percent a year.
    • The earnings leg carries the return
      • The re-rating leg is the swing factor, worth two points in total at 8.5x and thirteen points a year either way at 10x or 6.5x.
    • The price below which the case clears 20 percent a year is about 2,070 pence on the eighteen-month path and about 1,785 pence on both horizons.
  • Where the chain breaks: the rate cycle below 3 percent acting on the net interest half of income, in a stock that has twice this year fallen 6 to 9 percent in a day on news that was not about earnings.
    • On the plausibly worse drop-through (rates 100 basis points lower, credit cost at 40 basis points, tax held at 27 percent) FY2028 EPS falls about 12 percent to 2.84 USD and the return to 7.9 and 8.4 percent a year, below the D band
      • The adverse earnings path alone takes the 'Modal return' from C to F.
  • The pattern it fits: 'Major Corporate Change', not crisply, because the change is at full output and the pattern pays for upgrades still to come
    • 'Compounder' is the secondary fit, a proven upgrader without a crisply mis-modelled line.

  • The case therefore is compelling as a business and not compelling as an entry at 2,151.0 pence: a bet that pays 17 percent a year on the modal path and 20 percent a year below about 2,070 pence, with a coin toss on rates deciding which side of the bar it lands.

  • Rating = C, because the expected change is real and well advanced but, net of what the price already captures, not material enough: the 'Change Forces' (B) are broad and documented, the 'Financial Outputs' (B) are improving on every horizon, and the 'Modal return' (C) falls short of 20 percent a year at both horizons at 2,151.0 pence because the multiple sits at the top of its own range and our earnings path sits at consensus

    • The parts combine as a chain whose last link binds, since the return is the test of materiality and it is a C.
    • Not B, because a B needs a modal return at about 20 percent a year and this path gives 17.0 and 12.8
    • Not D, because the forces and the outputs are both B and the return has a visible path to B on a pullback.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The risks research's "false friend" verdict: rate tailwinds have masked structural inefficiency, so the 17.6 percent RoTE of H1 2026 is the top of a rate cycle mistaken for a structural inflection.
    • The case against, at its strongest.
      • The deposit franchise has paid 70 basis points more than HSBC and DBS over 25 years with "limited strategic rationale"
      • Operations cost 18 percent more than peers relative to revenue
      • The 57 percent cost-to-income target for 2028 is above HSBC's and DBS's
      • Wealth product margins fell to 1.0 percent from 1.2 to 1.5 percent and a 40 USDbn custody transfer flattered the asset growth
      • Underlying profit of 5,360 USDm against reported 4,558 USDm in 2025
      • Bohai carried at 1,018 USDm against a 265 USDm market value
      • Three finance chiefs in recent periods
      • Three executives selling 850,000 shares at the highs in August 2026 with no open-market buying
      • Net interest income "highly sensitive" below 3 percent benchmarks with a hedge maturity of 2.5 to 2.7 years
      • Hong Kong a third of profit
      • A tier-one bear case at 743 pence and a 33 percent downside case at 1,190 pence.
      • In this reading, when rates fall below 3 percent the net interest leg collapses faster than the fee leg can grow.
    • What we concede.
      • The deposit premium, the cost gap, the rate sensitivity, the earnings-quality gap of 2024 and 2025, the Hong Kong concentration and the insider selling are facts, and each is carried into a rating below.
      • We also concede the price: at the 94th percentile of its own five-year multiple the stock leaves little room for the counter-case to be partly right.
    • Why we hold our view on the business.
      • The fee leg is not a rounding: non-interest income is 49 percent of revenue against a UK peer average of 32 percent, Wealth Solutions income grew 38 percent in the half and 43 percent in the quarter, and the 22 percent asset-growth rate excluding the custody transfer still beats the 18 percent peer average.
      • The cost delivery is dated: 54.6 percent cost-to-income in H1 2026 with the margin already falling, 1.1 USDbn of run-rate savings, a 2 percent cost beat in Q2.
      • The rate exposure has been cut by more than half and the company raised net interest income guidance into the cutting cycle.
      • The buyback converts 6 to 7 percent revenue growth into 13 percent EPS growth, and the balance sheet (CET1 14.2 percent, LCR 148 percent, a 26 basis point loss rate) funds it.
      • The earnings-quality items are the kind that end: the restructuring charge stops with the programme in 2026, and the Bohai gap of 753 USDm is about 1.1 percent of market capitalisation.
    • Where we do not hold our view, on the price.
      • The counter-case does not need to be right about the business to be right about the entry
      • It needs rates below 3 percent, which is a coin toss on the evidence, and at that point the return is 8 percent a year and the multiple can fall a quarter on a sector de-rating with earnings unchanged.
      • That is why 'Investment Thesis' is a C and not a B, and why the recommendation is a park at this price rather than a progression.
