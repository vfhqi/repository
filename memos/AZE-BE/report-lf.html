Following the May 2026 AGM, Azelis achieved a 75% board independence ratio with six of eight directors classified as independent, accomplished through the voluntary resignation of EQT nominees Bert Janssens and Kristiaan Nieuwenburg in February 2026 and removal of legacy EQT nomination rights from the Articles of Association. This governance transformation represents an outlier speed and completeness for European specialty distributors transitioning from private equity ownership to fully democratic public governance structures.

Both the Audit and Risk Committee and Remuneration and Nomination Committee are entirely composed of independent directors, achieving a governance scaffolding quality that compares favorably to peer IMCD where five out of five supervisory board members are independent but was executed in a compressed twelve-month transition window. The capital structure maintains all issued shares belonging to the same single class with equal one-vote-per-share rights and no voting restrictions, while EQT's stake fell to 9.93% via Akita and shareholders eradicated special controls in May 2026, completing a total elimination of dual-class structures and special voting arrangements.

The transformation becomes statistically extreme when compared to typical private-equity-to-public transitions that maintain founder shares, special voting blocks, or gradual PE representation reduction over three to five year periods. The sudden exit of EQT and rapid board restructuring may trigger short-term strategic instability, yet the completeness of democratic governance restoration positions Azelis as an institutional governance reference case among European mid-cap chemical distributors.
