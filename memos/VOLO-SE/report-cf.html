EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Volati AB has completed a fundamental structural transformation following the June 2026 spin-off of Salix Group, transitioning from three business areas to five distinct platforms (Ettiketto Group, Communication, Corroventa, S:t Eriks Group, and Tornum Group) to enhance transparency for equity investors. The group pursues a decentralized M&A model with platform-level co-ownership programs, though high leverage of 4.8x adjusted EBITDA including preference shares severely limits near-term acquisition capacity.

Group-level executive leadership is finally stabilizing after prolonged CFO churn, with Charlotta Nyberg establishing operational control since April 2026, while leadership alignment is secured via long-term warrants targeting the newly consolidated executive committee. However, platform-level leadership shifts across Corroventa, S:t Eriks, and Ettiketto are introducing transition friction, and board governance risks have escalated following co-founder Karl Perlhagen's November 2025 exit amid transaction investigations.

The external environment presents severe cyclical headwinds across multiple fronts. S:t Eriks faces severe volume declines in building segments due to Swedish housing market weakness, though public investments in

Nordic rail and electrification keep infrastructure product volumes highly resilient. European farmers are deferring capital investments, leading to low demand for grain dryers and silos, though Tornum's SEK 500 million Lantmännen project substantially mitigates broader agricultural weakness into H1 2026. Weak market conditions have heightened price-based competition in grain handling, compressing Tornum's margins, while Middle East conflict routes caused acute raw material shortages for Ettiketto in early 2026, and label operations remain highly sensitive to upstream paper manufacturer strikes.

Financial profile deterioration is accelerating across key metrics. Group net sales increased 11% to SEK 1,335 million in Q2 2026 primarily driven by acquisitions, but organic sales growth rebounded to only positive 2% in Q2 2026 versus flat in full year 2025. EBITA margin fell to 7% in Q2 2026 compared to 11% in the prior period, remaining significantly below the peer average of 16% for proven Nordic compounders.

LTM cash conversion reached 86% in Q2 2026, while net debt to adjusted EBITDA decreased to 2.9x from 3.0x in early 2026, though total leverage is stretched at 4.8x when including group preference shares, limiting near-term acquisition growth pace post the Salix Group divestment.

The Q2 2026 earnings delivery revealed continuing operations EBITA of SEK 91 million, down 31% year on year, with group EBITA margin compressing to 7%, representing actual EBITA 18% below the broker forecast of SEK 111 million. Ettiketto Group net sales surged 37% to SEK 436 million though EBITA margin fell to 15%, as acquired Interket Group contributed 24% of sales but diluted historical profitability. S:t Eriks Group EBITA plunged 57% to SEK 20 million due to lower production rates, while Tornum Group EBITA dropped to SEK 6 million from SEK 19 million, pressured by intense competition.

Peer-relative momentum positions Volati in the bottom decile of Swedish serial acquirers. Volati's organic growth of 2% in Q2 2026 trails the sector median of 4% for Swedish compounders, while Volati's Q2 2026 operating margin of 7.7% is nearly half the median of 13.2% across compounders. Volati's 12-month adjusted EPS revisions of negative 71% represent a major negative outlier driven by the Salix spin-off, with EPS revisions of negative 68% over 3 months confirming continuing margin deterioration for continuing group platforms.

Sell-side coverage is highly concentrated on DNB Carnegie through analysts Carl Korsheden and Robert Redin, with rating transitioning from persistent Buy to Hold reflecting post-divestment margin dilution risks, and target price plunging from SEK 113 to SEK 27 driven by restated continuing operations projections. Lead analysts argue risk-reward remains finely balanced at current EV/EBITA multiples, with neutral commentary stressing that 4.1x leverage including preference shares severely restricts near-term M&A expansion.

Forward EPS drivers center on five primary factors with mixed plausibility. Ettiketto add-on M&A drives top-line label consolidation across European core markets, with management highlighting favorable conditions and DNB Carnegie projecting margin reaching 16.3% by 2028e. S:t Eriks and Tornum cost restructurings protect consolidated group operating margins from severe volume deleverage, with management prioritizing restoring profitability and DNB Carnegie projecting S:t Eriks margins recovering to 3.9% by 2028e.

However, Nordic construction market recovery unlocking structural operating leverage faces low plausibility as high interest rates continue delaying any residential recovery starts, with continued housing market weakness pushing S:t Eriks Q2

EBITA down by 57% year on year. Guidance credibility is proving structurally unachievable as Volati targets average annual EBITA growth per ordinary share of 15% over a full business cycle, yet management admits creating a significant growth gap since underperforming the 15% target after 2023. To close the growth gap, management aims for 2027 EBITA of SEK 1.1 billion to 1.5 billion, requiring accelerated organic recovery and steady acquisitions across remaining platforms.

Volati has missed its annual 15% EBITA target in three of the last four years, with the Salix divestment reducing historical baseline EBITA margins to only 6.8% for continuing platforms, and continuing EBITA growth plummeting to negative 31% in Q2 2026. - Earnings Calls - Earnings Calls The historical delivery track record confirms prolonged cyclical freeze in Nordic construction could prevent closing the EBITA growth gap entirely, forcing covenant breach risk, leaving the forward EPS outlook structurally challenged absent synchronized macro recoveries across construction, agriculture, and weather normalization.

Appendix

Plausible problems to monitor

Volati's fundamental operating profile reveals multiple structural weaknesses that position the group in the bottom tier of Swedish serial acquirers, with organic growth of 2% in Q2 2026 trailing the proven peer median of 4%, and operating margins of 7% falling substantially short of the 16% peer average for Nordic compounders. These performance gaps are not merely cyclical artifacts but reflect deeper execution challenges that persist across multiple reporting periods and business platforms.

The most alarming structural weakness centers on balance sheet leverage, with net debt to EBITDA standing at 2.9x –more than double the 1.2x average achieved by proven Nordic peer compounders. When including preference shares, total adjusted gearing reaches a stretched 4.8x, severely constraining the group's ability to execute its decentralized M&A strategy and forcing management into defensive capital preservation mode.

This leverage handicap directly undermines the core value creation thesis predicated on continuous platform acquisitions, as detailed in Section 4 (Financial Profile Change) and Section 2 (Strategy, Structure and Ambition Change). Consensus estimate revisions paint an equally troubling picture, with adjusted EPS estimates declining 71% over the last twelve months and 68% over the last three months –representing a massive negative outlier driven by the Salix spin-off and continuing margin deterioration across remaining platforms.

This negative revision trajectory contrasts sharply with the peer median EPS growth of positive 15%, underscoring fundamental momentum divergence detailed in Section 7 (Peer-Relative Earnings Momentum). Capital efficiency metrics reveal persistent underperformance, with Volati's three-year average return on capital employed of 5% falling far below the 13% achieved by peer compounders. Cash conversion of 53% similarly lags the peer median of 79% for Swedish compounders, reflecting working capital management challenges and elevated capital intensity in construction-exposed platforms.

These efficiency gaps, explored in Section 4 (Financial Profile Change), constrain self-funded acquisition capacity and force greater reliance on external financing at elevated leverage multiples. Historical sales performance demonstrates prolonged weakness, with Volati's three-year sales CAGR of negative 12% dramatically underperforming the peer median of positive 7% growth. The company has missed its annual 15% EBITA growth target in three of the last four years, with continuing operations EBITA growth plummeting to negative 31% in Q2 2026, creating an unresolved strategic growth gap that management has explicitly acknowledged.

This persistent target underperformance, detailed in Section 11 (Historical Delivery Track Record), raises fundamental questions about the credibility of management's ambitious 2027 EBITA target of SEK 1.1 billion to 1.5 billion. Near-term forecasting accuracy remains problematic, with actual Q2 2026 EBITA coming in 18% below broker expectations, indicating poor visibility and execution uncertainty during critical platform transitions.

Governance risks escalated following board co-founder Karl Perlhagen's resignation amid investigation into share transactions, leaving Patrik Wahlen as sole remaining co-founder and raising concerns about institutional oversight during a period of accelerated leadership churn. These execution and governance headwinds, examined in Section 1 (Leadership and Governance Change) and Section 6 (Most Recent Earnings Delivery), compound operational challenges across cyclically depressed platforms.
