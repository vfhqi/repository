  1. Historical energy disruptions show that physical asset damage and contract losses frequently require multi-year recovery timelines.
  2. While Saudi crude infrastructure has demonstrated rapid mechanical resilience, specialized LNG facilities face prolonged repair schedules due to complex supply chains.

Geopolitical Oil and Gas Outages

1.
Severe disruption: The research analyzed twelve relevant sources, three companies, and four historical precedents to understand energy recovery timelines, all of which were fully utilized in this analysis. Bernstein Research documented that the 1979 Iranian Revolution removed 4.8 million barrels per day, representing 7% of world oil supply. This geopolitical disruption required partial recovery over several years but structurally depressed OPEC's market share from 45% to 28% by 1986. Investors should note that long-term volume losses can permanently reshape global market positions and erode buyer relationships over time.
2.
Vulnerable operations: Global energy supply patterns after the 2011 Libyan crisis highlight the extreme vulnerability of regional liquefied natural gas operations. The Libyan conflict halted 1.55 million barrels of daily oil exports and completely shut down the nation's primary liquefaction plant. While oil flows recovered within several months, the associated LNG exports never returned, permanently displacing Libyan gas in Europe. This precedent indicates that complex processing facilities face much higher structural restart barriers than crude pipelines following regional conflicts.
3.
Contractual erosion: European gas purchase patterns following the 2022 Russian supply shock illustrate how rapidly buyer loyalty can dissolve during geopolitical crises. Although Russian pipeline infrastructure remained completely intact, voluntary buyer self-sanctioning and legal mandates permanently reshaped the regional import mix. Russia's share of the European gas market collapsed from 45% to approximately 7% to 12% within two years, with contracts banned through 2027. This shift demonstrates that contractual trust is often lost far more quickly than physical infrastructure can be rebuilt.

Crude Versus LNG Resilience

4.
Mechanical speed: Saudi Arabian oil infrastructure has historically demonstrated exceptional recovery speed due to widespread component availability and modular designs. The September 2019 drone attacks on Abqaiq and Khurais temporarily knocked out 5.7 million barrels of daily production. Although initial estimates suggested several months would be required for full repairs, Saudi Aramco restored normal operations within three weeks. This rapid recovery shows that crude oil systems can rebound much faster than LNG liquefaction plants.

Deutsche Bank Research reported that the massive AP-X liquefaction trains commissioned by Air Products utilize custom cryogenic vessels, compressor strings, and specialized gas turbines. These custom-built components require narrow supply chains and have delivery lead times of two to four years. Consequently, repairing damaged LNG infrastructure takes years rather than weeks or months, unlike standard crude oil processing units.

The incident took the entire 15.3 million tonnes per annum Texas terminal completely offline for more than eight months. Although partial operations resumed in early 2023, the facility was not fully restored to peak capacity until May 2024. This precedent highlights that even non-conflict technical failures can trigger multi-year volume constraints for downstream natural gas portfolios.
