Section 2 – Committed Debt Facilities and Leverage Policies

Section summary
  1. Pets at Home Group Plc maintains a highly conservative balance sheet with a robust net cash position and substantial headroom under its committed debt facilities.
  2. The group's primary committed facilities comprise a £300m revolving credit facility and a £16.3m asset-backed loan, ensuring minimal near-term refinancing risks.

Debt Facility Analysis

1.
Robust liquidity: Pets at Home Group Plc maintains a significant liquidity buffer through an unsecured revolving credit facility of £300m. This core corporate facility is scheduled to expire on 30 September 2028. The group has drawn down £20.0m under this facility as of 8 October 2026, compared to £40.0m in the prior year. Interest on this drawn debt is priced at SONIA plus a margin tied to pre-IFRS 16 leverage, which keeps the unsecured funding costs highly efficient.
2.
Secured funding: Pets at Home Group Plc utilizes an asset-backed loan facility with HSBC to fund capital item purchases. This facility carried a face value of £16.3m on 8 October 2026 and matures on 27 March 2030. The group has pledged property, plant, and equipment totaling £16.3m as collateral to secure the funding. Monthly repayments are required on this loan, which carries an interest rate of BoE base rate plus 1.35%, allowing the group to finance equipment on stable terms.
3.
Abundant facilities: CVS Group Plc completed a major refinancing in May 2026, securing total credit facilities of £350m. These facilities comprise a £125.0m term loan and a £225.0m revolving credit facility that both mature in May 2030. The facilities are provided by a syndicate of eight commercial banks with an option to extend for an additional year. This capital structure provides the competitor with substantial firepower for overseas acquisitions.
4.
Competitive pricing: CVS Group Plc also maintains an unutilised overdraft facility of £5.0m to support short-term liquidity. Interest on the group's drawn bank debt is charged at 1.25% to 2.50% above SONIA. During the fiscal year ended 30 June 2026, these borrowings carried a weighted average rate of 1.6% above SONIA. This competitive pricing demonstrates how peer groups secure favourable debt pricing via refinancing.

Leverage and Accounting Policies

5.
Conservative leverage: Pets at Home Group Plc reports an exceptionally strong balance sheet with an adjusted net cash position. As of 8 October 2026, the group held cash of £36.6m against total face-value debt of £36.3m. This net cash of £0.3m represents an actual leverage ratio of 0.0x underlying EBITDA. This represents a strong improvement from the prior year when the group carried net debt of £12.0m, meaning that balance sheet risks remain minimal.
6.
Adjusted metrics: Pets at Home Group Plc defines pre-IFRS 16 leverage as adjusted net debt divided by rolling proforma EBITDA less rental charges. This specific alternative performance measure is utilized because it represents the core covenant metric for the lenders. The lease liabilities on the balance sheet totaled £338.0m on 8 October 2026, including £75.3m of current lease liabilities. Excluding these lease liabilities from leverage calculations allows the group to maintain a highly conservative pre-IFRS 16 profile.
7.
Higher leverage: CVS Group Plc operates with a significantly higher leverage profile compared to the net cash position of Pets at Home. The peer reported a bank-test leverage ratio of 1.63x as of 30 June 2026, up from 1.18x in the prior year. This leverage ratio further increased to 1.94x as of 31 October 2026, driven by ongoing share buybacks and acquisitions. The peer's elevated leverage demonstrates a more aggressive capital deployment strategy in veterinary.
8.
Flexible limits: CVS Group Plc maintains a strict capital allocation framework that targets leverage of no more than 2.0x net debt to bank-test EBITDA. However, management is willing to temporarily exceed this 2.0x ceiling to fund attractive corporate acquisitions. Jefferies notes that the peer's ongoing £50.0m share buyback program is expected to push leverage close to this limit. This flexible policy contrasts with Pets at Home, which focuses on maintaining a virtually unleveraged corporate balance sheet.

This covenant limit can be temporarily increased to 3.75x during a designated twelve-month Acquisition Spike Period. Section 3 – Share Buybacks and Share Count Reduction (FY2023 to FY2027)

This strategic shift allows the group to return excess cash to shareholders while supporting a more resilient and profitable capital structure over the medium term.
2.
Steady execution: Pets at Home Group Plc has made consistent progress on its cash return initiatives over multiple financial periods. The company stated in its interim results that it has completed £150m in share buybacks over the past four years. The group is currently 50% through its £50m program for fiscal 2027, having completed £25m of repurchases in the first half of the year. This steady deployment of cash demonstrates the company's commitment to returning surplus cash to equity holders when trading remains robust.
3.
Repurchase pricing: Pets at Home Group Plc achieved distinct pricing outcomes across its historical buyback tranches. The group's fiscal 2026 preliminary results report that during the period ended March 2025, the company repurchased and cancelled 8.42m ordinary shares. Market execution: Pets at Home Group Plc executed its market transactions through authorized financial intermediaries to maintain compliance with regulatory safe harbors. The parent company announced in October 2026 that it purchased 1.24m ordinary shares through Deutsche Numis at the beginning of that month. These market purchases are carried out under the £50m buyback program initially announced in June 2026. This structured buying protocol ensures that the company remains on track with half-year targets while minimizing short-term price volatility.

The group's interim report published on November 24, 2026, confirmed that the outstanding shares in issue have been reduced by approximately 13% to date. This compares to a cumulative 10% reduction reported at the end of fiscal 2026. This steady retirement of share capital helps to permanently shrink the equity base and raise the value of remaining shares.

Forecasted benefits: Financial analysts project that the ongoing shift toward buybacks will yield substantial multi- year benefits for Pets at Home's earnings profile. This reallocation of capital is viewed positively because it increases shareholder yields without changing underlying forecasts. The enhanced buyback activity ensures that long-term dividend cover is strengthened even as total cash returns remain flat.

Section 4 – Employee Share Schemes and Dilution Dynamics

Section summary
  1. Pets at Home Group Plc manages employee share scheme dilution tightly through strict headroom limits and an Employee Benefit Trust that purchases shares in the open market.
  2. The dilutive impact of employee share awards is significantly offset by the company's aggressive share buybacks, resulting in a net reduction in outstanding share count and positive EPS accretion.

Compensation Schemes and Award Structures

1.
Moderate dilution: Pets at Home Group Plc utilizes several equity-settled share plans to align employee rewards with performance. The group operates the Company Share Ownership Plan, Save As You Earn options, and the Restricted Stock Plan, which had a combined outstanding balance of 6.68m awards as of 26 March 2026. This outstanding balance represents a decrease from the 8.20m awards outstanding in the previous fiscal year. The reduction in outstanding awards helps to limit the potential equity dilution for current shareholders over the long term.
2.
Transitioning plans: Pets at Home Group Plc transitioned its long-term incentive structure during its annual general meeting on 2 June 2026. Shareholders approved replacing the existing Restricted Stock Plan with a new Performance Share Plan to reinforce the link between reward and performance. While existing awards granted under the previous plan remain unaffected, all subsequent discretionary grants are issued under this new framework. This transition ensures that executive rewards remain performance-linked under modern governance.
3.
Non-executive restrictions: Pets at Home Group Plc enforces strict boundaries regarding participation in its incentive arrangements. The non-executive directors and the company chair do not participate in any of the company's share option or incentive schemes. This separation prevents conflicts of interest and ensures that board oversight remains independent. Consequently, the non-executive board has no direct exposure to dilution from share-based payments or equity performance.

Share Option Movements and Exercises

4.

The group granted 3.60m new restricted stock awards while employees exercised 1.05m awards and forfeited 2.34m awards during the period. This level of option exercise indicates how employee awards gradually convert into equity over time.

The group reported that the 2022 SAYE scheme matured in fiscal 2026 but unfortunately ended up underwater, leading to no exercises. This followed the previous year's trend where the 2021 SAYE scheme similarly matured underwater. These underwater options reduce employee share dilution because participants do not exercise unviable options at maturity.
6.
Reduced balances: Pets at Home Group Plc closed the fiscal year ended 26 March 2026 with a significantly smaller outstanding share option balance. The outstanding Save As You Earn options fell to 1.58m from 3.08m in the previous period, carrying a weighted average exercise price of 253.3 pence. This decline was driven by the forfeiture of 1.26m options during the year. Lower SAYE participation protects the group from unintended dilution during market recovery periods.

The company utilizes a binomial model for option grants and a Black-Scholes or Monte-Carlo simulation for Restricted Stock Plans depending on performance criteria. These calculations incorporate assumptions like a 30% expected volatility and a 2% dividend yield. Accurately measuring these values is critical because they determine the annual share-based payment non-cash expense recognized.

Funding Trusts and Headroom Limits

8.
Proactive funding: Pets at Home Group Plc relies on an Employee Benefit Trust to satisfy share-based employee incentives. The trust held 6m ordinary shares as of 26 March 2026, representing an increase from 5.67m shares held in the prior year. The average purchase price of these trust-held shares stood at 338.2 pence per share. Funding option schemes through market purchases helps prevent the issuance of new shares and avoids gross dilution.
9.
Strict boundaries: Pets at Home Group Plc limits the dilutive potential of its incentive schemes through strict contractual limits. The rules of the share plans restrict total newly issued shares in any ten-year period to 10% of issued capital across all employee schemes. Discretionary schemes are further restricted to a tighter 5% limit over ten years. These strict boundaries guarantee that long-term equity dilution remains capped within acceptable thresholds.
10.

The peer reported using 5.57% of its 10% ten-year headroom limit as of 30 June 2026. Furthermore, Galliford Try avoided using any of its 5% discretionary limit by satisfying those awards through market-purchased shares. This precedent demonstrates that UK corporations actively prioritize market-purchased share satisfaction to eliminate discretionary dilution.
