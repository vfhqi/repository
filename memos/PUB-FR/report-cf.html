EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF) 6 findings

Publicis Groupe has engineered a fundamental transformation from a traditional advertising holding company into an AI-powered marketing services platform, underpinned by the proprietary Epsilon identity graph and a country-based operating model that systematically delivers peer-leading organic growth and margin expansion. The group achieved +4.8% organic net revenue growth in Q2 2026, with food and beverage client sectors driving 18.1% organic growth while financial services contributed 7.2% organic growth, widening the competitive gap against legacy agency competitors facing structural decline.

This Q2 acceleration to +4.8% outperformed WPP's -5.0% organic contraction and expanded the net organic growth gap versus peer averages to 610 bps. However, the investment case faces material execution dependencies on the successful integration of the USD 2.17 billion LiveRamp acquisition, the recovery trajectory of the structurally challenged Publicis Sapient consulting division, and the preservation of Epsilon's competitive neutrality as rival holding groups migrate to alternative data platforms.

1.
Strategic and Leadership Materiality: Arthur Sadoun's consolidation of the combined Chairman and CEO roles on May 29, 2024 has accelerated decision-making velocity but simultaneously amplified key-person risk in an organization heavily dependent on his strategic vision and platform architecture. The group carries substantial

lease commitments with right-of-use lease assets of EUR 1,513 million and total lease liabilities of EUR 2,142 million as of June 30, 2026, creating fixed-cost exposure that requires sustained revenue momentum. Board independence temporarily fell to 45.45% in May 2026, dropping below governance code recommendations following the reclassification of Marie-Josée Kravis and the appointment of Benjamin Badinter, though specialized technology expertise was enhanced through the addition of Microsoft Chief Scientist Jaime Teevan. The strategic pivot toward AI-powered agentic marketing services represents a multi-billion-dollar bet that Publicis can defend and monetize proprietary data assets faster than generative AI tools commoditize traditional creative production, with early signals suggesting the platform model is delivering but cultural frictions and regional talent turnover remain elevated.

Top EPS Drivers (Ranked by Impact and Plausibility): The forward earnings trajectory is driven by five primary structural levers with differentiated confidence levels. Connected Media market expansion contributes +300-400 bps to group growth with HIGH plausibility and HIGH confidence, driven by client digital budget migration. Net new business pipeline wins secure +200-250 bps of organic growth with HIGH plausibility and HIGH confidence, supported by client transition speed.

Epsilon-LiveRamp identity synergy is projected to add +100-200 bps to revenue growth with HIGH plausibility but MEDIUM confidence, dependent on regulatory clearance and successful platform integration. AI-enabled cost savings are expected to expand operating margins by +20 bps with HIGH plausibility and HIGH confidence, contingent on internal tool rollout speed. Publicis Sapient recovery could contribute +70 bps to group organic growth with MEDIUM plausibility and MEDIUM confidence, heavily dependent on global IT capex spend normalization.

The compounding joint probability of achieving the upper-end organic growth target approaches 40% under base-case assumptions, though stacked downside scenarios including LiveRamp client defections and permanent Sapient consulting decline could compress EPS to EUR 7.50 versus consensus expectations near EUR 7.82.

2.
Recent H1 2026 Delivery: The group delivered Q2 organic growth of +4.8%, beating company-supplied consensus of +4.7% and accelerating sequentially from +4.5% in Q1 2026 despite tougher comparables. Headline H1 operating margin of 17.5% met consensus and expanded 17 bps year-on-year, though adjusted diluted EPS of EUR 3.52 missed Bloomberg consensus of EUR 3.60 due to higher tax and interest expenses. Marketing services grew organically by +6.5% in Q2 while Publicis Sapient declined over 6% organically as technology clients paused discretionary digital transformation projects. Personnel costs fell 110 bps to 65.6% of net revenue driven by offshore headcount leverage, though efficiency gains were partially offset by higher G&A and licensing costs supporting AI deployments. Free cash flow before working capital requirements reached EUR 957 million, representing robust underlying operational performance despite extreme seasonal cash outflows. The earnings delivery confirms bifurcated segment momentum with Connected Media and Intelligent Creativity substantially outperforming while technology consulting faces structural project delays tied to geopolitical tensions and enterprise IT budget pauses
3.
Forward Guidance Credibility: Management operates as a habitual conservative sandbagger, consistently

establishing low initial guidance floors that enable predictable beat-and-raise cycles throughout the fiscal year. Publicis surpassed its initial FY25 organic growth target of +4% to +5% to deliver +5.6% actual growth, while free cash flow before working capital exceeded initial expectations of EUR 1.9 billion to reach EUR 2.03 billion.

Strong first-half FY26 momentum prompted management to raise the organic growth floor from +4% to +4.5% and sequentially upgrade full-year free cash flow guidance to circa EUR 2.2 billion. A 50 bps step-up in net revenue growth converts to approximately 30% operational profit flow-through, while a 1 percentage point change in terminal operating margin shifts group fair value by 2%. However, the credibility framework remains highly dependent on Sapient's recovery trajectory and the preservation of LiveRamp's industry neutrality, with tracking metrics flagging warning thresholds if Sapient organic decline exceeds -6.4% or Middle East revenue drag surpasses 30 bps.

The multi-year track record of consistent beats suggests guidance management is structurally conservative rather than operationally opportunistic, though Deutsche Bank notes that recent operational upgrades have been fully offset by financial headwinds, leaving consensus EPS estimates flat.

4.
Sell-Side Positioning: The analyst pool exhibits a highly concentrated bullish stance with 83% Overweight

ratings, 11% Equal-weight, and only 6% Underweight, though Morgan Stanley's July 31, 2026 coverage termination due to governance conflicts removes a vocal bull from the consensus. Price targets range from Deutsche Bank's conservative EUR 90 to Bernstein's EUR 115 Outperform rating, with Rothschild maintaining a Buy recommendation at EUR 110. Bulls emphasize Epsilon's proprietary identity graph as a core differentiator, argue that LiveRamp acquisition significantly strengthens data collaboration capabilities, and justify Publicis' 11x 2026 P/E premium based on consistent organic outperformance.

Bears warn that generative AI tools could commoditize standard creative tasks and that competitor migration to alternative clean rooms threatens LiveRamp's standalone value. Hold-rated analysts at Deutsche Bank highlight ongoing discretionary IT budget pauses at Publicis Sapient and persistent Middle East geopolitical headwinds as key growth constraints. The crowded bull consensus leaves minimal room for execution errors, with any deterioration in Sapient recovery or LiveRamp integration likely triggering rapid rating migrations that compress the premium valuation multiple.

5.
Peer-Relative Ranking: Publicis secures the top-quartile fundamental momentum position among global agency

holding groups, demonstrating superior organic growth, margin expansion, and cash conversion versus legacy competitors. The group delivered +4.8% net organic growth in Q2 2026, widening the growth gap against the consolidated peer average to a massive 610 bps. H1 2026 operating margin reached a record 17.5%, versus WPP's peak historical margin of 13%, establishing structural profitability leadership driven by offshore labor arbitrage and platform automation.

Average net financial debt leverage was maintained at 1.0x EBITDA for H1 2026, highly favorable compared to WPP's elevated 2.4x leverage ratio, providing substantial balance sheet capacity to fund strategic M&A. Win-loss ratios demonstrate competitive dominance with Publicis at 3.5 versus Omnicom at 2.5, WPP at 1.3, and Dentsu at 0.5. However, Epsilon's COREid identity graph targeting 250 million US consumers and the USD 2.17 billion LiveRamp acquisition face neutrality risks as Omnicom and WPP plan contract terminations, potentially isolating Publicis' data advantage and triggering the unraveling of its premium peer valuation.

6.
Investment Case Verdict: Publicis Groupe represents a structurally differentiated growth compounder trading at a justified premium to legacy agency peers, underpinned by proprietary first-party data assets and platform

operating leverage, though the investment case carries material binary risks tied to LiveRamp integration success, Sapient consulting recovery timing, and the preservation of Epsilon's competitive neutrality against rival data consortia and generative AI disintermediation–warranting a selective long position for investors with conviction in management's platform transformation thesis and tolerance for elevated key-person dependency and competitive platform defection risks.

Appendix

Plausible problems to monitor

Employee sentiment reveals a culture of fear at the top and disruption from frequent corporate restructurings, while local talent frictions led to the departure of India pillar Tanmay Mohanty, signaling elevated regional talent turnover that exceeds peer retention norms in high-growth geographies (see Section 1). Management's reluctance to increase base salaries inline with global inflation during recent fiscal years compounds these cultural pressures, creating a compensation practice that lags behind competitors actively recruiting AI and data science talent.

The combined effect of these personnel signals suggests that Publicis' aggressive efficiency drive may be generating organizational frictions that could impair execution quality or trigger critical talent defections. Ongoing Middle East conflicts caused a 30 bps organic growth drag in Q2 2026, with regional net revenue in the Middle East and Africa contracting 6.8% organically in H1 2026, representing a geographically concentrated weakness that significantly underperforms the peer median organic growth rate of positive 2% to 3% in emerging markets (see Section 3 – External Change). While this drag is primarily driven by exogenous geopolitical factors rather than company-specific execution failures, the magnitude and persistence of the contraction highlight Publicis Sapient's disproportionate exposure to discretionary consulting projects in volatile regions. & Co Redburn Publicis Sapient net revenue declined by over 6% in Q2 2026, heavily impacted by Middle East geopolitical headwinds and diluting group organic growth by 30 bps, marking a structural consulting project slowdown that falls well below the sector base rate for digital transformation services growth of positive 3% to 5% (see Section 4 – Financial Profile Change and Section 6 – Most Recent Earnings Delivery).

This segment-specific weakness reflects broader client capex pauses and technology budget freezes, but Sapient's decline is steeper than peers such as Accenture Interactive or Deloitte Digital, suggesting either a more concentrated client base or weaker competitive positioning in enterprise consulting engagements. & Co Redburn Right-of-use lease assets stood at EUR 1,513 million as of June 30, 2026, with total lease liabilities reaching EUR 2,142 million, representing a material off-balance-sheet commitment that exceeds the peer median lease-toequity ratio by approximately 15% to 20% (see Section 4).

While Publicis maintains investment-grade credit metrics overall, the elevated lease liability exposure creates a structural fixed-cost burden that limits operational flexibility during potential revenue downturns and constrains the company's ability to rapidly adjust its physical footprint in response to evolving hybrid work models.

Morgan Stanley analyst Laura Metayer discontinued coverage of Publicis Groupe SA effective July 31, 2026, following disclosure that a Morgan Stanley affiliate serves as a Publicis director, representing a sell-side coverage contraction that reduces independent research availability and may signal governance complexity concerns among financial intermediaries (see Section 8 – Sell-Side Commentary Survey). While the termination stems from conflict-of-interest protocols rather than fundamental concerns, the loss of a major bulge-bracket voice removes a key source of independent validation for the company's premium valuation multiple.

Importantly, several anticipated plausible problem to monitor across financial metrics, margin trajectory, and cash conversion did not materialize in the body analysis. Publicis' operating margins of 17.5% in H1 2026 significantly exceed the peer median of 12% to 14%, cash conversion rates of 99% of operating income outperform sector norms by 5 to 10 percentage points, and leverage ratios of 1.0x to 1.2x EBITDA remain well below the peer average of 2.0x to 2.5x.

The company's organic growth outperformance gap of 610 bps versus peers and consistent beat-and-raise guidance cadence further confirm that fundamental execution strength dominates the overall profile. The Plausible problems to monitor assessment therefore isolates governance independence, cultural and talent retention pressures, geographically concentrated revenue contraction, consulting segment structural decline, elevated lease commitments, and sell-side coverage reduction as the primary areas of relative underperformance.

These signals warrant monitoring as potential amplification points under adverse scenarios, particularly if LiveRamp integration complexity, client capex freezes, or geopolitical tensions intensify beyond current base case assumptions. However, the limited scope and magnitude of these weaknesses relative to Publicis' structural data asset advantages and market share gains suggest they represent manageable execution risks rather than disqualifying fundamental flaws.
