[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = C

Thesis. Gaztransport & Technigaz is a monopoly licensor riding a demand inflection that the market has already paid for. LNG carrier orders have turned from 17 in H1 2025 to 65 in H1 2026, and because about half of each licence is recognised two to three years after the order, that turn lifts earnings in FY2027 and FY2028 rather than in FY2026, which is a trough year. At EUR 213.0 the shares already sit at their own ten-year median multiple, so the modal return is about 12 percent a year, not the 20 percent the framework asks for.

  • The change is external, sector-level and dated.
    • Sanctioned liquefaction capacity and a replacement cycle have turned the one series that drives GTT's revenue, and the turn is contracted into a backlog tabled by year.
    • Orders: 65 in H1 2026, 56 of them LNG carriers delivering 2028 to 2030, against 17 in H1 2025 and 32 in Q1 2026
      • Order intake had fallen 67 percent in the first nine months of 2025.
    • Backlog: EUR 1.85 billion at June 2026, up 16 percent from a late-2025 low of EUR 1.6 billion
      • EUR 300 million scheduled for H2 2026, EUR 655 million for FY2027, EUR 603 million for FY2028 and EUR 295 million for FY2029 before any order booked after June 2026.
    • Outlook: management raised its ten-year outlook in July 2026 to approximately 550 LNG carriers by 2035 from "450++", with 250 vessels tied to projects already sanctioned
      • 107 to 116 MTPA of liquefaction capacity has been sanctioned or approved since early 2025.
  • The change inside the company is consolidation, not transformation.
    • An external chief executive since 5 January 2026 has restructured into One GTT with Energy and Marine divisions, cut the loss-making Elogen unit to an R&D option and integrated the EUR 194 million Danelec purchase
    • None of it changes the economics of the licence, which is the engine.
  • The 'Financial Outputs' lag the forces by two to three years, and the near year is a trough.
    • FY2026 revenue is guided to EUR 740 to 780 million against EUR 803 million in FY2025, and earnings per share grow about 2 percent
    • The recovery is FY2027 and FY2028, at 7 to 11 percent a year.
    • Our path: earnings per share EUR 11.5 in FY2026, EUR 12.4 in FY2027 and EUR 14.0 in FY2028 from EUR 11.20 in FY2025 (brokers' basis), a compound rate of about 7.7 percent a year over three years
      • Consensus is EUR 11.46, EUR 12.21 and EUR 13.54.
    • The margin: at 67 to 68 percent of revenue the EBITDA margin is at its ceiling and can be held, not raised
      • The one strong output is the staircase of dated order news.
  • The change has travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain, including the estimates and the price.
    • It shows in the company's resources (the new chief executive), its choices (the 550-vessel outlook, the Marine division, the Elogen cut), its actions (65 orders, the Danelec integration), its key operating metrics (the EUR 1.85 billion backlog), its financial statements (H1 2026 EBITDA EUR 263.6 million at a 68.1 percent margin) and the sell-side's estimates (FY2028 consensus earnings per share up 30.1 percent in twelve months).
    • The price: the shares rose 35.3 percent over twelve months and 37.0 percent over nine, the period in which the FY2028 consensus rose 30.1 percent
      • The first leg has been paid for.
  • What it costs: EUR 213.0 on 2026-10-05, or 16.05 times 24-month-forward consensus earnings per share of EUR 13.27.
    • Own history: the ten-year median is 16.20 times and the ten-year 75th percentile 17.90 times
      • On the five-year series the same reading is the 68th percentile against a median of 14.71 times.
    • Peers: the highest price-to-earnings multiple in the dashboard sector, against 13.4 times for Elecnor and 13.0 times for Tenaris
      • The premium is explained by a 67 percent EBITDA margin against a peer median of about 13 percent, and the set is too poorly matched to test it.
  • What it returns: about 12.1 percent a year over eighteen months and 9.1 percent over three years, built from a 4.3 to 5.1 percent dividend yield, earnings growth of 7 to 8 percent a year and no re-rating
    • The modal case holds the multiple at 16.0 times, a blend of a re-rating, a hold and a de-rating on the one condition the case turns on, the 2027 order intake.
    • Where the bar clears: at about EUR 192, 10 percent below today, on the unchanged earnings path
      • Or at EUR 213.0 only if 2027 intake matches 2026's and the multiple reaches the ten-year 75th percentile of 17.9 times, which is possible, not probable.
  • The pattern it fits: 'Demand Inflection', at B, crisp in structure and late in timing
    • The pattern's output, major earnings-per-share trajectory upgrades, has largely happened.

  • Where the chain holds and where it breaks.

    • It holds from the forces through to the earnings, each step contracted and public.
    • It breaks at the link from price to return, because the market has absorbed the inflection.
    • The case is therefore a top-decile business whose investment case is parked on return, not on quality.

  • Rating = C, because the input change is strong and well-evidenced ('Change Forces' B), the outputs it drives improve only at a mid-to-high single-digit rate after a flat FY2026 ('Financial Outputs' C), and the return at today's price is about 12 percent a year ('Modal return' D)

    • The materiality of the change is real but the price has absorbed most of it, so the chain holds to the earnings and breaks at the return.
    • Not B, because a B thesis needs the outputs and the return to follow the forces, and here earnings growth sits below the framework's 12 to 20 percent band at every horizon and the return sits below the bar.
    • Not D, because the demand inflection is sector-level, contracted into a dated backlog and already lifting the out-year estimates
      • A D thesis would have an input change the outputs had not yet confirmed.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The bull case, in Bernstein's form with a EUR 245 target: the backlog inflection is happening now, consensus upgrades for 2028 to 2031 follow, free cash flow runs at about EUR 400 million a year for six years, and a lower cost of capital justifies a re-rating.
    • We concede the direction: the FY2028 consensus has risen 30.1 percent in twelve months, and the 2026 order surge is not yet fully in FY2029 numbers that only two to three brokers forecast.
    • We do not concede the magnitude.
    • To clear 20 percent a year the shares need both earnings per share near EUR 16 by FY2029 and a multiple near the ten-year 75th percentile of 17.9 times
      • Each is possible, the product of the two is not probable, and the modal case is the one written in the 'Modal return' Element below.
    • Where the bull case would win is a 2027 order intake above 100 carriers, which would move the re-rating branch from possible to probable
      • That series is the one to watch.

    • The financial statements show it from FY2027.
  • Not A, because the demand force is a cyclical wave on a structurally growing base, and the company's own actions (Danelec, the Marine division) are secondary to the external cycle.

  • Environmental rules: EU ETS coverage rises to 100 percent of shipping emissions in 2026
