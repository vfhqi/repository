Section 2 – Concentration of 2026-2030 Financial Drivers

Section summary
  1. PGE's financial transition between 2026 and 2030 will be shaped by concentrated regulatory decisions on coal support and grid returns rather than diffuse operational drivers.
  2. PGE's rising capital expenditures on offshore wind and gas-fired generation will increase depreciation charges and debt levels, which may pressure short-term earnings per share.

Policy and Generation Drivers

1.
Decreasing revenues: PGE faces a major earnings transition as its conventional coal capacity payments are set to terminate after the end of 2028. According to a report by Bernstein on May 8, 2025, PGE outsmarted its domestic peers by securing coal capacity revenues of PLN 2.25bn in 2024 that run through 2028. This contractual cushion stabilizes cash flows, but the sudden termination of these payments will significantly depress earnings per share after 2028 unless new state support is legislated.
2.
Critical transition: PGE and its partner Ørsted are progressing the construction of the massive 1,498 MW Baltica 2 offshore wind project. An investor presentation from Ørsted on November 9, 2026, confirmed that the project has reached approximately 45% completion and remains on target for commercial operations in the second half of 2027. This commissioning timeline is critical because the new wind assets will provide inflation-indexed contracted revenues that begin to replace the cash flows from decommissioned conventional coal plants.
3.
Regulatory uncertainty: PGE is currently in negotiations with the Polish government regarding the operational future of conventional lignite assets. According to Bernstein on January 16, 2026, the utility is seeking a longterm funding mechanism to manage these high-emission units beyond the 2028 capacity market cliff. If the company fails to secure alternative subsidies, the narrowing of power spreads will severely reduce conventional generation margins and weigh heavily on net income.
4.
Narrowing margins: PGE's retail division is highly sensitive to the household energy tariffs approved annually by the Energy Regulatory Office. A PGE presentation in March 2026 indicated that client activity and adaptive load profiles are necessary to stabilize supply revenues amid volatile pricing environments. Any downward revision in regulated household tariffs would squeeze retail margins, directly dragging down the group's net profits.

Regulated Grid and Peer Benchmarks

5.
Growing returns: PGE plans to double its distribution regulated asset base through massive grid investments over the next decade. A PGE investor presentation in January 2026 highlighted that the average allowed distribution WACC is targeted to exceed 9% through 2035. This highly visible asset expansion will support a steady growth in regulated earnings, providing a predictable financial base that isolates the utility from merchant market volatility.
6.
Peer comparison: Enea has already experienced a steeper decline in conventional generation subsidies than its larger peer PGE. A broker report from Bernstein on May 8, 2025, noted that Enea and other Polish peers saw their capacity payments expire in 2025 because they initiated five-year contracts in 2021. This earlier subsidy cliff forced Enea to accelerate its transition, highlighting the structural advantage PGE maintained by locking in coal capacity revenues until the end of 2028.
7.
Grid integration: CEZ represents a comparable regional utility that is also shifting its financial mix toward low- carbon and regulated grid assets. According to a Morgan Stanley report on November 28, 2024, CEZ is targeting an increase in networks EBITDA exposure to 37% by 2030, up from 19% in 2024. This transition illustrates a sector-wide trend where utilities sacrifice merchant upside to secure higher multiples, a path PGE is replicating with its own distribution-led expansion.
8.

A broker report from Morgan Stanley on November 28, 2024, noted that CEZ expects its coal EBITDA exposure to drop to 7% by 2030 from 17% in 2024. This rapid generation shift requires substantial capital investment in renewables, which limits near-term dividend growth but establishes a cleaner long-term asset profile.

Section 3 – Coal Capacity Revenue Cliff and Perimeter Differences

Section summary
  1. PGE faces a steep drop in conventional capacity revenues after 2028 as current coal subsidies expire, while apparent analytical discrepancies are resolved by distinguishing PGE's perimeter from Tauron's credit assumptions.
  2. European peers like CEZ, RWE, and LEAG highlight that the transition away from coal-fired generation requires accelerated asset depreciation or negotiated state-backed compensation frameworks to protect utility valuations.

Resolving Capacity Revenue Perimeters

1.
Operational stability: PGE secured significant near-term financial protection by locking in high capacity market revenues for its coal-fired fleet through the end of 2028. According to a Bernstein broker report dated May 8, 2025, PGE's Conventional Generation segment earned PLN 2.25bn from capacity market contracts in fiscal year 2024 compared to PLN 2.2bn in fiscal year 2023. This contract coverage provides a substantial revenue buffer that supports capital spending on clean energy assets but leaves the company exposed to a steep cliff once the current subsidy framework terminates.
2.
Perimeter clarity: The apparent discrepancy in projected capacity revenues is resolved by recognizing that the lower figures belong to a peer utility's rating case rather than PGE's financial statements. A Fitch credit rating report on September 28, 2026, assumed annual conventional capacity revenues averaging PLN 914m in 2026- 2027 and PLN 534m in 2028-2030 for Tauron rather than PGE. Consequently, the wider PGE perimeter includes its massive lignite and hard-coal assets, whereas the Fitch projection represents Tauron's smaller, standalone conventional generation fleet.
3.
Contrary evidence: PGE may face an accelerated timeline for capacity payment erosion if regional environmental regulations or grid constraints limit the dispatch of its contracted coal plants. According to a Bernstein broker report on May 8, 2025, the utility did not contract any old coal-fired capacities during the latest two capacity auctions, choosing instead to focus on battery storage and renewables. This shift suggests that the actual revenues captured by the coal fleet could decline faster than historically modeled if market conditions deteriorate.
4.
Analytical confusion: Financial analysts must distinguish between PGE's consolidated energy group and individual peer ratings to avoid underestimating Polish capacity market dynamics. A BNP Paribas broker report dated October 27, 2026, noted that regulatory changes in Poland frequently shift the cash flow profiles of statecontrolled utilities. Because the Fitch report on September 28, 2026, focused exclusively on Tauron's rating assumptions, applying those lower figures to PGE's larger asset base would lead to a significant understatement of its true cash flow generation.
5.
Strategic timing: PGE management successfully extended the operating horizon of key conventional assets to align with its ongoing decarbonization timeline. A Bernstein broker report on July 29, 2026, reported that PGE decided to extend Rybnik coal plant operations to 2028 because the 920 MW unit continues to receive capacity market payments. This strategic alignment ensures that legacy thermal assets remain profitable while the company constructs replacement gas-fired and offshore wind capacities.
