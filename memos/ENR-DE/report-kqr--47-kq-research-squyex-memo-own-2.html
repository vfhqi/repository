Section 2 – Brand Licence Fee and Omterra Rebrand Costs

Section summary
  1. Siemens Energy's transition to the independent Omterra brand will eliminate a costly margin-linked brand fee paid to Siemens AG.
  2. While the rebrand will incur near-term separation costs, historical precedents show that removing trademark royalties drives long-term margin expansion.

Brand Fee Burden and Omterra Transition

1.
Punishing fees: Siemens Energy faced rising licensing expenses under its legacy branding agreement with Siemens AG. The group's brand license fees grew from €51m in fiscal year 2024 as profitable non-Gamesa segments expanded. This rising baseline demonstrates why escaping the legacy brand fee represents a substantial earnings catalyst.
2.
Escalating estimates: Siemens Energy's margin-linked brand fee was estimated by Jefferies on 14 July 2026 to reach €300m for fiscal year 2025 and €400m for fiscal year 2026. This steep upward trajectory reflects the contractual terms that scale the fee from 0.3% to 1.2% of non-Gamesa revenues based on EBITA margins. Eliminating these payments would provide a significant EBITA margin tailwind of ninety basis points once the transition is complete.
3.
Strategic transition: Siemens Energy announced preparations on 14 July 2026 to transition its operations to the independent Omterra brand name. The group expects to launch the brand in late 2026 and complete the phase-in over a year or slightly more due to corporate entity complexities. Resolving this timeline is critical because the active trademark license agreement dictates when royalty payments finally cease.
4.
Contrary evidence: Siemens Energy cannot guarantee an early termination of the Siemens brand payments despite the initial Omterra launch. Chief Executive Officer Christian Bruch stated on 5 August 2026 that it is too early in the rebranding process to confirm if payments will end before the scheduled 2030 contract expiration. This timing risk could delay the projected EPS expansion if transition timelines slip.

This peer experience highlights why one-off rebranding expenses must be weighed against long-term royalty savings.
8.
Substantial upside: Siemens Energy is highly likely to achieve meaningful earnings expansion by terminating its legacy brand fee. While near-term rebranding costs will weigh on special items, the elimination of a €400m annual royalty represents a permanent margin driver. Other side of the story: Siemens Energy's rebranding benefits could be muted in the near term by substantial execution friction and persistent trademark liabilities.
