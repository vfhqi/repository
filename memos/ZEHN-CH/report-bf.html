EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Strategic transformation and portfolio mix

1.
Structural portfolio pivot: Zehnder has systematically transformed from a legacy radiator manufacturer into a high-efficiency indoor ventilation platform, with the ventilation segment now generating approximately 66% of group sales (EUR 501.7 million in FY25) and management positioning the firm as an integrated indoor climate solutions provider. This ventilation focus delivers superior profitability versus local peers, with Zehnder's ventilation EBIT margin reaching 12.50% in FY25 compared to Systemair's 9.40%, Lindab's approximately 9%, and Munters' EBITDA margin of 15.70% (though Munters benefits from data center exposure).
2.
Extreme segment margin divergence: The ventilation platform accounts for roughly 96% of group EBIT despite representing only two-thirds of sales, while the mature radiator division contributed just EUR 2.4 million in adjusted EBIT during FY25, highlighting a severe profitability imbalance across the portfolio. The Gränichen plant closure–responding to radiator volume declines exceeding 30% since pre-pandemic periods and triggering EUR 35.9 million in FY24 restructuring charges–has lowered the radiator segment's break-even point and restored segment EBIT margin to 0.90% in FY25 from negative 8.20% in FY24.
3.
Strategic M&A execution: The EUR 86 million acquisition of Spanish ventilation leader Siber in July 2024 provides mid-price ventilation solutions tailored for Southern European multi-family apartments and supported reported ventilation sales growth by 5% in FY25, expanding Zehnder's addressable market beyond premium single-family homes. Concurrent portfolio pruning included the divestment of the loss-making European Climate Ceiling Solutions division in mid-2024, removing EUR 23 million in low-margin commercial office sales through a cash-neutral transaction that triggered a EUR 10 million one-off loss.
4.
Revenue predictability expansion: Service and maintenance contracts are expanding to reduce cyclical new-build housing dependency, with after-sales service representing approximately 15% of ventilation segment sales in FY24 targeting over 1 million European installed units, while the industrial Clean Air Solutions division operates a product-as-a-service subscription model delivering a 99% recurring revenue rate across more than 3,000 active service contracts.

Financial recovery and peer-benchmarked returns

5.
Unstable top-line recovery: Group revenues fell from EUR 812.5 million in FY22 to EUR 705.8 million in FY24 before recovering to EUR 760.7 million in FY25, driven by 7.80% growth (7% organic plus Siber integration), though the long-term 2017-2022 CAGR of 6.90% was disrupted by restructuring and market cycles. Segment performance diverged sharply, with ventilation sales growing 18% to EUR 501.7 million (15% organically, supported by strict UK building standards) while radiator sales fell 8% to EUR 259 million, suffering from weak European renovation activity and radiator volumes declining approximately 30% versus pre-pandemic baselines.
6.
Margin inflection and operating leverage: Group adjusted EBIT margin rebounded to 8.60% in FY25, up from 7.10% in FY24 and 8.30% in FY23, with adjusted EBIT rising 30.20% year-over-year to EUR 65.2 million, benefiting from structural radiator capacity cuts after margins bottomed at 2% in FY24 (excluding restructuring charges). Reported EBIT recovered to EUR 63.4 million in FY25 from a depressed EUR 14.1 million in FY24, when EUR 35.9 million in restructuring provisions (primarily from closing Swiss radiator assembly) severely impacted profitability, with residual EUR 1.8 million charges in FY25 from selective German and Chinese radiator adjustments.
7.
Exceptional capital return rebound: Return on capital employed surged to 22.10% in FY25 from 15.40% in FY24, driven by capital employed dropping to EUR 234.7 million through radiator plant divestments, substantially outperforming the historical ROCE baseline of 22.60% in FY21, 18.90% in FY22, and 17.90% in FY23. This ROCE performance positions Zehnder favorably against local capital goods peers, with Systemair delivering an adjusted operating margin of 9.40% and Lindab achieving approximately 9% EBIT margin, while Munters' 15.70% EBITDA margin reflects its advantaged data center thermal management exposure.
8.
Balance sheet restoration: Zehnder returned to a net cash position of EUR 32.8 million at the end of FY25 after slipping into EUR 11.8 million net debt in FY24 due to Siber acquisition cash outflows, recovering from stronger historical net cash positions of EUR 70.8 million in FY23, EUR 45.4 million in FY22, and EUR 123.2 million in FY21. Cash flow from operating activities rose to EUR 80 million in FY25 from EUR 60.6 million in FY24, with operating cash conversion reaching 91.60% of EBITDA (up from 78.70%) and unlevered free cash flow rebounding to EUR 64 million from negative EUR 57 million in FY24.

Industry structure and competitive positioning

9.
Massive addressable markets with fragmentation: The global industrial air filtration and commercial kitchen ventilation market exceeds SEK 200 billion annually, while the European Union's building efficiency investment requirements total €297 billion annually over 2024-2030, leaving an estimated annual energy-retrofit investment gap of €149 billion. The European ventilation market remains highly fragmented, with Volution holding only 5-6% market share in residential ventilation, contrasting with the consolidated European steel panel radiator segment where peer Stelrad controls a leading 25.40% volume share.
10.
Leading local market positions: Zehnder maintains the number 1 market position in ventilation sales within Germany and the Netherlands, though its European radiator segment faces persistent structural demand declines, while peer Purmo Group maintains a top-4 European position in radiant heating and cooling volumes. Competitor behaviors show mixed discipline, with Lindab achieving organic ventilation sales growth primarily by grabbing market share from peers in Western Europe, indicating intensifying competitive pressure despite generally rational industry pricing.
11.
Structural regulatory tailwinds: Awaab's Law legally requires UK social housing landlords to address damp and mould within mandatory timeframes, triggering a 285% surge in ventilation-related customer enquiries, while the Decent Homes Standard requires English social homes to reach an Energy Performance Certificate C rating by 2030 and the UK's Future Homes Standard (effective March 2027) drives a 3x to 5x uplift in product value per dwelling, with mechanical ventilation with heat recovery penetration reaching 41.20% in UK new builds.
12.
European energy mandates accelerate demand: The European recast Energy Performance of Buildings Directive requires member states to finalize national transposition by May 2026, targeting a 16% reduction in residential primary energy use by 2030 and a 60% building greenhouse gas emission cut by 2030, creating multi-year structural demand drivers for high-efficiency ventilation systems.

Value chain position and margin capture

13.
Advantaged upstream-downstream integration: Zehnder captures superior margins by specializing in premium residential system assembly rather than asset-heavy component manufacturing, while owning the CORE heat exchanger brand provides critical technology lock-in that protects against supplier squeeze, with heat exchangers generating approximately 11% of total ventilation segment sales through both internal supply and external OEM sales. System supplier premium yields structurally superior pricing power, with integrated units managing ventilation, temperature, and humidity simultaneously, driving ventilation segment EBIT margins to 12.50% in FY25 while stand-alone decorative radiator margins collapsed to 0.90% due to cheap alternatives and structural adoption of underfloor heating eroding the radiator value pool.
14.
Extreme division economics divergence: The capital and margin profiles of Zehnder's two business segments remain highly unbalanced, with assembly-driven ventilation producing an excellent 12.20% historic segment margin through outsourced manufacturing, contrasting with capital-heavy radiator plants yielding a weak 4.60% historic margin burdened by low capacity utilization from weak residential renovation activity in Germany and France.
15.
High-margin service capture: Post-installation service and recurring filter replacements capture the most resilient margin pool, supported by over 1 million active European home ventilation systems providing stable after-sales revenues, while industrial Clean Air Solutions uses a high-margin monthly subscription model with recurring CAS service contracts providing a defensive buffer against cyclical residential construction.

Customer dynamics and switching costs

16.
Diversified customer fragmentation: Zehnder maintains a highly fragmented client base supporting over 3,000 active customers across various global geographies and sectors, with operating segments managed independently to prevent material single-client or single-industry revenue concentrations, minimizing single-client credit and concentration risks.
17.
Critical installer network control: Professional installers represent the primary purchase influencer in the residential climate solutions market, with the German AUFWIND partner initiative registering approximately 700 specialized installers since its 2019 launch, receiving prioritized enquiry processing and individualized local marketing support for customer acquisition. Extensive training programs at Zehnder Academies build substantial, non-visual installer switching costs, training over 20,000 HVAC partners and plumbers annually at global Academy sites where plumbers practice in simulated living environments, reducing installation errors and building strong product familiarity.
18.
Contractual revenue expansion: Zehnder prioritizes its highly profitable aftermarket service business to generate recurring revenue streams, targeting replacement sales across its European installed base of approximately 1 million active ventilation units with offerings spanning filter subscriptions, digital remote monitoring, system cleaning, and profitable direct component replacements. The Clean Air Solutions asset-light model operates through predictable monthly subscription fees requiring zero upfront capital expenditure, with Zehnder bypassing all wholesale intermediaries to control 100% of sales and service delivery across more than 3,000 active service contracts in 20 countries.

Operator quality and foundation strength

19.
Experienced executive leadership: CEO Matthias Huenerwadel has systematically accelerated Zehnder's transformation since his 2018 appointment, successfully leading the operational shift from decorative radiators to energy-efficient indoor climate solutions, divesting the loss-making Climate Ceilings business in 2024, and overseeing the successful post-acquisition integration of Spanish ventilation leader Siber. CFO René Grieder brings deep corporate finance expertise since his 2015 appointment (Master of Advanced Studies in Corporate Finance), maintaining solid cost controls during the 2024 market downturn and successfully resolving legacy accounts receivable issues from Chinese property developer Evergrande.
20.
Aligned incentive architecture: Short-term and long-term incentives are directly tied to profitability and share performance, with GEC members participating in the variable long-term compensation plan using real share allocations and the Management Share Plan further aligning executive wealth with shareholder success, driving disciplined capital allocation and operational focus. This alignment delivered a stellar 22.10% ROCE in FY25 (up from 15.40% in FY24), outpacing local HVAC capital goods competitors through excellent capital deployment efficiency, with asset returns boosted by divesting low-margin radiator assets and lowering the group's capital employed base to EUR 253.30 million in FY24.
21.
Durable component moat: Zehnder owns the internationally leading CORE heat exchanger brand, with self-supplying plate heat exchangers protecting Zehnder from upstream supply chain margin squeezes while CORE simultaneously generates high-margin revenues by selling directly to third-party HVAC competitors, creating a dual-sided competitive advantage in critical thermal management technology. Regulatory tailwinds provide structural demand insulation, with imminent EU and national building mandates creating structural demand insulation for heat recovery ventilation, transitioning to the Future Homes Standard in 2027 delivering a 3x to 5x product value uplift, while brand experience is supported by the 5,000 square meter Center of Climate in Lahr.
22.
Mechanical gravitational margin pull: Group margins benefit mechanically from the organic growth of Ventilation, which grew to represent 66% of total group sales in FY25 while contributing roughly 96% of group adjusted EBIT, with ventilation segment margins reaching 12.50% in FY25 substantially outperforming mature radiator margins, demonstrating powerful through-cycle gravitational pull toward higher-return activities. Asset-light transformation accelerates returns, with ventilation relying on assembly-only operations lowering property, plant, and equipment investment requirements and this structural shift driving unlevered free cash flow to EUR 64 million in FY25, validating the strategic pivot toward capital-efficient, high-margin climate solutions.

Outliers worth noting

This structural imbalance concentrates roughly 96% of total group EBIT within the ventilation platform despite representing only 66% of sales, creating a profoundly unbalanced economic profile where assembly-driven ventilation achieves 12.20% historic margins while capital-heavy radiators yield weak 4.60% historic margins.

The magnitude of this 1,289 basis point EBIT margin gap between divisions materially exceeds typical multi-segment industrial peer divergence ranges and represents a genuine top 5% outlier versus comparable European building products operators. [Cross-ref: Section 2 + Section 4 + Section 9]

Zehnder delivered a remarkable return on capital employed rebound to 22.10% in FY25 from 15.40% in FY24, driven by capital employed contraction to EUR 234.7 million alongside stable ROCE baselines fluctuating between 17.90% and 22.60% over FY21 to FY23. This 670 basis point year-over-year ROCE expansion substantially outpaces industry peers: Systemair delivered 9.40% adjusted operating margins, Lindab approximately 9% EBIT margins, and Munters recorded 15.70% EBITDA margins in their latest reporting periods.

Zehnder's FY25 ROCE of 22.10% positions the group within the top 5% of European HVAC capital goods manufacturers on capital efficiency metrics, representing a genuine positive outlier driven by aggressive radiator asset divestment and mechanical mix shift toward asset-light ventilation assembly.
