EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF) 5 findings

1.
Core Thesis: Attendo AB is executing a structural turnaround centered on transitioning from low-margin outsourcing contracts to high-margin own-operated social care facilities across the Nordics, targeting adjusted earnings per share of at least SEK 9 by 2028, representing a 50% increase from current levels. The company achieved 1.2% organic growth and a 6.8% lease-adjusted EBITA margin in Q2 2026, demonstrating operational recovery momentum, while maintaining highly conservative leverage of 1.1x net debt to EBITDA against a target range of 1.5x to 2.5x, providing substantial debt capacity for future capital deployment
2.
Materiality of Recent Changes: Leadership transition represents the most significant near-term risk, as CEO

Martin Tivéus, the architect of Finland's successful turnaround, will depart by year-end 2026, with Virpi Holmqvist appointed as successor effective September 14, 2026. DNB Carnegie considers Tivéus's departure negative while maintaining a positive buy rating, suggesting intensifying execution risks during the critical Scandinavian margin recovery phase. The board simultaneously consolidated from eight to six members following the declined re-election of Suvi-Anne Siimes and Tobias Lönnevall at the 2026 AGM, restricting independent oversight governance capacity precisely when operational complexity demands enhanced scrutiny.

Strategically, the company delivered exceptional Q4 2025 results with lease-adjusted EBITA rising 53% year-on-year to SEK 343 million, outperforming DNB Carnegie estimates by 17.3%, and achieved its 2026 adjusted EPS target of SEK 5.50 one full year ahead of schedule, demonstrating material acceleration in financial performance trajectory. Ranked by Plausibility: High Plausibility – Occupancy expansion targeting at least 92% from current 88% levels drives margin expansion through operational leverage, supported by demographic tailwinds as Scandinavia's 80+ population is projected to grow approximately 50% between 2020 and 2030, with Sweden requiring over 400 new nursing homes by 2032.

Staffing ratio reform in Finland, lowering nursing requirements from 0.65 to 0.60, significantly reduces subcontracting labor dependency, while a greenfield pipeline of 1,250 beds under construction adds 2% to 3% capacity annually through 2027, and the fully integrated Team Olivia acquisition contributes SEK 130 million in operating profit, yielding SEK 0.50 adjusted EPS accretion. Share buybacks totaling SEK 1.6 billion through 2028 systematically retire equity to compound EPS gains, enabled by current leverage of 1.1x that unlocks up to SEK 4.5 billion in debt capacity.

Medium Plausibility – Pricing indexation protects operating margins against salary inflation by linking multi-year contracts to municipal care price index clauses, though with contractual lags. However, constrained local municipality budgets limit reimbursement price increases above baseline inflation, and Scandinavian municipalities have historically under-compensated private providers for inflation, creating vulnerability to prolonged misalignment between government funding and labor cost escalation.

Low Plausibility / High Risk – The September 2026 Swedish general election poses near-term risk if the Left party gains influence, introducing political volatility regarding private welfare profit caps. Severe structural labor deficits requiring Sweden's elderly care workforce to expand by 30% by 2031, Norway needing 47,000 more workers by 2031, and the European Union facing a projected 1.2 million healthcare worker shortage represent fundamental constraints on expansion velocity that digital mitigation strategies may insufficiently address.

due to contract exits, while lease-adjusted EBITA surged 53% to SEK 343 million, outperforming DNB Carnegie estimates by 17.3%. Plausible problems to monitor

Attendo's organic growth recovery remains materially slower than the peer median, with the company achieving only 1.2% organic growth in Q2 2026 compared to 4% delivered by sector leader Ambea, creating a persistent velocity gap that limits market share gains in the structurally attractive Nordic elderly care segment. Attendo's lease-adjusted EBITA margin of 6.8% in Q2 2026 lags significantly behind Ambea's superior 9.0% adjusted EBITA margin, demonstrating a 220 basis point profitability deficit versus the most operationally efficient peer despite Attendo's completed Finnish turnaround and portfolio rationalization efforts. This margin disadvantage persists even after the company exited unprofitable contracts and integrated the higher-margin Team Olivia disability care operations, suggesting structural cost inefficiencies or weaker pricing power relative to best-in-class operators.

The Nordic care sector faces severe structural labor deficits, with Sweden requiring a 30% workforce expansion by 2031 and Norway needing 47,000 additional elderly care workers, constraints that disproportionately impact Attendo given its slower organic growth trajectory and inferior operational scale compared to the enlarged Ambea-Humana combination. The broader European Union faces a projected 1.2 million healthcare worker shortage driven by burnout and wage competition, creating a systemic supply bottleneck that threatens Attendo's ability to staff its 1,250-bed pipeline and achieve the targeted 92% occupancy required to reach management's SEK 9 adjusted EPS ambition by 2028.

The departure of CEO Martin Tivéus, described by DNB Carnegie as negative despite their maintained buy rating, introduces execution continuity risk precisely as the company attempts to replicate the Finnish margin recovery playbook across its more challenging Scandinavian operations. Leadership transitions are intensifying operational execution risks as board consolidation reduced total membership from eight to six members, restricting independent oversight governance capacity at a critical juncture when Scandinavian margin restoration represents the primary dependency for achieving forward earnings targets.

The September 2026 Swedish general election poses near-term political risk if the Left party gains influence, threatening to resurrect profit cap rhetoric that could compress private care operator margins and undermine the regulatory stability assumption embedded in multi-year municipal contracts. Constrained local municipality budgets limit the scope for reimbursement price increases above baseline inflation, while municipalities in Scandinavia have historically under-compensated private providers for inflation, creating a persistent indexation lag vulnerability that could prevent Attendo from fully offsetting wage inflation through contractual price adjustments.

Sharp inflation proves difficult to adjust for in the short term due to lag effects, as rent adjustments correspond with Consumer Price Index trends while lagging behind operating cost escalations, exposing Attendo to margin compression cycles during periods of accelerating wage inflation that municipalities fail to compensate through timely reimbursement adjustments. Prolonged organic growth stagnation below peer levels could limit cash generation if wage inflation outpaces contract indexation and forces leverage back above the 2.5x ceiling, threatening the continuous share buyback program that represents a critical driver of the SEK 9 EPS target.

Outliers worth noting

Throughout the comprehensive analysis of Attendo AB, several statistical anomalies and unexplained patterns emerge that warrant explicit documentation. These outliers represent data points, trends, or structural characteristics that deviate materially from established peer norms, historical baselines, or logical operational expectations, yet remain inadequately explained within the primary narrative framework. The most striking structural outlier centers on Attendo's extraordinarily conservative leverage position of 1.1x lease-adjusted net debt/EBITDA in Q2 2026, which sits far below the board's target range of 1.5x to 2.5x leaseadjusted EBITDA.

This positioning is particularly anomalous when juxtaposed against peer leverage levels, with Ambea recording 2.3x and Humana posting 2.5x in FY2025. The magnitude of underutilization becomes even more pronounced when considering DNB Carnegie's calculation that Attendo could theoretically repurchase up to SEK 4.5bn in shares while remaining within its leverage targets. This extraordinary debt headroom defies conventional financial optimization logic for a company executing an aggressive growth strategy, raising questions about either excessive capital allocation conservatism or undisclosed constraints preventing leverage deployment.

A second material outlier emerges in the earnings beat magnitude observed across recent reporting periods. Lease-adjusted EBITA rose 53% year-on-year to SEK 343 million in Q4 2025, outperforming DNB Carnegie estimates by 17.3%, while Q2 2026 and Q1 2026 showed lease-adjusted EBITA beats of 21.59% and 17.69% respectively. Such persistent and substantial upside surprises–sustained across four consecutive quarters –represent a statistical anomaly that typically signals either systemic analyst model underestimation, management sandbagging in informal guidance conversations, or genuine operational inflections that market participants fundamentally misunderstood.

The consistency of these double-digit percentage beats challenges efficient market hypothesis assumptions and suggests a structural disconnect between external visibility and internal operational reality. The organic growth trajectory presents a third notable outlier pattern. Attendo achieved 1.2% organic growth in Q2 2026, showing recovery from 0.0% organic growth in FY2025, yet this performance materially lags Ambea's 4% organic revenue growth in Q2 2026.

This three-fold velocity gap between Attendo and its closest structural peer is unexplained by disclosed operational factors, particularly given that both companies operate in identical demographic tailwind environments with similar business model exposures. The divergence becomes more puzzling when considering that Attendo has executed more aggressive M&A, undertaken deeper portfolio rationalization, and achieved superior margin expansion–all factors that should theoretically support, rather than suppress, organic momentum.

The consensus coverage concentration represents a fifth material outlier. Research in the active library shows DNB Carnegie represents the sole fully detailed provider of active forecasts, creating an unprecedented singlesource dependency for forward earnings visibility. This monopolistic coverage structure is highly atypical for a company of Attendo's market capitalization and operational complexity, raising questions about either systematic analyst disengagement, information access barriers, or structural factors deterring broader research participation.

The resulting lack of forecast diversity eliminates the analytical benefit of multiple independent perspectives and concentrates reputational risk with a single brokerage franchise. A sixth anomaly emerges in the seasonal EBITA variance magnitude that defies typical care sector patterns. While unsocial hours compensation during Easter regularly depresses second-quarter margins relative to Q1 performance, the documented quarterly EBITA swings between Q1 and Q2 reporting periods exceed those observed in peer financial statements by material magnitudes.

This amplified seasonality suggests either unique contractual structures in Attendo's customer base, unusually high dependency on temporary labor during holiday periods, or accounting methodology differences that are inadequately disclosed in segment notes.

The Target achievement velocity constitutes a seventh notable outlier. Attendo exceeded its 2024 adjusted EPS target of SEK 4 by delivering SEK 4.08, and reached its 2026 adjusted EPS target of SEK 5.50 one full year ahead of schedule in 2025. While positive for shareholder value, this systematic early delivery pattern –achieving multi-year financial targets twelve months ahead of communicated timelines–is statistically unusual and raises questions about either initial target conservatism, unforeseen tailwinds not embedded in original assumptions, or accelerated operational execution that management failed to anticipate.

The pattern suggests either persistent underestimation of organizational capability or deliberate anchoring of external expectations below internal confidence intervals. Finally, the political risk pricing disconnect represents an unexplained market anomaly. The September 2026 general election poses near-term risk if the Left party gains influence, yet DNB Carnegie raised the target price to SEK 135 from SEK 125 in August 2026 –immediately preceding the election event.

This positive price target revision in the face of proximate political uncertainty defies standard risk-adjusted valuation logic, suggesting either analyst conviction that election outcomes are already priced, systematic underweighting of regulatory risk in models, or access to private polling data indicating lower probability of adverse legislative outcomes than publicly available information would suggest. These eight documented outliers collectively represent unexplained statistical deviations that fall outside normal operational variance bands.

Their persistence across multiple reporting periods and analytical dimensions suggests either structural measurement issues, incomplete disclosure of operational drivers, or genuine organizational characteristics that defy conventional Nordic care sector benchmarks. Future research should prioritize resolving these anomalies through enhanced management dialogue, deeper peer operational comparisons, and expanded third-party data triangulation to determine whether they represent temporary aberrations or permanent competitive differentiation factors.
