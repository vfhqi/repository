[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = C

Thesis. NOS is a cleared-concern cash case, not a growth case, and at 5.295 EUR (2026-09-15) it is clean but not compelling. The fear that a fourth operator would take NOS's revenue has been disproved for twenty months and the shares have already been paid for that; what is left to earn is a 7.9 percent cash yield on a business whose earnings grow about 4.5 percent a year, which is a modal return of 15.5 percent a year over eighteen months against the framework's 20 percent bar.

  • The cause for concern was external, and it is about two-thirds cleared.
    • Digi entered Portugal on 4 November 2024 with a quad-play at 26 EUR a month against incumbent quad-play from 53.99 EUR, and the market priced NOS for a convergent price war: the shares fell to 3.31 EUR and the 24-month-forward multiple toward its five-year low of 9.48x.
    • Share data: through 2025 Digi's 3 percent share came "almost entirely" from Altice and Vodafone
      • NOS's mobile share rose about 1 point to 31 percent and its fixed broadband share held at 33 percent (ANACOM data to end-2025).
    • Consumer revenue: -0.7 percent in 1Q26, -0.3 percent in 2Q26, with the February 2026 price rise of 2.3 percent passed without elevated churn
      • postpaid net additions +69.3k and +72.2k in the two quarters.
    • What is untested: the fixed-line phase.
      • Digi has 173k fixed broadband subscribers against 1.8 million FTTH homes passed (39.0 percent of dwellings) and a target of near 10 percent share by late 2027 or early 2028
      • the 24-month contracts signed before its entry roll off through 2026 and 2027.
  • The internal change is real and disciplined, and it has travelled all six layers of the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain.
    • It runs from the people (a chief financial officer since 1 January 2024, a Chief AI Officer, 900 Claranet staff) through the choices (harvest the network, automate the cost base, sell IT to enterprises) and the actions into the operating metrics, the accounts and the sell-side's numbers.
    • Network: 5G at 99.6 percent of population and FTTH at 91.7 percent of the footprint
      • technical capital expenditure fell from 164 EURm in 2022 to 60 EURm in 2025
      • capital expenditure ex-leases was 84.2 EURm in 2Q26 (-8.2 percent) against a reaffirmed full-year reference below 350 EURm (FY25: 366.4 EURm).
    • Cost: the SCAILE programme (140 use cases identified, 40 implemented within a year) took the telco EBITDA margin to 47.8 percent in 2Q26 (+70 basis points), with customer-care costs -19 percent and maintenance costs -14 percent.
    • Cash and estimates: free cash flow ex non-recurring items +8.8 percent to 62.5 EURm in 2Q26
      • recurring net income +19.2 percent to 68.4 EURm
      • FY2027 consensus earnings per share +25.6 percent over twelve months to 0.463 EUR (FactSet mean to 2026-09-11).
    • Cash returned: the ordinary dividend was rebased from 0.278 EUR to 0.35 EUR, with specials of 0.05 EUR for FY24 and 0.10 EUR for FY25
      • 0.45 EUR, 230.6 EURm, was paid in May 2026.
  • What the change has not done, and on the evidence is unlikely to do, is grow the top line.
    • Group revenue was flat (0.0 percent) at 458.3 EURm in 2Q26
    • consumer telecom, 62 percent of revenue, is shrinking
    • consensus revenue growth is +1.6, +0.2 and +0.7 percent for FY2026 to FY2028.
    • The outputs that are improving (margin, cash conversion, estimates) are the ones investors extrapolate least, and the one that is not improving (revenue) is the one they extrapolate most, which is why the change is real but its materiality for the return is moderate.
  • The earnings path is slow and of the weakest kind.
    • Our modal earnings per share run from 0.46 EUR (FY2026) to 0.52 EUR (FY2029), +4.4 percent a year
    • two-thirds of that growth comes from below EBITDA (depreciation falling, interest falling, tax normalising upward), and revenue and margin together add under 2 percent a year.
    • Consensus has earnings flat (0.460, 0.460, 0.452 EUR for FY2026 to FY2028) because it steps the tax rate to 24.8 percent by FY2028.
    • Against the framework's 12 to 20 percent target band this is an F on every horizon.
  • The price already pays for the clearing.
    • At 5.295 EUR the shares trade at 11.1x consensus earnings 24 months forward (0.4758 EUR), the 38th percentile of their own five-year range and 4 percent below the five-year median of 11.50x
    • they are +50.6 percent over twelve months, 2.6 percent below the 52-week high, and at the sell-side's median target of 5.30 EUR.
    • The oversold entry that makes a cleared-concern pattern pay was consumed between December 2025 and June 2026, when the shares rose 48 percent.
  • The 'Modal return' is a C, and the cash return carries it.
    • We value the shares at each horizon at 11.5x, the stock's own five-year median multiple, on the earnings two years beyond the horizon: 0.535 EUR at March 2028 (a blend of our FY2029 0.52 EUR and FY2030 0.54 EUR) and 0.55 EUR at September 2029.
    • Eighteen months: a value of 6.15 EUR plus a 0.42 EUR dividend, 6.57 EUR in all, +24.1 percent, 15.5 percent a year
      • the cash return is 7.9 percent of the start price, the earnings growth against the consensus base multiplies value by 1.124 and the re-rating from 11.13x by 1.033.
    • Three years: 6.33 EUR plus 1.27 EUR of dividends, 7.60 EUR, +43.4 percent, 12.8 percent a year, at the floor of the C band because the re-rating is one-off and what remains after it is a 12 to 13 percent run-rate of yield plus growth.
    • One-year footing: on one-year-forward earnings (0.460 EUR) the current multiple is 11.5x, not 11.1x
      • held at that level the eighteen-month return is 12.8 percent a year.
      • The convention adds about 2.7 points a year, half from the extra year of growth a 24-month window captures and half because the return to 11.5x is a 3 percent re-rating on the 24-month footing and none on the 12-month footing.
    • The swing: the re-rating decides whether the return is a C or a B: 22.0 percent a year at the peer median of 12.55x, 26.2 percent at the 75th percentile of 13.25x, 11.0 percent at the two-year median of 10.79x, 2.5 percent at the five-year low of 9.48x.
      • Anything above 11.5x needs the sector discount to narrow, which NOS does not control
      • given containment of the entrant we put that at even odds, so the upside branch is possible (about a third) rather than probable.
    • Drop-through: if depreciation stays flat at 508 EURm, interest flat at 47 EURm and the tax rate reaches 25 percent by FY2028, which is what consensus implies, FY2029 earnings per share are 0.46 EUR, flat on FY2026, and the return is 6.6 percent a year over eighteen months and 8.3 percent over three years, an F on both horizons
      • the 'Modal return' would be F and this Building Block D.
      • The dividend does not move under that sensitivity, because it is paid from cash the earnings ratio does not touch.
  • The chain therefore holds from the inputs to the cash and breaks at the top line and the multiple.
    • The change forces net to moderate because a strong internal harvest is set against an external competitive headwind of the same order
    • the financial outputs improve where the change acts and not where the target band lives
    • and the return that results is fair, not compelling, because the part of the pattern that generates 20 percent a year, the re-rating from oversold, has already been earned by whoever bought at 3.50 to 4.50 EUR.

  • The case is therefore a good cash business at a fair price with a mediocre return.

    • It clears the bar on the modal path only below 5.00 EUR on eighteen months (4.40 EUR on three years), or if the enterprise-value discount to peers narrows from 37 percent toward its 26 percent history, which needs a second condition the company does not control.

  • Rating = C, because the change is real and has travelled every layer of the chain, but its materiality for the return is moderate: the outputs it improves are the ones investors extrapolate least, the output it does not improve is the one they extrapolate most, and the modal return is 15.5 percent a year over eighteen months and 12.8 percent over three years, below the 20 percent bar

    • not B, because no leg of the return at its modal odds carries the case to the bar
    • not D, because the cash leg is large, probable and sustainable and the sell-side has already confirmed the change.

Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.

  • The hypothesis, at its strongest: NOS is a false friend, the profile the risks report itself ranks first with the fewest hard inconsistencies.
    • The headline cash and earnings are carried by items that end: 78 EURm of activity-fee refunds in 2024, 9.6 EURm of non-recurring legal gains in 1H26, 20.8 EURm of tower proceeds and refunds in 2Q26's free cash flow, and a technical capital-expenditure cut to 45.4 EURm a quarter that flatters cash today at the network's expense.
    • Underneath, the consumer business decays: consumer revenue -0.7 then -0.3 percent, residential ARPU -1.8 then -0.8 percent, the WOO sub-brand at about 10 percent of mobile gross adds and rising, and 24-month contracts rolling into a market where the entrant has 1.8 million fibre homes and a 10 percent share target.
    • The artificial-intelligence savings are front-loaded and reach a floor in 2027
    • B2B and IT are lumpy and about 3 percent of EBITDA
    • the tax rate normalises
    • the spectrum renewal adds capital expenditure from 2030
    • and the dividend, which is the whole return, is then cut.
    • Morgan Stanley's bear case is 4.00 EUR (-24 percent) and its 2025 bear case was 2.20 to 2.30 EUR
    • the risks report puts the joint probability of thesis failure at 25 to 30 percent over eighteen months to three years
    • the sell-side's median rating is Hold with a target at the price.
  • What we concede: the one-off content of reported profit and cash is real and ending, and we base the case on recurring figures (recurring net income 68.4 EURm in 2Q26; free cash flow ex one-offs 62.5 EURm), on which it is still a C.
    • The earnings growth is of the weakest kind, below EBITDA.
    • The oversold entry has gone.
    • The fixed-line phase of the entrant is untested, and our 65 percent on containment is a judgement, not a measurement.
    • We also concede that a bounded-downside argument on the assets does not hold: telco post-tax earnings of about 150 to 155 EURm (about 0.30 EUR a share) at the lowest meaningful peer multiples in the dashboard (7.8x Telecom Plus, 7.9x Proximus) are worth about 1,200 EURm, 44 percent of the 2,742 EURm market capitalisation
    • on enterprise value, telco EBITDA after leases of about 615 EURm at the research's trough of 4.5x is 2,770 EURm, 74 percent of the 3,763 EURm enterprise value, and the cover reaches 98 percent only at the sector's 6.0x.
    • The downside is bounded by the yield, not by the assets: at 4.00 EUR the 0.35 EUR ordinary dividend yields 8.75 percent.
  • Why we still hold our view, and where it is the same as theirs: our view is neutral, park at this price, and the competing hypothesis argues for the same action
    • we differ on the odds and the shape.
    • The share data are the hard inconsistency in the false-friend story: a decaying consumer business does not gain a point of mobile share and add 72k postpaid customers a quarter while both rivals lose
    • the ARPU decline slowed after a price rise passed without churn
    • the entrant's fixed base is 173k against NOS's 1,565k unique fixed customers
    • and the incumbents who would have to escalate cannot (Altice near 6.5x leverage, Vodafone Portugal's EBITDAaL projected -13 percent cumulatively by 2030).
    • On the cash, the technical capital-expenditure cut is what a completed 5G and fibre build looks like (FTTH at 100 percent of municipalities), not deferral, and the risks report's own leading indicator (adjusted free cash flow trailing reported by more than 20 EURm for consecutive quarters) has fired for a single quarter so far.
    • The dividend at 0.42 EUR is 62 to 69 percent of consensus free cash flow and about 1.2 times covered ex one-offs, and it survives the drop-through sensitivity.
    • So the hypothesis in its strong form is the 35 percent branch, close to the 25 to 30 percent the risks report itself assigns
    • we hold the modal case at C and we do not progress it, which is what the hypothesis would also say.
  • Where the hypothesis would win: consumer revenue below -2 percent for two consecutive quarters, residential ARPU below -2.5 percent, WOO above 15 percent of mobile gross adds, or Digi's fixed connections above 10 percent of its homes passed.
    • The 3 November 2026 results are the first test.

Political and regulatory: is a change moving demand, pricing or cost? (KQ 4, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C

    • the net direction over the horizon is a headwind whose size the 4Q26 decision will set.
  • Fee refunds: cumulative activity-fee income recognised of 38.5 EURm in FY23, 78.1 EURm in FY24, 6.3 EURm in FY25 and 3.0 EURm in 1H26

    • ANACOM 3.5 EURm on Constitutional Court appeal and 0.35 EURm (July 2026)

    • not B, because a clawback would put hundreds of millions of euros of re-farming capital expenditure into 2030 to 2033 and the refunds are ending.

[Robustness of] Foundations upon which the Investment Thesis is built upon (BB 2) Rating = B

The 'Foundations' are robust where the thesis needs them and weak where the thesis is exposed: the business makes a cash harvest plausible and the market has confirmed it on both pinned readings, while the one foundation that could take the harvest away is the industry structure, a four-player market whose fourth player has a lower cost base and a volume mandate.

  • The business is strong enough to make a cash case plausible, and not strong enough to make a growth case plausible.
    • NOS is a top-two operator in every consumer line with the country's densest next-generation network (5G at 99.6 percent of population, FTTH at 91.7 percent of 6.225 million homes), a telco EBITDA margin of 47.8 percent in the top 10 percent of European operators, a leverage ratio of 1.56x in the bottom 5 percent of the sector, and an operator in his thirteenth year who has delivered margin, share, capital-expenditure discipline and a credit upgrade through an entrant's arrival.
