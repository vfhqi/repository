Restricted growth: Volati's leverage post-Salix remains stretched at 4.5x net debt to EBITDA, restricting its acquisition capacity post-Salix spin-off. If the cost of capital escalates further, key platforms like Ettiketto and Tornum will be unable to execute add-on acquisitions. This would stall the group's target of 15% annual EBITA per share growth. Consequently, the lack of M&A compounding would severely depress long-term earnings per share expansion.
28.
Diluted returns: The Ettiketto Group relies on a scalable platform to acquire lower-margin businesses and systematically improve them. Higher capital costs would severely undermine the incremental returns on these acquisitions. Consequently, the platform's historical 20% margin target would become financially unviable. This erosion of returns would hamper Ettiketto's ability to operate as a self-sufficient European platform.
29.
Depressed margins: Tornum Group is already suffering from the slowest European agricultural market in many years. If parent funding dries up due to blockholder changes, Tornum's internal restructuring and profitability restoration would be delayed. This funding constraint would suppress overall consolidated cash conversion. Therefore, any delay in industrial recovery would extend the earnings drag on the parent company.
30.
Upward spiral: High interest rates coupled with elevated debt would consume too much of Volati's operating cash flow. The company's average interest rate on bank loans was 4.4% in late 2024. Any upward refinancing spiral would prevent the group from reinvesting capital in existing platforms. Thus, the inability to refinance on favorable terms would directly threaten the group's financial covenant safety margins.

Debt Refinancing and Preference Pricing

31.
Delayed dividends: Volati's 1,603,774 preference shares require a fixed annual cash dividend of SEK 64 million. If earnings decay due to blocked acquisitions, the priority dividend would consume an unsustainable portion of cash flow. This pressure would force the board to postpone dividends under legal precautionary principles. Such a postponement would severely damage the company's capital market reputation and access.
32.
Structural locking: Under a de-leveraging delay, the preference share pricing would collapse to reflect a massive risk premium. The preference shares can be redeemed at SEK 625 after the tenth anniversary, but high leverage makes redemption highly unlikely. This structural locking would depress preference valuations further. Consequently, preference investors would be trapped in an illiquid instrument with dwindling coverage.
33.
Subtle indicators: Subtle changes in short-term trading liquidity and index weights serve as early indicators of institutional rotation. Volati's tiny Carnegie Small Cap Index weight of 0.05% reflects a lack of passive support. Any further compression in daily volumes would signal that blockholders are starting to exit. Investors must monitor these trading volumes to detect early signs of a broader sell-off.
34.
Other side of the story: While sudden blockholder divestments would cause short-term market distress, Volati's underlying decentralised model provides robust operational insulation across platforms. The stable cash flows from platforms like Ettiketto and Communication should continue to cover the fixed preference dividends. Over the long term, organic deleveraging from 3.9x down to historical levels would restore institutional appetite and stabilize the capital structure. Therefore, the business can withstand temporary ownership shifts without experiencing fundamental operational distress.
