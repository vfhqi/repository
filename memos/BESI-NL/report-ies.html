BE Semiconductor (BESI-NL): what the third-quarter 2026 results do to the existing case

These results weaken the case as it was rated, materially: the fulcrum driver (logic hybrid bonding) came through stronger than the case assumed, but the memory leg has been pushed out, the earnings-upgrade pattern the case was built on has stalled, and the two momentum ratings no longer describe the stock. The one large offset is that the case's central objection, the price paid, has largely gone.

1. What changed

  • Delivery was in line to ahead. Third-quarter 2026 revenue was EUR 283.4m against consensus of EUR 283.1m. Orders were EUR 342.6m, up 17.0% on the second quarter and 11% ahead of consensus of EUR 308.7m. Backlog reached a record EUR 513m, up 112% year on year. Gross margin was 65.2% against consensus of 64.3%; operating margin 45.8% against 44.5%; basic earnings per share EUR 1.33 against EUR 1.31. Net cash rose to EUR 246.6m.
  • The guide was below consensus. Fourth-quarter revenue is guided flat, plus or minus 5%, against consensus of EUR 294.7m, because certain advanced-packaging customers asked for delivery in the first half of 2027. Gross margin is guided at 63% to 65% (consensus 64.8%) and operating expenses up 5% to 10% on EUR 55.1m, which Morgan Stanley puts at an operating-profit miss of about 7%.
  • Memory was pushed out. Memory makers de-specified certain high-bandwidth-memory products to lower stack heights, which keeps thermo-compression bonding in use. The review puts the delay at twelve to eighteen months. The case carried this as a risk it was tracking, with first-adopter qualification expected around June to July 2026. That date has passed without it.
  • Hybrid bonding outside memory broadened. Year-to-date hybrid-bonding orders are more than double the 2025 total; customers stand at 23 and cumulative demand above 215 systems. The case had 20 customers. Co-packaged optics is expanding faster than the company expected.
  • Two things the case did not have. Applied Materials agreed to lift its stake from 9% to about 20% by buying roughly 9 million shares from existing holders, subject to US antitrust clearance, with no board seat sought. And the long-term revenue model is EUR 1.7bn to 2.2bn with a 45% to 55% operating margin, against the EUR 1.5bn to 1.9bn and 40% to 55% the case used.
  • The sell-side split. Bernstein removed memory hybrid bonding from 2027, cut 2027 earnings per share to EUR 6.20 from EUR 6.70 and 2028 to EUR 8.68 from EUR 9.50, and cut its target to EUR 280 from EUR 320. Morgan Stanley downgraded to Equal-weight with a EUR 170 target. Deutsche Bank cut its target to EUR 220 from EUR 240 but raised 2027 earnings per share to EUR 7.18 and 2028 to EUR 8.98. Redburn held Buy at EUR 250. Targets fell everywhere; estimates did not.
  • The price. The shares were 147.0 on 9 November 2026, against the case's entry at EUR 273.10, and 31.1% below the 200-day average. The price-to-earnings ratio is 17.4x, at the 35th percentile of its own ten-year history. The case was capped by 42.9x at the 100th percentile.

2. The challenge

Reading one: the memory leg is smaller, not later. The case holds memory as a delay risk. The stronger reading is that the shortage has shown memory makers they can ship without hybrid bonding, and that the economics give them reason to keep doing so. The review carries the evidence: cost per bond has to fall to roughly 73 cents for volume memory adoption, which needs about a doubling of productivity; wafer-to-wafer bonding is estimated at 11 cents per bond and a mixed architecture would halve the number of die-to-wafer bonds; memory makers prefer to dual-source and local Korean suppliers are qualifying, with Bernstein putting the company's eventual memory share at 70%. Most telling, a leading memory customer ordered both hybrid-bonding and thermo-compression tools from the company in the quarter. Management reads that as commitment. It reads equally well as a customer keeping the cheaper route open. For this to be right, sixteen-high stacks would have to reach volume on thermo-compression bonding and Samsung's custom base-die insertion in 2027 would have to slip or stay small. If it is right, the raised long-term model is the thing at risk, because the sub-micron part of it (EUR 0.6bn to 1.1bn) is where memory sits.

Reading two: the record backlog is the first sign of digestion, not proof of demand. The case treats bookings as its cleanest evidence. But the same quarter that produced record orders produced a flat guide, because logic customers asked for later delivery to match fab readiness. Orders that customers do not yet want delivered are a weaker signal than orders they are pulling in. The review itself names this as the first open question: whether the delayed shipments convert in the first half of 2027 or are postponed again. For this reading to be right, deferrals would have to extend past mid-2027, which is plausible if hyperscaler capital spending is trimmed or if data-centre projects slip, as the review reports about 20% of planned 2028 US projects doing. Against it: management reports no sign of digestion and expects order momentum to continue into the fourth quarter, and the leading foundry customer's expansion is described as on track. We rate this possible rather than probable, but the case has no place for it, and with memory out of 2027 models there is no second driver to absorb it.

Reading three, which cuts the other way: this is no longer the case that was rated. The memo's load-bearing tension was franchise quality against price paid. The franchise evidence is intact or better on the fulcrum driver, and the price has nearly halved. With memory removed from 2027 estimates and targets cut, part of the sell-side reset the case said was absent has now happened. That is closer to a change-from-current clearing setup than to the momentum-backed upgrade setup the case identified. For this to be right, 2027 and 2028 estimates have to hold near where Deutsche Bank and Bernstein now bracket them without memory. The Applied Materials purchase supports it: the best-informed industrial party doubled its holding after the fall. The caution is that the same purchase puts a large programmed buyer in the market for a period, so the current price is not a clean read of independent demand for the shares.

We do not put up the ASML entry rumour as a reading. Management rebutted it, the patents described point to wafer-to-wafer methods, and nothing in the evidence supports it.

3. What it does to the case

  • MR1 (Technical momentum), set at B: far more pressure. The pinned letter is now F, with relative strength at the 5th percentile against the case's 97th.
  • MR2 (Sell-side earnings momentum), set at B: far more pressure. The pinned letter is now D. Target prices are down 17% over one month; the buy share is unchanged at 57%.
  • E1.RA1 (Change forces present), set at A: the same on logic and optics, more pressure on the memory component.
  • E1.RA2 (Robust base), set at C: less pressure. Its cap was the 100th-percentile multiple, and the low-trading-multiple question graded F on that basis. Neither description holds at 17.4x and the 35th percentile. Against that, the headwinds question takes more pressure, because the memory delay it listed as a risk has occurred.
  • E8.RA2 (Big demand change-driven EPSU), the primary setup, set at B: more pressure. Demand is there; the upgrades have stopped, and brokers disagree on direction.
  • E8.RA3 (Strong corporate change), set at B: less pressure, on the raised model, the new EUR 100m buyback and the larger Applied Materials stake.
  • E8.RA5 (Huge change-from-current clearing event), set at C: less pressure, per reading three.
  • E11.RA2 (Competitive supportiveness or aggression), set at C: slightly more pressure. Shibaura now offers a complete solution that has been evaluated at TSMC, and the review puts the company's share at about 70% to 80%, against the case's 90%. We cannot tell whether those are like-for-like. The case's competitive reversal trigger, confirmed production allocation to a rival inside TSMC, has not been met: an expert call on 22 October 2026 reports no qualified second source there.
  • E7.RA5 (Conservative investor relations), set at B: the same. Gross margin again beat the guide, so a soft fourth-quarter guide is consistent with the pattern.
  • The valuation reversal trigger (the case parks if 42.9x already prices the full target range) is under much less pressure.

4. What would settle it

  • Fourth-quarter results, expected January 2027. They should show whether deferred deliveries are converting on the first-half 2027 schedule, which bears directly on reading two. They are also the date by which Morgan Stanley says volume memory evidence must be visible.
  • Memory orders. The review's marker is memory bookings above EUR 150m in the fourth quarter of 2026 or the first quarter of 2027. We see no commitment from the company to disclose orders by memory customer, so this may have to be inferred. That makes reading one harder to close than it looks.
  • Samsung's custom base-die qualification and volume production by mid-2027. This is the cleanest test of smaller against later, and it sits with a customer, not the company.
  • The TSMC evaluation of Shibaura. Nobody has committed to disclosing the outcome. This is an open question nothing scheduled will answer.
  • US antitrust clearance for the Applied Materials purchase, and the end of the purchase window, after which the price is a cleaner read.
  • The second review, four working days on. It will carry any further broker estimate and target changes and a more settled consensus for 2027 and 2028, which bears on reading three and on MR2. It will not carry any new company disclosure on memory or on deliveries. The main broker responses are already in hand, so we expect it to refine the picture, not change it.

  • Moves: MR1 (Technical momentum) set at B, pinned letter now F, down; MR2 (Sell-side earnings momentum) set at B, pinned letter now D, down; E1.RA2 (Robust base) set at C, under upward pressure as the case's 42.9x, 100th-percentile cap no longer holds at 17.4x and the 35th percentile
