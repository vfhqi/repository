[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = D

Thesis. Addlife is a self-help case that has largely delivered and now asks to be re-classified as a compounder. At SEK 170.7 the shares already pay for the deal pace consensus models, so the 'Modal return' is about 9 percent a year over three years against the framework's 20 percent bar; the case becomes compelling at about SEK 130 to 145, not here.

  • The change is internal, and it is real.
    • A 2022 change of chief executive and finance director turned a leveraged post-pandemic distributor into a disciplined one, and the outputs show it.
    • Margin: adjusted EBITA margin 11.3 percent (FY2024) to 12.1 percent (FY2025, excluding the SEK 158m divestment gain) to 12.6 percent (Q2 2026)
      • ten consecutive quarters of expansion.
    • Leverage: net debt to EBITDA 4.2x (2023 peak) to 3.2x (end-2024) to 2.2x (end-2025), on internally generated cash.
    • Exits: Camanio closed, AddVision's headquarters dismantled, the UK endoscopy contract sold on 1 December 2025 for SEK 158m against SEK 140m of annual sales.
  • The change has travelled all six layers of the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain, which is why the market has priced it.
    • It shows in the people (a new executive pair, a refreshed board, an expanded central deal team), the choices (margin before volume; niche deals below EUR 50m at below 7x EV/EBITDA), the actions (the exits, the pruning, five bolt-ons between December 2025 and September 2026), the key operating metrics (the margin; return on working capital 62 percent in 2025), the financial statements (FY2025 operating cash flow SEK 1,392m, +27 percent; H1 2026 EPS SEK 2.11 against SEK 1.80, +17 percent) and the sell-side's estimates (an upgrade to Buy in February 2026; estimates up 1 to 3 percent after the Q2 2026 beat).
    • Re-rating done: the shares moved from about SEK 151 before the Q4 2025 results of 3 February 2026 to SEK 164.90 by 20 April 2026 and SEK 170.7 now, +26.4 percent over six months
      • the price has moved with the news, not against it.
  • The external forces are a modest net tailwind, not an inflection.
    • European Medtech grows about 3 percent a year and clinical diagnostics 2 to 3 percent
    • Addlife's organic growth was 3 percent in FY2025 and 4 percent in Q2 2026.
    • The strongest external force, MDR and IVDR compliance pushing small owners to sell, feeds the acquisition pipeline rather than demand
    • currency, Spanish doctors' strikes and UK NHS capital budgets are live headwinds.
  • 'Financial Outputs' are improving, but at a rate consensus already carries.
    • On our modal path adjusted EBITA grows about 10 percent a year over FY2026 to FY2028 against the company's 15 percent target
    • EPS on the post-amortisation basis grows about 18 percent a year, but roughly 7 percentage points of that come from a fixed amortisation charge, falling interest and a stable tax rate, the part the framework says investors do not extrapolate.
    • Our EPS sits within about 3 percent of consensus in every year, so upgrades of a few percent are possible and material upgrades are unlikely.
  • The price assumes the pattern's payoff has already arrived.
    • At 27.9x 24-month-forward EPS (dashboard, 2026-09-23), about 18x EV to FY2026 adjusted EBITA, the shares pay for a 12.5 to 13 percent margin and a deal pace near the 6 to 8 a year the models carry
    • consensus books "Future M&A" revenue of SEK 38m, 426m and 968m for FY2026 to FY2028.
    • Basis break: the consensus EPS basis changed during 2026 from an EBITA-based figure to a post-amortisation one, so the 88th-percentile placing overstates the premium
      • like for like on the EBITA basis the stock is at about 19.6x against its ten-year median of 21.70x, roughly fair, while the only true listed peer, Asker, has de-rated to 17.2x.
  • The chain therefore holds to the accounts and breaks at the return.
    • With the like-for-like multiple held, which is 26x on the post-amortisation 24-month-forward EPS, the 'Modal return' is about 7 percent a year over eighteen months and about 9 percent a year over three years: earnings growth is the whole of it, the cash return adds about 1 percent a year, and the re-rating leg is slightly negative.
    • Other multiples: holding the post-amortisation multiple at 27.9x gives about 12 percent a year (a D)
      • the compounder branch at 30x gives about 14 to 18 percent a year (a C), and we rate that branch unlikely at about 14 percent
      • the de-rating branch at 20x gives about nil.
    • Drop-through: if acquisitions are debt-funded at a leverage that does not fall, EPS growth is about 13 percent a year rather than 18 and the three-year return about 6 percent a year
      • the below-EBITA half of the growth is the fragile half.

  • The case is therefore a sound business, priced.

    • The pattern is 'Major Corporate Change' at C: the inputs and the operating outputs fit, the payoff (major EPS trajectory upgrades) has not arrived, and the visible part has been re-rated once already.
    • The case clears the 20 percent bar below about SEK 128 at three years and SEK 144 at eighteen months on the modal multiple, so we set the entry level at about SEK 130 to 145.

  • Rating = D, because the materiality question fails on its last clause: the input forces are real but mature (C), the outputs are improving at a rate consensus carries (C), and the modal total shareholder return at SEK 170.7 is about 9 percent a year over three years (F), so the return binds and sets the level

    • not F, because the change is genuine, has travelled all six layers of the chain and gives a 20 percent a year case below about SEK 130 to 145
    • not C, because nothing in the forces or the outputs is strong enough to clear the bar at this price without a re-rating we cannot argue for.

  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.

  • The strongest case against the thesis is the "False Friend" the risk research puts first: the margin gains are tail-cutting and a cyclical Medtech recovery rather than structural improvement, the acquisitions are opportunistic and unintegrated (a former Head of M&A, 13 March 2024, and a former Vision Ophthalmology Group chief technology officer, 22 April 2022, both describe an integration-free model), and leverage re-rises when cash conversion slips, at which point the multiple contracts to 13x EV/adjusted EBITA and the price to about SEK 105 (the sell-side's bear range is SEK 100 to 150).
  • We concede three things: adjusted EBITA growth on our path is 10 percent a year, not 15
    • return on capital employed is 10 to 11 percent against Lifco's 20 to 25 percent
    • and two former executives, not one, describe the model as integration-free.
  • We hold that the outputs are real: ten quarters of margin expansion across both segments, leverage cut from 4.2x to 2.2x on cash the business generated, and cash conversion of about 100 percent of EBITA over the last twelve months are facts, not framing.
  • But the competing hypothesis wins on the price without needing to be right: at 27.9x the shares already assume the deal pace and the margin, so the case fails the bar even if the operations go to plan.

  • Addlife is a self-help case that has largely delivered and now asks to be re-classified as a compounder.
  • A 2022 change of chief executive and finance director turned a leveraged post-pandemic distributor into a disciplined one: the adjusted EBITA margin rose from 11.3 percent (FY2024) to 12.1 percent (FY2025) and 12.6 percent (Q2 2026), net debt to EBITDA fell from 4.2x (the 2023 peak) to 2.2x (end-2025) on internally generated cash, three loss-makers were closed, restructured or sold, and the acquisition engine restarted with six bolt-ons since April 2025 at multiples below 7x.
  • The change has travelled all six layers of the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain, from the two executives and the refreshed board, through the choice of margin before volume and the exits, to ten quarters of margin expansion, FY2025 operating cash flow of SEK 1,392m (+27 percent), H1 2026 EPS +17 percent, and sell-side estimates up 1 to 3 percent after Q2 2026.
  • What it has not done is produce the earnings growth the price now needs: adjusted EBITA grows about 10 percent a year on our path against a 15 percent target, and EPS on the post-amortisation basis grows about 18 percent a year, of which nearly half is falling interest and a fixed amortisation charge.
