Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. The evidence shows that the 2025-2026 rabies surge is real in end-market terms, but it does not show how much of it will last. Rabipur/RabAvert revenue grew by 39% in the first nine months of 2026, and the US rabies market grew by 36%, with the company holding a 79% share. Independent public-health indicators point the same way. The drivers that would make the surge structural (pharmacy access, low pre-exposure penetration) are plausible but only partly verified. The drivers that would make it transient (localized wildlife outbreaks, alertdriven awareness, wholesaler stocking) are better documented. Overall confidence is medium, which matches the report's own overall rating. The central uncertainty is whether 2027 normalizes toward historical growth or holds a higher base. That matters because high operating leverage ties the EPS path to rabies volumes.

Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The mid-term EPS trajectory of Bavarian Nordic depends on whether the 2026 travel rabies market surge represents a permanent expansion of pre-exposure vaccine uptake or a temporary stocking event.

Defining the Key Question

1.
Primary inquiry: "Bavarian Nordic A/S (BAVA-DK), a Danish specialty vaccine maker with two segments: Public Preparedness (the smallpox and mpox vaccine JYNNEOS / Imvanex / Imvamune and related government work, sold mainly to US BARDA, France, the European Commission's HERA, Canada and other governments) and Travel Health (Rabipur/RabAvert rabies, Encepur tick-borne encephalitis, Vimkunya chikungunya, Vivotif typhoid and Vaxchora cholera, sold mainly out of pocket in the US and Europe), with bulk manufacturing at Kvistgaard (Denmark) and a second site in Bern (Switzerland). How much of the growth in US and German rabies vaccine markets in 2025-2026 (Bavarian Nordic reports US market growth of 20% in the first half and 36% in the first nine months of 2026, German growth of 16-22%, and growth of 55% in European markets outside Germany) came from post-exposure against pre-exposure use, from animal rabies incidence, from awareness campaigns, from pharmacy-chain access, from wholesaler stocking, and from price against volume; what do US animal rabies surveillance data, CDC enquiry data and pharmacy dispensing data show for late 2026; and how did rabies vaccine demand behave after earlier awareness-driven surges?"

Why the Question Matters

2.
Strong margin impact: Bavarian Nordic expects that completing the Rabipur manufacturing transfer from GlaxoSmithKline to Kvistgaard will yield a 15 to 20 percentage point gross margin improvement by 2027. Jefferies reported on October 8, 2026, that the upgraded full-year 2026 EBITDA margin of 32% reflects 68% operating leverage on incremental travel sales. Consequently, if the recent rabies market growth is structural rather than temporary wholesaler stocking, this operating leverage will secure elevated Travel Health margins over the next three years.
3.
Severe operational constraints: Bavarian Nordic reported that Q3 2026 other production costs rose to DKK 259 million due to Kvistgaard manufacturing disruptions from a planned campaign switch between Encepur and Rabipur. DNB Carnegie noted on November 16, 2026, that Encepur declined 14% year-over-year because of these supply constraints and campaign shifts. If rabies demand remains elevated, constant capacity shifts will likely continue to trigger elevated scrap rates and supply deficits for Encepur.
4.
Surging public exposure: Kamada reported on September 17, 2026, that a CDC health advisory highlighted a significant nationwide year-over-year increase in human rabies exposures and post-exposure prophylaxis use. Since PEP treatments require co-administration of vaccines and immunoglobulins, this epidemiological trend has directly accelerated the retail volume of RabAvert. Consequently, tracking US surveillance data is essential to assessing the immediate run-rate of Travel Health sales.
5.
Volatile EPS outlook: DNB Carnegie on November 16, 2026, modeled Bavarian Nordic adjusted EPS at DKK 13.4 in 2026e, DKK 9.8 in 2027e, and DKK 11.6 in 2028e. This projected trajectory relies heavily on the sustained growth of Rabipur to mitigate the inherent volatility of the high-margin biodefense division. If the 2026 rabies surge rapidly decelerates, the company will struggle to achieve its long-term profit targets.
6.
Other side of the story: Jefferies argued on October 8, 2026, that the historical discount of Bavarian Nordic is primarily driven by the volatility of the high-margin Public Preparedness segment. Therefore, even if Travel Health rabies vaccine growth decelerates, the overall EPS trajectory will remain dominated by binary government biodefense contract timing and potential strategic acquisitions rather than incremental retail travel demand.

  1. Bavarian Nordic achieved exceptional rabies sales growth in 2026, primarily driven by animal exposure events in the United States and clinical transitions.
  2. The company optimized its pricing power and manufacturing margins, but faced severe operational friction at Kvistgaard during planned production switches.

Clinical Drivers of Rabies Sales Growth

1.
Strong acceleration: Bavarian Nordic reported that Rabipur/RabAvert revenue grew by 39% in the first nine months of 2026. This rapid expansion was driven by a combination of continued market growth and strong commercial execution. The overall US rabies vaccine market grew by 36% in the first nine months, allowing the company to maintain its dominant 79% market share on par with the prior year.
2.
Divergent dynamics: Bavarian Nordic rabies demand exhibits highly distinct geographic drivers between its key Western markets. Jefferies reported on August 26, 2020, that travel restrictions during lockdowns heavily impacted pre-exposure European sales, whereas US demand was much more resilient because over 50% of US volume is designated for post-exposure emergency treatments. Consequently, the US segment remains highly insulated from travel declines, while European markets fluctuate based on tourist activity.
3.
Epidemiological tailwinds: Jefferies highlighted on October 8, 2026, that Bavarian Nordic raised its full-year Travel Health revenue guidance to DKK 3,400 million. This upgrade was primarily driven by higher rabies vaccine sales in the United States. Management attributed this demand surge to increased animal rabies activity, which led to a significant rise in human exposure and a corresponding increase in both pre- and postexposure vaccinations.

Pricing Power and Manufacturing Transfers

4.
Improved margins: Bavarian Nordic finalized its five-year technology transfer for Rabipur/RabAvert from GlaxoSmithKline to its own manufacturing facilities in 2024. This strategic relocation allows the company to ensure a reliable vaccine supply while driving substantial product gross margin improvements starting in 2026. Consequently, bringing manufacturing in-house provides the firm with strong operating leverage to accelerate its earnings per share growth.

Robust monetization: Bavarian Nordic achieved strong commercial momentum in mid-2024 as Rabipur and Encepur sales surpassed expectations. This rapid growth triggered a sales milestone of DKK 186 million under the original 2019 asset purchase agreement with GlaxoSmithKline. The company capitalized this milestone as an addition to product rights, demonstrating its ability to extract deep value from its acquired travel portfolio.
6.
Contrary evidence: German rabies market growth has historically suffered from severe volatility due to localized supply chain disruptions. Bavarian Nordic reported that the German market dropped by 27% in the first quarter of 2024. This sharp decline was entirely driven by a temporary stockout of both Rabipur and competitor products, demonstrating that supply bottlenecks can severely restrict short-term volume growth regardless of underlying demand.

Manufacturing Switches and Inventory Dynamics

7.
Operational friction: Bavarian Nordic experienced significant manufacturing disruptions at its Kvistgaard facility in the third quarter of 2026. Other production costs rose to DKK 259 million in the third quarter of 2026 compared to DKK 210 million in the prior year period. Management stated that these elevated expenses were primarily caused by production interruptions from a planned campaign switch from TBE to rabies manufacturing.

Negative feedback: DNB Carnegie reported on November 16, 2026, that Encepur revenues declined by 14% year- over-year in the third quarter to DKK 123 million. This contraction highlights how manufacturing campaign switches at Kvistgaard directly drew down wholesaler stock levels for tick-borne encephalitis vaccines. Consequently, prioritizing rabies production during high-demand surges creates negative revenue spillover effects for other travel products.

Competitive Landscape and Peer Comparisons

9.
Competitive threat: Sanofi announced on November 19, 2026, that the European Commission approved VaxRabeo for both pre-exposure and post-exposure rabies prophylaxis. This new vaccine is manufactured using advanced Vero-cell technology and is designed to minimize allergic reactions. It offers improved thermostability and longer shelf life than current Sanofi rabies shots, posing a direct threat to Bavarian Nordic's European market dominance.
10.
Intensifying rivalry: Sanofi's clinical program for VaxRabeo demonstrated comparable protective antibody levels to its established Verorab and Imovax Rabies vaccines. The vaccine also showed high boostability of the booster dose administered up to 36 months after pre-exposure prophylaxis. If Sanofi successfully commercializes VaxRabeo across Europe, this state-of-the-art alternative could erode Bavarian Nordic's pricing power.
11.
55 56 57 6K Other side of the story: Bavarian Nordic maintains a highly resilient competitive moat, boasting a 97% German market share in the first nine months of 2026. Therefore, even with Sanofi's launch of VaxRabeo, Bavarian Nordic's entrenched distributor networks and strong brand recognition are highly likely to protect its long-term travel segment earnings.

Section 5 – Historical Demand Behavior and Post-Surge Precedents

Section summary
  1. Historical vaccine surges are frequently followed by sharp demand normalization or distributor destocking as emergency alerts and public awareness stabilize.
  2. Bavarian Nordic faces significant sustainability risks for Rabipur into 2027 based on past travel vaccine normalization curves and rising competitive pressures.

Bavarian Nordic reported in 2023 that Rabipur/RabAvert sales grew 74% to DKK 879 million in 2022 due to a rapid travel recovery and market expansion. However, Jefferies noted in September 2020 that travel vaccine growth typically stabilizes at a modest 3% long-term rate once immediate demand surges subside. This indicates that the 2026 rabies demand surge could decelerate to historical single-digit growth as public exposure alerts normalize.
2.
Lockdown disruptions: Rapid normalization occurred in past periods of supply-driven stocking and travel disruption. Bavarian Nordic reported that Covid-19 restrictions severely impacted pre-exposure rabies sales in Europe, while US post-exposure sales remained resilient. Jefferies reported in 2021 that lockdowns created prolonged headwinds for travel product volumes, demonstrating how quickly public demand shifts can interrupt sales growth. This historic precedent suggests that travel-driven rabies vaccine volumes can contract sharply after temporary surges as consumer priorities shift.
3.
Outbreak dependencies: Volatile trends characterize the international distribution of specialty vaccines during regional outbreaks. Bavarian Nordic reported that Vaxchora sales decreased by 29% in the first nine months of 2026 due to the niche, outbreak-driven nature of the cholera market. Management noted that Vaxchora previously spiked during a localized cholera outbreak on the island of Mayotte in 2024 before normalizing. This epidemiological cycle proves that specialty travel revenues remain highly dependent on transient events rather than sustained structural growth.

Contrary evidence: Bavarian Nordic management expects that the Travel Health segment will maintain a strong long-term organic growth rate of 10% to 12% from a higher base. Jefferies reported in October 2026 that the underlying rabies vaccine market grew by 20% to 25% in the third quarter of 2026. This commercial momentum suggests that expanded pharmacy clinic footprint and direct awareness campaigns can prevent a sudden demand cliff in 2027.

Portfolio Normalization and Supply Dynamics

This pattern demonstrates that distributor stocking patterns can distort actual in-market consumption data during vaccine rollouts.
