Section 1 – Key question and brief

Section summary
  1. The key question explores why European exchanges, despite successfully transitioning into predictable subscription-driven models, continue to trade at significant valuation multiple discounts relative to global information services peers.

Core Thesis and Mechanism

1.

2.

3.

Section 3 – Pricing power and contract structures

Section summary
  1. European exchanges command strong recurring pricing power through highly proprietary, deeply embedded datasets, though annual price increases remain constrained by regulatory scrutiny and competitive pressure

Contract Designs and Price Caps

1.
Stable pricing: LSEG's annual report published on 27 March 2025 notes that standard subscription agreements are structured as enterprise-wide contracts with one to two year durations featuring annual price reviews on 1 January.
2.
CPI indexation: Morgan Stanley argues in its 6 August 2025 report that LSEG's strong market position supports regular price reviews, driving corporate confidence in delivering pricing uplifts of at least three percent annually.
3.
Benchmark increases: A director at S&P Global Market Intelligence stated in a 6 September 2025 expert interview that enterprise clients are actively scrutinizing software returns on investment in response to historical increases up to seven percent by global benchmark providers.
4.
Bloomberg baseline: Rothschild & Co Redburn's 11 December 2024 report highlights that Bloomberg implements a blanket terminal price increase of six point five percent every two years to track inflation on a lag.
5.
Indexation limits: Rothschild & Co Redburn's 11 December 2024 report notes that competitive pressures and client budget constraints typically limits price increases above inflation for traditional front-end terminal products.

Proprietary Scarcity versus Commoditization

6.
Unreplicable feeds: LSEG's annual report published on 11 March 2026 highlights that approximately ninety percent of data revenues are secured by highly proprietary datasets and exclusive real-time feeds that cannot be replicated.
7.
Embedded workflows: Morgan Stanley's 3 September 2026 report argues that highly proprietary financial datasets retains durable strategic value even as the physical interface layer faces structural disintermediation.
8.
Commoditizable feeds: Rothschild & Co Redburn's 18 June 2026 report argues that non-real-time public datasets face direct pricing pressure as artificial intelligence lowers replication costs for generic information aggregators.
9.
Euronext defensibility: Deutsche Bank's 17 June 2026 report notes that Euronext is insulated from synthetic data risks because its revenues are heavily overweight proprietary real-time data generated directly by its own exchange venues.
10.
Subscription sustainability: Euronext's head of data Nicolas Rivard stated in a 31 July 2026 earnings call that the extreme vast majority of revenue within Advanced Data Solutions is recurring and subscriptionbased.

Regulatory Frictions and Repricing Windows

11.

Exchange fee pressure: Deutsche Boerse's prospectus dated 30 April 2026 warns that aggressive competitor actions could force lower subscription fees for proprietary data on core platforms like Xetra.
13.
Repricing windows: Morgan Stanley's 3 September 2026 report suggests that the window to implement hybrid and usage-based models will unfold gradually through 2028 as corporate client procurement processes adapt.
