[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = C

Thesis. Croda is a good business whose margin fell from 24.8 percent in 2021 to 17.2 percent in 2024 because destocking emptied its eleven shared plants while its cost base and a pharma capacity build ran ahead of sales, and that cause for concern is clearing: half through £100m of cost savings the company controls, half through plant utilisation it does not. At 3236p the shares already pay for the recovery to a 20 percent margin, so the change is material to the earnings and not, at this price, to the return.

  • The change is internal and largely controllable, and it is evidenced in delivery rather than in slides.
    • A new chief financial officer (Stephen Oxley, from 1 April 2025) upscaled the efficiency programme from £40m to £100m of annualised savings by the end of 2027
    • £28m was delivered in FY2025, £18m in H1 2026, £46m cumulatively.
    • Beside the savings sit minimum order values, the CrodaOn portal now serving 18 percent of customers, 30 percent of SKUs reviewed, globalised procurement, finance shared services, and capital expenditure cut to 4.9 percent of sales in H1 2026.
    • The 2026 bonus is 90 percent on constant-currency adjusted operating profit and the share plan carries a 25 percent return-on-invested-capital weighting, so the incentives point the same way as the programme.
  • The external forces net to a mild tailwind with wide dispersion, and none of them is a sector-level demand explosion.
    • Prestige beauty demand is strong (Beauty Actives +27 percent in Q2 2026) and crop destocking has ended (Crop Protection +5 percent in Q2 2026 after −8 percent in Q1).
    • Against that, Pharma Solutions fell 17 percent in H1 2026 with the recovery guided on order books rather than shown, Fragrances & Flavours slowed to 8 percent from mid-teens, and sterling is a £20m headwind to FY2026 profit.
  • The outputs have turned, at a moderate pace from a low base.
    • The adjusted operating margin went 17.2 percent in FY2024, 17.4 percent in FY2025, 17.7 percent in H1 2026
    • adjusted earnings per share rose 9 percent in H1 2026
    • shared-site utilisation is 93 percent of the 2019 baseline.
    • The sell-side's downgrade cycle ended in February 2026 and every one of the four named brokers raised its target after the H1 2026 results, but the estimate series itself is flat (+0.7 to +1.0 percent over three months), because consensus already sits at the company's FY2028 target.
    • Cash is the output that has not turned: H1 2026 free cash flow of £38.3m against £68m consensus after a £67.8m working-capital outflow, with net working capital at 30.9 percent of trailing revenue, the highest in the block's eleven periods.
  • The change has travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain on its self-help half and only part-way on its volume half.
    • New people, new decisions and new actions show in utilisation, savings and margin, in the financial statements, and in a sell-side that has stopped cutting.
    • The volume half stops at the operating metrics: utilisation is 7 points short of the target and Pharma Solutions is still falling.
  • What we think it earns is about 9.6 percent a year, below the framework's 12 to 20 percent band.
    • Our modal path takes revenue up about 4.5 percent a year and the margin to about 19.8 percent by FY2028, a little below the company's 'above 20 percent' and consensus' 20.4 percent, giving adjusted earnings per share of about £1.92 in FY2028 against £1.46 in FY2025
    • consensus is £1.98.
    • The growth is of good quality, margin and mix rather than tax or one-offs, but only the FY2027 year (+11 to 13 percent) is inside the band.
  • What it costs is the problem: the price already carries the 20 percent margin.
    • At 3236p the shares stand at 17.0 times the dashboard's 24-month-forward consensus earnings of £1.90 (the financials block reads 16.85 times) and 17.2 times our own path's £1.88.
    • That is cheap on the long history (the 12th percentile of ten years; a ten-year median of 22.6 times) and median on the short (the 55th percentile of the last two years, median 16.56 times), because the market has priced Croda as a lower-margin, more volatile business for two years.
  • The 'Modal return' is about 11 percent a year, a D against the 20 percent bar, and the multiple is the swing factor.
    • On a multiple blended at 40 percent odds of a re-rating to 20 times and 60 percent of staying at 16 times (17.6 times), applied to the earnings two years beyond each horizon, the total return is about +17.1 percent over eighteen months (11.1 percent a year) and +32.5 percent over three years (9.8 percent a year).
    • The cash return carries about 3.5 percent a year and is predictable
      • earnings growth carries about 10 percent over eighteen months on the 24-month-forward base
      • the re-rating carries about 3.5 percent on the blend.
    • On the cleared branch alone (20 times) the eighteen-month return is 20.7 percent a year, a B
      • on the uncleared branch (16 times) it is 4.5 percent a year, an F.
    • A plausibly worse drop-through from EBIT to earnings per share (a further £10m of sterling translation, a £10m depreciation step-up, £25m of finance costs, a 27 percent tax rate) takes FY2028 earnings per share to about £1.76, the modal return to about 5.2 percent a year (F) and the cleared branch to about 14.3 percent a year (C).
      • The case has no margin of safety in its drop-through at this price.
  • The pattern is 'OSHQC CfC Clearing', crisply in mechanism and less crisply in timing.
    • A moat that held the customers through the downturn, a named concern, hard clearing evidence and a multiple at the bottom of its long range
    • but the market has been buying the clearing for eight months and the concerns number three, one over the pattern's limit.
  • Where the chain holds and where it breaks.
    • It holds from the new chief financial officer through the savings into the margin and the end of the downgrade cycle.
    • It breaks in two places: the drop-through from volume to margin, where divisional EBIT was 14 percent below consensus in H1 2026 and volume in 2025 was bought with price (price/mix −3.0 percent)
    • and the price, which already pays for the 20 percent margin.

  • The case therefore is a genuine, well-evidenced margin recovery in a good business, priced for most of its recovery.

    • It clears the framework's bar only on a re-rating we rate possible (about 40 percent), or at a price about 11 percent lower (about 2,880p on the eighteen-month horizon).

  • Rating = C, because the change is material to the earnings but not, at this price, to the return: the internal forces are strong and evidenced ('Change Forces' at B), the outputs are improving at a moderate pace with consensus already at the company's target ('Financial Outputs' at C), and the 'Modal return' on our path is about 11 percent a year ('Modal return' at D)

    • the parts bind through the return leg, which the price sets.
    • Not B because the return leg fails the 20 percent bar on the modal path and clears it only on the cleared branch of the multiple
    • not D because the forces are real, the earnings growth of about 10 percent a year is of good quality and the cleared branch is a live possibility at about 40 percent.

  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.

  • The strongest counter-case is Deutsche Bank's and Jefferies': the margin recovery is volume-dependent and Croda has bought volume with price (price/mix −3.0 percent in FY2025, single-digit cuts in the least-differentiated 20 percent of Beauty Care), so the drop-through from filling the shared sites is low, the 20 percent margin is reached late or not at all, and the shares at 17 times earnings that assume it are fairly valued.
  • We concede the drop-through evidence: divisional EBIT 14 percent below consensus in H1 2026, and organic sales +7 percent with flat EBIT margins in 2025.
  • We hold our view on the half of the thesis that does not need volume: the £100m programme is delivering on schedule (£46m cumulative by H1 2026) and is independent of utilisation, and H1 2026 showed the mix lever (Consumer Care EBIT 6 percent ahead of consensus, Beauty Actives +27 percent in Q2).
  • The counter-case is why our modal margin is 19.8 percent and not 20.4 percent, and why the multiple is blended rather than cleared.
  • It does not overturn the direction
    • it lowers the return.

  • This is a cause-for-concern-clearing case in a high-quality business, not a compounder and not a demand inflection.
  • Croda's adjusted operating margin fell from 24.8 percent in 2021 to 17.2 percent in 2024 because destocking emptied eleven shared manufacturing sites while the cost base and a pharma capacity build ran ahead of sales
    • the clearing is half self-help (a £100m savings programme, £46m delivered by H1 2026, run by a new chief financial officer) and half cyclical (utilisation back to 93 percent of the 2019 baseline, crop destocking over, premium beauty demand strong).

  • It is a good business, recovering, priced for most of its recovery.
  • The case clears the bar only on a re-rating we rate possible, or at a price about 11 percent lower.
