Standards

Qualification barriers and switching friction

1.
Rigid validation: Space systems designed for low-Earth orbit satellites require extremely rigorous component qualification that typically spans multiple years. Morgan Stanley reported on July 13, 2026, that satellite components must endure intense vibration, thermal vacuum, and radiation testing under temperature ranges from below -100°C to over 150°C. These demanding testing protocols mean that satellite manufacturers face years of qualification before deploying new materials. This slow adoption cycle delays Nolato's materials sales ramp but secures long-term contract lock-in.
2.
Onerous certifications: Aerospace and defense hardware OEMs maintain direct, multi-stage oversight of supplier manufacturing processes to ensure mission-critical material consistency. An expert from Northrop Grumman explained on December 24, 2025, that additional layers of qualification testing make defense programs highly unlikely to switch parts once specified. These strict regulatory and performance standards mean that Nolato enjoys unusually high customer retention rates in its specialized military shielding business. Consequently, defense revenue maintains strong visibility and protects long-term earnings per share.
3.
High barriers: Hyperscale data center operators require lengthy testing cycles before integrating new thermal interface materials into high-performance computing clusters. Rothschild & Co Redburn noted on May 26, 2026, that validation timelines for new GPU platforms at hyperscalers typically run between six and twelve months. An expert from Hermes Semiconductors added on May 1, 2026, that critical chip qualification can take up to 24 months. These extensive timelines create prohibitive switching costs for OEMs trying to replace established material suppliers like Nolato.

Technological shifts and cooling bypass risks

4.
Disrupted architectures: Emerging direct-to-chip liquid cooling standards threaten standard polymer demand by completely eliminating secondary thermal interface material layers. Jefferies reported on November 20, 2026, that next-generation chip designs like NVIDIA's Vera Rubin utilize three-piece removable lids that allow direct coldplate mounting. This structural shift bypasses the traditional TIM2 layer entirely, creating a severe technology risk for standard thermal material suppliers. If this architectural design becomes standardized across all AI accelerators, Nolato's traditional data center materials addressable market could contract significantly.
5.
Contrary evidence: Advanced direct liquid cooling systems cannot completely bypass specialized interface materials because of persistent mechanical flatness and surface roughness limitations. Sourcing firm MES reported on November 18, 2026, that copper cold plates still exhibit micro-voids and warping that require specialized thermal materials to prevent localized chip overheating. Bernstein Research added on July 5, 2026, that even high-performance cold plates rely on specialized thermal interface materials to fill microscopic gaps and ensure heat conduction. This technical reality ensures that high-power AI chips will continue to require advanced thermal polymer formulations.
6.
Other side of the story: While mechanical surface imperfections preserve a baseline need for thermal interface materials, the rapid commercialization of direct-to-die packaging represents an inevitable long-term threat to traditional thermal paste manufacturers. Bernstein Research noted on July 16, 2026, that direct-to-die systems route liquid coolant directly beneath high-power chip regions, completely eliminating the thermal resistance of traditional interface materials. If hyperscalers aggressively adopt these integrated semiconductor architectures over the next three years, Nolato's high-margin Materials division could face sudden structural demand erosion that jeopardizes its 12% group EBITA margin target.

This sequential relief allowed group margins to begin normalizing as the contract lags expired.
