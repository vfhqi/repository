Section 1 – Brief company description and history

1.
Strong leadership: Vitrolife operates as a leading player in reproductive health, developing and supplying advanced medical devices, culture media, and genetic testing solutions to assisted reproductive clinics worldwide. The company's comprehensive product portfolio is designed to assist clinics throughout every stage of the IVF journey to maximize clinical outcomes.
2.
Stable positioning: Vitrolife is positioned within the healthcare sector under the reproductive health and life sciences tools and services industries. Global market demand is supported by long-term structural tailwinds, including falling fertility rates and rising maternal age, which together support mid-single-digit global cycle growth annually.
3.
Pressured revenues: Vitrolife experienced a period of top-line pressure over the last three years, with net sales fluctuating in absolute terms. Group sales expanded from SEK 3,512 million in 2023 to SEK 3,609 million in 2024, representing a 3% growth. However, sales declined to SEK 3,440 million in 2025, which was heavily dragged down by severe foreign exchange headwinds during that year.

4.
Resilient margins: Profitability margins remained solid despite revenue fluctuations, showcasing the strong defensive characteristics of the core consumables and technology lines. The company reported an EBITDA margin of 32.3% in 2023, which expanded to 34.0% in 2024 due to operational efficiencies. The EBITDA margin then contracted to 27.6% in 2025, or 29.2% when adjusted to exclude genetic services restructuring costs, as currency headwinds and deliberate selling investments in North America diluted near-term margins.
5.
Depressed earnings: Net income was severely impacted by non-cash adjustments, leading to substantial volatility in reported earnings over the three-year context. Reported net income stood at SEK -3,851 million in 2023 due to an asset write-down, but recovered to SEK 514 million in 2024. The bottom line collapsed again to SEK -5,013 million in 2025 because of a massive goodwill impairment of SEK 5,357 million related to the Igenomix acquisition.
6.
Lagging performance: Peer comparison shows that Vitrolife struggled to match the steady performance of other women's health leaders. While Gedeon Richter achieved robust growth in focus brands in 2025, Vitrolife experienced underperformance due to delayed US reimbursement, the exit of sanctioned markets, and PGT- A litigation. Such operational divergence led to a significant valuation discount relative to peers, with Vitrolife trading far below historic medtech averages.
7.
Volatile returns: Stock price performance remained highly volatile and lagged the broader Swedish market index in the months leading to the Q1 2026 report. Share price trends over the five years from 2021 to 2025 highlight a severe multiple contraction, which left the stock trading at trough valuation multiples by early 2026. Such prolonged sell-off has suppressed shareholder returns even though core fertility market fundamentals remain robust.
8.
Downward revisions: Carnegie analyst Trattner reduced his target price over the six months prior to the Q1 2026 earnings release. The analyst maintained a buy rating but lowered his target price from SEK 230 in December 2025 to SEK 215 in February 2026. Target price reductions reflected currency-driven adjustments and severe near-term visibility constraints in China and the US.
9.
Undervalued operational: Carnegie argued that Vitrolife's trough valuation was completely disconnected from its underlying operational performance. The broker pointed out that the company traded at a 50% discount to Nordic medtech peers, which is unjustified given the non-cyclical nature of global IVF demand. Such a wide valuation gap provides an attractive entry point for investors as profit growth begins to recover.
10.
Conservative valuation: RedEye analyst Einarsson adjusted the company's valuation framework in the months preceding the Q1 update. The broker maintained a base case fair value of SEK 126 in late 2025, noting that restoring scalability in genetics remained a critical medium-term challenge. RedEye's base case valuation reflected high execution risk but still offered substantial upside from the prevailing trading price.
11.
Oligopolistic dominance: Expert commentary confirms that Vitrolife and CooperSurgical dominate the global IVF market as a highly consolidated duopoly. TMRW Life Sciences consultant noted in September 2024 that these two companies touch almost all global fertility laboratories, with culture media serving as a highly profitable recurring revenue engine for Vitrolife.
12.
Other side of the story: The company's optimistic recovery path could be severely disrupted if structural price pressure in genetic testing intensifies. While management anticipates high-single-digit organic growth, persistent macroeconomic pressures on out-of-pocket spending or macroeconomic weakness slowing cycle volumes would directly hit profitability. Such unfavorable conditions would prevent the anticipated multiple expansion, leaving the stock to trade at a substantial discount to Nordic medtech peers.
