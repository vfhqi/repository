This arrangement leverages a centralized shared-services model to handle the complex requirements of managing critical rig equipment and spares across the fleet. By utilizing this specialized provider, Odfjell Drilling maintains lean internal operations while securing consistent access to maritime logistics networks.

Section 16 – Conclusion

Odfjell Drilling entered the 2026-Q1 earnings period as a highly profitable market leader in the harsh-environment offshore drilling sector, and the first quarter results strongly validated this positioning. The company generated operating revenues of USD 284 million and an EBITDA of USD 154 million, which significantly exceeded the USD 204 million in revenue and USD 100 million in EBITDA recorded in the first quarter of 2025. This exceptional financial delivery was accompanied by a record EBITDA margin of 54% in Q1 2026, up from 49% in Q1 2025, demonstrating the company's ability to translate its premium fleet positioning into superior unit economics compared to global peers.

The single most important change identified across all sections of this report is the accelerating earnings power driven by the Deepsea Bollsta acquisition combined with sustained margin expansion. The acquisition of the rig owning company of Deepsea Bollsta, which was renamed Deepsea Bergen, for USD 480 million in late 2025 is projected to add approximately USD 100 million in annual EBITDA with very limited incremental capital expenditure required going forward.

When combined with the group's demonstrated ability to expand its EBITDA margin from 46.4% in 2022 to 50.5% in 2025 across the entire fleet, this acquisition represents a structural step-change in the company's cash generation capacity. Backed by robust earnings and low leverage, the company raised its Q1 2026 dividend to USD 0.25 per share, representing a 9% increase over the USD 0.23 paid in Q4 2025. This combination of fleet expansion, margin improvement, and accelerating shareholder distributions fundamentally alters the company's long-term earnings trajectory and positions it to deliver sustained double-digit dividend yields through the cycle.

However, the strongest counter-argument to this bullish primary conclusion centers on the company's extreme operational and geographic concentration risk. Any unforeseen event or operational downtime affecting just one rig can have an immediate and materially negative impact on the company's cash flows compared to larger, more diversified competitors. This vulnerability was starkly demonstrated when the blowout preventer on the Deepsea Atlantic sunk to the seabed in mid-April 2026, forcing the rig off-hire and introducing substantial timeline and capital expenditure uncertainty.

With only five owned units in active operation, each representing approximately 20% of the owned fleet's earning capacity, any single-unit disruption creates an immediate 20% reduction in near-term cash flow generation before insurance recoveries. Furthermore, the company's extreme regional concentration in Norway exposes its earnings to local regulatory shifts that larger global competitors can easily mitigate through geographic diversification.

Stricter Norwegian carbon taxes and potential licensing slowdowns could severely impact long-term rig demand, while larger global competitors can easily redeploy assets across different deepwater basins. If Norwegian operators scale back drilling activity in response to political or fiscal pressures, Odfjell Drilling's limited fleet size and regional focus would severely constrain its ability to maintain current utilization levels and dayrates.

Second, if Norwegian sixth-generation harsh-environment dayrates decline below USD 400,000 per day on a sustained basis, this would indicate that the regional supply-demand balance has shifted unfavorably and that the company's premium pricing power is eroding. Third, if the company's net debt-to-EBITDA leverage ratio rises above 2.5x for more than two quarters, this would suggest that management has abandoned its conservative capital structure discipline or that operational cash generation has deteriorated materially.

Fourth, if Equinor or Aker BP publicly announce a strategic pivot away from Norwegian shelf development in favor of international deepwater basins, this would undermine the long-term demand visibility that underpins the company's contract backlog. Fifth, if the company fails to secure at least one major contract renewal or extension by Q4 2027 as its current firm backlog begins to roll off, this would raise serious concerns about the company's ability to maintain fleet coverage during the next contracting cycle.

Finally, if quarterly sick leave rates remain above 6% for three consecutive quarters, this would indicate a structural deterioration in workforce health and morale that could drive up crewing costs and threaten operational reliability. Looking ahead to the next earnings disclosure cycle, two critical questions must be answered to validate the company's forward trajectory. First, what is the precise timeline and total capital cost for returning the Deepsea Atlantic to full operational service, and how will this incident impact the rig's long-term contract coverage and customer relationships?

The company has provided preliminary estimates of three to four months of downtime, but any extension beyond this window will directly reduce second and third quarter earnings and strain the progressive dividend narrative. Second, what concrete progress has management made in securing new contract awards or extensions for the owned fleet as firm backlogs begin rolling off in late 2027 and 2028, particularly in high-growth international markets like Namibia and Canada?

The company's strategic ambition to expand internationally is critical to offsetting any potential softening in Norwegian shelf activity, and tangible evidence of contract momentum in these emerging basins will be essential to maintaining investor confidence in the longterm growth story. Other side of the story: While Odfjell Drilling has successfully positioned itself as the premium harsh-environment drilling contractor with industry-leading margins, secured backlog, and a rapidly deleveraging balance sheet, the company's business model remains fundamentally exposed to the extreme cyclicality of offshore drilling activity and the political stability of its core Norwegian market.

The pure-play strategy leaves Odfjell Drilling completely exposed to the highly cyclical floater market, and unlike its previous integrated structure, the company can no longer rely on stable service-related margins to offset potential downturns in offshore drilling activity, which could severely pressure future earnings power if regional dayrates contract. If geopolitical tensions ease and Brent crude prices fall below USD 70 per barrel over the next 18 months, Norwegian operators will likely defer discretionary drilling campaigns, compress dayrate negotiations, and prioritize cost reduction over asset quality– all of which would directly threaten the company's ability to maintain its current premium pricing and near-perfect utilization rates.
