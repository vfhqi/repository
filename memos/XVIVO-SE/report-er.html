Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. XVIVO's Q3 2026 earnings delivered a decisive beat on sales and profitability, driven by exceptional thoracic momentum and accelerated regulatory pathways, positioning the company for substantial EPS acceleration over the next 18 to 36 months despite near-term headwinds from a major customer platform transition. XVIVO reported net sales of SEK 253 million for Q3 2026, representing a 34 percent year-on-year increase that exceeded the Modular Finance consensus estimate of SEK 238 million by 6 percent. The company achieved exceptional organic growth of 32 percent in local currencies, significantly surpassing pre-reporting consensus forecasts of 25 percent and demonstrating robust underlying commercial momentum across its multi-organ portfolio. Operating income reached SEK 28.0 million, beating published consensus by 22 percent, while EBITDA of SEK 52.0 million delivered a 14 percent beat against expectations and translated to a solid 21 percent margin. Consolidated earnings per share came in at SEK 1.42, representing a massive outperformance versus the

consensus expectation of SEK 0.54 per share, though this figure was materially influenced by positive tax effects and higher net financial income. The thoracic segment served as the primary engine of growth, generating SEK 177 million in sales with lung sales surging 67 percent organically. However, this exceptional performance was supported by two significant one-off items that will not recur with the same intensity: a SEK 8.5 million retrospective revenue recognition related to finance lease arrangements for XPS machines delivered to Organ Procurement Organizations, and a SEK 10 million inventory build-up by the company's largest US customer LungBio that is expected to weigh on fourth-quarter thoracic sales.

Adjusting for the retrospective OPO accounting adjustment, underlying Q3 sales were approximately SEK 244 million, which still represented a 5 percent beat versus RedEye's pre-report projection. The company's strategic pivot to a 510(k) regulatory pathway for US Liver Assist submission in the first half of 2027 represents a transformational shift that drastically accelerates market entry by bypassing the expensive and time-consuming DeLIVER premarket approval clinical trial.

This regulatory change enables a targeted US commercial launch by early 2028, providing clear visibility on abdominal segment market access and significantly de-risking the long-term growth story. The early CE certification for Heart Assist Transport in August 2026 removed a key regulatory overhang and enabled a controlled European rollout targeting 33 clinics across France, the Netherlands, Belgium, and Austria, with France already securing full nationwide reimbursement.

Despite these positive developments, the company faces material near-term headwinds from its largest US lung customer LungBio transitioning to its own ex vivo lung perfusion platform following regulatory approval in June

2026. Analysts estimate this transition will create a revenue drag of approximately 5 to 6 percent of projected

2027 group sales, though the impact is expected to be partially offset by the rapid expansion of the Organ Procurement Organization hub-and-spoke service model, which had three active OPOs in Q3 2026 with five more in the pipeline. Sales of ex vivo lung perfusion disposables to OPOs and transplant centers grew by 183 percent in the third quarter, demonstrating the commercial viability of this decentralized distribution strategy.

Overall gross margin contracted to 70 percent from 75 percent in the prior year's period, primarily dragged down by the services segment where gross margins plunged to negative 19 percent due to deliberate investments in expanding surgical capacity ahead of heart launches. Management emphasized that building out US commercial infrastructure for the heart, liver, and kidney platforms will likely put temporary pressure on margins in the second half of 2027, though the company maintains its ambitious target of achieving an EBITDA margin above 30 percent and EBIT margin above 20 percent by 2027.

Following the Q3 report, DNB Carnegie reiterated its BUY recommendation and maintained its target price of SEK 330, while raising its 2026 adjusted EPS forecast by 10 percent to SEK 3.9 from SEK 3.5, driven by higher net financial income and positive tax effects. However, the firm cut its adjusted EPS projections for 2027 by 4 percent to SEK 5.7 and for 2028 by 3 percent to SEK 8.6, reflecting lower projected abdominal sales over the forecast period.

RedEye lowered its DCF-based fair value estimate from SEK 385 to SEK 360, introducing a new valuation approach consisting of 90 percent DCF and 10 percent peer-based multiples to reflect near-term uncertainties around the LBE transition. Despite these adjustments, both brokerages maintained a positive long-term outlook, with RedEye forecasting European heart sales will gradually ramp during 2027 before exceeding SEK 550 million in 2028 following a subsequent US launch.

The company's leadership transition remains orderly, with CFO Kristoffer Nordström moving to the newly created role of Senior Vice President Corporate Strategy & Business Development while retaining active CFO responsibilities until a suitable successor is recruited. This structural reorganization positions experienced corporate leadership directly in the United States to manage the complex multi-organ commercial launches over the next three years.

However, the company's employee net promoter score declined sharply to 27 in 2025 compared to 51 in 2024, though it remained within a stable and positive range. Competitive intensity is rising following high-profile medical technology acquisitions, including Getinge's purchase of Paragonix at 11x EV/Sales and Terumo's acquisition of OrganOx at 21x EV/Sales, which provide strong benchmark valuation support for XVIVO's current premium multiple of approximately 7x 2026 EV/Sales.

The company's valuation remains attractive relative to historical peaks of 14x to 16x EV/Sales during the 2022 to 2024 period, though elevated short interest of approximately 8 percent reflects persistent market skepticism regarding commercialization timelines. Supply-side conditions are shifting favorably for XVIVO over the long term following the successful completion of a major project to increase disposable production capacity ten-fold in mid-2026, resolving historical capacity bottlenecks that had previously constrained abdominal product sales.

The company's asset-light manufacturing model relies on carefully selected tier 1 contract manufacturing partners located in Sweden, Switzerland, Germany, the United States, and the Netherlands, though supplier concentration risks remain a key vulnerability during rapid commercial rollouts. Management's strong defensive pricing power in critical clinical settings enables proactive price increases with minimal customer resistance, as demonstrated by the ability to implement 10 percent price increases on its gold-standard Perfadex solution, which represents only 0.2 to 0.3 percent of total lung transplant costs.

The clinical adoption of machine perfusion continues to gain momentum, driven by a structural organ shortage where only 10 percent of annual transplant needs are currently met. Machine perfusion technologies represent a paradigm shift offering a market potential ten times larger than traditional cold static storage, providing a robust runway for long-term clinical adoption across all major organ types. Clinical data demonstrates that XVIVO's hypothermic oxygenated perfusion technology delivers a 76 percent risk reduction in severe primary graft dysfunction for heart transplantation, with 12-month follow-up data revealing a 38 percent risk reduction in severe complications and a 92 percent survival rate compared to 86 percent in the control group.

Looking forward over the next 18 to 36 months, XVIVO is poised for substantial EPS acceleration driven by the European commercial rollout of Heart Assist Transport, the anticipated US heart approval in late 2027 to early 2028, and the accelerated US liver market entry via the 510(k) pathway in early 2028. While near-term financial performance may be volatile due to the LBE customer transition and margin pressure from commercial investments, the company's dominant lung market share of approximately 90 percent globally, combined with its differentiated multi-organ cold oxygenated perfusion platform, positions it to capture significant value from the massive underpenetrated transplant market.

The key risk to this outlook remains the pace of European heart adoption, which could be hampered by healthcare capacity constraints or slow establishment of regional reimbursement pathways across Germany, Italy, and other key markets.
