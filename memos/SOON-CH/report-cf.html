EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Consensus EPS Outlook

Earnings are expected to keep growing, with current-year (FY2026/27) adjusted EPS consensus at CHF 11.10 per DNB Carnegie's post-H1 broker note in November 2026. Next-year (FY2027/28) EPS sits near CHF 12.3-12.9, as DNB Carnegie projects CHF 12.31 and Morgan Stanley estimates CHF 12.86, roughly one percent ahead of the broader consensus. Upward revision risk is skewed positive near term, because H1 adjusted EPS beat the Vara consensus of CHF 4.52 by 19%.

Drivers of Future Earnings

The Phonak EON/Sphere platform is the largest positive driver, as Sphere models already make up twothirds of EON units and EON could add about four percent to EPS over the next year.

Cost savings and custom products add further upside, with the efficiency programme targeting around CHF 90 million of annual savings and roughly five percent EPS expansion and custom products contributing about two percent to EPS growth. Retail bolt-on M&A is a steady but medium-confidence contributor, as the annual CHF 80-100 million acquisition ambition could support a one to two percent sales CAGR contribution.

The Amplifon sourcing shift and the Cochlear Implants segment are the main negatives, because the expected Amplifon loss is a low single-digit share of group sales and already embedded in guidance while the US regulatory bottleneck is the single point of failure for the implant upgrade cycle. Dual-chipset costs could cap gross margin gains, as DNB Carnegie warns that deep-neural-network processors could inflict negative gross margin effects and prevent the targeted 10-13% core EBIT growth.

Organizational and Strategic Change

Change is extensive and concentrated in a short period, with a new CEO, CFO (Elodie Carr-Cingari), COO (Roberto di Fiore, December 2025) and Board Chair (Gilbert Achermann). The structure is being reshaped, as Sonova moved to a four-region model on 1 April 2026 and started a process in March 2026 to divest Consumer Hearing, a business that historically dragged group profitability by about 200 basis points. Ambition has been raised but is not a formal commitment, since the CHF 6 billion revenue goal for 2030/31 is categorized as an ambition rather than a financial target, alongside mid-term targets of 5-10% sales CAGR and 7-12% core EBIT CAGR.

Latest Financial Results

H1 2026/27 was a clear beat, with sales of CHF 1,834.1 million, up 7.5% in local currencies and 1% above consensus.

Profit beat by more than sales, as core EBIT of CHF 384.0 million grew 22.9% in constant currency, 10% above consensus, with a 20.9% margin against 19.2% expected. Divergence between segments is sharp, with wholesale sales up 11.0% in local currency while Cochlear Implants fell 10.2% and segment core EBIT dropped 49.1%.

Cash conversion was strong but flattered by timing, as free cash flow reached CHF 266.2 million against CHF 60.3 million although temporary tax payment timing helped and will normalize.

Reliability of Guidance

Recent guidance looks conservative, since FY2026/27 guidance was raised to 6-8% sales growth and 10-13% core EBIT growth and Morgan Stanley sees the midpoint as implying a material second-half slowdown. The record is good but not flawless, as FY2024/25 adjusted EBITA growth landed at the bottom of the guided range, misses tend to cluster around executive transitions and restructurings, and skeptics attribute frequent beats to low-balled guidance.

Broker Positioning

Sell-side sentiment is balanced rather than bullish, with Refinitiv data published by Morgan Stanley on 13 November 2026 showing 41% Overweight, 45% Equal-weight and 14% Underweight. Only one of the six named brokers is constructive, as Bernstein rates Outperform while DNB Carnegie, Jefferies and Deutsche Bank rate Hold, Morgan Stanley Equal-weight and Redburn Neutral. Valuation, not operations, is the main brake, as Jefferies argues Sonova trades at a peak premium to European Medtech so good news is fully priced.

Position Relative to Peers

Sonova's wholesale franchise and margins are strong, though peers lead on some growth measures, with 8% organic wholesale growth versus Demant at 11% and Amplifon at nearly 4%.

Balance-sheet flexibility is a relative advantage, since net debt of 1.1x EBITDA contrasts with elevated leverage at Demant and Amplifon. The implant business is a sub-scale challenger to Cochlear, as Cochlear holds about 60% of the market and Advanced Bionics about 15%, while GN's 18% EBITA margin was boosted by one-off tariff refunds that analysts treat as temporary.

External Forces

Demand and pricing are supportive, with the global hearing aid market growing about 5% in the first half and premium products at roughly 70% of prescription hearing aid sales. Regulation, currency and consumer health are the main external headwinds, including China's roughly 75% cut in public-hospital implant list prices, a CHF 13.7 million FX hit to H1 sales and high sensitivity of US private-pay sales to inflation.

Activist Investor Activity

There is no activist presence, as AlphaSense documents show no active activist campaigns at Sonova and the Rihs family group jointly controls 6.18% of share capital.

Most Critical Risks

The first risk is a delay in US FDA clearance of the Integro processor, which management acknowledges could prevent positive implant growth for the full year.

The second risk is a faster-than-expected Amplifon shift to GN Hearing, which Jefferies expects to create a second-half wholesale headwind and which could prevent Sonova from maintaining its historical market share in Europe. The third risk is that the margin gains prove temporary, as dual-chipset costs could inflict negative gross margin effects and retail expansion has already diluted margins over the past two years.
