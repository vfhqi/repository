EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Section 1 – Business Model and Organisation

Grifols is a single-engine, North America-weighted business: it is a vertically integrated plasma producer in which Biopharma generated 86% of 2025 revenue, and Morgan Stanley estimates North America at 60% to 70% of sales. 1 20F Execution under new leadership has been credible but the plan is demanding: Grifols met its FY2025 adjusted EBITDA guidance at EUR 1,902 million, while its Capital Markets Day plan targets EUR 10 billion of revenue by 2029.

Section 2 – Financial Profile Compared with Peers

Grifols' growth has been bought rather than organically built, and on a smaller scale than its peers' deals: Jefferies notes it relied heavily on buying smaller operators, including a EUR 1.5 billion, 70% stake in Biotest, while Takeda's Shire deal was a USD 62 billion transformation. Profitability sits well below the best-in-class peer, because fixed collection costs and weaker site productivity weigh on margins: gross margin slipped to 38.80% in 2025 from 39.49%, against 49.60% at CSL Behring.

Cash conversion is structurally constrained by the plasma cycle, as donation-to-release takes six to nine months, inventory was still EUR 3,296 million in 2025, and free cash flow of about EUR 0.5 billion remains modest relative to EBITDA of EUR 2 billion. - Earnings Calls Leverage is the dominant balance-sheet issue, since net debt is forecast at EUR 8,461 million at end-2026, roughly 4.40x adjusted EBITDA, even though the EUR 1,564 million Shanghai RAAS sale was applied to debt reduction. Hidden capital needs sit in Biotest, because it is financed by EUR 637 million of subordinated Grifols shareholder loans and its equity ratio of 30.30% is well below its 40% target.

Headline figures reconcile closely across sources, with Bernstein reporting 2025 revenue of EUR 7,523 million against EUR 7,524 million in company presentations, and capital returns have only just restarted, as the EUR 0.15 interim dividend in August 2025 was the first payment since June 2021.

Section 3 and Section 4 – Industry Structure and Value Chain

The industry is a rational, high-barrier oligopoly with long-run volume support, as consolidation cut the major players from over 20 to five, immunoglobulin treats at most 34% of potential patients in the US and Europe, and rational pricing prevents destructive volume wars. Profits concentrate in fractionation rather than collection, because independent centers earn only 8% to 12% margins while incremental liters processed into immunoglobulins and albumin earn blended margins of about 38%; the most damaging chain change flagged is non-plasma substitution, led by Sanofi's recombinant alpha- 1 therapy, which threatens a business worth 13% of sales.

Section 5 and Section 6 – Customers, Suppliers and Channels

Customer risk is low in concentration but high in price pressure, since no single account exceeded 10% of gross revenues in 2024 and 2025 and multi-year contracts carry annual escalators of up to 5%, yet Takeda cut prices significantly between late 2022 and 2024 to win Grifols accounts. Supply risk is geographic and technological, because the US supplies about 68% of global plasma, border restrictions on Mexican donors previously caused major collection declines, and Haemonetics' NexSys hardware carries extremely high switching costs.

Section 7 and Section 8 – Competitors and External Signals

Competitive positions are stable, with Grifols gaining in subcutaneous products, as global immunoglobulin shares are CSL 34%, Takeda 29% and Grifols 25%, Grifols lifted its US subcutaneous share from 2% to 14%, and it lost less Chinese albumin revenue than CSL (6% against 25%). External signals support the working-capital improvement but warn of a power shift toward buyers, as receivable days fell to 31 and inventory days to 258 in 2025, while Deutsche Bank sees downstream bargaining power shifting toward consolidated healthcare buyers and expects the China drag to delay margin normalization through fiscal 2027.

Section 9 – Management Quality and Strength of the Business

Management has been disciplined on liquidity and execution, with Nacho Abia leading a fully non-family team, the 2027 maturities refinanced into a EUR 3 billion equivalent Term Loan B and USD 2 billion revolver, extending maturities to October 2028, and Deutsche Bank judging Grifols on track for guidance. Governance complexity is the principal blemish on management quality, as Grifols consolidates Scrantonowned Haema and BPC Plasma through call options, with cash-pooling balances of EUR 35 million that create minority-shareholder governance risk, and EUR 87 million and EUR 26 million of 2025 dividends to Scranton Plasma were settled non-cash by offsetting cash-pooling balances.

The moat is real but still narrower than CSL's, since Grifols runs over 400 centers, yet CSL's profit per liter was 38% higher in 2024; the gap is being addressed through nomograms in 80% of US centers yielding 9% to 12% more plasma per donation and the closure of 29 underperforming US centers. Pricing power is evidenced but modest, with average annual increases closer to 4% for legacy immunoglobulins, and the top risk is FcRn inhibitors and recombinant proteins cannibalizing immunoglobulin and alpha-1 volumes, with a build-up of channel inventory the key early warning sign.

Section 10 – Disruption and Long-Term Fit

Long-term disruption looks segment-specific rather than category-wide, because five to seven years of approvals and heavy capital make entry uneconomic, FcRn agents will only partially displace plasma therapies over the next decade, and synthetic substitutes remain an unsolved challenge. Grifols appears positioned among the long-term winners but with a clear China caveat, since scaling Egyptian sourcing to three million liters by 2029 secures its competitive position, while price-driven procurement is depressing international albumin revenues.

Most Critical Strength and Weakness

The most critical strength is the combination of scale and regulatory barriers with rational industry pricing, because a footprint that erects virtually insurmountable barriers sits within a market where rational pricing prevents destructive volume wars. The most critical weakness is leverage of roughly 4x EBITDA combined with modest free cash flow, which leaves limited margin for error during operational setbacks and leaves deleveraging exposed to the China, GPO-pricing and recombinant-substitution risks identified above.

Section 3 – Industry Structure, Concentration and Where Profits Are Made

Section summary
  1. The global plasma-derived therapies industry is a highly consolidated, high-barrier market driven structurally by resilient, underpenetrated demand for immunoglobulins.
  2. A highly consolidated global oligopoly dominates the plasma fractionation sector, while regional markets like China remain heavily fragmented.
  3. Dominant industry players maintain highly rational pricing by prioritizing margin preservation and yield optimization over market share expansion.
  4. Structural margin profiles are capped by high collection costs, while emerging recombinant proteins and biosimilars threaten long-term pricing power.

Global Market Dynamics

1.
Sizable market: The global plasma-derived therapies market is valued at approximately USD 27 bn to USD 30 bn. Global demand for these critical treatments continues to expand at a steady mid-to-high single-digit rate. Long-term growth is driven primarily by the rising diagnosis of primary immunodeficiencies and autoimmune conditions. Consequently, global collection volumes are projected to rise consistently to support this expansion.
2.
Underpenetrated demand: Immunoglobulin represents the main therapeutic and commercial driver of the global plasma fractionation industry. Jefferies Research estimates that immunoglobulin therapies currently treat at most 34% of potential patients in the United States and Europe. Similarly, albumin therapies address only 21% of the potential patient population in these major markets. Untreated patient backlogs provide a long runway for structural volume growth over the next decade.
3.
Sourcing concentration: The United States serves as the primary source for the global plasma industry because of its permissive donor compensation laws. Jefferies Research reports that approximately 5% of the global population in the United States supplies 60% of the world's plasma. Paid donor models remain restricted in most other developed countries. Consequently, global fractionators rely heavily on concentrated US-based collection networks to feed their production plants.
4.
Industrial expansion: Global manufacturing capacity is expanding rapidly to satisfy rising clinical demand for plasma-derived therapies. Octapharma completed a EUR 200 million expansion of its Vienna facility to increase production by 50%. A major market study predicts the global plasma fractionation market will grow from USD 35.2 billion to USD 62.8 billion by 2032. Sizable capital investments of this scale are essential to supporting long-term volume growth.

Extensive mergers and acquisitions over the past two decades reduced the number of major global players from over 20 to just five key companies. Dominant global operators include CSL, Grifols, Takeda, Octapharma, and Kedrion. High market concentration supports high pricing discipline across the industry.

Competitor Strategies and Pricing

9.
Rational pricing: The global plasma market operates under highly disciplined competitive dynamics. Major fractionators expand their collection capacities and inventories in close alignment with organic demand. Spot pricing for immunoglobulins remains stable with annual mid-single-digit price increases. Rational pricing behavior prevents destructive volume wars among the dominant players.
10.
Profitability focus: Takeda intentionally prioritizes margin protection over aggressive volume growth in its plasma therapeutics business. The Japanese fractionator has consciously avoided price-cutting competition, accepting mid-single-digit immunoglobulin growth to defend its operating profit. Competitors that prioritize volume expansion risk diluting their overall corporate profitability. Consequently, industry leaders choose to maximize return on capital rather than chase market share.
11.
Scale advantages: CSL Behring leverages its immense scale to achieve the highest gross profit per liter in the industry. Relentless investment in new therapies widens the competitive lead over smaller peers.

The European Medicines Agency's approval of Egyptian-sourced plasma allows Grifols to lower its raw material costs.
