What the company does

Neste is a Finnish refiner with two engines. Renewable Products makes renewable diesel and sustainable aviation fuel from waste and residue feedstocks (91% of renewable raw materials in the second quarter of 2026) at Porvoo, Rotterdam and Singapore; Oil Products runs the conventional Porvoo refinery; Marketing and Services sells fuel. Market value is 24,399 million euros at a price of 31.47 euros on 2 October 2026. The card places it in Energy, downstream distribution, where Richard's interest level is D, Nonplussed.

A plausible investment thesis

Self-help plus mandated demand. A new executive team under Heikki Malinen, appointed October 2024, has pulled Neste back from speculative ventures to the two core businesses, and the performance improvement programme reached a 594 million euro run-rate by June 2026, beating a 350 million euro target a year early. The change-forces digest of 20 September calls this a shift from chronic over-promiser to honest tracker.

The mechanism to earnings per share runs through three channels: cost taken out of fixed operating expense, which reaches the bottom line whatever the commodity does (15% estimated impact, high plausibility); physical demand from EU RED III, where the end of double counting from January 2026 and Germany's transposition add 1.5 million tonnes of annual demand (20% impact, high plausibility); and the Rotterdam expansion, 2.5 billion euros lifting renewable capacity to 6.8 million tonnes, due to start in 2027 (25% impact, the largest driver, medium plausibility). The drivers to track are the Renewable Products comparable sales margin (a record 1,223 dollars a tonne in the second quarter against 361 dollars a year earlier), utilisation (75% against an 80% target), Rotterdam's start date, mandate enforcement, US D4 credit prices and leverage, now 29.9% against a 40% ceiling.

Business quality

Mixed. The positives are real: record group comparable EBITDA of 1,203 million euros in the second quarter of 2026 against 341 million a year earlier, Renewable Products at 859 million (a 9% beat), net debt to EBITDA down to 1.2 times from 2.7 times at the end of 2025, and Moody's affirming A3 stable. Against that, management called 75% utilisation unsatisfactory, sustainable aviation fuel production fell to 126,000 tonnes from 290,000, Oil Products missed consensus by 17% on volume, and net working capital days rose to 48.9 from 38. This is a good operator in a commodity business, not a franchise with pricing power.

The strongest counter-case

The record quarter is a snapshot of extraordinary conditions, not a run-rate. Strip out the Middle East distillate premium, let oil and hydrotreated vegetable oil prices ease, add three long turnarounds and top-of-range capital expenditure, and Neste's margin vulnerability shows while the shares are priced on Stage 2 enthusiasm. Consensus already has earnings falling 13% a year; if Rotterdam slips past 2027 while Asian capacity compresses industry margins, earnings fall further and the re-rating the plausible return depends on never arrives. For a book that prizes predictable earnings per share over 18 months to 3 years, the honest objection is not that Neste will do badly but that nobody can say with confidence what it will earn in 2028.
