Helios Towers (HTWS-GB): what the Q3 2026 update of 5 November 2026, and the response to it, do to the case

These results modestly strengthen the operating half of our case, leave the earnings-per-share half where it was, and break the order of events the case was built on; with the shares at 238.8p on 13 November 2026 against the 205.2p the case was struck at, the net is a small positive for the thesis and a materially thinner entry.

What changed

  • Volume. 924 organic tenancy additions in the quarter to 30 September 2026 against consensus of 662; 3,435 in the nine months; 35,379 tenancies in total, up 12.2 percent year on year. The case's own test was "above 900 means a raise", and its model had about 850. Additions still fell for a second quarter running (1,406, 1,105, 924).
  • The quarter. Revenue 252.5 USDm, up 16.85 percent and 4.71 percent above company-compiled consensus; Adjusted EBITDA 135.2 USDm, up 12.57 percent, about 1 percent ahead of broker expectations and slightly below one house's own 136.5 USDm. The revenue beat is largely passed-through energy. The Adjusted EBITDA margin was 53.5 percent, against 54.7 percent in Q2 2026 and 55.5 percent in Q1 2026.
  • FY2026 guidance. Additions approximately 4,000 (from 3,500 to 4,000); Adjusted EBITDA approximately 535 USDm, the top of the range; recurring free cash flow above 235 USDm; discretionary capex 230 to 245 USDm. The case carries about 4,100 additions and 531 USDm.
  • FY2027, given four months before the case expected it. More than 3,000 additions, against prior consensus that brokers put at 2,200 to 2,500. The case models 3,000 and expected a March 2027 opening range no higher than 2,500 to 3,000 that "will read as a deceleration".
  • Cash and balance sheet. Recurring free cash flow 133.7 USDm for the nine months, up 10.22 percent, with a sequential step-down in Q3. Working capital absorbed 76.8 USDm in nine months. Headline free cash flow was a deficit of 5.2 USDm against a surplus of 48.7 USDm, on discretionary capex of 172.8 USDm against 72.0 USDm. Net debt rose to 1,818.4 USDm from 1,723.3 USDm and net leverage was 3.4x at 30 September 2026, unchanged since December 2025.
  • The response. Jefferies to 315p from 300p, estimates up 2 to 5 percent, expecting consensus Adjusted EBITDA to rise 1 to 3 percent. Deutsche Bank to 290p from 285p, FY2026 EBITDA up 1 percent and FY2027 up 3 percent, implying about 14 percent growth; on roughly 535 USDm that is about 610 USDm, above the case's 598 USDm and inside its 578 to 618 USDm cone. Morgan Stanley held Overweight and 310p, having raised from 295p and lifted estimates 3 to 4 percent two days before the results; its FY2028 EBITDA of 698 USDm stands against the case's 649 USDm. All ratings stay positive. The case's operating figures no longer sit above the sell-side.
  • The price. 238.8p on 13 November 2026, 16 percent above the case's strike, above the 50-day and 150-day averages (212.7p and 211.1p) and above the 215p the case set as the sign of momentum turning. It is below the 247.6p May 2026 high the case uses as its marker for the broadening leg.

The challenge

Reading one: the quarter's beat was built, not leased. The case does not hold this. The site count rose from 15,246 at Q2 2026 to 15,688 at Q3 2026, which is 442 new sites in a quarter that added 924 tenancies. In the first half the estate added about 500 sites against 2,511 tenancies. On that subtraction, tenancies added to existing towers fell from roughly 1,000 a quarter in the first half to roughly 480 in Q3, while new sites rose from about 250 a quarter to 442. The tenancy ratio stood still at 2.26x. Management describes the forward mix as 75 percent colocation and 25 percent build-to-suit; Q3 was nearer half and half. This matters because the case converts every tenancy at its own flat rule (27.7k USD of revenue at an 80 percent incremental margin, on about 10k USD of capital for a colocation), and the lease-up of a fixed base is the mechanism the whole setup rests on. A new site with one tenant earns about 12 percent on capital against 25 percent with a second. If this reading is right, the headline "above 900" test was passed by the capital-heavy kind of volume, the high-return kind decelerated far faster than the headline shows, and that is why leverage has not moved in nine months of record growth. For it to be right, the Q3 split has to be a trend and not the timing of a build programme; one quarter cannot say which. Against it: every new site is a future colocation slot, and management's stated mix for the year is consistent with the nine-month total (about 940 sites on 3,435 tenancies).

Reading two: the case's edge has been spent and the price has taken it. The day-one piece raised this reading, and it has gained force. The first unfavourable event the case dated arrived as a beat. The largest favourable event it dated to March 2027 is public. Three houses have already added 1 to 5 percent to their estimates in one event, against the 2 to 4 percent on FY2027 EBITDA that the case budgeted for the whole period to mid-2027. The entry is dearer again: against the case's own values, 238.8p leaves about 18 percent to its unchanged-multiple value of 281p and about 41 percent to the 337p at its 20x fair multiple, and puts its 145p to 180p trough 25 to 39 percent below. Broker targets of 290p to 315p are now 21 to 32 percent above the price, where the case recorded 41 percent. What the case still holds that the market has not priced is weighted to the negative and to March 2027: the EPS print it calls near-certain to miss now arrives without the guidance news meant to sit beside it, and nothing below EBITDA was guided. For this reading to be right, the buyers since 5 November must be paying for the operating upgrade and must mark the shares on EPS in March. The case's own answer still stands against it: the market values this company on EBITDA and recurring cash.

Reading three: the cycle is prolonged, and that is not simply good news. The day-one piece raised the first half of this reading too; it has neither gained nor lost force, because the brokers' response is agreement and not new evidence. Arithmetic cuts both ways: approximately 4,000 for FY2026 implies about 565 additions in Q4 2026, while more than 3,000 in FY2027 needs an average above 750 a quarter. Either Q4 guidance is the usual floor, or 2027 needs a re-acceleration from the exit rate. The results still say nothing about FY2028, where the case's front-loading judgement is really tested. What the case does not hold is the second half: taken with reading one, a prolonged cycle is a prolonged build. The case's second leg needs leverage through 3.0x and cash per share as the headline in 2027. Recurring free cash flow above 235 USDm now needs more than about 101 USDm in Q4 2026 alone, over twice the nine-month quarterly average; management attributes the Q3 step-down to customer payment timing and expects "some" reversal. The better the volume news, the later the cash story plausibly arrives, and the case put what little risk of a guidance cut it saw on exactly that line.

An alternative that still fails. A 40 percent beat on tenancies with a 1 percent beat on EBITDA invites the reading that volume converts badly at the EBITDA line. It does not hold: on the case's own rule, 262 tenancies above consensus are worth under 1 USDm inside the quarter they arrive in, and the margin fall is fuel pass-through, which dilutes the percentage and not the dollars.

What would settle it

What the response settled, and did not. It shows the re-basing of operating estimates (1 to 5 percent), the new target range (290p to 315p) and that every rating held. A fourth house lifted its target; the figure is not in hand. It does not show whether consensus EPS for FY2026 or FY2027 rose on the volume news, which was the mechanism of reading two; the higher sell-side momentum letter is consistent with it but does not identify the year. It explains the cash step-down as timing without resolving the Q4 ask. It carries nothing below EBITDA and nothing on FY2028 beyond one house's estimate.
