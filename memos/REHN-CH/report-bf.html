EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF) 41 findings

Romande Energie operates as a Western Swiss regional utility delivering electricity across four integrated business units – Grids, Energy, Markets, and Building Solutions –to more than 300,000 customers spanning Vaud and Valais cantons. The company's revenue model balances highly predictable regulated grid tariffs with market-exposed generation and building services, while Strategy 2030 targets adjusted EBITDA expansion to CHF 170-190 million through disciplined regional acquisitions and accelerated thermal network densification.

Financial performance during FY2025 exhibited substantial volatility, with operating revenues declining 5% to CHF 788 million due to lower regulated energy components, yet adjusted EBITDA surged 26% to CHF 152 million driven by favourable energy supply margins. The group maintains an exceptionally strong balance sheet with equity representing 76% of total assets and total debt of CHF 339 million anchored by long-dated green financing carrying an average interest rate of 2.35%.

However, structural returns remain weak, with ROIC of 1.84% and ROCE of 2.25% in FY2025, lagging diversified Swiss peers BKW and Alpiq that achieve returns exceeding 6% through merchant trading operations and scale advantages.

Appendix

Plausible problems to monitor

Romande Energie exhibits several structural performance gaps relative to Swiss utility peers, with return on invested capital (ROIC) of 1.84% and return on capital employed (ROCE) of 2.25% in 2025 significantly lagging behind the peer median. Larger diversified Swiss peers generate superior returns on capital employed by leveraging merchant trading operations and scale, with BKW achieving 6.20% ROCE, Axpo reaching 5.50%, and Alpiq posting 4.85% in the same period. This capital efficiency deficit reflects the company's highly regulated, capital-intensive infrastructure focus and limited exposure to higher-margin merchant activities that characterize more diversified competitors. The revenue trajectory declined 5% to CHF 788 million in 2025 from CHF 830 million in 2024, with the 2024 figure itself representing a 10% decline from the CHF 928 million peak in 2023, indicating persistent top-line contraction driven by regulatory tariff pass-throughs and normalized energy components. Permitted grid returns face systemic pressure as the weighted average cost of capital (WACC) compressed from 4.13% to 3.98% and then to 3.43% for 2026, eroding the foundation of the company's most profitable business segment.

The distribution grid segment, despite generating a 14% EBIT margin as the highest-margin stage, faces ongoing margin compression risk from regulatory intervention that threatens the structural economics of the monopoly infrastructure business. Upstream renewable generation delivered an exceptionally low 1% EBIT margin in 2025, heavily burdened by substantial multi-year depreciation and amortization charges on wind and solar projects, positioning generation assets as value-destructive relative to the regulated grid base.

Unregulated commercial volumes contracted by 21% or 54 GWh during 2025 as selected commercial contracts were not renewed, highlighting competitive vulnerability in non-captive market segments where Romande Energie faces direct price competition from larger national utilities. The regulator eliminated the CHF 60 gross margin per invoice recipient starting January 2026, forcing retail pricing to strictly reflect procurement and operating costs, which removes a historical profitability buffer in the retail supply business.

Building Solutions energy efficiency renovations delivered near-breakeven results with 0% EBIT margin in 2025, indicating that the downstream services segment operates at the threshold of viability despite representing a strategic growth pillar in the company's long-term roadmap.

Capital expenditures reached CHF 163 million in 2025, absorbing 106% of operating cash flow as grid investments ramped up, demonstrating that organic cash generation cannot self-fund the necessary infrastructure expansion without balance sheet leverage or capital recycling. This negative free cash flow position contrasts with more diversified peers that maintain positive free cash generation while simultaneously funding substantial capital programs through superior operational cash conversion.

The company's structural inability to generate returns above its cost of capital across the majority of its business portfolio, combined with persistent top-line contraction and margin compression in both regulated and unregulated segments, signals fundamental challenges in translating defensive monopoly positions into sustainable shareholder value creation within the constraints of Swiss regulatory frameworks and regional market dynamics.
