Section 2 – Stellantis Capital Return Policy and Management Liquidity Statements

Section summary
  1. Stellantis targets an ordinary dividend payout of 25% to 30% of net profit supported by a fortress balance sheet.
  2. Management remains highly committed to returning to positive industrial free cash flow in 2027 to resume capital returns.

Capital Return Framework Targets

1.
Established framework: Stellantis maintains a formal capital return policy that targets a specific dividend distribution. The company's framework targets an ordinary dividend payout ratio of 25% to 30% of net profit from the prior financial year. However, the board of directors retains sole discretion over the final payout based on prevailing market conditions. This means a dividend resumption directly depends on net profit recovery.
2.
Historical distributions: Stellantis demonstrated its commitment to shareholder returns by distributing record amounts of capital before the downturn. The company returned €7.7 billion to its investors in 2024 through dividends and share buybacks. This historical return included a dividend of €1.55 per share, representing the lower end of its targeted range. Such payouts highlight the substantial cash generation capability the company aims to restore.
3.
Clear priorities: Stellantis outlines a structured capital allocation philosophy that balances industrial investments with investor returns. The first priority of the company remains funding product electrification and software development to maintain competitiveness. Once these essential capital expenditures are covered, management allocates excess cash to shareholder rewards. Resuming dividends therefore requires clearing these baseline investment hurdles.
4.
Strict connection: Stellantis anchors its cash distribution calculations in reported accounting profits rather than adjusted measures. While some analysts inquire about moving to adjusted metrics, the payout remains tied to statutory net income. The recent collapse in profitability directly led to the cancellation of the dividend. Earnings recovery is therefore the primary legal driver for dividend restoration.
5.
Cash conversion focus: Stellantis intends to drastically improve its cash conversion efficiency over the medium term to support sustained capital returns. Management aims to increase its cash conversion rate by 10 percentage points over historical levels in the coming years. Achieving this structural improvement requires intense operational discipline across all manufacturing regions. Reaching this conversion goal would significantly boost the company's long-term capacity to pay dividends.

Balance Sheet Liquidity Requirements

6.
Fortress targets: Stellantis defines a specific range of balance sheet liquidity to withstand cyclical automotive downturns. The company targets total available industrial liquidity of 25% to 30% of trailing twelve-month revenues. This liquidity cushion was intentionally built to protect operations during cash burn periods. Maintaining this target range is a critical condition for resuming share buybacks and ordinary dividends.
7.
Secure liquidity: Stellantis successfully kept its cash reserves within the targeted safety margins despite recent financial stress. The company reported industrial available liquidity of €42.7 billion as of September 30, 2026. This liquidity level represents 26% of trailing twelvemonth revenues, sitting securely within the target threshold. This stable buffer suggests the balance sheet is healthy enough to support future payouts.
8.
Balanced financing: Stellantis utilized capital market issuances to reinforce its balance sheet and manage upcoming debt maturities. The company issued €5 billion in hybrid notes in March 2026 to optimize liquidity. This financing strategy helped offset cash outflows and kept industrial net cash at €5.4 billion. Managing these debt levels is crucial to ensure payouts do not compromise credit ratings.
9.
Rating downgrades: Stellantis faced ratings pressure as agencies responded to the company's deteriorating operational performance and negative cash generation. S&P revised its credit rating for the automaker from BBB to BBB minus with a negative outlook in early 2026. Concurrently, Moody's downgraded the company to Baa3 while shifting its outlook to stable. Defending the investment-grade status is a high priority that may force management to conserve cash rather than resume payouts.
10.
Contrary evidence: Stellantis suffered severe cash drain during the first nine months of fiscal year 2026. The company recorded an industrial free cash absorption of €4.93 billion during this period. While this represents an improvement from the €7.31 billion burn in 2025, it highlights persistent cash leakage. This ongoing drain makes any near-term resumption of capital returns highly unlikely until operations stabilize.

Expected recovery: Stellantis management repeatedly committed to restoring positive cash generation under its long-term strategic plan. Achieving this target is critical for the group to rebuild its dividend payment capacity.

This disciplined spending is designed to support more than 60 vehicle launches under the FaSTLAne 2030 plan. Restricting capital intensity is necessary to ensure that operational recovery translates into free cash flow.

The company aims to converge its US dealer inventory to a healthy range of 320k to 350k units. Cleaning up this excess inventory is a prerequisite to stopping the heavy discounting that eroded margins. Achieving this optimal inventory balance is crucial to stabilize industrial free cash flow.
14.
Projected turnaround: Stellantis anticipates a major cash flow reversal as working capital dynamics normalize in subsequent quarters. The negative cash flow of 2026 was driven by lower production volumes and seasonal working capital movements. Management expects reversing working capital will be a key contributor to positive 2027 cash flows. Resolving these inventory issues is the main operational catalyst for restoring the dividend.

Management indicated that the equity required to support Stellantis Financial Services is relatively limited through 2027. Consequently, establishing a long-term track record of cash generation serves as the primary prerequisite for restoring payouts.

Early Indicators of Cash Turning Points

5.
Severe headwinds: Stellantis faces multi-billion euro inflationary pressures through 2027 and 2028 from rising raw material and semiconductor prices. These projected electronics and memory chip cost surges threaten to offset the automaker's targeted €2.4 billion in annual savings. If these inflationary pressures persist, they could permanently depress operating margins and delay any ordinary dividend resumption past 2028. This would force the company to prioritize debt reduction over equity returns.
6.
Pessimistic projections: Analysts at Morgan Stanley expect that Stellantis's cash burn could extend if macroeconomic or tariff headwinds intensify. The broker projects that the dividend suspension will likely continue through 2027, followed by a very gradual return in 2028. Under these conditions, the automaker will be forced to prioritize repairing its balance sheet over shareholder rewards. This conservative path represents the baseline expectation unless working capital releases surprise the market.
7.
Aggressive pruning: Stellantis is reportedly preparing to exit its high-profile motorsport investments to reassess spending priorities. The company is expected to withdraw Peugeot from endurance racing and pull Citroën and Opel out of Formula E. These cost-cutting moves represent a significant step under CEO Antonio Filosa's value creation strategy to stabilize industrial free cash flow early. Tracking these aggressive corporate cost interventions serves as a primary leading indicator of a potential dividend surprise.
8.
Other side of the story: While a prolonged dividend suspension is possible, a rapid working capital release could trigger a dividend resumption far exceeding conservative consensus estimates. Stellantis management is highly confident in achieving positive industrial free cash flow in 2027 by resolving its North American dealer inventory issues. If working capital reversals release billions in cash earlier, the board could resume historical payout ratios rapidly, immediately restoring Exor's primary dividend income stream.
