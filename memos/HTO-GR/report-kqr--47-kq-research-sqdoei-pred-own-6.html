Section 2 – Asset Lives and Spectrum Renewals

Section summary
  1. Hellenic Telecom is positioned to manage its upcoming mobile spectrum renewals with minimal impact on cash flow.
  2. Offsetting tax breaks will fully cover the expected spectrum license renewal expenditures in late 2027.
  3. Peer comparisons indicate that establishing long infrastructure useful lives will shelter earnings from high depreciation.

Overview of asset accounting and spectrum

1.
Extensive research parameters: Analysts examined five major telecommunications companies and reviewed thirteen distinct industry documents to evaluate long-term depreciation trajectories across the European sector. The research incorporated detailed financial statements from Proximus, Swisscom, and Orange Polska to establish clear sector benchmarks for infrastructure assets. All identified peer precedents and regulatory reports were utilized in this evaluation to ensure maximum analytical depth. Consequently, this broad peer sample provides a robust statistical baseline for assessing OTE's accounting configurations.
2.
Peer duct depreciation: Swisscom historically extended the useful lives of its physical cable and duct infrastructure from 20 to 40 years. This accounting adjustment was implemented on 1 January 2008 following a formal regulatory decision by the Federal Communications Commission. According to Swisscom's interim report on 4 November 2008, this modification reduced the group's depreciation expenses by 120 million Swiss francs for the full year 2008. This past case suggests that extending infrastructure asset lives can significantly boost reported operating income.
3.
Proximus infrastructure guidelines: Proximus applies conservative useful life estimates for its technical and network equipment in its official accounting policies. The company's annual report published on 17 March 2025 states that cables and ducts are depreciated over 15 to 20 years. In comparison, more active technical systems like network switches are depreciated over 8 to 10 years, and radio access networks have a shorter life of 6 to 7 years. These shorter active lives mean that rapid technological transitions will lead to accelerated depreciation charges.
4.
Active equipment lifetimes: Peer operators consistently apply shorter asset useful lives to active network components than to passive infrastructure. Proximus reports in its annual account of 17 March 2025 that active equipment installed at client premises has useful lives ranging between 2 and 8 years. This rapid depreciation cycle is necessary because consumer technologies and hardware standards evolve very quickly. Consequently, active equipment requires continuous capital replacement, which keeps capital intensity high even after fiber backbones are built.
5.
Accounting estimation volatility: Asset useful lives are subject to annual management assessments, which can introduce earnings volatility if operational conditions change. A Companies House filing on 8 September 2026 by BSO Network Solutions notes that depreciable asset lives are reviewed annually based on technological shifts and future market conditions. For OTE, any unexpected technological obsolescence in its active fiber nodes could force management to shorten estimated useful lives. Such accounting revisions would instantly elevate annual depreciation expenses and weigh on operating profit.
6.
Peer commercialization efforts: Peer telecom operators are utilizing their long-life physical duct networks to expand wholesale revenues and offset depreciation. Orange Polska's interim report on 28 July 2026 states that the company actively monetizes its telecom ducts and poles by leasing them to competing operators. This infrastructure sharing strategy provides a highly profitable revenue stream that mitigates the high capital costs of fiber rollouts. Implementing a similar wholesale sharing model could boost OTE's return on assets and support its earnings per share.
7.
Spectrum asset treatment: Proximus amortises its valuable mobile spectrum licenses strictly in accordance with their regulatory license durations. The integrated annual report of Proximus published on 12 March 2026 indicates that spectrum licenses in the 800 MHz, 900 MHz, 1800 MHz, and 2100 MHz bands are amortised over 20 years. This long-term amortisation schedule contrasts with the 15 years applied to its 2600 MHz band and 17.67 years for the 3600 MHz band. These long asset lives help reduce annual amortisation charges and protect reported net income.

Deutsche Bank Research reported on 6 February 2026 that the regulator's proposals are highly favorable for Greek mobile operators because of balanced terms. The upcoming process targets the renewal of spectrum licenses in the 900 MHz and 1800 MHz bands, which are scheduled to expire in late 2027. Consequently, this proactive regulatory engagement reduces spectrum risk for OTE and supports its long-term network planning.
9.
Expected renewal expenditure: Hellenic Telecom faces an estimated capital expenditure of 78 million euros to secure its 900 MHz and 1800 MHz spectrum renewals. Deutsche Bank Research estimated on 16 February 2026 that the total renewal cost should remain at this manageable level. This cost is relatively low compared to historic spectrum auctions in other European jurisdictions. This modest pricing minimizes the cash outflow burden on OTE, protecting its medium-term capital structure.
10.
Positive tax offsets: Hellenic Telecom stands to offset its spectrum capital expenditure entirely through a major tax benefit from a recent asset sale. Deutsche Bank Research noted on 16 February 2026 that OTE expects a 125 million euro tax break in Greece from its disposal of Telekom Romania Mobile. This exceptional tax benefit is scheduled to be realized in FY2026, which is well ahead of the spectrum payments. This offset means that OTE's net free cash flow remains highly protected despite the upcoming regulatory renewal costs.
11.
Spectrum cash management: Hellenic Telecom has carefully integrated the upcoming spectrum renewals into its official financial planning. Swisscom AG's peer report on 6 August 2026 notes that OTE's free cash flow guidance specifically assumes a spectrum auction will occur in 2027. This proactive modeling ensures that the company's dividend distribution capacity is not unexpectedly disrupted by regulatory cash demands. Unless the auction competitive dynamics worsen, the spectrum acquisition will be executed smoothly without threatening the group's capital allocation targets.
12.
Regulatory framework security: The regulatory environment in Greece appears highly supportive of network operators, reducing the risk of punitive auction designs. Deutsche Bank Research highlighted on 6 February 2026 that the Hellenic Telecommunications Commission's initial spectrum proposals are incrementally positive for the net present value of Greek telecom providers. This collaborative regulatory approach reduces the likelihood of unexpected administrative fees or coverage obligations. This stable framework ensures that OTE can plan its long-term network investments with high confidence.
13.
Contrary evidence: Contrary evidence: OTE's spectrum costs could escalate beyond initial expectations if competitive bidding from rival operators intensifies during the 2027 auction process. Deutsche Bank Research cautioned on 16 February 2026 that while they estimate the renewal cost at 78 million euros, the ultimate pricing depends on final regulatory terms and competitor behavior. If Vodafone Greece or Nova bid aggressively for the same frequencies, OTE may face significantly higher spectrum liabilities than modeled. This competitive pressure would inflate amortisation charges and drag down future earnings per share.
14.
Long-term amortisation outlook: Spectrum license renewal costs are capitalised as intangible assets and amortised over their legal lifespans. Proximus' annual report of 12 March 2026 demonstrates that spectrum is consistently amortised over 15 to 20 years. If OTE applies a similar 20-year amortisation to its 78 million euro renewal, the annual expense would be just 3.90 million euros. This nominal annual headwind will have a negligible impact on OTE's future earnings per share.
15.
Other side of the story: While OTE's upcoming spectrum renewals appear highly manageable, peer comparisons show that infrastructure useful lives are subject to regular revision and impairment risk. Section 3 – Broadband Infrastructure and Copper Decommissioning

Section summary
  1. Hellenic Telecom is accelerating its rural fiber deployment through the strategic acquisition of Terna Fiber to capture high-growth broadband subscriptions.
  2. The legacy copper network transition remains a complex challenge with over three million active lines requiring adaptive, operator-driven timelines.
  3. Completing the copper switch-off is projected to deliver substantial operational efficiencies and expand OTE's operating margins through high-margin wholesale fiber migration.

Rural broadband rollout dynamics

1.
Strategic rural expansion: Hellenic Telecom has moved to consolidate its rural footprint by acquiring Terna Fiber to revive stagnant network projects. This combined venture involves several major stakeholders including GEK TERNA, Grid Telecom, PPC, and Vodafone Greece. While the takeover enables a quicker rollout of services in underserved areas, incorporating this infrastructure requires extensive financial planning. Managing these capital outlays effectively is critical because high integration costs could temporarily weigh on future earnings per share.
2.
Subsidized network funding: Hellenic Telecom utilizes public funding structures to support its extensive regional infrastructure investments. The company's deployment is heavily bolstered by state-backed public subsidy programs like the Ultra-Fast Broadband initiative. These subsidy schemes are supplemented by the state's Gigabit Voucher Plan to encourage rapid consumer transition. This governmental support will accelerate fiber customer take-up rates without requiring excessive capital expenditures from OTE.
3.
Accelerating national growth: Greece is projected to experience a monumental shift in its digital network infrastructure through the end of the decade. Point Topic forecasts that active fiber subscriptions in Greece will grow at a compound annual rate of 20.8% through 2030. This projected growth rate is the second- highest in Europe and sits far above the average. Achieving these targets will substantially expand OTE's customer base and raise long-term service revenues.
4.
Record subscription momentum: Hellenic Telecom is successfully converting its physical network coverage into active household subscriptions. The company added a record 62,000 net new fiber-to-the-home customers during the second quarter of 2026. This surge pushed OTE's total fiber subscriber base to 686,831 users out of 2.22 million homes passed. These rapid connections are crucial because higher subscriber density improves capital expenditure return profiles for investors.

Legacy copper network status

5.
Heavy copper dependency: Hellenic Telecom still faces a massive challenge with legacy systems despite its rapid fiber deployment. The company currently has approximately 3.5 million active copper lines out of 4.7 million total fixed connections. This massive copper footprint exists alongside a wide fiber-to-the-cabinet and vectoring coverage reaching 60%. Consequently, OTE must support double network maintenance costs until it can fully transition these active subscribers to fiber.
6.
Protective regulatory rules: The Greek national regulator has established strict guidelines to govern the migration process away from copper networks. EETT's 2023 market analysis ruled that Hellenic Telecom cannot charge termination fees to competing operators during technical closures. This framework is designed to protect customer choices and ensure fair competition in wholesale access. However, these administrative rules could limit short-term wholesale revenues as alternative operators transition away from copper.
7.
Unfulfilled legacy targets: Hellenic Telecom has historically struggled to execute its legacy network decommissioning goals. The company announced plans in 2019 to shut down 17 copper exchange centers over five years. This program was never successfully implemented, and no further development occurred before the regulator intervened in 2023. This past failure shows that copper switch-offs require extremely robust customer migration strategies to succeed.

Timetable and transition challenges

8.
Proposed European mandates: Hellenic Telecom must navigate upcoming legislative changes from the proposed European Digital Networks Act. The draft act mandates that member states prepare a transition plan by 2029 and achieve copper-free networks by late 2035. This European framework seeks to phase out copper in areas with 95% fiber coverage where affordable retail options exist. This strict timeline will require elevated capital expenditures if local adoption fails to meet targets.
9.
Flexible migration requirements: Hellenic Telecom advocates for an operator-driven transition process tailored to local market realities rather than rigid mandates. Company representative George Stathopoulos argued that a 95% fiber coverage target is more realistic than 100%. Additionally, OTE emphasizes that a market-driven transition has proven highly effective in Greece. Securing this regulatory flexibility is vital to protect OTE's earnings because it prevents premature network shutdowns.
10.
Customer protection requirements: Hellenic Telecom must prioritize consumer choice and open access to prevent regulatory penalties during the network migration. The Body of European Regulators for Electronic Communications stresses that copper switch-offs must avoid forcing customer provider changes. OTE views open access as an essential prerequisite to maintain fair market competition. Adhering to these principles is crucial because regulatory compliance secures long-term subscriber retention and limits churn.
11.
Contrary evidence: Hellenic Telecom risks facing intense resource bottlenecks and escalating operational costs if forced to shut down copper networks too quickly. George Stathopoulos warned in June 2026 that enforced copper switch-offs would exacerbate construction resource constraints and drive up rollout costs. This bottleneck would directly impair the efficiency of fiber deployment. Consequently, a rushed decommissioning schedule would elevate capital intensity and drag down near-term cash flows.

Economics of network decommissioning

12.
Substantial peer efficiencies: Peer operators demonstrate that completing the copper transition delivers massive long-term structural efficiencies and cost savings. Telefonica reported that shutting down its entire Spanish copper network boosted its EBITDA margin by more than 1 percentage point. Similarly, France's Orange embarked on copper decommissioning after reaching a major fiber subscriber milestone. These examples suggest that OTE can unlock significant operational savings once copper is fully retired.
13.
Wholesale revenue progression: Hellenic Telecom can improve its long-term profitability by migrating wholesale customers to higher-margin next-generation products. Deutsche Bank Research estimates that OTE earns an incremental 2 euros monthly per line on fiber-to-the-cabinet and up to 4 euros on fiber-to-the-home relative to legacy copper. Migrating wholesale customers will therefore improve the company's revenue quality. This structural shift will directly expand operating margins as the high-margin wholesale base grows.
14.
Other side of the story: Hellenic Telecom faces severe structural risks if its copper decommissioning process leads to localized infrastructure monopolies or overbuilding. The Body of European Regulators for Electronic Communications noted that post-switch-off markets often witness regional monopolies that restrict long-term competition. Furthermore, overbuilding in highly competitive urban areas reduces overall investment opportunities in underserved rural zones. If competitive rivalries from players like PPC or Nova intensify, OTE's projected wholesale revenue margins could erode.

Section 5 – Peer Precedents and Post-Peak Fibre Dynamics

Section summary

  2. Non-cash depreciation charges remain elevated for several years postpeak due to accelerated technological obsolescence.

KPN post-peak trajectories

1.
Peak rollout transition: Royal KPN experienced a notable capital intensity drop as its major fibre construction neared completion. Morgan Stanley reported on 28 September 2026 that KPN's capital expenditure will decline by 20% in 2027 as the Dutch fibre-to-the-home build slows. This reduction will lower annual spending from 1.25 billion euros to just 1.0 billion euros. Consequently, this post-peak drop will generate material free cash flow benefits for the operator.
2.
Wholesale pricing power: Post-peak operators utilize their infrastructure dominance to generate high-margin wholesale revenue streams. Deutsche Bank Research noted on 17 September 2026 that KPN's strong wholesale terms help protect its copper base as clients migrate to next-generation products. Because competitor overbuild remains extremely low, KPN retains robust retail and wholesale pricing power in its home market. This strong market structure will ensure steady operating margin growth and support earnings per share.
3.
Slower rollout realities: KPN originally aimed to reach 80% coverage in the Netherlands by the end of 2026. Deutsche Bank Research noted on 5 November 2025 that KPN extended this timeline to achieve 85% coverage by 2030 instead. This structural shift ensures that KPN avoids costly overbuilding in areas already served by competing alternative networks. This prudent strategy means the capex decline will be more gradual than previously expected by analysts.
4.
Operating cost efficiencies: Implementing a widespread fibre network allows KPN to systematically phase out its expensive legacy copper infrastructure. Deutsche Bank Research reported on 26 March 2021 that KPN's accelerated FTTH rollout delivers massive operational opex savings from copper decommissioning. These systemic savings will offset inflationary pressures on field technician labor costs. Thus, the copper switch-off will directly support long-term operating profit growth and lift EPS.

Orange Polska margin expansion
