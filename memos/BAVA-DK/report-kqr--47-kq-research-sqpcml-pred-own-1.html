Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The entry of Sanofi's next-generation rabies vaccine VaxRabeo threatens Bavarian Nordic's near- monopoly in Germany and dominant US position, risking its Travel Health segment margins and long-term EPS.

Defining the Key Question

1.
Critical inquiry: "Bavarian Nordic A/S (BAVA-DK), a Danish specialty vaccine maker with two segments: Public Preparedness (the smallpox and mpox vaccine JYNNEOS / Imvanex / Imvamune and related government work, sold mainly to US BARDA, France, the European Commission's HERA, Canada and other governments) and Travel Health (Rabipur/RabAvert rabies, Encepur tick-borne encephalitis, Vimkunya chikungunya, Vivotif typhoid and Vaxchora cholera, sold mainly out of pocket in the US and Europe), with bulk manufacturing at Kvistgaard (Denmark) and a second site in Bern (Switzerland). What are Sanofi's launch plans, timing, list prices, discounts and channel strategy for its next-generation rabies vaccine VaxRabeo (SP0087) in Germany, the rest of Europe and the US following its EU approval on 19 November 2026, what is the status and expected timing of a US filing and approval, how do German travel clinics, wholesalers and public purchasers choose between rabies vaccines, and what happened to incumbents' share and price when new rabies or tick-borne encephalitis vaccines entered established markets in the past, so as to judge the effect on Bavarian Nordic's Rabipur and RabAvert in 2027-2029?"

Impact on Travel Health

Any competitive market share loss to Sanofi's VaxRabeo would directly compress this stable and highly profitable revenue stream.

In Germany, Rabipur commands a 97% market share, while in the United States, RabAvert maintains a 79% market share. Since these commercial rabies segments are highly concentrated under Bavarian Nordic's control, Sanofi's launch of VaxRabeo represents the first major therapeutic challenge in years.

Direct leverage: Bavarian Nordic completed its end-to-end technology transfer of rabies manufacturing to Kvistgaard in 2024 to increase supply chain flexibility. This Kvistgaard bulk drug substance site requires high volume utilization to absorb indirect production costs and avoid costly idle capacity charges. Trajectory of Earnings

5.
Potential volatility: Bavarian Nordic relies on Travel Health to balance the significant volatility of its Public Preparedness division. While smallpox and mpox vaccine orders fluctuate with government stockpiling needs, the travel vaccine franchise provides highly predictable cash flows. Section 5 – Historical Competitive Dynamics and Vaccine Market Entry Precedents

Section summary

Analysis of Historical Vaccine Precedents

1.
Extensive tracking: Research into historical vaccine competitive dynamics identified 12 relevant sources, 6 prominent biopharmaceutical companies, and 4 comparable past vaccine entry cases. Vaccine market entries demonstrate that clinical superiority is the single most powerful driver of rapid market share shifts. Analyzing these historical curves helps project how Bavarian Nordic's rabies franchise will perform.
2.
Rapid displacement: GlaxoSmithKline's Shingrix launch in 2017 demonstrates how superior clinical efficacy can quickly dismantle an incumbent's monopoly. Shingrix entered the shingles market with 97% overall efficacy, compared to only 51% for Merck's established live vaccine, Zostavax. Consequently, Zostavax rapidly lost market share and was finally withdrawn from the United States market in 2020. 167 168 precedent suggests that Rabipur could face severe volume erosion if a new competitor offers a vastly superior clinical profile.
3.
Strong adoption: GlaxoSmithKline quickly scaled Shingrix sales to reach £3.6 bn globally in 2025, with £1.2 bn captured in the United States alone. Merck's Zostavax sales had previously peaked at $764 mm in 2014 before the product fell into commercial decline and eventual discontinuation. The rapid shift was accelerated by the CDC's Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices awarding Shingrix a preferred recommendation. This commercial trajectory highlights that preferred regulatory recommendations trigger rapid adoption at the expense of established incumbents.
4.
Price discounting: GlaxoSmithKline launched its pneumococcal vaccine Synflorix at a 50% discount to Wyeth's Prevenar 13 to capture market share in India. Synflorix was priced at Rs 2,000 per dose compared to Prevenar 13 at Rs 3,800 per dose. Despite this aggressive pricing strategy, pediatricians continued to prefer Prevenar 13 because it covered 13 bacterial variants compared to only 10 variants for Synflorix. This past case suggests that price discounts are often insufficient to overcome clinical spectrum disadvantages.

Prevenar 13 reported annual domestic sales of Rs 330 mm in early 2012, representing a 137.1% annual growth rate. Pediatricians favored Prevenar 13 because it protected against highly harmful strains like 19A that Synflorix missed. This precedent indicates that incumbent market share remains protected if the challenger lacks clinical spectrum equivalence.
