Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The proposed RMT merger reshapes both companies by turning Unilever into a pure-play HPC firm and expanding McCormick's global flavor footprint.
  2. The CMA paused its Phase 2 merger reference on November 25, 2026, after McCormick offered to divest Unilever's entire Colman's brand to address competition concerns. The package covers dry ingredient mixes and sauces where the merged entity would have become the largest UK supplier by far.

The Core Strategic Question

1.
Critical inquiry: "Unilever PLC (ULVR-GB), a global home and personal care company reporting Beauty & Wellbeing, Personal Care and Home Care, plus a Foods business being combined with McCormick through a Reverse Morris Trust (completion guided for mid-2027 at the latest); Ice Cream was demerged in December 2025 as The Magnum Ice Cream Company (19.9% retained); the company moves to six regional P&L units from 1-Jan-2027. For Unilever PLC (ULVR-GB) and McCormick, status of the UK review of the Foods combination after the remedies deadline of 18-Nov-2026 (remedies offered, undertakings accepted, or a Phase 2 reference and its statutory timetable), what is the status of the other merger-control and shareholder approvals, how close are any required divestments to McCormick's contractual cap of $1.4bn of fiscal 2025 net sales, and what do the companies and brokers now say about the completion date and the risk of termination?"
2.
Accelerated transition: Unilever aims to accelerate its strategic transition into a pure-play leader by divesting its lower-growth Foods division. This transaction is highly critical for investors because it establishes a EUR 39 billion focused enterprise with leading positions in highly attractive categories. The exit from Foods aligns with management's explicit strategic goal of generating over 65% of sales from its core beauty and personal care segments.
3.
21 22 425 Substantial dilution: The proposed Reverse Morris Trust transaction is expected to cause immediate earnings dilution for Unilever shareholders before share consolidation. Pro forma analysis indicates Unilever's earnings per share could decline by 32%, or 21% when excluding stranded costs. To address this dilution, management is expected to execute a share consolidation similar to the one implemented during the recent ice cream demerger.

Impact on Capital and Leverage

4.
Deleveraging momentum: Divesting the Foods segment significantly improves Unilever RemainCo's balance sheet strength and capital allocation flexibility. Following the transaction, Unilever's leverage is projected to drop from 1.9x net debt to EBITDA down to 1.2x. This rapid deleveraging is further supported by a $15.7 billion cash payment from McCormick, enabling Unilever to execute up to 6 billion euros in share buybacks.
5.
Significant gearing: McCormick will take on substantial pro forma leverage of approximately 4x net debt to EBITDA at the close of the transaction. This high debt load remains a major source of concern and investor skepticism for McCormick's shareholders. 30 To address these concerns, management plans to deploy 1.5 to 2 billion dollars in cash for debt reduction within the first two years.
6.
Accretion potential: McCormick expects the Foods merger to deliver mid-to-high single-digit adjusted EPS accretion during the first twelve months post-close. Fully synergized earnings accretion is projected to reach mid-to-high teens by the third year. This positive outlook is underpinned by a targeted $600 million net cost synergy program that management plans to execute over three years.

Unilever estimates these gross stranded costs will range from 400 million to 500 million euros. Without executing additional high-growth bolt-on acquisitions to replace the lost Foods cash flow, Unilever's long-term valuation expansion could remain fragile.

Post-Remedies CMA Status

10.
Regulatory reprieve: The UK Competition and Markets Authority announced on November 25, 2026, that it is formally considering structural undertakings offered by McCormick in lieu of an in-depth Phase 2 merger reference. This announcement followed the statutory remedies deadline of November 18, 2026, which was established after the CMA's Phase 1 decision on November 11, 2026. Under the Enterprise Act 2002, the regulator has paused the automatic referral of the transaction to Phase 2 while it conducts a public consultation on whether to accept the proposed remedy. If the CMA formally accepts the undertakings, the merger will receive conditional clearance without an in-depth statutory Phase 2 review. This positive development keeps the transaction on track for the guided completion timeline of mid-2027. 45 (GOV.UK)
11.
Unilateral dominance: The CMA's Phase 1 inquiry concluded that the £33.8 billion transaction raised realistic prospects of a substantial lessening of competition within the UK. Specifically, the regulator determined that combining Unilever's Knorr and Colman's portfolios with McCormick's Schwartz brand would create the largest supplier of dry ingredient mixes and dry sauces by far. The CMA's market analysis showed that supermarket own-label products and alternative brands, such as Nestlé's Maggi in dry ingredient mixes and Premier Foods' Bisto in dry sauces, exert only limited competitive constraints on the merging parties. Furthermore, the watchdog noted that limited retail shelf space and high brand equity make entry or expansion by third-party suppliers highly difficult in these UK categories. (UAE) (GOV.UK)

Colman's Divestment Package

12.
Brand exit: To resolve the horizontal overlaps in dry sauces and cooking ingredient mixes, McCormick offered to divest the entire Colman's business. This comprehensive remedy package includes Colman's dry recipe mixes, dry sauces, and its historical mustard and cold sauces portfolio. The divestment will be made to a purchaser approved upfront by the CMA before the merger is allowed to close. Unilever's management had already anticipated these antitrust concerns by putting the English mustard brand up for sale in August 2026, working constructively with the regulator as discussions with potential buyers progressed. 52 (GOV.UK)
13.
Distinct categories: The CMA separately analyzed the UK mustard retail segment and concluded that the transaction did not raise horizontal competitive concerns there. The regulator found that McCormick and Unilever principally supply different styles of mustard that consumers do not view as close substitutes. Specifically, McCormick supplies American-style mustard under the French's brand, while Unilever's legacy portfolio focuses on English-style Colman's and French-style Maille mustard. Supermarkets generally stock these as distinct sub-categories, meaning there is negligible direct competition between them. However, because the dry mixes are inextricably linked to the Colman's brand equity, the entire brand is being divested to secure regulatory clearance. 56 (GOV.UK) 57.

Packaged Food Precedents

14.
Precedent outcomes: UK merger control precedents in the packaged food sector demonstrate that the CMA strictly enforces upfront buyer conditions for brand carves-outs to ensure market competitiveness. In the anticipated acquisition of Bakkavor Group by Greencore Group, the CMA insisted on an upfront buyer condition because the divestment business had to be carved out from a broader corporate infrastructure. Similarly, during a Phase 1 investigation into a chilled sauces joint venture, Greencore resolved competition concerns by offering to sell its only chilled sauce and soup manufacturing plant in Bristol to an approved buyer. These precedents suggest that McCormick and Unilever must secure a financially robust, independent buyer to satisfy the CMA's criteria. (GOV.UK)
15.
Process acceleration: Merging parties can achieve rapid clearance in the UK if they concede competition concerns early and offer clean, structural remedies. A notable precedent is Vandemoortele's acquisition of Délifrance, where the CMA cleared the transaction after the parties conceded competitive overlaps in frozen laminated dough products. By cooperating and offering targeted structural divestments, the inquiry group cleared that merger seven weeks ahead of its statutory Phase 2 deadline. Conversely, if merging parties fail to secure an upfront buyer within the statutory Phase 1 remedy window, the undertakings can lapse, forcing a referral to a full Phase 2 investigation as occurred in the bakery sector.

Contrary Evidence and Alternatives

16.
Contrary evidence: The divestment of the historic Colman's brand represents a significant loss of legacy UK revenue and brand heritage for the pro forma Foods entity, contrary to management's initial acquisition plan. In August 2026, Unilever's management was forced to put the English mustard brand up for sale specifically to head off these anticipated regulatory concerns. While the divestment satisfies the CMA, it reduces the UK market synergies that McCormick expected to achieve through the transaction.
17.
Other side of the story: Although the divestment of Colman's reduces pro forma UK sales, it completely eliminates the risk of an expensive and lengthy Phase 2 review that could delay the entire global transaction. Accepting a structural remedy at Phase 1 allows Unilever and McCormick to preserve the vast majority of their global transaction, which spans major markets in North America and continental Europe. Given that the CMA's concerns were highly localized to UK dry ingredient mixes, sacrificing Colman's is a minor tactical concession that secures the wider £33.8 billion deal. Section 4 – Divestment Progress and the Contractual Sales Cap

Section summary
  1. Antitrust remedies remain well below the contractual sales cap of $1.4 billion, minimizing any near-term risk of transaction termination.
  2. Unilever's historical divestment program highlights a consistent strategy of pruning non-core brands to focus on high-growth categories.

Contractual Headroom and Remedy Scale

1.
Comfortable headroom: McCormick and Unilever have established a contractual cap of USD 1,400 mm of fiscal 2025 net sales for mandated antitrust divestments. If regulatory authorities require remedies exceeding this threshold, either party retains the right to terminate the Reverse Morris Trust transaction. Current required remedy packages, including the UK dry mixes business, remain exceptionally far from breaching this contractual limit.
2.
Minor revenue impact: The proposed divestment of the Colman's brand in the United Kingdom represents only a minor fraction of the contractual cap. Morgan Stanley research indicates that Colman's, Marmite, and Bovril generated combined aggregate annual revenues of approximately EUR 200 mm before the transaction. Because the required divestment is limited to the Colman's brand, the sales impact remains negligible relative to the USD 1,400 mm threshold.
3.
Negligible termination risk: Sell-side analysts expect the total volume of required global antitrust divestments to stay comfortably under the contractual termination limit. Regulatory reviews across more than ten jurisdictions represent the principal gating variables for closing the transaction. Consequently, the risk of transaction termination due to a cap breach is considered highly remote by market participants.

In Mexico, mayonnaise is an everyday essential with over 95% household penetration and per capita consumption five times the global average.
