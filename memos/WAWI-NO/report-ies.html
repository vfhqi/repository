Wallenius Wilhelmsen (WAWI-NO): what the third-quarter results do to the existing case

These results leave the case broadly where it was, because the case was written with them in hand; what they add is a small net lean against it, on two points the case sized too comfortably and one point of fact that is still in dispute.

1. What changed

Against the case's assumptions, very little. The company reported on 4 November 2026 for the quarter to 30 September 2026.

  • Revenue USD 1,401m, up 5.26% on the year, about 1% above Bloomberg consensus of USD 1,391m.
  • Adjusted EBITDA USD 428m, 2.51% below Bloomberg consensus of USD 439m and in line with DNB Carnegie's USD 430m. Reported EBITDA was USD 414m. The gap includes USD 12m of digital transformation cost and USD 3m of restructuring.
  • Net profit to owners of the parent USD 192m, 8% below Bloomberg consensus of USD 209m, on a larger minority share inside EUKOR.
  • Guidance held at about USD 1.6bn of adjusted EBITDA for 2026.
  • Contracts: shipping backlog USD 5.8bn at 2.8 years, book average about USD 54 per cbm, and USD 1.7bn of awards after the quarter end. Management expects to contract the vast majority of 2027 to 2029 capacity by the end of 2026.
  • Balance sheet: net debt USD 2,119m, leverage 1.3 times, liquidity USD 1,026m, slightly above the internal minimum. USD 300m of equity raised in October at NOK 168 a share.
  • Sell-side: DNB Carnegie moved to Buy on 3 November and to a target of NOK 210 on 5 November, cut 2026 EBITDA by 1% and raised 2027 and 2028 by 6% and 15%, leaving it 7% and 22% above Bloomberg consensus. The review found no other large broker updating in the window, so the response it carries is one house's.

All of this is what the case assumed: meets EBITDA, misses a little below it, no raise, peak being contracted.

2. The challenge

We find two readings the case does not accommodate, and one smaller point that cuts the other way. We do not find a third threat worth putting up: the review's other bear arguments (oversupply after 2027, tariffs, a Chinese export gap, customers building their own fleets) are ones the case already holds and has dated.

Reading one: the quarter's recovery was fuel, not price, so it is not evidence that price reaches profit. The case needs about 70% of rate gains to reach EBITDA in 2027, against 35–50% in 2024–2025, and it cites this quarter as the demonstration that it can happen once costs stop rising. The review's own figures point elsewhere. Adjusted EBITDA rose USD 67m on the quarter, from USD 361m to USD 428m. Net bunker cost fell USD 44m, from USD 109m to USD 65m, as surcharge recovery caught up. That is about two thirds of the improvement, and it is a lag reversing, not a price being won. The remaining USD 23m or so came on USD 96m more revenue. The review also records a USD 17m gain in the shipping segment on a ship sold to the government services division, eliminated at group level; if the segment's quarter-on-quarter figure includes it, the segment evidence is weaker still. And costs have not stopped rising: one-year charter-in rates reached USD 100k a day by October 2026, up 120% from the late-2025 trough, and the review describes net bunker cost as now normalised, so that help is probably spent.

For this reading to be right, the net bunker figures must sit inside adjusted EBITDA on a comparable basis in both quarters, and charter renewals must keep repricing upward through 2027. If it is right, the case's 2027 step is intact as a price on paper but lands in profit at something nearer the old 35–50%, and the case's rule that each USD 100m of EBITDA is USD 0.20 a share does the rest.

Reading two: a Suez return is two to three times larger than the case sizes it. The case carries Cape routing as absorbing about 5% of capacity and treats a return as small for 2027 earnings and large mainly for the shares. The review puts the capacity that routing absorbs at about 12% to 15% of the global fleet, with steaming distances 43% longer, and argues that a normalisation could cut freight rates by 20% to 30% within six months. On that sizing a return is not one year of fleet growth arriving early; it is more than the case's 6.6% and 3.6% of fleet growth for 2027 and 2028 combined, arriving at once, in a market where idle tonnage is 1.9% (October 2026). That would probably reach contracted earnings as well as the multiple, through volumes customers may defer and through the terms of every renewal still unsigned.

For this to be right, the 12% to 15% must be right. We flag plainly that the review states it without a source, and that it is the review's inference about what consensus assumes. If the lower figure holds, the case's sizing stands. This is a question of fact, not of judgement, and it is checkable.

The smaller point, in the case's favour on risk but against its lower renewal price. The case's first piece of evidence for a renewal step of about USD 15 per cbm, below the model's USD 18, is that the equipment manufacturer's USD 950m contract renewed at similar rates and is a fifth to a quarter of the renewing tranche. The review describes that contract as covering both ocean shipping and land-based logistics. Its ocean part is therefore smaller than USD 950m, and its weight as evidence about shipping rates is lighter than the case gave it. Separately, the review's own trip-wire treats new signings below USD 50 per cbm as a 25% fall from current booking levels, which implies bookings near USD 67 per cbm, about USD 13 above the book. That figure is also unsourced. It sits close to the case's USD 15, so we read the two together as leaving the renewal price where the case has it, with less confidence in either direction than the case states.

One caution on figures. The review says the USD 1.5bn fleet commitment is about 94% of market capitalisation. That does not square with the placement terms the same review reports, and we do not rely on it.

3. What it does to the case

  • Pricing power, operating and financial leverage (55): more pressure. The quarter was the case's one live example of price reaching profit, and it reads better as fuel.
  • Riskiness and simplicity of the forecasts (35), and Case Riskiness (40): slightly more pressure, conditional on the Suez sizing. The case ranks Suez as sudden, trackable and large for the shares; the review would make it large for earnings too.
  • The judgement on the renewal price, and the forecast financials (17): the same. The evidence thins on both sides.
  • Guidance conservatism and delivery (40): the same. A small EBITDA miss, an 8% miss at net profit and held guidance are the record the case describes.
  • Lifecycle (40): unresolved. The case counts it as a sign of stalling that one controlling family was allocated 17% of the placement against a 38% holding. The review still has that holder taking its pro-rata 37%. If the review is right, one of the case's four stalling signs falls away.
  • Scope for upgrades (32) and current momentum (90): the same. One broker's upgrade is in the case already.
  • Price. At NOK 165.6 (13 November 2026) the shares have crossed none of the case's thresholds. They remain below the 50-day average of NOK 173.1 and the NOK 168 placement price, above the 200-day average of NOK 141.9, short of the NOK 186 high the case names as the sign of a broadening thesis, and well above the NOK 125–135 at which the case says the return clears the bar.

4. What would settle it

  • The second review, four working days on. It is likely to carry the other brokers' estimates and targets, and so show whether 2027 and 2028 consensus moved toward the one house that has published or stayed below it. It can also settle the family subscription, which is a matter of record in the placement announcements. It cannot carry the renewal price, the backlog after the round or any 2027 guidance, because the company has not reported them.
  • The fourth-quarter report, which the case dates to 17 February 2027. The freight-rate and bunker bridges for a quarter in which fuel is flat are the clean test of reading one. The book average rate tests the renewal price; the case's own marks are USD 60 or more, and USD 57 or less.
  • The Suez sizing can be settled now from fleet and routing data, without waiting for the company. The financial effect of a return cannot: the review asks for a company scenario analysis and the company has not committed to one. That part stays open until transits resume.
  • Minimum volumes in the new contracts. The company has not committed to disclosing them. Nothing scheduled answers this.

VERDICT

  • Direction: negative
  • Grade: minor
  • Moves: none
  • Reason: The case was built on these results, so nothing would move a full grade today; the net lean is slightly negative because the quarter's profit recovery reads as fuel recovery, not price, and the review sizes a Suez return well above the case, against one small point that favours a firmer renewal price.
