Section 2 – Elekta's Cost Base Split and Historical Margin Leverage

Section summary

  3. Seasonality severely dampens first-quarter profitability due to fixed-cost under-absorption while the fourth quarter provides peak earnings leverage.

Cost base structure and split

High materials concentration: Elekta's internal cost analysis reveals that its cost of goods sold structure is dominated by physical supply chain inputs. According to Morgan Stanley notes from an analyst meeting in London on May 30, 2023, the company's cost of goods sold is two-thirds materials and logistics costs and one-third operating expenses. This high concentration of materials expenses means that margins are highly sensitive to electronics component prices.
2.
Significant logistics headwinds: Global supply chain disruptions can severely inflate the variable component of Elekta's logistics costs. A report by Jefferies on March 7, 2022, estimated that supply chain challenges, service costs, and conditions in the freight markets dragged the operating margin down by 300 to 500 basis points. These freight headwinds were primarily driven by pandemic-induced logistics bottlenecks and expensive air freight.
3.
Tariffs and currency drag: Import duties and unfavorable exchange rate movements consistently erode Elekta's gross margin potential across key clinical markets. In an interim report published on March 5, 2026, Elekta disclosed that tariff costs and FX headwinds reduced its third-quarter gross margin by 100 and 130 basis points respectively. These headwinds offset some of the gains achieved through pricing actions and new product rollouts.
4.
Elevated R&D commitment: Elekta maintains a high level of research and development expenditure relative to sales compared to standard medical technology peers. A research report by Jefferies on December 1, 2025, noted that Elekta's gross R&D spend remains high at approximately 12 percent of revenues. This elevated spending is historically supported by capitalization policies that management is currently trying to reduce.
5.
Heavy opex pressure: Administrative and selling expenses also create substantial fixed overhead that limits EBIT expansion when sales volumes are flat. A report by Morgan Stanley on June 19, 2023, pointed out that selling and administrative expenses had remained flat or increased as a percentage of sales over the prior four years. This fixed overhead prevents Elekta from expanding operating margins unless sales growth is sufficiently strong.
