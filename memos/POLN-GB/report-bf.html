Section 3 – Industry Structure + Concentration + Value Chain Economics

Section summary
  1. European private credit is experiencing a powerful secular transition toward asset-backed finance as bank retrenchment under Basel III/IV creates a massive supply vacuum

Industry landscape and structural tailwinds

1.
Rapidly expanding market: Moody's projects global private credit assets under management will exceed USD 2 trillion in 2026.
2.
Aggressive long-term expansion: Moody's forecasts private credit assets to approach USD 4 trillion by 2030 as allocations rotate toward asset-backed finance.
3.
Mass addressable TAM: Partners Capital highlights a USD 30 trillion TAM for private credit as bank balance sheets continue to contract.
4.
Broad funding migration: Partners Capital expects USD 5 trillion to USD 6 trillion of assets to shift to private credit over ten years.
5.
Tighter banking constraints: European banks supply over 85% of corporate loans but face severe balance sheet contraction under Basel III/IV.
6.
Accelerating direct-lending dominance: Direct lending captured 71% of sponsored European middle-market loan volume in 2025, up from 33% in 2014.

Asset-backed lending economics vs corporate credit

7.
Discrete collateral underwriting: Asset-backed finance structures secure loans against identifiable, cash- flowing asset pools rather than enterprise value.
8.
Defensive risk isolation: Underlying assets are typically held in bankruptcy-remote special-purpose vehicles to protect investors from corporate-level defaults.
9.
Dynamic covenant activation: Asset-based lending structures feature real-time operational monitoring and cash controls that activate faster than traditional covenants.
10.
Favourable capital recycling: Asset-backed finance transactions typically feature shorter loan durations of two to four years to mitigate long-term macroeconomic volatility.
11.
Low public correlation: Asset-backed lending performance is driven by asset-level cash flows, maintaining lower correlation to public equity and corporate credit markets.
12.
Insulated bilateral execution: Bilateral mid-market deals insulate managers from syndication agent reliance while giving them full control over underwriting and documentation.

Key success factors and scale thresholds

13.
Severe fundraising bifurcation: European managers raising under USD 5 billion saw a 60% year-on-year contraction in capital raised in 2026.
14.
Extreme capital concentration: Private credit funds targeting USD 1 billion or more capture 71% of all capital raised.
15.
Dominant manager advantage: Large institutional scale represents a key structural barrier to entry as larger managers capture market share.
16.
Lower mid-market premium: Lower middle-market and non-sponsored lending strategies yield higher spreads and tighter covenants than upper-market club deals.
17.
Robust pricing premium: European mid-market pricing averaged 553 basis points in Q2 2026, maintaining a 40-basis-point premium over large-cap pricing.
18.
Extreme margin compression: Quieter M&A deal flow drove average software-related private credit margins down by 50 basis points.
19.
side of the story: rapid institutional capital inflows into direct lending could compress yield premiums if aggressive underwriting behavior drives covenant dilution across the sector.
