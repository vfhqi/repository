[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = D

Thesis. AQ Group is a compounder bought late. A proven, net-cash, decentralised engineered-components manufacturer has added a higher-margin product line for AI data centres, raised its structural margin standard and lifted organic growth to 9.1 percent, and at SEK 248.0 (2026-10-06) the shares already pay for all of that change and more. The business is compelling; the investment from this price is not, because the modal return is below zero over eighteen months and the case clears the 20 percent bar only below about SEK 185.

  • The change is external and structural, and it is new to this company at scale.
    • Two sector-level demand forces with two-plus years' visibility, medium-voltage transformers for hyperscale data centres and European defence, have turned the company's own cycle from the inside, while the rest of the book sits in a flat-to-weak industrial cycle.
    • Data centres: inductive components for data centres went from negligible sales in 2024 to 6 percent of group sales by Q2 2026
      • Medium-voltage UPS transformer output doubled sequentially in Q2 2026 to 70 systems a month, with further expansion expected in 2027
    • Defence: UK defence sales doubled year on year in Q2 2026
      • SEK 60m has been invested in northern Sweden
      • Nominations on several large European requests for proposals were expected during or after the summer of 2026
    • Organic growth: minus 6.9 percent (FY2024), 2.0 percent (FY2025), 6.3 percent (Q1 2026), 9.1 percent (Q2 2026), volume-led rather than price-led
    • The limit: the structural niches are about 12 percent of sales (data centre 6 percent; defence 5 to 7 percent), and heavy trucks, buses and agricultural machinery are a headwind, with the Mexico and Plattsburgh wiring plants underutilised
  • The change inside the company is an improvement of degree, not a transformation.
    • The board raised the EBT margin target from 8 percent to above 10 percent on 14 July 2026 after 13 to 14 consecutive quarters above 8 percent, the quarter itself delivering 9.9 percent
    • A new independent chairman arrived in April 2026
    • Acquisitions resumed with one small deal (Time24, May 2026, for £1 plus about SEK 50m of debt)
    • There is no chief-executive change, and the standards programme's own measured outputs slipped in the latest quarter (delivery precision 92.7 percent against a 98 percent target; inventory turns 2.9 against 3.5).
  • 'Financial Outputs' have turned up on every line but cash.
    • Q2 2026 revenue rose 9.9 percent, EBIT rose 17 percent to SEK 255m (9.9 percent of sales) and beat consensus by 6 percent, and FY2027 consensus earnings per share have risen 6.9 percent in six months
    • Operating cash flow fell to SEK 23m in Q2 2026 from SEK 232m a year earlier on a SEK 295m working-capital build, which the company explains as late-quarter deliveries and inventory for Q3.
  • The change has travelled all six layers of the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain, and the share price has travelled further.
    • Capacity and people are invested, the target is raised and M&A resumed, the key operating metrics are moving, the financial statements show it, and the sell-side has raised earnings per share by 5 to 8 percent and its targets by 15 percent over six months
    • The shares rose 39.6 percent over twelve months to a 52-week high and lagged the index by 1.3 percent in the three weeks after the record quarter, which is the price moving before the event.
  • What we think it earns: earnings per share of SEK 7.38 (FY2025, reported basic) rising to about SEK 8.20 (FY2026), SEK 9.17 (FY2027) and SEK 10.07 (FY2028), about 10.9 percent a year
    • 3 to 5 percent below consensus (SEK 8.45, 9.68, 10.50 on the brokers' basis), mostly because we take the tax rate from about 20.5 percent to 22 percent as Pillar II minimum-tax payments begin in 2027 on the Bulgarian, Estonian and Lithuanian profits.
  • What it costs: SEK 248.0, or 24.5 times consensus 24-month-forward earnings of SEK 10.13 (2026-10-06), the 97th percentile of the stock's own ten-year range, the highest multiple among the 15 peers (next: Senior at 21.8 times), and 18 times 2027 EV/EBITA against the Nordic contract-manufacturing cohort's 13 times.
  • The return does not clear the bar on any combination of the files' figures.
    • On our path and a modal 24-month-forward multiple of 21.4 times (a 40/60 blend of a cleared branch at 23.4 times and an uncleared branch at 20.0 times), the 'Modal return' is about minus 1 percent a year over eighteen months and about 4 percent a year over three years
    • Holding today's 24.5 times gives 8 to 9 percent a year
    • Consensus earnings at today's multiple held gives about 12 percent a year
    • The bar is 20 percent.
  • The chain holds in the company and breaks at the shareholder.
    • Every layer from resources to sell-side estimates confirms the change, so the thesis is right about the business
    • The price has moved first, so the thesis is wrong about the return
    • The case begins to clear at about SEK 185 (the eighteen-month test) and clears on both tests at about SEK 165, which is the 52-week low.
  • The pattern it fits is 'Compounder', at a C crispness.
    • A proven upgrader of earnings with high quality on all three of the framework's dimensions, whose consensus model is roughly right rather than mechanically wrong: the data-centre volume line is a candidate for being under-modelled and the tax line is over-modelled, and the two offset.

  • The case therefore is a good business at a price that already pays for the change.

    • We park it at a level, because below about SEK 185 the same thesis would be a B, and we do not dismiss it.

  • Rating = D, because the question is whether the expected change is material all the way to a sufficient return, and it is not: the input change forces are strong ('Change Forces' B) and the financial outputs are improving ('Financial Outputs' B), but the 'Modal return' is F because the share price at SEK 248.0 (2026-10-06) already carries the change at the 97th percentile of the stock's own multiple history, so the chain that is complete in the company breaks at the shareholder.

    • The parts combine by binding: the return is the output the thesis exists to deliver, and no strength in the inputs makes a negative eighteen-month return material to capital.
    • Not C, because C would say the change is of middling materiality, when the forces and outputs are above average and the return is well below the bar
    • The rating records the break, not an average.
    • C was live because at a price below about SEK 185 the same thesis would clear the bar and the Building Block would read B.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The case against, at its strongest: the Risks report's "false friend", that AQ's record quarter is top-line growth concealing a deterioration in the business beneath it, so that the 10 percent EBT margin is a cyclical peak inflated by a surge of high-margin data-centre deliveries, the working-capital build is the first sign the ramp is beyond the sites' capability, and fair value is SEK 140 (2027 sales SEK 9,000m, an 8 percent EBIT margin, 15 times EV/EBIT).
      • Cash flow: operating cash flow SEK 23m in Q2 2026 against SEK 232m a year earlier, a 90 percent fall during a record profit
        • Net working capital up from 28.7 to 32.0 percent of trailing revenue in one quarter
      • Inventory: turns 2.9 against a 3.5 target, a 17 percent miss, with SEK 185m of inventory write-downs in 2025
      • Delivery: precision down to 92.7 percent from 94.7 percent during the most important ramp in the company's history, after 90 percent (2022) and 92 percent (2021) when volumes last surged
        • Quality defects in the new data-centre transformers in late 2025
      • Concentration: one customer about half of the data-centre segment
      • Margin ceiling: both the bull broker before July and the bear report call 10 percent a ceiling for a contract manufacturer with no intellectual property facing OEMs who demand 3 to 5 percent annual productivity cuts
      • Currency and governance: zero currency hedging on 73 percent of sales
        • A founder-only remuneration committee and warrants without performance hurdles
      • Price: a multiple at 20 times EV/EBITA against a nine-year peer median of 10.6 times, in the report's words "a behavioural finance anomaly" that leaves no margin of safety
    • What we concede: the working-capital build and the delivery slip are real execution strain, not noise, and they recur at this company whenever volume surges
      • The concentration in data-centre sales is a single point of failure for the margin story
      • The multiple leaves no margin of safety and the bear's SEK 140 is a fair trough value, because our own trough arithmetic lands at SEK 137 to 175
      • And the governance of pay is below the standard of the peers the company is compared with.
    • Why we still hold our view on the business and not the bear's: the cash drain is a single quarter with a stated, checkable cause (SEK 219m of receivables from late-quarter deliveries and SEK 70m of inventory for Q3 deliveries), H1 2026 operating cash flow was SEK 362m, FY2024 operating cash flow was SEK 1,197m, the balance sheet holds SEK 817m of net cash excluding leases, and the pattern of build-then-recover has run twice before (2021 to 2023).
      • Margin behaviour: the EBT margin has held above 8 percent for fourteen quarters including a minus 6.9 percent organic year, which is the opposite of how a cyclical peak behaves
      • Management behaviour: management admits every problem the bear lists and has a record of fixing them (mdexx from minus 7 to plus 7 percent EBT within a year)
      • The bear's own test: the Risks report's analysis of competing hypotheses gives the "false friend" profile three hard inconsistencies and the bull nine, which is a verdict on the bull's cash-flow claims, not on the company's solvency or margin
    • Where the case against wins anyway: on the price.
      • We do not need the business to deteriorate to reach a park
      • The multiple does it alone, and that is where our recommendation comes from.
Change Forces: are the input change forces sufficiently strong? (E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because the external forces (data-centre electrification, European defence, nearshoring) are strong, structural and new to this company's scale, the internal forces are real but of medium breadth (a raised profitability target, a new chairman, resumed M&A, capacity investment, no chief-executive change), and both have already propagated into the outputs.
  • External: two sector-level demand forces are tailwinds with two-plus years' visibility
    • One cyclical force (commercial vehicles, agricultural machinery) is a headwind
    • Input costs and currency are headwinds with a lag.
  • Internal: the framework's screen rule (at least three forces strong) is met on our count: the target raise, the new-product push into data-centre transformers, and the capacity and standards programme
    • M&A is resumed but small.
  • Not A, because there is no chief-executive change, no capital-markets-day step-up in ambition beyond the margin target, and the M&A engine that the long-term target relies on (5 percent a year acquired growth) did one small deal in the last eighteen months.
External forces: are there sufficiently strong external forces acting on the company, new or intensifying, and in which direction? (RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because the strongest external forces acting on AQ are new, intensifying and tailwinds (hyperscale data-centre power equipment; European rearmament; nearshoring and local-for-local sourcing), while the headwinds (the heavy-vehicle cycle, raw-material and currency lags, European industrial stagnation) are familiar and already in the base.
  • Data-centre force: sector-level and evidenced by customer action
    • Output of medium-voltage UPS transformers doubled sequentially in Q2 2026 across Hungary, Finland, the US and the Czech Republic, capacity reached 70 systems a month, and management expects further expansion in 2027 (14 July 2026).
    • A tailwind.
  • Defence force: sector-level and multi-year
    • SEK 60m invested in northern Sweden in CNC machines and welding cells
    • UK defence sales doubled year on year
    • Several large European defence requests for proposals in the pipeline with nominations expected during or after the summer of 2026.
    • A tailwind.
  • Heavy-vehicle cycle: a headwind
    • Lower ordering volumes in Mexico
    • The wiring-system plants in Mexico and Plattsburgh underutilised
    • Marine transformers and food-packaging machinery below their 2024 to 2025 peaks (23 April 2026).
  • Not A, because the structural forces touch roughly 12 percent of sales today (data centre 6 percent; defence estimated at 5 to 7 percent by DNB Carnegie, June 2026) and the rest of the book is cyclical European and North American industrial demand.
Demand: is end-market demand changing, structurally or cyclically (a swing with two-plus years of visibility)? (KQ 1, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because end-market demand is changing structurally in the two niches that drive the thesis and cyclically elsewhere, and the net effect has already turned organic growth from minus 6.9 percent (FY2024) and 2.0 percent (FY2025) to 6.3 percent in Q1 2026 and 9.1 percent in Q2 2026.
  • Data centres: inductive components for data centres from negligible in 2024 to 6 percent of group sales by Q2 2026
    • The ABB HiPerGuard medium-voltage UPS relationship feeds hyperscale builds such as Applied Digital's Polaris Forge 2 campus
    • HVAC inductive components grew 30 percent organically in Shanghai on data-centre demand
    • Gas-turbine parts demand rose as data centres seek on-site power (23 April 2026).
  • Defence: European defence procurement is multi-year
    • Defence sales grew 18 percent in 2025, which management called disappointing against the order inflow
    • UK defence sales doubled in Q2 2026.
  • Cyclical markets: construction-equipment orders in Europe rose in Q2 2026, ahead of expectations
    • Heavy trucks, buses and agricultural machinery remain weak.
  • Not A, because the structural niches are still a minority of sales and one electrification customer is about 50 percent of data-centre sales (chief executive, Q2 2026 call), so the demand change is concentrated as well as strong.
Revenue and demand cycle: where is the company's own cycle, and is it turning? (KQ 6, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because the company's own cycle has turned up: organic growth minus 9 percent in Q4 2024, minus 6.9 percent for FY2024, 2.0 percent for FY2025, 6.3 percent in Q1 2026 and 9.1 percent in Q2 2026, and the turn is volume-led, not price-led, unlike 2022.
  • Mix, not market: the upturn is driven by the structural niches rather than a broad cyclical recovery
    • European industrial demand is "highly uncertain" in DNB Carnegie's words, and Germany's 2026 GDP forecast was cut to 0.5 percent (3 June 2026).
  • Early upcycle: global PMIs at 52.6 suggest an early upcycle (25 June 2026).
  • Not A, because the cyclical half of the book has not turned
    • The company's cycle is turning on mix, not on the market.
Political and regulatory: is a change moving demand, pricing or cost? (KQ 4, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because the regulatory and political forces net to a tailwind for a local-for-local manufacturer: nearshoring and domestic-sourcing rules of the DFAR kind push defence primes and OEMs toward regional sub-suppliers (expert call, 20 April 2026), tariffs have had negligible direct impact on management's account, and the USD 3.7m Wytheville expansion puts transformer output inside the US tariff wall.
  • Headwinds: EU compliance mandates arriving "almost monthly", called a constant tax on productivity by the chief executive (Q2 2025 call)
    • The EU Steel Overcapacity Regulation from 1 July 2026 doubling out-of-quota duties to 50 percent
    • Pillar II minimum-tax payments expected from 2027, which will raise the average tax rate because of profits in Bulgaria, Estonia and Lithuania (interim report, 14 July 2026).
  • Not A, because the tax and steel-duty headwinds are dated and real, and the Pillar II effect bears directly on our earnings path.
Supply side: input costs, availability, concentration? (KQ 5, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because input costs are rising (copper above USD 14,000 a tonne in May 2026 with Goldman Sachs's 2027 forecast at USD 13,800; steel up 36.7 percent year on year at a three-year high; electronic components rising on AI demand) and AQ passes them through with a 3 to 6 month lag and no derivative hedging, so the direction is a headwind with a lag rather than a structural problem.
  • The lag: the single most-cited margin mechanism across the research
    • The clauses do recover the cost in time.
  • Geopolitical supply: insulated
    • No units or significant suppliers in the Middle East, Russia, Ukraine or Belarus.
  • Not D, because pricing was on a par with or slightly below inflation in Q2 2026 and growth was volume-led (chief executive, Q2 call), so the lag has not yet bitten
    • Not B, because the copper and steel moves are large and recent.
Disruption: is a disruption force strengthening or being disproved? (KQ 3, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because the disruption forces are two-sided and mid-term: the 800V DC data-centre architecture (orders expected from 2028, developed with NVIDIA; 40 to 50 percent of the AI market by 2030 on DNB Carnegie's reading) could bypass component suppliers if AQ is not designed into the reference architectures, while vehicle electrification creates demand for battery enclosures.
  • Value migration: Deutsche Bank (28 June 2026) warns that value migrates to large grid-to-rack vendors as architectures centralise
    • Morgan Stanley (1 July 2026) sees automotive suppliers (Aptiv, Lear) extending high-voltage expertise into data-centre power, with high qualification hurdles.
  • Solid-state cooling: under 5 percent of the liquid-cooling market (28 May 2026)
    • Not yet a force.
  • Not D, because adoption of DC is phased (arc-flash safety, an immature ecosystem) and AQ's current medium-voltage AC output is in demand
    • Not B, because the next-generation design wins are not yet evidenced.
Competition: is competitive intensity easing or tightening? (KQ 2, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because competitive intensity in contract manufacturing is regional, intense and unchanged in direction: price competition in India has compressed peer Incap's EBITA margin to 9.0 percent in H1 2026 from a 2015 to 2025 median of 13.4 percent (31 July 2026), while AQ's own margins have held and risen.
  • Customer pressure: existing customers squeeze contract manufacturers' margins at renewals (17 June 2026)
    • Blue-chip OEMs demand annual productivity price cuts of 3 to 5 percent.
  • AQ's defence: the decentralised, customer-close model and lower overheads defend share (Schaffner could not compete with AQ's Bulgarian cost base, on DNB Carnegie's account)
    • Peer KB Components pursues a similar roll-up.
  • Not D, because there is no evidence of share loss or a price war touching AQ's niches
    • Not B, because nothing is easing.
Macroeconomic: is a macro force acting on this company more than on the universe? (KQ 7, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because the macro forces act on AQ about as they act on the universe's industrials: currency is the one that acts more (73 percent of sales in non-SEK currencies, a 10 percent move worth SEK 664m of sales, no hedging; annual report 2025), and it was a headwind of SEK 267m in 2025 and SEK 113m in H1 2026 but only SEK 3m in Q2 2026.
  • Interest rates: help rather than hurt
    • Net cash excluding leases SEK 817m at Q2 2026
    • A 1 point rate change is worth about SEK 4m of profit (annual report 2025).
  • Krona: a "structurally supportive" outlook for the Swedish krona in the research is a translation headwind for a company reporting in SEK with 73 percent foreign sales.
  • Not D, because the balance sheet removes the financing risk and the natural hedge is real
    • Not B, because the currency exposure is an outlier against hedged peers (Note hedges 40 percent of EUR; Scanfil holds EUR 146m of forwards).
Catch-all: any other material external dynamic, positive or negative? (KQ 8, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) optional Rating = C
