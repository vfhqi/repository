[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = D

Thesis. Volex is a self-help compounder with a cyclical overlay, and the price has moved before we can. Ten years of guiding under and delivering over have taken the margin from 2 percent to 10.2 percent, and the last year's doubling of high-speed copper sales to hyperscalers has carried that change through the whole chain into the sell-side's numbers and the share price. At 635.0p, about 14 times the earnings two years out and the 84th percentile of the stock's own multiple history, our modal return of about 5 to 7 percent a year does not clear the 20 percent bar, because the one leg that would carry it, the multiple holding while the largest customer keeps spending, is an even bet that the price already assumes.

  • The change of the last twelve months is external, structural in its market and concentrated in one relationship.
    • Hyperscaler spending on copper interconnect inside the AI rack doubled Volex's data-centre revenue to about 236 USDm in FY2026 from 118 USDm, took the largest customer from 8 percent to 18.7 percent of group revenue, and lifted organic growth to 14.2 percent.
    • The force is of 'Demand Inflection' strength in that segment, and that segment is about a fifth of revenue.
    • Two other fifths, Consumer Electricals and Medical, fell 7.8 percent and 10.5 percent organically in FY2026 on Chinese pricing diverted into Europe and on OEM destocking, so the aggregate external picture is mixed, not a tailwind.
    • Competition is tightening in the very line that carries the growth: Volex is a secondary supplier there, pricing 5 to 8 percent below the tier-one leaders, on contracts of one to two years.
  • The change inside the company is the scaling of a ten-year self-help, not a new direction.
    • The same team delivered its five-year plan a year early, raised guidance twice inside FY2026 and beat it, reset the targets at the April 2026 Capital Markets Day to 2.0 billion USD of revenue and a 12 percent margin, and bought the rest of Kepler SignalTek in July 2026.
    • At least four of the framework's internal forces are strong.
    • None of them is new.
  • 'Financial Outputs' have turned up where the equity is priced, and not where cash is made.
    • Underlying operating profit rose 19.9 percent to 127.3 USDm, the margin broke a five-year corridor of 9 to 10 percent, underlying EPS rose 19.8 percent to 43.5 US cents, and consensus EPS for the first forecast year rose 33.5 percent in the twelve months to 2026-09-18.
    • Cash conversion fell to 63.9 percent, inventory rose 36 percent to 269.1 USDm and receivable days stretched to 72, because the hyperscaler's hub model puts its inventory on Volex's balance sheet.
  • The change has travelled all six layers of the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain, which is precisely why the price has moved.
    • The same people, a new plan, dated actions, key operating metrics, the financial statements and the sell-side's estimates have all moved.
    • The shares rose 79.4 percent in the twelve months to 2026-09-21 and the 24-month-forward multiple rose from a five-year median of 10.90x to 13.82x (2026-09-18).
  • What we think it earns: underlying EPS from 43.5 US cents in FY2026 to about 0.51 USD in FY2027, 0.565 USD in FY2028, 0.63 USD in FY2029 and 0.70 USD in FY2030, about 13 percent a year over three years, inside the framework's 12 to 20 percent band on every horizon.
    • About 9 percent a year of that is revenue and about 3 percent a year is margin, the two components investors extrapolate
      • the below-EBIT lines and the share count are near neutral.
  • What it costs: 8.19 USD (613p) on 2026-09-18, 13.82 times the 24-month-forward consensus EPS of 0.593 USD
    • above the ten-year 'defendable' band of 12.40x and below the 'mania' band of 15.60x
    • 14 percent below the sector median of 16.4x, in a set whose members earn 15 to 25 percent margins against Volex's 10 percent.
  • The return fails the bar, and the multiple is the reason.
    • On the framework's convention the modal return is about 5 percent a year over eighteen months and about 7 percent a year over three years, because the earnings leg (+18 percent and +35 percent on the 24-month-forward figure) is taken back by a re-rating leg of about -10 percent, from 13.82x to a blended 12.4x, and the cash leg is about 1 percent a year.
    • At an unchanged 13.82x the same earnings give about 12.5 percent a year over eighteen months and about 11 percent a year over three years, a D.
      • Only the upside branch, the plan's 12 percent margin delivered with the multiple held at 14.5x, clears 20 percent, and that needs two conditions at odds of roughly one in five to one in six.
  • The pattern is 'Compounder', at B.
    • A proven upgrader with a named under-modelled line, the operating margin, which consensus carries at 11.2 percent by FY2029 against a 12 percent target that Zeus excludes on purpose.
    • The fit stops short of crisp because the mis-modelling is a chosen conservatism rather than a mechanical error, and because the market has already paid for part of the upgrade it expects.
  • Where the chain holds and where it breaks.
    • The inputs are strong (B), the outputs are improving (B), and the third link, the return, fails (F) at this price.
    • In this Building Block the parts bind, because capital is committed for the return and not for the earnings.

  • What would make it a case: a price at or below about 500p, where the modal path clears the bar over eighteen months, or evidence at the H1 FY2027 results on 5 November 2026 that the data-centre line held with the largest customer's share stable and cash conversion recovering above 70 percent, which would move the multiple we apply from 12.4x toward 14x.

  • Rating = D, because the expected change is material to the earnings (change forces B, outputs B, EPS growth inside the target band) but not, at this price, to the return: the modal return is about 5 to 7 percent a year against a 20 percent bar, and the re-rating that the framework counts as one of three legs has already happened.

    • Not C, because even at an unchanged multiple the modal return is only about 11 to 12 percent a year.
    • Not F, because the earnings chain is B-rated and the upside branch is a real, named mechanism with a record behind it: about 16 percent a year over eighteen months with modal earnings at a held multiple, and above 20 percent with the plan's earnings.

Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.

  • The case against, at its strongest: the change is a continuation of a ten-year self-help that the market has now fully recognised, and the last year's step-up came from one customer doubling its orders under a hub model that put the inventory on Volex's balance sheet.
    • That customer treats Volex as a secondary source for capacity and price, holds it to one-to-two-year contracts, and can move allocation to Amphenol as supply loosens or to optics as speeds rise.
    • If its spending normalises, revenue growth falls to the mid-single digits the other segments deliver, the margin slips toward the 10 percent floor as the most profitable line shrinks, cash conversion does not recover because the inventory was built for volumes that do not come, and the multiple returns to its two-year median of 12.0x or lower.
    • Two brokers, Deutsche Bank and Jefferies, say part of FY2026's doubling was customer pull-forward and competitors' capacity shortfalls that may not recur
      • Wistron classifies Volex as a reliable but limited secondary supplier
      • Huatai expects Amphenol to become sole supplier of copper connections for Nvidia AI systems.
  • What we concede: the doubling was partly a capacity event
    • the contracts are short
    • the customer holds the power, and the working capital shows it
    • the multiple at the 84th percentile leaves no cushion
    • and the modal return fails the bar on our own arithmetic.
    • This is why the rating is D and the recommendation is to wait.
  • Why our view holds, as a view on the business rather than the shares: the competing hypothesis needs the other four segments and the plan's internal levers to be inert, and they are not.
    • Off-Highway grew 20 percent organically to 279.3 USDm on a major European and US defense vehicle programme and North American agricultural and construction ramps
      • Kepler adds a 10 percent-growth medical line at above-group margins
      • the 25 USDm Mexico vertical-integration project and the Turkish site consolidation are dated cost actions.
    • Consumer and Medical are at troughs rather than in structural decline: Consumer recovered 9.6 percent organically in FY2025 before the tariff diversion, and the medical OEMs describe their destocking as cash optimisation.
    • The record of guiding under and delivering over is the one fact in the evidence that no source contradicts.
    • If the data-centre line halved rather than flattened, the group would still grow revenue in the mid-single digits and hold a margin near 10 percent
      • the sell-side's own downside is 465p, at an 8.5 percent margin and sales 20 percent below estimates.
  • Where the case against wins: on the entry price.
    • At 613p to 635p the shares carry the bull branch and not the bear one.
    • The competing hypothesis describes the downside branch of our own case, not a different case
    • we give it even odds on the data-centre condition and price it into the multiple.

Disruption: is a disruption force strengthening or being disproved? (KQ 3, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because the optical substitution force is neither strengthening decisively nor disproved: copper is being extended to 800G and 1.6T by retimers and remains favoured inside the rack on power and cost, while the physical limits at 3.2T and co-packaged optics are acknowledged by the sell-side and, on Jefferies' reading, possibly downplayed by management.
  • For copper: copper eliminates optical engines and lasers, giving a competitive total cost of ownership and power savings
    • Jefferies holds that copper's lifespan in the data centre is secure because power is the primary constraint.
    • NVIDIA GB200 racks contain 12,000 to 14,000 USD of copper interconnect content.
  • Against copper: attenuation and crosstalk rise at higher speeds and optics are expected to dominate in the long term
    • BizLink has bought Xin Fu Sheng Optoelectronics for fibre capability and Amphenol bought CommScope's connectivity business.
    • The risks report ranks the optical crossover as the highest-plausibility failure pathway.
  • Not D, because the near-term direction (copper content per rack rising with power density) is a tailwind.
    • Not B, because the company has no stated optical capability and its largest customer could move the mix.
Supply side: input costs, availability, concentration? (KQ 5, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = D
  • Rating = D, because input costs and labour inflation are rising (copper +32 percent year on year to 13,303 USD per tonne in June 2026; Turkish wages rising twice a year) and pass-through, though contractual, carries a one-to-two-month lag and does not cover local overheads, while component suppliers' bargaining power is rising with AI orders.
  • Pass-through: copper and PVC volatility is fully protected by indexation in most contracts with a one-to-two-month lag
    • where indexation is absent, LME-linked forwards are used.
    • Hedged copper fell to 710 tonnes in FY2026 from 837 tonnes.
  • Turkey: labour costs rise twice a year
    • lira depreciation broadly offsets in euro terms
    • 500 jobs were removed at Murat Ticaret.
    • The change-forces report's warning threshold is annual labour inflation in Turkey above 30 percent.
  • Components: suppliers are moving to capacity-reservation agreements with price flexibility
    • vendor utilisation is rising
    • customer-approved vendor lists restrict substitution.
  • Not C, because the direction of every supply-side line is adverse in the last twelve months.
    • Not F, because the pass-through mechanisms are real and currency had only a 0.3 percentage-point adverse margin effect in FY2026.
Political and regulatory: is a change moving demand, pricing or cost? (KQ 4, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because tariffs move demand toward Volex's multi-site footprint, a mild tailwind the company sells as transfer capability, while the Turkish tax change and the risk of EV mandate rollback move cost and demand the other way.
  • Tariff exposure: only 12 percent of group revenue is exposed to US tariff changes (2 percent direct China, 4 percent non-USMCA Mexico, 6 percent other)
    • 100 percent of direct tariff costs are passed through.
    • Customers relocate programmes across the 23 sites and the company has gained share from "China + 1" sourcing (Zeus).
  • Turkish tax: Turkey cancelled inflation adjustments for corporate tax for 2025 to 2027 in December 2025, a 4.2 USDm adjusting charge in H2 FY2026 and, in the company's words, severe effective tax rate volatility.
  • EV and defense: EV incentive and mandate rollback risk exists in Western markets
    • the Canadian Off-Highway partnership (49 percent retained) qualifies as indigenous-owned for restricted defense tenders.
  • Not B, because the Turkish tax change is a recurring cost for three years, and the tariff advantage is temporary relative to competitors replicating the footprint (Dhoot in the UK and Slovakia).
