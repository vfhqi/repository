Volex (VLX-GB): what the half-year results of 12 November 2026, and the response to them, do to the existing case

These results and the fortnight since strengthen the case moderately: the half beat the case's profit range and the brokers raised, the defining risk (one account at renewal) is untouched, cash is better but not resolved, and the share price has fallen through both thresholds at which the case itself said the set-up turns unusually positive.

What changed

Reported for the half year to 30 September 2026, set against what the case assumed.

  • Profit above the case's range. Underlying operating profit $80.6m, up 40.9% from $57.2m; margin 11.1% against 9.8% a year earlier. The case expected $75m to $77m and 10.6% to 10.8%. The beat against the pre-results consensus of $77.5m was 4%; against Jefferies, 2%. Management says start-up costs at Suzhou, Batam and Pune took 0.6 percentage points off the margin, and that progression is unlikely to be linear.
  • Revenue as flagged, with a timing benefit. $723.6m, up 23.9% from $583.9m; organic growth 23.6%, or 21.8% excluding about $10.0m pulled forward from the second half by agreement with customers during the US site consolidation. All five end-markets grew. Organic growth excluding data centre was 19.5%.
  • The gain was below gross profit. Underlying gross margin 23.0% against 23.3%. Underlying basic EPS 27.7 cents, up 40.6%, in step with operating profit.
  • Segments. EV and Electrification $134m from $90m, up 46.8% organically, on more content with the principal EV customer rather than on vehicle volumes. Data centre up 47%, described by management and by Zeus as normalising in the second half as prior programmes annualise and temporary sourcing gains unwind. Off-Highway $144m, up 12.6%; Consumer up 9.5%; Medical up 1.8%. Kepler SignalTek contributed $11.3m. Centres of Excellence carried 49% of revenue, from 24% five years ago.
  • Cash better, not resolved. Underlying free cash flow $22.3m against $4.8m; cash conversion 56.5% against 33.4%; working capital still an outflow, $29.1m against $32.4m, with inventory held high by choice to serve data-centre customers. Net debt excluding leases $203.7m against $121.5m at March 2026, mainly the acquisition; covenant leverage 1.1x against 0.8x. ROCE 23.3% for the twelve months. The review carries no working-capital ratio and no inventory figure. Interim dividend 1.7p, up 6.3%; the buyback, cut to £20m from £40m to fund deals, returned £16.9m in the half and is complete.
  • Guidance held, not raised. Full-year expectations maintained; second-half revenue guided broadly in line with the first excluding the $10.0m, the buffer-stock drawdown offset by a full six months of Kepler SignalTek; dual-running costs in the US in the second half; capital expenditure about 5% of revenue for the year against $23.7m net in the first half. Medium-term targets ($2bn revenue, 12% margin, ROCE about 20%) reiterated. M&A discussions described as having accelerated.
  • Sell-side. Only Jefferies and Zeus published. Jefferies on 12 November raised sales and EBITA 1% to 2% for FY2027 to FY2029: FY2027 revenue to $1,435.6m from $1,415.1m, EBITA to $155.4m from $153.2m, EPS to 53.4 cents from 52.5; FY2028 EBITA up 1.9% to $165.2m; the FY2027 net debt forecast cut to $206.0m from $224.5m; Buy, 750p unchanged. Company-compiled consensus moved to $153.9m of FY2027 operating profit (range $151.5m to $156.3m) from $150.4m in October; Zeus says the second half has started well and supports the upper end. On the dashboard the buy share is 100%, consensus EPS is up 7% over three months and the sell-side letter is still A.
  • The implied second half. On our arithmetic from the review's figures, consensus implies second-half operating profit of about $73.3m on about $687.5m of revenue, a margin of about 10.7%; Jefferies implies about $74.8m on $712.0m, about 10.5%. Before the results, consensus implied about $72.9m. The beat went into the year; the second half was left where it was, 7% to 9% below the first on profit. The review's own warning line for the second half is a margin below 10.5%.
  • Price. 454p on 25 November, against 540p the day before the results and 590p when the case was written. Zeus puts the October to November fall at 14.5%, among the worst in UK electrical equipment alongside XP Power at 14.4%, and attributes it to rotation out of AI hardware "despite a firmer full-year outlook". The price is 19.1% below its 200-day average and below the 50-day (587p) and 150-day (586p); relative strength is at the 16th percentile from the 85th at the memo date; the technical letter is D from B; the valuation letter is C from F, with the P/E at 9.9x and the 45th percentile of its own ten-year range.
  • One figure to treat with care. The review states that high-speed copper cabling was 30% of first-half revenue. On the case's figures the data-centre line is about 17% of this half and the whole Complex Industrial Technology segment about 30%. We read the 30% as the segment. If it is the line, the case's central quantity is nearly twice what the case holds, and every renewal figure scales with it.

The challenge

First, the reading that still fails. Data centre up 47% is not a re-acceleration. The case put the line at about $85m a year earlier and about $125m in this half, a 47% rise and a sequential fall from about $150m; management and Zeus both call the line normalising. The plateau stands, and the review's phrase "temporary sourcing gains unwound" is the case's own account.

The gearing reading gained a little force, and the sell-side has not taken it. The case says operating leverage is modest (incremental margins of 10% to 14%) and that EPS outgrows revenue below the line. This half: revenue up $139.7m, operating profit up $23.4m, an incremental margin of about 17%, with gross margin down, so all of it came from overheads growing slower than sales. New since the morning of the results is management's statement that start-up costs took 0.6 points off the margin, which puts the underlying first-half margin near 11.7%, the case's FY2030 figure. Below the line added nothing: EPS grew 40.6% against operating profit's 40.9%. If this is right, the 12% target is reachable without acquisitions and the case's margin path (10.8%, 11.1%, 11.4%) is a year or two slow. For it to be right, the second-half margin has to hold near 11% on flat revenue through the dual-running costs. Against it: the brokers who heard the same call raised FY2027 by 1% to 2% and left the second half at 10.5% to 10.7%; the case has seen a 17% incremental margin before, on data-centre mix; and a management with this gearing in hand would probably have raised the year. Plausible, not probable; unchanged in standing.

The "flat guidance is information" reading splits. Its first leg, that an unchanged full year after a 4% beat means a lower second half, is dropped: the brokers already carried a second half below the first before the results, passed the beat into the year and did not cut the half, and Zeus reports the half starting well. There was no new information in the flat guide. Its second leg stands and is unanswered. Capital expenditure steps up to about 5% of revenue, roughly $47m in the second half on our arithmetic against $23.7m in the first, into new sites at Suzhou, Batam, Pune and Bangalore described as customer-backed, with no disclosure of what backs them, a year before the renewal the case treats as the defining risk. Either management has sight of an allocation the case gives about a 20% chance, or fixed cost is going in ahead of a decision the customer controls, in which case the case's renewal-loss arithmetic ($16m to $19m of lost contribution) understates the loss because it omits stranded fixed cost and depreciation. Which it is depends on contract terms the company does not publish.

The concentration reading gained force, and the review now carries it as an open question. EV and Electrification was $134m in the half, 18.5% of revenue on our arithmetic, up from $90m, and management attributes the growth to a larger share of its principal customer's requirements. Annualised, the half runs about $268m against the case's about $227m for FY2027. The review's own conclusion asks whether double-digit EV growth can hold "as the primary customer's vehicle volumes normalize" and whether other automakers diversify the base "away from Tesla", and it names Tesla concentration as a route to margin compression. The case files EV charging in the defended, price-making half and rests its 5.5% to 6% organic growth on breadth. The alternative is that the group now has two wallet-share surges at two customers, the second a year behind the first, and that the case's "two customers at 29.9% of revenue" is already higher. For this to be the bad version, the EV programmes would need the short tenure and re-award exposure of the data-centre work; the licence and certification position, and the fact that content rather than volume is driving it, argue against. We move this toward probable as a description of the revenue mix, and leave its risk consequence at plausible.

The new reading is about the price, and it is the one that matters most. At 454p the shares sit, on the case's own values, at about 9.5x its FY2028 EPS, below the ten-year "prudent" median of 10.2x; the renewal-loss branch at 10x (525p) is now 16% above the price and the design-loss branch at 9x (420p) about 7% below it. From 454p the case's unchanged September 2027 value of 7.31 GBP is about +61% before dividends (about +54% on the cross-check basis), and its March 2029 value of 8.73 GBP about +92%. The case said at 590p that the renewal risk was "the one the narrative has priced least"; at 454p it is, on the case's arithmetic, roughly fully priced. There are two readings of that, and the case holds only one. The first is the case's: a 1.77 beta to the data-centre theme in a risk-off regime maps a 10% basket fall to about an 18% Volex fall before any company news, and the set-up becomes unusually positive "if the shares fall toward 500p on sector noise without company news". The evidence favours it: Zeus attributes the fall to rotation, XP Power fell alongside, BizLink beat its third quarter with a strengthened outlook, Jefferies' 24 November AI server power outlook describes continuing specification migration, no estimate was cut, no rating changed, the buy share is 100%, and the company's own news was a beat with guidance held. The second reading is that the market is pricing something about the account that the case and the brokers do not have: the sequential fall in the line, the pull-forward, the buffer stock held rather than released, leverage up to 1.1x and capital going into capacity are read together as a peak-and-roll, and the Investors Chronicle headline on the day was "Data centre slowdown hits Volex shares". For this to be right, a concession or allocation change would have to be visible to someone; nothing in the review supports that, the renewal is a year away, and the demand series the case watches are all up. We rate the first reading probable and the second possible. The honest qualification is that the case's condition said "without company news" and the fall straddled results; the results were not adverse to the case, but the case's words were stricter than that.

One further reading the case does not hold: the working capital is strategic, not transitory. The case's cash judgement treats the 28.4% working-capital ratio as the by-product of a hub build that unwinds as the line plateaus, taking the ratio to 26.5% by March 2027. This half the line fell sequentially and working capital still absorbed $29.1m, and management described holding more buffer stock than contract manufacturers as "a powerful commercial competitive advantage" that secures rapid delivery for data-centre clients. If the inventory is a chosen weapon, the ratio does not fall on the plateau, the case's path has no sponsor inside the company, and the case's own penalty applies: EPS about -1%, the multiple about a point, about 10% of value. For it to be right, the March 2027 ratio stays near 28% and full-year cash conversion sits well below the recovery toward 100% that Zeus projects. Against it: free cash flow more than quadrupled, conversion rose 23 points, Jefferies cut its FY2027 net debt forecast by 8%, and management says the drag normalises as reinvestment moderates. Plausible. At 454p the multiple point it costs is already more than paid.

What it does to the case

The ratings store carries no Headline or Building Block entries; we name the four Master Ratings the memo set and the topics beneath them. On Setups' Fit/Crispness the three sources disagree (C in the memo, B in the ratings file, D on the dashboard); we take the memo's C.

  • Thesis's Change Forces' Strength : less pressure. FY2027 is more secure, the margin is ahead of the case's path, and Jefferies lifted FY2028 EBITA 1.9%. Not a full grade on one half: the forces are still a continuation and the gearing reading is unconfirmed.
  • Foundations' Robustness : the same. Delivery and ROCE of 23.3% support it; cash conversion of 56.5%, leverage up and a second concentrated line press the other way.
  • Setups' Fit/Crispness : the same. The mechanical-EPS element was not visible (EPS grew in step with operating profit); the serial-acquirer element has accelerated discussions and no deal.
  • Case Riskiness : the same, with the pressure running slightly adverse: capacity and leverage rising a year ahead of the renewal, and the EV line now looking like the data-centre line did a year ago. The price fall does not change the riskiness of the forecasts.
  • Pricing power and operating leverage topic : less pressure, on the gearing reading and the 0.6-point start-up drag.
  • Guidance and delivery topic : the same; a 4% beat with guidance held is the record.
  • Scope for upgrades topic : the same, but the case's forecast has been used: the 1% to 3% drift it expected on FY2027 happened on the day (consensus $150.4m to $153.9m), so what remains is what the case said, nil on FY2028 without the gearing reading or a deal.
  • Current momentum topic : clearly more pressure, by a full grade on its own measures: relative strength 16th percentile from 85th, price 19.1% below the 200-day average and below all three averages, technical letter D from B. Sell-side momentum is still A and the buy share 100%; the case's decay to B or C by January is arithmetic still ahead.
  • Lifecycle topic : more pressure toward stalling confirmed. The cash evidence the case set for 12 November did not arrive and the data-centre share was not restated; the price series now says stalling plainly. Nothing yet says collapsing: no cut, no downgrade, sector demand series up.
  • Fair multiple topic : less pressure by at least a full grade. The F rested on a trailing P/E at the 92nd percentile; the dashboard now shows 9.9x at the 45th, and the pinned valuation letter has gone from F to C. On the case's forward EPS the shares are at about 9.5x against its 12.0x to 12.6x fair range.
  • Total shareholder return topic : less pressure by at least a full grade. The case's values are unchanged; from 454p its base return to September 2027 is about +61% before dividends, its renewal-loss branch about +16%, its design-loss branch about -7%, and its 1.6T-plus-deals branch about +85% to +94%. On the case's own probabilities the weighted return is about +50% against the +17% that set the C.
  • Price against the case's thresholds: 525p (9x base, 10x renewal-loss earnings) crossed; 500p crossed; the price now sits between the case's 470p (renewal loss at 9x) and 420p (design loss at 9x), and below the 465p to 490p the case recorded as Jefferies' downside scenario. The case's test of unusually positive skew is met on its arithmetic if the fall is read as sector-driven; whether it is so read is the memo's call, not ours.

What would settle it

On the questions left to this review:

  • Did the second-half consensus move down? Settled: no. The beat went into the year and the second half stayed at about $73m to $75m of operating profit, a margin of 10.5% to 10.7%, where it already was. Only two brokers published; the dashboard's consensus EPS of 0.588 and target of about 747p carry the rest.
  • Working capital and net debt on the case's basis. Not settled by this review, which carries net debt excluding leases ($203.7m) and the working-capital flow ($29.1m out) but not the ratio or the lease-inclusive figure. On the case's own lease adjustment of about $30m the comparable figure is roughly $235m against its $200m to $215m, on our arithmetic. The ratio is in the published half-year balance sheet now; the next scheduled statement of it on the case's basis is the June 2027 results.
  • The second-half margin. Not settled. Above about 11% confirms the gearing reading; the brokers sit at 10.5% to 10.7%; the review's warning line is 10.5%; the case's unrevised FY2027 figure implies about 10.4%. Direction at the April 2027 pre-close, the figure at the June 2027 results. A January 2027 update is possible on last year's pattern and is not committed.
  • Customer shares, and the size of the line. The June 2027 annual report carries the largest customers' shares; that resolves both the EV concentration reading and the 30% question above. Nothing earlier does.
  • What backs the capacity. Not committed. Nothing scheduled answers it before the November 2027 half-year, when the renewal itself shows.

Still open after this review:

  • Whether the fall is sector noise. Partly settled in favour: the attribution, the peer fall, the demand series. The remaining test is behaviour, not disclosure: whether the shares recover with the AI complex or stay down while it recovers, whether a first downgrade appears, and the external series the case already names, hyperscaler 2027 capital-expenditure guidance in late January 2027, OFC on 7 to 11 March and GTC on 14 to 18 March 2027.
  • Whether the working capital is strategic. The March 2027 ratio and FY2027 cash conversion at the June 2027 results. Management's own words this half are the first evidence, and they point the wrong way for the case.
  • The renewal. November 2027. The review adds that hyperscalers move to next-generation high-speed cables from FY2028 and that co-packaged optics is unlikely to affect copper before 2029, which bounds the technology leg of the risk but says nothing about the allocation.
