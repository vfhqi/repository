Section 4 – Access Agreement Classification: IFRS 16 Lease Splits and Service Costs

Section summary
  1. Zegona's access agreements are split between IFRS 16 lease liabilities and operational service costs using incremental borrowing rates.
  2. The lease liability calculations use present value discounting, directly shifting cash outflows from operating to financing activities.
  3. Broader telecom peers utilize comparable fiber joint venture structures to optimize off-balance sheet capital expenditure and financing.
  4. Zegona faces structured wholesale network costs across its Spanish fiber joint ventures.
  5. Legacy cable migration costs are estimated at €150 per subscriber but incur no incremental wholesale opex
  6. Pricing dynamics are improving as cheap own-network tariffs are removed to protect retail margins under wholesale-only economics.

IFRS 16 Lease Classification and Discount Rates

1.
Accounting policy baseline: Zegona's accounting policies state that lease liabilities are initially measured at the present value of the lease payments that are not paid at the commencement date.
2.
Incremental borrowing rates: The company discounts these future lease payments using the incremental borrowing rates of the applicable Group entity because the rate implicit in the lease is not readily determinable.
3.
Lease valuation sensitivity: Zegona's annual reports indicate that a key assumption in assessing the fair value of right-of-use assets is the incremental borrowing rate, where a change of +/-0.25% would impact the fair value of the leases by +/-€0.3m.
4.
Historical rate sensitivity: In earlier reporting periods, Zegona disclosed that a +/-0.25% change in the incremental borrowing rate would increase or decrease the fair value of the leases by a much larger +/-€3.4m.
5.
Right-of-use asset matching: At the inception of a lease, Zegona recognizes a right-of-use asset equal to the initial lease liability, adjusted for direct costs, which is subsequently depreciated on a straight-line basis.
