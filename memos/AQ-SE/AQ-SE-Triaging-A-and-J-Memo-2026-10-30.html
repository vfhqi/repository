#0 Summary: key insights, implications and conclusions

What this means for Richard: hold for the next two reports, buy at SEK 200 to 210, re-rate on a deal

AQ's fundamental outcomes over the next 18 months are well underwritten and skewed modestly positive. At SEK 227, however, the probability-weighted 18-month return is only +6 to +8 percent, because the share price already sits near the top of AQ's normal valuation range. The practical plan has three parts:

  1. At SEK 227, hold AQ into the February and April 2027 reports. Both reports are more likely to beat than to miss, and sell-side upgrades are likely to continue until then.
  2. Near SEK 200 to 210, buy. If the AI-financing scare pushes the price to 18 to 19x two-year-forward EPS while estimates stay intact, the same scenarios give an expected 18-month return of about +15 percent.
  3. At any price, re-rate the stock if the new chair opens the balance sheet. A sizeable acquisition is the only source of upgrades that can run through 2027-28.

Estimate upgrades are likely for two more reports and then turn negative on operations. The case fits Viewforth's Target Setups only partly: AQ is a Demand-driven EPSU (earnings-per-share upgrade) inside a compounder, and most of that upgrade is already delivered and priced.

What AQ is. AQ is a proven, net-cash, decentralised contract manufacturer. It has made a profit in every quarter since 1994, and its EPS has compounded at 17 percent a year since 2019.

AQ is two businesses in one set of accounts. A small, fast-growing, higher-margin structural slice (medium-voltage data-centre magnetics, defence and grid) sits on top of a large cyclical base (harnesses, sheet metal, moulding and systems). The structural slice has lifted the group margin to a record 10 percent and has relabelled the stock from "Nordic electronics manufacturing services" to "AI-power and defence compounder". The cyclical base has just come through a two-year trough and is growing again.

The next 18 months deliver steady EPS growth but no acceleration. EPS should grow about 10 to 11 percent a year, and EBIT is unusually predictable. AQ's organic growth rate, however, peaks now (Q3-Q4 2026) and slows on schedule from Q2 2027, falling through the company's own 10 percent marker.

The return is almost entirely earnings, so the multiple decides the outcome. At the guideline multiple of 22.8x the 18-month return is +21.7 percent, and nearly all of it comes from earnings growth. The multiple is the swing factor. About three-quarters of the multiple's movement depends on AQ's own growth rate and about one-quarter on the AI-infrastructure trade, which the market is currently selling.

Eight insights that matter most.

1
Q3 2026's 17.9 percent organic growth came mostly from the cyclical base, not from data centres. Data centres (about 7 percent of sales, growing above 60 percent) added about 2.6 points and defence about 1 point. The base grew about 12 percent organically against a normal comparison quarter (Q3 2025 organic growth was +4.6 percent, not a trough). The 2027 slowdown is therefore mainly a question about European trucks and machinery, and only secondly a question about data centres. The market's framing (DNB Carnegie attributes the acceleration to data centres and defence) is wrong in both directions: it overstates AQ's dependence on AI capital spending and understates AQ's exposure to the base cycle. The base cycle holds both the near-term upside (Volvo truck orders +26 percent; Volvo expects North American Class 8 truck volumes +13 percent in 2027) and the second-half-2027 downside (North American dealer inventories at 3.3 months; agriculture at the bottom of its cycle).
2
AQ's earnings are predictable; its multiple is not. The plausible range for FY2027 EPS is about minus 15 to plus 10 percent around SEK 9.78. The plausible range for the multiple over the same window (15.7x to 24.6x, on the stock's ten-year bands) moves the share price by minus 31 to plus 8 percent. The earnings risk is skewed up through the February and April 2027 reports and skewed down thereafter.
3
Consensus EPS for FY2027-28 is 3 to 4 percent too high, for reasons that are dated and knowable. First, consensus implies a tax rate falling to 19.7 percent in 2027, although AQ's Pillar II (global minimum tax) accruals already run at 20 percent; the tax note in the annual report, due in week 13 of 2027, will settle the question. Second, consensus needs about 10 percent organic growth in 2027 with no acquisitions, which the base cannot deliver in the second half. Even so, the next two quarterly reports are more likely to beat than to miss. Revision breadth should run four-of-four brokers raising, then mixed, then net negative by autumn 2027.
4
AQ's balance sheet is the upside that no forecast includes. In the base case net cash compounds to about SEK 1.9bn by 2030. The company's ambition to double EPS by 2031 needs about SEK 3bn deployed in acquisitions at 6x EBITA. Management now says explicitly that it wants more deals, and consensus excludes acquisitions by convention, so every deal mechanically raises estimates. Acquisitions are the only engine that can keep the stock in the Broadening phase after mid-2027.
5
AQ's margin is capped near 10 percent, and price increases add nothing to it. AQ is a price-taker selling to concentrated original equipment manufacturers (OEMs). In data centres ABB captures the pricing rent: the transformer makers Hammond and Tamura report price gains, while AQ reports none. Two threats arriving in 2027-29 are not discussed by any broker covering AQ, so AQ's shareholders are unlikely to know about them: new North American competitor capacity (Hammond in Fort Worth, Tamura in Tijuana), and the re-award of supply contracts as data-centre power moves to 800-volt direct current and solid-state transformers.
6
The guideline multiple of 22.8x is the top of the range I would defend. My central multiple over 18 months is 21x, between the five-year median of 20.9x and the ten-year 75th percentile of 22.1x. The 18-month return is about +14 percent at 21x, about +9 percent at 20x and +21.7 percent at the guideline 22.8x.
7
October's de-rating came from the AI theme, not from AQ's fundamentals. The share price fell 9 percent from SEK 248 while consensus FY2027 EPS rose 8 percent and all four brokers raised their numbers. The forward multiple fell from the 91st to the 49th percentile of its five-year range. Near-term sentiment is therefore weaker than near-term fundamentals, which is the favourable part of the setup.
8
At today's price the probability-weighted 18-month return is only mid-single digits; the skew Richard looks for appears near SEK 200 to 210. Weighting the bear case at 25 percent (about minus 30 percent), the base case at 55 percent (about +14 percent on 21x) and the bull case at 20 percent (about +30 percent) gives roughly +6 to +8 percent (calculated). At about SEK 200 to 210 (18 to 19x two-year-forward EPS) the same scenarios give roughly +15 percent. A sizeable acquisition announcement would create the same skew at the current price.

Scorecard

#1 Tailwinds in the operating environment, and the absence of headwinds

Key messageStrong structural tailwinds act on AQ's best 14 percent of sales, while the headwinds act mostly on timing, tax and the multiple. One factor, financing conditions, links the data-centre and base-business risks, so the bear case is one shock rather than two.

Evidence: three structural tailwinds act on AQ's highest-margin products, with two or more years of visibility, and the cyclical base has turned up.

  • Data-centre power. AQ's inductive components for electrification grew more than 60 percent year on year in Q3 2026. Output of medium-voltage transformers for uninterruptible power supplies (UPS) doubled sequentially in Q2 to 70 systems a month, and every AQ data-centre factory runs at full capacity. DNB Carnegie says demand is "developing faster than management expected". ABB, one tier up the supply chain, confirms the demand: ABB Electrification's Q3 2026 orders were about USD 7.7bn, up 70 percent organically, with data-centre orders up by triple digits and a book-to-bill of 1.41.
  • Defence. AQ has added SEK 60m of new capacity in northern Sweden, its UK defence sales have doubled, and it is bidding on several large requests for proposal, with supplier nominations expected in late 2026.
  • Grid. The Swedish and Finnish grid operators, Svenska kraftnät and Fingrid, plan EUR 25bn of investment over 2026-35, and grid projects regained momentum in the second half of 2026.
  • The cyclical base has turned. AQ's organic growth moved from minus 6.9 percent (FY2024) to 6.3, 9.1 and 17.9 percent in Q1-Q3 2026. The global manufacturing purchasing managers' index (PMI) is about 53. Volvo's net truck orders rose 26 percent in Q3, and Volvo's 2027 outlook is 320,000 European heavy trucks (+3 percent) and 300,000 North American Class 8 trucks (+13 percent). Volvo Construction Equipment orders rose 16 percent, and the US short-cycle index is 62 percent above its 2019 level.

Evidence: the headwinds are real, but they act mostly on timing, tax and valuation rather than on AQ's earnings power.

  • Metals. Copper above USD 14,000 a tonne and steel up 37 percent sound alarming, but the 2025 annual report puts AQ's metal content at about 2.4 percent of sales. A 10 percent copper move is therefore worth about 0.2 percent of sales, and a price spike costs 0.2 to 0.4 points of margin for one or two quarters until AQ's 3-to-6-month pass-through catches up.
  • Wages. Wage inflation in Eastern Europe, where 65 percent of AQ's staff work, is persistent.
  • Tax. Pillar II lifts AQ's tax rate from 18 percent toward 21 to 22 percent.
  • Currency. The krona turned from a 4.8 percent drag on growth in Q1 2026 to a small help in Q3 and may reverse (Morgan Stanley is bullish on the krona). 73 percent of AQ's sales are in other currencies and are unhedged.
  • Soft spots in the base markets. European truck orders are falling sequentially at AQ's own customers; North American dealer inventories are 3.3 months against a normal 2.4; September Class 8 orders fell 7 percent; agriculture is at the bottom of its cycle; German GDP forecasts for 2026 are 0.5 percent.
  • The macro and financing regime. The US 10-year yield is near 5.1 percent, credit spreads on neocloud (specialist AI-cloud) borrowers have widened about 300 basis points since August, and the AI complex is "cracking at its financing, not its demand".

Inference: the external picture is better for AQ's earnings than for its share price. The tailwinds act on volume and mix in the 14 percent of sales where AQ has design responsibility and scarce capacity. The headwinds act on timing (through pass-through lags), on a tax increase with a known date, and on the multiple. The hidden link is that both of AQ's growth engines depend on one factor, financing conditions: hyperscalers' and neoclouds' build schedules and the demand of European OEMs' customers both depend on the cost and availability of credit. A tightening of credit would therefore hit the structural slice and the cyclical base at the same time, so the bear case is one shock, not two independent ones.

So what. The model's 2027 growth for the structural slice (+50 percent in data centres) is set by AQ's capacity rather than by demand, which makes it the most predictable large driver in the forecast. The model's 2027 growth for the cyclical base (+3 percent) is the least predictable driver. The OEMs' evidence points to a first half above the model and a second half below it.

Strongest counter-case: the slice and the base could slow together. Morgan Stanley projects that hyperscaler capital spending growth will moderate to low double digits by 2028. If any of the four hyperscalers cuts its 2027 capital-spending guidance in late January 2027, the cut would reach AQ's inductive-components line within about two quarters, first as delivery deferrals. If European truck makers also cut production as dealer inventories unwind, the structural slice and the cyclical base would slow at the same time.

What would change my view. Up: ABB's Q4 2026 data-centre book-to-bill staying above 1.2, and Volvo's firm 2027 production plan at or above its outlook. Down: a hyperscaler capital-spending cut in January, Traton's book-to-bill (0.9x in Q3) falling further, or AQ reporting deferrals driven by customers rather than by its own capacity.

Versus the market. The sell-side and the share price both treat AI power as AQ's main driver. I agree that AI power is the strongest force, but the base cycle is the larger near-term variable, and the restocking pulse that helped Q3 is expected to plateau around Q4 2026. I agree with the market that defence and grid demand are durable and slow-growing.

Score: 70. Structural tailwinds with multi-year visibility act on the best part of the book, but they touch only 14 percent of sales and the headwinds, while mostly timing-driven, sit on the multiple.

#2 Internally driven corporate change forces, and the absence of problems

Key messageAQ's internal changes are positive but incremental: a higher margin standard, a richer mix and new capacity. The execution KPIs are moving the wrong way during the ramp, and delivery precision is the KPI to watch before the 2027-28 contract re-awards.

Evidence: three strong internal forces are at work.

  1. A higher margin standard. On 14 July 2026 the board lifted the target for the margin on earnings before tax (EBT) from 8 percent to above 10 percent, after 13 consecutive quarters above the old level. AQ immediately printed 9.9 percent (Q2) and 10.0 percent (Q3). The Q3 EBIT margin of 10.1 percent came in a seasonally weak quarter and on a clean base: the SEK 22m gain on the Gävle property sits in 2025 financial items, not in 2026 EBIT.
  2. A richer mix. Inductive components rose from 6 percent of revenue in 2018 to 25 percent in 2025. AQ has delivered solid-state transformer prototypes to a leading electrification customer, with production expected in 2027-28.
  3. New capacity, including acquisitions bought for capacity. Norlake (Cleveland) cost an enterprise value of USD 13.5m, mostly property and assumed liabilities; at full utilisation it can add up to SEK 300m of sales, which effectively doubles AQ's US inductive capacity under the Virginia team. AQ is also expanding at Wytheville and has promised further transformer lines for 2027. Acquisitions restarted after a deliberate pause in 2025 on price grounds: Time 24 cost GBP 1 plus about SEK 50m of debt, and Gumboda cost SEK 2m.

Supporting changes: an independent chair (Åsa Landén Ericsson) since April 2026; entry to the Large Cap segment; the Monitor enterprise resource planning (ERP) system rolled out across acquired plants; and short-term bonuses weighted 80 percent on EBT and 20 percent on inventory turns.

Evidence: AQ's problems cluster where its growth is.

  • Delivery and working capital. Delivery precision was 91.1 percent in Q3 2026 (92.7 percent in Q2, 94 percent for 2025, against a 98 percent target), the lowest since 2022; management calls it "unacceptable". Inventory turns are 2.9 against 3.5, and net working capital is 31.9 percent of trailing sales against 28.7 percent a year earlier.
  • Problem sites. AQ replaced the leadership of its Indian inductive-components factory after persistent quality and productivity problems. Time 24 lost SEK 5m after tax in Q3, and AQ sent staff from its Rockford plant to run it. The Mexico and Plattsburgh wiring plants are under-utilised after AQ lost bus volumes.
  • Quality and people. AQ made quality provisions for design defects in data-centre transformers in Q4 2025. Staff turnover is 20 percent, and one manager, Dan Day, runs both Virginia and Norlake through the ramp.
  • Governance gaps. The remuneration committee consists only of the two founders, and AQ's warrants carry no performance conditions.

Inference: AQ is improving an existing compounding engine by degree; it is not transforming. The strongest force, data-centre transformers on which AQ has design responsibility, is also where the strain shows. Manual winding does not automate, skilled winders are scarce, and AQ's organisation is lean by design. AQ's history (2017 and 2021-22) shows that growth surges create local bottlenecks, which the decentralised model usually fixes within two to four quarters. The 2022 episode was slower: AQ took three years to recover four points of delivery precision and never reached the target.

So what. Carry the margin near the new target with confidence, because the 8 percent floor has held for 14 quarters. Expect friction on cash and on the margins of acquired sites through 2027. Treat delivery precision as the KPI that matters for the 2027-28 contract re-award window: in 2023 AQ lost a SEK 250m battery-systems account over quality, which shows what a persistent delivery or quality miss costs.

Strongest counter-case: growth is outrunning the organisation. The same few managers are stretched across several turnarounds during the fastest ramp in the company's history. A second quality event in medium-voltage transformers would hit both the margin and the narrative.

What would change my view. Up: delivery precision back above 93 to 94 percent by Q1 2027, and Norlake consolidating at an annualised run-rate above USD 10m. Down: delivery precision flat for a second quarter, a Q4 provision above SEK 30m, or any disclosure that a customer is moving work to another supplier.

Versus the market. DNB Carnegie names margin dilution from acquisitions as the main internal risk. I think acquisition dilution is small and predictable (about 0.1 point a year) and that the operational KPIs are the real watch item. The market has also not priced the possibility that the new chair changes capital allocation; I put that possibility at about 20 to 25 percent by February 2028, which is material.

Score: 62. A credible, funded, narrow set of positive changes concentrated in one product area and one country step, with execution KPIs moving the wrong way during the ramp.

#3 Industry structure, business model, financial profile and management

Key messageAQ earns above-industry returns from how it operates, not from where it sits in the industry. The market's "compounder" label is half-earned: AQ's culture and capital discipline justify it, but AQ reinvests little and its growth absorbs working capital.

Evidence on industry structure: customers hold the power. European contract manufacturing is fragmented at the bottom (more than 1,900 companies, 94 percent of them small) and consolidating at the top (the largest 112 companies raised their market share from 79 to 89 percent in a decade). AQ's buyers are concentrated: Volvo Group takes about 15 percent of AQ's sales, ABB and Scania about 9 percent each, and the top ten customers about half. OEMs dual-source, demand annual productivity price cuts of 3 to 5 percent, use termination-for-convenience clauses and protect their own aftermarket margins. AQ owns no intellectual property. The industry's normal EBIT margin is 7 to 8 percent (Note 7.7 percent and Incap 8.8 percent in Q2 2026; HANZA under 4 percent for most of 2016-22).

Data-centre magnetics are the exception, but the exception is temporary and runs through one gatekeeper. Qualified capacity is scarce, equipment lead times exceed six years, and AQ is "winning every US quote". That scarcity is a capacity cycle, however, and it closes as new capacity arrives: Hammond's Fort Worth site (CAD 250m of capacity from late 2027), Tamura's Tijuana expansion and its acquisition of EMG, and programmes at Forgent, Eaton, Schneider and Siemens. AQ's access to the market also runs through one gatekeeper: ABB was about half of AQ's data-centre sales in mid-2026, although AQ now describes the business as spread across "several customers".

Evidence on the business model: AQ's advantage is operational and cultural, not structural. The advantage has five parts:

  • a cost culture, with decentralised profit-and-loss accountability across more than 40 companies;
  • a low-cost workforce, with 65 percent of staff in Bulgaria, Poland, Lithuania and Estonia;
  • a local-for-local footprint in 17 countries, which customers value as supply chains regionalise;
  • co-engineered products embedded in vehicle programmes that last five to six years;
  • a repeatable acquisition playbook: buy distressed capacity at about 6x EBITA in downturns and fill it with group volume (eight deals since 2019; at mdexx, the EBT margin went from minus 7 to plus 7 percent in about 18 months).

After-tax ROIC rose from 11.9 percent (2019) to 16.7 percent (2025) while AQ was acquiring, which is the healthy version of the serial-acquirer pattern.

Evidence on the financial profile: strong margins and earnings quality, weak cash conversion.

  • Margins and returns above the industry. EBIT margin was 9.3 percent in FY2025 and 10.1 percent in Q3 2026.
  • Best-in-class earnings quality. AQ reports no adjusted measures, capitalises no R&D and amortises conservatively; brokers' EPS is only 1 percent above reported EPS.
  • A strong balance sheet. Net cash is SEK 467m including leases (SEK 817m excluding leases), and the equity ratio is 67 to 68 percent against a 40 percent floor.
  • Below-average cash conversion. Free cash flow has averaged 83 percent of net income over seven years on FactSet's loose definition, swinging from minus 34 to 157 percent by year. The cause is net working capital of about 30 percent of sales (Bekaert 15 percent, Comer 22 percent), which absorbs roughly SEK 300m for every SEK 1bn of growth. Consensus has free-cash-flow conversion of 0.5x, 0.7x and 0.9x of net income for FY2026-28, the lowest in the peer table.
  • A lower EBITDA margin than sector peers, for a benign reason. AQ's EBITDA margin of 13 percent is below Comer's and SFS's 16 percent and Daetwyler's 19 percent because AQ is less capital-intensive, not because it is worse run.

Evidence on management: operator-stewards, with a thin bench below them. CEO James Ahrgren (since April 2021) has grown revenue 66 percent and EPS from SEK 4.00 to 7.38 over 2021-25, and DNB Carnegie scores the management 9 out of 10. He and CFO Christina Hegg (since 2020) are operator-stewards in the Lifco mould: candid about failures, disciplined on price (no deals in 2025), and setting targets in profit-and-loss terms (growth above 15 percent, EBT margin above 10 percent, EPS doubling every five years, with the next doubling due by 2031). The founders hold 34.3 percent and have never sold, and there have been no insider sales in twelve months. The bench of site leaders is the binding constraint on growth.

Inference: AQ is a high-return, moderate-reinvestment compounder. Industry structure does not give AQ pricing power, so AQ's above-industry economics come from how it operates, not from its position. Returns on new capital are high: above 30 percent pre-tax on organic volume while capacity exists, and about 17 percent at entry on acquisitions. The volume that AQ can deploy at those returns, however, is well below its cash generation: capital expenditure runs at 2.6 to 3.0 percent of sales and acquisitions at 2 to 4 percent of revenue a year. That gap is why net cash accumulates.

So what. The market pays about 19x EV/EBITA for AQ, between proven Nordic compounders (about 21x) and unproven ones (about 14x), and AQ has only half-earned that "compounder" rating. AQ's acquisition engine runs at 2 to 4 percent of revenue a year, not the 8 percent that DNB Carnegie's acquisition scenario needs, and weak cash conversion shows that AQ's growth is funded by working capital. A margin level of about 10 percent is sustainable in normal conditions and defensible in downturns, but further margin expansion is close to exhausted.

Strongest counter-case: the market could value all of AQ as a contract manufacturer. AQ's execution moat has to be re-earned in every cycle and depends on a thin bench of managers. The data-centre line could be relabelled as "the least protected revenue in the sector", because its contracts are short, it has one or two customers and it is funded by working capital. In the Halma Avo precedent, the market applied 13x to that kind of revenue against 19.4x for the core business. If the same happened to AQ, the market could apply a contract-manufacturer multiple to the whole company.

What would change my view. A disclosed segment or product-area margin confirming the structural slice at 13 percent or more would raise my view of AQ's foundations. A second quality event, or a customer deciding to dual-source its data-centre products, would lower it.

Versus the market. DNB Carnegie benchmarks AQ against Lifco and Addtech. I would benchmark AQ against the better engineered-component makers (SFS, Comer) on margins and returns, and against Nordic electronics manufacturing services companies on pricing and cash. The market's compounder framing is right about AQ's culture and capital discipline and wrong about its reinvestment volume.

Score: 58. An execution moat, clean earnings and net cash earn an above-average financial profile for a contract manufacturer, held back by price-taking, a 30 percent working-capital ratio and a wide, undisclosed perimeter.

#4 Conservative guidance, sell-side expectation management, and the track record

Key messageAQ's beat record comes from giving no guidance, not from deliberate sandbagging. The pattern favours owning the stock into the February and April 2027 reports and reviewing it in July 2027, but estimates carry no cushion if the cycle turns.

Evidence: with no numeric guidance, good news reaches the market as beats. AQ gives no numeric near-term guidance as a matter of policy. In Q2 2026 EBIT was 6 percent above consensus. In Q3 2026 revenue was 7.5 percent above Bloomberg consensus, EBIT was 16.7 percent above DNB Carnegie's forecast, and organic growth was 17.9 percent against 9 percent expected.

Evidence: formal targets move rarely and late, and the growth target is regularly missed. The board raised the margin target once, after three years of evidence. AQ missed its growth target (above 15 percent) in 2024 (minus 4.6 percent) and 2025 (+6.0 percent) and will miss it in 2026 (about +12.8 percent). The CEO's April 2024 pledge to reach SEK 10bn of sales was missed by about 15 percent. The EPS-doubling target set from FY2021 was met in the twelve months to Q3 2026.

Evidence: in downturns AQ's record is exceptional. EPS rose in FY2020 and again in FY2024. In FY2024 organic growth was minus 6.9 percent, yet the margin rose from 8.8 to 9.6 percent after AQ cut about 1,000 mainly temporary staff.

Evidence: the sell-side has calibrated to this pattern. Four brokers are visible (DNB Carnegie, Nordea, Pareto and one restricted), and all rate AQ Hold. Their FY2027 EPS forecasts lie within SEK 10.00 to 10.40, and their target prices within SEK 232 to 260 (mean SEK 257, 13 percent above the share price). Consensus adopts the margin target (a 10.1 percent EBIT margin for FY2027) and discounts the growth target (revenue growth of 11.2 percent for FY2027 and 7.5 percent for FY2028, with no unannounced acquisitions). Consensus FY2027 EPS has risen 11 percent over twelve months, helped by two beats, after a trim in the first half of 2026 (FY2026 consensus fell from about SEK 8.38 to a low of 7.94 on tax and a soft Q4 2025).

Inference: the absence of guidance partly manufactures AQ's beat record. Without guidance, brokers extrapolate cautiously and AQ beats the extrapolation. That is "sandbagging by structure", not by intent, and it produces a predictable pattern. For the next two reports, management's informal commentary runs ahead of consensus: data-centre demand "ahead of expectations", capacity doubling, a recovery in the Q4 backlog, and a wish for more deals. From mid-2027, management's commentary is likely to be more cautious than the run-rate, covering truck orders, Mexico, Time 24 and the temporary nature of Q3's high utilisation.

So what. AQ's estimates carry no expectations cushion if the cycle turns: brokers have raised estimates broadly, and nothing has been rebased or written down in a "kitchen-sink" quarter. The beat pattern favours owning AQ into the February and April 2027 reports and reviewing the position in July 2027.

Strongest counter-case: one weak quarter could read as a break. With no formal guidance to miss, a single quarter that disappoints on margin would read as a break in the story rather than a wobble. Q4 is AQ's clean-up quarter (provisions in Q4 2025; SEK 185m of inventory write-downs in 2025). Broker ratings have also moved with the share price rather than with fundamentals: DNB Carnegie rated AQ Buy at lower prices and has rated it Hold since April 2026.

What would change my view. A first full year with an EBT margin above 10 percent (FY2027, reported in February 2028) would justify a further target increase or a capital markets day; I put that at about 25 percent. A Q4 2026 EBIT below SEK 250m would end the beat pattern early.

Versus the market. I agree with the market's calibration: the margin target is credible and the growth target is aspirational. I differ on two points. Consensus treats the tax rate as flat, which is wrong. Consensus also assumes no acquired growth toward the growth target, which is conservative.

Score: 60. A strong record of profit beats and downturn delivery, achieved through structural rather than deliberate conservatism, with no cushion built in.

#5 Pricing power, operating leverage, financial leverage, cost-out and feedback loops

Key messageAQ has no pricing power, its operating leverage is fading, and its balance sheet is unused. Its feedback loops protect the downside more than they extend the upside, because a downturn releases working capital into a cheaper acquisition market.

Pricing: none

  • The CEO said on the Q2 2026 call that price was a very small part of growth, at or slightly below general inflation.
  • OEMs take 3 to 5 percent productivity cuts a year.
  • Real price exists only in US medium-voltage transformers, and it shows in Hammond's gross margin (29.2 to 31.5 percent "on price increases") and in Tamura's raised guidance "and price increases", not in AQ's commentary. ABB, holding multi-year agreements, appears to keep that scarcity rent.

Operating leverage: fading

  • Incremental EBIT margins were 15 to 16 percent in 2026 (Q2 15.9 percent, Q3 15.0 percent, calculated) against an average margin near 10 percent, so each 1 percent of organic revenue moved EBIT by about 1.5 percent.
  • From 2027 the model carries incremental margins of only 11 to 13 percent, because acquired units dilute and step-fixed capacity (Norlake, defence lines, new winders) arrives before it is filled.

Downside leverage: historically near zero, probably higher next time

  • In 2020 and 2024 AQ's decremental margins were near zero, but those years had falling metal prices and no new dedicated capacity.
  • The next downturn is more likely to show decremental margins of 15 to 20 percent on lost base revenue, with an 8 percent EBIT margin (the old target, never breached in 14 quarters) as the floor.

Financial leverage and cost-out: unused or small

  • AQ has no financial leverage; net financial items are about SEK minus 1 to 2m a quarter.
  • AQ has no cost-out programme, only a set of site fixes worth SEK 55 to 85m of EBIT by 2028 (Time 24 to break-even, filling Norlake, lower delivery-precision costs, India and Mexico). After OEM price cuts and wage inflation, only about 0.3 to 0.4 points of those fixes reach the reported margin.

Evidence: six feedback loops, ranked by strength.

  1. Reinforcing: data-centre capacity and design wins. Strong through 2027; weakening in 2028 as competitor capacity and the contract re-award start to bind.
  2. Balancing: working-capital absorption. Strong now; reverses when growth falls below about 8 percent.
  3. Balancing: delivery and quality strain.
  4. Reinforcing and counter-cyclical: buy cheap capacity and fill it. Weak in an upswing and strong in a downturn: AQ did five deals in 2024 as organic growth fell, none in 2025 on price, and three cheap ones in 2026.
  5. Balancing: customer and channel leverage, which caps AQ's real price increases at zero.
  6. Balancing: management bandwidth.

Inference: AQ's revenue growth no longer converts into outsized profit growth. Over 2019-25 EPS grew 17 percent a year on 10 percent revenue growth, because the margin rose 2.9 points and the tax rate fell. Over 2025-30 the model has revenue growing 9 percent a year, EBIT 11.5 percent and EPS 10.9 percent, so EPS growth is about 1.2 times revenue growth rather than 1.7 times.

Inference: the loops hedge each other. That is why the range of AQ's cash outcomes is skewed in the opposite direction to the range of its earnings outcomes.

So what. The base case's EPS growth is a revenue story with a small mix benefit and a tax drag. The upside cases need one of two things. What would change my view. Any statement that AQ is achieving data-centre price increases, or a disclosed margin for the structural slice, would lift my view of AQ's pricing. A leverage framework stated by the board would move financial leverage from a scenario to the base case.

Versus the market. The sell-side models 50 percent long-run cash conversion (DNB Carnegie) but does not treat working capital as a feedback loop. I think the counter-cyclical release of working capital is an under-appreciated source of downside protection. The market also credits AQ with more operating leverage on the way up than AQ will show from 2027.

Score: 45. No pricing power, moderate and fading upside operating leverage, an unused balance sheet and loops that protect the downside more than they extend the upside.

#13 Summary conclusion on the business and its investment case

The skew in one paragraph. AQ is fundamentally positive and well underwritten for the next six months, balanced to negative on estimate revisions from mid-2027, and unfavourable on the multiple at SEK 227 unless the AI-financing regime eases or a deal lands. At this price the expected 18-month return is mid-single digits; at SEK 200 to 210 it is mid-teens with the same fundamentals. Own AQ for the February and April 2027 reports, review it in July 2027, and treat any sector-driven fall toward 18 to 19x with estimates intact as the setup worth acting on.

AQ is worth understanding well because it combines strengths that rarely sit together.

Why the business is attractive

  • A thirty-year record of profit in every quarter, and margin defence in every downturn.
  • A net-cash balance sheet and a clean profit-and-loss account.
  • A candid, operator-led management.
  • A genuine structural growth niche in data-centre power and defence, growing at the pace of its own capacity, with demand confirmed one tier up at ABB.
  • An 18-month earnings case: EPS of SEK 9.78 and 10.77 for FY2027-28, growing about 10 percent a year, with EBIT more predictable than at almost any contract manufacturer and the next two reports more likely to beat than miss.

Why to be careful, with dates

  • The organic growth rate peaks now and falls through the company's 10 percent marker by mid-2027.
  • Consensus EPS is 3 to 4 percent too high because of tax and second-half comparisons.
  • The margin is near its ceiling, with no pricing power behind it; in data centres the pricing rent sits with ABB.
  • Competitor capacity and the 800-volt contract re-award arrive from late 2027, and AQ's shareholders are unlikely to know about them.
  • The multiple, already at the top of its normal range on the guideline, is the swing factor.

The balance sheet is the one lever that could extend the Broadening phase, and management has now said that it wants to use it.
