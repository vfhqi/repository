What the company does

ASML makes the machines that print circuit patterns onto silicon wafers, a step called lithography. It is the only maker of extreme ultraviolet lithography machines, which every leading-edge chip needs, and holds over 90 percent of lithography overall. Servicing and upgrading installed machines is a growing annuity. Sales in 2025 were 32.7 billion euros.

A plausible investment thesis

Demand is the thesis. Artificial intelligence spending raises the number of extreme ultraviolet exposures in each new generation of logic and memory chips. Customers pay up front, with 18.9 billion euros of down payments held at the end of 2025, and the 2027 capacity of about 85 machines is close to fully ordered. In July, 2026 sales guidance rose to 43 to 45 billion euros, about 35 percent growth. The Investment Analyst's memo of 23 September rated Thesis's Change Forces' Strength C: the forces are among the strongest in the universe, but the return at this price is weak.

Fit with the Target Setup Profiles

It scored 'Delivering - Serial Acquirer - Mechanical EPS Upgrader' and 'Big Corporate Change-driven EPSU' as partial fits and the three cause-for-concern and trough setups as failures. My read: the fit is clean, because only one setup is claimed, but late, read after estimates and the price have moved, and the pattern treats an inflection as cyclical until proven structural.

Case Riskiness and simplicity

Outside forces carry the risk. The memo rated Case Riskiness C: no company red flag, but two outside fronts open. A bipartisan bill in the United States Congress would ban shipping and servicing advanced lithography tools to China, which Jefferies estimates could cut revenue by about 15 percent; mainland China was 29.1 percent of 2025 sales. The multiple is cycle-driven: a fall to its ten-year tenth-percentile multiple is about 17 percent on unchanged earnings. Simplicity is good: one product family, three key drivers (extreme ultraviolet units, gross margin, China policy) and four straight quarters of sales above the guided midpoint.

Business quality

Exceptional, and unambiguous. The memo rated Foundations' Robustness A, the only A on foundations in the Pool of Stocks Eligible for Middle Innings Portfolio Selection. Post-tax return on invested capital was 39.0 percent in 2025, double 2017's; gross margin rose from 45.0 percent in 2017 to 52.8 percent in 2025 and 54.0 percent in the second quarter of 2026; prices rise 5 to 8 percent a year with no discounting. The fragility is concentration: Carl Zeiss is the sole optics supplier, and four customers were 61.2 percent of 2025 sales.

The strongest counter-case

The bulls say lithography intensity is rising structurally: each chip generation adds exposures, customers prepay, and Zeiss's supply limits stop the cycle overshooting.
