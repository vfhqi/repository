Section 2 – Disclosed and Estimated 2027 and 2028 Hedge Rates and Coverage

Systematic Treasury Operations

1.
Systematic hedging: Adidas AG operates a rolling 24-month treasury policy that secures currency requirements well ahead of active retail seasons. The company typically locks in the vast majority of seasonal hedging volume approximately six months before product sourcing actually commences. This structured system protects the operating results from abrupt spot fluctuations because purchases are priced in US dollars. This strategic shielding stabilizes intermediate earnings per share estimations.
2.
Early coverage: Adidas AG already initiated transactional hedges for its 2027 sourcing requirements during the 2025 fiscal year. Management stated in late 2025 that the company had largely covered anticipated currency needs for the upcoming 2026 calendar year. This proactive schedule means that the gross margin trajectory for 2027 is already being locked in at current forward market levels. These actions provide some buffer against near-term dollar volatility.
3.
Historic parameters: Adidas AG previously managed currency risks on a shorter 18-month timeline during past retail cycles. Former Chief Financial Officer Harm Ohlmeyer confirmed in August 2017 that hedging out up to 18 months was the standard guideline. The extension to a rolling 24-month horizon in subsequent years provides the treasury team with additional flexibility to optimize sourcing costs. This longer horizon reduces the steepness of sudden multi-year cost roll-overs.

This difference underscores why currency strategies dictate competitive gross margin performance.

Section 3 – Sensitivity of Gross Margin to US Dollar Exposure and Hedge Rates

Section summary
  1. Severe structural imbalances in foreign currency cash flows make the gross margin of adidas AG highly sensitive to the euro-dollar exchange rate.

Structural Sourcing Deficits

1.
Extreme structural exposure: Adidas AG faces a persistent operational mismatch because its global manufacturing costs are heavily denominated in US dollars while its retail sales are collected in local currencies. The company purchases more than 80% of its products from suppliers located in Asia. Because these Asian supplier billings are tied directly to the US dollar, any appreciation of the dollar relative to selling currencies like the euro immediately escalates cost of sales. This persistent currency mismatch represents a major headwind that directly suppresses intermediate earnings per share when spot rates turn hostile.
2.
Substantial dollar deficits: Adidas AG managed a massive gross US dollar cash flow exposure of approximately 8.7 billion dollars at the end of the 2025 fiscal year. Corporate treasury disclosures indicate that about 6.9 billion US dollars of this net cash deficit was managed against the euro. This exposure expanded dramatically from the 3.6 billion US dollars net deficit recorded against the euro at the end of 2024. This growing deficit means that the company's gross margin is increasingly vulnerable to euro-dollar fluctuations over the next several sourcing seasons.
3.
Peer exposure comparison: Nike Inc maintains a structurally different currency profile because its primary reporting currency is the US dollar. While Nike faces transactional exposures across international markets, its centralized buying power mitigates some of the absolute dollar deficits that European sportswear companies face. Consequently, adidas exhibits a higher transactional margin sensitivity to dollar strength than its American rival. This baseline mismatch highlights why foreign exchange fluctuations represent a far more volatile variable for european sportswear players.
4.
Contrary evidence: Some analysts argue that currency translation benefits can completely offset these transactional headwinds under specific market conditions. Specifically, Rothschild and Co Redburn reported that a strengthening US dollar boosts the translated euro value of overseas sales and profits. This positive translation effect can provide a helpful cushion for the consolidated bottom line when transactional margins are under pressure. Therefore, focusing solely on gross margin sensitivity can overstate the negative impact of dollar appreciation on overall corporate earnings.

5.

Alternatively, a 10% weaker euro would increase equity reserves by 484 million euros. These massive fluctuations reflect changes in the fair value of cash flow hedges that are deferred into equity before flowing into the cost of sales.

A 10% stronger euro in 2024 would have decreased equity by 280 million euros, while a 10% weaker euro would have increased equity by 355 million euros. The year-on-year increase in equity sensitivity from 280 million euros to 395 million euros reflects the expanding size of the dollar hedging portfolio managed by the treasury team. This escalation underscores how expanding operations multiply balance sheet currency risks.
