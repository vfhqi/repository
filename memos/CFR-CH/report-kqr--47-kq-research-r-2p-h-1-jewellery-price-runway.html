Section 3 – Precious Metal Cost Inflation and Margin Pass-Through

Section summary
  1. Luxury houses and affordable jewellers face highly divergent exposures to soaring gold and silver costs, with recognition lags delaying margin impacts by up to fifteen months.
  2. Pandora is leading the category in hedging and material substitution through its transition to platinum plating, which is projected to structuralise lower input costs by the end of the decade.
  3. Richemont faces a high concentration in its flagship Love and Alhambra franchises, which represent over a third of group profits, but active diversification through secondary lines is successfully mitigating product obsolescence risks.
  4. Leading luxury Maisons are expanding historical collections and launching contemporary lines like Clash and Quatre to recruit younger consumers and reduce structural reliance on entrylevel designs.

Headwinds and Recognition Lags

1.
Delayed recognition: Richemont faces a prolonged delay before precious metal price movements affect its financial statements. Bernstein Research noted on September 1, 2026, that gold price fluctuations typically feed into the group's cost of goods sold with a nine to fifteen month lag. This delayed recognition means that the cooling of bullion prices in late 2026 will not support operating margins until the second half of calendar year 2027. This lag provides excellent margin visibility but delays near-term profitability relief.
2.
Substantial margin pressure: Richemont's profitability has been significantly impacted by the combination of commodity inflation and external headwinds. Morgan Stanley reported on July 16, 2026, that the group's gross margin was severely compressed by higher gold costs, which rose fifty-six percent in US dollars in fiscal year 2026. Although price adjustments and better capacity utilization softened the blow, the group's overall gross margin contracted by 250 basis points to 64.40% in fiscal year 2026. This material compression shows how severe raw material inflation can be before pricing actions fully catch up.
3.
Abating margin headwinds: Richemont is expected to experience a progressive rebuild of its operating profitability as macro pressures ease. Deutsche Bank Research forecast on July 20, 2026, that the gold price headwind will improve sequentially through the year and become a margin tailwind by early calendar year 2028. The broker upgraded Richemont's fiscal year 2027 earnings per share forecast by 5% to 7.38 euros on the back of lower gold costs. This recovery suggests that the worst of the raw material squeeze has already passed for the Jewellery Maisons.
4.
Targeted pricing actions: Cartier is opting for a highly disciplined pricing strategy in Europe despite facing significant input cost inflation. Morgan Stanley reported on September 9, 2026, that gold prices were up 44% in US dollar terms and 38% in euros, yet Cartier avoided domestic list price increases in 2026. Instead, the brand planned high-single-digit adjustments in Asian markets to align regional price corridors and protect international margins. This approach prioritizes market share and long-term brand equity over immediate margin maximization.

Luxury Brand Cost Mitigation

5.
Elevating average unit retail: LVMH has successfully defended its high-end jewelry margins through aggressive product mix elevation and pricing. During the second-quarter earnings call on July 27, 2026, management stated that Tiffany and Co managed to improve its operating margin despite severe gold price headwinds. This margin expansion was achieved because Tiffany significantly elevated its average unit retail prices, which offset higher raw material and store renovation costs. This strategy demonstrates how ultra-luxury brands can neutralize cost inflation through brand desirability.
6.
Resilient performance: Kering's jewelry division has managed to absorb rising input costs through tight investment controls and sales growth. Kering reported in its first-half 2026 disclosure that its jewelry recurring operating income rose to 32 million euros, representing an operating margin of 6.20%. Management noted that tight control over growth investments helped absorb the impact of higher precious metals costs. This margin improvement suggests that Kering's brands have sufficient pricing flexibility to defend their profitability.
7.
Depressed watchmaking margins: Swatch Group has experienced severe margin compression due to currency headwinds and its unique labor-retention policy. Swatch Group reported on July 21, 2026, that its operating profit fell 23.50% to 52 million Swiss francs, representing a thin operating margin of 1.70%. This compression was driven by a massive 200 million Swiss francs foreign exchange hit and management's decision to preserve manufacturing jobs and factory capacity rather than cutting hours. This structural choice leaves Swatch highly exposed to volume fluctuations and input costs.

Pandora Hedging and Plating Transition

8.
Hedging protection models: Pandora relies on a structured hedging program to defer the financial impact of volatile precious metal costs. Pandora disclosed in its second-quarter 2026 report that its policy is to hedge at least 70% of expected silver and gold purchases on a rolling twelve month production plan. The company has a substantial delay of five to ten months before realized hedges impact the cost of goods sold. This hedging model provides strong near-term visibility but delays the benefit of falling spot prices.
9.
Locked input costs: Pandora has secured a major portion of its near-term raw material requirements through derivative contracts. Bernstein Research reported on October 13, 2026, that Pandora is fully hedged for 2026 and ninety to one hundred percent hedged for calendar year 2027. The brand's silver costs are locked in at 32 US dollars per ounce for 2026 and approximately 65 US dollars per ounce for 2027. These fixed hedging rates mean that recent spot market declines will not affect near-term margins.
10.
Drastic material substitution: Pandora is executing a major structural pivot to reduce its long-term exposure to volatile silver prices. Morningstar reported on July 6, 2026, that Pandora is introducing platinum plating as an alternative to solid sterling silver. This material transition is designed to insulate the group's gross margin from wild precious metal price swings. By shifting away from pure silver, Pandora aims to protect its mid-price positioning and consumer affordability.
11.
Complex plating transition: Pandora's transition to platinum-plated jewelry will reshape its long-term cost structure and margin profile. DNB Carnegie reported on October 29, 2026, that precious metal costs are estimated to peak at 13.26% of sales in 2027 before dropping to under five percent by 2029. The broker notes that while platinum plating is more labor-intensive than crafting solid silver, the massive reduction in raw silver usage will drive gross margins back to 79% by 2028. This structural change supports Pandora's long-term profitability goals.
