EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Section summary
  1. The evidence shows that TT Electronics plc has successfully completed the physical transfer of key medical and cable-harness programmes from Suzhou to Kuantan, significantly reducing near-term execution risks, but the site has not yet achieved full-rate mass production, leaving the final margin trajectory and earnings impact unproven until stable high-volume shipments materialize over the next 12 to 18 months. The complex relocation of machinery and customer programs from China to Malaysia has been fully executed, with the transfer of the major customer's production complete as of the H1 2026 interim report, though the facility has not yet reached its maximum production rate. Shipment volumes during the first half of the year were temporarily suppressed by £14 million year-on-year due to safety stocks built up prior to the move, but orders are now returning to growth from the transferred customer as these stocks unwind. Financial analysts at Jefferies forecast that adjusted earnings per share will rise from 6.78 pence in fiscal 2025 to 11.61 pence in 2026, reaching 17.82 pence by 2028, with this rapid growth depending on manufacturing volume recovery within the global EMS segment.

The company executed critical warehouse expansions and logistical upgrades to prepare the Malaysian plant for much larger production runs, with these upgrades designed to support higher upcoming volumes and enable efficient materials handling for expanding high-mix electronic manufacturing services. The Kuantan facility is not a new greenfield project but a highly established manufacturing site that celebrated 50 years of operations in 2025, providing a stable operational baseline for the current transition. Management spent 2025 recruiting and training specialized teams to support future mass production, with securing skilled assembly operators underpinning the planned volume expansion in Malaysia. Despite operational readiness efforts, the initial phase of the customer transfer severely depressed regional profitability, with Asia's adjusted operating profit reducing by 24% year-on-year due to transfer activities.

The Malaysian site secured contracts to provide printed circuit board assemblies for mass spectrometers used in life sciences, and was awarded three new contracts for PCBA requirements from a long-standing life science customer who also uses TT Electronics' facilities in Suzhou, Cleveland, and Mexicali. A major multi-year agreement with Rolls-Royce to supply power electronics for large civil aircraft engines extends a partnership spanning more than four decades, securing high-margin aerospace volumes for the group's divisions and providing stability during the EMS ramp. The United States government initially proposed a 24% reciprocal tariff on Malaysian imports, though Malaysia subsequently reduced its US reciprocal tariff rate to 19% under the Agreement on Reciprocal Trade, representing a modest improvement over initial proposals but remaining higher than historic levels.

Most customer contracts contain co-terms where the buyer directly absorbs any additional import tariffs, with this commercial structure avoiding direct tariff costs for the manufacturer in the short term. However, forcing customers to pay import duties eventually raises questions about regional competitiveness, with customers burdened with extra costs potentially seeking alternative geographic suppliers in non-tariffed areas.

TT Electronics budgeted approximately £2 million in transition costs to complete the complex transfer of medical manufacturing lines from Suzhou to Kuantan, with this focused budget preserving crucial cash reserves during the operational transition period. Adjusted operating cash conversion fell to 42% from 135% in the prior year period, driven by a strategic working capital outflow of £12.7 million, resulting in flat free cash flow of negative £0.4 million.

XP Power's transition from China to Southeast Asia demonstrates that physical footprint transitions require at least twelve months of operational commissioning before shipping commercial volumes, with construction of its new Malaysia factory completed in late 2025 and commissioning taking place during 2026 to prepare for full production in Q4 2026. XP Power reported that its adjusted gross margin increased by 450 basis points to 45.9%, with management attributing this improvement directly to cost efficiencies gained from closing the China site and transferring production to Vietnam.

Cicor's strategic supply agreement with Mercury Systems to relocate production from Geneva to Newport was scheduled to take 18 months to complete, proving that regulated manufacturing transfers require long transition timelines to satisfy strict end-market compliance. Consensus broker expectations favor the successful ramp scenario, with Jefferies Research projecting substantial EPS expansion predicated on volume recovery, though execution risks remain elevated until the site demonstrates sustained full-rate shipments and optimal capacity utilization across transferred medical, aerospace, and industrial programmes.
