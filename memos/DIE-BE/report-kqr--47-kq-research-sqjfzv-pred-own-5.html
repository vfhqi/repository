Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Resolving the PHE put options will not dilute D'Ieteren's earnings but will increase reported net debt when holding cash is deployed.

The Core Financial Thesis

1.
Key Question: "D'Ieteren Group (DIE-BE), a Belgian family-controlled investment holding; segments are Belron (50.3 percent, equity-accounted; vehicle glass repair, replacement and ADAS camera recalibration through Safelite in the US and Carglass and Autoglass in Europe), D'Ieteren Automotive (100 percent; exclusive importer and retailer of Volkswagen Group brands in Belgium), Parts Holding Europe (91 percent; independent car-parts distribution in France, Spain, Italy, Benelux and Ireland), TVH Parts (40 percent, equity-accounted; aftermarket parts for forklifts and industrial equipment), Moleskine (100 percent; notebooks) and D'Ieteren Immo (property). For D'Ieteren Group (DIE-BE), what are the terms of the put options granted to minority holders of Parts Holding Europe (EUR 194.0m over the 9 percent of PHE's holding company maturing at the end of 2026, and the options over the 49 percent of Polaris and Regueira within EUR 307.4m of minority puts at June 2026): who holds them, how the exercise price is set, whether D'Ieteren's reported group share of PHE's profit already reflects them under the anticipated-acquisition method, and how D'Ieteren expects to fund an exercise?"
2.
Neutral impact: D'Ieteren Group applies the anticipated-acquisition method, meaning that non-controlling interests are derecognized and 100% of PHE's profits are already attributed to the Group. Because these profits are already fully consolidated in the profit or loss statement, the physical exercise of the put options will not alter the reported net income or group share of earnings. Consequently, this accounting treatment shields the group's reported earnings per share from any sudden dilution or downward adjustments when minority holders exit.
3.
Elevated risk: D'Ieteren Group's definition of net financial debt excludes the value of outstanding put options granted to minority shareholders at both the PHE holding and subsidiary levels. At June 30, 2026, these unconsolidated liabilities totaled €194.0m for the PHE holding company and €307.4m for the subsidiaries. Once these options are exercised, the cash outflow will immediately increase reported net financial debt, which stood at a leverage ratio of 3.5x EBITDA at the end of June 2026.
4.
Contrary evidence: Jefferies reported that PHE represents only 7% of D'Ieteren's overall net asset value, indicating that any debt consolidation from the options is immaterial to overall valuation. Since the bulk of D'Ieteren's SOTP-based valuation is driven by its 50.3% stake in Belron, minor changes in PHE liabilities have negligible impact on the group's target price.

Liquidity and Ownership Dynamics

5.
Abundant liquidity: D'Ieteren Group possesses substantial holding-level cash reserves following the massive €4.3bn extraordinary dividend issued by Belron. This recapitalization transaction yielded a direct cash injection of €2.2bn for the D'Ieteren Group in late 2024. This immense liquidity buffer ensures that funding the upcoming end-of-2026 put option exercises will not require additional holding-level debt issuance.
6.
Strategic focus: Deutsche Bank notes that D'Ieteren behaves as an active capital allocator aiming to maintain disciplined control of key platforms like PHE. The potential buyout of minority shares aligns with the corporate objective of securing direct, long-term ownership of resilient aftermarket distribution assets. Consequently, settling these put options resolves ownership ambiguity and enables D'Ieteren to fully capture future compounding growth in the European spare parts market.
7.
Other side of the story: Although holding-level cash is currently robust, compiling the cash outflows of €501.4m for all PHE put options could constrain future strategic M&A capacity across other platforms. Since D'Ieteren Automotive is experiencing severe market pressure, with operating profits down two-thirds in H1 2026, corporate cash might be diverted to domestic restructurings. Therefore, a simultaneous exercise of these options might restrict the group's ability to execute opportunistic bolt-on acquisitions during the next 18 months.

Holding Company Option Terms

2.
Concentrated ownership: D'Ieteren Group granted put options over approximately 9% of PHE's holding company to a select group of minority investors. These options are held by PHE's key management personnel, several corporate partners, and independent distributors who invested alongside the group. Securing this structure ensures that key operational stakeholders remain financially aligned with the platform prior to exit.
3.
Formulaic valuation: D'Ieteren Group calculates the exercise price of the 9% holding company put options using a specific performance-based metric. The pricing is measured at fair value based on an EBITDA multiple of PHE at the end of 2026. This pricing formula directly links the final cash outflow of the option exercise to the consolidated operating performance of the spare parts business.
4.
Dual instruments: D'Ieteren Group divided the holding company put options into ordinary and preference shares to structure the minority buyout. The carrying value of these put options at the holding level amounts to €194.0m of liabilities as of June 30, 2026. This represents a minor decrease from the €196.2m valuation reported at the end of December 2025 due to fair value adjustments.
5.
Vesting mechanics: D'Ieteren Group incorporates a specialized Management Reward Plan that features specific ratchet shares granted to key personnel. These ratchet shares are treated as cash-settled share-based payments under IFRS guidelines. In 2025, the group recognized a share-based payment expense of €8.4m to account for the vesting and fair value changes of these management instruments.
6.
Historical progression: D'Ieteren Group has tracked a steady increase in the fair value of the 9% holding company put options since their inception in October 2022. The liability stood at €160.5m in June 2023, representing a €23.1m increase compared to December 2022. This escalation demonstrates how improving financial performance at PHE consistently drives the option's fair value upward.

Section 3 – Accounting Methodology and the Anticipated-Acquisition Method

Section summary
  1. D'Ieteren Group accounts for PHE put options using the anticipated-acquisition method, which consolidates 100% of PHE's profits into the Group share.
  2. Subsequent remeasurements of the PHE holding company put options are recognized directly in equity rather than affecting reported net income.
  3. The chosen accounting methodology prevents earnings dilution upon exercise but excludes substantial put liabilities from the group's reported net debt definition.

Consolidated Profit Allocation Mechanics

1.
Expected consolidation: D'Ieteren Group utilizes the anticipated-acquisition method to account for the put options granted to minority holders of PHE. Under this method, non-controlling interests are derecognized from the balance sheet because the underlying shares are deemed already acquired by the parent. Consequently, the group's reported share of PHE's earnings is shielded from future dilution.
2.
Complete ownership: D'Ieteren Group attributes 100% of PHE's profits and losses directly to the Group share in its consolidated financial statements. This treatment means there is no attribution of net result to noncontrolling interests subject to these put options. The lack of NCI allocation simplifies the reported net income presentation for public shareholders.
3.
Stable earnings: D'Ieteren Group ensures that the group's underlying earnings per share calculation already reflects full ownership of the PHE segment. Because PHE's net income is fully absorbed by the parent from inception, the physical exercise of the options will not alter reported EPS. This accounting choice minimizes short-term EPS volatility when minority partners choose to exit.
4.
Alternative policies: Financial reporting standards allow alternative methods like the present-access policy where non-controlling interests continue to receive profit allocations. Under such alternative methods, the group would only consolidate its actual 91% ownership share of PHE, leaving 9% of segment profits allocated to NCI. D'Ieteren Group's selection of the anticipated-acquisition method prevents this split profit presentation.

Balance Sheet and Equity Adjustments

5.
Present valuation: D'Ieteren Group records the initial financial liability of the PHE put options at the discounted present value of the expected redemption amount. This liability is recognized on the balance sheet against the group's equity rather than affecting the income statement. The carrying value of these options was adjusted to €194.0m at the end of June 2026.
6.
Direct adjustments: D'Ieteren Group processes subsequent remeasurements of the PHE holding put liability directly through consolidated reserves. Any changes in the fair value of the underlying business or the unwinding of the discount bypass the profit or loss statement completely. In 2025, the group recorded a negative €12.5m movement in equity related to these put option fair value changes.

8.
Compensation costs: D'Ieteren Group's inclusion of cash-settled management reward options introduces recurring expenses to the consolidated income statement. These compensation costs fluctuate alongside the estimated fair value of PHE, meaning that strong operating performance raises expenses and lowers basic EPS. Consequently, while ordinary puts bypass the P&L, management incentives remain linked to reported earnings.

Precedents and Peer Presentation Differences

9.
Historical precedent: D'Ieteren Group has managed minority put option liabilities in the past, notably within its Belron segment. For put options granted in Belron after January 2010, the group recognized remeasurements in the consolidated income statement as finance costs, differing from the current PHE treatment. This historical policy created significant bottom-line volatility before Belron was ultimately equity-accounted.
10.
Derivative treatment: D'Ieteren Group treats outstanding put options related to its equity-accounted investee Belron as derivatives under IFRS 9 guidelines. Because equity-accounted investees are not fully consolidated, these commitments do not generate financial liabilities on the consolidated balance sheet. Instead, they are measured at fair value through profit or loss, which was reported as nil in December 2023.
11.
Debt presentation: Deutsche Bank notes that D'Ieteren's sum-of-the-parts valuation model adjusts for minority put options as a debt-like item. However, the company's official definition of net financial debt excludes all minority put liabilities of €501.4m. This exclusion makes the group's reported leverage appear lower than the economic reality that SOTP models capture.
12.
Other side of the story: Although the anticipated-acquisition method successfully insulates reported group EPS from minority dilution, it overstates the group's economic interest in PHE's current cash generation. By consolidating 100% of PHE's profits, D'Ieteren displays earnings that exceed its actual 91% legal ownership. Furthermore, this accounting masks the cash drain of the upcoming €194.0m redemption, which will severely reduce actual holding-level cash without any corresponding increase in reported operating profits.

Section 4 – Funding Strategy and Liquidity Management for Option Exercises

Section summary
  1. D'Ieteren Group leverages robust segment dividend upstreams and holding-level facilities to fund the upcoming PHE and Spanish subsidiary put options.
  2. Corporate cash balances and a €425m undrawn revolving credit facility provide ample liquidity to cover the €194.0m PHE holding puts.
  3. Future option exercises will increase consolidated net debt but remain governed by conservative group leverage and loan-tovalue targets.

Holding Liquidity and Cash Resources

1.
Abundant liquidity: D'Ieteren Group maintains solid cash balances at the corporate level to address short-term commitments. As of June 30, 2026, the Corporate & Unallocated segment held €123.4m in cash and cash equivalents. These holding-level reserves provide an immediate, accessible funding buffer before drawing on credit lines. This cash cushion helps mitigate refinancing risks as the 2026 put options mature.
2.
Robust credit: D'Ieteren Group relies on a dedicated club deal with four Belgian banks to secure backstop funding. This agreement provides a €425m revolving credit facility that was entirely undrawn at the end of December 2024. The facility is unsecured and carries sustainability-linked terms alongside a leverage covenant of under 3.0x. Utilizing this facility would allow the corporate segment to easily cover the €194.0m PHE holding-level puts.
3.
Disciplined refinancing: D'Ieteren Group successfully restructured its corporate debt profile to optimize capital costs. The group completely repaid its €500m short-term bridge loan early in June 2025 using segment distributions. This prepayment left the group with a single €500m Senior Secured Term Loan maturing in 2029. Consequently, the holding company has no imminent debt maturities that would conflict with option settlements.
4.
Flexible options: D'Ieteren Group utilizes its commercial paper program to manage short-term liquidity needs. The group maintains a €225m commercial paper program to support general working capital swings. While this program is intended for short-term adjustments, it provides supplementary liquidity alongside the revolving credit facility. This combination ensures the group has the financial tools needed to manage cash outflows.

Standardized pricing: D'Ieteren Group structures its PHE put options based on EBITDA multiples, which aligns closely with the performance-linked pricing formulas used by other European investment holding companies. The exercise price calculation mirrors standard private equity exit mechanisms where valuations fluctuate with operating profitability. Multiple variations: Wendel SE utilizes variable pre-tax Fee Related Earnings growth multiples to structure its path to buy out the remaining 25% stake of Monroe Capital from historical partners. These Monroe options are valued at variable multiples between 12x and 20x to ensure that pricing is linked to performance. This structure differs from D'Ieteren's flat EBITDA multiple approach by introducing sliding-scale valuation bands.

Settlement flexibility: Wendel SE also granted put options for €407.0m in connection with its acquisition of IK Partners. Unlike D'Ieteren's cash-only liabilities, Wendel retains the unilateral discretion to settle the IK Partners puts in either cash or Wendel shares, which offers superior holding-level liquidity flexibility. This flexibility helps Wendel protect its holding cash reserves during periods of market stress.
5.
Exercisability windows: Swedish holding firm Röko AB structures its subsidiary put and call options with long duration time horizons to align interests. These Röko agreements span multiple years and are typically only exercisable between years 5 and 10 of the investment, avoiding the immediate short-term liquidity calls faced by D'Ieteren. This extended duration reduces near-term funding pressures on the parent company's revolving credit lines.

Strategic protections: Sofina SA regularly acts as a minority shareholder in approximately 70 private equity portfolio companies across various geographies. Divergent Accounting and Net Debt Definitions

8.
Financial definition: D'Ieteren Group excludes all of its €501.4m in outstanding minority put options from its reported net financial debt calculation. This exclusion presents a lower reported leverage profile for the holding company. However, sum-of-the-parts valuation models from research brokers adjust for these puts as debt-like items to reflect the true economic burden on holding-level cash.
9.
Peer integration: Swedish acquirer Röko AB applies a different definition by fully integrating all subsidiary put and call liabilities directly into its reported net debt. Because these options represent approximately 80% of Röko's reported net debt, the ultimate cash outflow has virtually zero impact on net debt since the liability is merely replaced by bank credit facility drawings. This method provides a more transparent representation of the holding's total financial obligations.
10.
Equity presentation: Wendel SE applies a similar presentation to D'Ieteren by recognizing its €890.5m of minority put liabilities directly within financial liabilities against consolidated equity. Any subsequent fair value adjustments to Wendel's principal investment puts bypass the income statement and are recognized in consolidated reserves. This treatment keeps the holding company's reported net income isolated from volatile valuation swings.
11.
Profit and loss volatility: Ackermans & van Haaren accounts for cash-settled option plans at consolidated subsidiaries like Delen Private Bank under IFRS 2 guidelines. The subsequent valuation increases or decreases of these option liabilities, which totaled €19.5m in December 2023, are recognized directly in the consolidated profit and loss statement. These recurring remeasurements create bottom-line earnings volatility that impacts adjusted group earnings.
12.
Contrary evidence: Eurazeo SE manages management co-investment plans through specialized CarryCo funds that do not trigger balance sheet debt. Instead, these plans are structured around carried interest shares and only require direct cash-settled buyouts by Eurazeo under specific exit windows or change-of-control clauses. This structure avoids the massive financial liability recognition that characterizes D'Ieteren's PHE put options.
