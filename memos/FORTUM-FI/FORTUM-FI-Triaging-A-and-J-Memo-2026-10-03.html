[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = D

Thesis. Fortum is a recovery with a contracting option attached, priced as a structural growth story. At 23.62 EUR the shares already pay for the volume recovery, the September forward curve, the Google contract and part of a second one, so the modal return from here is about 0.3 percent a year over eighteen months against the framework's 20 percent bar.

  • The change that is real is a recovery, not growth.
    • Comparable EPS fell from 1.28 EUR in FY2023 to 0.82 EUR in FY2025 on nuclear outages, dry hydrology and lower hedge prices, and the company's own cycle troughed in Q3 2025.
    • H1 2026 comparable operating profit rose 8.6 percent to 627 EURm, and hydro volumes rose 19 percent in Q2 2026.
    • Nuclear availability was 75 percent in FY2025 against a 90 percent target, so normalisation alone returns 3 to 5 TWh of volume at near-full drop-through.
    • Our modal FY2028 EPS of 1.15 EUR sits below FY2023's 1.28 EUR
      • Consensus's 1.21 EUR does too.
      • Over the five years FY2023 to FY2028 the compound rate is about -2 percent a year.
  • The one decisive new force is one contract on about 10 percent of volumes.
    • The 22-year Google nuclear PPA of 2026-09-09 covers up to half of Loviisa's output from 2028 at an estimated EUR 30/MWh above the forward curve, about EUR 125 million of pre-tax profit, about 10 percent.
    • It proves the fleet can be sold to a hyperscaler at a premium, which is why we take a second contract as plausible, at about 45 percent odds by the eighteen-month horizon.
    • It does not change the price of the other 90 percent, which is hedged at EUR 40 to 41/MWh for 80 percent of the rest of 2026 and 65 percent of 2027, below the FY2025 achieved price of EUR 51.4/MWh.
  • The strongest force acting on the stock in 2026 is the forward curve, which is exogenous, cyclical and 35 percent unhedged for 2027.
    • The curve rose 13 to 15 percent in September 2026 and consensus FY2027 EPS rose 17 percent in three months to 1.10 EUR.
    • Every EUR 1/MWh is EUR 47 million of comparable operating profit, so the curve, not the company, drove most of the upgrade cycle.
    • The share price implies a long-term Nordic power price of about EUR 58/MWh against a curve of EUR 51/MWh and EUR 40 to 42/MWh beyond 2028.
      • The industrial demand that would tighten the balance was flat in H1 2026 and the green-steel and hydrogen projects are delayed to 2027 and beyond.
  • The change has reached the sixth layer of the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain before the fifth.
    • Sell-side estimates have moved (FY2027 consensus EPS +17 percent in three months; three large houses off Sell or Hold within a fortnight), while the accounts in Q2 2026 still showed a 13 percent miss on EBIT and a 7 percent fall in achieved price.
    • That is the chain holding at the top and breaking in the middle: estimates ahead of statements, statements showing the old pattern of outage, price and miss.
  • What it costs is the problem. 20.3 times 24-month-forward earnings of 1.166 EUR at the 88th percentile of the stock's own ten-year history, just below the 'mania' band of 20.40 times, and the second-highest multiple of the 27 peers with one in the dashboard sector.
    • A merchant utility with a 90 percent payout and 6 percent earnings growth has commanded 14.9 times over ten years.
      • The contracted slice and the retail mix justify a floor at the 'defendable' 17.50 times, not the 'mania' band.
    • Our modal multiple is a blend, 17.25 times at eighteen months and 16.8 times at three years, so the re-rating leg is negative and larger than the earnings leg.
  • The return fails the bar by a wide margin, and the yield is the only leg that carries.
    • Cash return 3.8 to 4.2 percent a year
    • Earnings growth about 6 percent a year on the 24-month-forward figure
    • Re-rating -14.9 percent over eighteen months.
    • Together 0.3 percent a year over eighteen months and 3.0 percent a year over three years.
    • Even with the multiple held at 20.26 times the return is 10.8 percent a year over eighteen months, the top of the D band at best.
    • The case clears the bar on our modal path only below about 18 EUR, which is where two independent houses put bear or fair value.
  • The pattern is 'Major Corporate Change' fitted least badly, at D.
    • A disciplined operator is converting an irreplaceable but merchant-priced asset into contracted premium cash flows one counterparty at a time, while volumes recover from an outage trough.
    • 'Demand Inflection' is the pattern the share price assumes, and its mandatory condition fails today.

  • The case therefore is a real recovery with a real option, priced as if the option had been exercised.

    • It is not compelling at 23.62 EUR
    • It would be a reasonable case near 18 EUR, or at today's price with a second PPA and a 2027-2028 curve above EUR 52/MWh.

  • Rating = D, because the input change forces are of middling strength ('Change Forces' at C), the financial outputs are a recovery to below the FY2023 level dressed as growth ('Financial Outputs' at C), and the 'Modal return' fails the framework's 20 percent bar by a wide margin (F)

    • The parts bind, so the return sets the level.
    • Not C, because a C would say the change is material enough to be worth a position at a better price
    • That is the entry-price discussion in the Written Summary, not the rating of the case at 23.62 EUR.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The bull case at its strongest.
      • The Nordic balance tightens from 2027 as wind additions fall below 500 MW a year, while Fingrid's 5 GW of data-centre connection agreements and Google's EUR 13 billion of Finnish investment convert into load
      • The 2027-2028 curve moves to EUR 55 to 60/MWh
      • Two or three more hyperscaler contracts follow the Google blueprint within twelve months, taking contracted output towards the 25 percent at which, in Deutsche Bank's words, wholesale price dependency falls dramatically
      • FY2028-FY2029 EPS runs to 1.40 to 1.55 EUR
      • And the market keeps paying 20 times for a clean, contracted, growing baseload fleet, so the shares reach EUR 30 to 33, Morgan Stanley's bull case, 26 percent a year.
      • The evidence for it is the evidence the momentum readings carry: estimates up 17 to 25 percent in three months at FY2027-FY2028, a Stage 2 price with relative strength at the 85th percentile, and a sell-side that moved from Sell to Hold and Buy within a fortnight of the contract.
    • What we concede.
      • The direction of every short-term indicator is the bull's.
      • The Google terms are better than anyone modelled.
      • If a second PPA of 100 MW or more is signed within six months, the stock very likely holds 20 times through the eighteen-month horizon.
    • Why we hold our view.
      • The framework's bar is 20 percent a year, and the bull branch needs two conditions, a second PPA and a sustained EUR 58/MWh curve, whose joint odds we put at 15 to 20 percent after allowing for their positive correlation
      • The options market prices the EUR 33 outcome at 9.4 percent.
      • The research that looked hardest for the failure modes found the "false friend", a landmark contract read as fleet-wide pricing, the most consistent profile.
      • The broker that likes the long-term story best rates the shares Hold at a target of EUR 25, "fairly valued at 20 times 2027".
      • The clearing price on our modal path, about 18 EUR, is where Morgan Stanley's bear case and DNB Carnegie's base DCF sit.
    • Where the bull would win.
      • The Q4 2026 hedge book showing 2027-2028 realisations above EUR 42/MWh, and a second announcement by end-Q1 2027.
      • Those are the review's own tests and ours
      • They would move 'Investment Setup' to 'Demand Inflection' and the 'Modal return' to a C or B from a lower starting multiple, and they are why the recommendation is a park and not an invalidation.
Change Forces: are the input change forces sufficiently strong? (E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because the external forces are mixed and the internal forces, though genuine, are narrow: a contracting strategy with its first large proof, a retail acquisition, a coal exit and a completed cost programme do not meet the screen's rule of three strong forces or chief-executive change plus a guidance increase, and the one decisive new force, the Google PPA, is one contract on about 10 percent of volumes.
  • Forward curve: the strongest force acting on the stock in 2026 is the Nordic forward curve, which is exogenous, cyclical and unhedged for 35 percent of 2027 volumes (as at 2026-07-21).
  • Customer tenors: the research says industrial customers still prefer three-to-five-year contracts, so the contracting strategy's second proof is not in the company's hands.
  • Not B, because the external force that is strongest is also the one the company does not control, and the internal forces would score as robustness in 'Foundations' rather than as materiality here.
External forces: are there sufficiently strong external forces acting on the company, new or intensifying, and in which direction? (RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because demand is structurally supportive but not yet inflecting, the supply side (wind and batteries) is a headwind of equal weight, and the political layer in Finland moved against the data-centre thesis in 2026.
  • Sector demand: flat.
    • Nordic consumption was 395 TWh in 2025, flat on 2024, and Nordic industrial power demand was broadly flat in H1 2026.
  • Forward curve: the single most important external force has moved with the case, up 13 to 15 percent in September 2026.
  • Wind additions: slowing to below 500 MW in 2026 against a 2 GW a year run-rate since 2020, which eases the supply headwind.
  • Not B, because sector-level demand data is flat and the 'Demand Inflection' test is not met.
  • Not D, because the curve and the wind slowdown are both moving in the case's direction.
  • The direction calls here are read again as headwinds under 'Simplicity' (RA 1, E 2, BB 4): batteries, disruption to the premium and Finnish policy are the three headwinds.
