EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Storskogen has entered a new strategic phase in late 2025 by shifting focus from balance sheet deleveraging back to growth, following years of debt reduction and operational improvements that prepared the company for this transition. The company has successfully lowered its financial risk through a prolonged deleveraging process, with interest-bearing net debt to adjusted RTM EBITDA reaching 2.3x at the end of 2025, down significantly from peak levels in prior fiscal years.

This comprehensive analysis examines the strategic repositioning of Storskogen following its multi-year transformation from aggressive acquirer to disciplined compounder, evaluating whether the group can successfully resume inorganic growth while managing persistent cyclical headwinds in construction and retail segments.

Scale of Change at Storskogen

The magnitude of organizational and strategic transformation at Storskogen over the last twelve months represents a fundamental pivot from survival mode to measured expansion. The election of Adam Parker to Storskogen's Board of Directors on 6 May 2026 followed Robert Belkic's decision to decline re-election, signaling a smooth governance rotation with minimal disruption as Parker brings fresh international investment expertise to the board, supporting long-term oversight stability. However, Jesper Kronstrand succeeded Peter Ahlgren as Head of Business Area Services in early 2026, but Kronstrand was later replaced by Johan Ekström as interim Head of Services in Q2 2026, highlighting a period of operational leadership instability within Storskogen's business area that could delay integration synergies. Storskogen consolidated its internal organization by reducing the number of verticals within its three business areas, with the 2025 annual report highlighting that this restructuring aimed to balance business unit autonomy with central support, reducing administrative overhead and improving operational margins across segments.

The company divested its Motavo hair salon chain in late 2025 to refine its retail portfolio, with the chain generating annual sales of Swedish Krona 280 million but recording a margin below the group's 10% target, improving the group's consolidated margin profile. The combination of leadership flux at subsidiary level, structural consolidation, and portfolio pruning demonstrates that Storskogen is actively reshaping its operational foundation to support a higher-quality growth phase.

Main Drivers of Future Earnings (Ranked by Impact and Plausibility)

The forward earnings trajectory for Storskogen depends critically on the successful execution of five distinct but interconnected operational and financial drivers. Acquisition capacity serves as the highest-impact driver for forward earnings development because the group's balance sheet is now fully deleveraged, with financial modeling by DNB Carnegie suggesting that resuming mergers and acquisitions can drive a 5% annual boost to earnings per share, representing a substantial portion of management's long-term annual EBITA growth target of 15 percent.

Reducing financial debt interest expenses acts as a highly plausible second-ranked driver of future earnings per share, with refinancing high-cost debt estimated to lower interest expenses by SEK 20 million per quarter, contributing SEK 80 million in annual savings that will directly flow to the bottom line, driving an immediate 5% increase in annual EPS. Secular demand trends in automation and digitalization serve as a highly reliable, third-ranked driver of future earnings, with the Industry segment estimated to contribute a substantial 4 percentage point boost to group organic sales growth annually, directly expanding consolidated EBITA margins as higher-margin industrial business units scale up.

Refining the portfolio by divesting underperforming assets serves as a critical fourth-ranked driver of future earnings per share, with divesting lowmargin entities estimated to lift the group's adjusted EBITA margin by 40 basis points, directly supporting management's strategic goal of achieving a consolidated EBITA margin above 10 percent. A cyclical turnaround in depressed Scandinavian construction and retail sectors acts as a fifth-ranked driver of future earnings, estimated to drive a substantial 13% increase in adjusted sales growth by late 2027 and 2028, directly boosting Services area margins as delayed infrastructure projects finally commence.

These drivers exhibit moderate to high plausibility individually, but their combined success remains uncertain due to persistent macroeconomic volatility.

Latest Results and Operational Performance

Storskogen's most recent quarterly performance delivered a modest positive surprise relative to consensus expectations, signaling stabilization after a challenging first quarter. Storskogen's second-quarter net sales reached Swedish Krona 8.86 billion, representing a solid year-on-year increase of 5% compared to the prior year, with DNB Carnegie reporting that this performance was 2% above broker consensus expectations because of recovering underlying demand, suggesting that organic sales growth is accelerating across key industrial divisions. Adjusted Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, and Amortisation grew by 2% year over year to reach Swedish Krona 864 million in the second quarter of 2026, coming in 2% above the consensus forecast tracked by Modular Finance, demonstrating that the company's underlying profit engines are recovering from cyclical lows.

The Industry business segment delivered a robust performance with quarterly net sales increasing by 6% to nearly Swedish Krona 4 billion, driven by broad-based demand across all verticals with a particularly strong sales finish in June, indicating that secular automation trends support margins. The divergent segment performance highlights that consolidated earnings momentum remains heavily dependent on industrial automation trends offsetting retail weakness.

The company maintains a medium-term target of 15% annual EBITA growth, but DNB Carnegie notes that achieving this specific target for the 2025 to 2027 period will be highly challenging, with Storskogen likely to miss this strategic target unless macroeconomic conditions in the Nordic construction sector improve rapidly. Storskogen delivered net sales of SEK 8,861 million in the second quarter of 2026, beating the Modular Finance consensus forecast by 2%, with adjusted EBITA of SEK 864 million also exceeding consensus expectations by 2%, signaling that the underlying profit engines are stabilizing after a prolonged deleveraging phase.

Storskogen's projected revenue growth of 3% for fiscal year 2026 ranks at the bottom of its peer group, significantly trailing the 12% average growth delivered by competitors such as Addtech, Lifco, and Volati. This substantial growth underperformance reflects persistent structural headwinds in the company's constructionexposed segments and weak Nordic consumer demand, which collectively constrain the group's ability to generate organic top-line expansion at rates comparable to proven serial acquirers.

The group's adjusted EBITA margin of 9.4% during fiscal years 2024 and 2025 remains firmly below its stated long-term strategic target of more than 10% and lags considerably behind the 16.1% average margin achieved by Addtech and the sectorleading 23.0% delivered by Lifco. This profitability gap demonstrates that Storskogen continues to face operational efficiency challenges and adverse business mix effects that prevent it from achieving the margin quality exhibited by its more mature Nordic peers.

Storskogen recorded an organic EBITA contraction of 4% in the first half of 2026, which stands in stark contrast to the positive median organic growth rate of 2% for the wider compounder sector. This negative organic profit development highlights persistent operational integration challenges within the group's decentralized subsidiary portfolio and suggests that underlying business unit performance remains structurally weaker than that of comparable holding companies.

The company's return on capital employed of 6.8% for fiscal year 2026 falls dramatically short of the 21.0% delivered by Addtech and the exceptional 101% achieved by Lifco. This substantial capital efficiency deficit explains why Storskogen trades at a deep valuation discount relative to its proven peers and indicates that the group has yet to demonstrate the disciplined capital allocation discipline that characterizes successful serial acquirers over full economic cycles.

Storskogen's return on equity stood at 6.1% for the twelve-month period ending June 2026, which remains materially lower than its peer group due to compressed profit margins and historical over-leverage. This weak return metric signals that shareholders are receiving inadequate compensation for the elevated operational and financial risks embedded in Storskogen's business model. The group's projected adjusted EPS growth of 6% for fiscal year 2026 lags significantly behind the 18% expected for Addtech and 15% for Lagercrantz, demonstrating that Storskogen's earnings momentum remains subdued relative to its closest competitors.

This depressed profit growth trajectory reflects the combined impact of weak Services segment performance, ongoing construction market headwinds, and the group's inability to leverage operational improvements into sustained bottom-line expansion at rates matching industry leaders.

Storskogen's actual fiscal year 2025 financial outcomes delivered an adjusted EBITA margin of only 9.4% and EBITA growth of negative 3%, falling substantially short of management's medium-term targets of above 10% margin and 15% EBITA growth. This significant target shortfall demonstrates a troubling pattern of management over-promising and under-delivering on strategic financial objectives, which undermines investor confidence in the credibility of forward guidance and raises questions about the feasibility of achieving stated long-term ambitions without fundamental improvements in operational execution and end-market conditions.
