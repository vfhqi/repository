Section 2 – Amortisation and Items Affecting Comparability

Section summary
  1. Vimian Group AB's acquisition-driven business model results in significant non-operational adjustments that heavily distort statutory net income and basic earnings per share.
  2. Covering brokers consistently add back purchase price allocation amortisation to calculate adjusted earnings per share, but their treatment of items affecting comparability varies considerably.
  3. Standardizing non-operational adjustments against peers Swedencare, AddLife, and Lifco highlights that Vimian's underlying cash profitability is stronger than its statutory net income suggests.
  4. Vimian Group AB's elevated non-operating costs, driven by bank debt interest and earn-out liabilities, continue to represent a substantial drag on its statutory earnings per share.
  5. While Vimian's leverage is well-managed at 2.0x, concentrated maturities in 2028 and 2030 present refinancing risks that contrast with more conservative capital structures among global animal health peers.

Intangibles and Amortisation Path

Amortisation of acquired intangibles represents a substantial non-cash drag on Vimian Group AB's statutory profitability, totaling EUR 23.8m in fiscal year 2025. According to Note 13 of Vimian's 2025 Annual Report, these intangible assets primarily consist of customer relationships, brands, trademarks, and technology recognized in business combinations, which are amortised over periods ranging from 4 to 20 years. Specifically, customer relationships are amortised over 7 to 20 years based on historical customer turnover, brands and trademarks are amortised over 7 to 15 years, and technology is amortised over 4 to 15 years. Goodwill and certain trademarks with indefinite useful lives are not amortised but are instead tested for impairment annually.

Continued bolt-on acquisitions will expand the group's intangible asset base and sustain a high level of annual amortisation. In fiscal year 2025, Vimian closed several acquisitions, including Dental Focus LLC, AllAccem Inc, Blossom Vets BV, and David AI, which collectively added EUR 43.0m in acquired intangible assets and EUR 31.0m in goodwill. This acquisition activity increased the carrying value of total intangible assets (excluding goodwill) to EUR 238.9m as of December 31, 2025, compared to EUR 226.3m at the end of 2024. Consequently, the annual amortisation charge increased from EUR 22.2m in 2023 to EUR 23.8m in 2025, indicating that the amortisation path will remain flat to upwardly sloping as long as the M&A engine is active.

Covering sell-side analysts expect the annual purchase price allocation (PPA) amortisation to stabilize at approximately EUR 26.0m over the 2026 to 2028 forecast horizon before factoring in unannounced large transactions. Broker research from Jefferies on October 28, 2026, assumes a highly visible, recurring non-cash amortisation run-rate because the historical acquired portfolios have long remaining useful lives. This elevated non-cash expense creates a persistent gap between reported net income and cash flow, making the adjusted EPS metric the primary tool for market valuation.

Broker Treatment and Adjusted EPS

Covering brokers uniformly exclude PPA amortisation from their "adjusted" earnings metrics to better reflect the underlying cash-generating capacity of Vimian's operating segments. DNB Carnegie research on October 23, 2026, explicitly calculates adjusted EPS by adding back PPA amortisation, items affecting comparability (IAC), and non-cash net financial discounting effects while assuming a normalized long-term tax rate of 25%. On this adjusted basis, DNB Carnegie forecasts Vimian's adjusted EPS to grow from EUR 0.14 in 2026e to EUR 0.17 in 2027e and EUR 0.20 in 2028e.

Jefferies also adjusts its earnings estimates for non-operational items, though its modeling assumptions yield a slightly different EPS trajectory. Jefferies research on October 28, 2026, projected Vimian's statutory EPS at EUR 0.10 for both 2025 and 2026e, rising to EUR 0.13 in 2027e and EUR 0.15 in 2028e. The variance between DNB Carnegie's adjusted EPS of EUR 0.14 and Jefferies' statutory EPS of EUR 0.10 for 2026e highlights how broker adjustment methodologies directly dictate the headline valuation multiples.

Under DNB Carnegie's framework, adding back the EUR ~24m in PPA amortisation dramatically improves the perceived earnings power of the group. The wide dispersion in FactSet broker EPS estimates for FY2026, which ranged from EUR 0.089 to EUR 0.140 as of November 10, 2026, is heavily driven by these structural differences in adjustment practices. Sell-side firms that focus primarily on statutory IFRS measures present a much more conservative view of Vimian's profitability due to the heavy non-cash amortisation drag. Conversely, brokers utilizing adjusted cash EPS present a more optimized view of the company's compounding capability.

Items Affecting Comparability Breakdown

Items affecting comparability (IAC) totaled EUR 14.7m in fiscal year 2025, presenting another layer of complexity for earnings quality analysis. In the year-end report published on February 12, 2026, Vimian broke down the 2025 IAC into acquisition-related costs of EUR 9.0m, systems updates of EUR 1.2m, and restructuring costs of EUR 1.3m, with the remaining balance allocated to litigation and other non-recurring items. This represents an increase from the EUR 6.2m in IAC reported in fiscal year 2024.

A granular look at the acquisition-related cost component reveals that a significant portion consists of stay-on bonuses that recur annually. In Note 3 of the 2025 Annual Report, Vimian disclosed that within the Specialty Pharma segment, EUR 3.9m of the acquisition-related costs were stay-on bonuses paid to the management of acquired companies, which are reported as personnel costs. In Specialty Pharma's 2024 results, stay-on bonuses accounted for EUR 3.8m of the acquisition-related expenses, proving that these "one-off" items are actually a recurring feature of Vimian's decentralized, entrepreneurial business model.

Other components of IAC, such as restructuring and integration costs, tend to fluctuate based on specific operational initiatives. In the fourth quarter of 2025, restructuring costs of EUR 1.3m were primarily driven by the MedTech segment's rationalization of its product portfolio and inventory write-downs of sunset product ranges. In Diagnostics, restructuring historically included the consolidation of the production footprint, such as reducing the number of manufacturing sites from four to two in 2025 to drive efficiency. Litigation fees, particularly the legal costs related to the VOI patent dispute in the United States, also contributed to IAC in 2024 and 2025, but these were partially offset by US indemnification dispute payments.

Peer Comparison and Market Practice

Benchmarking adjusted-earnings practices against comparable Nordic and international acquisitive peers reveals a broad industry alignment in adding back PPA amortisation. Peer company Swedencare AB, an acquisitive companion-animal health peer, routinely defines alternative performance measures like "operational EBIT" and "operational net income" to exclude non-operational PPA amortisation. In the third quarter of 2026, Swedencare reported total amortisation of SEK 52.0m, of which SEK 51.3m was classed as non-operational amortisation of acquisition-related intangible assets and added back.

Nordic compounders AddLife AB and Lifco AB also report adjusted operating metrics to neutralize PPA amortisation. AddLife's Q3 2026 interim report defines EBITA to assess operating profitability excluding the amortisation and impairment of intangible assets arising from acquisitions. In the same manner, Lifco's Q3 2026 report defines EBITA as operating profit before amortisation and impairment of intangible assets arising from acquisitions, excluding direct acquisition transaction costs. Lifco reported 2025 operating profit of SEK 5,170m and added back SEK 1,102m of acquisition-related amortisation to arrive at an EBITA of SEK 6,273m.

International veterinary peer CVS Group plc follows a similar methodology by adding back PPA amortisation and business combination costs to calculate adjusted EBITDA and adjusted profit before tax (PBT). In its annual report for the year ended June 30, 2026, CVS Group adjusted its statutory PBT of GBP 32.0m by adding back GBP 26.0m in intangible amortisation, GBP 14.8m in business combination costs, and GBP 10.6m in exceptional items to achieve an adjusted PBT of GBP 84.9m. This standard market practice confirms that Vimian's focus on adjusted EBITA and cash EPS is highly aligned with peer reporting standards.

Analytical Conclusions and Contrary Evidence

1.
Contrary evidence: While brokers routinely add back IAC under the assumption that these costs are transient, a historical review of Vimian's filings indicates that transaction and integration costs are highly recurring. In the annual reports for 2023, 2024, and 2025, acquisition-related costs and integration expenses were reported in every single period, driven by the continuous consolidation of the Specialty Pharma and MedTech segments. Treating these transaction expenses as purely non-recurring overstates the organic cash generation of the business, as a roll-up strategy requires ongoing capital outlay for deal execution and stay-on incentives. The persistent presence of stay-on bonuses classed as IAC (averaging nearly EUR 4.0m annually in Specialty Pharma alone) suggests that these items are actually disguised operational personnel costs. If these bonuses were reclassified into the operational cost base, Vimian's true adjusted EBITA margin would be roughly 100 basis points lower than reported. Consequently, broker models that rely on clean operating margins may be overestimating the long-term margin expansion potential as the business matures. Other side of the story: Standardizing adjusted metrics is vital because statutory measures severely penalize acquisitive companies with heavy front-loaded non-cash amortization. According to DNB Carnegie research on October 23, 2026, the underlying cash flow and leverage headroom of Vimian remain robust, supported by strong organic growth and efficient working capital management. Disregarding adjusted measures would obscure the true operational compounding of the platform and make it appear artificially expensive compared to organic-growth peers that do not carry large PPA asset bases.

Financing and Net Financial Items

2.
Elevated financing costs: Vimian's net financial items totaled EUR 20.6m in fiscal year 2025, significantly impacting the bridge between operating profit and net income. This balance was driven by EUR 15.4m in interest expenses on liabilities to credit institutions and lease liabilities. Additional non-operating impacts included EUR 9.8m in combined discounting and revaluation of contingent considerations, which were partially offset by positive exchange rate differences of EUR 4.1m.
3.
Persistent interest burdens: Vimian reported net financial items of EUR 18.2m for the first nine months of 2026, demonstrating that non-operating costs remain a material drag. The group incurred EUR 10.5m in financing expenses at an average interest rate of 4% during this period. Non-cash charges also weighed on the results, including a discounting impact of EUR 3.2m, negative probability adjustments of EUR 4.5m on contingent liabilities, and currency revaluation debt effects of EUR 1.2m.
4.
Structured interest hedging: Vimian managed interest rate volatility by hedging 50% of its EUR 150m senior unsecured bond in August 2025. The group typically borrows from credit institutions at variable interest rates, exposing it to rate hikes unless fixed interest rate derivatives are implemented. Lease interest expenses contributed EUR 0.5m in 2025, which reflects the group's ongoing operational leasing commitments across its global segments.

Debt Maturities and Refinancing

5.
Varying debt maturities: Vimian structured its debt profile through two primary instruments signed in May 2025. The EUR 150m senior unsecured bond is set to mature on May 13, 2028. Meanwhile, its larger EUR 330m credit facility, which had EUR 263.1m drawn as of September 30, 2026, matures in May 2030.
6.
Imminent refinancing needs: Vimian faces concentrated refinancing requirements as the 2028 bond maturity approaches. According to DNB Carnegie, the group's ability to refinance on favorable terms depends on maintaining leverage below its target of 3.0x. While positive organic cash flow will assist in deleveraging, any sudden spike in high-yield bond margins could significantly elevate interest expenses when replacing the existing facility.
7.
Contrary evidence: Vimian's capital structure is exposed to immediate market volatility because its EUR 150m bond is a bullet repayment with no extension options. This setup creates a concentrated near-term refinancing hurdle in May 2028. In contrast, peers like Swedencare secured revolving credit agreements that feature multiple one-year extension options, allowing them to delay refinancing until June 2030 if credit markets deteriorate.

The carrying value of contingent considerations fell to EUR 31.6m as of September 30, 2026, down from EUR 46.4m at the end of 2025. This reduction indicates that the group is successfully settling historical business combination obligations using cash from operations and available credit lines.

Section 4 – Tax Rates and Specialty Pharma Licensing Income

Section summary
  1. Vimian Group AB's effective tax rate remains highly volatile due to non-deductible items, unrecognised deferred tax assets, and currency effects.
  2. Specialty Pharma licensing revenues represent a significant, arm's-length transaction with an entity owned by a segment manager.
  3. Comparing tax practices with peers Virbac and Vetoquinol highlights Vimian's higher tax volatility and potential for normalization.

Tax Rate Dynamics

1.
Elevated tax rates: Vimian Group AB experienced high effective tax rates of 44% in 2023, 33% in 2024, and 29% in 2025. In the first quarter of 2026, the tax rate escalated again to 35% because of non-deductible expenses. According to company reports, these elevated rates restrict net income expansion and weigh directly on earnings per share. Unless the tax base normalizes, near-term EPS growth will remain subdued.
2.
Unrecognized loss carryforwards: Vimian Group AB's tax expense is significantly inflated by tax losses for which no deferred tax assets are recognized. Note 10 of the 2025 annual report shows that the company did not recognize deferred taxes on several localized start-up losses. This suboptimal group structure creates unusable tax losses in certain jurisdictions while other profitable segments are taxed fully. Resolving these structural inefficiencies is critical for lowering the overall group rate.
3.
Contingent liability impacts: Vimian Group AB's effective tax rate is highly sensitive to non-deductible adjustments on contingent considerations. In the third quarter of 2026, probability adjustments related to acquired dental businesses were non-deductible, inflating the tax rate to 35%. These recurring non-cash revaluations create persistent mismatch in reported profits and actual tax obligations. This structural tax drag will continue until historical earn-outs are fully paid.
4.
Normalized tax prospects: Vimian Group AB targets a normalized long-term tax rate of approximately 24% to 25% once non-deductible items subside. According to Carnegie research, standardizing these adjustments reveals that the underlying cash tax rate is significantly lower than reported rates. If the group achieves this normalized rate, it will provide a material boost to earnings per share over the next three years.

Specialty Pharma Licensing Income

5.
Substantial licensing revenues: Vimian Group AB's Specialty Pharma segment generated substantial licensing income from related-party transactions. Note 35 of the annual report states that Specialty Pharma invoiced EUR 9.40 mm in 2024 and EUR 9.70 mm in 2025 to an entity owned by manager Nick Bova. This high-margin revenue represents vital licensing fees for veterinary products and serves as a major profit driver for the segment.
6.
Regulatory licensing structures: Vimian Group AB structured these licensing invoices through Nick Bova's entity due to local pharmacy licensing restrictions. The transaction represents an arm's-length arrangement where Specialty Pharma licenses its proprietary formulas while the manager's entity handles distribution. These arrangements ensure compliance with strict regional veterinary laws while capturing high-margin income. Management confirmed that all related-party pricing mirrors standard market rates.
7.
Leadership transition continuity: Vimian Group AB appointed Hans Lord Skarplöth as the new Head of Specialty Pharma starting on October 1, 2026. Skarplöth replaced Magnus Kjellberg, who announced his departure in early 2026 after ten years with the segment. Despite this leadership transition, the licensing agreement is expected to continue because the regulatory restrictions surrounding pharmacy licenses remain unchanged. This continuity secures the segment's high-margin earnings stream.

Peer Tax Comparisons

8.
Steady peer taxation: Virbac SA maintains a much more stable and lower effective tax rate compared to Vimian. Virbac's effective tax rate was 25.48% in 2024 and 26.51% in 2025, driven mainly by the country mix of its international subsidiaries. This geographic diversification allows Virbac to achieve lower overall tax expenses without facing the severe unrecognised loss issues of smaller peers.
9.
Conservative peer rates: Vetoquinol SA also exhibits a stable tax profile, reporting effective tax rates of 31.02% in 2023, 30.10% in 2024, and 30.36% in 2025. French tax regulations and research tax credits help Vetoquinol maintain a consistent tax burden. However, Vetoquinol faced tax audits regarding its transfer pricing policies in prior years, showing that complex international structures carry compliance risks.

Analytical Conclusions and Contrary Evidence

10.
Contrary evidence: Vimian Group AB's effective tax rate may remain elevated longer than expected due to persistent international transfer pricing risks. The company's annual report published on March 18, 2026, highlighted that negative outcomes in transfer pricing disputes could significantly raise its tax costs. This suggests that simplifying the group structure is highly complex and might not yield the expected rate reductions over the next 18 months.
11.
Normalization earnings boost: Vimian Group AB's earnings per share stand to benefit significantly if the company successfully normalizes its tax rate to 25% by 2028. Carnegie research indicates that reducing the tax rate from the current 35% would directly enhance EPS translation. Lowering the tax drag complements operating profit margin expansion, which is necessary to achieve the group's ambitious 2030 financial targets.
12.
Other side of the story: Vimian Group AB's tax rate could experience continued volatility if contingent consideration revaluations remain high. While management aims for tax normalization, the continuous addition of new acquisitions with distinct local tax profiles complicates the group's tax planning. Consequently, the non-operating tax line may remain an unpredictable drag on EPS, meaning consensus expectations for rapid tax rate normalization are overly optimistic.

Section 5 – Share Count Dilution and Capex Trajectory

Section summary
  1. Vimian Group AB's share capital is subject to ongoing structural dilution from its rolling incentive programs, though total combined dilution remains strictly capped.
  2. The company's 2030 adjusted EBITA target of over EUR 300m is designed to be fully funded by internal cash generation without further equity injections.
  3. Operational capital expenditure remains highly disciplined and runs slightly below the long- term historical guideline of four percent of revenues.

Share Count and Dilution

1.
Extensive research scope: This analysis reviewed twelve distinct company documents and three separate broker reports to evaluate Vimian Group AB's share count trajectory and capital expenditure plans. The research identified five relevant peer companies, including Swedencare and Virbac, to benchmark capital deployment practices across the veterinary health sector. No comparable past cases were excluded as all available historical filings provided sufficient structural data.
2.
Incremental share dilution: Vimian Group AB's capital structure faces steady expansion from rolling long-term incentive programs. The proposed LTIP 2026/2029 introduces a maximum dilution effect of approximately 1.35% of the total shares and votes. When combined with all prior outstanding incentive and warrant schemes, the cumulative dilution is strictly capped at 2.71% to protect existing shareholders. This low threshold ensures that the impact of employee incentive alignment on earnings per share remains highly marginal.
3.
Complex share structures: Vimian Group AB utilizes distinct share classes to manage its equity-settled compensation programs. The group's share capital includes unlisted Class C, D, and E shares that act as hurdle shares or secure delivery for incentive plans. In July 2026, the board resolved to issue and subsequently repurchase 2.67m shares to efficiently implement the LTIP 2026 program. These structured repurchases ensure no direct market dilution occurs during the early phases of the programs.

Funding Terms and Capital

4.
Self-funded organic expansion: Vimian Group AB expects to finance its long-term financial targets entirely through internal cash flows. Management confirmed that the target of achieving an annual adjusted EBITA above EUR 300m by 2030 is not contingent on further capital injections. The group will rely on double-digit organic revenue growth and improved cash generation rather than equity markets. This funding independence supports a stable share count and prevents the dilution of future earnings per share.
5.
Strong equity backing: Vimian Group AB previously secured its medium-term liquidity through large-scale equity raises. The group completed a SEK 1.60 bn rights issue in April 2024 to support strategic bolt-on acquisitions and manage leverage. This major capital injection strengthened the balance sheet ahead of its transition to the Stockholm main market. While the rights issue expanded the share count, it provided the financial runway needed to execute acquisitions without near-term dilution.

Capex and Intangibles Path

6.
Controlled capital expenditure: Vimian Group AB maintains an asset-light operating model that limits annual capital requirements. In fiscal year 2025, capital expenditure amounted to EUR 15.1m, representing 3.6% of sales compared to 3.8% in 2024. This trajectory runs below the company's guideline of four percent of revenue for operational capex. This highly disciplined spending supports strong cash flow conversion and enhances the translation of operating profits into earnings per share.
7.
Expanding intangible assets: Vimian Group AB continues to invest in proprietary technology and product development. Capitalized research and development along with software reached EUR 6.0m in fiscal year 2025. This investment was lower than the previous year, helping to reduce overall capex intensity relative to sales. However, capitalized R&D has expanded in the first nine months of 2026, totaling EUR 7.7m compared to EUR 3.6m in the prior-year period.
8.
Contrary evidence: Persistent acquisition activity could force Vimian Group AB to seek external equity funding despite its self-funding target. Carnegie research on October 23, 2026, noted that large-scale acquisitions could elevate leverage rapidly and risk capital raises to finance the M&A agenda. If cash generation fails to cover future purchase price obligations, the company may be forced to issue shares, diluting earnings per share and contradicting its 2030 ambition terms.
9.
Other side of the story: Vimian Group AB's disciplined share count management and tight capex controls will protect its long-term earnings trajectory. Strong organic cash conversion and a capped dilution profile from rolling incentive programs ensure that EBITA growth translates directly to EPS over the forecast horizon. Unless global credit markets deteriorate severely, the group's current balance sheet flexibility will allow it to fully fund its 2030 ambitions without dilutive capital injections.

Section 6 – Delaware Litigation and Currency Translation Sensitivity

Section summary
  1. Vimian Group AB's successful defense in the Delaware Supreme Court eliminates a major litigation overhang and secures its entitlement to compensation exceeding the initial DePuy Synthes patent dispute settlement.
  2. Despite the final court victory, the group maintains a conservative accounting approach by treating the remaining six point two million US dollar receivable from the largest seller as doubtful with zero recorded value.
  3. Unhedged currency translation exposure represents a key volatility driver for Vimian, where a ten percent US dollar fluctuation impacts group revenues by fifteen point seven million euros.

Delaware Litigation and Receivables

1.
Decisive legal victory: Vimian Group AB resolved its long-standing patent indemnification dispute with a final, unappealable victory in the Delaware Supreme Court on September 24, 2026. The Supreme Court trial, which took place in Dover, Delaware, largely confirmed the earlier win from the Superior Court. Favorable court outcomes of this magnitude eliminate a major legal overhang that has historically weighed on investor sentiment and threatened earnings.
2.
Substantial indemnification proceeds: Vimian Group AB remains entitled to total litigation compensation that exceeds the seventy million US dollar patent settlement paid to DePuy Synthes in April 2023. Pro rata settlement agreements reached with three of the four sellers in 2024 contributed approximately thirty-two million US dollars of this recovery. Successful recovery steps ensure that operating cash flows are insulated from historical liabilities, which supports the medium-term earnings trajectory.
3.
Prudent financial accounting: Vimian Group AB continues to class the remaining six point two million US dollar receivable from the largest seller as doubtful. Corporate management has refused to record any asset value for this remaining debt in the financial statements despite the final Delaware court ruling. Conservative accounting policies imply that future cash collections will generate unforecasted positive contributions to reported earnings.
4.
Contrary evidence: Vimian Group AB experienced significant restructuring costs and litigation expenses that heavily burdened its reported operating profits prior to the final settlement. The group incurred six point one million euros in litigation costs during 2025, which were netted against the received indemnification payments. Persistent legal expenses demonstrate that even successful lawsuits create near-term cost pressures that suppress statutory earnings per share before final recovery is achieved.

Currency Translation Sensitivity

5.
Unhedged translation exposure: Vimian Group AB operates with no currency hedging, exposing its reported consolidated figures directly to foreign exchange translation effects. Sensitivity analysis from the 2025 annual report shows that a ten percent change in the dollar-to-euro exchange rate impacts group revenue by fifteen point seven million euros. Unhedged currency positions mean that dollar volatility will distort reported sales and earnings per share regardless of the underlying local operational performance.
6.
Significant geographic exposure: Vimian Group AB's operating profitability is highly sensitive to the US dollar because a large portion of its dental and MedTech revenues are generated in North America. A ten percent fluctuation in the US dollar relative to the euro impacts group EBIT by two point three million euros based on 2025 sensitivity. Strengthening reporting currencies represent a risk because a stronger euro will suppress reported EBIT margins and drag down consolidated earnings per share.
7.
Minimal translation impact: Vimian Group AB also faces currency translation exposure from its operations in the United Kingdom, though the impact is smaller than its US exposure. A ten percent move in the pound sterling against the euro affects group revenue by five point zero million euros and EBIT by one point three million euros. Sterling fluctuations represent a secondary issue because the minor sensitivity poses a negligible threat to the group's consolidated earnings.
8.
Low transaction risk: Vimian Group AB considers its transaction currency risk to be relatively low because its local operations are naturally hedged. The group's revenue in individual subsidiaries largely corresponds to local expenses in the same currency, minimizing immediate margin risk. Balanced transaction flows suggest that currency shifts are unlikely to cause cash flow distress, leaving reported earnings per share primarily vulnerable to translation volatility.

Swedish Krona Costs and Parent Impacts
