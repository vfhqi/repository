Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Unilever is undergoing a major structural shift to become a focused home and personal care company by separating its Foods business.
  2. The plausibility of this transition hinges on maintaining underlying volume growth while executing margin expansion and organizational changes.

Defining the Strategic Transition

1.
Defining the challenge: "how plausible is the change now expected for this company, given its record of delivery and the strengths the business demonstrably has, rather than strengths it would need to acquire?"
2.
Significant reorganization: Unilever is transitioning into a pureplay home and personal care business by demerging Ice Cream and combining Foods with McCormick through a Reverse Morris Trust guided for completion by mid-2027. The continuing business will operate across three core segments consisting of Beauty & Wellbeing, Personal Care, and Home Care. This structural focus is designed to streamline operations and eliminate stranded costs of Foods separation, directly impacting future earnings per share.
3.
Balanced contributions: Unilever pro forma 2025 financials show that the new household and personal care core is balanced across its three main divisions. Beauty & Wellbeing represents 33% of turnover, Personal Care accounts for 34%, and Home Care accounts for 30%. Successful execution across these segments is essential because they support the midterm sales growth target of 4% to 6%.
4.
Volatile valuations: Unilever is valued by some analysts on a ten-year discounted cash flow model that is sensitive to foreign exchange rates, interest rates, and commodity costs. The stock currently trades at 17x headline next twelve months price-to-earnings but over 19x for the core business. Achieving the transition is highly sensitive to these variables, as failing to execute could drag valuation multiples down to 14x.
5.
Evolving estimates: Deutsche Bank models like-for-like organic sales growth of 4.6% for the standalone home and personal care business in 2027 with 10 basis points of operating margin improvement. This model projects adjusted earnings per share of €3.40 in 2027, rising from €3.23 in 2026. However, margin improvements in 2027 may be back-end loaded because of increasing cost of goods sold.

Plausibility of Financial Assumptions

6.
Rising projections: Bernstein Research forecasts Unilever adjusted earnings per share of €3.25 for 2026 and €3.51 for 2027. Their updated model raised estimates following strong third-quarter 2026 results which showed solid volume delivery despite pricing acceleration. The continuing business must sustain this momentum to support its premium valuation relative to peers.

Section 2 – Volume Growth and Inflationary Pricing Power

Section summary
  1. Unilever faces the challenge of protecting its gross margins through price increases while maintaining its underlying volume growth floor of 2%.
  2. Historical precedents show that aggressive pricing actions by Unilever in Europe and developed markets historically resulted in severe volume contraction.
  3. Peer execution highlights that pricing power is stronger in emerging markets than in highly consolidated European retail channels governed by buying alliances.

Stated Growth Framework and Inflation

1.
Ambitious volume targets: Unilever is evaluated across a dataset containing 18 relevant sources, 5 named peer companies, and 4 comparable past pricing cases. Chief Executive Officer Fernando Fernandez stated at the November 2026 investor meeting that the company targets 2% to 3% volume growth in normalized environments. This target is designed to prevent market share losses because the company's current turnover-weighted market volume growth sits at 2%. Maintaining this volume momentum is crucial to protect the standalone company's earnings per share from dilutive competitive pressures.
2.
Significant material inflation: Unilever guided for a net material inflation impact on cost of goods sold of €550m to €750m in fiscal year 2027. Bernstein Research reported that this cost headwind represents 3.2% of the company's total €20bn cost of goods sold base. The broker expects that the company will rely more on pricing than volume in 2027 to recover these commodity costs. This cost headwind means Unilever must successfully implement price increases to prevent operating margin deterioration during its portfolio transition.
3.
Intensified volume sensitivity: Unilever expects that recovering high material inflation through pricing will trigger some volume sensitivities. Bernstein Research reported in November 2026 that consumer ingredients volume elasticity becomes highly evident when pricing increases exceed a 3.5% threshold. The broker's analysis of historical data shows that pricing explains 29% of volume growth variation above this limit. This elasticity risk suggests that Unilever may struggle to defend its volume growth floor if it prices too aggressively in developed markets.
4.
Contrary evidence: Bernstein Research reported on November 24, 2026, that Unilever value growth in US scanner data softened to 2.6% as volumes contracted by 3.9% despite a strong 6.8% price contribution. This volume contraction suggests that consumer elasticity is already intensifying in developed markets before the 2027 price increases land. If this trend persists into 2027, the company's volume growth will fall below its guided 2% floor and depress overall earnings per share projections.

Historical Pricing Precedents

5.
Severe pricing shock: Unilever experienced a significant volume contraction in fiscal year 2022 when it implemented double-digit price increases to offset soaring input costs. The company reported full-year underlying sales growth of 9.0% driven by an 11.3% price increase while underlying volume fell by 2.1%. This volume decline caused Unilever's competitiveness metric to dip below 50% as the company prioritized profit protection. This precedent shows that aggressive pricing actions historically disrupted volume-led growth algorithms across key household categories.
6.
Localized volume contraction: Unilever faced severe volume pressure in Europe during fiscal year 2023 as it priced aggressively against localized cost inflation. The company reported that European underlying sales grew by 4.1% with pricing up 12.8% and underlying volume down by 7.7% for the full year. This severe volume contraction was exacerbated by consumer down-trading to private label alternatives in discretionary and nutrition categories. This outcome demonstrates that high price increases in consolidated developed markets can severely damage market share and volume health.
7.
Calibrated pricing dynamics: Unilever Home Care segment highlights the operational challenges of balancing volume and price during raw material spikes. Deutsche Bank reported in November 2026 that Home Care pricing growth of 3.6% in the third quarter was relatively low compared with rising surfactant costs. This low pricing strategy supported double-digit volume growth in emerging markets but compressed short-term margins. The broker notes that Unilever must generate substantial productivity gains to avoid catch-up pricing that would derail volume recovery.
