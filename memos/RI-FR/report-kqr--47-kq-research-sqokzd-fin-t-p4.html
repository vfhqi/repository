Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Below-operating-profit items will dominate Pernod Ricard's EPS development over the next three years, as operating profit remains flat to modestly rising.

Defining the Key Question

1.
Strategic investigation: The Key Question is "what does the evidence say about the specific inputs that will drive this company's earnings per share over the next 18 months to four years, namely: FX, finance costs, the hybrid, tax, an India listing, share count, perimeter, restructuring, and dividend policy"

Below operating profit leverage

2.
Significant leverage: Pernod Ricard experienced a reported operating profit decline of 17.9% in FY2026, bringing recurring operating profit to €2,423m. This profit pressure means below-the-line items such as FX, taxes, and finance costs will exert massive influence over EPS development. Understanding these factors is critical for investors because management targets a leverage ratio below 3.0x by FY2029.
3.
Elevated expenses: The group recorded net financing costs of €440m in FY2026, alongside corporate income taxes of €482m. These heavy financial burdens have driven credit rating agencies S&P and Moody's to maintain negative outlooks on Pernod Ricard's BBB+ and Baa1 ratings. Consequently, any improvement in financing rates or tax disputes will directly impact reported net profit.

Such balance-sheet preservation is necessary because the group expects organic net sales to be broadly stable for the full year in FY2027.

Restructuring and India catalysts

Jefferies estimates that selling a 20% stake at a €12bn valuation would raise €2.5bn and reduce group leverage by 0.9x. This capital injection could support future acquisitions or improve financing conditions without relying on dilutive share issues.

Pernod Ricard expects to fully deliver its accelerated €1bn operational efficiency program by FY2028. Understanding the persistence of these non-recurring charges is essential for determining true normalized EPS growth.

Capital allocation: Pernod Ricard has maintained its dividend at €4.70 per share for FY2026 despite elevated leverage of 3.7x ND/EBITDA. Bernstein notes that this payout policy was maintained even as gross margin contracted due to negative price-mix and cost inflation. If operating profit remains flat, any failure to reduce interest or tax expenses could pressure dividend safety.

Currency Fluctuations and Volatility

11.
Unfavorable translation: Pernod Ricard remains highly exposed to unhedged currency translation risk because it does not actively hedge the conversion of foreign affiliate financial statements. This exposure is particularly acute for key currencies such as the US dollar, Indian rupee, and Turkish lira. While the group uses forward instruments to manage operational transaction exposures, the unhedged translation risk can directly inflate or compress reported net sales and operating equity. Consequently, fluctuations in these currencies directly impact the group's consolidated reported earnings per share trajectory.
12.
Significant sensitivity: The group's sensitivity analysis confirms that a 1% appreciation of the US dollar generates a positive pre-tax profit impact of €12m. This movement is driven by a €13m increase in profit from recurring operations, which is offset by a €1m negative impact on the financial result. However, a stronger US dollar also expands the group's reported balance sheet leverage, inflating net debt by €32m. Thus, while dollar strength supports operating profits, it simultaneously complicates management's goal of reducing net leverage below 3.0x.
13.
Contrary evidence: Bernstein Research notes that foreign exchange translation impacts can change rapidly, forcing analysts to trim EPS estimates by 0.5% for FY2027. The broker also adjusted its FY2028 earnings forecasts downward by 0.7% due to shifting transaction FX expectations. This volatility shows that even as the company guides for a negligible full-year currency impact, currency swings can disrupt near-term earnings models.
14.
Positive transition: The severe €268m foreign exchange translation headwind suffered in FY2026 is projected to ease significantly. Rothschild & Co Redburn expects a positive currency upgrade of 2% to 3% as the euro weakens against main operating currencies. This currency movement has led the broker to upgrade its absolute operating estimates despite underlying organic top-line downgrades. This tailwind will help stabilize reported profits, mitigating persistent organic volume pressures in key regions.

Section 3 – Financing Costs, Refinancing Profile, and the Hybrid Note

Section summary
  1. The hybrid bond refinancing of October 2026 will technically deleverage Pernod Ricard's balance sheet but dilute its normalized earnings per share due to high coupon costs.
  2. Rating agencies treat the new deeply subordinated notes as half equity, which supports credit metrics despite negative outlooks on the senior unsecured ratings.

Refinancing and Bond Tender Details

1.
Rising expenses: Pernod Ricard reported net financing costs of €440m for FY2026, reflecting elevated interest rates. The group's weighted average cost of debt rose to 3.4% in FY2026 compared to 3.2% in the previous fiscal year. While gross debt remained highly fixed at 96%, this rising interest rate environment continues to pressure the group's net financial expenses.
2.
Strategic financing: Pernod Ricard set terms for a €1.5bn euro-denominated undated non-call deeply subordinated hybrid notes issue in October 2026. The dual-tranche issue consists of a €750m perpetual non-call 5.25- year tranche with a 5.625% initial coupon and a €750m perpetual non-call 8-year tranche with a 6.125% initial coupon. The net proceeds are intended to optimize the group's capital structure and fund concurrent bond buybacks.
3.
Proactive management: Pernod Ricard launched a simultaneous tender offer in October 2026 to repurchase outstanding senior notes maturing between 2027 and 2032. This tender successfully repurchased and cancelled €1,219.4m of notes, with settlement completed on October 30, 2026. The company redeemed several series to proactively manage its expected debt maturity profile.
4.
Contrary evidence: Pernod Ricard is replacing cheaper traditional bonds with much more expensive hybrid notes, which will increase the overall cash interest burden. The repurchased notes included October 2027 notes carrying a 0.500% coupon, whereas the new hybrid tranches carry coupons of 5.625% and 6.125%. This substantial rate differential means that actual funding costs will rise despite the technical reduction in reported gross debt.

Accounting Treatment and EPS Mechanics

5.
Favorable accounting: Pernod Ricard will account for the new undated deeply subordinated hybrid notes entirely as equity under IFRS rules. Under this standard, the hybrid coupons are treated as equity distributions rather than interest expenses. This treatment means that the group's reported financing costs in the income statement will technically fall as traditional bonds are replaced.
6.
Diluted returns: Morgan Stanley calculates that the hybrid bond refinancing could result in 3% to 4% EPS dilution for Pernod Ricard. Although reported net income appears higher because hybrid interest avoids the finance cost line, the coupon cost is deducted directly from equity to calculate EPS. This mechanism means that the high cash cost of the hybrid will directly reduce the earnings available to ordinary shareholders.
7.
Technical deleveraging: Standard & Poor's and Moody's treat these equity-classified hybrid bonds as 50% equity and 50% debt for credit metric calculations. This 50/50 debt-equity treatment allows Pernod Ricard to execute a technical deleveraging, reducing its reported net debt for rating purposes by approximately €500m per €1bn raised. Consequently, the hybrid issue helps defend the group's investment-grade profile during a period of earnings pressure.
8.
Negative pressure: Pernod Ricard's long-term senior unsecured debt carries a negative outlook from rating agencies, with Moody's rating it Baa1 and S&P rating it BBB+. Reflecting their subordinated status, the new hybrid notes were rated Baa3 by Moody's and BBB- by Standard & Poor's. These negative outlooks highlight the critical need for technical deleveraging to prevent a downgrade into lower credit categories.

Peer Comparisons and Capital Structure Paths

9.
Disciplined deleveraging: Peer Diageo manages its capital structure by targeting adjusted net leverage of 2.5x to 3.0x to maintain an A-band credit rating. Diageo suspended share buybacks as leverage peaked at 3.6x, aiming to return within its target range by FY2028. This peer comparison indicates that global spirits leaders must prioritize debt reduction over shareholder returns when leverage exceeds historical norms.
10.
110 6K 111 Comparable execution: Carlsberg pursued a very similar refinancing path in May 2026, combining a hybrid bond issuance with a senior note tender. This predecessor transaction allowed Carlsberg to achieve technical deleveraging, though it resulted in an estimated 2% EPS dilution in the following fiscal year. The Carlsberg case confirms that consumer staples groups are increasingly willing to trade near-term EPS accretion for balancesheet safety.
11.
Paused expansion: Davide Campari-Milano has paused its active M&A strategy to focus entirely on reducing net debt to EBITDA leverage. Campari's leverage rose to 3.6x following its Courvoisier acquisition, but the company targeted rapid deleveraging through strict SG&A discipline and portfolio streamlining. This peer behavior underlines a sector-wide commitment to deleveraging, as Campari expects to maintain a comfortable level of leverage through disciplined capital allocation.

Other side of the story: While management utilizes the hybrid notes to technically lower leverage and defend its investment-grade credit rating, Morgan Stanley argues that this refinancing is fundamentally dilutive to earnings per share. The substantial cash cost of the perpetual 5.625% and 6.125% coupons outweighs the savings from repurchasing lower-yielding traditional bonds. Therefore, this capital structure optimization represents an expensive balance sheet repair that actually creates a headwind for normalized EPS growth over the next 18 months to three years.

Section 5 – The Partial Listing of Pernod Ricard India

Section summary
  1. A partial listing of Pernod Ricard India represents a highly effective deleveraging mechanism that could reduce net debt by nearly one turn.
  2. While the transaction crystallizes massive local valuations, it introduces long-term dividend leakage to minority shareholders.
  3. Dilution from the final FY2026 scrip dividend will temporarily expand Pernod Ricard's share count and lower near-term earnings per share.
  4. Share buybacks will remain suspended until net leverage falls below the strict three times net debt to EBITDA threshold.
  5. Peer capital return paths show that spirits groups must prioritize balance sheet repair over equity repurchases during cyclical downturns.

This corporate presentation highlighted that local anchoring is a key strategy to counter rising patriotism in emerging markets. Consequently, management expects this optionality to provide valuable long-term strategic benefits for local development.
