What the call should settle, and why

The case in one line: one of Europe's most predictable earnings streams, priced near its ten-year median multiple and the bottom of its five-year range because the market fears artificial intelligence will reprice Legal, whose earnings over eighteen months rest on contracted price, controlled cost and a capital framework applied since 2009. Six judgements decide whether and when that is worth buying.

  • Judgement 1, Legal's price and the artificial intelligence tier. Legal grew 10% in H1 2026 and at nine months; about 3 points of that is the price step as customers migrate to Lexis+ with Protégé (90% of new sales value, about 75% of United States renewal value, more than half the United States base migrated). Our base is 9% in FY2027, 8% in FY2028, 7% after. If the step is still being paid at each renewal and RELX has a next paid tier, the base holds and the FY2028 to FY2030 fade is too cautious; if migration is nearly complete or upgrades are being given to hold renewals, Legal at 7% in FY2027 costs about 1% of EPS, and the bear case (4% from FY2028) takes FY2030 EPS about 11% below base.
  • Judgement 2, the margin path and the investment step. Our group margin is 35.6% in FY2026 and 36.1% in FY2027 (consensus 35.8%), then +50 to +60 basis points a year. Each 50 basis points is £53m of profit and 1.5% of EPS; a forced artificial intelligence investment step of about a point of revenue, as Thomson Reuters took in its third quarter of 2026, would cost about 100 basis points in the year.
  • Judgement 3, the 2027 buyback. £2.25bn in 2026 (£1.75bn done in H1 at an average 2,529p) took leverage from 2.0 to 2.3 times. Our base is £1.75bn for 2027; consensus share counts imply about £2bn; Bernstein has £1.5bn. Small for EPS (each £0.25bn is about 0.15%), large for the day: £1.5bn reads as a cut, £2.0bn as a raise.
  • Judgement 4, the Legal wording. The nine-month statement dropped "double-digit growth in law firms and corporate legal", which the first-quarter and H1 statements carried. If that is drafting, nothing changed; if it marks law firms slowing toward the 9% we model, the FY2027 sentence in February is the place it shows, and Legal at 8% at the annual general meeting update would start a cut cycle.
  • Judgement 5, the entrants and the interface. Harvey's annualised recurring revenue rose from $190m to more than $350m in a year, Legora's from $100m to $200m in under six months, and Google launched a legal product with Thomson Reuters, Harvey and Legora as partners and LexisNexis absent. If RELX's content sits inside the entrants' tools on paid terms and its large-firm renewals are unaffected, the threat is to the interface, not the revenue; if a top-50 firm moves its research contract, the memo's view changes.
  • Judgement 6, what RELX will disclose. Whole-number growth, half-yearly profit, no price and volume split, no artificial intelligence or seat-linked revenue. Half the share price variance is the peer group's; better disclosure is the company's only lever on the multiple between results, and the memo's monitoring has to be external without it.

Range B: the key risks, optionality and drivers of the case

B1. Legal pricing and the artificial intelligence tier

B1.1 ★ Where the migration stands. "You have said about 90% of new sales value and about three-quarters of United States renewal value are on Lexis+ with Protégé, and more than half the United States installed base has migrated. When does the migration reach the bulk of the base, and what share of Legal's growth comes from that price step?"
- Why: the step is about 3 points of Legal's 10%; it is paid once per customer at the renewal on which it migrates. Half-migrated on the base but three-quarters on renewal value means the fade could come in FY2027 or FY2028; a one-point fall in the uplift costs about £20m of profit and 20 basis points of group margin with almost no visible revenue signal.
- Tell: "a long tail of the base still to migrate, outside the United States too" = 9% in FY2027 holds; "largely done in the United States" = the fade arrives a year earlier than we model.

B1.2 ★ The second renewal. "The first customers who moved to Lexis+ with Protégé on three-year contracts renew from 2027. What do you see in the early renewals: is the price held, raised again, or traded for scope?"
- Why: the memo calls the second renewal the decisive test of the case, a 2027 to 2029 event the market will read first in Thomson Reuters' disclosures; the bear case (5% given back on United States Legal from 2028) takes FY2027 EPS to about 149p and FY2030 to about 190p against 156.5p and 215p.
- Tell: "renewals at or above the escalators with usage rising" = the market's fear is early; "too early to say" = expected, and the question for the H1 2027 results; any mention of discounting to hold renewals = the memo's reason to change its view.

B1.3 ★ A next paid tier. "Karnov charges about 30% more for its artificial intelligence tier and a further step for workflow; Thomson Reuters sells CoCounsel as a separate paid step. You have bundled Protégé upgrades into the existing price. Is there a next priced tier, such as agentic products, and how will you charge for it?"
- Why: without a next rung, about 35 basis points of group margin from the fading uplift disappears from 2028 and our +50 basis points a year becomes +15 to +25; a priced agentic tier is the bull case (Legal 10% to 11%, FY2027 EPS about 161p).
- Tell: "a paid agentic tier in 2027" = the bull case's mechanism; "we keep bundling to drive adoption" = the fade is real and the margin path flattens. Or save for management.

B1.4 Seats against usage. "Thomson Reuters plans consumption-pricing options from 2027. How much of Legal's revenue is linked to seats, and would you follow if large firms ask for usage-based terms?"
- Why: the weak form of the artificial intelligence threat is that pricing moves away from seats; fewer lawyers per matter on seat pricing is the mechanism. Legal subscriptions are 85% of the division on three-year terms.
- Tell: "mostly enterprise and firm-wide, not per seat" = the threat is smaller than feared; "a high share per seat" = the key exposure, worth a follow-up with management.

B2. Competition at the legal interface

B2.1 ★ Harvey and Legora. "Harvey and Legora have grown quickly and Legora launched United States primary legal research in August. Where do you meet them in practice, and have you lost a large-firm research contract to either?"

- Tell: "they sit on top of research, and our content is inside them" = the interface threat, not a revenue threat; "we compete for new small firms" = bounded; any large-firm loss = the case's first break.

B2.2 ★ Content inside other platforms. "LexisNexis content is distributed inside about 25 partner platforms, including Harvey. On what terms are you paid, and is that revenue growing faster than direct Legal?"
- Why: this is RELX's hedge if the entrants win the screen; Harvey reportedly accesses about 1% of the database. If the partnerships pay on usage, RELX gains from the entrants' growth.
- Tell: "a growing, paid channel" = the hedge works; "small and strategic" = the hedge is not yet worth much.

B2.3 Google and Thomson Reuters. "Google's legal product launched with Thomson Reuters, Harvey and Legora as partners. Why is LexisNexis not among them, and do you see Thomson Reuters gaining research share?"
- Why: the United States research market is a duopoly of 85% to 90% (Thomson Reuters about 55%, RELX 30% to 35%); most large firms dual-source, so a budget squeeze lands on the number two first.
- Tell: "our choice, on content terms" = a negotiating position; "share stable in renewals" = the duopoly holds; any hesitation = follow up with management.

B3. STM, academic publishing and artificial intelligence

- Tell: "recurring, small, growing" = optionality not in our numbers; "we do not license" = no one-off risk, no upside either.

B3.3 United States grant rules and India. "How much of STM revenue is exposed to United States limits on publication fees from grants, and to India's national subscription?"
