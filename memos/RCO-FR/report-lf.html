EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Evidence on the chief executive

Franck Marilly became CEO in June 2025 with over three decades of leadership experience in luxury and beauty enterprises, including 17 years at Chanel and a move to Shiseido in 2018.

His main career gap is that he has no direct beverage alcohol experience, and his regional CEO titles at Chanel and Shiseido EMEA may overstate his standalone profit-and-loss autonomy, since decisions were approved by centralised headquarters.

Against the six qualities tested in Section 16, the strongest evidence is for drive and adaptability, because he introduced the RC Forward plan to reverse declining cognac volumes, and for capital discipline, because industrial capital expenditure was cut to €37 million in fiscal year 2026. The delivery record is still unproven: sales fell from €1,194.1 mm in FY24 to €935.3 mm in FY26, operating margin compressed from 25.5% to 17.7%, and EPS fell from €3.64 to €1.51, even though the shares rallied 42% year-to-date by October 2026 against 2% for the European spirits sector.

His history at Shiseido shows a pattern of leaving before the group's Action Plan was fully executed, and an expert view that he often leaves before decisions show results on the bottom line.

Governance robustness

1.
Board independence is solid on paper: seven independent members represent a 58% independence rate among voting directors, and independent directors make up 75% of the Audit-Finance Committee. Control is heavily concentrated, because the family owns 58.05% of the share capital and double voting rights turn this into 71.34% of total voting rights. Control and compliance are mixed: the company reports no material weaknesses or restatements and uses two co-auditors, PricewaterhouseCoopers Audit and Forvis Mazars, and the board formalised a chair succession plan in 2025-26. The main governance limitation is that the Nomination and Remuneration Committee lacks an independent majority, while central leadership roles and committee chairs remain tightly held by the family or close allies.

Director and officer share dealing signals

The only insider transactions found were non-cash dividend elections, such as Orpar SA taking 249,842 shares at €44.33 as a stock dividend on 1 October 2025. Broader board participation was similar: a 76.8% take-up of the dividend option led to 256,395 new shares on 1 October 2026, which is a routine liquidity-preserving signal rather than evidence of conviction. By contrast, Pernod Ricard's CEO bought shares worth €1.74 million on the open market, while Remy Cointreau insiders only added shares through pre-approved dividend distribution options.

What experts say against management's story

Management said in June 2025 that the US market had reached its bottom and was rebounding, but a former executive argued that excess retail and distributor inventory will take years to normalise, preventing a return to pre-pandemic growth for five to seven years. A former Remy Cointreau CEO said the company was too arrogant on pricing power and that holding high prices while cutting promotion cost it market share to Hennessy.

Management points to positive green shoots and growing e-commerce momentum in mainland China, yet Mid-Autumn Festival sales finished significantly below internal expectations. Market data show that 57% of the group's top twenty SKUs saw price and volume fall together, against 13% at Pernod Ricard, which undercuts the value-first narrative.

Single largest risk to management's credibility

The biggest credibility test is the RC Forward recovery timeline: the plan targets €100 million of gross value creation by fiscal year 2029, but only 10% is expected in the current transitional year, with the bulk deferred to fiscal years 2028 and 2029. Delivery risk is compounded because Bernstein calls the plan a demanding agenda for a relatively small organization, 85% of operating profit comes from cognac, and the company has already withdrawn its fiscal year 2030 targets, including the 33% operating margin ambition.
