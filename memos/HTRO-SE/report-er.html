Management reversed its earlier plan to downsize the Clinton, South Carolina facility after North American sales grew 80% year on year, demonstrating that US demand exceeded the gradual recovery previously embedded in both company and broker models. The beat came despite rather than because of European recovery, as Fiber Solutions sales in Europe declined 9% due to weak activity in the United Kingdom and DACH markets, confirming that North America has become the growth anchor, representing 48% of Q3 sales versus Europe's 43%.

The geographic mix shift improves earnings quality by reducing dependence on structurally challenged European FTTH markets, although it also concentrates exposure to US rollout cycles and customer behavior.

Harsh Environment sales of SEK 393 million were 25% above the prior year and 4% above Redeye's forecast, while adjusted EBITA of SEK 43 million matched the estimate and produced an 11.0% margin, meaning this segment contributed modest incremental upside rather than a comparable revaluation to Data Center or Fiber Solutions. Defense demand remained strong while oil-and-gas demand remained muted, leaving the segment's earnings trajectory dependent on whether defense can continue offsetting energy-project delays.

Redeye described a clear sales and earnings beat, retained a positive view and expected to raise forecasts and the underlying Base Case slightly, while explicitly discounting the Q3 Data Center outperformance because management expected Q4 normalization, but the accessible record contains only one post-results broker note, preventing verification of a broader sell-side or private-investor consensus reaction. Redeye noted that its risk-free-rate assumption had increased from 2.5% to 3%, which could offset part of the benefit from higher operating forecasts, meaning valuation uplift may be smaller than the operating beat alone suggests.

Adjusted leverage fell to 1.3x from 1.7x in Q2 and 1.9x at year-end 2025, while available liquidity increased to SEK 2,090 million versus SEK 1,797 million at year-end 2025, creating material acquisition capacity that management prioritizes toward Data Center and Harsh Environment rather than Fiber Solutions. Hexatronic acquired Communication Zone in November 2025, JOWO Systemtechnik in April 2026 and Superior Fiber & Data Services in June 2026, and the 2028 ambitions explicitly include M&A, meaning future EPS growth depends partly on deal execution, integration and accretive capital allocation rather than solely on organic recovery.

Operating cash flow was SEK 228 million versus SEK 154 million in Q3 2025, representing 72% cash conversion compared with 84% in Q2, while first-nine-month working capital consumed SEK 205 million of cash, demonstrating that cash conversion lagged the adjusted EBITA beat and raising the importance of EPS-to- cash quality in future quarters. Reported EBIT included SEK 71 million of net non-recurring effects, including a SEK 67 million reversal of a prior Clinton impairment, so adjusted EBITA was the cleaner measure of recurring operating improvement.

Management disclosed that glass fiber and selected connectors were beginning to restrict growth, with increasing backlogs and unavoidable lead-time extensions in some cases, which was more serious than the earlier emphasis on demand and market opportunity because the constraint can cap revenue, delay customer deliveries and increase working-capital needs even when demand remains strong. This means supply availability, not demand, may limit near-term growth, reversing the pre-results debate that centered on whether North American recovery and Data Center momentum could sustain double-digit group organic expansion.

Management said it was validating new suppliers and increasing prices to offset higher fiber and transportation costs, while noting that resin costs had stabilized at lower levels, but Q3 did not establish the speed or completeness of pass-through, leaving pricing power operationally unproven despite management's assertion. Redeye's assessment was that Hexatronic operates in a fragmented market containing commodity-like products, with limited ability to increase prices above inflation without risking customer churn, so the company's response demonstrates mitigation rather than secured margin protection.

Pernilla Lindén is moving from Group CFO to President Fiber Solutions on 1 November 2026, while an external search for her successor was ongoing but had not identified a named replacement, meaning execution capacity is more uncertain than the Q2 reporting baseline precisely as acquisitions, supply management and margin defense require stronger financial and operating leadership.

Section 1 – Brief company description and history

Section summary
  1. Hexatronic entered the 22 October 2026 results as a transformed but still incomplete diversification story, with Data Center and Harsh Environment offsetting a structurally weak European fiber market while acquisition dependence and the missing three-year market-performance data limited confidence in the longerterm EPS case.
1.
Diversified connectivity platform: Hexatronic was founded in Sweden in 1993 and listed on Nasdaq Stockholm, developing, manufacturing and selling fiber-infrastructure products alongside systems, support and training. Its business now spans Fiber Solutions, Harsh Environment and Data Center, meaning the investment case is no longer a pure fiber-to-the-home supplier, although Fiber Solutions remained 56% of Q3 2026 sales. The group is diversified, not yet independent of fiber.
2.
Fiber Solutions remains the legacy core: Fiber Solutions supplies cables, ducts, cabinets, distribution products, installation tools and related services for fiber-to-the-home, 5G, transport, metropolitan and submarine networks, mainly to telecom operators, network owners and distributors. In 2025 it generated SEK 4,870 million of sales, or 65% of Group revenue, with organic growth of negative 5% and an adjusted EBITA margin of 6.4%. FTTH still dominates the earnings base.
3.
Data Center is the principal growth engine: Data Center combines high-density cabling and connectivity products with design, installation, integration, project management, migration and operational services for hyperscalers, colocation providers, cloud customers and enterprises. Its 2025 sales reached SEK 1,409 million, up organically 37%, while adjusted EBITA rose to SEK 252 million at a 17.9% margin. Data Center carries disproportionate profit weight.
4.
Harsh Environment adds defensible niches: Harsh Environment supplies dynamic cables, specialist connectors and fiber-optic sensing solutions for offshore energy, marine, defense, aerospace and industrial applications. The segment generated SEK 1,241 million of 2025 sales, 11% organic growth and an adjusted EBITA margin of 11.2%, making it materially more resilient than Fiber Solutions but less profitable than Data Center. Specialty markets improve mix quality.
5.
Geographic exposure has shifted toward North America: In 2025, Europe represented 56% of Group sales, North America 35% and Asia-Pacific 9%, compared with 54%, 37% and 9% respectively in 2024. By Q3 2026, North America had become the largest regional market at 48% of quarterly sales, ahead of Europe at 43% and Asia-Pacific at 9%, with January-to-September sales up 44% in North America but down 6% in Europe. North America is now the growth anchor.
