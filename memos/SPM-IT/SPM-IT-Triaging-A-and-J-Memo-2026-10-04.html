[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = D

Thesis. Saipem is a repaired cyclical with a corporate event attached: a fully booked deepwater fleet in the strongest offshore sanctioning cycle since 2013, a nil-premium merger with Subsea 7 awaiting European clearance, and a lease book that starts to roll off in 2027. At EUR 4.322 the shares already pay for the cycle, the FY2027 margin step and the merger; on an earnings footing the stock sits at the top of its peers, so the modal return is negative over eighteen months and about nil over three years. The business case is compelling; the investment case is not, at this price.

  • The change that matters most is external and cyclical, and it is strong.
    • Global offshore sanctioning capital spending is forecast to rise 30% to USD 149bn in 2027, the highest since 2013, with fleets booked through 2027 to 2028 and a plateau named after 2028.
    • Saipem's own evidence is intake, not revenue: Q2 2026 order intake of EUR 4,068m (+86.9% year-on-year), about EUR 8.0bn year to date by July, and an 18-month pipeline of EUR 63bn, up from EUR 58bn.
    • The force reaches Saipem through price and mix, because the fleet is already full: contract rates up 3% to 5% and day rates up 5% to 7%, with capacity allocated into 2028 and 2029 at premium terms.
  • The internal change is mostly delivered and already in the numbers.
    • The chief executive has been in place since 31 August 2022
    • "value over volume", the shallow-water exit, the standards programme and the dividend restart are done, which is why revenue has tripled from the 2022 base and the EBITDA margin has doubled to 11.1%.
    • The one large internal force still ahead is the Saipem7 merger, agreed on 24 July 2025 at nil premium, with ten of sixteen clearances in hand and a European Phase 2 decision due by 26 November 2026.
    • The year's only guidance move was a cut: FY2026 adjusted EBITDA from about EUR 1.90bn to about EUR 1.75bn on 28 July 2026, for EUR 140m of Middle East transit costs.
  • 'Financial Outputs' have turned up on margin, cash and net debt, and not on revenue or near-year earnings.
    • The EBITDA margin has risen every year since 2022, free cash flow after leases was EUR 792m in FY2025 against a EUR 500m floor, and the balance sheet has gone from a EUR 2bn rights issue to EUR 1,078m of net cash before leases.
    • Revenue growth has slowed from +22.5% in FY2024 to +6.5% in FY2025 to +1.9% in H1 2026, and consensus holds it flat to FY2028.
    • Consensus FY2026 earnings per share has been cut 38.8% in twelve months while FY2027 and FY2028 have been nudged up
      • The 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain has reached the statements and the out-year estimates and has run backwards on the current year.
  • The earnings growth ahead is large but of the less valued kind.
    • On our path earnings per share on the brokers' basis rises from EUR 0.154 (FY2025) to about EUR 0.26 (FY2027) and EUR 0.31 (FY2028), about 26% a year, 6% to 15% below consensus.
    • Of that, revenue contributes about +1% a year and margin about +6% a year
      • About +18% a year comes from below EBITDA, as depreciation, interest and tax fall with the lease roll-off and deleveraging.
    • The framework's guidance weights revenue and margin above below-EBIT gains, because investors extrapolate the first two and not the third
      • We weight the same way.
  • What it costs is where the chain breaks.
    • At EUR 4.322 the shares are 12.93x 24-month-forward consensus earnings (14.4x our own), the 68th percentile of the stock's five-year range, the 75th of its two-year range, and above every cohort peer on the dashboard's price-to-earnings ratio.
    • The cheapness the sell-side cites is on enterprise value to EBITDA (3.8x FY2027 before leases)
      • The gap between the two lenses is EUR 1.07bn of depreciation, much of it on leased vessels, net interest and a 35% to 41% tax rate.
    • The shares are up 79.1% in twelve months
      • The entry levels at which the modal path clears the bar (about EUR 2.97 for eighteen months, EUR 2.45 for three years) were last seen near the 52-week low of EUR 2.0869.
  • The modal return fails the bar on both horizons.
    • Applying 11x to the earnings two years beyond an eighteen-month horizon and 10x at three years gives -6.4 percent a year and -0.7 percent a year including dividends
    • Holding today's multiple on consensus earnings gives about +11 percent a year, still short of 20%.
    • The earnings leg carries (+13% and +23%), the cash return is steady (about 4% a year), and the de-rating from 14.4x our earnings to 11x and 10x is the swing factor and it is negative.
    • On the plausibly worse drop-through (tax 40%, interest EUR 150m, depreciation EUR 1,100m, 2,247m shares) the eighteen-month figure is -17.3 percent a year
      • The rating does not change.
  • The pattern it fits is 'Demand Inflection', half-crisply.
    • The input half is crisp (a sector-wide cycle, a top-three position, an oligopoly, fixed-cost leverage)
    • The output half is not yet visible in Saipem's own trajectory, so the fit is a C.

  • The case therefore is a compelling business story that the price has already bought.

    • We are bullish on the business and neutral on the shares
    • The case re-opens on price, around EUR 3.00, or on evidence that moves the multiple argument.

  • Rating = D, because the chain holds from the forces ('Change Forces' B) through the outputs ('Financial Outputs' C) and breaks at the return ('Modal return' F): the expected change is material to the business and not to the shareholder at this price, and the framework's question is whether the change drives 20 percent-plus a year in the modal case, which it does not.

    • C was live on the strength of the margin and cash outputs and is not taken for that reason.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The case against, at its strongest, is the sell-side's: the right lens is enterprise value to EBITDA, and on it the shares are cheap.
      • Eight of thirteen brokers sit at buy or overweight with a mean target of EUR 4.89 and a top of EUR 6.00.
      • On enterprise value to EBITDA Saipem at 3.8x FY2027 (before leases) sits at a 38% discount to its own upcycle average and far below Subsea 7 (about 5.8x) and TechnipFMC (about 12.9x), because lease depreciation overstates economic depreciation and the tax rate normalises.
      • Free cash flow after leases of EUR 600m, rising toward EUR 700m to 800m as leases fall, gives a 7% to 11% yield that compounds into a 55% cumulative return by 2030 on Redburn's arithmetic.
      • Consensus FY2027 to FY2028 earnings are themselves too low, because cost recovery (EUR 70m), the drilling recovery and the EUR 300m of synergies (8.8% of combined EBITDA) are not in them.
      • A cleared Saipem7 is the global leader of a market with no new supply to 2028 and deserves at least Subsea 7's multiple on those higher earnings, which gives EUR 5.50 to 6.00 and 20 percent-plus a year.
      • The evidence for it is real: cash conversion has beaten every forecast, order intake is running 27% above expectations, and the balance sheet is the sector's strongest.
    • What we concede.
      • On the free-cash-flow lens the shares are fairly valued rather than dear (EUR 3.70 to 4.30 at an 8% required yield)
      • The merger adds quality at nil premium and 12.3x is a defensible ceiling for the cleared branch
      • Cost recovery is upside the price does not carry
      • And our FY2027 EBITDA may be EUR 60m too low.
    • Why we hold our view, first: the framework's metric is earnings per share.
      • On that footing the stock is at the top of its peers and in the upper quarter of its own cleaner history, so the "discount" is a statement about leases and tax, not about value to the shareholder.
    • Second: the competing hypothesis's own numbers do not clear the bar without a re-rating above today's multiple.
      • Consensus earnings at 12.93x held give about 11 percent a year, a D
      • The sell-side's own mean target is a 13% capital return plus a 4% yield over twelve months, about 17 percent, below the bar.
    • Third: the free-cash-flow story rests partly on working-capital advances that reverse.
      • Working capital runs at -37 days and H1 2026 carried a EUR 170m release that the research itself cannot fully explain
      • Advances reverse when projects complete.
    • Fourth: the merger's per-share earnings effect is unknown from the evidence we hold.
      • Subsea 7's depreciation, interest and tax are not in it
      • On EBITDA per share the combination is broadly neutral after synergies.
      • We make no "the division covers the enterprise value" claim, because none can be made: Asset Based Services' consensus one-year-forward EBITDA of EUR 1,490m at Saipem's own 3.8x is EUR 5,662m, 63% of the enterprise value of EUR 8,929m (56% once EUR 1,187m of lease liabilities are added)
        • At a 5x peer median it is 83% (74% with leases)
        • Only at Subsea 7's 5.8x does it approach cover, at 97% (85% with leases).
    • The second competing hypothesis is the risks research's "false friend": cyclical tailwinds masking unresolved structural execution deficits, with downside prices of EUR 1.25 to 3.00.
      • We do not adopt it as the modal, because the evidence of a changed company (three guidance beats, legacy projects from seven to two, hybrid contracting, net cash) is stronger than it allows
      • It is the reason 'Riskiness' is a D and the trough multiple is Saipem's own.
    • Where the case against would win: the shares holding above 13x our earnings through the FY2026 results in February 2027 with FY2027 EBITDA guided at or above EUR 2.1bn, or sanctioning forecasts for 2028 to 2029 rising rather than flattening.
      • Either would say the market is right to capitalise the cycle at a full multiple, and the de-rating we model would not come.
Change Forces: are the input change forces sufficiently strong? (E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because the external force (a sector-wide deepwater sanctioning cycle with two-plus years of booked visibility) is strong and in the right direction, while the internal forces are mostly already delivered, with one large one (the merger) still ahead
    • The one strong headwind (Middle East transit costs) is external and quantified.
  • Where the strength sits: the strongest force is the cycle, not the company.
    • The internal record is a 2022 to 2025 story that the outputs already show
    • The only live internal change that would move the outputs materially from here is Saipem7.
  • Not A, because the forces' effect on Saipem's own revenue is nil in FY2026 (consensus +0.6%): the fleet is already full, so the cycle reaches Saipem through price and mix rather than volume, and the Middle East headwind is taking about 8% of the year's EBITDA.
External forces: are there sufficiently strong external forces acting on the company, new or intensifying, and in which direction? (RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because the demand force meets the framework's 'Demand Inflection' test (sector-level, evidenced by sanctioning forecasts and sector-wide order books, with fleets booked into 2028), and the headwinds (Middle East logistics, a slight tightening of competition in Brazil and onshore) are real but smaller.
  • Direction by force: demand is a strong tailwind
    • Competition a mild headwind
    • Disruption neutral to a mild tailwind
    • Political and regulatory mixed
    • The supply side a tailwind for a vessel owner and a headwind for a buyer of components and labour
    • Saipem's own cycle mid-to-late
    • Macro a mild headwind.
Demand: is end-market demand changing, structurally or cyclically (a swing with two-plus years of visibility)? (KQ 1, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because global offshore sanctioning capital spending is forecast to rise from USD 115bn in 2026 to USD 149bn in 2027 (+30%), the highest since 2013, with greenfield decisions rising to 71% of the total by 2028 from 31% in 2024, and tier-one fleets booked through 2027 to 2028 (Redburn, Jefferies).
  • Breakevens: deepwater breakevens of USD 35 to 44 per barrel against Brent averaging about USD 70 in 2025 insulate the sanctioning from near-term oil-price swings.
  • Saipem's own evidence: Q2 2026 order intake EUR 4,068m (+86.9% year-on-year, book-to-bill 1.1x)
    • About EUR 8.0bn year to date by July
    • FY2025 intake of EUR 12.9bn to be "comfortably" exceeded
    • An 18-month pipeline of EUR 63bn, from EUR 58bn (28 July 2026).
  • Visibility: cyclical, not structural.
    • The research says long-cycle offshore spending grows through 2028 and then stabilises, and post-2028 subsea growth slows as the South Atlantic basins plateau (Morgan Stanley, Bernstein).
  • Conflicting series: two of the research's own subsea award series disagree in direction, subsea award capital spending USD 28bn in 2026 to USD 31bn in 2027 against SURF award capital spending USD 29bn in 2026 to USD 23bn in 2027 (Redburn).
    • We read the sanctioning total as the stronger series and the SURF-award dip as timing.
  • Not A, because the swing is cyclical with a named plateau two to three years out, and Saipem's own revenue does not inflect (consensus revenue FY2026 to FY2028 EUR 15,584m, EUR 15,850m, EUR 15,854m).
Supply side: input costs, availability, concentration? (KQ 5, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because the scarcity that constrains the industry is Saipem's asset: 20 high-end rigid pipelay vessels exist worldwide across nine companies, none is under construction, and Saipem and Subsea 7 hold eight (40%) between them and five of the six globally tendering J-lay units.
  • Input costs: subsea component lead times are out to 18 to 24 months against 12 to 15 assumed before the print
    • Hydraulic hammers over three years
    • Skilled-labour shortages are named by expert calls
    • Strait of Hormuz logistics cost EUR 70m in H1 2026 with about EUR 70m more expected in H2.
  • Contract protection: new contracts carry indexation and client-procured long-lead items on about 50% of scopes.
  • Not A, because the Middle East cost is live and uncompensated, and the labour constraint is the research's most repeated operational warning.
Revenue and demand cycle: where is the company's own cycle, and is it turning? (KQ 6, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because Saipem is mid-to-late in its own cycle: the fleet is 100% booked for 2026 and 2027 and allocating into 2028 and 2029, backlog of EUR 29,861m (30 June 2026) covers over 95% of H2 2026 revenue, but backlog has fallen from EUR 31,120m a year earlier and revenue growth has slowed from +22.5% (FY2024) to +6.5% (FY2025) to +1.9% (H1 2026).
  • The turn ahead: it is in intake, not revenue.
    • Order momentum (+33.4% in H1 2026) rebuilds the backlog for 2027 to 2029 execution.
  • Not D, because the intake acceleration is real and large
    • Not B, because the top line has stalled for two years on consensus.
Competition: is competitive intensity easing or tightening? (KQ 2, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because the deepwater core is a three-player market with firm pricing (average contract rates +3% to 5% in late 2026; day rates +5% to 7% per an expert call), but at the edges competition is tightening: Allseas won Búzios 10 and Atapu 2 in Brazil, Petrobras is piloting direct charters of pipelay vessels, and Asian yards press onshore margins.
  • Integrated competitor: TechnipFMC takes about 80% of its intake by direct award and has 50 integrated awards worth USD 22.4bn since 2016 against 23 worth USD 7.0bn for the Subsea Integration Alliance
    • That is a share shift toward the integrated competitor which Saipem's merger does not directly answer.
  • Not B, because the Brazilian evidence is recent and goes the wrong way in the largest SURF market.
Political and regulatory: is a change moving demand, pricing or cost? (KQ 4, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because the changes net out: demand tailwinds from European energy-security policy and Saudi Aramco's National EPC Champion programme (the EUR 900m Uthmaniyah award in a 50/50 local consortium), and the removal of two overhangs (the Nigerian tax settlement of 18 June 2026, clearing an unprovisioned claim of about EUR 220m; Brazilian public-contracting reinstatement of 20 December 2024), against the European Commission's Phase 2 review of the merger (opened 22 July 2026, decision by late November 2026) and EU maritime carbon costs of up to USD 300 per tonne of fuel if not passed through (Bernstein).
  • Not D, because every named broker expects the merger review to end in at most minor vessel remedies (Berenberg, Redburn)
    • Not B, because that expectation is not yet a decision.

Summary: Written Summary of the Investment Case (not rated) (E 3, BB 4)
The case in 8 to 10 sentences (the thesis as tested), kept in full at both ends of the record (Part 1, E 3, BB 4)
  • This is a cyclical recovery with a corporate event attached, not a compounder and not a clearing: a large, asset-heavy offshore contractor that repaired itself after 2022 and now sits with a fully booked fleet in the strongest deepwater sanctioning cycle since 2013, a nil-premium merger with its higher-margin peer awaiting European clearance, and a lease book that starts to roll off in 2027.
  • The change has already done most of what it will do to the outputs: EBITDA on the brokers' basis has tripled from EUR 595m (FY2022) to EUR 1,716m (FY2025), the margin has doubled to 11.1%, the balance sheet has gone from a EUR 2bn rights issue to EUR 1,078m of net cash before leases, and dividends are back.
  • What remains is a margin step from 11.3% to about 13% in FY2027 as EUR 140m of Middle East transit costs drop out and the drilling and onshore legs recover, and a mechanical fall in depreciation, interest and tax that takes earnings per share from about EUR 0.155 (FY2026) to about EUR 0.26 (FY2027) and EUR 0.31 (FY2028) on our path, 6% to 15% below consensus.
  • The 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain has travelled from the resources and the decisions through the actions into the operating metrics (utilisation, intake, backlog quality) and the statements (margin, cash, net debt) and has reached the sell-side on the out-years
    • On the current year it ran backwards in July.
  • The business case is compelling
    • The investment case is not, because the price of EUR 4.322 (2026-10-02), up 79.1% in a year, capitalises the out-year consensus at 12.93x, the upper quarter of the stock's own five-year range and the top of its peers on an earnings footing.
  • On our path and our multiple the modal return is -6.4 percent a year over eighteen months and -0.7 percent a year over three
    • Even consensus earnings at today's multiple held give about 11 percent a year.
  • The case clears the framework's bar only below about EUR 2.97 (eighteen months) or EUR 2.45 (three years).
  • We are not betting at EUR 4.32: the price already pays for the cycle, the margin step and the merger
    • At about EUR 3 we would bet that a physically scarce deepwater fleet earns high-teens margins through 2028 and that Saipem7 clears with minor remedies.
