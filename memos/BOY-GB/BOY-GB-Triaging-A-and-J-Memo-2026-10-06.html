[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = D

Thesis. Bodycote was a 'Major Corporate Change' case with an aerospace demand tailwind, and someone else has completed it. At 910.5p the shares offer a 29.5p spread to a recommended 940p cash offer that is expected to complete in Q1 2027, against a fall of about 155p to the undisturbed price if the offer lapses; the forces and the foundations deserve B ratings, and the price has already paid for them.

  • The internal change is real, broad and almost finished.
    • A new chief executive (May 2024) and chief financial officer (February 2023) re-cut the group into Specialist Technologies, Precision Heat Treatment and a Non-Core segment that is being closed, exited 27 of 31 commodity sites, sold ten French automotive plants, completed a £120m buyback and launched a further £80m.
    • Six of the framework's twelve internal forces are strong against a threshold of three, so the inputs pass the screen on breadth rather than on a raised guidance figure, because the 2028 targets were reconfirmed in 2026 rather than lifted.
  • The external change is a net tailwind that acts on the half of the company where the margin is highest.
    • Aerospace and Defence core organic revenue rose 24.6 percent in H1 2026 on narrowbody engine build rates and military flight hours, Industrial Gas Turbines rose 11.0 percent on data-centre power demand, and both are backlog-evidenced with two-plus years of visibility
    • Automotive fell 4.4 percent and is structurally challenged in Western Europe, so about 44 percent of revenue faces flat or contracting markets.
  • The change has travelled every layer of the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain, with the last layer moving least.
    • It shows in the people, the decisions, the actions, the key operating metrics (core organic growth +9.6 percent and the margin +110 basis points to 16.0 percent in H1 2026), the financial statements (adjusted earnings per share +18.3 percent to 25.2p in H1 2026) and, modestly, the sell-side's estimates (FY2026 consensus earnings per share +2.2 percent over six months to 2026-10-02 and still -1.7 percent over twelve).
    • A 7 percent operating-profit beat produced only a 1 to 2 percent upgrade, because H2 2026 is guided to moderate on tougher comparatives.
  • The standalone earnings path is sound and sits just below the framework's band.
    • Consensus adjusted earnings per share run from 44.3p (FY2025) to 61.5p (FY2028), +11.6 percent a year, with margin the largest leg (+5.9 percent a year), reported revenue the smallest (+2.3 percent a year, because Non-Core exits and the surcharge unwind mask core growth of 6 to 10 percent), and the buyback worth about +1.3 percent a year.
    • Our own standalone path is at consensus
    • The parts reconcile to the claim.
  • The private market has paid for all of it.
    • On 2026-09-01 the board unanimously recommended a cash acquisition at 940p per share, a 24.3 percent premium to the undisturbed price, about £1.65bn of equity and £1,852m of enterprise value, after approaches at 885p (Apollo, May 2026), 915p (CVC) and 914p (Veritas) and with CVC withdrawn in September 2026
    • Every shareholding director signed an irrevocable undertaking.
    • The offer is the largest internal change force in the record, and it closes the public case rather than opening it.
  • So the chain holds at every link but the last, and the last link decides the materiality.
    • From 910.5p the 'Modal return' is the 940p received at completion: +3.2 percent in total, which is 2.2 percent a year over eighteen months and 1.1 percent a year over three years, and 6.6 percent a year even annualised over the six months to completion.
    • The return is below the framework's F threshold at every horizon.
    • Strong forces and improving outputs that a shareholder cannot own past Q1 2027 do not make a material thesis.
  • What the price costs on the valuation, and why the standalone branch does not rescue the case. 910.5p is 14.74x the 24-month-forward consensus of 61.77p, the 65th percentile of the stock's ten-year range and the 90th percentile of five years
    • The offer itself is 15.22x the same earnings.
    • If the offer lapses the shares first revert toward about 756p before the standalone case can earn anything, and from today's price that branch returns 5.6 percent a year over eighteen months and 6.8 percent a year over three years at the ten-year median multiple of 13.1x, a loss on the plausibly worse drop-through.
    • The standalone case clears the 20 percent bar only from about 752p on eighteen months and 642p on three years.
  • The pattern it fits is 'Major Corporate Change', crisply on inputs and operating outputs and less crisply at the sell-side layer, and the fit is now a description of a completed event.
    • The mechanism is an operator removing the commodity tail from a cyclical processor with an accreditation moat and letting the aerospace cycle drop through a fixed cost base
    • It is exactly what the acquirer paid 940p for.

  • The case therefore is a six-month merger-arbitrage spread of 3.2 percent that the framework's bar does not reach, sitting on top of a B-grade standalone case that is only available again at a lower price.

    • We park it until the offer completes or lapses.

  • Rating = D, because the input change forces are strong (B) and the financial outputs are improving (B), but the recommended 940p cash offer caps the modal total return from 910.5p at about 3 percent in total to a Q1 2027 completion, so the thesis is immaterial to a public shareholder buying today

    • The parent sits below two of its three Elements because the chain's last link binds.
    • Not C, because no path from 910.5p reaches the 20 percent a year bar at any horizon
    • Not F, because the change is genuine, the offer may lapse, and on a lapse the same forces would support a B-grade case from a lower price.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The case against, at its strongest: the offer undervalues the business.
      • The standalone case is a B-grade 'Major Corporate Change' with an aerospace franchise that covers most of the enterprise value on its own, so a shareholder should hold through completion for a higher bid, or through a lapse for the re-rating the sum of the parts implies.
      • Sum of the parts: Deutsche Bank argued on 2026-03-30 that civil aerospace alone covers 82 percent of enterprise value at mid-cycle multiples and that the rest of Bodycote traded at about 1x EV/EBIT.
      • Aerospace weight: Aerospace and Defence is about half of group operating profit and grew 24.6 percent in H1 2026
        • Specialist Technologies margins of 26.1 percent rival US aerospace suppliers that trade at 25x to 49x forward earnings.
      • Targets above the offer: Deutsche Bank's 960p (2026-08-06) and RBC's 950p with a Hold (2026-09-25).
    • Our response, the cover tested.
      • The profit line is divisional operating profit and the multiple is an enterprise-value multiple, so the comparison is like for like.
      • Aerospace and Defence operating profit is about half of group operating profit, so about £61m of FY2026 consensus EBIT of 122 GBPm.
      • At the house's own multiple: at about 16x EV/EBIT, the multiple Deutsche Bank implied for aerospace and gas turbines, that profit is worth about £976m, 53 percent of the offer's £1,852m enterprise value.
      • At the lowest comparable multiple in the files: at Morgan Advanced Materials' 8.9x EV/EBIT it is worth about £543m, 29 percent of the offer's enterprise value.
      • What an 82 percent cover needs: about 25x EV/EBIT on aerospace profit, which is a US aerospace-supplier multiple rather than a UK industrial one.
        • The cover is an assertion at the preferred multiple, not a bound.
    • What we concede.
      • The mix does argue for a multiple above the stock's ten-year median, and at the conceded anchor of 15.2x the lapse branch returns 16.3 percent a year over eighteen months, still below the bar from today's price.
      • The offer, at 15.2x 24-month-forward earnings and about 17x FY2026 EBIT, is a full price by the stock's own history and a modest one by aerospace peers'.
    • Why we hold our view.
      • A higher bid is unlikely with the rival withdrawn and the board irrevocably committed, and even a 960p outcome returns 5.4 percent.
      • The competing hypothesis is a reason the acquirer is paying 940p
      • It is not a reason for us to pay 910.5p.

Disruption: is a disruption force strengthening or being disproved? (KQ 3, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because the disruption forces named in the research strengthen demand for the company's Specialist Technologies rather than disprove them.
  • Additive manufacturing: printed parts need Hot Isostatic Pressing to remove 2 to 5 percent porosity, and post-processing is up to 60 percent of an additive part's cost
    • The HIP market is projected to reach $1,087.7m by 2033, a 5.6 percent compound rate from 2026.
  • Furnace electrification: EU ETS carbon costs push OEMs to retire captive furnaces
    • About 50 percent of Bodycote's 1,000-plus furnaces are electrified.
  • The disproving side is long-dated: binder-jet sintering or in-situ annealing could reduce the need for HIP in lower-tier applications, and OEM captive printing cells with local vacuum heat treatment are possible.
  • Vehicle electrification: removes internal-combustion heat-treatment volume, a headwind to Automotive partly offset by higher-torque steel gear requirements.
Political and regulatory: is a change moving demand, pricing or cost? (KQ 4, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
