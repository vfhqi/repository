Section summary
  1. Vitrolife's reported earnings are depressed by non-cash acquisition accounting, and that drag fades gradually. The extent to which the market sees the improvement depends on how brokers and consensus databases define EPS. In 2025 Vitrolife reported a net loss of SEK 5,013 mm, driven by a non-cash impairment of SEK 5,357 mm related to the Igenomix acquisition. The ongoing amortisation charge is the narrower issue this report examines. The evidence supports a clear direction for amortisation through 2028 but leaves the 2029– 2030 path and several broker submission practices unresolved. Amortisation step-down. Vitrolife recorded SEK 260m of acquisition-related amortisation in 2025, projected to remain unchanged for 2026. DNB Carnegie then models a step-down to SEK 239m in 2027 and a further fall to SEK 206m in 2028, which means SEK 54m of the annual burden will have fallen away since 2025. The asset base supports this direction. Customer relationships carried SEK 1,053m at the end of 2025, down from SEK 1,313m a year earlier. Trademarks have indefinite useful lives and trigger no recurring amortisation. The step-down is not a path to zero, however. Customer relationship useful lives were extended to 5–25 years from 5–10 years. Production technology acquired in 2021 has a 20-year life and will not expire until 2041. Broker models provide detail only through 2028, so the 2029 and 2030 step-downs remain an information gap. The reduction in amortisation expands reported profitability. DNB Carnegie projects the EBIT margin rising from 20.7% in 2026e to 24.3% in 2028e, and reported EPS expanding from SEK 3.75 to SEK 5.94. This is largely a mechanical, non-cash improvement, and underlying cash flows remain unaffected by these accounting adjustments. How consensus databases capture the numbers. The main discrepancy is definitional. DNB Carnegie defines adjusted EPS by adding back non-cash acquisition-related amortisation of surplus values, while other firms may default to reported earnings. This inconsistency dilutes the reliability of aggregate EPS metrics. The effect of model definitions is visible in practice. Buyback and go-forward modelling. The board launched a SEK 500 mm buyback to optimise the capital structure.

DNB Carnegie reflects a share count of 132.7m from 2026e, down from 135.4m in 2025, a 2.0% reduction. RedEye uses the same share count and raises its net debt assumption to SEK 743m. Specific annual step-downs for 2029 and 2030 are missing. Broker disclosure is selective. The most useful next steps are Vitrolife's upcoming annual reports, direct confirmation of each broker's submission basis to FactSet and Modular Finance, and the completed buyback figures.
