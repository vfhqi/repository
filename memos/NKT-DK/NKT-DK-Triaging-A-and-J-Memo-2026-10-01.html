[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = B

Thesis. NKT is a demand-inflection case in its contracted form: a structural European grid cycle has already been converted into a 13.0bn EUR Transmission backlog (30 Jun 2026) priced at about twice the 2019-20 level per kilometre, and the company is doubling its high-voltage capacity for 2027 so that the backlog converts into volume. At 875.5 DKK (29 Sep 2026), 16.0x earnings twenty-four months forward, the shares pay nothing for that conversion and discount a slip in it. The case is compelling over eighteen months and merely adequate over three years, and it turns on one dated, physical event: the commissioning of Karlskrona's third extrusion tower, the Cologne expansion and the NKT Eleonora in 2027.

  • The change is external, structural and already contracted, which is why 'Change Forces' is the strongest Element in the record.
    • European transmission system operators have roughly doubled their annual grid investment and are booking scarce 525kV HVDC capacity years ahead under frameworks that run to 2033
    • NKT is one of three qualified European makers and has converted the demand into a backlog rather than a forecast.
    • The backlog stands at 13.0bn EUR (30 Jun 2026), from 10.8bn EUR at end-2023, and excludes more than 2.5bn EUR of prospective TenneT framework awards and about 2.5bn EUR of booking commitments.
    • Average contract value on 525kV HVDC awards rose to 1.44m EUR per kilometre for 2024-26 from 0.62m EUR in 2019-20, with inflation indexation and full metal pass-through, so the margin ahead is a conversion of signed contracts rather than a hope.
  • The company's own change is one thing done well: capacity into contracted demand.
    • It is not a multi-dimensional reset, which is why the internal forces rate B and the external A.
    • About 2bn EUR of capital expenditure over 2025-28, funded from the balance sheet, doubles high-voltage capacity to about 12,000 km
      • The tower is erected, the vessel is launched and outfitting in Norway, and management says both are "fully on track for 2027 operations".
    • Guidance has been raised mid-year in each of 2024, 2025 and 2026, and the 2030 ambition (operational EBITDA above 900m EUR) was set 10 percent above consensus in November 2025
      • The chief executive, in post since May 2023, is an operator executing the plan rather than a catalyst.
    • The one scratch is the 300m EUR (about 40 percent) overrun on the Karlskrona budget in December 2024, and it matters because the whole case runs through that site.
  • 'Financial Outputs' have turned up where the framework weights most, and go the wrong way where a screen reads first.
    • That split is the case's shape in 2026 and the reason the Building Block is B rather than A.
    • Operational EBITDA rose from 344m EUR (2024) to 390m EUR (2025) to guidance of 400 to 430m EUR for 2026, raised on 13 Aug 2026
      • The Transmission margin printed 20.2 percent in Q2 2026 against a 15.7 percent consensus, 500bps up on the year, the first quarter in which the post-2022 pricing shows in the profit-and-loss account.
    • Organic revenue is -7 percent in H1 2026 as the subcontracted Champlain Hudson scope rolls off
      • Free cash flow is -341m EUR in H1 2026 on a 225m EUR working-capital outflow
      • Reported earnings per share fall this year as the 6.5 percent tax rate of 2025 normalises to about 25 percent.
    • The sell-side has raised estimates on every window (earnings per share +1, +3 and +7 percent over one, three and six months; the earnings-momentum pin is A), while the buy share is only 36 percent and two of nine visible brokers moved to Sell in August and September 2026 on valuation.
  • The change has travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain except for the one output that decides the case, volume.
    • It shows in the company's resources (a Karlskrona operator as chief executive), its choices (the capital programme, the 'Charging Forward' strategy), its actions (the tower, the vessel, Photonics sold, SolidAl bought, Champlain Hudson delivered ahead of target), its key operating metrics (price per kilometre, the Transmission margin), its financial statements (EBITDA) and the sell-side's estimates.
    • Volume waits for the 2027 capacity
      • Until it commissions, the 2026 profit-and-loss account says little about 2027 kilometres, and that is the gap the price is pricing.
  • What we think it earns.
    • Earnings per share of 4.20 EUR in FY2026 rising to 5.40 EUR in FY2027, 7.55 EUR in FY2028 and 9.00 EUR in FY2029, 2 to 5 percent below consensus (4.17, 5.50, 7.90, 9.54 EUR; 25 Sep 2026) for the timing risk of the ramp.
    • The growth sits in the two components investors extrapolate: revenue about 14 percent a year from FY2026 to FY2028 as the backlog converts at the new capacity, and margin about 15 percent a year as post-2022 contracts replace legacy ones on a fixed-cost base of towers and vessels
      • The below-EBIT lines add little and the share count is flat.
    • From the FY2025 reported base of 4.93 EUR the three-year compound rate is about 15 percent a year, inside the framework's 12 to 20 percent band
      • The FY2027-28 steps of 29 and 40 percent a year are the inflection the pattern looks for, and we shade FY2028 below consensus for the framework's caution on single years above 40 percent.
  • What it costs on the valuation: nothing for the re-rating, and a discount on the earnings the multiple is applied to.
    • At 875.5 DKK the shares are 16.0x consensus earnings twenty-four months forward (29 Sep 2026), the 13th percentile of the stock's own five-year range and the 26th of ten years, below Prysmian's 17.6x for a company growing earnings two to three times as fast.
    • The price has moved against the case since the summer: down 23 percent from the 52-week high of 1,135 DKK and 10.8 percent over three months, through a guidance raise, on a sector sell-off, a free-cash-flow miss and the two new Sell ratings.
    • On our own path the same price is 16.7x the twenty-four-month-forward figure and about 22.9x the one-year-forward figure
      • The convention looks through the transition year to the 2028 step and prices that step at a discount.
  • The return clears the 20 percent bar over eighteen months on the modal path and falls to the framework's C band over three years.
    • Earnings growth carries the return
    • The re-rating is the swing factor.
    • Eighteen months (March 2028): 18.0x on the earnings two years beyond that horizon (9.25 EUR on our path; 69.3 DKK on the dashboard's scale) gives 1,247 DKK, +42 percent, about 27 percent a year, if the 2027 capacity commissions on time
      • 18x is the stock's own 25th percentile and its ten-year "conservative" band, a point above Prysmian today.
    • Three years (September 2029): 16.0x, today's multiple, on the earnings two years beyond that horizon (10.53 EUR; 78.9 DKK) plus about 13 DKK of a first dividend gives 1,275 DKK, +46 percent, about 13 percent a year, because by then the earnings two years out are 2031's and the company itself expects the market to approach balance "entering the 2030s".
    • The arithmetic blend of the cleared branch (65 percent, 1,247 DKK) and the uncleared one (35 percent, a ramp a year late at 13.6x, about 725 DKK) is about 1,064 DKK, +22 percent, about 14 percent a year
      • The modal case clears the bar, the expected value across branches does not, and that is the chief reason the Building Block is not A.
  • The pattern is 'Demand Inflection', and the fit is crisp.
    • A sector-level, structural, evidenced demand explosion
    • A dominant position in a three-player oligopoly that behaves on value over volume
    • Genuine fixed-cost leverage
    • The one condition it fails, one-to-many scalability, is the one a cable manufacturer cannot pass and is why free cash flow is negative through the inflection.
  • Where the chain holds and where it breaks.
    • It holds from the demand to the backlog to the price per kilometre to the Transmission margin, every link contracted or printed
    • It breaks, if it breaks, at one physical event in one year, and the sector's own base rate is that "virtually no major high-voltage capacity expansions finalise without at least temporary margin disruption".
    • What would change our mind: a postponed site-acceptance test at Karlskrona, a further capex revision, a Transmission margin below about 14 percent for a quarter, Eleonora's outfitting slipping into 2027, or a quarter in which net working capital unwinds by more than 300m EUR.

  • The case therefore is: a contracted, twice-priced backlog converting into volume when the 2027 capacity commissions, bought at a multiple that pays for the doubt and not for the delivery

    • Compelling over eighteen months on a condition we judge probable at about 65 percent, adequate over three years, and monitorable on two series.

  • Rating = B, because the change is very material and already contracted (a 13.0bn EUR backlog at roughly twice the 2019-20 price per kilometre, capacity doubling from 2027) and the modal return clears the 20 percent bar over eighteen months (about 27 percent a year at 18x), but over three years the return falls to the C band (about 13 percent a year) with the multiple held at 16x for a business whose growth matures after 2030, and the outputs the market reads first (organic revenue, free cash flow, reported earnings per share) go the wrong way in 2026 before they go right in 2027.

    • Not A, because A needs the return to clear the bar on both horizons without a re-rating, and the expected value across the cleared and uncleared branches (about 14 percent a year) does not clear it
    • Not C, because the modal return clears the bar over eighteen months on a condition we judge probable, and the earnings growth that carries it is contracted rather than hoped for.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The case against, at its strongest: NKT is a capital-intensive project business whose reported strength is optical, and the modal branch is the uncleared one.
      • Reported revenue is held up by metal pass-through while standard-metal revenue falls (-7 percent in H1 2026)
        • The margin beats lean on repair windfalls and vessel utilisation that do not repeat.
      • Free cash flow is deeply negative (-341m EUR in H1 2026 against a -85m EUR consensus for Q2), and the -1.5bn EUR working-capital position is customer prepayments that reverse as intake normalises, which is already happening (net cash 963m EUR to 591m EUR in six months to 30 Jun 2026).
      • The capacity programme has overrun once by 300m EUR
        • The sector base rate says the ramp brings margin disruption
        • The CFO and two operational EVPs left inside the ramp year and the grievance data spiked
        • The antitrust density exceeds peers'.
      • Every competitor is adding capacity and vessels for 2027-30 and the company itself expects market balance in the 2030s, so the earnings the twenty-four-month multiple is applied to are the peak
        • The long is crowded with no visible short interest, two brokers have moved to Sell, and the shares fell 23 percent from the high through a guidance raise.
      • On that reading: a year's slip, penalties and charter costs, earnings two years forward of about 7.1 EUR at March 2028 and a multiple of 13.6x or below, about 725 DKK (-17 percent), with a stress case near 635 DKK (-27 percent) at the ten-year "crash" band of 11.9x.
    • What we concede.
      • The 2026 profit-and-loss account tells us little about 2027 volumes
      • The cash-flow reversal is real and larger than the sell-side modelled
      • The sector base rate on capacity ramps is against a clean 2027
      • The governance and culture signals are what strain looks like
      • The terminal value is capped by the company's own words on equilibrium
      • The Q2 Transmission margin had favourable mix and utilisation inside it
      • And the price has moved against us since the summer for reasons that are not all noise.
    • Why we still hold our view.
      • The backlog is contracted at 1.44m EUR per kilometre with indexation and metal pass-through, so the margin is not a forecast but a conversion, and the 20.2 percent Transmission print of Q2 2026 is the first quarter of that conversion, with Grid Solutions and Accessories (the repair business) reported separately from it since January 2026.
      • The tower is erected and the vessel launched
        • Management raised guidance in the transition year with the ramp costs inside it, and its record on the earnings line is four years of beats against initial midpoints.
      • The cash burn is chosen and covered twice over by liquidity (1.25bn EUR at 30 Jun 2026 against consensus FY2026 free cash flow of -405m EUR), and consensus already carries the burn.
      • The multiple already sits in the bottom sixth of the stock's range and at 7.8x FY2028 enterprise value to EBITDA, so the doubt is in the price and the delivery is not: the uncleared branch costs about 17 percent and the cleared branch pays about 42 percent, at odds we put at 35 to 65.
      • We make no claim that any division covers the enterprise value, and none is needed: this is a growth case with a bounded, dated, monitorable failure mode, not a bounded-downside case.
    • Where the competing hypothesis is right, on the terminal value, we have already used it, in the 16x we apply at three years and in the three-year C band that keeps the Building Block at B.
Change Forces: are the input change forces sufficiently strong? (E 1, BB 1) Rating = A
  • Rating = A, because the external force (structural, sector-wide grid demand, evidenced by TSO capital plans, framework contracts running to 2033 and a record order book) is as strong as any in the universe, and the company's internal response (capacity doubling, pricing at twice the old level, guidance raised three years running) is strong and resourced.
  • Parent above child: the Element sits above its weaker child (internal forces B) because the external force is not a forecast but a contracted backlog, and the framework asks here for strength, not for breadth of internal change.
  • Not B, because the demand force meets every part of the 'Demand Inflection' test with hard evidence.

Demand: is end-market demand changing, structurally or cyclically (a swing with two-plus years of visibility)? (KQ 1, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = A
  • Rating = A, because European grid demand is structural, sector-level and evidenced by order books, customer capital plans and policy, which is the framework's own test of "sufficiently strong".
  • TSO investment: selected European transmission system operators have doubled annual grid investment against historical levels of about 5 percent
    • DNB Carnegie calls high-voltage cable "the primary bottleneck in the green transition" (6 Aug 2026).
  • Framework horizons: moved from two to six years for distribution operators
    • Eastern Green Link 3 is scheduled for commissioning in 2033
    • TenneT awards multi-year frameworks in place of spot bidding.
  • Order books: NKT's backlog 13.0bn EUR at 30 Jun 2026 (13.5bn EUR at Q1 2026; 10.8bn EUR at end-2023), excluding more than 2.5bn EUR of prospective TenneT framework awards and booking commitments
    • Nexans' adjusted high-voltage backlog 7.7bn EUR (29 Jul 2026).
  • Grid age and market size: over 40 percent of the European grid is older than 40 years, needing an estimated 584bn EUR by 2030
    • The addressable high-voltage market exceeds 10bn EUR a year over 2024-30
    • ENTSO-E projects 2031-35 offshore network investment 41 percent above 2025-30.
  • Tailwind.
    • The framework's caution is that inflection demand is cyclical until proven structural
    • The proof here is policy and contracted capital plans rather than a spot market.

Competition: is competitive intensity easing or tightening? (KQ 2, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because competitive intensity in high-voltage transmission has eased (prices per kilometre doubled; an oligopoly behaving on value over volume) while the pipeline of new capacity dates a tightening for 2028-2030, and medium-voltage Central Europe is already tight.
  • Pricing: average contract value on 525kV HVDC rose to 1.44m EUR per kilometre for 2024-26 awards from 0.62m EUR in 2019-20 (DNB Carnegie, 6 Aug 2026)
    • Prysmian's Transmission EBITDA margin 21.2 percent in Q2 2026 from 17.1 percent a year earlier
    • Nexans' 13.7 percent in H1 2026.
  • Entrants: Sumitomo's Scottish subsea HVDC factory (mid-2026)
    • Hellenic Cables qualified 525kV systems (Aug 2026) but lacks the balance sheet and vessels for the largest turnkey projects
    • LS Cable positioned to be a top-three producer by 2030
    • Taihan building a 640kV plant and buying an installation vessel
    • Prysmian adding three vessels by 2027
    • Nexans commissioning Electra (June 2026) plus a 40 percent grid-capacity expansion.
  • Medium voltage: a 290bps Applications margin contraction in early 2025 from Central European competition, recovered to 12.0 percent then 10.5 percent in Q2 2026 with ramp costs and input inflation.
  • Direction: easing now in the business that carries 75 to 80 percent of EBITDA
    • Tightening on a three-year view.
    • Not A, because the entrant pipeline is real and dated
    • Not C, because qualification barriers and full backlogs push its effect beyond 2028.
Political and regulatory: is a change moving demand, pricing or cost? (KQ 4, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because policy is moving demand and bankability in NKT's markets (Germany, the UK, the EU) while the negatives (France's target cut, US offshore wind, Danish connection pauses, permitting) are peripheral or already insulated.
  • Germany and the UK: the draft Offshore Wind Act amendment (Aug 2026) brings two-sided contracts for difference replacing negative bids, 35-year permits, 70 GW by 2045 and NZIA local-content criteria capping Chinese components
    • The UK CfD round secured 8.4 GW

    • A Clean Industry Bonus of 27m GBP per GW.
  • Headwinds: France cut offshore targets from 18 GW to 15 GW
    • Energinet paused new connection requests with waits up to ten years (Jun 2026)
    • The RED III permitting transition
    • US offshore wind faces "severe delay or cancellation risks", but NKT's backlog is less exposed than Nexans' (48 percent offshore wind) or Prysmian's (28 percent).
  • Antitrust: the competition authorities are read as a regulatory actor at (KQ 4, RA 1, E 1, BB 2)
    • Here the direction call is a tailwind on demand with a live legal negative noted.
Disruption: is a disruption force strengthening or being disproved? (KQ 3, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because the live disruption force (the shift to 525kV and 640kV extruded DC) raises the qualification bar in NKT's favour, and the disruptions that could hurt (superconductors, 800VDC data-centre architecture) sit outside its core.
  • Higher voltages: 525kV extruded DC is becoming the standard for long-distance transmission, doubling capacity over older systems
    • NKT has commercialised a 640kV system.
  • Peripheral threats: high-temperature superconducting cables are being phased out of utility grids on cost and resurging only inside data centres (expert calls, Apr and Aug 2026)
    • 800VDC data-centre power delivery could cut low-voltage copper volumes, a small line for NKT.
  • Tailwind.
    • Not A, because the force strengthens the whole qualified oligopoly, not NKT alone.
Catch-all: any other material external dynamic, positive or negative? (KQ 8, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) optional Rating = B

  • Negatives: no in-house metallurgy or recycling
    • Maillefer is the leading maker of the extrusion equipment that 525kV needs
    • The Strait of Hormuz closure raised selected input costs in Q2 2026 and depressed Distribution margins.
  • Mild headwind.
    • Not D, because pass-through is contractual in the business that matters.

    • The core (TSO interconnectors) is state-backed critical infrastructure.
