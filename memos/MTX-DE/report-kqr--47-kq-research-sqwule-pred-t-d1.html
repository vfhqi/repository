EXPECTED CONVERGENCE DIVERGENCE

1.
Severe downside: Extreme outcomes could emerge if MTU Aero Engines fails to meet its mid-term targets due to persistent commercial maintenance challenges. Rothschild and Co Redburn maintains a Sell rating on the stock because the market likely underestimates these structural aftermarket headwinds. If these challenges persist, the company will struggle to transition to its planned capital return framework. This divergence from standard expectations would severely depress long-term earnings per share growth.
2.
Delayed cash: Accumulating uncollected balances presents a substantial threat to the company's financial turnaround over the next decade. Bernstein Research highlights that the pre-financed geared turbofan shop visits have built up an aftermarket imbalance liability of 1.1 billion euros. If airline customers experience deep financial distress, this pre-financed balance may not be collected on schedule. This collection failure would prevent the anticipated cash conversion inflection.
3.
Compressed margins: Persistent profitability bottlenecks threaten to keep the commercial maintenance division's performance locked at historical lows. Rothschild and Co Redburn models show the adjusted MRO margin crawling to only 8.0% in 2028 and 8.2% by 2030. This long-term compression occurs because lower-margin geared turbofan shop visits continue to dominate the revenue mix. Consequently, MTU would remain far below its target corridor of 8.5% to 9.5%.

DOWNSTREAM STAKEHOLDER CONSTRAINTS

4.
Negative pressure: Operational disruptions at the joint venture level would rapidly cascade down the commercial aerospace supply chain. Risk-sharing partners like Melrose Industries division Engines continue to absorb substantial cash drags from the geared turbofan program. If MTU is forced to prolong its pre-financing activities, tier-one suppliers will face extended cash flow constraints. These persistent capacity bottlenecks will hamper supplier investments in repair capabilities.
5.
Structural strain: Unresolved MRO capacity issues will directly damage the financial profile of prime manufacturers. Morgan Stanley estimates that the total cost of the geared turbofan powder metal contamination is shared across risk-sharing partners. Prolonged shop turnaround times of over 300 days force Pratt and Whitney to expand its spare engine inventory. This massive capital reallocation will prolong operating losses on new-build engines.
6.
Restrained operations: Severe engine shortages continue to impose critical capacity restrictions on low-cost carrier operations. Jefferies Research indicates that geared turbofan engine groundings remain the primary operational swing factor for airlines like Wizz Air. These prolonged grounding periods erode passenger yields and force carriers to operate less fuel-efficient legacy fleets. Such ongoing flight disruptions will limit the airlines' ability to pay for scheduled overhauls.
