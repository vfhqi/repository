EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Strategic Change Materiality Swissquote completed the full acquisition of digital finance app Yuh in July 2025 for CHF 180 million, consolidating its digital market share and streamlining retail brand positioning, though full ownership of Yuh raises cost exposure and might dilute near-term pre-tax margins if international expansion costs escalate rapidly. The company is building an on-premises Sovereign AI Platform powered by a GPU compute farm to enhance operational efficiency, with automated AI initiatives rolling out in customer support, software development, and new product offerings from H2-2026. Management retained its CHF 500 million pre-tax profit target by fiscal year 2028 despite near-term headwinds, maintaining a dual brand approach with the high-end Swissquote brand alongside the retail-focused Yuh app to capture diverse client cohorts.

- Earnings Calls Top Ranked Forward EPS Drivers Net interest income decay: Jefferies forecasts net interest income to drop from CHF 227 million in FY26 to CHF 222 million in FY27 as the temporary P&L boon from high rates reverses; plausibility is HIGH given central bank policy directions toward rate cuts. Core fee income expansion: Jefferies projects net fee and commission income to expand from CHF 288 million in FY26 to CHF 379 million in FY28, supported by Swissquote's focus on affluent segments with average portfolios of CHF 117k and average order sizes of CHF 15k to CHF 20k that enable high-margin fee levels; plausibility is HIGH given strong asset gathering trends. eForex volatility spread capture: Jefferies forecasts eForex-driven net trading income to grow from CHF 217 million in FY26 to CHF 262 million in FY28, with geopolitical upheaval supporting elevated currency volatility; plausibility is MEDIUM due to unpredictable global currency volatility cycles.

Crypto trading optionality: Jefferies expects crypto revenue to decline from CHF 81 million in FY25 to CHF 75 million in FY26 following H1 2026 trading volumes that collapsed by 60% year-on-year to CHF 2.6 billion, though Deutsche Bank argues that Swissquote's crypto exposure is an embedded call option with high operational leverage; plausibility of rapid near-term recovery is LOW given current risk-off retail behavior.

Yuh integration synergies: Deutsche Bank notes Swissquote has taken full control of its digital onboarding funnel via Yuh, with management revising its net revenue target upwards to CHF 950 million, though operating margins were adjusted downwards to 53% due to Yuh's comparatively less profitable profile, while Jefferies reported that Yuh added over 24,000 new accounts in the first half of 2026; plausibility of long-term retail client monetization is HIGH but margin execution risk is MEDIUM.

Recent Earnings Delivery Performance Swissquote reported H1 2026 net revenues of CHF 364.2 million (up 1.7% year-on-year), missing compiled consensus expectations of CHF 380.8 million by 4.4%, while pre-tax profit of CHF 182.9 million (down 1.2% year-on-year) missed sell-side consensus estimates of CHF 194.5 million by 6.0%. Net crypto assets income collapsed 66.2% year-on-year to CHF 14.6 million (versus consensus of CHF 31.2 million, a 53.2% miss), driven by cryptocurrency trading volumes that plunged 60.0% year-on-year to CHF 2.6 billion.

Crypto results included a loss of CHF 5.3 million incurred by the remeasurement of crypto assets inventory, representing roughly a $6.5 million negative mark-to-market adjustment on the SQX exchange. Noncrypto operational offsets were robust: net fee and commission income excluding crypto grew 13.0% year-on-year to CHF 123.7 million, client assets reached a record CHF 96.3 billion (up 19.8% year-on-year and 2.0% ahead of consensus), and net interest income increased by 7.2% year-on-year to CHF 115.9 million.

Management lowered its full-year 2026 net revenue guidance to approximately CHF 730 million (from CHF 760 million) and cut pre-tax profit guidance to approximately CHF 365 million (from CHF 385 million).

Forward Credibility Archetypes Swissquote has historically set conservative baseline targets that are regularly upgraded during the fiscal year: initial FY23 pre-tax profit target was set at CHF 230 million but the company delivered CHF 255.4 million; initial FY24 pre-tax profit guidance of CHF 300 million was upgraded to CHF 320 million but actual FY24 pre-tax profit reached CHF 345.6 million; actual FY25 pre-tax profit of CHF 420.2 million substantially beat the initial target of CHF 355 million; the systematic sandbagging pattern indicates management operates under a highly conservative forecasting archetype.

However, the H1 2026 pre-tax profit of CHF 182.9 million missed sell-side expectations of CHF 194.5 million by 6.0%, forcing Swissquote to lower its FY26 pre-tax profit guidance to CHF 365 million from CHF 385 million, representing the first material downgrade. Management reiterated its long-term pre-tax profit target of CHF 500 million by fiscal year 2028, though full consolidation of Yuh diluted the pre-tax margin target to 53% from 55% previously.

Sell-Side Positioning The sell-side coverage pool for Swissquote is concentrated and consists of two primary active international brokers: Jefferies (Julian Roberts, Tom Mills, Laura Gris Trillo, and Fangfei Li) and Deutsche

Bank (Miriam Killian and Benjamin Goy); the rating mix per Bloomberg consensus as of August 2026 shows a split between Buy and Hold recommendations, with price targets showing high dispersion spanning from a low of CHF 36 to a high of CHF 59. Jefferies downgraded its price target from CHF 41 to CHF 36 following the weak crypto revenue print and maintained its Hold rating, arguing that current year earnings expectations remain overly optimistic.

Deutsche Bank adjusted its target price from CHF 62 to CHF 59 prior to the earnings release and reiterated its Buy rating, claiming the core business fundamentals remain strong despite moderated crypto trading. Jefferies analysts highlight that the 66% drop in crypto revenues proves Swissquote's dependency on highly volatile transactional income, remain highly sceptical of near-term AI-driven cost savings and incremental revenue realization from the Yuhlia assistant, and forecast a PBT margin of only 49.8% in FY26.

Deutsche Bank argues that Swissquote's diversified multi-pillar model cushions the downside from volatile crypto trading, highlights the consistent inflows of net new money and robust client account growth as key drivers, notes that resilient net interest income and transactional fees offset the cyclical decline in digital assets, and identifies Germany's upcoming pension reform as a major structural tailwind for long-term retail client growth.

Fundamental Peer Positioning Swissquote's H1-2026 revenue expanded just 1.7% year-on-year, severely underperforming FlatexDEGIRO's 26% growth print, with net crypto revenue crashing 66.2% year-on-year while peers lacked direct digital asset exposure; Avanza achieved a 70% operating margin in H1-2026, comfortably ahead of Swissquote's pre-tax margin, and Swissquote's pre-tax margin of 50.2% in H1-2026 sat significantly below Nordnet's margin exceeding 70%.

Swissquote reduced its full-year 2026 pre-tax profit guidance to CHF 365 million from CHF 385 million, with Jefferies lowering its Swissquote FY26 EPS forecast by 12% to CHF 2.06 post-H1, while FlatexDEGIRO upgraded its FY26 net profit guidance to EUR 200-230 million on robust transaction activity and Deutsche Bank raised its flatexDEGIRO FY26 net income forecast to EUR 216 million following strong momentum. Offsetting factors include Swissquote's robust H1-2026 net new money of CHF 5.1 billion demonstrating solid core asset gathering, Nordnet's customer base growing by 13% year-onyear (adding 280,000 new savers) and total savings capital climbing 29% year-on-year in Q2-2026, and net interest income at Swissquote rising 7.2% to CHF 115.9 million partially offsetting trading fee contractions.

Activism Presence No active activist presence in AS library for Swissquote. However, former Yuh partner PostFinance divested its stake to focus on domestic digital banking services, reducing its ownership in Swissquote Group by selling 3.5% of its 5% shareholding in December 2025, though PostFinance retains a 1.5% stake and continues to use Swissquote's e-trading platform under long-term agreements.

Major Material Risks Risk One–Simultaneous interest rate and crypto contraction: Deutsche Bank research indicates that if central bank policy rates decline rapidly, deposit reinvestment yields and transactional revenues will face severe simultaneous pressure, with core revenue growth remaining highly fragile as if central bank rate cuts accelerate, net interest income will decay, removing the buffer that historically masked severe transactional crypto contractions. Risk Two–Hidden capital intensity under Category 3 banking: if balance assets exceed CHF 17 billion, higher capital requirements will be triggered, potentially constraining capital deployment priorities as the CHF 300 million excess capital over the 18% board limit is earmarked for inorganic client asset growth. Risk Three–Yuh margin dilution from international expansion: escalating international expansion costs on Yuh could dilute group-level margins if mass-retail users fail to migrate to premium services, amplified by cost exposure that might dilute near-term pre-tax margins if international expansion costs escalate rapidly.
