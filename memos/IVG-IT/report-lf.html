EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF) 8 findings

Olof Persson assumed operational control of Iveco Group NV on July 1, 2024, transitioning from Independent Non-Executive Director after co-architecting the March 2024 Capital Markets Day strategic plan. The experienced heavy-industrial operator brings extensive CEO exposure from Volvo Group, though his tenure at AB Volvo ended with activist Cevian Capital ousting him in April 2015 due to delayed margin realization versus peers. Within twelve months at Iveco, Persson delivered a transformational strategic realignment via the €1.7B Defence divestment to Leonardo and €1,550m extraordinary dividend distribution, yet operational execution was marred by severe plant rework costs and unfinished inventory build in the Bus unit at Annonay, absorbing €200m in free cash flow during FY25.

Chief-executive quality and repeatable operator patterns

1.
Persson's prior track record demonstrates disciplined industrial cost-cutting, expanding Bombardier Transportation EBIT margins from 2.7% in FY06 to 5.2% in FY11 while growing sales from $6,653m to $8,600m. At Volvo Group, he engineered a SEK 10 billion structural cost reduction program eliminating 6,500 whitecollar positions and unlocked over SEK 20 billion through non-core divestitures. However, Volvo Group operating margins dropped from 8.7% in FY11 to 6.5% in FY15 during European downturns, generating negative relative TSR versus heavy-truck peers Scania and PACCAR. At Iveco, Persson exhibits plain-spoken directness focusing squarely on industrial execution and product quality, executing the Fire Fighting sale, Leonardo Defence transaction and Tata tender recommendation within 12 months of operational appointment. He instituted "2026: The Year of Quality" protocols to eliminate post-production plant rework, yet withheld formal FY26 quantitative guidance during pending transaction clearances, though revised FY25 targets promptly when light-duty demand slowed. The operational archetype reveals a repeatable structural restructuring template that risks slow margin realization during cyclical downswings, offset by decisive M&A execution velocity

Governance robustness and board independence constraints

2.
Iveco's board comprises nine members with two Executive Directors and seven Non-Executive Directors, where five of seven Non-Executive Directors qualify as independent under Dutch Corporate Governance Code guidelines, representing a 56% independent majority. However, governance architecture is defined by four directors maintaining direct ties to primary shareholder Exor, including Executive Chair Suzanne Heywood and Non-Executive Directors Alessandro Nasi and Clara Fain. Reference shareholder Exor N.V. holds 43.11% voting power via 73.38m Special Voting Shares despite owning only 27.06% of common equity, achieved through a loyalty program awarding one special voting share per common share held continuously for three years. Audit Committee is chaired by independent director Essimari Kairisto with two of three members meeting independence criteria, while Compensation Committee is chaired by Linda Knoll and comprises three independent members alongside Exor representative Alessandro Nasi. Non-executive directors bring deep sector experience including Rolls-Royce CEO Tufan Erginbilgic and Baker Hughes CEO Lorenzo Simonelli, achieving 100% board and committee meeting attendance during 2025. Governance safeguards include annual re-election of all nine directors at each AGM, eliminating entrenched multi-year classified terms, and recusal protocols where Chair Suzanne Heywood and director Alessandro Nasi recused themselves from board deliberations on the Tata transaction. Structural vulnerabilities persist as FPT Industrial maintains a 10-year Engine Supply Agreement providing €656m in 2025 sales to CNH Industrial, alongside Master Service Agreements and Financial Service Agreements renewed through 2027. The governance verdict: dominant Exor representation and referenceshareholder influence limit formal independence despite experienced independent directors overseeing key committees.
