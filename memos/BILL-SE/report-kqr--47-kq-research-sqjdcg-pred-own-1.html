This structural adjustment removes historical multi-year pricing lag and directly exposes Billerud to annual market fluctuations. Consequently, the group expects to adjust prices more rapidly to volatile input cost changes.
2.
Stable volumes: Billerud maintained its commercial relationships with primary beverage carton manufacturers by preserving volume frameworks while shifting pricing terms. During the Q4 2025 earnings call on January 30, 2026, management stated that 2026 contracted volumes remained in line with 2025 volumes. This strategy prioritizes operational mill utilization over aggressive volume growth during market downturns. This framework ensures stable baseload demand but limits near-term market share expansion.
3.
Historical comparison: Billerud historically relied on multi-year agreements that protected buyers from short-term inflation but left the producer vulnerable during cost spikes. Jefferies reported on November 16, 2022, that these longer-term contracts in liquid packaging board represented a major margin squeeze risk during periods of high inflation. The rigid structures prevented the company from passing through escalating Nordic wood costs. This commercial vulnerability prompted the eventual strategic pivot toward flexible annual price cycles.
4.
Complex qualification: Billerud faces high barriers to changing its core volume commitments because of the intensive technical requirements of carton converters. Jefferies noted on August 2, 2024, that liquid packaging board is a niche highly specialized product requiring extensive testing and machine certification. Section 3 – Input Costs and Wood Fiber Dynamics

Section summary
  1. Lower Scandinavian pulpwood costs provide Billerud with significant margin relief, but oversupply and buyer resistance limit pricing benefits.
  2. Nordic pulpwood prices fell substantially from their peak, though persistent diesel and chemical costs prevent a complete return to historical baselines.

Nordic Wood Cost Easing

1.
Falling costs: Billerud expects sequential input cost improvements to accelerate due to favorable Nordic wood fiber dynamics. In its fourth-quarter earnings call on January 30, 2026, management guided for a SEK 130 mm total cost relief in the first quarter of 2026. This cost deflation is driven primarily by falling pulpwood costs, which are anticipated to accelerate into the second quarter of 2026 as lower-priced inventory cycles through the mills. These operational cost savings directly support the group's near-term recovery in earnings per share.
2.
Lagged benefits: Billerud experienced delayed earnings benefits from wood fiber deflation due to balance sheet inventory cycles. Jefferies reported on April 28, 2026, that a three-to-six month lag exists before lower stumpage costs become visible in pulp and paper mill earnings. This cost delay meant that sequential wood relief was only SEK 110 mm in the second quarter of 2026, which disappointed initial broker projections. Consequently, this lag delayed the recovery of European packaging margins and pressurized near-term earnings.
3.

Jefferies reported on October 16, 2026, that Nordic pulpwood prices fell 20% to 30% from their peaks because of reduced demand and economic downtime. In contrast, Central European wood costs remained highly constrained and actually rose in Germany, Czechia, and Poland. This regional variance gives Billerud a distinct cost advantage over non-Nordic peers, directly supporting its European competitive positioning.

Section 5 – The Nippon Dynawave Outage and Restart Timeline

Section summary
  1. The tragic Nippon Dynawave mill outage removed eight percent of global liquid packaging board capacity, significantly tightening the market.
  2. While Nippon Dynawave targets a gradual December restart, the supply shock has temporarily boosted the bargaining power of alternative board producers. Impact of the Washington Mill Outage

1.
Sudden supply disruption: The catastrophic white liquor tank collapse at the Longview mill on May 26, 2026, instantly halted Nippon Dynawave's North American operations. According to Morgan Stanley research on June 15, 2026, the facility represents approximately eight percent of global liquid packaging board capacity. This unexpected removal of 300,000 annual tonnes of qualified supply created immediate tension in the specialized bleached board market. Consequently, industry operating rates tightened as customers sought to replace lost volumes.
2.
Severe human and financial toll: The catastrophic industrial disaster resulted in eleven fatalities and multiple serious injuries at the Washington facility. Parent company Nippon Paper announced on November 6, 2026, that the accident contributed to an estimated operating loss of 133 million Dollars at the subsidiary. Additionally, Nippon Paper revised its full-year forecasts to project a consolidated net loss of 40 billion Yen. This severe financial distress has limited the parent group's ability to fund immediate, large-scale capital restorations.
