EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

This comprehensive risk assessment of ReNew Energy Global PLC synthesizes findings from company filings, broker research, and expert transcripts to evaluate the investment thesis under systematic stress-testing. The analysis reveals a FALSE FRIEND profile as the dominant failure mode, where superficial operational milestones mask structural equity return erosion driven by uncompensated grid curtailment, regulatory forecasting penalties, and execution bottlenecks.

While ReNew demonstrates robust capital recycling capabilities and industry-leading currency hedging discipline, the investment faces medium-probability downside risks concentrated in transmission infrastructure delays and manufacturing margin compression. The take-private proposal at USD 7.02 caps near-term public market upside, warranting a hold-and-watch stance until the special committee vote resolves or material curtailment data emerges.

Published short and bear case credibility

The bear case exhibits strong fact-based grounding on operational execution delays and capital intensity but remains speculative on manufacturing technology risks. No active or historical published short-seller reports exist for ReNew, with the total absence of activist short campaigns suggesting operational and reporting structures do not exhibit clear targets for public short attacks. Current short interest stands at only 0.9% of shares sold short, indicating minimal overall market bearishness. However, ReNew's wind projects face delays of up to two years from scheduled completion due to complex agricultural land acquisition, with many wind projects bid at aggressive tariffs yielding low single-digit equity IRRs in reality. Grid congestion and transmission delays forced backing down of power, causing an estimated Rs 100 crore curtailment loss in FY26. The bear narrative forms a cohesive operational loop where high leverage and lower wind plant load factors compound capital constraints, making asset recycling a structural necessity rather than strategic choice.

Winning pre-mortem hypothesis

The FALSE FRIEND profile emerges as the most coherent narrative with the absolute fewest hard inconsistencies in the Analysis of Competing Hypotheses matrix. Assuming the investment has failed 24 months from now, the FALSE FRIEND profile wins because it possesses the absolute fewest hard inconsistencies. Superficial success indicators like improved receivable days and capacity additions actually mask structural decline in project-level equity returns.

The framework identifies fifteen evidence items with six highly diagnostic indicators, revealing that falling receivable days to 63 days represent a hard inconsistency for outright failure, while multi-year wind project delays are highly inconsistent with seamless execution assumed by the bull profile. The bull thesis accumulates multiple hard inconsistencies from severe margin compression, execution delays, and consortium withdrawal, while the catastrophic failure scenario is contradicted by successful bond refinancing and operational EBITDA growth.

Highest-plausibility failure modes

Three correlated failure clusters present compounding joint probability risks to equity returns. First, transmission grid bottlenecks and daytime solar curtailment represent the most immediate operational threat, with delays of up to 46 months at Rajasthan substations blocking power evacuation and India's grid curtailing 11% of solar power in the June 2026 quarter. Second, Chinese upstream supply chain pricing volatility represents a structural margin threat, with cell costs rising from 3.5 cents to over 6 cents due to silver inflation and silver cost share in modules rising from 10% to 36% over 2023-25.

Third, elevated global capital costs and leverage compound refinancing risks, with net debt standing at 9.3x EBITDA in F24 and ReNew facing over USD 1.5 billion in cumulative debt maturities over 12 months. The most dangerous unmonitored vector is CERC's tightened Deviation Settlement Mechanism starting April 2026, with deviation penalties not qualifying as change in law and therefore preventing ReNew from passing these costs to state off-takers.

Key false friends masking structural risks

Four false friend indicators create dangerous misinterpretation risks for investors tracking superficial metrics. First, improving receivable days hide mounting grid curtailments that destroy asset-level equity internal rates of return, as late payment rules successfully liquidate old dues but fail to compensate for active grid backing down. Second, early refinancing of the July 2026 HoldCo bond represents a classic false friend, providing temporary liquidity relief while debt remains elevated.

Third, accelerating capacity additions mask unrepresentative runrate EBITDA metrics on newly commissioned assets, as newly added assets lack full-year operating histories and obscure high structural development overheads. Fourth, merchant battery storage profitability appears attractive in early high-margin periods but faces reversion risk as spreads collapse toward low regulated returns. These false friends directly validate the pre-mortem framework by demonstrating how operational progress metrics decouple from actual project-level cash generation.

Net currency exposure verdict

ReNew demonstrates industry-leading hedging discipline that substantially mitigates transaction risks while remaining exposed to translation headwinds. Approximately 90% of USD principal debt is fully hedged, with crosscurrency swaps and coupon hedges protecting 100% of foreign currency interest payments against Rupee fluctuations. Rupee depreciation of 10% in FY26 increased interest costs by only 30 basis points due to hedging, though the absolute transaction loss still reached INR 2,300 million.

However, rupee depreciation from 85.43 to 93.83 created a massive translation drag, with USD-reported revenue growth dragged down to 24% in fiscal 2026 and rupee depreciation eroding over twelve percentage points of reported growth. The critical risk boundary lies at the 92 per dollar hedge call spread breach, where unhedged transaction losses will compound on bond principal once the strike limits are exceeded. Consensus models a short-term Rupee recovery to 93 by late 2026 before depreciation resumes toward 97 in 2027, creating a persistent translation drag on reported dollar EPS of two to three percentage points over the next eighteen months.

Cross-part risk correlations

Systemic risk correlations create compounding failure scenarios that magnify individual operational shocks. Grid curtailment directly exacerbates leverage stress by reducing operating EBITDA available for debt service, with curtailment reducing volume sales and leaving developers to absorb Virtual PPA merchant exposure. Import duties and cell inflation create a capital expenditure mismatch against locked-in tariffs, as reverse auctions lock tariffs meaning raw material inflation cannot be passed to utilities, while vertical integration capex further delays deleveraging goals and strains parent-level equity return profiles.

Currency depreciation beyond hedge boundaries would simultaneously inflate imported equipment costs while triggering unhedged debt service losses, directly validating the return-erosion failure narrative constructed in the pre-mortem analysis. These correlations demonstrate that ReNew's operational performance cannot be assessed through isolated metrics but requires monitoring of shared leading indicators including monthly substation commissioning trends in Rajasthan, global silver and Chinese wafer spot prices, and local currency interest rate movements.

Overall risk verdict and recommendation

ReNew Energy Global presents a medium-probability downside case with the investment warranting a hold-andwatch stance rather than immediate exit or aggressive accumulation. Morgan Stanley's quantified downside scenario projects a sum-of-the-parts share value of USD 1.20, representing an 80% discount below the base price target of USD 6.03, though the probability of reaching this extreme downside is assessed as low due to asset recycling capital cushions.

The investment must sit in hold-and-watch status as the best-and-final proposal of USD 7.02 caps the near-term public market return potential of the stock. The FALSE FRIEND profile dominance indicates that investors tracking capacity additions, receivable improvements, and refinancing successes may systematically underestimate structural return decay from uncompensated curtailment and forecasting penalties. Critical data gaps requiring resolution include the shareholder vote outcome on the revised scheme of arrangement, monitoring of Rajasthan substation delays of up to 46 months, and quantification of central forecasting rule financial impacts on wind-heavy portfolios.

The pre-mortem must be re-run if the special committee rejects the USD 7.02 proposal or if EBITDA losses from uncompensated power backing down exceed Rs 100 crore, triggering immediate thesis reassessment.

Plausible problems to monitor

The comprehensive bear case investigation across sections one through nine reveals five systematic plausible problem to monitor where ReNew Energy Global performs demonstrably worse than peer medians or historical base rates, each representing subtle but compounding operational vulnerabilities that merit close monitoring. The first plausible problem to monitor emerges from receivable cycle deterioration relative to peer efficiency standards. While ReNew successfully improved its Days Sales Outstanding metric to 63 days by late fiscal 2025 from a distressed peak of 152 days in fiscal 2023, this achievement remains structurally inferior to Adani Green Energy's exceptional 2-day receivable cycle as of June 2026.

This 31.5x performance gap suggests that ReNew continues to face working capital constraints that its largest peer has entirely eliminated, indicating either weaker contractual enforcement mechanisms or exposure to lower-quality off-taker counterparties despite late payment surcharge regulatory frameworks. The second plausible problem to monitor manifests in persistent wind resource underperformance against historical norms and auction assumptions.

Multiple quarters of below-average wind speeds have triggered repeated downward EBITDA guidance revisions, with management forced to cut fiscal 2025 targets by Rs 200 crore due to volatile wind generation. This systematic pattern exposes a critical vulnerability in ReNew's portfolio construction, as many wind projects were aggressively bid at tariffs assuming 40% plant load factors but now deliver low singledigit equity internal rates of return in reality.

The frequency and magnitude of these resource disappointments exceed the volatility experienced by pure solar-focused peers, highlighting an asymmetric exposure to weather variability that compounds capital constraints. The third plausible problem to monitor appears in manufacturing scale disadvantage relative to integrated competitors. ReNew's announced 4GW solar cell facility represents only 15.5% of Waaree Energies' 25.8GW capacity and 36% of

Premier Energies' 11.1GW module capacity, creating severe procurement, logistics, and raw material pricing disadvantages. This subscale positioning becomes particularly dangerous as domestic cell capacity additions accelerate toward late fiscal 2027, threatening to compress upstream manufacturing spreads precisely when ReNew needs captive production margins to offset power generation volatility. The company lacks the critical mass to negotiate favorable silver and wafer contracts during inflationary cycles, making it a structural price-taker in input markets where larger peers operate as price-makers.

The fourth plausible problem to monitor emerges from execution velocity gaps in commissioning timelines. Wind projects face multi-year delays from scheduled completion dates due to complex agricultural land acquisition bottlenecks in Karnataka and Maharashtra, contrasting sharply with the relatively streamlined solar project execution in Rajasthan's contiguous desert tracts. These systematic commissioning slippages force management into repeated guidance resets and delay the achievement of contracted power purchase agreement cash flows, creating a temporal mismatch between capital deployment and return realization that exacerbates leverage concerns.

The pattern suggests weaker project management capabilities or more challenging site conditions compared to peers who demonstrate faster time-to-revenue conversion. The fifth and most insidious plausible problem to monitor materializes in valuation discount persistence despite operational improvements. ReNew trades at a 1.2x price-to-book multiple on US public markets, representing a massive discount to the 4x to 5x book multiples observed in private Indian renewable transactions and the steep premium valuations commanded by domestic-listed peers like Adani Green.

This structural listing fatigue persisted even as the company successfully reduced receivables, refinanced holdco bonds, and expanded operational capacity, suggesting that public market investors fundamentally distrust either the reporting currency mismatch, the governance structure, or the long-term return profile. The December 2025 Masdar consortium withdrawal and subsequent best-and-final USD 7.02 takeover proposal validate this skepticism, as sophisticated institutional buyers refuse to pay prices that would reconcile ReNew's public discount to private market asset valuations.

These five plausible problem to monitor collectively form a coherent operational profile of a company struggling with second-tier positioning across multiple dimensions–working capital efficiency, resource predictability, manufacturing scale, execution velocity, and market valuation–relative to both historical base rates and current peer benchmarks. Each signal independently appears manageable, but their simultaneous manifestation creates compounding risks that align directly with the pre-mortem FALSE FRIEND hypothesis where superficial capacity growth masks structural return erosion.
