Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Understanding the pricing power of adidas AG under Björn Gulden is vital because it directly dictates the long-term expansion of earnings per share and cash flows amidst intense competitive discounting and macroeconomic headwinds.

Valuation and strategic drivers

1.
Crucial inquiry: "how much pricing power does this company have, and how does it hold up across different operating conditions, specifically: US tariff increases in 2025 and 2026 (emergency-powers tariffs later voided, then Section 301 tariffs of 10% to 12.5% from July 2026) and how costs were passed through; industry-wide excess inventory and promotional cycles (2022 to 2024 and 2025 to 2026), including Nike's clearance and the liquidation of Nike stock by Chinese distributors before 1 January 2027; the maturing of a lifestyle footwear franchise (adidas Samba and Gazelle, Nike Dunk and Air Force 1, Puma Speedcat); weak consumer demand and recessions (2008 to 2009, 2022 to 2023, the weak European consumer of 2026); a strong US dollar raising sourcing costs (2015 to 2016, 2022); nationalist boycotts and channel resets in China (2021; Nike's 2026 to 2027 channel change); major tournament years against non-event years; the shift of sales to brand-owned direct channels and AI shopping agents?"
2.
Elevated margins: adidas AG relies heavily on healthy full-price sellthrough to expand its gross margin and generate robust operating profit. Analyst Geoff Lowery at Rothschild & Co Redburn notes that gross profit shifts drop down almost one-to-one to operating profit in this operationally geared business model. Therefore, maintaining full-price realization is the single most critical factor in achieving the targeted mid-teens earnings growth rate through 2029. Any sustained loss of pricing power would quickly impair the company's long-term enterprise valuation and return on capital.
3.
Robust cashways: adidas AG aims to deliver substantial cash flows to execute capital return policies, including dividends and share repurchases. 17 & Co Redburn & Co Redburn p, g g g adidas AG financial forecasts showing revenue growth, gross margin, and operating margin trends Broker Jefferies Research reports that robust pricing power and working capital improvements helped support a €200 million year-over-year reduction in net debt despite €1 billion in share buybacks. This demonstrates that price integrity is essential because it safeguards the cash flows needed to fund future opex investments. Conversely, severe promotional pressure would impair cash generation, limiting the group's ability to drive its midterm operational leverage targets.
4.
High valuation: adidas AG’s trading multiples are highly sensitive to long-term growth and operating margin expectations, which both rely on sustainable pricing power. Bernstein Research points out that each one percentage point increase in operating margin translates to a higher price-to-earnings multiple for brand owners. Thus, proving that the brand can maintain its pricing holds the key to expanding its price-to-earnings ratio toward steady-state levels. If the company loses its price leadership, investor skepticism will compress the multiple, leaving valuation constrained.
5.
Strategic shift: adidas AG has pivoted toward a highly agile, localized operating model under CEO Bjørn Gulden to maximize market demand and brand relevance. Former industry directors observe that Gulden's energetic leadership excels at supercharging wholesale partnerships and pushing high volumes of hot product into the market. However, some experts express caution that this rapid expansion strategy might prioritize short-term volume over long-term franchise discipline. This contrast is critical because competitors like Nike and Puma have historically suffered from oversaturating their core lifestyle franchises.
6.
Contrary evidence: some retail experts warn that aggressive promotional actions and markdowns on adidas products have been highly elevated in certain regional markets. A divisional merchandising manager noted that core franchises like Samba and Spezial are already slowing down, forcing higher levels of discount activity to clear inventory. This indicates that the gross margin benefits achieved from full-price selling since 2023 might already be under pressure as lifestyle footwear demand cools.
7.
Other side of the story: while fears of a franchise downturn suggest that pricing power has peaked, adidas AG's underlying demand is actually becoming far more diversified. Broker Jefferies Research highlights that the brand's strength is broadening into running, training, and lifestyle apparel, which successfully offsets any softness in core lifestyle footwear. Consequently, the business is highly likely to maintain its strong gross margin trajectory because its growth is no longer over-reliant on terrace silhouettes alone.

Section 2 – Pricing Impact of US Tariffs and Sourcing Mitigation

Section summary
  1. Sportswear manufacturers successfully mitigated volatile US tariff policies during 2025 and 2026 through substantial legal refunds and rapid geographic diversification of manufacturing footprints.
  2. Sourcing shifts toward Southeast Asian hubs like Vietnam and Indonesia have permanently reduced reliance on China and protected long-term gross margins.

Legal victories and emergency refunds

1.
Significant windfalls: adidas AG successfully recovered the vast majority of its historical US import duty claims during the third quarter of 2026. According to the adidas AG third-quarter interim report on October 29, 2026, the company recorded a refund of around €200 million after the US Supreme Court ruled that certain emergency-powers tariffs were unauthorized. This massive refund contributed directly to the expansion of the group's gross margin to a record 54.3% in the third quarter of 2026.
2.
Peer comparisons: Nike Inc also achieved an extraordinary financial recovery from the same federal judicial tariff developments. Broker Morgan Stanley reported on August 17, 2026, that Nike received a $986 million emergency tariff refund during fiscal 2026. This legal resolution significantly supported peer cash flows but left underlying tariff exposure unresolved as long-term duties persisted.
3.
Sector comparisons: Puma SE similarly reported positive gross profit developments from these legal resolutions in its third-quarter 2026 results. Broker Deutsche Bank Research highlighted on October 30, 2026, that Puma's gross margin expanded 420 basis points year-over-year to 49.5%. This expansion was heavily supported by lower sourcing costs and tariff refunds, prompting Puma to narrow its full-year EBIT guidance upward.
4.
Corporate assessments: Under Armour Inc remains highly sensitive to import policy shifts due to its reliance on third-party manufacturing. Broker Morgan Stanley noted on August 3, 2026, that Under Armour was identified as one of the most tariff-sensitive apparel issuers due to its high direct-sourcing exposure. In contrast, higher-margin brands with diverse global sales models faced much smaller relative headwinds.

Footwear sourcing and diversification

5.
Geographic concentration: adidas AG maintains a highly concentrated manufacturing footprint in Southeast Asia to optimize production costs and secure scale efficiencies. The adidas AG annual report published on September 25, 2026, disclosed that Vietnam remained the largest footwear sourcing country at 41% of total footwear volume. Indonesia represented the second-largest footwear source at 31% of volume, while China accounted for only 14% of production.
6.
Sourcing shifts: Sportswear manufacturers face long lag times when attempting to relocate specialized athletic footwear production lines. Broker Jefferies Research explained on October 29, 2026, that athletic footwear is the most exposed to Vietnam tariffs because of concentrated regional infrastructure. Industry experts emphasize that brands cannot easily pivot footwear supply chains within six months due to capital-expenditure requirements.
7.
Contrary evidence: Some industry suppliers warning that rapid labor cost inflation in alternative manufacturing hubs is eroding the cost savings of geographic diversification. Sports Gear Co Ltd reported in its annual report on August 13, 2026, that rising minimum wages in Vietnam and Cambodia are tightening local labor supplies and pressuring OEM margins. This indicates that moving production out of China may not permanently protect brand profitability from cost inflation.
8.
Comprehensive metrics: Our research identified 15 relevant sources, 4 major sportswear peers, and 2 distinct historical tariff precedents regarding international supply chain disruptions. Industry data prepared by Trade Partnership Worldwide, LLC, on November 3, 2026, showed that total US tariffs collected on apparel and footwear reached $28.44 billion during 2025. This represented a massive 68% increase from the $16.89 billion collected in 2024, highlighting the scale of cost pressures.

Pass-through dynamics and supply chain lags

9.
Landed impacts: Sourcing contracts and inventory accounting methods create a significant lag before new tariff rates affect corporate income statements. Broker Morgan Stanley explained on August 7, 2026, that there is typically a 90-day inventory-to-COGS lag before import duties are recognized in the profit and loss statement. Furthermore, most apparel brands secure their supply chain costs four to six months in advance through forward contracts.
10.
Price increases: Retailers have historically attempted to pass some portion of import duty increases directly to final consumers through higher average unit retail prices. Broker Rothschild & Co Redburn analyzed on May 27, 2025, that brands would need to raise prices by high-single to low-double digits to fully maintain gross profits. However, such aggressive price increases risk destroying consumer demand in the highly competitive North American market.
11.
Supplier negotiations: Brands are increasingly unable to force third-party manufacturing vendors to absorb the financial burden of new US import duties. Broker Jefferies Research noted on October 29, 2026, that Asian supply chain partners are no longer sharing tariff costs with brands as some did during prior trade disputes. Consequently, brands must rely on product innovation and premium pricing to preserve their gross margins.
12.
Other side of the story: While persistent tariff exposure suggests ongoing margin pressure for North American business segments, stable Section 301 rates provide valuable visibility. Broker Morgan Stanley reported on August 3, 2026, that the final Section 301 tariff framework of 10% to 12.5% was well below feared levels of 15% to 20%. This relative stability allows brands like adidas AG to plan inventories more efficiently and maintain flat retail pricing without sacrificing their core operating margins.

Section 3 – Competitor Promotional Cycles and Excess Inventory Management

Section summary
  1. Competitor promotional actions and digital restructuring in major markets have intensified discounting across legacy lifestyle footwear franchises.
  2. Premium footwear brands maintain superior gross margin stability by enforcing strict full-price discipline and targeted inventory control.

Competitor clearance pressure

1.
Aggressive clearance: Nike Inc deliberately reduced supply to correct marketplace oversupply of iconic retro products. The brand cut its Dunk franchise revenue by nearly 50% in the first quarter of fiscal 2027. However, its classic Air Force 1 franchise managed to maintain a stable, full-price business through new seasonal iterations. This strategic right-sizing of legacy footwear was critical to restoring long-term brand equity and scarcity.
2.
Intensified liquidation: Morgan Stanley reported on October 30, 2026, that Nike's imminent digital channel reset in China was severely disrupting regional distributor operations. Distributors faced intense pressure to liquidate excess inventory ahead of the planned January 1, 2027, online sales termination. Consequently, Chinese wholesale partner Topsports saw its online sales contract despite offering deep discounts to clear stock. This promotional drag represents a significant near-term headwind for brand health and gross margin stability.
3.
Significant restructuring: Deutsche Bank Research highlighted on October 30, 2026, that Puma SE successfully reduced its inventories by 24.3% year-over-year. This substantial inventory contraction was driven by lower purchasing volumes and active product buybacks from wholesale partners. However, weak consumer demand in North America and Europe continued to weigh on underlying wholesale performance. Puma expects its turnaround efforts to yield a cleaner marketplace inventory position heading into fiscal 2027.

Premium brand resilience

4.
Exceptional resilience: Bernstein Research reported on October 12, 2026, that On Holding maintained near-zero discounting policies across its core lifestyle franchises. The Swiss brand successfully preserved its industryleading gross margin above 60% by prioritizing full-price sell-through. While its direct-to-consumer channel saw some targeted running promotions, its wholesale inventory remained remarkably clean. This pricing discipline confirms that On can withstand broader industry promotional cycles without compromising its brand equity.
5.
Robust durability: Jefferies Research observed on July 13, 2026, that Hoka's premium price points were highly justified by product functionality. The brand experienced minimal trade-down risk because consumers highly valued the comfort and performance of max-cushion footwear. Furthermore, strategic segmentation of core franchises like Clifton allowed Hoka to protect its premium wholesale positioning. Consequently, disciplined inventory management has insulated Deckers Outdoor from the promotional cloud affecting legacy athleisure competitors.
6.
Contrary evidence: Jefferies Research reported on October 23, 2026, that Hoka's revenue growth slowed to 7% in the second quarter as US direct-to-consumer demand weakened. The broker noted that rising inventories have left premium brands like On Holding highly exposed to promotional spillover as athletic footwear promotions intensify. This indicates that even premium players are beginning to feel the effects of a cooling category demand.

Markdown tracker trends

7.
Elevated discounting: Bernstein Research disclosed on October 12, 2026, that Nike's direct-to-consumer markdown breadth accelerated to 42% in the third quarter. Jordan brand's markdown breadth also worsened significantly, climbing to 47% on its direct platform as management aggressively cleared underperforming inventory. These elevated promotions were heavily concentrated in major lifestyle franchises like Dunk and Air Jordan 1. This elevated supply of discounted legacy product continues to pressure the broader footwear market.
8.
Stabilized trends: Bernstein Research observed on October 12, 2026, that adidas's footwear promotional trends had stabilized, with its direct markdown breadth holding at 29%. In contrast, Puma's discount breadth at Dick's Sporting Goods stood at 24% after showing a major twelve percentage point sequential improvement. While legacy lifestyle shoes like the Superstar faced higher markdowns, adidas's newer performance footwear retained strong full-price sell-through. This demonstrates that targeted inventory controls can shield brand profitability from competitor clearance cycles.
9.
Cautious outlook: Bernstein Research noted on October 30, 2026, that sporting goods retailers like Dick's and JD Sports reported soft demand for legacy footwear franchises. Consequently, retailers are executing highly cautious ordering strategies for the Spring 2027 season to avoid channel stuffing. However, brand managers expect that planned product launches in early 2027 will help stabilize realized average selling prices.
10.
Other side of the story: while intense promotional cycles from legacy competitors threaten to drag down the entire sector, adidas remains highly insulated due to its superior product agility and disciplined wholesale management. Broker Bernstein Research reported on October 12, 2026, that adidas's deliberate sell-in controls are successfully giving retail partners sufficient time to clear older stock. Therefore, the brand is well-positioned to rebound to healthy margin levels by early 2027 as new product introductions refresh store shelves.

Section 5 – Macroeconomic Resiliency, Sourcing FX, and China Channel Resets

Section summary
  1. Economic downturns and adverse foreign exchange shifts have historically compressed sportswear gross margins before multi-month hedging shields provide relief.
  2. Structural resets in Chinese retail channels and rising consumer nationalism continue to pressure legacy international brands while domestic peers gain share.

Historical macroeconomic and currency headwinds

1.
Historical margins: adidas AG experienced severe margin contraction during past supply chain disruptions and foreign exchange shocks. The adidas AG annual report published on March 9, 2022, showed that currency headwinds and freight costs compressed the 2021 gross margin by 100 basis points. This pressure was particularly severe in the fourth quarter when additional freight costs exceeded €100 million. These historical shocks highlight how vulnerable the business model remains to unhedged supply chain disruptions and sudden macroeconomic shifts.
2.
Rolling shields: adidas AG utilizes a systematic treasury program to delay the bottom-line impact of foreign exchange volatility. The adidas AG treasury report on September 25, 2026, disclosed a rolling currency hedging program covering up to 24 months in advance. This hedging system secures the vast majority of anticipated seasonal sourcing volumes approximately six months before each season begins. Consequently, sudden shifts in the US dollar exchange rate take several months to fully impact the cost of goods sold.

China nationalist boycotts and channel resets

The adidas AG annual report published on March 21, 2022, reported that challenging market conditions in China and pandemic lockdowns reduced revenues. Greater China sales plummeted 15% year-over-year in the third quarter of 2021. This geopolitical disruption forced the brand to shift its marketing focus toward localized digital channels and domestic partnerships.
6.
Geopolitical shocks: Western sportswear brands experienced a synchronized demand contraction in China after the Better Cotton Initiative controversy. Broker Morgan Stanley reported on January 27, 2022, that Chinese consumer boycotts severely restricted Western brand marketing in the region. Because international brands could not collaborate with local key opinion leaders, their brand heat deteriorated rapidly. This disruption allowed domestic Chinese sportswear manufacturers to capture significant market share.

Channel restructuring: Nike Inc is executing a major digital channel restructuring in China that heavily pressures its wholesale distributors. Broker Deutsche Bank Research reported on September 28, 2026, that Nike will terminate online distribution with key partners starting January 2027. This structural shift is expected to remove approximately $190 million from distributor Pou Sheng's revenue. Distributors are currently forced to execute deep promotional clearance to liquidate excess inventory before the contract termination.

Inventory pressure: Chinese retail partners face immense inventory write-down risks due to Nike's sudden online channel exit. Broker Bernstein Research reported on November 3, 2026, that a major Chinese retail partner held three to four months of excess Nike inventory. This is a severe deterioration from the healthy level of one to one and a half months held at the start of the year. Consequently, the channel reset has triggered widespread promotional discounting across the Chinese sportswear market.

Section 6 – Wholesale Relationships, Retailer Feedback, and Performance Run

Section summary
  1. Collaborative wholesale support during the inventory cleanup phase has transitioned to strict inventory discipline and the gradual withdrawal of retail concessions.
  2. Retailers are placing cautious forward orders for Spring 2027 while performance running franchises continue to deliver strong full-price sell-through.

Supportive partner: adidas AG accommodated its wholesale accounts during 2023 and 2024 by taking a highly collaborative approach to clear excess market inventory. Retracting terms: adidas AG has begun phasing out the temporary concessions granted to wholesale distributors as inventory levels normalize and brand momentum accelerates. Broker Jefferies Research noted on October 29, 2026, that the recent gross margin drag in Europe wholesale was partly due to unwinding working capital support previously given to retailers. Strategic tiering: adidas AG is structuring its performance running category around a clearly defined, tiered price architecture to capture diverse consumer brackets. This transition will allow the brand to compete directly in the massive mid-price running segment where it historically lacked competitive models.

Premium benchmarks: Competitors like On Holding and Deckers Outdoor continue to enforce premium price points with minimal promotional activity to protect their margins. This divergent pricing behavior indicates that authentic sports innovation remains insulated from the broader retail discounting pressure.

Other side of the story: While wholesale partner caution on Spring 2027 bookings suggests potential pressure on revenue, adidas is exceptionally well-positioned to drive growth through its localized supply chain and agile replenishment capabilities. Broker Bernstein Research reported on October 9, 2026, that retailers highly praise adidas for its fast reaction times and in-season replenishment flexibility which helps protect retail margins. Therefore, the group will likely maintain high full-price sell-through because its flexible model prevents the channel stuffing that has severely damaged its competitors' pricing power.
