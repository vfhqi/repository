Section 2 – Ettiketto Group and Label Market Pass-Through Mechanics

Section summary
  1. Confidence is high that Ettiketto Group can sustain its pricing power because of technical machine integration and a highly fragmented customer base.
  2. Regulatory compliance costs and upstream pass-through lags represent manageable, temporary risks backed by solid historical precedents.

Pass-Through Mechanics and Contract Lags

1.
Delayed cost recovery: Ettiketto implemented price increases in Q2 2026, aiming for a full effect in H2 2026. These pricing actions were necessary to offset high freight and raw material costs. However, contractual pass-through lags of three to six months mean that short-term earnings remain vulnerable to rapid input cost changes. This delay initially dilutes margins before full adjustment occurs.
2.
Multi-year contract constraints: Ettiketto's revenue generation relies partly on framework agreements that dictate pricing terms over extended periods. Individual orders under these agreements represent performance obligations. These structures limit rapid, ad-hoc price adjustments when material costs surge. Consequently, Ettiketto must wait for scheduled indexation windows to recover margins.
3.
Margin recovery cycles: Ettiketto's price increases are designed to exceed cost increases over a complete cycle rather than just recovering them. The platform's organic growth and margins historically surprise on the upside during periods of stabilizing material costs. This pattern suggests that Ettiketto retains pricing gains even when raw material prices fall. Structurally expanding margins over time will support future EPS.

Upstream Shocks and Regulatory Pressures

4.
Upstream strike precedents: The 2022 paper mill strike at UPM-Kymmene highlights the extreme vulnerability of label converters to raw material supply. The strike caused severe material shortages and negatively impacted earnings in early 2022. Although pent-up demand boosted Q4 2022 sales, the event proved that converters bear substantial initial margin hits during upstream labor disputes. Margin normalisation only occurred toward the end of the year.
5.
Middle East shipping disruptions: The logistics shocks in Q2 2026, driven by Middle East shipping disruptions, escalated transit costs for label materials. These regional conflicts generated over USD 600 million in additional cash costs and high fuel surcharges. For Ettiketto, these supply chain delays squeeze gross margins until freight surcharges are passed to customers. This demonstrates the vulnerability of converters to sudden transport spikes.
6.
Contrary evidence: DNB Carnegie's report on 20 October 2026 shows that Ettiketto's net sales grew by 37% year-over-year in Q2 2026 to SEK 436 million despite shipping disruptions. This strong performance suggests that freight shocks had a negligible impact on top-line momentum. Therefore, the threat of logistics disruption may be smaller than feared because demand remains robust.

This regulatory shift forces Ettiketto to invest in recyclable material alternatives, increasing compliance costs. These compliance capabilities are essential for competing in international markets.

Acquisition-driven margin dilution: The integration of European acquisitions with lower historical profitability presents a short-term margin challenge. The recently acquired Interket Group does not currently match Ettiketto's industry-leading margins. This structural difference means that overall segment profitability will depend on achieving synergies and raising acquired prices. These acquisitions establish Ettiketto's presence in the UK and Netherlands.
