Section 5 – Customer Side Analysis

  1. Aalberts benefits from exceptional customer lock-in and pricing power, driving structural margin resilience despite extreme concentration in the Semicon division.
  2. High switching costs across mission-critical applications effectively neutralize traditional customer purchasing leverage, enabling systemic inflation pass-throughs.

1.

2.

3.
Top-5 segment reliance: The top five Semicon customers account for roughly 80% of segment sales, including Zeiss, KLA, Applied Materials, and LAM.
4.
Mitigated group exposure: At the consolidated group level, this ASML exposure dilutes to a more manageable 8% of total revenue.

Fragmented building base: The Building division mitigates overall group concentration by relying on a highly fragmented network of local European wholesalers and installers.
6.
Aerospace customer base: The Industry division serves major global OEMs like Boeing and Airbus through a dispersed network of tier-1 aerospace suppliers.

7.
Exceptional product lock-in: Once engineered into a lithography platform, switching costs virtually eliminate OEM purchasing leverage over Aalberts.
8.
Prohibitive integration risks: A former advanced mechatronics CEO confirms that the technical risks for an OEM to change suppliers are immense.
9.
High qualification barriers: An electrophysics engineer at Boeing notes that Aalberts' surface treatment processes require extensive iterative testing and qualification.
10.
Lifetime single-sourcing: This rigorous procurement process mechanically results in single-sourcing arrangements that last for the entire lifecycle of the product.
11.
Sticky commercial relationships: Customers rarely switch third-party providers post-integration unless severe quality or delivery failures force a disruptive re-qualification.
12.
IP transfer limitations: Contractual obligations and proprietary process knowledge severely limit a customer's ability to transfer production to a cheaper competitor.
13.
Civil aerospace hurdles: Competitor Bodycote highlights that aerospace customers require safety-critical part histories to be maintained for up to 50 years.
14.
Formidable moat: These draconian certification requirements create a near-insurmountable barrier to entry for standard manufacturing competitors.

Pricing power and pass-through execution

15.
Systemic pass-through capability: Management consistently utilizes deep customer lock-in to effectively pass through raw material and wage inflation without volume loss.
16.
Automatic pricing mechanisms: Long-term contracts in the Semicon and Industry divisions feature automatic price-adjustment clauses tied directly to input costs.
17.
Margin protection: These contractual clauses systematically defend the company's profitability against sudden spikes in copper or energy prices.
18.
Dynamic catalog pricing: In the Building segment, Aalberts updates list prices continuously while hedging raw material requirements four to five months forward.
19.
Resilient margin delivery: These pricing execution capabilities enabled the group to maintain a robust 63.8% added-value margin despite severe cyclical volume deleverage.
20.
Firm pricing discipline: Management demonstrates strong commercial discipline, refusing to compromise on price or engage in margin-dilutive competitive bidding.

Customer satisfaction and channel dynamics

21.

Supplier footprint demands: Global OEMs are increasingly pressuring regional suppliers to globalize their manufacturing footprints to support localized supply chains.

M&A strategic response: Aalberts acquired GVT in Southeast Asia specifically to address customer demands for local back-end semiconductor support.
25.
Distributor loyalty deficit: In the Building segment, a former divisional CEO notes that wholesale distributors show minimal brand loyalty.

28.
Other side of the story: Extreme ASML concentration means any structural shift in lithography architectures could instantly obsolete Aalberts' mechatronics lock-in, bypassing traditional switching costs entirely.

Management explicitly targets greater than 4% organic growth and EBITA margins beyond 18% by 2030, both heavily dependent on sustaining this elevated innovation velocity.

The structural commitment to continuous product renewal mechanically differentiates Aalberts from commoditized peers and creates a compounding barrier against vanilla contract manufacturers lacking comparable R&D intensity.
