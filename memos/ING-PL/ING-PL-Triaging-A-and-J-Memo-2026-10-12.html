[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = D

Thesis. ING Bank Slaski is a quality compounder at a fair price with a legislated tax trough in the middle of it: not a recovery, not an inflection, and not a self-help story large enough to matter. At PLN 451.2 the shares already pay for the best-returning franchise in Polish banking and for the tax reversal everyone can see, so what is left is about 9.5 percent a year against the framework's 20 percent bar.

  • The visible change is a tax, and it reverses on a timetable the market already models.
    • The bank corporate income tax rose from 19 percent to 30 percent for 2026, legislated to 26 percent in 2027 and 23 percent from 2028.
    • So reported earnings per share fall about 11 percent in 2026, to about PLN 31.8 from PLN 35.59 reported for 2025, and rebound about 15 percent in 2027 and 12 percent in 2028 on consensus.
    • That rebound is in law, not in hope, which is why we treat it as probable.
    • It is also already inside the 24-month-forward earnings the multiple is struck on, and the sell-side's FY2028 mean rose 18.1 percent over twelve months as it was modelled.
  • Beneath the tax, the forces acting on the bank are of ordinary strength and net to a mild headwind.
    • The National Bank of Poland has cut 200 basis points since May 2025 and holds at 3.75 percent, with about 50 basis points more expected in 2027.
    • The state-controlled banks price corporate credit below the 44 basis point asset tax, and ING refuses to follow.
    • Against that, credit is under-penetrated at 32 percent of GDP, new mortgage agreements rose 50 percent year on year in the first four months of 2026, and the cohort's Swiss-franc and WIBOR worries are clearing.
    • The internal change is continuity: a chief executive from inside the bank since April 2025, a strategy to 2035 that lowers the return target, one small acquisition, and a payout already at the regulator's 75 percent ceiling.
  • 'Financial Outputs' have turned up at the operating line and down at the reported line, and the sell-side is trimming, not raising.
    • Total income rose 11 percent in Q2 2026, pre-provision profit 14 percent and fees 16 percent, while net interest income grew 7 percent year on year through the cutting cycle.
    • H1 2026 net profit still fell 6 percent, because the tax took it.
    • Consensus earnings per share for FY2026 and FY2027 were cut 1.8 percent over three months, the mean rating is Underweight, three of six named brokers say Sell, and the buy share is 14 percent.
  • The change has travelled five of the six layers of the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain and stopped at the sixth.
    • Resources, choices and actions are done: the chief executive, the 2035 strategy, the payout rise, the ING TFI purchase, the restructuring, the subscription accounts.
    • The key operating metrics show it (record retail acquisition, fees up 16 percent, cost-to-income 41.0 percent) and so do the financial statements (total income up 11 percent).
    • The sell-side's estimates do not, because the sell-side believes the net interest margin, now 3.07 percent and down 13 basis points in a quarter, keeps falling.
  • The share price did not wait for the estimates.
    • The shares rose 56.2 percent over twelve months while FY2026 consensus rose 1.7 percent.
    • The rise rode a cohort re-rating, Poland "let out of its box" on Swiss-franc and WIBOR worries, that this bank never had: its active Swiss-franc principal is PLN 180 million at 142 percent coverage.
    • The stock now trades at 11.1x 24-month-forward earnings, the top multiple among the nine Polish banks in the dashboard (the other eight range 7.8x to 10.8x) and the 83rd percentile of its own five-year range
    • Only the ten-year window, which includes a regime of near-zero rates and no bank tax, makes it look cheap at the 31st percentile.
  • What we think it earns.
    • Earnings per share of about PLN 31.8 in 2026, PLN 36.6 in 2027, PLN 39.6 in 2028 and PLN 41.5 in 2029 on our modal path, with pre-tax profit growing 5.6 percent a year to 2029: total income up about 6.5 percent a year on 8 to 9 percent loan growth against a margin held between 2.95 and 3.05 percent, a point or so a year from the cost ratio, and the cost of risk normalising from 33 basis points toward 45 to 55.
    • We sit at consensus in 2026 and 2027 and 3.7 percent below it in 2028, because we normalise provisions faster.
  • What it costs, and what that leaves.
    • At PLN 451.2 the 24-month-forward earnings grow about 4.6 percent a year from today's PLN 40.58 to PLN 46.5 at the three-year horizon, because the tax reversal is already inside the forward figure.
    • Hold the multiple at 11.0x, add dividends of about PLN 81.0 over three years at a 75 percent payout, and the value including cash returns is PLN 592.5, or 9.5 percent a year.
    • The dividend carries about 6 points of that.
    • Over eighteen months the figure is 5.5 percent a year on a strict dividend-date count.
    • The case clears the bar only below about PLN 343, or at 15.0x on PLN 46.5, a multiple above the stock's five-year high of 14.40x.
  • The pattern it fits.
    • 'Compounder', at a C: a correctly modelled high-quality business whose one mis-modelled line, net interest income through the hedge roll and the deposit franchise, is named by the sell-side that doubts it but not sized by any research we hold.

  • Where the chain holds and where it breaks.

    • It holds from the forces to the financial statements: the bank grew net interest income 7 percent through 200 basis points of cuts and holds return on equity at 20 percent.
    • It breaks at the estimates, which are being trimmed, and at the price, which has moved ahead of them.
    • The case is therefore a good business without a case at this price: a bet that nothing changes, which pays 9.5 percent and not 20.

  • Rating = D, because the change is not material enough to clear the bar: the input forces net to a mild headwind ('Change Forces' at C), the outputs improve at the operating line but fall at the reported line while the sell-side cuts ('Financial Outputs' at C), and the modal return of 9.5 percent a year, 10.8 percent on the upper-modal path, falls well short of 20 percent ('Modal return' at D).

    • The parts bind rather than carry, because a good franchise growing 7 percent underlying with a 6 percent yield and no re-rating is exactly the arithmetic of a D.
    • Not C: a C would need either a re-rating we could argue is ours to earn or growth inside the 12 to 20 percent band on an underlying basis, and neither is in the evidence
    • The underlying earnings growth is 4 to 7 percent and the multiple is cohort-driven.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The bull case, stated at its strongest: the market is mis-reading a legislated tax trough as a structural earnings downshift.
      • FY2026 earnings per share of PLN 31.7 is the floor and FY2028 of about PLN 41 is the run-rate
      • A 20 percent return-on-equity digital leader at 11x the 2028 figure is cheap against its ten-year median of 12.1x and 75th percentile of 13.1x
      • And the fee pivot (investment products PLN 83 billion, up 174 percent) plus the hedge roll, which Erste expects to lift net interest income as loss-making swaps expire, make net interest income surprise positively through the 2027 cuts.
      • On that view 13x on about PLN 48 of 2031 earnings gives about 17 percent a year with dividends.
      • The evidence for it is real: pre-tax profit up 15 percent in H1 2026
        • Total income up 11 percent in Q2
        • Retail acquisition at a six-year high
        • A cohort that has just re-rated.
    • What we concede.
      • The tax reversal is contractual.
      • The franchise is the best in Poland by return and cost.
      • The ten-year median multiple is a legitimate anchor a bull may name, and net interest income is the line where consensus is most likely wrong, in the bull's direction.
    • Why we hold our view.
      • The forward multiple already values the 2028 earnings
      • That is what 24-month-forward means at this date.
      • The price rose 56 percent in twelve months while near-year estimates rose 1.7 percent.
      • The sell-side mean is Underweight with the near year being cut.
      • The net interest margin is falling 13 basis points a quarter into a cutting cycle.
      • And the stock sits at the 83rd percentile of its five-year multiple range, the five years being the regime of bank tax, credit holidays and a 30 percent CIT that the case will be lived in.
      • On our earnings the bull's 13.1x gives 15.2 percent a year at three years, a C.
        • On the bull's own earnings (FY2028 PLN 43.5, above the highest named broker at PLN 42.60) and 13.1x it gives about 19.2 percent a year, the top of the C band and still short of the bar
        • On the bull's earnings at our 11.0x it is 13.2 percent, a C.
    • Where the bull would win.
      • If the sell-side's buy share rises through 50 percent on an operating upgrade to net interest income rather than a tax one, the mis-modelled line has been sized by the market and the re-rating branch opens.
      • That is the fulcrum, and it is why the bull case is a C at best and not the modal case.
Change Forces: are the input change forces sufficiently strong? (E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because the external forces net to a mild headwind (rate cuts, the 2026 tax step and state-bank price competition against credit under-penetration, a mortgage upswing and a cohort-wide worry clearing) and the internal forces are real but incremental (a continuity chief executive, a 2035 strategy that lowers the return target, one small acquisition, a payout step already taken).
    • Nothing here is strong enough on its own to drive a 20 percent return.
  • Direction of the forces: the two forces moving the outputs most, the 30 percent bank tax and the policy rate, are headwinds
    • The demand and funding forces are tailwinds
    • The internal change is continuity with better execution.
  • Propagation: the forces have reached the company's actions and its financial statements, and have not reached the sell-side's estimates, which is stated at the head of this Building Block.
External forces: are there sufficiently strong external forces acting on the company, new or intensifying, and in which direction? (RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because the strongest external forces (Polish monetary easing, the 30 percent bank corporate income tax, state-bank price competition in corporate lending) point against the case, while the tailwinds (credit penetration at 32 percent of GDP, a 50 percent rise in new mortgage agreements, the Swiss-franc worry clearing across the cohort) are real but either cyclical or already in the price.
  • Policy rate: a headwind.
    • The National Bank of Poland has cut 200 basis points since May 2025 and holds at 3.75 percent.
    • Every 100 basis point cut compresses the net interest margin by about 17 basis points with a lag, or costs about PLN 130 to 160 million of annual net interest income.
    • Management expects no cuts for the rest of 2026 and about 50 basis points in 2027
    • Morgan Stanley models 25 basis points in April and July 2027 to 3.25 percent.
  • Bank tax: the largest headwind.
    • The corporate income tax on banks rose from 19 percent to 30 percent for 2026, legislated to 26 percent in 2027 and 23 percent from 2028
    • The effective tax rate is guided at 37.3 percent for 2026.
    • The asset tax falls 10 percent in 2027 and 20 percent from 2028.
  • Credit demand: a tailwind.
    • Loans stand at 32 percent of GDP against 80 percent in the euro area
    • Private credit grows 7.6 percent
    • New mortgage agreements rose 50 percent year on year to PLN 47.4 billion in the first four months of 2026
    • EU funds are expected to lift corporate credit 7 to 8 percent.
  • Cohort re-rating: a tailwind at group level that is not this company's.
    • The Banks Sector Digest reads Poland as "let out of its box": Swiss-franc provisions over-full, the CJEU ruling for the banks on WIBOR, the first euro AT1 by a Polish bank oversubscribed, the state-controlled cohort up 67 percent over twelve months.
    • For this bank the worry was never material (active Swiss-franc principal PLN 180 million, coverage 142 percent), so the re-rating is cohort-driven, not company-driven.
  • Not B: the forces with the largest effect on earnings point the wrong way, and the tailwinds have been in the price for a year.

Value Chain and Industry Structure
  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: Credit under-penetration
    • Polish household and corporate credit is small against the economy and against the euro area.
    • It gives the bank a long runway of volume growth without taking share.
    • The mortgage market is in a cyclical upswing and the corporate market is flat.
  • ‘Case/SP Key Risk’: The regulatory and fiscal take
    • The state sets the bank tax rate, the capital buffer, the payout ceiling and consumer-credit rules, and it has reached into bank profits three times in a decade.
    • It decides whether the legislated tax step-down that the forward earnings already contain is delivered, so it bears on the case being right and on the share price.
    • An extension of the elevated tax is possible rather than probable, large if it comes, and the direction is unchanged this quarter.
  • ‘Case/SP Risk’: State-bank corporate pricing
    • The two state-controlled banks price corporate credit for share, below the asset tax.
    • It costs the bank corporate share rather than margin, because the bank refuses to follow.
    • It is a share-price risk through volumes, not a case risk.
Business
  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: The deposit franchise
    • Most retail current accounts are low-cost Direct accounts, and deposits are abundant and granular.
    • It is the source of the bank's funding advantage and of its ability to hold net interest income through rate cuts.
    • Its value falls as rates fall, which is the one thing the business does not control.
  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: The digital cost base
    • Nearly all sales and service run through the app and automated processes.
    • It is why the cost ratio is the lowest among large Polish peers and improves as volumes grow.
    • Wage inflation is the counter-force, and in 2025 it won at the annual level.
  • ‘Case/SP Key Risk’: The core-system migration
    • The bank is replacing its core banking platform with a cloud-native system from a single vendor.
    • It is the highest-plausibility internal failure mode in the risks research and the least monitored.
    • A failure would hit the cost base, the customer franchise and the regulator's view at once, so it threatens the case and the share price; the probability is low, the magnitude large, the trend unknown because it is undisclosed.
