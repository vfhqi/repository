Outliers worth noting

Metso's operating cash flow of EUR 206 million missed consensus by 17% in Q2 2026, despite achieving a 98% rolling 12-month operating cash conversion rate, creating a statistical anomaly in the quarterly versus trailing performance. Peer companies achieving similar trailing conversion rates–such as Weir's targeted 90-100% range –typically exhibit quarterly volatility within 5-8% of consensus expectations, making Metso's 17% miss an outlier deviation.

Management attributed the shortfall to high inventory levels tying up operating cash, yet the persistence of inventory accumulation during a period of strong order intake and stable production suggests potential supply chain bottlenecks, project-specific working capital requirements, or customer prepayment timing issues not fully disclosed in public commentary. [Cross-ref: Section 6] Weir Minerals' margin fell 170 basis points to 20.1% in H1 2026, while Metso's Minerals segment adjusted EBITA margin rose 180 basis points year-over-year to 18.3%, representing a rare divergence in competitive margin trajectories within the same end market.

Historically, Weir and Metso Minerals margins have moved directionally together within 50-80 basis points due to shared customer dynamics and input cost exposure, making this 360 basis point swing a statistical outlier requiring explanation. Potential drivers include Metso's richer aftermarket mix at 68% of segment revenue versus Weir's lower services intensity, differing geographic exposure to highmargin copper regions, or transient factors such as Weir's negative foreign exchange headwinds that may reverse in subsequent periods.
