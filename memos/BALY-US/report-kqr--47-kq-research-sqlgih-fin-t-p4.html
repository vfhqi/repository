Section 2 – Interest Expense, Tranches, and Debt Repricing Risks

Section summary
  1. Bally's proactive debt refinancing has extended maturities through 2031, but the regional casino operator faces escalating repricing risks and high variable-rate exposure.
  2. The termination of capitalized interest and high-cost project debt on developments in the Bronx create severe headwind pressures on future earnings per share.
  3. Bally's escalating rent schedule under its GLPI master leases represents a substantial and expanding fixed overhead burden.
  4. Divergent accounting methods across regional gaming peers heavily distort reported leverage and expense visibility for public investors.

Refinanced Corporate Term Debt

1.
Substantial repricing: Bally's entered into a new $1.1 billion term loan facility due 2031 on February 11, 2026, to overhaul its balance sheet. This transaction allowed the company to pay off its previous $1.47 billion term loan facility due 2028 in full. This proactive debt management helps push out near-term maturity pressures but leaves the operator exposed to variable interest rates on the remaining balance.
2.
Significant adjustment: Bally's continues to carry massive fixed-rate unsecured liabilities, comprising $750 million of 5.625% notes due 2029 and $750 million of 5.875% notes due 2031. As of June 30, 2026, these notes had carrying values of $599.01 million and $530.77 million, respectively, due to merger-related fair value adjustments. These non-cash accounting adjustments will amortize through interest expense, net, using the effective interest method. This dynamic will weigh on reported earnings per share over the next several years.
3.
Favorable retirement: Bally's successfully eliminated its most expensive tranche of debt prior to the 2026 refinancings. The company paid down the entire $500 million outstanding balance of its 11% first-lien notes due October 2028 in October 2025. This redemption removes a severe annual cash interest burden of $55 million, supporting pro forma cash flows.
4.
Contrary evidence: Bally's has significantly improved its debt profile by extinguishing high-cost debt and extending maturities. The combination of the Intralot transaction, a portion of the new term loan, and the Lincoln saleleaseback enabled the operator to fully pay down its outstanding $1.47 billion term loan. This comprehensive restructuring provides the company with much-needed near-term financial flexibility as it funds its largescale development projects.
