Section 2 – Asker's Covenant Definitions: Net Debt and EBITDA

Section summary
  1. The refinanced senior credit facility bank covenant leverage ratio of 3.50x excludes long-term put and call liabilities and IFRS 16 lease liabilities while pro-forming acquired EBITDA and adding short-term earn-outs.
  2. Excluding these long-term liabilities keeps Asker's bank leverage ratio at a comfortable 2.43x, providing significant headroom compared to the headline bank limit of 3.50x.

Covenant definitions of Net Debt

1.
Bank debt definition: Under the Senior Facilities Agreement dated 17 March 2025, Asker Healthcare's banks define net debt for covenant purposes by excluding long-term put and call options and IFRS 16 lease liabilities while including M&A payments due within 12 months. During the 1Q 2026 earnings call, management confirmed that this definition is used for the bank leverage covenant and differs from reported figures. Consequently, this excluding treatment of leases lowers calculated leverage and protects the company's room for future acquisitions without violating bank covenants.
2.
Lease liability treatment: The exclusion of lease liabilities from the covenant net debt represents a departure from standard IFRS 16 accounting where all leases are capitalised. Carnegie's initiation report dated 05 May 2025 notes that Asker's reported net debt of SEK 3,091m in 2024 excludes SEK 956m of lease liabilities. This treatment keeps bank leverage lower because including leases would increase debt by almost one billion Swedish kronor, thereby threatening earnings per share if interest margins step up.
3.
Earn-out inclusion rules: The bank covenant definition requires Asker Healthcare to include contingent considerations and earn-out payments due in the next 12 months in its net debt. In their Q2 2026 presentation, Asker reported that outstanding M&A payments due in 12 months are added to debt, while long-term liabilities remain excluded. This inclusion creates a realistic view of short-term cash drains, which helps investors anticipate cash conversion impacts on earnings per share.
4.
Put and call option exclusion: Long-term liabilities related to compound put and call options for acquiring minority interests are completely excluded from the bank's covenant net debt definition. According to Asker's 2025 annual report, these put and call options stood at SEK 127m while total contingent considerations were SEK 870m. This exclusion keeps leverage low because including these long-term commitments would artificially inflate the leverage ratio and restrict the pace of acquisitions.
5.
Balance sheet indebtedness details: The prospectus dated 17 March 2025 outlines that the new senior credit facility comprises SEK 1,025m, EUR 175m and CHF 25m term loans alongside a SEK 1,600m revolving credit facility. Asker's 2024 annual report shows that this refinancing successfully replaced more expensive shareholder loans and consolidated the group's debt. Consolidating these loans under a single covenant framework improves interest expense predictability, which directly benefits net income and supports earnings per share growth.

This treatment supports earnings per share growth because it instantly lowers covenant leverage, ensuring that the company maintains its target high pace of M&A.

Asker's Q2 2026 report lists rolling 12-month EBITDA adjusted for leases and items affecting comparability at SEK 1,900m. This adjustment ensures alignment between debt and earnings definitions, which means that any changes in leasing costs do not unintentionally trigger covenant breaches.

Items affecting comparability: The covenant EBITDA definition allows for the add-back of non-recurring items and acquisition-related expenses to prevent temporary earnings distortions. Allowing these add-backs protects the leverage ratio from spikes during active acquisition quarters, thereby stabilizing borrowing costs and enhancing earnings per share stability.
