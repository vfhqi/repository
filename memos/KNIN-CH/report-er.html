Section 1 – Brief company description and history

Section summary
  1. Prior to earnings, Kuehne + Nagel maintained strong commercial momentum but faced high sell-side valuation skepticism due to mid-term normalization risks.
1.
Resilient asset-light business model: Kuehne + Nagel operates as a prominent global logistics group headquartered in Schindellegi, Switzerland, delivering services across sea, air, road, and contract logistics. The company focuses on asset-light freight forwarding, serving as a key strategic partner for global trade without owning the underlying heavy transportation assets like container ships or cargo aircraft.
2.
Sustained global market leadership: Listed on the Zurich stock exchange, the company is included in the Swiss Market Index and maintains a global number one market position in both Air and Sea Logistics. Despite intense competition, it remains the world's largest freight forwarder by total ocean and air volumes, supporting approximately 400,000 customers worldwide.
3.
Concentrated shareholder structure, stable: The ownership of the logistics group remains highly concentrated, with Kuehne Holding and associated vehicles controlled by Klaus-Michael Kühne representing a majority stake of 53% to 56% of the registered shares. This stable majority ownership contrasts with peer DSV, which operates with a highly fragmented free float, and DHL, which maintains a more diversified institutional investor base.
4.
Post-pandemic normalization trajectory, slowing: Following historical freight peaks, the group's net turnover normalized to CHF 24,476 million in 2025, generating a recurring EBIT of CHF 1,380 million and a conversion rate of 14.1%. This represents a significant contraction from peak 2022 profitability, when conversion margins reached 34% before resetting to structural pre-pandemic averages.
5.
Acquisitive peer expansion model, contrasting: Standalone rival DSV achieved total revenue of DKK 247,331 million and an operating profit of DKK 19,611 million in 2025, driven by the consolidation of Schenker. This consolidation inflated DSV's asset base compared to Kuehne + Nagel's organic model, as DSV relies heavily on large acquisitions to drive scale and structural synergies.
6.
Asset-heavy competitor structure, comparison: Peer DHL Group operated a diversified conglomerate model, delivering total revenue of EUR 82,855 million and an EBIT of EUR 6,103 million in 2025. This conglomerate structure requires a far greater ratio of capital employed compared to Kuehne + Nagel's pure-play asset-light forwarding structure, which remains focused on flexible, organic logistics delivery.
7.
Strong absolute share performance, rising: Over the 12 months preceding the October 2026 print, the stock delivered an absolute performance of plus 44.3% in Swiss francs. This outpaced the broader EDME index return of 10.9% over the same period, indicating robust market recovery and positive reassessment of the company's cost cutting strategy.

Section 7 – Changes in leadership

Section summary
  1. The passing of majority shareholder Klaus-Michael Kühne transfers ownership to his charitable foundation, securing long-term governance stability despite operational management de-layering.
1.
Transition of majority shareholding: Following the passing of major shareholder Klaus-Michael Kühne on August 23, 2026, the company's ownership structure faces a long-term transition. According to German regulatory filings, FROMER Services GmbH was appointed executor of his estate on August 31, 2026, to distribute his holdings according to his instructions. His estimated $49 billion estate, including a 53% to 55.75% stake in the logistics group, is preparing for a transfer to the Kühne Foundation. This transition removes the threat of hostile takeover or block liquidation, securing long-term capital stability.

Management Board succession execution: The logistics group successfully completed major leadership successions within its executive Management Board over the last 12 months. Marcus Claesson was appointed Chief Information Officer on September 1, 2025, to succeed long-serving CIO Martin Kolbe. Additionally, Eduardo Razuck succeeded Gianfranco Sgro as Executive Vice President of Contract Logistics in October 2025. To complete this transition cycle, Søren Schmidt took responsibility for global Road Logistics on May 1, 2026, succeeding Dr. Hansjoerg Rodi.

Board of Directors and IR stability: The company maintained continuity at its highest governing level as Dr. Joerg Wolle was confirmed as Chairman of the Board of Directors during the Annual General Meeting on May 6, 2026. Meanwhile, the primary corporate contacts for the market remained highly stable, led by Chief Financial Officer Markus Blanka-Graff. Investor relations activities continue to be managed by Christopher Combé alongside Andrea Kobi. This executive stability helps reassure the market during times of structural corporate restructuring.

Section 8 – Change in strategy and structure

Section summary
  1. The strategic priority of organic-led, high-value verticalization is supported by tactical bolt-on acquisitions rather than large scale mergers.
  2. Structural efficiency is maintained through decentralized country P&L accountability combined with complete control over proprietary IT platforms.
1.
Streamlined organizational structure: The company's structural framework has transitioned to a progressively de-layered management setup where country organizations report directly to the Group Management Board. Under this model, country managers are empowered with highly decentralized local P&L accountability to maintain customer proximity. By contrast, this streamlined design helps eliminate administrative overhead and speeds up local decision-making.
2.
Unified proprietary IT platform: A crucial competitive differentiator is the group's reliance on a single, unified IT and data platform that supports all global operations. The group maintains strict control over its core systems through its proprietary transport management platforms, SALOG, AirLOG, and SeaLOG. Unlike peers who outsource software, this internal IT sovereignty enables Kuehne + Nagel to deploy new capabilities across the global network rapidly and independently.
3.
Contrasting organizational philosophies: While both industry leaders employ decentralized country-level execution, their corporate architectures differ. Standalone rival DSV utilizes a single line of P&L control where country managers carry total responsibility for synergy realization and integration targets. Conversely, Kuehne + Nagel operates a more complex matrix organization that balances country structures with mode-specific logistics segments.
