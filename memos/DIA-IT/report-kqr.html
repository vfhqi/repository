Section 2 – DiaSorin Historical Valuation Phases and Financial Metrics (2011 to Present)

Section summary
  1. DiaSorin experienced a severe de-rating between 2011 and 2013 as aggressive competition in the Vitamin D market compressed its forward price-to-earnings multiple to 12 times.
  2. A structural menu expansion toward high-margin specialty assays run on the LIAISON XL platform drove a successful valuation rerating back to 24 times earnings from 2014 to 2019.
  3. The COVID-19 pandemic windfall pushed multiples to an extraordinary peak of 30 times forward earnings before the cash-financed acquisition of Luminex diluted the group operating margins.
  4. A post-pandemic de-rating culminated in the November 2025 guidance cut before cost-saving programs and molecular platform roll-outs began driving a gradual operational recovery.

Severe De-Rating and Pricing Erosion

1.
Severe price pressure: DiaSorin experienced a major de-rating between 2011 and 2013 when aggressive competitor entry eroded its highly profitable Vitamin D franchise. Morgan Stanley reported in 2012 that Vitamin D accounted for approximately 35% of group sales and nearly 50% of operating profits. Competitors like Siemens offered drastic price discounts of up to half of legacy rates to win over large clinical laboratories. This competitive pressure compressed the company's valuation to a forward price-to-earnings multiple of 12 times by late 2012.
2.
Rapid contractual renegotiation: Management proactively reduced its pricing expectations to preserve its core customer footprint in North America. During the 2011 earnings call, DiaSorin downgraded its Vitamin D price projections to a 15% to 20% immediate decline for 2012. By late 2012, the company successfully extended 84% of its customer contracts for 24 to 36 months. These aggressive price concessions temporarily depressed immediate earnings per share growth but successfully protected market share.
3.
Resilient operational profitability: Despite severe price cuts in its largest franchise, the company maintained exceptionally high operating margins. DiaSorin reported an EBITDA margin of 41.7% in the third quarter of 2012 despite a 20% year-on-year drop in Vitamin D pricing. Management mitigated the pricing headwind through manufacturing efficiencies and a fast-growing laboratory instrument footprint. This operational resilience proved that the legacy portfolio could sustain solid group cash generation under stress.
4.
Subdued top-line growth: Overall revenue growth slowed to low-single-digits during this transition phase as non-Vitamin D revenues struggled to offset the headwind. The company reported a modest revenue increase of 1.6% at constant exchange rates for the first nine months of 2013. Reagent sales of specialty clinical chemistry tests grew 21% but could not fully offset the legacy price declines. This top-line slowdown limited the immediate multiple expansion of forward earnings through 2013.

Menu Expansion and Multiple Re-Rating

5.
Accelerating organic sales: DiaSorin entered a prolonged re-rating phase between 2014 and 2019 as Vitamin D pricing stabilized and new specialty assays drove growth. Morgan Stanley highlighted in 2014 that organic sales growth was set to accelerate to 5.1% in 2015 as legacy headwinds rolled off. The company successfully shifted investor focus to specialty immunoassay panels run on the LIAISON XL system. This menu diversification successfully drove a robust multi-year operational recovery for the business.
6.
Substantial valuation expansion: The successful transition to high-margin specialty assays triggered a massive expansion in DiaSorin's forward trading multiples. Morgan Stanley reported that the forward price-to-earnings multiple expanded to 24 times by late 2015. Investors rewarded the company's best-in-class return on invested capital which reached 28% in 2016. This premium valuation reflected rising confidence in specialty immunoassay market share gains across Europe.
7.
Elevated premium valuation: Investor enthusiasm for the company's automated specialty test pipeline sustained high valuation multiples through the late 2010s. Jefferies observed that the stock traded at a premium of 25 times forward earnings in 2017. Top-line growth was supported by the launch of the automated latent tuberculosis QuantiFERON-TB workflow. These premium multiples remained highly sensitive to any disruptive competitive technology entries in core markets.
8.
Strong cash conversion: High operational efficiency and margin expansion accompanied the mid-single-digit revenue growth of the late 2010s. DiaSorin reported an EBITDA of 255.4 million euros in 2018, equal to a 38.2% EBITDA margin. Gross profit margins improved to 69.1% by late 2019 due to a highly favorable product mix. This robust cash generation allowed the company to sustain high return on capital without debt.

Extraordinary Windfall and Capital Deployment

9.
Extreme valuation peak: The outbreak of the COVID-19 pandemic triggered unprecedented demand for molecular diagnostic tests and pushed DiaSorin's valuation to historic highs. Jefferies noted that the company's shares traded at an extraordinary forward multiple of 30 times earnings in early 2020. Single-target PCR testing for COVID-19 generated massive incremental revenues of up to 10 million euros per month. This dramatic windfall significantly boosted near-term earnings per share growth to record levels.
10.
Diluted operating profitability: While COVID-19 testing drove peak revenues, the large-scale acquisition of Luminex in 2021 structurally altered the company's margin profile. DiaSorin acquired the US-based molecular diagnostics specialist Luminex for approximately 1.8 billion dollars in an all-cash transaction.
11.
Morgan Stanley projected before the deal that the acquisition would dilute the company's exceptional return on capital. Integrating the lower-margin multiplexing assets initially compressed historical group operating margins post-closing.
12.
Peak financial results: The combined group reported record absolute profitability during the final phase of the pandemic windfall in late 2021. DiaSorin reported an adjusted EBITDA of 244.2 million euros for the first half of 2021, representing an adjusted EBITDA margin of 47.4%. This financial performance was heavily supported by high-margin antigen and molecular test volumes. However, these supernormal margins were unsustainable and destined to normalize lower post-pandemic.
13.
Elevated leverage overhang: The cash-financed acquisition of Luminex required significant external debt that temporarily strained the company's balance sheet. DiaSorin secured a senior bank facility of 1.0 billion dollars to fund the transaction. Management projected that combined leverage would peak at 2.5 times net debt to EBITDA post-closing. Deleveraging was highly dependent on rapid post-merger cost synergy extraction over three years.

Post-Pandemic Correction and Re-Basement

14.
Substantial valuation de-rating: The rapid wind-down of COVID-19 testing revenues and integration challenges at Luminex led to a severe post-pandemic de-rating between 2022 and 2025. Morgan Stanley noted that the company's shares underperformed the European market by over 50% during 2023. Reagent demand fell sharply as pandemic testing wound down, prompting over 30% downward revisions to consensus earnings estimates. The forward price-to-earnings multiple contracted to 16 times, down from pre-pandemic averages of 27 times.
15.
Contrary evidence: Despite expectations that the Luminex acquisition would immediately establish a high-growth molecular diagnostics franchise, commercial execution fell short of targets. An expert interview with a former Luminex vice president revealed that the post-merger integration severely disrupted product development and organizational culture. Slower-than-expected clinical placements of the newly launched LiaisonPlex multiplex system fell well behind peer platforms. This commercial lag significantly delayed the anticipated molecular diagnostics segment revenue contribution through 2025.
16.
Guidance reset shock: The post-pandemic de-rating culminated in a major profit warning on 5 November 2025 due to mounting operational headwinds. Deutsche Bank reported that DiaSorin cut its 2025 revenue growth guidance to 5% and lowered its adjusted EBITDA margin target to 33%. The guidance cut was driven by volume-based procurement pressures in China and weaker-than-expected molecular reagent sales. This rebasement of expectations triggered a sharp multiple contraction in late 2025.
17.
Gradual margin recovery: Following the guidance cut of late 2025, the company embarked on a structural recovery program focused on cost-saving initiatives and molecular platform roll-outs. Morgan Stanley reported that the planned closure of the German manufacturing site would generate 7 million euros in annual savings. The company's core immunodiagnostics franchise maintained strong pricing power to support a target EBITDA margin of 35% by 2026. This operational turnaround is expected to drive steady earnings per share expansion over the mid-term.
18.
Other side of the story: While bulls expect the core immunodiagnostics business and new molecular launches to support a valuation recovery, persistent headwinds from volume-based procurement in China could permanently depress margins. Slower clinical placements of the newly launched LiaisonPlex platform could prevent EBITDA margins from returning to historical prepandemic peaks of 38%. Slower platform adoption would restrict long-term molecular segment growth and keep valuation multiples depressed.
