Outliers worth noting

The bear-case evidence assembled across Part 1 reveals three extreme statistical outliers that distinguish thyssenkrupp from regional industrial peers and warrant intensive investor scrutiny. These anomalies represent structural deviations that cannot be explained by normal cyclical patterns or temporary operational setbacks, and each carries material implications for the investment thesis. The structural implication is that no rational third-party buyer would acquire Steel Europe without massive liability transfers or unprecedented government intervention, fundamentally undermining the portfolio simplification narrative that underpins the bull investment case. The second critical outlier emerges in thyssenkrupp's decarbonization capital commitments, where Morgan Stanley calculates that future net-zero commitments create a €2.2 billion NPV drag on the steel division's equity value, with total gross decarbonisation spending through 2045 expected to reach €6.9 billion even after accounting for early state subsidies.

The magnitude of this unfunded liability gap cannot be explained by normal project execution risk or government subsidy timing uncertainty. The third statistical outlier manifests in thyssenkrupp's operating cash flow dynamics, where operating cash flows plummeted by €1,328 million due to the absence of submarine prepayments in mid-2026.

This represents an extreme cash flow volatility event that reveals dangerous structural dependencies on working capital timing rather than underlying operational strength. Instead, it exposes how thyssenkrupp's historical cash flow metrics were artificially inflated by subsidiary project financing structures that are now being stripped away through portfolio separations. This outlier directly invalidates backward-looking cash flow analyses and suggests that the parent holding company faces immediate liquidity stress unless asset monetizations proceed on accelerated timelines.
