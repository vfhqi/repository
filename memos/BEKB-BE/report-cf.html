EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

This comprehensive financial analysis of Bekaert NV reveals a company navigating substantial strategic transformation amid challenging macro conditions, executing an aggressive footprint rationalization program while pivoting its portfolio mix toward high-margin specialty businesses and away from volatile commodity wire segments. The company demonstrates resilient underlying operational execution through €40 million in overhead savings and strong free cash flow generation of €314 million, yet faces material headwinds from delayed green hydrogen adoption, prolonged construction market weakness, and escalating US steel tariffs that threaten margin sustainability.

Management has proven to be an honest tracker of guidance, delivering precisely on revised FY25 targets of 8.0% EBITu margin after proactively adjusting expectations mid-year, though the credibility of maintaining stable margins in FY26 remains structurally constrained by external policy uncertainties and persistent volume softness across mature European and North American end-markets.

Strategic Transformation and Leadership Change

The unexpected CEO transition in June 2026, with Olivier Biebuyck replacing Yves Kerstens ahead of his 2028 mandate, represents a major structural shift in leadership agility at a critical juncture as the company executes concurrent portfolio exits, footprint consolidations, and specialty business integrations. The absence of active activist presence provides management with operational flexibility, though the heavy C-suite turnover across strategy, digital, and HR functions during 2025-2026 introduces execution risks during a period requiring seamless integration of the €40 million BEXCO synthetic ropes acquisition and the €60 million Bridgestone tire cord plant deal. The Latin American commodity wire divestments slashed regional sales exposure from 18% to approximately 4%, eliminating €57 million in cumulative translation losses while generating €20 million in disposal gains, though the €162 million in one-off restructuring charges during 2025 signals the painful cost of repositioning the industrial footprint through 13 plant closures since 2019. The temporary halt of green hydrogen production at Wetteren, Belgium, triggering a €55 million impairment, underscores the material gap between ambitious clean-tech targets and real-world regulatory delays in European and US hydrogen subsidy implementation, forcing a postponement of the >10% mid-term EBITu margin ambition.

Forward EPS Drivers and Earnings Momentum

Five key forward drivers anchor Bekaert's earnings trajectory, ranked by impact magnitude and plausibility: (1) the Bridgestone tire cord capacity acquisition adding approximately €80 million in annual sales with high plant utilization in Shenyang, China and Rayong, Thailand, secured by a long-term supply agreement; (2) ongoing costsaving initiatives delivering €40 million in overhead reductions through aggressive site rationalization and operational efficiency gains; (3) Sustainable Construction product adoption capturing double-digit volume growth in Middle East and India tunneling projects, including landmark contracts like the Dubai Metro Blue Line; (4) synthetic ropes integration in offshore energy markets, with BEXCO and Flintstone securing major deepwater mooring contracts and supporting a segment EBITu margin of 8.7%; and (5) the delayed green hydrogen PTL and low-iridium MEA scale adoption, which represents both a near-term drag through €55 million in impairments and a long-term opportunity via Toshiba's licensed technology enabling a 90% reduction in expensive iridium usage.

The compound probability of delivering both cost-savings and Bridgestone synergies is estimated at 80%, though the self-help recovery archetype faces medium investment confidence as macro headwinds could limit volume recovery if prolonged Chinese steel cord pricing pressure and delayed European grid projects persist.

Recent Earnings Delivery and Guidance Credibility

Bekaert's Q1 2026 consolidated sales of €917 million met compiled consensus with a 0.22% beat, delivering flat like-for-like sales at 0.0% supported by robust organic volume growth of 3.0% offset by negative price-mix effects of -3.0% and severe currency and disposal drags of -7.0%. Core division performance diverged sharply, with Rubber Reinforcement achieving +2% like-for-like growth driven by a +5% volume surge in Asia, Steel Wire Solutions expanding +6% on strong North American utility wire demand, while Bridon-Bekaert Ropes Group collapsed -11% due to delayed deepwater mooring awards and lower heavy lifting sling demand.

The FY25 execution delivered exactly on revised guidance, achieving sales of €3.71 billion and an 8.0% EBITu margin after management proactively trimmed expectations mid-year from an initial 8.0%-8.5% range, demonstrating an honest tracker posture that adjusts and delivers on trimmed guidance ranges. However, the historical accuracy table reveals a pattern of initial over-promising, with FY24 EBITu missing the original €391 million target by €43 million or -11% due to weaker-than-expected volumes.

The reiterated FY26 guidance of stable like-for-like revenues and margins translates to approximately €3.70 billion in sales and 8.0% EBITu margin, though sensitivity analysis indicates a 50bps margin compression would reduce EPS by approximately 5.8% through fixed cost under-absorption, while inability to pass through the 50% US steel tariff represents the single highest sensitivity risk to forward estimates.

Sell-Side Positioning and Peer Momentum

The compiled equity coverage pool exhibits a predominantly bullish rating distribution with 59% Buy and 41% Hold ratings and zero Sell recommendations, though narrative consensus highlights persistent geopolitical and tariff headwinds that cap immediate positive revisions, with Bekaert's cautious FY26 outlook expected to trigger an 8% cut to compiled consensus EBITu. Lead analyst Stijn Demeester at ING maintains a Hold rating with a €41 target price after rolling forward to FY27 multiples, citing low demand visibility in core Western markets and delayed hydrogen capacity ramp-ups, while trimming the group's FY26 adjusted EPS estimate by -14% to €4 following the Q1 trading update.

In the four-dimensional peer momentum ranking, Bekaert occupies a resilient mid-tier position (3rd of 5 peers), lagging behind Rajratan Global Wire's 24% revenue growth and Tokyo Rope's positive momentum reversal but outperforming Xingda International's -3.9% sales decline amid intense Chinese tire cord overcapacity. Bekaert's operating working capital optimization to 15.0% of sales represents a primary competitive advantage, significantly outperforming peer capital intensity and driving 63% free cash flow expansion to €314 million, though the price-volume competitive dynamic shows aggressive Asian peer strategies escalating pricing pressure in standard tire cord segments, forcing Bekaert to defend its position through long-term Bridgestone supply agreements and a shift toward high-end specifications.

Material Risks and plausible problem to monitor

Seven critical plausible problem to monitor warrant heightened monitoring: (1) the recurring pattern of massive one-off charges totaling €162 million in 2025 versus €52 million in 2024 indicates structural overcapacity, suggesting that headline "underlying" profitability numbers may be artificially inflated and unrepresentative of true throughcycle economic returns; (2) the extreme footprint restructuring closing 13 plants worldwide since 2019 could under-deliver if captive plant integrations suffer severe operational delays or if volume recoveries fail to absorb fixed costs; (3) the combination of prolonged US tariff escalations from 25% to 50% and delayed European hydrogen subsidies could permanently derail hydrogen and rope margins by trapping Bekaert with massive under-absorbed plant overheads; (4) the collapse in Bridon-Bekaert Ropes Group like-for-like sales by -11% masks structural deterioration in high-margin specialties, with delayed European grid projects threatening to extend under-absorbed plant overhead compression; (5) extreme regional volume declines in European construction and chronic Chinese tire cord overcapacity could compress margins below the 8.0% threshold, erasing structural overhead savings during downcycles; (6) the severe solar substitution driving a collapse in steel ultra-fine wire sales following rapid transition to non-steel alternatives highlights technology disruption risks across legacy product portfolios; and (7) extreme consensus optimism regarding margin recovery could collapse if persistent Chinese tire cord overcapacity triggers global price wars, neutralizing Bekaert's local manufacturing protection and 70% US production base that shields against pure import competitors facing full tariff exposure.

Outliers worth noting

Extreme footprint rationalization program since 2019 resulted in 13 production site closures globally, comprising five sites in Europe and four in Asia, representing an unprecedented scale of industrial consolidation that generated €162 million in one-off restructuring charges during 2025 alone, with only €8 million immediate cash impact, suggesting a massive non-cash asset impairment wave driven by structural overcapacity rather than temporary cyclical weakness across mature geographic markets.

Groundbreaking iridium reduction technology licensed from Toshiba achieved a 90% reduction in expensive iridium usage for membrane electrode assemblies in green hydrogen applications, representing a step-change innovation that could fundamentally alter the economics of electrolyzer production, yet the €55 million impairment of Wetteren hydrogen assets and temporary production halt indicate that commercial adoption lags technological capability by multiple years, creating a mismatch between deployed capital and market readiness.

Severe regional margin compression in Sustainable Construction manifested as a -14% sales decline in Europe and North America during H1 2025, driven by intense flooring price competition and elevated import pressure, while simultaneously the division captured double-digit volume growth in India and Middle East markets, revealing extreme geographic performance bifurcation that cannot be explained by product mix alone and suggests structural competitive displacement in mature Western markets.
