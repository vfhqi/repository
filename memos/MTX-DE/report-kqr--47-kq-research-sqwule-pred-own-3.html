Section 3 – Accounting and Diluted Adjusted EPS Share Count Mechanics

Section summary
  1. Standard accounting rules under IAS 33 dictate that potentially dilutive convertible bonds must be excluded from the share count when a net loss renders them anti-dilutive.
  2. MTU Aero Engines successfully applied the if-converted method to include its historical 2027 bond in diluted share counts only during profitable periods.
  3. Peer comparisons show Safran utilized active share buybacks to eliminate dilution while Delivery Hero completely excluded its bonds due to persistent losses.

Accounting Standards and Diluted Mechanics

1.
Standardized guidelines: Universal accounting regulations under IAS 33 govern how companies compute basic and diluted earnings per share. Diluted earnings per share calculations incorporate the potential ordinary shares that would be created if outstanding convertible bonds were converted into common stock. This methodology ensures that investors receive accurate warnings about potential equity dilution before actual share issuance occurs.
2.
Adjusted numerators: Incorporating convertible bonds into diluted calculations requires adjusting both the numerator and the denominator under the if-converted method. Under this standard, net income is adjusted upward by adding back the interest expense associated with the convertible bonds net of tax. This adjustment represents the interest savings the company would achieve if the bondholders converted their debt into equity.
3.
Dilution thresholds: Potential ordinary shares are only included in the diluted denominator when their conversion would decrease earnings per share. If conversion would actually increase earnings per share or decrease loss per share, the instruments are classified as anti-dilutive. In these specific situations, the potential shares are completely excluded from the denominator used for diluted calculations.
4.
Contrary evidence: The dilutive effect in MTU's first-half 2026 financial report arose from the newly issued 2033 convertible bond rather than the original 2027 bond. The 2026 interim report confirms that 1,055,054 potential shares from the January 2026 bond created the reported dilutive effects during that six-month period. This means the original 2027 bond had ceased to impact the denominator because of its early cash redemption.

MTU Specific Share Count Tracking

5.
Profitable calculations: MTU Aero Engines registered a solid net income rebound in the 2024 financial year. This profitable performance meant that the potential shares from the 2019 convertible bond were dilutive and therefore included in the diluted denominator calculation. Consequently, the company reported a basic EPS of €11.77 alongside a diluted EPS of €11.53 for the full year.
6.
Unusual exceptions: MTU Aero Engines experienced a major net income contraction in 2023 due to massive GTF engine fleet management charges. Because the resulting consolidated net income was negative, the 2019 convertible bond shares became entirely anti-dilutive under IAS 33 rules. As a result, the reported basic and diluted earnings per share for the 2023 period were identical at €-1.90.
7.
Ongoing dynamics: MTU Aero Engines tracked a diluted share count adjustment in its half-year report ending June 30, 2026. Basic earnings per share for the first half of 2026 reached €7.99 based on a basic share count of 53,856,502 shares. However, diluted earnings per share contracted slightly to €7.93 because of the dilutive potential shares outstanding.
8.
Interest reporting: MTU Aero Engines recorded a stable interest expense of €4 million on its convertible bonds during both 2024 and 2025. This interest expense must be added back to net income when executing the diluted EPS calculation. This stable cost compares with a rising total interest expense of €86 million in 2025 up from €60 million in 2024.
