Section 3 – Industry Structure + Concentration + Value Chain Economics 7 findings

Section summary
  1. the Mediterranean refining industry operates as a structurally tight, diesel-short oligopoly where prolonged capacity rationalization and geopolitical shocks have elevated mid-cycle margin floors.

Global and Mediterranean industry drivers

1.
Global refining market scale: the global oil refining market was valued at EUR 1,740 billion in 2025 and is projected to reach EUR 2,489 billion by 2035, reflecting an expected compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 3.6% over the ten-year period. Underlying demand trajectory: this growth is driven by rising emerging-market demand for transport, aviation, and petrochemical feedstocks, expanding fuel consumption 1.4 times faster than global refining capacity additions. Extended construction timelines: long-term supply remains structurally capped by capital discipline and extensive lead times, requiring 8 to 10 years to build a greenfield refinery from inception compared to 7 years for brownfield expansions. Persistent capacity rationalization: Western Europe has seen the closure of over 30 refineries since 2009, representing a cumulative distillation capacity reduction of over 1.1 million barrels per day
2.
Mediterranean product imbalances: the wider Mediterranean basin remains a structurally diesel-deficient market, importing roughly 22% of its regional diesel consumption and 31% of its aviation jet fuel

Regional consolidation and market concentration

3.
Oligopolistic domestic duopoly: the domestic Greek refining market is highly concentrated, controlled entirely by two operators managing four coastal refineries
4.
Dominant market leader: peer HELLENiQ Energy operates three integrated coastal refineries (Aspropyrgos,

Competitor conduct and pricing discipline

5.
Strict crude diversification: regional complex refiners demonstrate high operational agility, successfully replacing disrupted Iraqi and Middle Eastern crude grades with Libyan, West African, and US slates. Rationalized run policies: Mediterranean operators prioritize domestic supply commitments during regional supply crunches, shifting export volumes to capture heightened domestic and shipping/aviation margins
6.
Avoidance of greenfield additions: European competitors show strict capital discipline, refusing to invest in greenfield crude capacity for over a decade and focusing instead on deep de-bottlenecking and energy efficiency. Downstream price caps: domestic marketing margins face regulatory constraints, as the Greek government maintains regulated margin caps on key transportation fuels to protect consumers. Windfall profit taxation: domestic competitor conduct is periodically impacted by regulatory intervention, including extraordinary solidarity taxes of EUR 255 million levied on supernormal refining profits

Margin floors, ceilings, and success factors

7.
Structural margin floors: regional complex refining benchmark margins have established a structurally higher mid-cycle floor of USD 5 to USD 7 per barrel, compared to historical averages of USD 3 per barrel. Unprecedented margin ceilings: benchmark Med FCC margins surged to USD 16.40 per barrel in the first half of 2026, up from USD 3.50 per barrel in the first half of 2025 due to extreme product tightness. Exceptional realized netbacks: highly complex regional refiners achieved actual realized refining margins of USD 20.80 to USD 21.40 per barrel during peak geopolitical disruption cycles in 2026. Core complexity premium: the primary key success factor is refinery complexity, allowing operators with high Nelson Complexity Index (NCI) ratings–such as Motor Oil's Corinth refinery at 12.61–to maximize middle distillate yields and run heavier, cheaper crudes. Logistical integration advantage: successful operators integrate high-complexity assets with massive storage terminals and maritime deepwater ports to bypass regional shipping bottlenecks and capture trading arbitrage
