EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

ATOSS Software SE is navigating a planned founder-CEO transition to COO Pritim Kumar Krishnamoorthy at the turn of 2026/2027, following decades of executive leadership by Andreas Obereder, who will transition to the Supervisory Board. The CFO contract was extended through June 2028, securing continuity since 1999. However, the abrupt departure of Chief Revenue Officer Frederik Maris in 2025, shortly after his October 2024 appointment, forced founder-CEO Obereder to resume direct leadership of European expansion, exposing a keyman dependency in international execution.

This temporary international execution risk represents a meaningful near-term overhang despite otherwise orderly governance planning. The company maintains a dominant DACH position with over 16,000 customers, while cloud and subscription revenues reached 54% of H1 2026 sales, up from 48% in H1 2025. Legacy on-premise license sales have declined to just 2% of software sales in Q2 2026. ATOSS targets mid-market clients where competitive pressure from massive global enterprise suites is significantly less intense, while total recurring revenue rose to 70% of total sales in FY2025, with management modeling a target of approximately 75% by year-end 2027 and 80% by FY2030.

The opening of the Bengaluru AI Technology Hub in May 2026 scales R&D capacity to maintain best-in-class product innovation cycles.

External demand drivers remain structurally robust, with the global workforce management market valued at EUR 8-10 billion and expected to grow at a 9% CAGR through 2030. In Germany, 86% of employers faced massive difficulties finding candidate talent, representing the highest shortage rate in Europe, while half of the German population is currently over 45, forcing companies to optimize existing labor deployment. Regulatory complexity serves as a critical demand catalyst, with 5,580 collective agreements negotiated in Germany in 2025 alone, demanding instant audit-safe software updates.

The European Court of Justice decision mandating digital time tracking across industries accelerates software adoption. Artificial intelligence serves as a product differentiator layered on top of core subscription functionality, with process-embedded moats protected by domain-specific compliance accountability that horizontal large language models cannot own. The deployment of agentic assistants, including the ATC assistant launched in Q2 2026, accelerates cloud adoption rates. 6 8 ATOSS delivered best-in-class EBIT margins of 36.0% in FY2025, remaining significantly above the European software peer-median level of approximately 20% to 22%.

Gross margins expanded to 78.4% in FY2025, driven by cloud-scaling efficiencies and legacy license wind-down, while research and development expenses were maintained at approximately 16% of revenues to fund product innovation. The balance sheet reflects net cash of EUR 121.3 million in H1 2026 with zero financial debt, while Q1 2026 free cash flow surged 97% year-on-year to EUR 39.4 million. The board proposed a dividend payout of EUR 2.28 per share in FY2025, reflecting a stable payout ratio of 75%.

Scale advantages translate into a return on capital employed of approximately 75.8% in FY2025, far exceeding smaller European software peers.

Recent delivery remains exceptionally strong, with group revenue reaching EUR 103.2 million in H1 2026, up 12% compared to the prior-year period, while operating profitability remained high with an EBIT margin of 35%. Cloud and subscriptions order backlog increased by 25% to EUR 120.7 million by June 30, 2026. Cloud and subscription ARR expanded by 25% year-on-year to EUR 113.8 million, while net retention rate stood at 111% in H1 2026, confirming strong expansion with the existing customer base.

Management raised its FY27 EBIT margin guidance from at least 33% to at least 35%, prompting analysts to lift their FY27 EBIT margin forecast to 35.4% while keeping sales projections steady. 15 - Earnings Calls Forward guidance credibility is anchored in a deliberately sandbagged approach that permits routine quarterly raises. Initial FY2026 EBIT margin guidance was set at a conservative 32%, subsequently lifted to at least 34% in Q1 2026, while projected FY2027 margin expectations were raised to at least 35% from the prior floor of 33% during H1 2026.

Actual FY2025 EBIT margins reached 36% against an upgraded forecast of 34% and an initial floor of 31%. A 78.4% gross margin ensures incremental revenue flows directly to operating earnings with minimal added cost of sales. Model calculations suggest a 1% shift in total revenue alters FY2026 EPS by EUR 0.07 or 2.1%, while every 50 basis point change in the EBIT margin translates to an adjusted EPS variance of EUR 0.04.

Sell-side positioning reveals unanimous Buy recommendations across all five active analysts covering ATOSS in the AlphaSense library, including Johannes Schaller and Nicolas Herms at Deutsche Bank, Henrik Paganetty at Jefferies, Philipp Sennewald at NuWays, Alexander Zienkowicz at mwb research, and Felix Ellmann at Warburg. Price targets exhibit a wide spread of EUR 57, ranging from a conservative EUR 100 to a bullish EUR 157, with an average price target of EUR 135 following publication of the 2025 financial statements. Shares trade at a highly attractive 16x FY27 expected unlevered free cash flow according to Deutsche Bank. However, Jefferies trimmed its price target from EUR 140 to EUR 115 in May 2026 to incorporate long-term AI-related risks, adopting a conservative stance while warning that the tailwind from capitalized interest is fading. Jefferies models a bearish EUR 55 scenario assuming a 25% EBIT margin if AI-enabled in-house solutions lower barriers.

Forward EPS growth is structurally anchored in five to eight key drivers with varying impact, plausibility, and confidence levels. Cloud migration uplift offers a 30% to 40% like-for-like revenue increase with high plausibility and high confidence, dependent on maintenance attrition rates. European non-DACH expansion targets a 10% revenue share with medium plausibility and medium confidence, dependent on quota-carrier sales ramp-up.

AI product monetization projects a 15% ARR expansion add-on with medium plausibility but low confidence, dependent on cloud system migration. Crewmeister SME scaling anticipates a 30% CAGR through FY27 with high plausibility and high confidence, dependent on European digital time mandates. The Bengaluru hub and internal AI tools are expected to deliver a 150 basis point EBIT margin lift with high plausibility and high confidence, dependent on developer adoption metrics.

The materiality of change at ATOSS is substantial, driven by the definitive SaaS migration reaching critical mass, the opening of a scalable global R&D hub in India, and the rollout of agentic AI capabilities that accelerate cloud conversions while preserving seat-based pricing models. The key forward drivers center on high-margin cloud migrations yielding 30% to 40% revenue uplifts, international expansion into Benelux, France, Nordics, and Middle East doubling the addressable market, AI product monetization through freemium and hybrid pricing, Crewmeister scaling in the SME segment, and Bengaluru-driven R&D leverage.

Recent delivery confirms immaculate execution, with the 20th consecutive record year in FY2025, H1 2026 EBIT margins of 35% beating conservative guidance, cloud ARR expanding 25% year-on-year, and free cash flow surging 97% in Q1 2026. Forward credibility is exceptionally high, supported by systematic sandbagging of margin guidance, high-visibility cloud backlogs of EUR 121 million, sustained cloud churn below 2%, and upgraded FY27 margin targets to at least 35%.

Sell-side positioning is unanimously bullish with all five brokers maintaining Buy ratings, though price targets reflect systemic AI valuation compression, ranging from EUR 100 to EUR 157 with an average of EUR 135. Fundamental peer positioning is dominant, with ATOSS delivering EBIT margins of 35% versus the peer median of 22%, purely organic revenue growth of 12% versus the peer median of 10%, and return on capital employed of 75.8% far exceeding smaller competitors.

Activist presence is absent, with stable anchor shareholders Andreas Obereder holding 22.49% and General Atlantic Chronos holding 21.59%, both subject to a four-year lock-up until June 2027. Key risks include frequent sales leadership churn undercutting international expansion, sluggish non-DACH revenue traction remaining stuck at 6% of total sales versus the 10% target, prolonged European recession forcing SME insolvencies and job cuts that would trigger seat license contraction, AI-native scheduling tools lowering competitive barriers and compressing margins, rapid on-premise maintenance decay outpacing new cloud bookings, and the founder's Supervisory Board seat potentially diluting the new CEO's autonomy if over-assertive.

In a severe downside scenario, bearish models project sustainable EBIT margins of just 25% if AI lowers competitive barriers, customer budgets contract, and international execution stagnates, with sales CAGR falling to 8% versus the base case of 13%. However, these risks are partially offset by regulatory complexity serving as a critical moat, structural labor shortages driving persistent demand, high customer switching costs embedded in compliance-critical software, and rapid investment payback periods of 6 to 12 months insulating the company from severe cyclical IT spending cuts. The investment-case verdict synthesizes to a high-conviction positive view anchored in best-in-class profitability, structural recurring revenue scaling, and immaculate historical execution, tempered by near-term international GTM friction and long-term AI disruption tail risks that justify cautious position sizing rather than maximum conviction deployment.

Appendix

Plausible problems to monitor

The financial report reveals several discernible plausible problem to monitor that position ATOSS Software moderately below peer execution standards or industry norms, though none represent existential threats to the core business model. The abrupt departure of Chief Revenue Officer Frederik Maris in 2025, only months after his October 2024 appointment, forced founder-CEO Andreas Obereder to resume direct leadership of European expansion, exposing a key-man dependency that contrasts unfavorably with more institutionalized go-to-market structures at comparable European software peers [Cross-ref: Section 1].

This sales leadership churn has contributed to sluggish international revenue traction, with non-DACH markets accounting for only 6% of total sales in FY2025 despite multi-year expansion efforts into France, Benelux, Nordics, and the Middle East. This geographic concentration lags behind peers such as Nemetschek, which derives substantially higher proportions of revenue from international markets, and raises concerns about ATOSS's ability to double its total addressable market as planned [Cross-ref: Section 2, Section 3].

Forward-looking analyst commentary introduces additional caution, with Jefferies warning that the tailwind from capitalized interest is fading and modeling a bearish scenario that assumes a 25% EBIT margin if AI-enabled inhouse solutions lower competitive barriers [Cross-ref: Section 8]. Management has also embedded a negative deviation buffer of 3% into mid-term revenue targets to protect against macroeconomic volatility, signaling internal recognition of execution uncertainty relative to ambitious growth plans [Cross-ref: Section 10]. - Earnings Calls While these plausible problem to monitor reflect temporary friction points rather than structural deterioration, they collectively suggest that international expansion execution remains less mature than the core DACH business, sales force productivity is still ramping, and macro-sensitive customers are delaying cloud migration decisions.

The lack of publicly available eNPS peer benchmarks prevents rigorous cross-sectional comparison of employee satisfaction metrics, though ATOSS's internal target of at least 25 appears reasonable. Importantly, none of these plausible problem to monitor contradict the company's sector-leading 35% EBIT margins, 25% cloud ARR growth, or sub-2% churn rates, indicating that operational excellence in the core market continues to insulate the business from more severe competitive or cyclical pressures observed at lower-margin European software peers.
