Section 1 – Leadership and Governance Change

Section summary
  1. executive transitions maintain stability as the CFO and Wood Products head roles transition smoothly to experienced internal or returning hands
  2. divisional consolidation under Board and Paper streamlines operations and maintains stable talent metrics despite broader macro pressures

Executive and board changes

1.
Long-tenured CEO: Henrik Sjölund has served as President and CEO since 2014, providing highly stable leadership.
2.
Stable leadership: Sjölund originally joined the group in 1993, representing more than three decades of deep institutional knowledge.
3.
Orderly CFO transition: Anders Jernhall stepped down as CFO in spring 2025 to focus entirely on his role as Deputy CEO.
4.
Competent successor: Stefan Loréhn assumed the CFO position in spring 2025, returning to Holmen after serving as CFO at LKAB.
5.
Strategic executive streamlining: The separation of the CFO and Deputy CEO roles aims to free up management capacity for long-term strategic development.
6.
Close collaboration: Loréhn and Jernhall previously worked closely together for many years, minimizing operational disruption during the transition.
7.
Planned management departure: Head of Wood Products Johan Padel decided to leave Holmen during 2026 after 12 years of service.
8.
Promotion from within: Karolina Svensson will succeed Padel on June 1, 2026, bringing extensive experience from business development and strategy roles.
9.
Refreshed Board representation: Stefan Widing was elected to the Board at the 2025 AGM, succeeding departing member Lars Josefsson.
10.
External expertise: Widing serves as the CEO of Sandvik AB, strengthening the Board's industrial and corporate governance competence.
11.
Stable Board re-election: The 2026 AGM re-elected all nine board members, maintaining exceptional continuity under Chairman Fredrik Lundberg.
12.
Entrenched chairman: Lundberg has served as Chairman since 1988, which is exceptionally long compared to the typical Swedish peer median.

Divisional reorganisation and corporate culture

13.
Divisional consolidation: Holmen merged its Paperboard and Paper units into a unified Board and Paper division in early 2024.
14.
Internal leadership: Lars Lundin, previously head of Paper, was appointed to lead the newly combined business area.
15.
Successful restructuring execution: The divisional merger was designed to improve long-term competitiveness and consolidate marketing functions across industrial operations.

Positive integration: Management reported highly promising initial results and successful marketing department integration one year post-merger.

Healthy employee turnover: Holmen reported an employee turnover rate of 7.3% in 2025, reflecting stable workforce retention.

Moderate absenteeism: The group's absenteeism rate stood at 4.7% in 2025, which is aligned with regional manufacturing benchmarks.
19.
Concentrated ownership control: Principal shareholder L E Lundbergföretagen controls 62% of total voting power, creating a highly stable ownership structure.

Section 2 – Strategy, Structure and Ambition Change

Section summary
  1. Holmen's core strategy remains anchored on maximizing value from its premium forest land which represents over 75% of enterprise value
  2. industrial strategy is defined by selective, organic wood processing investments and defensive sawmill capacity curtailments during cyclical downturns
  3. corporate ambition has pivoted toward building out wind power capacity though future investments are capped until power prices recover

Activist shareholding presence

1.
No active activist presence in AS library for Holmen AB.

Strategic priorities and corporate identity

2.
Defensive asset focus: Holmen is structured as a forest-owning entity designed to extract long-term value from land holdings.
3.
Integrated business model: Divisional segments are designed to support each other, creating strong resource circularity and financial resilience.
4.
Clear CEO positioning: Henrik Sjölund stated in October 2025 that Holmen's fundamental strategy is to extract value from owned land.
5.
Confident business model: Sjölund highlighted in April 2026 that the company's diversified business model has been highly favorable.
6.
High asset concentration: Forest holdings comprise more than 75% of group enterprise value, dwarfing peer averages like UPM.

Forestry asset strategy and valuation

7.
Large land footprint: Holmen owns 1.3 million hectares of land in Sweden, making it one of the largest private landowners.
8.
Rolling transaction valuation: Forest assets are valued based on a rolling three-year average of actual property transactions.
9.
Peer-leading asset base: Holmen's forest integration of 50-60% matches SCA, representing the highest selfsufficiency among Nordic peers.
10.
Sticky asset prices: Despite Swedish wood price volatility, the forest property book value only declined 2% to SEK 57 billion.
11.
Stable valuation trend: Jefferies analyst Cole Hathorn notes forest assets are valued at SEK 296 per share after a 20% liquidity discount.

Wood Products M&A and organic investments

12.
Transformative sawmill acquisition: Holmen completed the SEK 1.0 billion acquisition of Martinsons in 2020 to double wood products capacity.
13.
Disciplined transaction pricing: The Martinsons transaction was completed at a low valuation multiple of 6.4x EV/EBITDA.
14.
Successful capacity expansion: The acquisition added Bygdsiljum and Kroksjön sawmills, expanding annual capacity to 1.5 million cubic meters.
15.
Planned organic investments: Holmen is investing SEK 400 million to modernize its Iggesund Sawmill, boosting long-term capacity by 20%
16.
Targeted processing focus: Organic investments are aimed at expanding production of high-value crosslaminated timber and glulam.
17.
Defensive production curtailments: Management implemented capacity cuts at Braviken and Linghem sawmills due to southern Sweden log shortages.
18.
Regional margin squeeze: Morgan Stanley's Ioannis Masvoulas notes a geographic mismatch between northern forests and southern sawmills.

Renewable Energy expansion and ambition

Expanded wind footprint: Holmen invested SEK 1.5 billion in the Blisterliden wind farm to expand fossilfree generation.

Substantial capacity additions: The 98 MW Blisterliden project adds 14 turbines, boosting annual electricity production by 360 GWh.

22.
Incremental output growth: Total renewable energy generation has increased by 20% to 2.2 TWh annually.
23.
Cautious growth stance: Sjölund announced a halt on new wind investments, stating no new wind farms will be built.
24.
Muted project economics: Morgan Stanley argues that low power prices in northern Sweden threaten capital value leakage from wind investments.
25.
Strategic grid contribution: Hydropower assets function as high-scale batteries, earning spot price premiums by stabilizing the grid.

Shareholder returns and capital allocation

26.
High capital discipline: Holmen prioritizes returning excess cash to shareholders over pursuing risky industrial expansion projects.
27.
Orderly capital distribution: The group has distributed SEK 13 billion through dividends and buybacks over the last five years.

Flexible buyback execution: Holmen resolved to repurchase up to 3 million Class B shares from May 2026.
29.
Rapid buyback completion: The board completed the 3 million share repurchase program in May 2026.

Sustained equity concentration: Capital returns adjust the corporate capital structure, increasing forest asset ownership per outstanding share.

Section 3 – External Change: Value-Chain Forces (Demand, Competition, Disruption, Regulation, Supply, Cycle, Macro)

Section summary
  1. consumer caution and luxury market fatigue continue to subdue packaging and paperboard demand, leaving operating rates well below full capacity
  2. competitive intensity is escalating as new low-cost board capacity enters Europe, limiting premium pricing power
  3. structural technological substitution remains a persistent headwind for graphic paper volumes, although niche product strategies offer temporary shelter
  4. regulatory pressures on forest management and carbon sink requirements threaten to restrict long-term Nordic wood supply
  5. pulpwood cost relief is emerging but persistently high sawlog prices continue to squeeze industrial margins
  6. the construction market remains in a deep cyclical trough, forcing production curtailments at southern sawmills
  7. northern Swedish SE2 power price volatility remains high, boosting energy segment returns while increasing industrial mill costs

Demand

1.
Weakening consumerboard consumption: Consumerboard and containerboard demand remains soft as cautious consumers hold back spending.
2.
Depressed packaging demand: European paperboard consumption has contracted by 10-12% from its peak in 2021, highlighting structural weakness.
3.
Soft luxury market: Fatigue in the post-pandemic luxury goods boom is dampening demand for high-end Folding Box Board.
4.
Weak graphic paper: European newsprint demand continues its long-term structural decline, falling at 7% to 8% annually.
5.
Stable book paper: Holmen's focus on niche book paper provides relative volume stability within a declining market.

Competition

6.
Excess European capacity: The consumerboard sector faces intensifying overcapacity as new paperboard machines ramp up.
7.
Low-cost competitor threat: Stora Enso is expanding its Oulu consumerboard machine to 750 ktonnes annually, increasing regional pricing pressure.
8.
High-cost mill closures: Competitors are forced to shut uncompetitive capacity, exemplified by Metsä Board closing its 210 ktonnes Tako mill.
9.
Intense marginal competition: Competition for marginal board volumes is fierce, particularly in export markets outside Europe.
10.
Graphic paper oversupply: Unlike previous years, the graphic paper industry has announced fewer capacity closures to balance supply.

Disruption

11.
Structural digital shift: Graphic paper is facing accelerating digital substitution, contracting at a 6.5% CAGR over the last 15 years.
12.
Blocked conversion exits: Converting old graphic mills into packaging is no longer an easy exit due to widespread packaging oversupply.
13.
Sustainable packaging substitution: Rising consumer preferences for biodegradable materials are driving a long-term shift from plastic to paper.
14.
Innovative product development: Holmen launched a lightweight packaging paper in 2025 to offer higher efficiency without losing strength.
15.
Advanced material threats: Competitors continue to develop alternative materials with improved recycling lifespans to challenge fresh fiber.

Regulation

16.
Tightening forestry policies: Proposed EU regulations aimed at increasing forest carbon sinks threaten to restrict harvesting volumes.
17.
Costly environmental compliance: Holmen must secure complex permits under the Swedish Environmental Code, adding compliance risk to expansions.
18.
Loss of free emissions: Holmen expects to lose 30% of its free carbon allocations as Iggesund exits the subsidy system.
19.
Trade tariff uncertainty: Rising global trade tensions and potential US import tariffs increase export market risks.
20.
Strict carbon accounting: Regulatory scrutiny on wood harvesting could challenge the industry's status as a clean carbon sink.

Supply

21.
Declining pulpwood costs: Scandinavian pulpwood prices are beginning to decline as industrial mill production curtailments reduce raw fiber demand.
22.
Sticky sawmill log prices: High log prices remain sticky, keeping wood raw material input costs elevated across Sweden.
23.
High southern sawlog costs: Extreme raw log costs in southern Sweden have forced Holmen to curtail sawmill utilization.
24.
Lagged margin relief: Holmen's near-term P&L will not see immediate relief because high-priced legacy harvesting rights must be worked through.
25.
Persistent structural shortages: Nordic roundwood supplies remain structurally tight after the ban on Russian timber imports removed 10% of regional supply.

Cycle

26.
Severe construction downturn: Weak construction activity across Europe has depressed timber consumption and sawn wood prices.
27.
Delayed building recovery: Analysts have pushed out the expected European construction and sawmill recovery into late 2H26 or 2027.
28.
Production rate adjustments: Low demand and extreme sawlog costs led Holmen's Wood Products to a SEK 91 million operating loss in Q1 2026.
29.
Late-cyclical board contract lag: Long-term pricing agreements make Holmen's paperboard business more resilient but late-cyclical in a downturn.
30.
Normalizing energy margins: The highly profitable winter energy spike of Q1 2026 is expected to moderate as weather patterns normalize.

Macro

31.
Depressed SE2 power prices: Excess hydropower and limited transmission capacity kept northern Swedish SE2 power prices unprofitably low in 2025.
32.
High winter power spike: Cold weather and limited wind in Q1 2026 caused SE2 prices to spike, boosting Renewable Energy EBIT to SEK 281 million.
33.
Increased industrial power costs: High electricity spot prices in Q1 2026 increased production costs for Board and Paper.
34.
Slower interest rate cuts: Rising geopolitical tensions and inflation risks threaten to delay interest rate easing, extending the construction slump.
35.
Nordic geographic pricing mismatch: The mismatch between northern forest assets and southern industrial mills exposes Holmen to high regional transmission costs.
36.
overcapacity will cap near-term industrial EBIT margins, neutralizing any geographic resource hedging advantage.

  2. Holmen maintains a highly defensive balance sheet with leverage of 1.1x Net Debt/EBITDA, outperforming more indebted peers
  3. capital discipline remains strong through ongoing buybacks and capex cuts despite subdued free cash flow yields

Segment margin evolution

1.
Stable Forest margins: Forest segment EBITDA margin reached 65% in Q1 2026, showing resilience compared to declining global pulpwood pricing.
2.
Squeezed board profitability: Board and Paper operating margin collapsed to 12% in Q1 2026 due to high energy costs.
3.
Negative Wood returns: Wood Products margin plummeted to -9% in Q1 2026 as high log costs squeezed sawmills.
4.
Volatile Energy margins: Renewable Energy operating profit reached SEK 281m in Q1 2026, recovering from SEK 18m in FY2025.
5.
Peer margin comparison: Morgan Stanley analysts note Holmen's 10-year average margin of 15% exceeds Stora Enso's 10% average.

Leverage and balance sheet strength

6.
Low net debt: Net debt declined to SEK 4,186m in Q1 2026, driven by working capital inflows.
7.
Conservative gearing targets: Management targets a net gearing ratio of <25% to maintain long-term capital flexibility.
8.
Superior leverage metrics: Holmen's Net Debt/EBITDA stands at 1.1x, which is well below the peer median.
9.
Strong peer positioning: Morgan Stanley models Holmen's leverage at 1.1x Net Debt/EBITDA, compared to Mondi's 2.2x.
10.
Underperforming peer returns: Holmen trades at a premium P/B of 1.2x despite posting below-peer return on equity.

Cash conversion and capital allocation

11.
Defensive capex reduction: Capital expenditure stepped down to SEK 247m in Q1 2026 to protect balance sheet liquidity.
12.
Robust cash flow: Cash flow before investments reached SEK 986m in Q1 2026, supporting recurring capital distributions.
13.
Disciplined share repurchases: Holmen completed a 3 million share buyback in May 2026 to optimize outstanding equity.
14.
Underwhelming dividend distributions: The 2025 ordinary dividend of SEK 9.50/share underwhelmed the market with no special payout.
15.
Stagnated cash yields: DNB Carnegie forecasts Holmen's 2026e free cash flow yield at 3.7% as industrial demand remains sluggish.
16.
Other side of the story: Persistent wood inflation and prolonged paperboard overcapacity could trigger industrial cash burn, forcing a dividend cut.

Outliers worth noting

Holmen AB exhibits several extreme statistical and qualitative characteristics that distinguish it sharply from typical European paper and packaging peers, beginning with its unprecedented forest asset concentration where 1.3 million hectares of forest holdings represent more than 75% of enterprise value, compared to just 22% for UPM, creating what is arguably the most land-asset-heavy capital structure in the sector.

The company's governance structure presents another profound outlier characteristic, with no active activist presence recorded in the AlphaSense library and principal shareholder L E Lundbergföretagen exercising 63.2% of total voting power, representing an exceptional concentration of control that insulates management from external pressure to a degree rarely observed among publicly listed Nordic industrials. The Renewable Energy division demonstrates extreme operational volatility through its sophisticated intraday power-trading capabilities, with high intraday electricity volatility in Q2 2025 supporting earnings by SEK 250 million to SEK 300 million through power optimization strategies, representing a profitability swing mechanism that far exceeds typical utility-scale generation assets and introduces unusual earnings unpredictability.

This volatility manifests in dramatic quarterly operating profit fluctuations, as Renewable Energy operating profit collapsed to SEK 18 million in 2025 from SEK 697 million in 2023 due to depressed SE2 power prices, before recovering sharply to SEK 281 million in Q1 2026, creating margin oscillations that dwarf peer renewable generation businesses. The geographic concentration of Holmen's electricity generation assets in the northern Swedish SE2 power zone represents a structural outlier, as forward earnings sensitivity to this single regional wholesale electricity market far exceeds typical diversified European utilities, with price recovery in the SE2 zone identified as a major driver of wind asset EBIT.

Regulatory exposure presents another distinctive characteristic, as Holmen expects to lose 30% of its free carbon allocations as Iggesund exits the subsidy system, representing a step-change reduction in implicit subsidy support that is unusually concentrated relative to peer portfolios.

Divisional margin dispersion reached extreme levels in Q1 2026, with the Forest segment achieving a resilient 65% EBITDA margin, Board and Paper collapsing to 12% operating margin, Wood Products plummeting to negative 9% margin, and Renewable Energy spiking to SEK 281 million operating profit, creating an inter-divisional profitability range that vastly exceeds typical integrated forest products peers and reflects profound business model heterogeneity.

Balance sheet positioning reveals a paradoxical valuation outlier, as Holmen maintains exceptionally low leverage at 1.1x Net Debt/EBITDA compared to Mondi's 2.2x, yet simultaneously trades at a premium P/B of 1.2x despite posting below-peer return on equity, suggesting the market assigns unusual scarcity value to forest land ownership that defies conventional return-driven valuation frameworks.
