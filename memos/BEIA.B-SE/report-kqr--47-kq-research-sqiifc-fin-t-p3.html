1.

DNB Carnegie reported on 12 November 2026 that the market willingness to fund acquired growth declines when sector multiples contract. Strategic acquisition plans are supported by low-cost equity-funded deals which subsequently elevate earnings per share. Any contraction in the group's average multiple will subsequently limit the pace of accretive transactions.
2.
Expanding valuation: Swedish compounders saw significant multiple contraction in 2026 as sector rotation favored early-cyclical stocks over high-growth names. DNB Carnegie highlighted on 12 November 2026 that median adjusted price-to-earnings multiples for both proven and less-proven compounders contracted by 3% over the year. Valuation multiples remain highly sensitive to macroeconomic interest rates because elevated borrowing costs reduce cash flows. Rising benchmark rates compress the premium the market is willing to pay for acquired EBITA.
3.
Contrary evidence: Bergman & Beving executive interviews from December 2024 suggest that decentralized serial acquirers are not entirely dependent on high stock exchange valuations to fund their acquisitions. The group's portfolio companies are historically self-financed through internal cash generation rather than equity issuance. This operational self-sufficiency ensures that acquisitions can continue even when the market refuses to assign a high valuation multiple.
4.
Squeezing arbitrage: Lagercrantz experiences valuation pressure because its high multiples leave limited room for further multiple expansion. Redeye research reported on 28 October 2026 that the company trades at 29.7x LTM EV/EBIT, which is 5% below its five-year median. This premium valuation reflects the market's high valuation of serial acquirers but means that future share price returns must be driven by earnings growth rather than multiple expansion. High historical multiples were supported by the business shifting toward proprietary products and higher operating margins.

DNB Carnegie notes that Storskogen was forced to halt its high M&A pace in 2023 and 2024 to focus on deleveraging. This leveraged position means that rising interest expenses could consume too much operating cash flow if benchmark rates increase. Consequently, Volati must limit its M&A focus to smaller bolt-on acquisitions until its balance sheet is deleveraged.

Deteriorating returns: Solwers demonstrates the operational dangers of relying too heavily on debt-funded acquisitions during industrial market downturns. Systemic risks: Swedish serial acquirers face heightened systemic threats when elevated leverage is paired with cyclical end-market exposure. DNB Carnegie warned in October 2026 that taking on excessive leverage during cyclical peaks significantly increases downside risk. The market historical context shows that groups like Embracer suffered severe de-ratings when aggressive acquisition loops hit funding bottlenecks. This demonstrates that maintaining conservative debt limits remains vital to preventing loop reversals.
