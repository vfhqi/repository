Section 2 – AI Economics: Productivity Retention, Startup Costs, and Client

Section summary
  1. Reply can retain short-term AI productivity gains on its fixed-price contracts, but long-term earnings face pressure as contract renewals force efficiency sharing with clients.
  2. Clients are actively reinvesting their AI-driven savings into broader digital transformation projects, maintaining robust overall IT budgets.
  3. The launch of 30 to 40 AI startups annually creates a short-term margin drag but drives Reply's high organic volume growth.

Reinvestment

Productivity Retention and Pricing Models

1.
Strong retention: Reply is well-positioned to retain productivity gains in the short term because fixed-price contracts account for approximately 70% of its revenues. Deutsche Bank Research estimates that Reply has achieved up to 30% efficiency gains in developer man days through internal AI use. However, these efficiency gains will ultimately be shared with customers during contract renewal negotiations.
2.
Severe pressure: Reply faces medium-term margin risks as clients mature and demand their share of the AI productivity dividend. Analysts at Bernstein Research argue that while service providers initially capture margin benefits, clients will eventually force renegotiations to claw back these savings. They maintain that competitive pressure will eventually pass all AI-driven savings through to the end customers.
3.
Evolving pricing: Capgemini and other European integrators are being forced to transition from labor-based billing toward outcome-based contracts. An industry expert notes that clients are beginning to demand 15% to 30% price markdowns to reflect automated developer efficiencies. This structural shift requires providers to reinvent legacy commercial models to protect their operating margins.

Client Reinvestment and IT Budgets

4.
Positive reinvestment: Reply expects its total addressable market to remain highly resilient because clients are redeploying their AI savings back into new technology projects. The company’s CTO highlights that savings from automated operations boost clients' overall investment capacity for developing new application suites. This client willingness to reinvest helps offset any deflationary headwind from automated software development.
5.
Sustained volume: Reply reports that efficiency gains leading to smaller project sizes are being fully offset by additional volume demand. According to the company's annual statement, the avoidance of commodity work and a focus on specialized niches provide high operational resilience against AI disruption. This historical demand pattern has consistently protected Reply's revenue growth during previous major technology transitions.
6.
Scaled validation: Accenture validates this reinvestment thesis by showing how AI-driven client efficiencies create capital that is immediately redeployed. Deutsche Bank Research notes that Accenture's management explicitly argues that client savings on legacy contracts fund the next wave of enterprise reinvention work. This continuous cycle of efficiency and reinvestment supports sustained long-term consulting demand across the sector.

Cost and Payback of AI Startups

7.
Accelerated incubation: Reply has significantly stepped up its organic startup creation engine to capture emerging AI opportunities. The group has launched 30 to 40 new startups since the beginning of 2026, with an estimated 95% of them dedicated entirely to artificial intelligence initiatives. Bernstein Research reports that this rapid expansion is driven by a decentralized network model and strong ground-level entrepreneurial culture.
8.
Near-term dilution: Reply faces immediate margin compression as the accelerated pace of startup creation drags down regional profitability. Bernstein Research highlights that the costs associated with building these new entities are already weighing on margins, leading to EBITDA margin declines in Region 1 and Region 3. Although these investments drag down near-term earnings per share, they secure the group's long-term organic growth runway.
9.
Measured payoffs: Reply accepts that its startup investments and M&A deals are structurally dilutive to the group's EBITDA margins in the short term. Analysts at Bernstein Research note that the historical margin of 14% to 16% is impacted by startup launch costs and delayed payback periods. This investment cycle requires careful execution, as the dilutive effects must be offset by rapid revenue scaling within two to three years.

Comparison with Peers and Model Contrast

10.
Scaled bookings: Accenture highlights a distinct competitive approach by leveraging its massive global scale to capture multi-million dollar AI modernization deals. Morgan Stanley Research notes that Accenture is building a Network of Businesses in faster-growing markets that is expected to double its revenue mix to 12% by FY29. This centralized scale contrast highlights Reply's reliance on boutique agility over massive infrastructure deals.
11.
Strategic integration: Capgemini has taken a different consolidation path by acquiring WNS to expand its presence in intelligent business operations. Bernstein Research observes that Capgemini is attempting to turn client-specific work into reusable assets and proprietary software agents. This platform-centric delivery model contrasts sharply with Reply's decentralized boutique network of 300 highly specialized entities.
12.
Execution drag: Kainos has faced short-term profitability pressure due to its heavy reliance on expensive contractors to deliver digital services projects. Deutsche Bank Research reports that Kainos's group gross margin declined to 43.1% in H1'27 reflecting limited near-term operational gearing. Unlike Reply's stable internal staff model, Kainos's high contractor mix exposes it to immediate margin volatility during rapid project scale-ups.
13.
Organizational agility: Reply's decentralized model of 300 boutique companies provides superior speed of execution compared to the rigid delivery structures of larger peers. Deutsche Bank Research highlights that this specialized setup allows Reply to adapt quickly to fluid partner landscapes and technological shifts. This boutique network model underpins Reply's premium organic revenue growth, which consistently outperforms the wider IT services sector.

Contrary Evidence and Alternatives

14.
Contrary evidence: The bullish view on AI developer productivity at European IT services firms is challenged by data showing that automated coding tools introduce massive hidden labor inefficiencies. Bernstein Research cites a Harness survey from 2026 showing that 31% of a developer's day is consumed by invisible AI work, such as reviewing inaccurate code and fixing subtle bugs. These friction points limit the actual cost savings that Reply or its peers can achieve from developer automation.
15.
Other side of the story: The pessimistic market view that AI will structurally deflate Reply's earnings fails to recognize its high-value consulting mix and nimble corporate structure. Bernstein Research highlights that Reply is among the least exposed to AI disruption because its work centers on high-end design and architecture, which are highly defensible against automation. While simple coding tasks may face price pressure, Reply's specialized boutique network will capture higher-value transformation spending.

Section 3 – Capital Allocation and Accounting: The Buyback, D&A, and Tax Guidance

Section summary
  1. Reply S.p.A.'s capital allocation is shifting toward share buybacks under a massive authorizations scheme, though management officially states these programs are not intended for capital reduction.
  2. The structural step-up in Reply's depreciation and amortization is primarily driven by right-of-use asset leases under IFRS 16, which offsets cost-efficiency improvements.
  3. Reply's actual effective tax rate continuously exceeds both official management guidance and consensus models, presenting a subtle drag on net earnings.

Buyback Mechanics and Share Count

1.
Ambitious authorization: Reply's shareholders re-authorized a massive buyback program on 23 April 2026 allowing the acquisition of up to 10% of the company's share capital for a maximum expenditure of €550 million within an 18-month timeframe. This program follows previous authorizations that have historically permitted the company to utilize treasury shares for strategic M&A or stock incentive plans. The persistent scale of these authorizations indicates that management is prioritizing equity-based transaction flexibility over traditional cash payouts.
2.
Accelerated execution: Reply accelerated its purchasing pace in the first half of 2026 by acquiring 746,854 treasury shares on the open market, which reduced the outstanding shares in circulation to 36,531,382 as of 30 June 2026. Management stated during the Q2 2026 earnings call that the purchasing bank has bought nearly 1 million shares on the company's behalf, expanding treasury holdings to 2.9% of share capital. This execution represents a substantial cash outflow of €64 million during the first half of the year.
3.
Optimistic modeling: Bernstein Research updated its valuation model on 19 May 2026 to incorporate the buyback by assuming it will lead to the cancellation of 1 million shares and lower the share count to 36.4 million. This reduction mechanically boosts modeled earnings per share, lifting their target price to €120. However, if these shares are instead used for employee incentives or acquisitions rather than being cancelled, this modeled EPS lift will fail to materialize.
4.
Contrary evidence: The company's official share buyback announcement on 9 April 2026 states that the program is not intended for capital reduction but rather to serve equity incentive plans and strategic M&A. This contradicts the common sell-side assumption of share cancellation. Therefore, the anticipated double-digit EPS accretion from a smaller share base may be entirely ignored by the company's actual operating reality.

Rising Depreciation and Accounting Shifts

5.
Structural step-up: Reply experienced a structural step-up in its depreciation and amortization charges from 1% of sales in 2018 to roughly 4% of sales by 2023, totaling €75 million. This expansion was primarily driven by IFRS 16 right-of-use asset lease depreciation which accounted for €32 million of the total. This rising lease cost base represents a permanent operational headwind that limits the cash conversion benefits of Reply's asset-light business model.
6.
Estimate modifications: Reply adjusted its accounting estimates under IAS 8 prospectively from 1 January 2025 to extend the useful lives of certain asset categories. This adjustment resulted in a reduction in depreciation and amortization of approximately €1 million in the consolidated income statement for 2025. This change provided a corresponding boost to operating profit, representing a minor accounting adjustment rather than an organic efficiency gain.
7.
Relatively flat: Reply's run-rate depreciation and amortization remained broadly constant during the first half of 2026 when excluding the non-recurring €19 million French goodwill impairment registered in 2025. The group reported total depreciation and amortization of €35.5 million for Q2 2026. This underlying stability indicates that lease additions and capital expenditures are currently balanced by maturing lease terms.

Gaps in Tax Guidance and Modeling

8.
Guidance gap: Reply continues to experience a gap between management's normative tax guidance and the group's actual tax run-rate. Sell-side analysts at Deutsche Bank model a stable tax rate of 30.6% in line with management's historical indications. However, this projection understates the actual tax burden, creating a risk that consensus EPS forecasts are set too high.
9.
Run-rate divergence: Reply's actual tax rate reached almost 32 percent in 2025 as confirmed during the fourth quarter results conference call. The group subsequently recorded an effective tax rate of 31.5% for the first half of 2026, which management described as their best estimate for the full year. This elevated rate is driven by geographic earnings mix and non-deductible local tax items.

Opposing dynamics: Alten's tax rate remains highly competitive relative to Reply, posting a consolidated effective tax rate of 27.62% in H1 2026. 15.
Other side of the story: The pessimistic view that Reply's buyback will not provide EPS support ignores the sheer scale of the €550 million authorization relative to Reply's market capitalization.
