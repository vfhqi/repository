Section 3 – Tax Rates and Currency Translation Sensitivity

Section summary
  1. Consolidated tax rate volatility arises from the varying profitability and tax regimes across D'Ieteren's distinct operational segments.
  2. Stated US dollar translation sensitivities highlight a significant underlying exposure to North American vehicle glass repair operations.
  3. Peer comparisons show D'Ieteren faces higher foreign exchange translation risks than its automotive distribution counterparts.

Segment adjusted tax dynamics

1.
Segmented tax differences: D'Ieteren's reported consolidated tax rate can vary dramatically depending on the performance of its underlying businesses. The group's actual consolidated effective tax rate was reported at 15.9% for the year ended 31 December 2025. However, excluding the share of equity-accounted investees, the effective tax rate historically rose to as high as 55.4% in prior periods. These wide variations show how segment profitability drives overall group tax volatility over time.
2.
Profitability mix effects: The relationship between accounting profit and actual tax expense is highly dependent on regional earnings. D'Ieteren operates in multiple jurisdictions with complex legal and tax regulatory environments. For example, the group's Belgian corporate tax rate stands at a nominal 25% as a baseline. Changing geographic mix or tax rate differentials will directly alter segment adjusted tax rates and overall group EPS.
3.
Non-deductible expense impacts: D'Ieteren's segment adjusted tax rate is frequently distorted by unique corporate adjusting items. In prior years, charges from Long-Term Incentive Plans and share-based payments in the PHE and Automotive segments were non-tax deductible. Furthermore, major goodwill impairments on Moleskine had no tax effect, which significantly pushed up the effective tax rate. These recurring adjusting items mean that investors must track segment-level underlying tax rates rather than relying solely on consolidated figures.
4.
Contrary evidence: D'Ieteren faces significant tax uncertainties that could trigger additional cash outflows if disputed by authorities. The company previously held €72.6m in current tax liabilities related to a major tax dispute from prior years. Although this specific tax notice was resolved positively in late 2025, future disputes could easily escalate the consolidated tax rate unexpectedly. Such tax disputes remain a key risk for the group's long-term earnings projections.

Currency translation sensitivity

5.
Stated dollar sensitivities: D'Ieteren's foreign currency translation exposure is heavily weighted toward the North American market. In H1-2026, Belron's total sales increased by 4.7% but suffered a negative currency translation effect of 3.3% mostly from the US dollar. Because North America generates 55% of Belron's sales, currency translation remains a major variable for group profitability. This high concentration means that a stronger Euro against the US dollar will dilute Belron's earnings contribution to the parent group.
6.
Translation balance sheet impact: Foreign currency translation effects also have a significant impact on D'Ieteren's consolidated balance sheet. During H1-2026, the group recorded negative translation differences of €55.7m within its other comprehensive income. This negative movement was primarily driven by unfavourable foreign exchange impacts on cash and external debt arising from a stronger USD and its effect on USD-denominated borrowings. These translation swings affect the group's reported leverage and credit metrics.
7.
Sterling translation exposures: D'Ieteren's exposure to sterling translation is smaller but remains a key driver for specific European operations. Historically, the group used average exchange rates of 1.18 euros for one GBP to translate its income statement. The group's sensitivity analysis indicated that a 10% move in the EUR/GBP pairing has a minor impact on continuing operations. Nevertheless, managing this sterling exposure is necessary to protect the group's European aftermarket margins from currency shocks.
8.
Broker exchange assumptions: Sell-side analysts closely monitor the foreign exchange assumptions that management uses to guide the market. For FY2026, Bernstein Research noted that D'Ieteren assumed a EUR/USD exchange rate of 1.17 in its adjusted PBT growth guidance. Bernstein Research currently projects D'Ieteren's adjusted EPS to reach €13.36 in FY2026 and €15.65 in FY2027. These broker projections assume that currency translation will not severely drag down Belron's growth.

Peer translation comparisons

9.

For its major currency pairs, including the Euro, USD, and Australian Dollar, Inchcape disclosed that a 1% currency movement impacts its annual adjusted PBT by £1m. This sensitivity highlights that diversified automotive distributors face highly fragmented currency translation risks across multiple regions. Consequently, Inchcape's translation risk is more spread out than D'Ieteren's highly concentrated US dollar risk.
10.
Peer hedging comparison: Inchcape manages its transactional currency exposures through forward exchange contracts to lock in purchase costs. Deutsche Bank reported in June 2024 that as Inchcape diversified, the risk of currency mismatch to group profitability lessened. However, translational FX headwinds can still reduce reported results, as seen when translational currency movements dragged down reported adjusted operating profit by 4% in prior periods. This peer context confirms that currency translation remains a major unhedged risk for international automotive distribution models.
11.
Boyd currency exposures: Boyd Group Services provides another relevant comparable peer with substantial US dollar translation exposure. Boyd's foreign exchange translation exposure relates to converting its Canadian operations' net asset values into US dollars. Since a substantial portion of Boyd's revenues and cash flows are generated in US dollars, fluctuations in the CAD/USD rate can materially affect its share price. Under this model, exchange rate translation risks impact consolidated net earnings reporting without affecting local currency cash flows.
12.
Boyd translation hedging: Boyd Group Services actively manages its transactional foreign currency risks to protect its corporate cash flows. During 2025, Boyd implemented cross-currency swaps to convert its Canadian dollar senior unsecured notes into fixed US dollar obligations. Additionally, Boyd designated $862.0m of US dollar cash as a hedge prior to closing a major US acquisition. These active hedging practices show that peers proactively insulate their capital structures from translation shocks.

Strategic translation outlook

13.
Group hedging strategies: D'Ieteren's official group policy aims to hedge the economic value of material foreign currency investments. The group implements derivative financial instruments, including debt in the currency of the investment, to limit exposure. For example, Belron utilizes cross-currency interest rate swaps to partially hedge its massive USD-denominated debt stack. These hedging activities help protect the group's net assets but do not completely eliminate translation impacts on earnings per share.
14.
Other side of the story: D'Ieteren's adjusted earnings remain highly vulnerable to unhedged translation risks that could depress group pre-tax profits during periods of Euro strengthening. While economic value is hedged, the translation of Belron's North American profits back into Euros is subject to constant foreign exchange volatility. If the Euro strengthens significantly against the US dollar, D'Ieteren's reported adjusted PBT would fall well short of broker consensus targets. This vulnerability indicates that foreign exchange translation remains unhedged for equity investors.
