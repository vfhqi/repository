Section 5 – Impact of the July 2026 Overhead-Line Law

Section summary
  1. The July 2026 overhead-line law introduces a significant shift in German transmission policy by prioritizing overhead lines for future corridors while grandfathering approved underground projects.
  2. Friedrich Vorwerk faces long-term volume risks and margin pressure in its electricity segment after 2030, necessitating a strategic resource reallocation toward natural gas and hydrogen.

Regulatory Changes and Corridor Scope

1.
Label: Accelerating policy shift: The German Bundestag approved a significant shift in transmission grid policy in July 2026 through new legislative amendments that prioritize overhead power lines over underground cables. This regulatory pivot aims to reduce construction costs by approximately €35 bn through 2045 and significantly accelerate the build-out of the transmission network. By removing the default underground cabling preference, the federal government seeks to simplify approval procedures and avoid lengthy planning bottlenecks. This structural change fundamentally alters the long-term addressable market pipeline for underground contractors.
2.
Label: Exempting ongoing corridors: The new overhead-line legislation applies exclusively to future transmission corridors and does not impact projects that are already approved or under construction. Major electricity highways like A-Nord and SuedLink retain their current underground designations and remain fully insulated from the legal shift. Friedrich Vorwerk continues to execute its multi-million euro underground cable-laying contracts on these secured corridors without any threat of retroactive conversion. This regulatory grandfathering preserves the group's near-term electricity segment revenues through the late 2020s.
3.
Label: Target future projects: The legislative amendment specifically targets future high-voltage direct-current transmission corridors that are still in early planning stages. This includes the newly added DC42 and DC42plus corridors, which are now legally mandated to be implemented as overhead lines instead of underground cable systems. The German Economy Ministry expects this technological reversal to save billions of euros on these specific corridors. Consequently, the pipeline of new underground civil works tenders after 2030 will shrink.
4.
Label: Tracking legislative progress: The Second Act Amending the Federal Requirements Plan Act received its first reading in the Bundestag in June 2026. Although the Federal Cabinet approved the draft bill in April 2026, the final parliamentary vote remains outstanding. Industry experts note that the legislative process has been slowed by intense debates regarding cost-benefit calculations. The delay in passing the law provides additional planning certainty for TSOs executing near-term projects.

Stakeholder Reactions and Opposition
