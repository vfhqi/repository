Section 10 – Pre-Mortem: Executive Summary 1 findings

The FALSE FRIEND profile has zero hard inconsistencies, explaining robust Adjusted EBITDA but negative holding-company cash flows, whereas the YES profile has 12 hard inconsistencies, making it highly unlikely to represent reality.

The NO profile recorded three inconsistencies as it cannot reconcile Allwyn's resilient lottery concession cash flows and leading digital positions with complete operational failure.

Critical inconsistencies include the EUR 456 million cash exit payment to OPAP shareholders, the EUR 1,011 million PrizePicks put option liability, and expected negative free cash flow in FY26 and FY27. Additional verifiable contradictions stem from complex minority ownership structures in key subsidiaries, the Austrian sports betting turnover tax hike to 5%, and the waived EUR 230 million parent company loan receivable.

These data points collectively demonstrate that complex minority holdings in CASAG and Betano are highly inconsistent with the YES profile's cash-generation thesis. The compound failure probability assessment reveals that multiple risk pathways possess elevated joint occurrence likelihood due to shared root causes. Furthermore, negative free cash flow compounding with heavy upfront licence fees increases parent-level default probability, with debt-funded acquisitions elevating net leverage and reducing the financial buffer to absorb tax shocks. The clustering analysis identifies that regulatory tightening, execution delays during platform migrations, and minority leakage represent the three dominant failure pathways, with consolidated group cash conversion serving as the most critical compound monitorable for tracking systemic distress, as cash extraction rates from non-wholly owned subsidiaries indicate hidden liquidity constraints.
