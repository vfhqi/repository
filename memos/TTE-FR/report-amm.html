APM Mini-Memo: TotalEnergies

Written by the Assistant Portfolio Manager, 1 October 2026. Ideas Bench rank 9 in the lists drawn from the Pool of Stocks Eligible for Middle Innings Portfolio Selection. Evidence: our card of 1 October 2026 (script ratings of 29 and 30 September, FactSet valuation of 30 September, change-forces report of 21 September, earnings review of 11 September, business-foundations digest of 29 July, master research digest); no Investment Analyst memo.

The one-line view. Neutral, working hypothesis: TotalEnergies is the lowest-cost, least-indebted European oil major with real volume growth and a well-covered dividend, but its earnings per share over the next eighteen months to three years ride on oil, gas and refining margins that are currently lifted by disruption, so the predictability you want is not there.

What the company does

TotalEnergies is a French multi-energy major with five segments: exploration and production, Integrated liquefied natural gas, Integrated Power, Refining and Chemicals, and Marketing and Services. Revenue was $182 billion in 2025 at a 20.7% margin before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation, and production grew nearly 4% to 2.53 million barrels of oil equivalent a day across about 50 countries. Exploration and production is the cash engine, at $15.6 billion of 2025 operating cash flow against $6.2 billion from refining, chemicals and marketing, $4.7 billion from liquefied natural gas and $2.6 billion from power. The shares closed at 75.7 euros on 30 September 2026, a market value of about €181 billion.

A plausible investment thesis

Real, but commodity-bound. The moat is integration plus cost: upstream operating cost below $5 per barrel of oil equivalent, a 12.2-year proved reserve life against Shell's 7.8 years, and gearing of 13.1% in the second quarter of 2026 against a peer median of 18 to 22%. The mechanism to earnings per share runs through four channels. Upstream production is targeted to grow 3% a year to 2030 from deepwater projects earning cash margins of $25 a barrel; liquefied natural gas volumes are targeted to grow 50% between 2025 and 2030; Integrated Power, after the €5.15 billion EPH flexible-generation deal closed on 29 April 2026, moves toward free cash flow breakeven in 2026 or 2027; and a $12.5 billion cost-savings plan over 2026 to 2030, plus buybacks of $1.5 billion a quarter, widen margins and shrink the share count, partly offset by the 95.4 million new shares issued for EPH.

The drivers to track are quarterly gas trading results, Brent and the European refining margin (a $1 a barrel move shifts net operating income by $0.3 billion), Strait of Hormuz access, Mozambique liquefied natural gas mobilisation toward a 2029 first cargo, the timing of the Venus deepwater investment decision in Namibia, and European power gross margins against €32 per megawatt-hour.

The Target Setup

No Investment Analyst memo exists, so there are no Bottom-up/Stock-driven ratings and this read is mine. The case matches none of the six crisply. The nearest is 'Demand-driven EPSU', where the demand is TotalEnergies' own volume growth in deepwater oil, liquefied natural gas and power rather than an end market pulling harder. It is not a cause-for-concern clearing: the shares sit 5.8% below their 52-week high and the European refining margin is near a cyclical high, not a trough.

Case Riskiness and simplicity

There is no Case Riskiness rating; the list-maker calls it "the largest and simplest energy major in the pool". The business is simple to understand; the outcome is not. Middle East blockages disrupted 15% of group production in the second quarter, easing to 8 to 10% in July, after the Strait of Hormuz was blocked again on 9 July 2026, and Hormuz-exposed production rises from 25.4% to 32.6% of the total by 2030, the highest among peers, precisely because the growth projects deliver. Add a June 2026 Paris court ruling ordering customer emissions into the company's vigilance plan, Mozambique security, and a combined Chairman and chief executive role under Patrick Pouyanné extended through the 2027 meeting.

Business quality

High for an energy company. The business-foundations report rates its confidence in foundation quality high. Return on average capital employed reached 13.9% for the twelve months to June 2026, up from 12.4%, return on equity was 15.9%, and proved reserve replacement was 120% in 2025. The weak spot is earnings quality: adjusted net income exceeded statutory net income by $2,460 million in 2025, the company rarely hedges, and Integrated liquefied natural gas missed consensus by 10% in the second quarter after an $800 million gas trading swing, even as cash flow from operations excluding working capital of $9.8 billion beat consensus.

Plausible returns and valuation

The shares trade on 8.9 times earnings (FactSet, 30 September), the 53rd percentile on its measure. The script's TSR/Valuation reading of 30 September is D, a valuation reading only with no Modal return. The consensus median target was €80.32 in July 2026, about 6% above the price, within a range of €52.88 to €93.79; Morgan Stanley cut to Equal-weight in September with a €78 target, Jefferies holds €88 and Redburn €90, while Jefferies' bear case is €47. On my arithmetic, the €0.90 quarterly dividend yields about 4.8% and buybacks add roughly 3%, so with modest volume growth a plausible return sits near the 12% a year anchor, well short of 25%. Anything more needs commodity prices to stay high.

Value chain and thematics: the pros and the cons

Pros. Upstream is the highest-margin stage of the energy chain and liquefaction a margin-earning bottleneck. Conventional discoveries fell from 55 billion barrels of oil equivalent in 2010 to under 5 billion in 2026, which rewards a low-cost holder of long-life reserves. Long-term contracts supply round-the-clock power to data-centre customers, and the list-maker sees it as a yield-and-inflation beneficiary.

Cons. Seventy percent of refining capacity is in Europe under carbon regulation; European power oversupply has pushed assumed gross margins down to €32 per megawatt-hour; low-carbon capital spending fell 40% from $5.8 billion in 2023 to $3.5 billion in 2025. The script's Thematic Fit/Momentum reading of 30 September is C, on the environment only and provisional.

The strongest counter-case

The balance-sheet improvement is a false friend and the earnings are borrowed from disruption. The pre-mortem's competing-hypotheses work left the false-friend profile with zero hard inconsistencies: the $3.34 billion fall in net debt leaned on a $1.2 billion working capital release and divestments, and reported second-quarter operating cash flow was $5,129 million. Refining margins of $13.50 a barrel against $4.70 a year earlier, and diesel margins above $100 a barrel in late August, are disruption prices. If Hormuz reopens permanently and oil falls below $50, the research models a 30% cut to buybacks and sharp downgrades. Estimates are already slipping: Redburn cut 2026 earnings per share 4% to $10.92 against $11.33 consensus, and the script's Sell-side Momentum reading of 29 September is C. A French election that unsettles the French government bond market would hit the shares regardless of where the cash is earned.
