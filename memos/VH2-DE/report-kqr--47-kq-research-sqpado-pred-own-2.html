Section 4 – Profit Recognition and Distribution Mechanics of the ARGEs

Section summary
  1. Structured joint ventures dictate how Friedrich Vorwerk recognizes and distributes its substantial infrastructure execution profits.
  2. Distinct accounting treatments under IFRS eleven create a notable lag between paper equity income and actual cash distributions.
  3. Sector peers apply identical accounting methods but experience different working capital dynamics depending on project complexity.

Profit Recognition Mechanics

1.
Consistent accounting: Friedrich Vorwerk accounts for its German working groups (ARGEs) as joint ventures under IFRS eleven standards. The group's proportionate share of ARGE results is recognized under income from equity investments in the consolidated income statement. This specific classification ensures that joint-venture profits bypass consolidated revenue and flow directly into operating profit.
2.
Systematic billing: Friedrich Vorwerk recognizes revenues from goods and services supplied directly to these joint ventures within its consolidated revenues. The underlying contract results are realized over time using the output-oriented method based on work completed. This structural arrangement allows the company to extract steady operational revenues during the early project phases.
3.
Onerous write-downs: Friedrich Vorwerk protects its balance sheet by immediately writing down expected losses on joint-venture construction contracts. If project costs are projected to exceed the contract value, the anticipated losses are recognized as expenses. This immediate provisioning ensures that unfavorable project deviations are neutralized before they impact future earnings per share.
4.
Complex balance sheet: Friedrich Vorwerk manages its joint-venture receivables and payables under trade balances. The capital paid into an ARGE is netted against withdrawals and accumulated profits or losses on the balance sheet. This accounting practice can obscure the underlying working capital trends of individual major projects.

Pronounced distribution lag: Friedrich Vorwerk faces a clear lag between recognizing equity-method income and receiving actual cash distributions from the ARGEs. In fiscal year 2025, the carrying amount of equity investments increased by €18.3 million, driven by €21.2 million in positive earnings. What is not known. The first and most material gap is the visibility of hidden project contingencies. The report documents the policy that expected losses on JV construction contracts are written down immediately when costs are projected to exceed contract value. It does not show the size of any provisions, claims or variation orders inside individual ARGEs. This matters because capital paid into an ARGE is netted against withdrawals and accumulated results, which can obscure working-capital trends of individual projects. The precedent also matters: the Brunsbüttel LNG connection suffered from soil conditions and permit delays and led to lengthy claims negotiations. A peer signal points the same way, as Strabag's losses from construction consortia rose to €40.2 million in H1 2026.

The second gap is project-level attribution. The projects are listed (ETL 179.200, ETL 182, SEL, the SuedLink lots and H2Coastlink 1), but the evidence does not allocate the €33.1 million among them. Some timing evidence is itself ambiguous. The 43 km SuedLink section is expected to complete in H2 2026, while overall corridor construction runs to 2028, so it is unclear how much JV margin has already been booked on that lot. The third gap is cash conversion and funding dependence. Gross contract assets of €839.9 million were offset by €785.9 million of advance payments, leaving net contract assets of €54.0 million.

The ARGE-level timing of distributions remains unknown. The net cash position of €262.7 million depends on prepayments and progress billing from active JV projects, so the pace of any unwind is an open question.

The fourth gap concerns demand and policy. Total project volume acquired declined 24% year-on-year in the first nine months, and the report lacks a clear view of which awards will replenish the JV pipeline. Finally, the 44.26% MBB stake at year-end 2025 exceeded the 40.6% assumption, and MBB later reduced its holding to 40.60%. The dated ownership trail should be reconciled.

Section 7 – If the Answer Differs from the Common View

Section summary
  1. Faster joint-venture earnings decay would significantly depress Friedrich Vorwerk's consolidated earnings per share and disrupt parent company capital allocation.
  2. Structural regulatory shifts and delayed hydrogen network approvals represent the primary catalysts for a divergence from current consensus expectations.

Earnings Volatility and Parent Reaction

1.
Accelerated earnings decline: Friedrich Vorwerk relies heavily on equity-method joint venture income, which contributed €33.1 million to the group's record returns in the first nine months of 2026. If these high-margin projects run off faster than the market expects, consolidated earnings per share will experience a rapid contraction. This negative outcome would occur because the group's own-contribution margins are structurally lower than those generated by the complex pipeline ARGEs. Consequently, a faster run-off would depress the medium-term organic profit outlook.
2.
Altered capital allocation: Parent company MBB SE reduced its shareholding in Friedrich Vorwerk to 40.60% during the first nine months of 2026, down from 44.26% at the end of 2025. This divestment indicates that the parent company is actively reallocating capital away from the infrastructure developer. If Vorwerk's jointventure profitability deteriorates, MBB SE might further reduce its exposure or redirect cash distributions to other subsidiaries. Thus, a faster earnings decay would limit parent company reinvestment support for Vorwerk.

Substantial valuation pressure: Financial analysts currently model highly stable organic expansion for Friedrich Vorwerk through the end of the decade. Negative cash consequences: Friedrich Vorwerk managed to expand its net cash position to €262.7 million by late September 2026, driven by excellent operational performance. However, this substantial liquidity cushion remains highly dependent on the prepayments and progress billing associated with active joint-venture projects. If these projects finish early without equivalent replacements, working capital cash flows would reverse sharply. This sudden reversal would deplete the group's net cash reserves.

Customer and Competitor Responses

5.
Delayed pipeline investments: Gasunie has pushed back the scheduled completion of the Dutch hydrogen network by three years to 2033, citing severe permitting and personnel challenges. This operator delay serves as a clear warning that clean energy pipeline projects face execution bottlenecks. If German operators like Gasunie or OGE delay similar domestic works, Vorwerk's future hydrogen order intake will stall. This bottleneck would disrupt the clean hydrogen segment ramp-up.
6.
Strained customer relationships: Transmission system operators like TenneT Germany face enormous pressure as they plan to invest €65 billion in grid infrastructure between 2025 and 2029. These operators are struggling with lengthy permitting processes and slow land acquisitions that delay approximately 60% of expansion projects. If Vorwerk fails to execute current joint-venture projects on schedule, frustrated customers might impose strict contractual penalties. This conflict would damage crucial operator framework agreements over time.
7.
Intensified peer competition: Diversified industrial services peer Bilfinger recorded a strong order backlog of 4.45 billion euros by late 2026. If major energy infrastructure tenders slow down, diversified peers and international competitors like ICOP SpA will aggressively bid for the remaining pipeline lots. This aggressive bidding behavior would drive down pricing and compress operating margins across the sector. This competitive squeeze would erode Vorwerk's high-margin pipeline positioning.

Regulatory and Structural Indicators

8.
Contrary evidence: Germany's federal network agency BNetzA is currently consulting on the potential postponement of up to 22 hydrogen infrastructure projects. This regulatory review represents a significant threat to the consensus view of rapid hydrogen grid expansion. If these projects are officially delayed, Friedrich Vorwerk's clean hydrogen backlog will not materialize as quickly as expected. This delay would severely restrict future earnings growth potential.
9.
Unfavorable policy transitions: Germany's new legislation in favor of overhead power lines represents a severe structural threat to the group's long-term order book. Because Friedrich Vorwerk currently has no exposure to overhead installations, future electricity segment growth will face severe headwinds. To maintain its organic margins, the group must reallocate its specialized labor force back toward natural gas projects. This policy transition would force expensive labor reallocations to gas.
10.
Unexpected pipeline upside: Germany's federal government is accelerating backup gas-fired power plant tenders for 12 gigawatts of capacity to be built by 2031. These new power plants must be built hydrogen-ready and will require substantial new pipeline connections. If these tenders accelerate, Friedrich Vorwerk could secure massive new high-pressure gas pipeline contracts. This windfall would offset any electricity segment decline easily.
11.
Other side of the story: Friedrich Vorwerk's dominant position in the German energy grid sector provides a strong operational buffer against localized regulatory delays. Management highlights that the group's €907.2 million own-contribution order backlog ensures high revenue visibility through the next twenty-four months. Furthermore, the strict public mandates governing critical corridors like SuedLink protect current joint-venture execution schedules from political interference. Consequently, structural grid demands will ensure steady joint-venture earnings run-off regardless of short-term policy debate.
