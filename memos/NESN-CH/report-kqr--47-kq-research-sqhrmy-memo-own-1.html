Section 3 – Nestlé Buy-Side and Investor Sentiment on Strategic Themes

Section summary
  1. Active institutional investors remain cautious about Nestlé's ability to maintain long-term dividend commitments under severe currency headwinds.
  2. Shareholders are debating whether monetizing the 20.1% L'Oréal stake is necessary to fund future share buyback programs.
  3. Turnaround comparisons with Unilever and Kraft Heinz highlight the risks of neglecting brand innovation during cost-cutting phases.

Volume Recovery and Strategic Exits

1.
Sequential volume acceleration: Institutional investors are carefully monitoring Nestlé's volume-driven growth recovery following its recent third-quarter sales report. Buy-side managers note that real internal growth reached 2.8% which represents a highly positive sequential acceleration. Many long-term shareholders traditionally view the group as a highly stable and safe bet with modest fluctuations. This volume-led recovery is expected to support future earnings per share expansion.
2.
Strategic exit challenges: Buy-side investors express mixed feelings regarding Nestlé's strategic exit from underperforming non-core categories. The company announced an agreement to sell its mainstream vitamins business for $1.0 billion in September. However, former employees point out that Nestlé has struggled to find buyers willing to pay acceptable valuations for these health assets without forcing the group to recognize massive losses. This exit demonstrates a clear shift away from previous aggressive health segment expansions.
3.
Portfolio optimization progress: The planned Water and Premium Beverages joint venture with Platinum Equity represents a major portfolio transformation step. Analysts at Rothschild and Co Redburn expect the transaction to be neutral to earnings and cash generation initially. However, the move is projected to improve long-term organic sales growth and lift Nestlé's group operating margin by approximately 30 basis points after its completion. This strategic focus could help Nestlé accelerate its underlying organic sales growth.
4.
Russia deconsolidation headwinds: Investors are analyzing the financial impact of Nestlé's decision to deconsolidate its Russian operations at the end of September. Analysts at Morgan Stanley projected that the absence of Russia will create a meaningful 20 basis point drag on Nestlé's operating margins. Furthermore, this deconsolidation will increase group net debt by approximately CHF 0.9 billion. This structural exit reduces the cash generation that would otherwise support Nestlé's earnings per share.

Structural Peer Transformation Comparisons

11.
Health transition comparisons: Nestlé's strategic attempt to shift its business mix toward nutrition and consumer health has faced growing investor scrutiny. Industry executives note that while Nestlé entered the health space earlier than peers, it has recently lost its competitive advantages in nutrition and wellness. Former officers point out that Unilever has historically been more cautious about undertaking high-risk acquisitions in this category. This strategic divergence shows that other consumer giants preferred organic expansion over expensive wellness acquisitions.
12.
Unilever turnaround comparisons: Institutional investors often compare Nestlé's current operational turnaround with historic restructurings at its close peers. Industry experts highlight Unilever as a leader in business transformation due to its successful shift toward high-margin personal care and premium prestige brands. This peer template was driven by rebooting specific category strategies and optimizing global supply chain assets. This operational consistency is what Nestlé is currently trying to replicate.
13.
Kraft Heinz restructuring failures: The buy-side remains cautious about aggressive cost-focused restructuring templates that neglect brand innovation. Former directors at Kraft Heinz recall that early financial improvements under private equity depended almost entirely on severe cost-cutting measures. This aggressive cost strategy left the brand highly vulnerable when consumer preferences shifted away from processed foods. Nestlé's new leadership is trying to avoid these pitfalls by balancing cost savings with marketing reinvestments.
14.
Other side of the story: While many investors are optimistic about Nestlé's back-to-basics strategy, some buy-side commentators warn that the company is facing structural volume stagnation. Former directors suggest that Nestlé's massive scale makes it increasingly difficult to sustainably outpace global gross domestic product expansion. If the core categories suffer from structural headwinds, the high dividend payout will leave the group with very little capital flexibility to invest in premium offerings. Under these stagnation conditions, Nestlé risks becoming a low-growth value trap.
