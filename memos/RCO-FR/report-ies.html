Implications — Remy Cointreau (RCO-FR): the 25 November 2026 half-year results against the existing case

These results weaken Viewforth's Park case on Remy Cointreau moderately: they do not disprove its central false-friend diagnosis, and the composition of the beat fits that diagnosis closely, but they open two of the case's three progression gates ahead of schedule, deliver the guidance increase the case itself named as its cleanest corporate-change signal, and turn one Headline Rating.

What changed

Remy Cointreau reported first-half FY27 results (six months to 30 September 2026) on 25 November 2026.

  • Organic sales grew 2.9%, in line with consensus. Current operating profit grew 7.8% organically against a 4.3% consensus; adjusted current operating profit of EUR 127m was 9.0% ahead. The current operating margin rose 260 basis points to 24.8% against 22.8% expected. Adjusted net profit was 14.5% above consensus; adjusted EPS was EUR 1.45 for the half.
  • The beat was margin, not sales, and inside the margin it was advertising. Gross margin fell 150 basis points; advertising and promotion fell 300 basis points; overheads rose 40 basis points. Management guided second-half profitability below first-half levels as brand investment steps up.
  • Cognac current operating profit grew 19.8% organically against 8.0% consensus, with a 34.1% margin against 28.9%, helped by a 390 basis point cut in divisional marketing. Cognac sales grew 10.3% organically on a 16.0% volume increase; price-mix was negative 5.7%. Liqueurs and Spirits operating profit fell 29.5% organically against a consensus decline of 9.2%; its margin fell 440 basis points to 11.9%.
  • Full-year guidance was raised from "slight" to "solid" organic improvement in the current operating margin, net of a EUR 5m China tariff impact, previously guided at EUR 20m. Third-quarter organic sales are guided roughly flat, accelerating in the fourth quarter. FX is now guided as a FY27 tailwind of EUR 15m to sales and EUR 3m to EUR 5m to operating profit.
  • Net debt fell EUR 43.8m to EUR 646.6m at 30 September 2026; net debt to EBITDA fell to 2.87x from 3.22x in March 2026 excluding the hybrid, 2.9x reported against 3.1x consensus. A EUR 250m hybrid bond at 6.75% was issued on 30 September 2026 and is accounted as equity; a EUR 75m private placement was redeemed early on 18 November 2026. The March 2027 leverage target was tightened to below 3.2x from 3.5x, below 2.7x with the hybrid as equity. First-half free cash flow was EUR 33.5m against negative EUR 16.6m a year earlier.
  • RC Forward was quantified: EUR 100m of gross current operating profit gains by FY29, phased 20% in FY27, 50% in FY28, 30% in FY29. EUR 50m comes from procurement and organisational simplification, including a 12% to 15% headcount reduction from the March 2026 base, with international reductions largely complete and a French voluntary plan open to the end of January 2027; EUR 50m from top-line projects, including doubling Global Travel Retail and Emerging Markets sales by FY29 and the April 2027 US launch of Remy 2.0. Non-recurring costs of up to EUR 50m fall mostly in FY27. Cumulative organic margin expansion of 300 to 400 basis points is targeted by FY29 and a margin above 22% by FY32; gross margin is guided flat at best over five years.
  • Demand stayed split. US distributor inventories held at about four months; US Cognac depletions improved from mid-single-digit declines in the first quarter to low-single-digit declines in the second, still negative. Mainland China Cognac sales grew mid-teens in the second quarter, but Mid-Autumn Festival depletions fell short and wholesalers entered the third quarter with elevated inventories requiring destocking. Global Travel Retail grew 47.3% organically and Emerging Markets 46.3%.
  • Regulation moved both ways. The Chinese price undertaking is in force and avoids the 38.1% provisional duty at the cost of a minimum import price. On 25 November 2026 the US Trade Representative proposed raising tariffs on EU distilled spirits from 10% to 15%; Remy imports about 90% of its US sales from Europe.
  • Sell-side response: Jefferies (Buy) raised estimates. Bernstein (Market-Perform) cut FY27 adjusted EPS 3.1% for advertising phasing, raised FY28 2.4%, lifted its target to EUR 55 from EUR 54 and judged the rally had already priced the results; its target sits below the EUR 58.15 close of 25 November 2026. Rothschild & Co Redburn (Sell) kept a 20% margin forecast for FY32 against the company's at least 22% and ROIC at a two-decade low of 4% in FY27, but acknowledged the low end of the new margin guidance implies a 14% upgrade to FY29 adjusted EPS, 18% with FX. Morgan Stanley was Underweight with a EUR 42 target on 16 November 2026, before the results.
  • The shares were up 28.7% over twelve months at 25 November 2026. The dashboard shows EUR 57.45 on 26 November 2026, 32.6% above the 200-day average, relative strength at the 96th percentile, sell-side EPS revisions of +7%, +7% and +9% over one, three and six months, target price revisions of +11% over one month, a 24% buy share, a consensus target of EUR 50.51, and a P/E of 25.6x at the 47th percentile of the stock's own ten-year range.

The challenge

The case's load-bearing finding is a false friend: a margin recovery produced by advertising cuts and shipments running ahead of depletions, masking structural decline in dark spirits. This print has exactly that shape. The 260 basis point margin gain is more than accounted for by a 300 basis point advertising cut; Cognac volume rose 16.0% while US depletions still fell and Chinese wholesalers over-stocked into a festival that disappointed. We would be wrong to pretend the evidence contradicts the case. The question is whether the same evidence admits readings the case never considered. We think it admits three, and none is easy to dismiss.

First reading: the earnings path no longer runs through demand, so the case is gating on the wrong variable. The case parks until Nielsen US depletions grow for two consecutive quarters. That made sense when recovery meant volume. RC Forward as now specified puts EUR 50m of the EUR 100m under direct management control, and the most bearish house on the street concedes the low end of the new guidance implies a 14% FY29 EPS upgrade. Leverage at 2.87x and positive free cash flow mean the balance sheet no longer needs volume to stay safe. On this reading Remy can deliver FY28 to FY29 EPS growth with flat US depletions; the case's first criterion could stay unmet for two years while the stock compounds; and the case's own fourth condition, advertising reinvested rather than held flat, would be satisfied by the guided second-half step-up. The criteria would be met in an order the case did not anticipate. What would have to be true: overheads fall by the targeted 4 percentage points of sales; gross margin holds flat rather than sliding as accessible formats launch; the second-half margin lands only modestly below 24.8% despite higher advertising; the French voluntary plan fills. The case's complaint that the thesis carries too many drivers would also soften, because this reading has one driver, and it is measurable.

Second reading: this is not a false friend but a deliberate de-premiumisation, and the market is paying a premium multiple for a lower-return company. The case framed Remy as a cyclical luxury compounder mid-downcycle, and its false friend is a mask that eventually slips. The print supports something different. Management has told the market there will be no return to 2022 premiumisation; that gross margin will be flat or slightly down for five years because accessible innovations dilute mix; that Cognac volume is needed to absorb fixed costs; and that smaller formats and an entry-level Remy 2.0 are the recruitment tools. Cognac price-mix of negative 5.7% on 16.0% volume growth, Cointreau's six consecutive quarters of negative US price-mix, and a Liqueurs and Spirits margin of 11.9% are consistent with a business trading mix for volume as policy, not as phasing. If this is right the case is wrong in a way it does not currently hold: not "the recovery is fake" but "the recovery is real and it is a recovery into a structurally lower gross-margin, cost-led spirits company," whose EUR 1,900.4m of aging eaux-de-vie at 31 March 2026 was laid down for a premium model, in an industry holding nearly ten years of stocks. The "high quality" and "solid quality" qualifiers in the case's setups and the inventory floor behind its value-trap read would both need re-examining. What would have to be true: price-mix stays near or below negative 5.7% while volumes grow; gross margin falls beyond the guided flat; Louis XIII and XO lose ground to the accessible tier; eaux-de-vie trade prices soften. This reading is bearish on the business and bullish on the stock for some time, which is the combination the case is least equipped to handle.

Third reading: the trough-on-trough tests the case failed may now be closer to passing than it graded. The case failed the cyclical-turn setup on two tests: competitors have not calmed and have not retrenched. The review's evidence can be read the other way. Pernod Ricard took a 25% organic sales decline in China in its first quarter to clear Martell inventories; Hennessy's mid-single-digit VS price cuts are a rollback of post-pandemic increases already made, not an escalation in progress; Campari is absorbing Courvoisier with diluted margins through FY27. Remy itself has cut eaux-de-vie purchases 20% on top of 25% to 45% in March 2025, targets a 12% to 15% headcount reduction against the 4% the case called modest, and has held US inventories at four months. US shipments fell 51% between FY20 and FY25 while depletions fell 24%, on the case's own figures; the review carries a sell-side estimate that shipments could recover 36% as they converge on depletions. With the dollar as a tailwind and easy comparatives, this is the mechanical rebound the case acknowledged as a technical tailwind and then discounted. What would have to be true: third-quarter organic sales at least flat as guided; China third-quarter destocking a clearance, not a new round; US depletions positive through the holiday season; Hennessy pricing stable from here. The evidence against is real: Mid-Autumn depletions missed, US Cognac category volumes still fall at double-digit rates, and a US tariff rise is on the table. But the case graded these tests F, and the print's facts are more consistent with D.

A fourth reading, that the half is a phasing beat that reverses in the second half, is the case's own reading and the review's main counter-argument. We note it; we do not count it as a challenge.

What would settle it

  • The second Earnings Review, four working days on. It will carry the full set of post-results broker estimate changes and targets, so the sell-side momentum reading and the consensus EPS and target will be re-based on the new guidance, and it will show whether the 14% to 18% FY29 EPS upgrade Rothschild & Co Redburn described as implied is actually taken into consensus. It will not carry any new company data: no third-quarter sales, no second-half margin, no depletions, no headcount outcome.
  • Third-quarter sales, guided roughly flat organic. Settles the third reading's near-term leg and the China destocking question: a clear miss against flat supports the case; flat with China inventories cleared supports the rebound reading.
  • Second-half margin against 24.8%. The decisive test between the first reading and the case's phasing view; the review's own threshold is a decline that is only modest despite the advertising step-up. Known with full-year results.
  • Cognac price-mix against negative 5.7%, with Remy 2.0 launching in April 2027. Improvement toward negative 3% with positive volume argues against the de-premiumisation reading; deterioration confirms it. The company reports price-mix at divisional level and has not committed to disclosing Remy 2.0 separately, so the mix effect of the launch is a question nothing is committed to answer.
  • US depletions through the holiday season, from Nielsen scanner data rather than company shipments: the case's first gate, and the false friend's cleanest disconfirming test, three consecutive quarters of sequential volume growth.
  • Headcount and overhead delivery. The French voluntary plan closes at the end of January 2027; overheads as a share of sales against the 4 percentage point target are the test of the first reading.
  • The US tariff proposal of 25 November 2026. No decision date is given in the evidence we hold; its outcome bears on E7.RA3 and E11.RA4 either way.
  • Not committed by the company: a shipments-to-depletions ratio, distributor days of cover by market, and advertising as a share of sales by half. These are the case's own tripwires for the false friend, and nothing on the calendar will answer them unless the company chooses to.
