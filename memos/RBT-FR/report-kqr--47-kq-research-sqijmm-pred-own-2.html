Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Robertet's massive capital deployment across five global sites and the UNIFY IT system will dictate its earnings trajectory through FY2028 by elevating near-term depreciation and testing operational efficiency.

Defining the core inquiry

1.
Strategic query: "What will Robertet SA's (RBT-FR) depreciation and amortisation be in FY2026-FY2028, and how quickly will its new and expanded sites (Karawang in Indonesia, India via Sonarome, Mexico, China, the Grasse modernisation) and the UNIFY information system move from adding cost to adding operating leverage: what are the commissioning dates, capacities, utilisation rates and break-even volumes, how much UNIFY spending is capitalised and over what life, and how did depreciation and margins behave at comparable mid-sized flavour and fragrance houses (Takasago, T. Hasegawa, Sensient, Treatt) after similar capex step-ups?"
2.
Significant investment: Robertet SA's extensive capital expenditure program, which reached peak levels in late FY2025, is poised to dramatically alter its cost structure through elevated depreciation charges. Analyst forecasts from Bernstein Research indicate that high capital expenditures from the second half of FY2025 will begin flowing through as substantial additional amortization in FY2026 because these assets did not contribute full-year depreciation in the prior period. Understanding this transition is critical for investors because the timing of site commissioning directly determines when these fixed costs hit the income statement and press earnings per share.
3.
Operational gearing: Closely related questions to Robertet SA's capital step-up center on how quickly volume growth can offset the new fixed costs from expansions like Karawang and Sonarome. The company's future operating leverage depends heavily on whether these new facilities can secure local customer wins to achieve break-even utilization rates. This research is constructed using a total of 104 relevant sources, covering 5 distinct companies and 4 comparable past case studies of capital step-ups.
4.
Projected trajectory: Bernstein Research expects that a country-by-country forecasting exercise will provide better visibility on customer pipelines and expected activity through FY2028. If volume growth lags behind capacity additions, the associated depreciation charges could severely squeeze operating income and operating margins. This makes tracking the specific break-even volumes and utilization rates across the five expanded sites essential to assessing Robertet's future profitability.

Analysing peers and contrary risks

5.
Peer comparison: Rothschild & Co Redburn notes that Sensient Technologies' operating gearing in its Color division will be more muted due to incremental depreciation on new equipment being ramped up throughout FY2027 and FY2028. This comparable case shows that the lag between high capital spending and margin expansion can be prolonged as depreciation increases. Such peer precedents illustrate how Robertet SA's own earnings could be depressed if its new asset base is not utilized rapidly.
6.
Precedent analysis: Jefferies Research reports that Treatt's net operating profit margin of 12% to 13% reflected the full-year depreciation of its new UK facility alongside stable administration costs. This historical precedent suggests that mid-sized fragrance houses face a temporary margin squeeze immediately following a period of heavy capital investment. Investors must weigh this peer behavior when projecting Robertet SA's potential for margin expansion as its industrial footprint grows.
7.
Contrary evidence: Bernstein Research reports that Robertet SA's medium-term target of achieving a 20% EBITDA margin by 2030 may actually be conservative compared to the 20.6% margin already achieved in FY2025. This indicates that the company might not suffer from a prolonged margin drag, as strong organic growth and favorable purchasing terms can offset the incremental depreciation from capital step-ups. If these positive operational dynamics persist, the transition to adding operating leverage could occur much faster than historical peer precedents suggest.
8.
Other side of the story: Jefferies Research notes that Robertet SA's organic sales growth guidance for FY2026 was revised downward to 3% to 5% from the previous target of approximately 5%. This slower organic growth momentum suggests that moving from adding cost to adding operating leverage may take longer than management initially anticipated. If demand remains soft in key delivery regions, the elevated depreciation from the company's capital step-up will continue to depress earnings per share through FY2028.
