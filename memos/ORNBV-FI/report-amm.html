APM Mini-Memo: Orion

The one-line view. Orion's earnings are rising mechanically on Nubeqa royalties, and the return from today's price depends on its own pipeline proving a second leg, with the first read-out now due a year early.

What the company does

Orion is a Finnish pharmaceutical company that has become, in effect, a global oncology royalty business. Its prostate cancer drug Nubeqa is sold by Bayer, and Orion earns a royalty that rises in tiers with Bayer's sales; about four fifths of operating profit now comes from that one stream, and Bayer is 43 percent of sales. Around the royalty sit older businesses: generics and consumer health with 553 million euros of 2025 revenue, branded products with 315 million, animal health with 141 million and the Fermion ingredients unit with 69 million. In 2025 the group made 1,890 million euros of revenue at a 33 percent operating margin, and earnings per share of 3.56 euros, up from 1.54 euros two years earlier.

A plausible investment thesis

The story is a mechanical earnings step-up that the sell-side has under-modelled eight times in a row. Bayer guides Nubeqa sales up about 50 percent in 2026, and because the royalty tiers reset each calendar year and rise with cumulative sales, the top tier is reached earlier every year and Orion's average royalty rate climbs from about 25 percent towards its 30 percent ceiling. The top tier was reached in the second quarter of 2026, royalties rose 77 percent in the first quarter, and management has beaten every initial guidance range for two years, raising 2026 guidance twice to 2,000 to 2,100 million euros of sales and 650 to 750 million of operating profit.

The transmission to earnings per share is one division. The Investment Analyst's modal path has earnings per share rising from 3.56 euros in 2025 to 4.09, 4.81 and 5.40 euros in 2026 to 2028, about 15 percent a year and 6 percent above consensus by 2028, before slowing to 6 to 7 percent a year as a United States price cut lands in 2029. Consensus 2028 earnings per share rose 37 percent in twelve months, and every visible broker raised after the second quarter.

The second leg is the pipeline, and its clock has moved forward. The memo priced the company's own oncology pipeline at nothing, and its trigger for progressing the case was an event that changes the pipeline odds; the weekly Lists judgement of 30 October records that the prostate cancer read-out is arriving a year early. The drivers to track are the weekly United States prescription data for Nubeqa, Nubeqa's United States net price once a generic enters its class in August 2027, and that read-out.

The Target Setup

The Target Setup is 'Delivering - Serial Acquirer - Mechanical EPS Upgrader', on its mechanical upgrader leg, which the Investment Analyst's memo of 17 September rated B for Setups' Fit/Crispness. The case matches crisply on the record of upgrades and on quality, and fairly on the mis-modelled line, because the within-year tier step-up is now three quarters spent and the remaining consensus gap is a judgement about class pricing rather than a model error. 'Demand-driven EPSU' describes what has happened but fails its sector-wide test, since the demand is one drug's adoption curve inside a three-brand class.

Case Riskiness and simplicity

The case is crisp and not robust, and the Investment Analyst rated Case Riskiness C on 17 September. Simplicity is high because the case turns on one drug's volume and price, tracked weekly, with a one-step transmission to earnings per share and a conservative guider. The risk is concentration with dates on it: one customer at 43 percent of sales, a generic rival in the class from August 2027 with payers already reported to be resisting Nubeqa's premium, a United States price cut of about a quarter from 2029, and loss of United States exclusivity in 2033, which the rest of the business, earning perhaps 80 million euros a year before tax, cannot cushion. What needs to go right is that Nubeqa prescriptions keep growing through the generic entry and that the pipeline proves a second product before 2033; the pipeline has failed three times outside oncology in two years, so the read-out carries real weight. What to track is the weekly prescription series and the read-out itself.

Business quality

Quality is top-decile on the numbers and borrowed on the moat. The adjusted operating margin of 33 percent and a post-tax return on invested capital of 37 percent on one broker's definition sit in the sector's top decile, and the Investment Analyst rated Foundations' Robustness B on 17 September. Nubeqa's advantage is tolerability rather than survival, and clinicians confirm it in their own words, but Bayer sells the drug, sets its price and negotiates with United States payers without Orion in the room. Capital allocation is the weak point, with about 2 billion euros of firepower unused, and Orion's articles cap any shareholder at 5 percent of the votes, so no bid premium underpins the shares.

Plausible returns and valuation

The return grade is F, and the grade is dated. At 100 euros on 30 October the shares trade at 19.2 times earnings, the 34th percentile of their own ten-year range, against 15.6 times and the 8th percentile when the memo was written at 81.65 euros on 17 September. The memo's modal path gave 9 to 11 percent a year over eighteen months from 83 euros; re-based to 100 euros on 30 October, the same path returns about minus 3 percent a year over eighteen months and about nil over three years, hence the Investment Analyst's TSR/Valuation rating of F, which disagrees by two grades with the valuation pin of B. The market has already paid most of the re-rating towards the five-year median of 18.5 times that the memo gave one-in-five odds. A return towards Richard's 20 percent bar now needs either the earnings path to be too low, which eight consecutive beats make plausible, or the early read-out to add a pipeline leg the path gives nothing for; the Portfolio Fitness for Fighting call has asked the Investment Analyst to re-base the path, and until then any return above the modal one is not in our files.

Value chain and thematics: the pros and the cons

The pros. Bayer is a strong, aligned partner growing the drug about 50 percent a year towards peak sales above 4.7 billion euros, the class is a three-brand oligopoly, and the Sector Digest of 22 October names Orion among the purest plays on specialist launches lifting mid-cap margins, a story it rates big and early. The weekly Thematic Fit/Momentum rating of 30 October is B, the weekly Technical Momentum rating of 30 October is B with the shares at a 52-week high in a proven uptrend, and the weekly Sell-side Momentum rating of 28 October is B.

The cons. Payers and regulators are moving against branded prostate cancer drugs on a timetable, and the 2027 to 2032 patent expiries are a story the Sector Digest rates big but no longer early. The Sector Digest also warns that once the market values a story the share price needs it delivered, and Orion has risen 38 percent over twelve months to 22 October against 15 percent for its mid-cap specialty peers, so the market now values this one.

In short

  • Earnings are rising mechanically and predictably: Nubeqa royalties climb towards their 30 percent ceiling, and the modal path gives about 15 percent a year of earnings per share growth to 2028
  • The price has caught up with the file: at 100 euros and 19.2 times earnings, the re-based modal return is nil to negative and the TSR/Valuation grade is F
  • The pipeline is the swing factor, and its clock has moved forward: the prostate cancer read-out named as the memo's trigger now arrives a year early
  • The risk is dated concentration: one partner, a generic in the class in 2027, a United States price cut in 2029 and exclusivity ending in 2033
  • The next step is the Investment Analyst's re-based path: the case earns Richard's bar only if the earnings path is too low or the read-out adds a second leg
