EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Whitbread is a UK-centred owner-operator in a deliberate, self-directed transition. The report does not publish an absolute consensus EPS figure for this year or next, so the direction of revisions is the best available evidence. Sell-side analysts upgraded adjusted earnings forecasts by about 11.5% on average after the UK government's business rates freeze. That restored expectations that had been severely downgraded since late 2025.

The rates relief therefore looks largely priced into consensus already. Drivers that could push EPS above consensus are the efficiency programme, which delivered £39 mm in the first half against a £60 mm full-year target after £83 mm the prior year, and German RevPAR growth of 9.5% in early Q3. Drivers pointing below consensus are a refinancing of maturing bonds that adds roughly £11 mm of annual interest, about £100 mm of added fixed lease costs from sale-and-leasebacks, and earnings momentum that remains back-end loaded.

The recovery therefore depends more on execution than on market tailwinds. & Co Redburn The scale of change is large. Inside the company, the executive team is stable: CEO Dominic Paul and CFO Hemant Patel remain in post. The board has been refreshed: Christine Hodgson became Chair in September 2025, and Jonathan Howell became Audit Committee Chair in January 2026. Strategy has been reset. The five- year plan was updated in April 2026 to prioritize margin expansion over volume. Gross capex is cut by £1 bn to £2.5 bn. The plan recycles £1.5 bn of freehold property and lowers freehold ownership from 50% to 30–40%. The German room target was trimmed to 18,000 from 20,000.

Externally, business rates are expected to be £45 mm to £60 mm lower by FY29, the minimum wage rose 3.9%, and payroll tax relief for younger workers adds a cost offset. - Earnings Calls latest results were better than they look on the surface. Statutory revenue rose 2.0% to £1,572 mm. Adjusted profit before tax fell 9.1% to £287 mm but beat consensus of £276 mm by 4.1%, and adjusted EPS of 125.1p beat the 122.0p consensus by 2.5% despite a 6.4% year-on-year decline.

Statutory figures were distorted: statutory profit before tax fell 62% to £109 mm as adjusting items rose to £178 mm from £28 mm. Management maintained its full-year view, and UK RevPAR growth reached 3.2% in early Q3. Cash conversion was weaker: adjusted operating cash flow declined to £388.9 mm from £406.4 mm, and lease-adjusted leverage rose to 3.4x from 3.2x against an internal limit of 3.5x.

The main earnings drivers, ranked by impact, interact. First is the Accelerating Growth Plan: management guides to a £30–40 mm profit-before-tax uplift in FY28 versus FY26, and plausibility is high. Second is Germany: £15–25 mm of incremental profit in FY28, though plausibility is medium given low brand awareness. Third is UK network expansion: 8,000 new UK rooms by FY31, with high plausibility. Fourth is RevPAR and pricing: every 1% change in UK RevPAR moves profit before tax by roughly £16.5–17 mm, and plausibility is medium. Fifth is business rates relief: a £30 mm tailwind in FY28, offset by a possible £65 mm cliff edge in FY30. Property recycling, cost efficiency and refinancing costs act as enabling or offsetting factors. The £1.5 bn of property proceeds is required to fund all growth investment, so the drivers do not succeed independently. The combined probability of all drivers succeeding is estimated at medium, around 50%. Management guidance has been reliable in the short term but is back-end loaded in the medium term.

The firsthalf profit beat of £287 mm against £276 mm fits a pattern of conservative planning, and the CEO has described conservative assumptions as appropriate given limited forward visibility. The caveat is that holding estimates had become unusual over the past year, with upward revisions arriving only after the October 2026 results. Management also changed the plan during the period. Broker positioning is cautious and split.

Morgan Stanley is the constructive outlier with Overweight ratings. Deutsche Bank, Jefferies and Redburn sit at Hold or Neutral, and Bernstein moved up from Underperform to Market-Perform. Dissenters focus on a 3% profit-before-tax decline for each 1% fall in RevPAR and on cash conversion pressure in an asset-heavy model. Against peers, Whitbread leads its closest domestic rival: EBITDAR rose 21% from FY23 to FY26 while Travelodge's fell 2%.

Against global franchisors it is structurally weaker. It earns over 90% of revenue in the UK, and its EBIT margins of 23–25% compare with 60%+ for assetlight peers. & Co Redburn The critical outside forces are cyclical and fiscal rather than structural. 53% to 46% of the budget segment, which supports pricing. On the other side, 77% of lease liabilities carry inflation-linked reviews, AI-driven search could introduce new intermediary fees, and German room rates fell 5% in local currency. Activist pressure has been resisted but not resolved. Corvex built a stake of about 7%. Three risks matter most. The first is operating leverage to a consumer or economic downturn, which the report identifies as the primary single point of failure. The second is funding and leverage: Fitch downgraded the rating to BBB-, and slower property recycling would force a choice between restricting capex and raising leverage. The third is simultaneous execution of restaurant conversions, German scaling and property recycling, a concern raised by a former AccorHotels executive. The business rates cliff edge compounds the first two.
