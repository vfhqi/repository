Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. LSEG's pivot to recurring high-margin data subscriptions represents a structural shift that could reduce its equity risk premium and drive double-digit earnings growth.

Structural Significance of Pivot

1.
Key Question: "how plausible is the change now expected for this company, given its record of delivery and the strengths the business demonstrably has, rather than strengths it would need to acquire?"
2.
Strategic evolution: LSEG has transformed from a transaction-led equities exchange into a global financial data and clearing powerhouse. According to a former corporate development executive at LSEG in a transcript dated March 2026, the group historically focused on London equities but transformed its model through major acquisitions like LCH and FTSE Russell. This structural pivot shifts earnings away from transaction volume dependency, which ultimately supports double-digit earnings growth over the medium term.
3.
Accelerating growth: LSEG's high-quality subscription businesses have demonstrated resilient and accelerating organic growth. Deutsche Bank reported on 13 November 2026 that aggregate subscription growth across Data and Analytics, FTSE Russell, and Risk Intelligence reached a new high of 6.6% in the third quarter of 2026. This acceleration indicates that LSEG is successfully pivoting toward highly predictable, recurring revenues, which reduces cyclical earnings volatility and enhances the defensibility of the business.
4.
Predictable pricing: LSEG's shift to enterprise agreements provides stable pricing power that supports its cost of capital. An expert from Citi noted in January 2026 that master agreements typically limit annual price increases to between 0% and 3% but secure long-term capital commitments. This predictable revenue structure helps lower LSEG's cost of equity by reducing long-term cash flow uncertainty for investors.
5.
Multiple expansion: LSEG's successful pivot to recurring data subscriptions could drive a significant re-rating of its valuation multiples. Jefferies reported on 5 October 2026 that LSEG trades at a free cash flow yield nearly 1 percentage point higher than its peer FactSet. This valuation discount appears excessive given that LSEG has best-in-class global franchises, which suggests that proving its subscription growth can narrow the peer valuation gap over time.

Section 4 – Strategic Integration and Plausibility of the Octus Acquisition

Section summary
  1. LSEG's US$3.535 billion acquisition of Octus is strategically sound but carries near-term earnings neutrality due to high debt-funding costs.
  2. Integrating private credit data introduces execution complexity, as demonstrated by historical data synchronization and technology migration bottlenecks across global peers.
  3. Regulatory decisions regarding European clearing equivalence present structural volume risks to LSEG's post-trade franchises over the medium term.
  4. Falling policy rates put downward pressure on collateral yields, while bank vendor consolidation and technological transitions reshape the data consumption landscape.

Rothschild and Co Redburn reported on 22 October 2026 that the US$3.535 billion cash consideration implies multiples of approximately 11.80x 2026 revenues and 29x EBITDA. This rich pricing means LSEG must achieve double-digit organic growth and margin improvements to justify the investment and avoid diluting group returns.
2.
High funding: LSEG's reliance on debt funding for the Octus acquisition introduces a substantial near-term interest expense drag that delays bottom-line contribution. Jefferies reported on 22 October 2026 that financing the deal with external debt at an assumed 5.50% funding cost will create roughly US$180 million of annual interest expense in 2028. This heavy financing burden effectively broadly matches Octus's profit contribution, rendering the transaction neutral to adjusted earnings per share until fiscal year 2029.
3.
Manageable leverage: LSEG's balance sheet is projected to remain resilient despite the combined leverage impact of multiple concurrent capital allocation initiatives. Rothschild and Co Redburn reported on 22 October 2026 that completing both the Octus and Trumid deals will push pro-forma leverage to the upper end of its 1.50x to 2.50x target range by the end of 2027.
