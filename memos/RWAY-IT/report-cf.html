EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Rai Way maintains highly stable senior leadership under CEO Roberto Cecatto, appointed April 2023, alongside CFO Adalberto Pellegrino and an experienced executive team including Giancarlo Benucci and Andrea Moretti, though the Board of Directors term expires with FY2025 financial statement approval, introducing potential governance transition risks in 2026. This orderly succession from former CEO Aldo Mancino was designed to strengthen strategic coordination with majority stakeholder RAI, which exercises dominant management and coordination influence through Chairman Giuseppe Pasciucco. Internal cultural metrics demonstrate sustained excellence, including nine consecutive years of Top Employers certification, 28.60% year-on-year increase in training hours during 2025, and comprehensive employee satisfaction monitoring covering 100% of the workforce.

The strategic pivot centers on digital diversification while defending core broadcast cash flows. Management unveiled a 2024-27 industrial plan targeting high-growth digital infrastructure and data centers, funded by stable broadcast revenues and supported by a €250 million development capex program over four years. The organizational structure was revamped into three distinct business units in 2024: media distribution managing broadcasting networks, infrastructure management optimizing real estate and power assets including solar development, and a dedicated datacenter division focused exclusively on rollout execution.

Management targets a 4% revenue and Adjusted EBITDA compound annual growth rate through 2027, though the ambitious plan includes up to 18 edge data centers and a centralized 35 megawatt hyperscale facility near Rome.

The most transformative strategic option remains industrial consolidation with domestic peer EI Towers. Evaluating potential aggregation with EI Towers remains the primary industrial consolidation option, with the memorandum of understanding between reference shareholders extended to June 15, 2026. The combined entity leverage would rise to approximately 2.0x Net Debt/EBITDA, well below peer medians, while Morgan Stanley estimated potential synergy value of €320 million to €370 million net present value. However, execution plausibility remains low, dependent on complex state broadcaster and antitrust clearances.

External forces present a mixed backdrop for the investment case. Core broadcast demand remains highly predictable through long-term contracts, with government service requirements mandating 100% population coverage for public broadcasting, though customer take-up for regional edge data centers may prove more gradual than initially expected. While Inwit and Cellnex dominate the Italian market with 45% and 40% market shares respectively, Rai Way operates as the sole operator of broadcasting networks carrying RAI's public signals, providing structural insulation from telecom-centric competitors.

Italian mobile operators recently formed a joint venture to build up to 6,000 alternative towers, and Inwit faces severe customer conflict as Swisscom and Vodafone seek to terminate master service agreements. Technological disruption from 5G acts as a long-term catalyst, requiring additional points of presence and increasing the size of mobile equipment installed on macro towers. Regulatory constraints remain stringent, with government decrees allowing RAI to reduce its stake only down to 30% and anti-trust guidelines restricting Rai Way from exceeding five multiplexes.

Inflation protection mechanisms through CPI-linked escalators provide robust organic protection during inflationary cycles, though higher interest rates exert severe valuation multiples pressure, partially offset by Rai Way's favorable 2025e FCF yield of 8.20% compared to Inwit at 7.50%.

The financial profile exhibits exceptional margin discipline but modest growth. Rai Way recorded core revenue growth of 2.6% year on year in Q1 2026, while achieving an outstanding Adjusted EBITDA margin of 67.2% in fiscal year 2024, up from 66.3% in fiscal year 2023. However, Morgan Stanley expects revenue growth to trend around 3.0% annually in the near term, lagging faster-growing peer Cellnex projected to deliver 7.0% annual revenue growth.

Balance sheet leverage remains ultra-conservative at 0.6x Net Debt to EBITDA in December 2023, with management targeting expansion to 1.4x by 2027 to fund diversification while remaining strictly below 3.0x, starkly contrasting major peer Cellnex carrying a substantial 7.1x Net Debt to EBITDAaL ratio and Inwit at 5.7x. Cash conversion remains robust at approximately 97.7% during seasonal peaks, with free cash flow to equity reaching €94 million over the first nine months of 2025 and Morgan Stanley estimating a 2026e RLFCF yield of 8.5% outperforming Inwit's 7.4%.

Corporate policy mandates distributing 100% of generated net income as ordinary dividends, with a proposed €0.33 per share in fiscal year 2025 representing an attractive 5.7% dividend yield.

Historical earnings trajectory demonstrates consistent outperformance against conservative guidance. Core revenues increased by 2.60% year on year to €71.90 million in Q1 2026, following stable growth at 2.90% year on year in both Q3 and Q4 of 2025. Adjusted EBITDA margin peaked at 70.50% in Q3 2024, supported by predictable contract indexation through long-term broadcasting contracts carrying CPI escalators ensuring stable inflation-hedged cash flows. Reported Adjusted EBITDA beat consensus by 7.60% in Q4 2025 and by 3% in Q3 2025, driven by tight operational cost control. Management maintains a conservative guidance pattern to ensure beat-and-raise delivery.

The most recent Q1 2026 results confirm resilient fundamentals despite elevated development investment. Q1 2026 core revenues of €71.90 million outperformed consensus by 0.60% versus the €71.50 million expectation, while Adjusted EBITDA of €47.30 million was exactly in line with consensus. Net income of €21.60 million missed the €22.10 million consensus estimate by 2.26% due to elevated depreciation from high capital spending on industrial plan projects.

Recurring free cash flow to equity accelerated to €34 million, up 6.25% against €32 million in Q1 2025. Management confirmed the 2026 outlook, projecting Adjusted EBITDA substantially in line with the 2025 baseline, with full-year development capex expected to rise driven by solar projects, DAB extension, and CDN expansion. Post-earnings, Morgan Stanley kept its Equal-weight rating unchanged but lowered the target price by 1.47% from €6.80 to €6.70.

Peer-relative positioning places Rai Way in the defensive lower-growth percentile across European towers. Rai Way sits in the 5th percentile overall for growth momentum, primarily constrained by its regional Italian broadcast footprint, while Helios Towers dominates the peer group on revenue momentum delivering +12.50% growth in Q1 2026. Rai Way maintained excellent operational efficiency with a 65.90% EBITDA margin in Q1 2026, while Inwit printed a sector-leading 89.94% EBITDA margin.

However, Inwit faces severe long-term margin pressure from its anchor contract dispute with Swisscom and Telecom Italia, with 2026 forecast revenue growth cut to low-single-digit levels post-dispute. Rai Way's revenue expectations remain modest at +2.70% for FY26 as data center contributions defer post-2027.

The sell-side maintains a highly bullish consensus with zero sell ratings. The consensus rating distribution stands at 80% Overweight and 20% Equal-weight with zero Underweight ratings, while consensus target prices range from a low of €6.20 to a high of €7.70, centering around a mean of €6.80. Morgan Stanley's Emmet Kelly maintains an Equal-weight rating with a price target of €6.70, highlighting stable cash flows but noting lower top-line growth of 3% versus faster-growing international tower peers.

Co-coverage focuses on the outstanding 2026e RLFCF yield of 8.50% which compares favorably to Inwit at 7.40%. The core contrarian hold case focuses on structurally lower top-line growth of 3% due to heavy broadcast exposure, lack of international geographical diversification limiting the long-term growth multiple, and first meaningful revenues from the Rome hyperscale data center deferred to 2027 and beyond. The downside scenario implies a valuation of €4.65 per share reflecting a potential 21.32% decline, driven by client rationalization and linear-to-OTT viewing shifts reducing terminal growth rates to 0.50% from 1.50%.

Forward EPS drivers center on six core mechanisms with varying plausibility and timing profiles. Traditional core broadcasting expansion and inflation-linked pricing deliver the highest plausibility and confidence, providing an estimated +1.50% and +1.20% YoY impact respectively. Edge data center rollout carries medium plausibility with an estimated +0.50% impact, while the Rome hyperscale facility represents a potentially transformative +2% impact but faces medium plausibility due to municipal permit bottlenecks. Solar photovoltaic generation offers high plausibility with an estimated +0.80% impact, while merger with EI Towers represents the largest potential catalyst at +15% but carries low plausibility dependent on government decree. The compound probability of all six drivers delivering simultaneously is approximately 25%.

Guidance credibility remains exceptionally high supported by a proven sandbagger posture. For fiscal year 2026, management projected Adjusted EBITDA to be broadly in line with the 2025 baseline assuming constant energy prices, with total development capital expenditure expected to step up driven by solar photovoltaic projects, DAB extensions, and CDN expansions. The company conforms directly to a sandbagger archetype deliberately setting conservative base targets, with initial guidance upgraded during H1 in both fiscal years 2024 and 2025 as underlying trends exceeded conservative baseline assumptions.

Approximately 84% of corporate revenues derive from the RAI Group contract carrying automatic CPI-linked escalators, while a +/-€10/MWh change in electricity tariffs impacts Adjusted EBITDA by +/-€0.70 million. Morgan Stanley models a bull case of €7.90 per share assuming persistent inflation and third-party revenue growth, a base case of €6.70 per share assuming inflation normalizes to 1.50% and hyperscale revenues stream in, and a bear case of €4.65 per share assuming negative inflation, customer rationalization, and higher terminal capex.

The long-term delivery track record confirms unbroken execution on financial commitments despite strategic project delays. Since listing, Rai Way has increased EBITDA by 60% organically and returned 95% of capital, with cumulative distributed dividends exceeding €580 million representing a nearly 2.5x increase. Actual 2023 Adjusted EBITDA reached €180.30 million against the original target of €154 million, with stated operating margin reaching 66.30% compared to the initial plan target of 62%, while cumulative recurring free cash flow reached €114 million versus the planned €98 million target.

Core revenues benefit from direct CPI indexation driving an organic growth benefit of 3.60% in fiscal 2022, with management successfully offsetting an €8.50 million energy cost headwind through tight cost controls. However, hyperscale data center revenues face a one-year delay to 2027 due to administrative permit bottlenecks, while potential EI Towers merger remains slow with reference shareholders extending the MoU to June 15, 2026.

Investment case verdict: Rai Way offers exceptional defensive quality and shareholder yield through inflation-protected broadcast cash flows and 100% dividend payout, but structurally modest 3% top-line growth, heavy parent RAI dependence, and prolonged datacenter monetization timelines constrain the valuation multiple, positioning the stock as a high-yielding bond proxy rather than a transformative infrastructure compounder until either hyperscale facilities commercialize post-2027 or EI Towers consolidation materializes.
