[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = D

Thesis. TotalEnergies is a quality income compounder bought at the top of its own earnings cycle, and at 74.46 EUR (2026-10-02) it is not compelling. The shares pay the ten-year-median multiple for FY2026 earnings that consensus itself expects to fall 10 percent in FY2027 and a further 8 percent in FY2028, so the modal return is about 11 percent a year over eighteen months and 9.5 percent a year over three years against the 20 percent bar. The case clears the bar only below about 57 to 66 EUR, or if Integrated Power earns the re-rating to Shell's and BP's multiple that we rate possible rather than probable.

  • The change is real, but it is two changes braided together, and the market has paid for the wrong one.
    • The company's own change is the next step of a six-year multi-energy build: the EPH 14 GW flexible-generation joint venture closed on 29 April 2026 for €5.15 billion in shares, the capital-recycling model moved to full speed (the KKR farm-down at €1.8 billion enterprise value, the Shell onshore renewables acquisition, the GIP $1.8 billion African midstream deal), the deepwater plan adds 3 percent a year of production at $25 per barrel of cash margin against $15 for the base, and gearing is heading to a 10 percent target possibly by end-2026 from 13.1 percent at 30 June 2026.
    • The market's change is a geopolitical oil and distillate price spike from the Strait of Hormuz that roughly doubled 2026 earnings against 2025.
    • Consensus FY2026 EPS is 11.80 USD against 5.99 USD reported basic for FY2025, the two bases differing by about 3 percent.
    • Brent tracked in the high $80s into the Q2 2026 print and European refining margins reached $13.50 per barrel in Q2 2026 against $4.70 in Q2 2025, while the same blockage curtailed 15 percent of the company's own liftings in Q2 and 8 to 10 percent in July 2026.
  • The company's own change has travelled the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain from choices and actions to the key operating metrics and the financial statements; the sixth layer moved for a different reason.
    • Power generation rose 28 percent year on year to 14.8 TWh in Q2 2026, production was 2.395 Mboe/d, net debt fell $3.34 billion to $19.71 billion in one quarter.
    • The sell-side's estimates rose far more, FY2026 consensus EPS up 70.1 percent in twelve months, but on the oil price: the two house cuts after Q2 (Redburn and Jefferies, 4 percent each) were on the company's own lines, gas trading and production, while every raise came from refining margins and oil.
  • 'Financial Outputs' have surged and consensus has them receding from here.
    • FY2026 consensus EPS of 11.80 USD falls to 10.60 USD in FY2027 and 9.74 USD in FY2028.
    • Our modal path sits 2 to 6 percent below consensus (11.3, 10.0, 9.6 and 10.2 USD for FY2026 to FY2029) because we run the company's own $60 to $70 Brent planning range rather than the brokers' decks and credit no gas-trading recovery beyond normal.
    • From the FY2026 peak the earnings compound at about minus 3 percent a year to FY2029
    • From the FY2028 trough they grow about 5 percent a year, from volume and the share count rather than from revenue at constant volume or margin.
  • The cash return carries the return and is well covered; the earnings leg is slow; the re-rating leg is zero on the modal path.
    • The dividend yield is about 4.8 percent (€0.90 a quarter, €3.60 a year, on 74.46 EUR) growing more than 5 percent a year by policy, with a dividend breakeven below $50 per barrel and an organic pre-dividend breakeven below $25 to $26.4 per barrel
    • The $6 billion a year buyback retires about 3 percent of the shares, about 2 percent net of issuance.
    • The multiple is 8.55 times 24-month-forward earnings, at the ten-year median of 8.70 times and the 84th percentile of five years, so the stock's own history argues for no expansion.
    • Held at 8.5 times on earnings two years beyond each horizon (our FY2030 10.8 USD at eighteen months; our FY2031 11.4 USD at three years), the shares are worth 87.08 EUR including dividends at 2028-04-02 and 97.71 EUR at 2029-10-02: 11.0 percent and 9.5 percent a year.
    • On the one-year-forward footing (8.4 times held on our FY2029 and FY2030 earnings) the returns are 6.3 percent and 7.4 percent a year
      • The convention adds two to five points a year because it reaches past the FY2027 to FY2028 trough, and the case is below the bar on either footing.
    • On a plausibly worse drop-through from EBITDA to EPS (a tax rate three to four points higher on the adjusted basis as production-sharing entitlements shrink at high prices, minorities taking a further 1 to 2 percent of net income as the KKR, Allianz and GIP stakes consolidate, depreciation growing a point faster than EBITDA), EPS is about 8 percent lower at each horizon and the return falls to 5.4 percent and 6.9 percent a year, F on the framework's anchors
      • The park would not change, and the price that clears the bar would fall to about 52 EUR at three years.
  • What the price costs is the full ten-year-median multiple at a cyclical earnings peak, and the discount to peers has named, persistent causes.
    • On the dashboard's P/E (2026-10-04) TotalEnergies at 8.7 times is the cheapest integrated major in the set (Shell 10.0, BP 10.8, Eni 9.6, Equinor 11.2 times) and a re-rating to about 10.0 times would be worth about 15 percent.
    • We rate that re-rating possible, at about 30 percent, because it needs two things at once, a demonstrated organic Integrated Power cash inflection and Brent holding at or above $70, and because the discount is explained by the French sovereign and CAC 40 linkage, Middle East exposure of 19 percent of production against Shell's 13 percent, the lower-return Power segment inside the group and capital intensity about 15 percent above the closest European peers.
  • The pattern it fits is 'Major Corporate Change', and it fits loosely.
    • A strong operator is reshaping the perimeter, adding a flexible-power business at scale and deleveraging, and those actions show in the operating metrics and the balance sheet
    • The pattern breaks at its last clause, "major EPS trajectory upgrades", because the upgrades of 2026 are the oil price's and the company's own change moves EPS by about 5 percent a year from the trough base.
    • 'Compounder' is a secondary fit at C on the framework's own rule that a correctly-modelled high-quality business is a C, not a D.
  • Where the chain holds and where it breaks.
    • It holds from the operator's choices to the financial statements and the dividend.
    • It breaks at the step from the outputs to a 20 percent return, because the 2026 outputs are a windfall, the price already stands at the ten-year-median multiple, and the one lever that could lift the multiple is not the company's alone to pull.

  • What the case therefore is.

    • A well-run, under-geared, low-cost major that can be bought with a covered 4.8 percent yield at the ten-year-median multiple and still fail the bar
    • The bet is to wait for the cycle or the price, not to own the company now.
    • A colleague should hear: great company, wrong point in the cycle, wrong price for the bar
    • Park it and name the price.

  • Rating = D, because the modal total shareholder return from 74.46 EUR is about 11 percent a year over eighteen months and about 9.5 percent a year over three years against the 20 percent bar, the input change forces are moderate and long-running rather than new, and the sharp improvement in the 2026 outputs is a commodity windfall from a geopolitical shock that consensus itself expects to fade from FY2027

    • Not C, because reaching the C band needs the multiple above 9 times on our earnings path, which we rate possible, not modal, and because the 2026 earnings are a peak the sell-side itself expects to recede from.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The case against our view, at its strongest: the highest-quality European supermajor is cheap against its peers and the market gives nothing for Integrated Power.
      • TotalEnergies is the highest-quality European supermajor on Redburn's reading, the only one with organic production growth to 2030, the lowest gearing (13.1 percent, heading to 10 percent) and the lowest unit cost (below $5 per barrel of oil equivalent), with a sector-leading free-cash-flow-per-share CAGR projected at 26 percent for 2025 to 2030 and a dividend that grows 5 percent a year through any cycle.
      • It trades at 8.7 times against Shell's 10.0 and BP's 10.8 on the dashboard's P/E, at a 14 percent discount to supermajors on EV/DACF (Redburn), and at the 50th percentile of its own ten-year history while the peers sit at the 55th to 80th.
      • The market gives nothing for an Integrated Power division that Morgan Stanley values at over $40 billion standalone by 2030 and the chief executive at a hypothetical $50 billion, which becomes net cash positive in 2027.
      • The consensus target of 88.39 (dashboard, 2026-10-04) plus the yield is about 24 percent in a year.
      • Oil may not normalise: the Hormuz blockage has recurred, OPEC+ is disciplined and non-OPEC supply declines below $60.
      • On that reading the case is a 'Compounder' mis-modelled on its Power line and the return is B.
    • What we concede.
      • The quality, every word of it.
      • The discount to Shell and BP on the dashboard's P/E, which is real.
      • The bounded downside: the dividend breakeven below $50 per barrel, the organic breakeven below $26 per barrel, and a balance sheet that would carry a $50 oil year without cutting the dividend.
      • The Power optionality: if two quarters of organic positive cash arrive in 2027, the sum-of-the-parts moves from assertion to evidence, and we have put 30 percent on the re-rating branch for that reason.
      • The floor: the contracted LNG (over 70 percent oil-indexed) and the capacity-paid flexible fleet argue that the fair trough is nearer the five-year median (7.33 times) than the five-year 25th percentile (6.57 times).
    • Why we still hold our view, first: the price of the quality is already paid.
      • The multiple is the ten-year median at the top of the earnings cycle, and the stock's own five-year history puts today's 8.55 times in the top sixth.
      • The discount to Shell and BP has named, persistent causes (the French sovereign linkage, the Middle East concentration, the lower-return segment, the capital intensity, the Russian LNG tail) that no event inside eighteen months removes.
    • Second: the sum-of-the-parts does not cover what the bulls say once like is applied to like.
      • Integrated Power earned about $2.2 billion of adjusted net operating income on an annualised basis in H1 2026 ($0.55 billion in Q1, $0.53 billion in Q2), a post-tax divisional profit line, and $2.6 billion of cash flow from operations in 2025, 10 percent of the group's segment cash flow.
      • A price-to-earnings multiple belongs on that post-tax line: at the group's own 8.55 times it is worth about $19 billion, 10 percent of the $190,234 million market capitalisation
      • At the lowest multiple among the integrated majors in the set (Repsol, 8.0 times) about $18 billion, 9 percent
      • At the lowest in the whole set (DNO, 5.3 times) about $12 billion, 6 percent.
      • The bulls' $40 to $50 billion is 18 to 23 times a segment that returns 10 percent on capital against a 12 percent target and missed its own consensus by 11 percent in Q2 2026.
      • The bulls' value is an assertion at a utility multiple
      • Our cover is a bound, and it is small.
    • Third: the bulls' return needs the re-rating, and the re-rating needs two things at once.
      • A move to about 10.0 times needs the Power inflection demonstrated (about 55 percent) and Brent holding at or above $70 so that the earnings base holds while the multiple expands (about 55 percent)
      • The two are close to independent, so the product is about 30 percent.
      • A 24 percent one-year return at 30 percent odds, against our modal 11 percent at 45 percent and the bear's 1 percent at 25 percent, is an expected return of about 12 percent a year, which is still D.
    • Fourth: the deleveraging the bulls point to was partly timing.
      • The 'FALSE FRIEND' profile the risks research's pre-mortem selects (deleveraging flattered by a $1.2 billion working-capital release and divestments) is exactly the pattern a bull would miss while headline gearing falls.
      • We do not think the cash engine is failing: organic free cash flow was $6.36 billion in Q2 2026.
      • But we want the next two prints before paying up.
    • Where the case against would win.
      • Two consecutive quarters of organic positive Integrated Power cash before farm-downs with Brent still above $70
      • That would lift the re-rating odds toward even, and we would change the view on that evidence or on the price.

  • The change is two changes braided together: the company's own, a six-year multi-energy build that took its next step in April 2026 when the EPH flexible-generation joint venture closed and the capital-recycling model moved to full speed, and the market's, a geopolitical oil and distillate price spike from the Strait of Hormuz that roughly doubled 2026 earnings against 2025 (consensus FY2026 EPS 11.80 USD against 5.99 USD reported basic for FY2025).
