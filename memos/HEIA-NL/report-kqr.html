Section 1 – Key question and brief

Section summary
  1. The European beverage sub-sector's earnings trajectory is highly bifurcated, with soft drink bottlers demonstrating resilient pricing power while brewers face volume pressure. The key question addressed by this research is: What is the 18-month to 3-year earnings per share (EPS) trajectory of major listed European brewers and soft drink companies–specifically Heineken (HEIA-NL), AB InBev, Carlsberg, Royal Unibrew, Coca-Cola Europacific Partners (CCEP), Coca-Cola HBC, AG Barr, and Fevertree–and what are the key strategic drivers dictating stock-picking returns across this sub-sector? This question is highly material to investors because the sector has significantly de-rated over the past several years, with large-cap beverage multiples contracting from a 50% premium relative to the market in 2019 to an 11% discount by late 2026, meaning that stock-picking returns over the next 18 months will be heavily driven by identifying companies capable of delivering robust self-help margin expansion and volume stabilization. Section 6 – Regulatory Pressures, Sugar Taxes, and Environmental Mandates

Section summary
  1. Escalating packaging regulations and sugar levies present persistent volume friction across Europe, but the financial burden is heavily mitigated by proactive product reformulations and sophisticated pricepack revenue management.
  2. Larger beverage players leverage superior scale and data capabilities to turn compliance mandates into structural market share gains against smaller competitors.

Tightening sugar levies and reformulation

1.
Imminent German levy: Rothschild & Co Redburn's 11 August 2026 report highlights the proposed 2027 German sugar tax, expecting to raise €650 million from the highly price-sensitive added-sugar segment as a significant short-term volume threat.
2.
Resilient reformulation strategy: Deutsche Bank's 4 September 2026 report indicates that AG Barr is highly insulated from sugar taxes, with 96% of its soft drinks portfolio already exempt from the sugar levy.
3.
Burdensome threshold tightening: Deutsche Bank's 4 September 2026 report notes the UK Soft Drinks Industry Levy will tighten in January 2028 by lowering the exemption threshold to 4.5 grams per 100ml, increasing taxation risk for unreformulated products.
4.
Price-pack tax mitigation: Jefferies' 18 October 2021 report argues that soft drink manufacturers successfully mitigate sugar tax hikes through pack and price architecture shifts, rebalancing revenues toward low-sugar drinks.
5.
Aggressive French taxation: Jefferies' 17 February 2026 report details that CCEP's French revenue grew organically due to pricing and the French sugar tax implementation, even as high-sugar options faced volume pressures.

Burdensome packaging mandates and deposit schemes

6.
Strict European PPWR: DNB Carnegie's 12 August 2026 report highlights that the EU Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation mandates a 90% separate collection rate by 2029, forcing countries to implement deposit schemes.
7.
UK deposit disruption: Deutsche Bank's 6 October 2026 report outlines the UK Deposit Return Scheme scheduled for October 2027, which will introduce a refundable 20p deposit that could temporarily disrupt retail sales of multipack PET bottles and cans.
8.
Profitable packaging lightweighting: Expert insights from a packaging specialist on 25 June 2026 suggest that innovations in PET resin reduction allow global bottlers to lower their per-unit packaging costs.
9.
Burdensome EPR costs: Deutsche Bank's 4 September 2026 report points out that Extended Producer Responsibility fees, starting in 2025, introduce recurring packaging waste disposal costs that heighten the importance of lightweighting.
10.
Competitive recyclability advantage: Expert insights from a former Coca-Cola HBC director on 26 June 2026 emphasize that returnable glass packaging offers superior long-term marginality because individual bottles are reused multiple times.

Health warning friction and marketing bans

11.
Onerous UK advertising bans: Rothschild & Co Redburn's 28 May 2026 report notes that the UK enacted a ban on high fat, sugar, or salt products in October 2025, enforcing a 9pm watershed on TV and a 24-hour ban on online ads to restrict promotion of less-healthy brands.
12.
Strict European restrictions: Morgan Stanley's 6 November 2017 report highlights that countries like Norway and France ban or heavily restrict alcohol advertising, which curbs impulse-driven alcohol occasions but widens the moats of existing brands.
13.
Defensive consumer health shift: Morgan Stanley's 1 November 2023 report suggests that while health regulations present long-term risks, soft drink companies mitigate calorie concerns through rapid portfolio reformulation and package downsizing.
14.
Other side of the story: If strict European Commission proposals for a blanket ban on alcohol advertising are implemented, brewers may face permanent volume erosion because they would be unable to promote low-alcohol alternatives.

This can be resolved through an expert call or waitand-monitor.

Unconfirmed market traction: Fever-Tree's mid-teens US volume growth forecast is heavily dependent on the execution of its new Molson Coors partnership, but near-term retail traction remains unconfirmed. Deutsche Bank's 6 October 2026 report notes that the market requires sustained US volume compounding closer to 20% to validate the long-term premium mixer opportunity. This gap can be resolved through a wait-and-monitor of US scanner data.
