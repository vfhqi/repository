Pre-earnings consensus and margins

Analysts at Visible Alpha consensus projected Neste's comparable EBITDA to reach EUR 1,181 million, showing substantial recovery from the weak prior-year period. Strong commodity price momentum raised the pre-earnings bar, which Sasikanth Chilukuru of Jefferies noted would require exceptional operational execution to sustain the stock's momentum over the next 18 months.
2.
Elevated margins: Neste was expected to maintain strong profitability in its core segment due to favorable European supply dynamics. Sasikanth Chilukuru of Jefferies projected a Renewable Products comparable sales margin of $1,089 per ton for the third quarter, representing a massive recovery compared to the $408 per ton margin recorded in the full year of 2025. Favorable market parameters in renewable diesel supported the elevated estimates, which eventually allowed the company to trade below its replacement cost prior to the earnings update.
3.
Exceptional cracks: Neste's traditional refining operations were expected to benefit from unprecedented tightness in the middle distillate markets. Financial analysts at Deutsche Bank forecast a strong Oil Products refining margin of $25.50 per barrel, nearly flat compared to the previous quarter's margin of $25.60 per barrel. Solid European product spreads were expected to partly offset lower refining volumes during scheduled shutdowns, thereby stabilizing short-term cash flow.
4.
Geopolitical tailwinds: Neste captured supernormal refining profits because of severe maritime transit disruptions in the Middle East. Shortages of mid-distillate products such as jet fuel and diesel significantly boosted global refining margins during the course of 2026. Record geopolitical volatility created a highly supportive environment for Neste's third-quarter refining and renewable margins, although analysts debated the durability of the cycle.

Scheduled downtime: Neste prepared for significant volume headwinds due to a major maintenance turnaround at its sole conventional refinery. Iiris Theman of DNB Carnegie projected that the eight-week Porvoo shutdown starting in August 2026 would reduce Oil Products sales volumes to 1.76 million tons, a decline of 43% compared to the prior-year period. Investor attention remained focused on the execution of the maintenance stop, which DNB Carnegie viewed as a necessary operational step before volume growth resumes.
