Hexatronic (HTRO-SE): what the Q3 2026 report does to the case

These results strengthen the existing case materially: the quarter met, with room to spare, the exact test the case set itself for moving from park to progress, and the one thing that stops us saying so without qualification is that the same quarter moved the binding constraint from demand to supply and made the cycle, not the corporate change, the larger author of the output.

What changed

  • Q3 2026 (1 July to 30 September, reported 22 October): sales SEK 2,498m against the four-contributor consensus of SEK 2,256m (+11 percent); organic growth 17 percent against 8.1 percent; adjusted EBITA SEK 281m against SEK 233m (+21 percent); margin 11.3 percent against 10.35 percent; diluted EPS SEK 0.81 against SEK 0.59 (+37 percent). Reported growth of 33 percent was 17 percent organic, 14 percent acquired, 1 percent currency.
  • Against the case's own lines: the pass line was Fiber Solutions organic growth below 5 percent or a group margin below 8 percent; the progress line was an adjusted gross margin above 38 percent with Fiber Solutions organic growth above 5 percent. Delivered: Fiber Solutions organic growth 13 percent and a 9.4 percent margin (third consecutive sequential rise, from 5.4 percent a year earlier); adjusted gross margin 39.8 percent from 37.5 percent; group margin 11.3 percent against the case's modal 9.8 percent for FY2026 and 10.5 percent for FY2027, and its alarm at 9 percent.
  • Data Center: sales SEK 692m, +107 percent, 44 percent organic; adjusted EBITA SEK 117m at 16.9 percent, against the covering broker's SEK 546m, about 22 percent organic, SEK 85m and 15.5 percent. About three quarters of the EBITA beat against the broker's model came from this segment. Management attributed part of it to favourable seasonality and project timing, expects Q4 activity to normalise toward the first-half pattern, sees no slowdown in underlying momentum, and expects Q4 submarine shipments similar to Q3.
  • Geography: North American sales +80 percent to SEK 1,190m; North America 48 percent of sales against Europe's 43 percent; the planned downsizing of Clinton, South Carolina reversed. European Fiber Solutions sales -9 percent (United Kingdom, DACH). Harsh Environment: SEK 393m, +25 percent, 6 percent organic, record adjusted EBITA SEK 43m at 11.0 percent; defence strong, oil and gas muted.
  • Supply: management disclosed that glass fibre and selected connectors were beginning to restrict growth, with rising backlogs and unavoidable lead-time extensions; fibre and transport costs higher, resin stabilised lower; new suppliers being validated and prices being raised, with the speed and completeness of pass-through not established. Group order book about four months of sales, unchanged from Q2.
  • Below EBITA and cash: reported EBIT SEK 328m included SEK 71m of net non-recurring items, among them a SEK 67m reversal of the Clinton impairment; net financial items -SEK 73m, including SEK 26m of net interest and SEK 28m of revaluation of contingent consideration; tax 29.6 percent. Operating cash flow SEK 228m, 72 percent conversion against 84 percent in Q2; nine-month working capital -SEK 205m; capex SEK 38m; net debt SEK 1,305m; adjusted leverage 1.3x from 1.7x; available liquidity SEK 2,090m. Nine-month diluted EPS SEK 1.88 against the case's FY2026 path of SEK 2.10.
  • Leadership: the Group CFO moves to President Fiber Solutions on 1 November 2026 with an external search and no named successor; the permanent Head of Data Center is not appointed, with the Head of M&A acting since 1 August.
  • Sell-side: one accessible post-results note (the covering broker, 22 October): a clear beat, positive view retained, forecasts and base case to rise "slightly", the Data Center outperformance discounted for Q4 normalisation, and a risk-free-rate assumption raised from 2.5 percent to 3 percent that offsets part of the uplift; no revised EPS, target or rating published; the pre-results base case was SEK 44. The challenge

The case already holds the restocking reading and the acquisition-led "false friend"; Q3 weakened both (a reversed capacity cut, 13 percent Fiber Solutions organic growth through a direct channel, organic growth in all three areas) without excluding them, since channel inventory is still undisclosed. Those are not the challenge. Three readings are, and the case accommodates none of them.

The margin test was passed on mix and timing, not on pass-through, and the real test is in the next two quarters. The case asked one question of Q3: does the margin survive raw-material inflation? The 39.8 percent gross margin that answered it carries a Data Center quarter that management says will not repeat, submarine shipments that management says continue only through Q4, and a Fiber Solutions beat the broker attributes to volume rather than margin (9.4 percent against 9.3 percent forecast). The fibre and transport cost rise the company now reports is the one that sits behind the case's three-month pass-through lag; the pricing response is asserted rather than demonstrated; and the broker's standing view is of fragmented, partly commodity-like markets with limited ability to price above inflation without churn. On this reading Q3 was the quarter before the pressure, not the quarter that absorbed it, and the case's trigger tested the wrong quarter. What would have to be true: the Q4 gross margin falls back toward the case's 38 percent alarm as Data Center normalises and the fibre costs land; the Fiber Solutions margin slips from 9.4 percent while volume holds; management repeats "temporary pressure". The case cannot hold this reading because it wrote the Q3 gross margin as the sufficient condition for moving the margin branch from plausible to probable; if the reading is right, the branch stays plausible and the recommendation stays park even though the trigger fired.

Supply, not demand, is now the binding constraint, and the case has no reading in which the demand is right and the revenue does not arrive. The case's Key Risk was a margin risk with a tail (a lag in price; a delivery failure that stops lines). Q3 reframed it as a volume cap: backlogs rising, lead times extending unavoidably, suppliers still being validated, and an order book flat at four months through a quarter of 17 percent organic growth. The broker's channel work has vertically integrated producers protected from the preform shortage and non-integrated cable makers exposed, utilisation above 90 percent, and a shortage that may last through 2028. The negative form: the US demand the case needs is real and the company cannot ship it, so revenue runs below the case's 7.5 percent a year path while working capital builds (nine months -SEK 205m; conversion 72 percent) and the larger integrated rivals (Corning winning 50 to 60 percent of bids; Amphenol with CommScope's connectivity business) take the allocation; the earnings upgrade becomes a working-capital build and cash conversion stays below the case's own 80 percent threshold. The positive form, which the review names and the case also lacks: scarcity lets the company prioritise Data Center, submarine and defence orders and price them, which would move the case's "nominal and regional" price-rises finding toward real pricing. What would have to be true for the negative form: Q4 sales short of demand with supply named as the reason, the order book lengthening while sales flatten, inventory and receivables rising further; for the positive form, realised price visible in the gross margin while volumes hold. The case's modal path assumes pass-through within about three months and revenue that follows demand; it has no branch for either.

The quarter re-authored the case from a corporate change to a cyclical and thematic beta, and the price should be judged against that. The case's setup is 'Major Corporate Change', with the cycle's share of the output the one point where the fit was not crisp. Q3 made the cycle the larger author: three quarters of the EBITA beat from a Data Center quarter the company itself calls timing; North America +80 percent; 14 percent of growth acquired; the internal change, the cost programme, complete and already in the base; the Fiber Solutions beat from volume. In its strongest form the company is now a two-engine beta on hyperscaler capital expenditure and the US carrier restart, carrying an acquisition-fed service layer, with Data Center's own boom-and-bust base rate (the case's words) and four months of order visibility. Under that reading the right multiple is not the ten-year median held flat but a cyclical's: higher at the top, where the consensus target of SEK 47.6 sits, and lower when hyperscaler spending pauses, where the case's own two-year median of 10x puts the shares near SEK 29.5 on unchanged earnings. The quarter widens both tails of the case's outcome skew at once. It also moves the setup's shape: 'Demand Inflection' failed its mandatory condition because Europe was half of sales; Europe is now 43 percent and falling, North America 48 percent and rising. What would have to be true: Q4 Data Center sales fall back to the first-half pattern (SEK 538m in Q2, on the case's figures) and the group margin toward 10 percent, confirming lumpiness; 2027 sales growth lands near the broker's pre-results 4 percent rather than the case's 9.9 percent; the shares trade with the AI-optical group rather than on the print. The case calls the multiple "ours to lose as well as to earn"; it does not hold a reading in which the earnings base rises and the fair multiple falls because the earnings got lumpier.

A fourth strand sharpens rather than challenges. The case's Key Risk of an M&A leadership vacuum now has two open seats, not one: the acting Head of Data Center and, from 1 November, a CFO search, at the moment SEK 2,090m of liquidity and 1.3x leverage make three to six deals a year likelier, with SEK 28m of earn-out revaluation already in a single quarter's financial items because the acquired businesses did well. That is the case's own risk worsening, and it compounds the third reading.

What would settle it

The second review, four working days on, is the piece that carries the post-results price against the case's bars, the broker's revised forecasts and base case (how far "slightly" is, and whether the 3 percent risk-free rate takes it back), FactSet consensus for FY2026 to FY2028 and the 24-month-forward figure the case values the shares on, the buy share and target (SEK 47.6 before the print), and whether the shares moved on the print or with the AI-optical group, which is the first test of the third reading. It will not carry Q4 Data Center sales, the gross margin after the cost lag, cash conversion for the year, or either appointment. It settles the price and the estimates; it settles none of the three readings.

  • The first reading settles at the Q4 2026 report in early February 2027 and the Q1 2027 report after it: adjusted gross margin against the case's 38 percent alarm once Data Center normalises and the fibre costs land; the Fiber Solutions margin against 9 percent; the Data Center margin against 15 percent after the timing effect fades.
  • The second reading settles partly at Q4, in whether sales fall short with supply named, in the order book's months, in year-end working capital and conversion. Partly it does not settle: the company discloses no order intake, book-to-bill, backlog ageing, realised price or channel inventory, and has committed to none, so the supply reading will be judged by inference from revenue against demand commentary. That is a question nothing will answer directly.
  • The third reading settles over two prints: Q4 Data Center sales against the first-half pattern; whether 2027 consensus growth lands near 4 percent or near 10 percent; and the multiple's behaviour against the case's alarm of below 11x with earnings intact.
  • The appointments are undated. The case's alarm is the Q4 2026 report, and the CFO handover on 1 November means both seats should be measured against it.
