7. The questions

1 ★ 2027 against the Street. "Consensus for 2027 is about £64m of EBITDA. In March '£64m-£65m' was '100% fine'; in June it was 'mid-£60s'. Is the right number today nearer £62m or £65m?"
- Pick from: "Where does your own compiled consensus sit now, and how many analysts have cut since September?" / "Will you give a 2027 EBITDA figure on 30 March?"
- Reads: "nearer £65m" lifts the odds of a £63m endorsement well above our 30%, and says our £58.3m is up to £5.6m too low (3.9p, 12% of our EPS). "Nearer £62m", or "we will update in March", means the drift to £58m to £62m has begun.

2 ★ 2028, which has already moved. "For 2028, £86m of EBITDA was comfortable in March and the steer was '£80m+' in June. Consensus is now about £78m. Where does it settle: nearer £75m or £80m?"
- Pick from: "Which line took the £8m out: the United States, the margin elsewhere, or the dollar?"
- Reads: "£80m" again says the Street is low and our £66.7m is £11m to £13m too low. "Nearer £75m" says the company itself is walking 2028 down: the first cuts to 2028 that the memo expects after March.

3 ★ The bridge to £64m. "From about £50m this year to about £64m next is +£14m. In March roughly £10m of that was the United States. How does it split today between the United States and everything else?"
- Pick from: "How much are the tariff refunds worth to this year's £50m?" / "Outside the United States, which is biggest next year in £m: price, volume or cost savings?"
- Reads: a split still near £10m and £4m, with a price rise named, supports the Street's £64m. Refunds of £3m or more in this year's base make the real step £17m or more. If the part outside the United States is £5m or more with no price rise named, that is the Street's weak line and our base stands.

4 ★ How to model United States profit. "United States EBITDA was £4.1m in the first half. The steer in March was about £8m this year, £18m next year and £28m in 2028. Which of the three has moved, and to what?"
- Pick from: "Is the second half the same £4.1m again, plus the refunds?" / "Deutsche Bank has about £29m for 2028. Right area?" / "If United States sales beat plan by $20m in 2027, how many £m reach your EBITDA?"
- Reads: £22m or more for 2027 is the memo's condition for an upgrade cycle: +£2m to £4m on our base, +1.4p to 2.8p. "Still about £18m" means our £20.0m is £2m high. "None of the extra $20m" confirms that the agreed profit schedule, not sales, sets profit.

5 ★ United States sales in dollars. "Consensus was $200m of United States sales this year, then $240m and $280m. With one month left, where does this year land, and what should we use for 2027: $240m or less?"
- Pick from: "The campaign laps in April. What growth do you plan for after that?" / "In June we were told 20% growth could become 30% or 40% if distributors tip. Has any month this autumn run above 25%?"
- Reads: $200m and $240m held keeps the 20% dollar growth and the narrative intact. Below $200m means the second half grew nearer 15% than 20%: a 1% to 3% trim to the Street's 2027 EBITDA in January, as the memo expects. A month above 25% is the first evidence for the upgrade the company itself described in June.

6 ★ Glass, energy, aluminium and price. "Glass makers are cutting capacity and complaining about prices. What percentage of your 2027 glass and energy is fixed today, and is the 2027 rate above or below 2026's?"
- Pick from: "The same two numbers for aluminium?" / "How much price are you taking for 2027, in percent?" / "Deutsche Bank has the margin outside the United States rising from 24.1% in 2025 to 26.5% by 2029. Right direction and size?"
