EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF) 42 findings

This comprehensive management assessment synthesizes findings across eighteen years of CEO tenure, examining leadership quality, capital discipline, governance frameworks, and alignment mechanisms at Gimv NV, a Belgian mid-market private equity specialist operating across Consumer, Healthcare, Smart Industries, and Sustainable Cities platforms in the Benelux, France, and DACH regions. The analysis reveals a management team characterized by highly experienced corporate dealmaking credentials established through foundational roles at Bank Brussel Lambert, Price Waterhouse, KBC Securities, and European private equity funds, delivering portfolio returns of 18.8% in H1 2026 that exceeded the long-term target of 17.5% while expanding net asset value per share to EUR 57.2.

However, this operational outperformance contrasts sharply with persistent long-term total shareholder return underperformance, recording a five-year TSR of -3.3% and ten-year TSR of -7.4% compared to peer medians of 2.4% and 9.8% respectively, creating a fundamental tension between portfolio execution and market valuation that defines the investment case.

Synthesis

CEO Koen Dejonckheere presents a highly credible operator-specialist profile with deep private equity and investment banking credentials across Europe, delivering eighteen years of stable executive stewardship since his 2008 appointment during market stress, with strong competence alignment between his technical and financial training and Gimv's diversified portfolio needs. His track record demonstrates consistent portfolio returns of 18.8% in H1 2026, exceeding the long-term target of 17.5%, with portfolio NAV expanding to EUR 2.4 billion and lifting net asset value per share to EUR 57.2, though the long-tenured internal executive team might lead to complacency in adapting to disruptive global industry shifts.

However, his tenure is marked by persistent long-term total shareholder return underperformance, with a five-year TSR of -3.3% versus the peer median of 2.4% and a ten-year TSR of -7.4% compared to the private equity peer median of 9.8%.

The animal-CEO archetype assessment yields mixed plausibility markers across the five dimensions. On Force, Dejonckheere demonstrates high plausibility through decisive portfolio action by discontinuing new Life Sciences investments in early 2026, demonstrating high decision velocity under pressure while targeting high-conviction growth and abandoning non-core legacy pipelines systematically. On Agency, he shows moderate-to-high plausibility through executive committee and board shareholdings exceeding EUR 10 million in Gimv stock, though his background is predominantly advisory, lacking direct ultimate P&L responsibility for a major listed corporation prior to taking the helm at Gimv.

On Dynamism, the CEO displays moderate plausibility through his Combining Intelligence strategy pairing human judgment with AI-enabled capabilities across companies, with AI integration covering the entire pipeline from opportunity screening to portfolio monitoring, asserting that protecting yesterday's structures is not a strategy while creative destruction is. On Capital- Allocation Discipline, he achieves moderate plausibility given the strategic decision to discontinue underperforming Life Sciences, securing a new EUR 400 million revolving credit facility doubling available bank credit lines, and maintaining total liquidity of EUR 531.4 million by balancing exits with active follow-on investments, but discontinuing life sciences and anchoring portfolios could constrain liquidity and drop returns if long-term asset rotation slows down significantly.

On Clarity, Dejonckheere demonstrates moderate plausibility through direct presentation style emphasizing operational resilience over defensive macroeconomic excuses during market volatility, yet sell-side analysts maintained a neutral stance following solid H1 2026 results, lowered their target price from EUR 43 to EUR 38.9, and model a forward 15% portfolio return which sits below the long-term target of 17.5%.

The absolute strongest bear case on management quality centers on structural value destruction masked by portfolio accounting flexibility. The persistent five-year and ten-year TSR underperformance against European listed private equity peers represents a fundamental failure to translate operational portfolio returns into shareholder value creation. High variable compensation components and performance multipliers may incentivize short-term risk-taking if portfolio asset valuations are aggressively marked up, creating a perverse incentive structure where CEO pay reached EUR 2.23 million in FY25 with 70.3% variable compensation, while other ExCo members received total remuneration of EUR 9.69 million despite sustained shareholder value destruction.

The intense focus on digital transformation might create excessive execution risks if portfolio companies fail to integrate AI, while underlying cash deployment drag could depress short-term return metrics if deal integration velocities slow down. Platform Head Tom Van de Voorde's departure after 19 years introduces near-term Smart Industries transition uncertainty, with succession details communicated later, and elevated keyperson dependency and departures could destabilize investment performance if replacement talent sourcing stalls. Employee turnover reached 9.5% in FY 2025, with departures rising to nine individuals from four in FY 2024, reflecting moderate structural friction, suggesting underlying organizational stress from the decisive discontinuation of Life Sciences investments and simultaneous platform leadership exits.

The key narrative-versus-reality disconnects emerge across three critical dimensions. First, management's confident messaging transitioning from inflation-defensive commentary to highlighting accelerated growth, with bold Capital Markets Day plans to double portfolio size from EUR 1.7 billion to EUR 3.5 billion and formally raised long-term portfolio return targets from 15% to 17.5% per annum stands in stark contrast to ING's calculation of a spot NAV of EUR 54.53 implying a substantial 21.4% discount to spot NAV and analysts lowering their target price from EUR 43 to EUR 38.9 in February 2025, modeling a forward 15% portfolio return which sits below the longterm target.

Third, the lack of direct expert network transcripts creates an information blind spot if underlying portfolio operational metrics deteriorate unnoticed by public markets, undermining management's ability to credibly validate operational claims beyond self-reported financial metrics.
