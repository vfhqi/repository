EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

CHIEF EXECUTIVE QUALITY Andreas Shiamishis presents a solid financial foundation established through top-tier global auditing at KPMG London and senior roles at Diageo, followed by a long-tenured internal progression at HELLENiQ Energy from CFO in 2005 through multiple Deputy CEO periods before his CEO appointment in 2019. His leadership style combines plain-spoken pragmatism with strong operational candor, exemplified by direct statements about not being "after big announcements," frustration with regulators "tampering with the free market," and maintaining a "realistic and very cautious" posture on renewable spending. The CEO diverges from international peer norms by refusing to publish formal financial forecasts, attributing this conservative policy to unpredictable fluctuations in crude prices and regional refining margins. ARCHETYPE MATCH AND DELIVERY RECORD

The group delivered robust operational cash flow with Adjusted EBITDA above EUR 1,026 million for consecutive years matching regional peer performance, while completing the Aspropyrgos turnaround safely in 1Q26 ensuring high plant availability. However, group net sales dropped to EUR 11,615 million in 2025 down 9.03% due to normalizing benchmark margins, with reported net income declining to EUR 173 million in 2025 from EUR 478 million in 2023.

Capital allocation shifted profitability towards a green utility platform through the acquisition and rebranding of Elpedison to Enerwave, expanding operational RES capacity to 552 MW in 1H26 while maintaining a disciplined EUR 0.60 per share dividend yielding 7%. Net debt was reduced by EUR 0.7 billion quarter-on-quarter to below EUR 2.0 billion in 2Q26, while the company actively executed its buyback program acquiring 429,083 own shares representing 0.14% of share capital.

Slowing green execution timelines and persistent grid curtailments in Greece could restrict double-digit returns on new solar investments.

GOVERNANCE ROBUSTNESS AND BOARD STRUCTURE Governance independence is constrained by concentrated ownership with the Greek State holding 31.2% through GROWTHFUND, Paneuropean Oil controlling 40.4% of the float, and the free float limited to 28.4% overall below the regional peer average of 45%. Committee structures combine independent directors with specialized external experts, with the Audit Committee comprising two independent board members and two external accounting experts including former EY executive Panagiotis Papazoglou, while independent members Maria Ioannidou and Maria Psylla joined the board in June 2026. Director equity ownership remains negligible with non-executive directors holding zero shares aligning with historical local practices, while the company does not include clawback provisions for recovering paid variable remuneration from executive board members. Related-party transactions with state entities represent substantial commercial exposure with sales rising to EUR 322 million in 1H26 from EUR 191 million in 1H25, while purchases jumped to EUR 350 million in 1H26 from EUR 8 million in 1H25. The heavy concentration of state and POIH representatives on the board could lead to strategic gridlock if disagreements arise over renewable capex.

CFO QUALITY AND SENIOR LEADERSHIP CFO Vasilis Tsaitas assumed the Group CFO role in February 2022 bringing deep investment banking experience with a business degree and MBA from INSEAD, having previously managed European energy M&A advisory at HSBC London providing pedigree superior to regional domestic-only peers. Tsaitas maintains disciplined balance sheet management reducing net debt by EUR 0.7 billion quarter-on-quarter with gearing decreasing to 36% with 1.3x leverage in 2Q26, while semester capex exceeded EUR 400 million in 1H26 representing the highest first-half spend in company history. The senior leadership team benefits from long-tenured operational specialists including Deputy CEO Georgios Alexopoulos combining a Harvard MBA and MIT chemical engineering degrees serving as executive director since 2007, with General Managers for refineries and oil supply representing long-serving internal promotions ensuring deep operational continuity. High working capital volatility and heavy near-term maintenance capex could restrict free cash flow if refining margins compress rapidly.

INSIDER SIGNALS AND COMPENSATION ALIGNMENT Individual executive pay is not separately reported limiting direct peer alignment benchmarking, with key management paid EUR 14.78 million in 2025 up 12% from EUR 13.17 million in 2024, while the management pool reached EUR 14.32 million in 2023 inflated by post-employment benefit payouts of EUR 6.23 million. The long-term incentive plan weighting splits with 60% assigned to financial targets and transformation and ESG goals each carrying 20% weight incorporating climate and carbon targets, with vesting of the first cycle occurring on December 31 2026 and shares distributed gradually over three years. Insider activity showed no open-market transactions by executive directors over the last 12 months, though the company executed treasury share buybacks totaling 27,000 shares to fund the LTIP program. The complete lack of clawback mechanisms combined with opaque individual targets could permit executive over-compensation despite potential long-term value destruction.

SELL-SIDE AND EXPERT SENTIMENT

Sell-side sentiment remains highly polarized with Refinitiv data showing 25% Overweight and 38% Underweight ratings, while Morgan Stanley ranks HELLENiQ low in the global refining preference order below regional peer Tupras maintaining an Equal-weight rating with a price target of EUR 13.50. Analysts note HELLENiQ trades at similar multiples as Motor Oil Hellas but at a premium to other CEEMEA refiners, with price targets progressing positively over 18 months as Morgan Stanley raised its target to EUR 13.50 from EUR 9.

The sell-side supports strategic renewable pivot to Romania where Shiamishis expects returns of 10-12%, while analysts flag that Greek grid connection delays will slow the deployment of renewable assets. Expert network insights reveal successful regional pipeline restarts improving Balkan logistics, though severe grid curtailments and regulatory delays inside Greece slow down the domestic 1.0 GW green transition milestone with operational wind and solar assets facing steep volumes of grid curtailments dampening renewable energy EBITDA generation.

SINGLE LARGEST CREDIBILITY RISK The single largest credibility risk centers on slowing green execution timelines and persistent domestic grid bottlenecks that threaten the Vision 2030+ strategy, with domestic solar deployment facing severe grid connection delays due to lack of regulatory framework clarity while operational renewable assets experience steep curtailments dampening EBITDA generation. This execution risk is compounded by the CEO's refusal to publish formal forecasts creating severe valuation friction for public investors, while strategic commentary diverges from operational realities where downstream refining remains the core EBITDA driver despite official green transition narratives. The combination of concentrated state-private blockholder control limiting minority shareholder influence, absent clawback provisions in executive compensation, and zero open-market stock purchases by board members over the past twelve months signals incomplete alignment with public shareholders during this critical transition phase.

Outliers worth noting

The comprehensive assessment of HELLENiQ Energy's management quality reveals three significant statistical outliers that position the organization and its leadership at the extremes of sector norms, reflecting both exceptional strengths and notable structural anomalies relative to the European refining peer group. The first major outlier concerns CEO tenure and internal progression depth, where Andreas Shiamishis represents an exceptionally rare executive profile among regional energy peers.

His 21-year continuous tenure within a single corporate entity, beginning as Chief Financial and IT Officer at Petrola Hellas in 2003, advancing to group CFO in 2005 following the landmark merger with Hellenic Petroleum, assuming responsibility for international subsidiaries in 2012, serving as Deputy CEO during multiple periods between 2014 and 2019, and ultimately becoming CEO in 2019, places him in the top 5% of regional energy executives for organizational continuity.

This pattern extends to CFO Vasilis Tsaitas, who assumed the Group CFO role in February 2022 following over a decade of internal progression, having joined in 2011 to lead investor relations and capital markets. This deep internal institutional knowledge contrasts sharply with the typical executive turnover patterns observed at Motor Oil Hellas, Tupras, and other CEEMEA refining peers, where external CEO appointments and shorter average tenures of five to seven years represent the sector median.

The sustained internal progression creates both exceptional operational continuity and potential risks of institutional groupthink that could limit strategic agility during industry transitions.

The third behavioral anomaly centers on management guidance philosophy and disclosure practices, where HELLENiQ represents a clear negative outlier. The company deviates from international peers by not generally publishing forecasts regarding future financial results, with management attributing this conservative policy to unpredictable fluctuations in crude prices and regional refining margins. Additionally, individual executive pay is not separately reported, limiting direct peer alignment benchmarking for specific corporate officers, although key management was paid EUR 14.78 million in 2025, up 12% from EUR 13.17 million in 2024, with the management pool reaching EUR 14.32 million in 2023, inflated by post-employment benefit payouts of EUR 6.23 million.

The company does not include clawback provisions for recovering paid variable remuneration from executive board members. This combination of zero formal guidance, opaque individual compensation disclosures, and absent clawback mechanisms places HELLENiQ in the bottom 5% of European refining sector transparency standards. Motor Oil Hellas, Tupras, PKN Orlen, and MOL Group all provide significantly more granular executive compensation breakdowns and varying degrees of earnings guidance, making HELLENiQ's disclosure posture a statistically significant negative outlier that materially constrains investor confidence and valuation frameworks despite the organization's operational competence.
