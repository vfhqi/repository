9.
Wind volatility: Offshore developers are increasingly exposed to wind-on-wind cannibalisation as massive wind projects connect to localized grids. When wind output peaks simultaneously across concentrated areas, the sheer volume of electricity depresses wholesale prices and triggers negative pricing risks. This regional pricing depression is compounded by grid constraints, which prevent surplus power from being transmitted to high-demand areas.
10.
Negative hours: European wholesale power markets are seeing an unprecedented rise in the frequency of zero and negative pricing hours. Major European union electricity markets experienced six hundred to eight hundred hours of zero or negative wholesale power prices in 2025. This surge in negative pricing hours erodes the profitability of traditional wind assets unless they can curtail generation or store power.
11.
Hybrid storage: Renewable developers are increasingly pairing solar and wind projects with battery storage systems to combat capture rate erosion. Hybrid storage deals now account for nearly one-third of all PPA offers across Spain and Germany. Although hybrid contracts carry 24% higher prices than standalone assets, they allow developers to capture higher evening power prices.

Spread capture: Co-located battery storage enables wind and solar operators to capture massive intraday pricing spreads. In Spain, average daily intraday electricity price spreads increased from seventeen Euros per MWh in 2020 to €98/MWh in 2025. Integrating batteries helps protect developer earnings per share by shifting generation sales from low-priced midday hours to highly lucrative peak periods.
