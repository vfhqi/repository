[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = B

Thesis. Made Tech is a self-help margin repair riding a public-sector demand recovery: a £74m AIM company that sells cleared digital engineers by the day to the UK state, whose next eighteen months are underwritten by a contracted £115m backlog and whose next three years depend on winning the same fight again at a thinner margin. At 49.5p the shares clear the 20% bar over eighteen months on earnings growth alone, by one percentage point, and do not clear it over three years.

  • The change is two-fold, and both halves are evidenced rather than promised.
    • Outside the company, the June 2025 Spending Review gave departments budget certainty to 2028-29 and the Procurement Act put a sovereign preference behind mid-market UK suppliers.
    • Inside it, management unwound a self-inflicted contractor spike and rebuilt the delivery model.
    • The contractor share of the billable base went from 9% in FY24 to 19% in FY25, back to 14% in H1 FY26, with a 10% target.
    • 108 permanent staff were hired (permanent headcount +29% to 482)
      • Bid qualification was tightened
      • Administrative expenses grew 5.5% against revenue growth of 27%.
    • There is no chief-executive change.
      • The founder has led since 2008, which is why the internal force rates C rather than B.
  • 'Financial Outputs' have arrived on every line that matters for earnings per share.
    • FY26 revenue rose 26.8% to £58.88m
    • Adjusted EBITDA rose 69% to £5.89m at a 10.0% margin, from 7.5%
    • Adjusted basic EPS rose 72.5% to 2.38p
    • Net cash reached £14.47m.
    • A £40m four-year consortium award in August 2026 took the backlog to a record £115m at 31 July 2026 and lifted FY27 guidance to £63m to £66m of revenue and £6.3m to £6.6m of adjusted EBITDA.
    • The one soft line is the revenue growth rate, which halves to about 10% in FY27 on guidance, after FY26 bookings fell 37.6% to £50.7m before the post-period win.
  • The change has travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain.
    • The choices and actions show in the headcount and contractor figures, the key operating metrics in backlog and utilisation, the financial statements in the margin, and the sell-side's estimates in a 40.9% rise in FY27 consensus EPS over twelve months to 2026-10-09.
  • What we think it earns: EPS of 2.38p in FY26 compounding at 21.3% a year to 4.25p in FY29.
    • Revenue contributes 11.2% a year (to £81.0m), margin 4.3% a year (adjusted EBITDA margin to 11.3%), the below-EBITDA ratio 5.2% a year, and dilution costs 0.6% a year.
    • Our estimate stands in for a Viewforth model.
    • We weight the below-EBITDA contribution lowest, as the framework asks, and it is where the earnings path is most exposed: a plausibly worse drop-through alone takes FY30 EPS 7% lower.
  • What it costs: 49.5p is 13.83x the 24-month-forward consensus EPS of 3.58p, on 2026-10-09.
    • That is the 39th percentile of the stock's own five years (median 15.05x) and the 20th percentile of the last two (median 17.76x)
    • On a one-year-forward footing it is 17.1x FY27 consensus of 2.9p.
    • The dashboard set's median is 12.9x, but the set is large, slow continental IT services at the bottom of its own ranges
      • The one true comparable, Kainos, is 19.2x.
      • A UK pure-play with a 30% gross margin and a 22% customer belongs about a third below Kainos, which is 13x to 14x.
    • The multiple has compressed while the estimates rose: the price has tracked the earnings, not re-rated.
      • A re-rating is not in our modal case.
  • The return clears the bar at eighteen months and not at three years, and earnings growth carries all of it.
    • At 14.0x applied to the earnings two years beyond each horizon, the shares are worth 65.8p at 2028-04-10 (20.9% a year) and 75.6p at 2029-10-10 (15.2% a year), with no cash return in either.
    • The eighteen-month figure clears 20% by less than one percentage point and rests on the forward window rolling two fiscal years because of the May year-end.
    • The three-year figure falls into the C band as growth decays to 10% to 12% and nothing is paid out.
    • On the adverse drop-through (depreciation held at £1.3m, tax at 27.8%, 155m shares) the eighteen-month return is 15.3% a year, the C band, without any revenue or margin condition failing.
  • Where the chain breaks is in the margin and in one fiscal event.
    • The gross margin has fallen from 34.2% (FY24 restated) to 32.0% to 30.4% while revenue grew 53%, so the margin leg must be earned from mix and utilisation by a business without pricing power.
    • Four customers are 57% of revenue and one payer, the UK state, is 100%.
    • A central procurement pause after the Autumn Budget would fail the revenue condition, the margin condition and the multiple together, which is why the odds on the earnings path are about 45% and on the B-band return about 30%.
  • The pattern it fits is 'Major Corporate Change', at a C.
    • The mechanism is a delivery-model reset that drops a demand recovery through at a higher margin
    • The inputs are narrower than the pattern's "major, multi-dimensional" standard, and the demand half of the case belongs to a box ('Demand Inflection') that the company's structure does not fit.

  • What the case therefore is: compelling as arithmetic over eighteen months and not compelling as a case for a concentrated book.

    • A good small business at a fair price, with its first test dated at the H1 FY27 results in February 2027.

  • Rating = B, because the change is material: forces at B, outputs at B, and a modal return that clears the 20% bar at eighteen months on earnings alone.

    • The parts combine so that the strength, forces moving outputs all the way to the sell-side's estimates and EPS compounding above 20% a year, carries a 'Modal return' Element whose weakness is structural (no cash return, decaying growth, a multiple we do not count on) rather than a failure of the change.
    • Not A, because the return has one leg and the three-year rate is 15.2% a year
    • Not C, because the change has already moved revenue 27%, adjusted EBITDA 69% and FY27 consensus EPS 41%, which is the definition of material in this universe.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The hypothesis, at its strongest: this is a false friend.
      • A record backlog won at tight public-sector rates in a competitive bidding market, carried by a permanent workforce hired ahead of conversion, in a client base where four departments are 57% of revenue and a single fiscal event moves all four.
      • The gross margin has fallen 380 basis points in two years while the backlog rose
        • Bookings fell 38% inside FY26 and were rescued by one award
        • The growth rate is halving
        • AI tooling will shrink the chargeable hours the whole model bills
        • The finance function has had three heads in three years.
      • The Autumn Budget arrives with £8bn of fiscal headroom and a manifesto pledge to halve discretionary consultancy spend.
      • If call-offs slip for two quarters, 482 permanent heads become bench cost, utilisation falls to the FY23 level of 68% to 70%, the EBITDA margin returns to 5% to 7.5%, the two brokers cut, and a company-driven multiple falls with the earnings to the 6x to 12x the shares have traded at within five years.
        • The risks research's bear-case fair value of 22p is that path: a 55% fall with no dividend to hold the shares.
    • The evidence for it is real.
      • The gross-margin trend (34.2%, 32.0%, 30.4% over FY24 to FY26) against a sector median of 37%
      • Bookings that moved -48%, +128% and -38% over three years
      • The FY24 precedent of a 4% revenue decline and a loss during a procurement pause
      • August 2026 borrowing of £18.3bn against a £14.8bn forecast
      • Peer evidence of discounting
      • And the risks research's own naming of the low-margin backlog as its highest-plausibility risk.
    • What we concede.
      • The margin leg is the weaker of the two and rests on execution a business without pricing power must deliver through mix.
      • The growth rate is halving and consensus is not low.
      • The riskiness is D for structural reasons that no half-year result will remove.
      • The market will not pay for a re-rating in this regime.
      • And the three-year return does not clear the bar at today's price.
    • Why we still hold our view, at low conviction.
      • The hypothesis needs the procurement pause to happen, and the evidence that it will not is stronger than the evidence that it will.
      • Departmental settlements are fixed to 2028-29
        • The Treasury's efficiency drive directs spend into digital, not away from it
        • The £115m backlog is contracted
        • Management has beaten twice on a conservative frame.
      • The margin fall is explained by a contractor hedge that has already been half-unwound, and the adjusted EBITDA margin rose 250 basis points while the gross margin fell
        • The second half of FY26 ran at a 12.7% EBITDA margin.
      • Net cash of about 9.7p a share, no debt beyond £0.8m of leases and free cash flow at 139% of net income mean the bear case is a de-rating, not a solvency event.
      • The eighteen-month arithmetic clears the bar on earnings alone, without any re-rating.
      • We make no claim that any part of the business covers the enterprise value, because none does: the enterprise value of about £59m to £65m is 10x to 11x FY26 adjusted EBITDA and no segment is separable.
    • Where the hypothesis would win: a gross margin below 29% or a contractor share above 15% at the H1 FY27 results, or an Autumn Budget that freezes departmental programme spend.
      • Our view therefore holds as a working hypothesis with the February 2027 test dated, not as a conviction.
