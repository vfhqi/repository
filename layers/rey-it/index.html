To what extent, and in which direction, is competitive intensity changing?

D4613th of 24

Layer 1 · External change forces

Judgement: Competitive intensity is rising modestly across European IT services, but little of it reaches Reply's core. The rise comes from large peers with idle capacity and weak growth, and from clients using AI to demand discounts. It bites on hourly-billed, large-deal and managed-services work, which is a small part of Reply.

  • Why intensity is rising. Large peers are growing slowly (Capgemini Continental Europe +2.8%, Accenture's 2027 guidance implying low-single-digit organic growth, EPAM cutting guidance to 2.5% to 5.0%, Globant -0.8%) and have capacity to fill. Capgemini prices 5% to 10% below Accenture to win large deals. Large integrators bid 5% to 10% below others on UK public frameworks (Kainos). Clients demand 15% to 30% off time-and-materials rates and 20% to 30% on managed-services renewals. Industry consolidation is under way (Persistent buying Nagarro at a 140% premium; Accenture's $9bn acquisition budget), and hyperscalers' forward-deployed engineers are a new kind of competitor.
  • Why Reply is less exposed. 71% of revenue is fixed-price turnkey work; projects average about €800k; application management is 14% and business process outsourcing 5%; 86% of staff work onshore. Morgan Stanley's checks show pricing broadly stable (28% of CIOs see vendors discounting harder, 35% see no change). Reply's revenue per employee held at €153k, and its organic growth lead over peers widened to 5 to 8 points.
  • Where it does reach Reply: the 29% of revenue billed on time and materials, concentrated in Germany and the UK, the weakest-margin regions; large multi-entity bids, where Reply's federation of boutiques is clumsy; and UK public frameworks.
  • Skew: the main tail risk is a large competitor using AI-built tooling to offer fixed-price outcomes 20% to 30% cheaper; no evidence of that yet.

How are input costs, input availability and supplier concentration changing, and is the net effect a headwind or a tailwind?

A631st of 24

Layer 1 · External change forces

Judgement: Net tailwind today. Talent, Reply's main input, is easier to hire, wage inflation is moderating and Reply has replaced costly contractors with employees. Direct services from hyperscalers (Microsoft has about 6,000 forward-deployed engineers) and from frontier labs. Client insourcing through captive centres. AI-native tools that clients deploy without consultants. Budget crowding-out (AI infrastructure spending of $780bn in 2026 rising to $1.4tn in 2028).
