Section 1 – Leadership and Governance Change 13 findings

Section summary
  1. executive transitions ensure orderly strategic continuity under CEO Walter Oblin as board refreshments add specialized international parcel logistics expertise
  2. corporate culture indicators remain stable but rising labor turnover to 17.7% highlights persistent recruitment and retention headwinds in Austria

Board and executive transitions

1.
Orderly CEO succession: Walter Oblin assumed the CEO role on October 1, 2024, succeeding Georg Pölzl after his planned 15-year tenure
2.
Successor executive profile: Oblin served as CFO since July 2012 and Deputy CEO since January 2019, ensuring deep operational familiarity
3.
External CFO appointment: Barbara Potisk-Eibensteiner became CFO on January 1, 2025, bringing 12 years of industrial financial management experience
4.
Strategic COO retention: Peter Umundum was reappointed as Deputy CEO and COO until March 2031, reinforcing continuity in logistics
5.
Executive Management Board of Österreichische Post AG and their terms of office Structured board refreshment: Eric Malitzke succeeded Peter E. Kruse as shareholder representative on April 9, 2025, to add international parcel expertise
6.
Mandated employee rotation: Veronika Schmidt succeeded Ulrike Ernstbrunner on September 18, 2025, as a designated employee representative

Corporate culture and labor metrics

7.
Elevated employee turnover: Group-wide employee turnover rose to 17.7% in 2025, up from 16.6% in 2024, exceeding the 17% target
8.
Stable engagement index: Employee engagement reached 82% in 2025 down from 84% in 2023, but remained at the 2030 target level
9.
Balanced gender representation: Female leadership representation rose to 36.2% in 2025 versus a 35.4% baseline, targeting 40% by 2030
10.
High collective coverage: Collective bargaining agreements cover 87.5% of Group staff and 99% in Austria, limiting labor disruption

Load-bearing execution risk: Maintaining stable labor relations is vital because personnel costs represent the largest expense item at 46.8% of revenue

Conditional retention impact: if demographic retirements accelerate, further staff turnover increases will challenge the group's 17% long-term target
13.
Other side of the story: Smooth leadership handovers may mask deep-seated operational friction; rising turnover could drive wage inflation that erodes CEE logistics profitability

Section 3 – External Change: Value-Chain Forces (Demand, Competition, Disruption, Regulation, Supply, Cycle, Macro) 44 findings

Section summary
  1. domestic e-commerce parcels drive structural volume gains while conventional letters suffer persistent high-single-digit declines
  2. competitive intensity is escalating via Amazon Logistics selfdelivery in Austria and dominant local networks in Türkiye
  3. automated lockers and direct shipping models reshape delivery but create severe volume volatility for legacy postal networks
  4. new customs regulations and upcoming domestic parcel taxes threaten cross-border Asian volumes in the near term Net read: escalating wage inflation pressures operating margins due to the highly labor-intensive nature of postal logistics
  5. post-pandemic demand patterns are normalizing to pre-crisis trajectories as physical retail channels fully recover
  6. hyperinflationary adjustments under IAS 29 and extreme currency devaluation introduce severe volatility into Turkish earnings
