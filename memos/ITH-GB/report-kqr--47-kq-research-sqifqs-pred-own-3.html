Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. The evidence comprehensively answers the Key Question regarding Ithaca Energy's $860 million acquisition of Suncor's offshore East Coast Canada assets. The transaction exhibits immediate cash flow accretion but carries substantial execution risks through elevated operating costs, material capital compliance obligations, and vessel uptime dependencies that could materially impact future earnings per share. This report presents a comprehensive analysis of Ithaca Energy's strategic entry into the Canadian offshore basin through its acquisition of Terra Nova (48% operated interest) and White Rose (40% interest) from Suncor Energy, alongside participation in the West White Rose extension project (38.6% interest). The transaction represents Ithaca's first international diversification beyond the UK Continental Shelf and adds 103 million barrels of 2P reserves with a 17-year reserve life. The analysis reveals that while broker consensus views the acquisition as immediately accretive to cash flow and dividends, the transaction's ultimate impact on earnings per share depends critically on achieving production ramp-ups, controlling unit operating costs that currently exceed $70 per barrel in the Atlantic region versus Ithaca's UK benchmark of $18 per barrel, and maintaining high vessel uptime on recently refurbished floating production units with historically volatile operational records. The transaction is structured with a locked-box effective date of 1 July 2026 and targeted completion in the first half of 2027, with an outside date of 31 December 2027. The $860 million upfront cash consideration will be adjusted for interim cash flows and capital expenditures generated between the effective date and completion, creating potential immediate value realization for Ithaca shareholders. Additionally, the deal includes up to $250 million in Brent-linked contingent payments over a 27-month period, with graduated thresholds of $80 per barrel to June 2027, $74 per barrel through 2027-2028, and $73 per barrel to September 2028, providing commodity price downside protection while allowing Suncor to participate in upside scenarios. Ithaca plans to fund the acquisition entirely through non-dilutive debt instruments, including cash on hand, its recently amended $1.8 billion reserve-based lending facility with improved pricing of SOFR plus 4.0 percent, and secured in-country Canadian financing. This financing strategy preserves immediate earnings per share accretion

by avoiding equity dilution, though it materially increases the company's leverage profile at a time when Moody's has reaffirmed Ithaca's Ba3 credit rating with concerns about rising indebtedness. The acquired assets are expected to deliver average net production of 30,000 barrels of oil equivalent per day over 2027-2031, ramping to peak levels of 35,000 to 40,000 boe/d by 2029 as the West White Rose extension achieves full production capacity of 80,000 barrels per day gross (45,000 boe/d net to operator Cenovus).

The transaction transfers C$500 million in regulatory well-compliance obligations commencing in 2027, with approximately $350 million concentrated in 2027-2028 to address subsea well integrity at the Terra Nova field as mandated by the Canada-Newfoundland and Labrador Offshore Energy Regulator. Furthermore, Ithaca assumes C$1.4 billion in present value abandonment and decommissioning liabilities, representing a fourfold increase over its existing UK North Sea provision of $321.65 million at year-end 2025.

Management expects to defer actual cash outflows into the 2040s based on projected field cessation dates of 2037 for Terra Nova and 2039 for White Rose, though early economic limits driven by sustained low commodity prices or operational underperformance could accelerate these obligations and severely compress future cash flows. Both the Terra Nova FPSO and SeaRose FPSO have undergone extensive asset life extension programs, with Terra Nova returning to production in November 2023 following drydock in Spain and SeaRose completing upgrades in early 2025 in Belfast.

Historical operational challenges include a January 2020 Dangerous Situation Order at Terra Nova due to fall hazards, a January 2018 regulatory suspension at SeaRose following an iceberg near-miss incident, and a November 2018 subsea flowline failure that released 250,000 litres of crude oil. These precedents underscore ongoing vessel reliability risks that could trigger production interruptions and impair the transaction's anticipated financial benefits.

The assets currently benefit from minimum provincial royalty rates of one percent due to substantial unrecovered carry-forward cost balances, remaining in pre-payout status. However, upon reaching tier-one payout, royalties escalate dramatically to 30 percent of net revenue at Terra Nova and 20 percent of net revenue at White Rose, with West White Rose subject to additional price-sensitive royalties of 1.25 percent to 12.50 percent when Brent exceeds $80 per barrel.

This tiered fiscal structure creates significant upside participation for the Government of Newfoundland and Labrador during high commodity price environments, which will materially reduce Ithaca's cash retention in future periods. Critical evidence gaps remain regarding the final locked-box cash adjustment amount that will reduce Ithaca's completion payment based on interim cash generation since 1 July 2026, the precise interest margins on secured Canadian debt facilities, and the detailed subsea engineering scope of the C$500 million well-compliance program.

Additionally, Cenovus Energy has not disclosed updated drilling schedules for West White Rose following marginal delays from the original late Q3 2026 first oil target, creating uncertainty around the timing of peak production volumes. The exact calculation mechanics of the Brent-linked contingent payment sharing formula adjusted for regional taxes and royalties remain proprietary, preventing definitive cash flow modeling until post-completion disclosures.

Alternative scenarios present material risks to consensus earnings expectations. If Brent crude prices decline below contingent payment thresholds, Ithaca avoids up to $250 million in deferred consideration but would face severe operating cash flow compression given the assets' elevated unit costs. Conversely, sustained high prices trigger both contingent payments to Suncor and stepped royalty escalations to the provincial government, creating a dual cash flow leakage that could dilute net margins. Operational delays at West White Rose or unscheduled vessel maintenance cycles would directly impair Ithaca's upgraded group production target of 140,000 to 150,000 boe/d and delay anticipated EPS accretion. Furthermore, if production declines accelerate due to reservoir underperformance or technical failures, the assets could reach premature economic limits before 2037, forcing Ithaca to fund the C$1.4 billion abandonment program decades earlier than projected and materially depressing future earnings per share.

Section 3 – Asset Economics, Royalties, and Capital Expenditures

Section summary
  1. The operating costs of Ithaca's newly acquired Canadian assets are structurally higher than its UK North Sea portfolio but are expected to decline as production scales.
  2. Both Terra Nova and White Rose remain in their minimum provincial royalty tiers due to substantial unrecovered carry-forward cost balances.
  3. Significant capital expenditure is required to fund the West White Rose extension and maintain the refurbished Terra Nova facilities.

Comparative Unit Operating Costs

1.
Elevated unit operating expenses: Ithaca Energy faces a significant cost differential as its new Canadian assets operate at a higher cost per barrel than its UK North Sea portfolio. Cenovus Energy reported that unit operating costs in the Atlantic region reached $70.87 per barrel in the third quarter of 2026. In contrast, Ithaca Energy reported a highly competitive UK unit operating expenditure of $18.0 per barrel in the first half of 2026. This cost gap highlights the near-term operational premium associated with early-stage offshore Canadian operations before production fully scales.
2.
Disciplined cost management: Ithaca Energy has successfully maintained its UK Continental Shelf operating costs well below the broader industry average. The company reported H1 2026 total operating costs of $419 million, resulting in unit costs that are significantly lower than the published UK basin average of $24 per barrel of oil equivalent. Management updated its full-year 2026 UK unit opex guidance on August 19, 2026, to a tight range of $17 to $19 per barrel of oil equivalent. This low-cost base in the North Sea provides a financial buffer to absorb the higher initial costs of the Canadian assets.
3.
Anticipated unit savings: Cenovus Energy expects that the start-up and subsequent ramp-up of the West White Rose extension will structurally lower operating costs in the Atlantic basin. Husky Energy noted in its May 2018 presentation that utilizing the existing SeaRose processing capacity for the extension will yield low incremental operating expenses. This shared infrastructure strategy is designed to drive down the overall per-barrel operating costs of the Atlantic region once peak volumes are reached. Consequently, the current high unit costs are expected to moderate significantly over the 2027 to 2031 period.
4.
Competitive peer benchmarking: Harbour Energy represents a lower-cost peer comparison with operating costs that remain structurally superior to Ithaca Energy. Fitch reported on September 9, 2026, that Harbour Energy maintains operating costs of approximately $15 per barrel of oil equivalent due to its larger scale of 500 kboe/d. This benchmark compares favorably to Ithaca's medium-term group target of approximately $20 per barrel of oil equivalent. Therefore, Ithaca must achieve stable operations in Canada to close this efficiency gap and protect future earnings per share.

Provincial Royalty Regimes

5.
Favorable royalty treatment: Both Terra Nova and White Rose are currently benefiting from the lowest provincial royalty tiers due to historical investments. Suncor Energy reported in its 2023 annual filing that these assets are calculated at the basic royalty rate because of substantial carry-forward cost balances. This minimum rate limits royalties to one percent of gross revenues. This pre-payout status will continue to insulate Ithaca's early cash flows until cumulative revenues exceed cumulative development costs.
6.
Stepped royalty tiers: Terra Nova's negotiated royalty framework features a tiered structure that increases significantly as the project achieves profitability milestones. Suncor Energy detailed that the basic royalty of one percent can graduate up to ten percent based on cumulative production. After tier-one payout, the royalty rate shifts to 30 percent of net revenue, with an additional tier-two rate reaching up to 42.5 percent. This progressive structure means that any major production windfall will eventually trigger substantially higher royalty payments.
7.
Negotiated fiscal terms: White Rose operates under a specific fiscal agreement with the Government of Newfoundland and Labrador that governs its tiered royalties. Suncor Energy reported that the basic royalty graduates to 7.5 percent before transitioning to a net royalty structure. The existing tier-one net royalty is calculated as the greater of 7.5 percent of gross revenue or 20 percent of net revenue, followed by a ten percent tier-two net royalty. This agreement ensures that the province captures a higher share of profits during periods of high commodity prices.
8.
Price-linked extensions: West White Rose is subject to an incremental royalty schedule that is directly tied to global Brent crude oil prices. Suncor Energy reported that the tier-one net royalty rate escalates from ten percent to 15 percent, and reaches 30 percent when Brent exceeds $80 per barrel before tier-two payout. An additional price-sensitive royalty ranging from 1.25 percent to 12.50 percent net revenue is applied based on price thresholds. Consequently, Ithaca's cash flows from the extension will face higher fiscal drag during high price environments.
