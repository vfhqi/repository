Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Portfolio operating leverage will determine the trajectory of Schouw & Co's earnings per share over the next four years.

Defining the core inquiry

1.
Defined core inquiry: "How much fixed-cost operating leverage does this business model carry, how much scope is there for it to work over the next 18 months to four years, and, where it is relevant to the company's strategy, how feasible is cost elimination or reduction, both incremental and in material large cuts?"
2.
Conglomerate earnings sensitivity: Schouw & Co's consolidated earnings per share remain highly sensitive to operating leverage across its seven segments because these businesses must navigate volatile input costs. The company generated DKK 34.1 billion in revenue and DKK 2.9 billion in EBITDA in 2025. This means even small changes in segment margins from ongoing footprint restructurings will directly impact the group's bottom-line EPS.
3.
Lean active ownership: Active ownership from a very lean headquarters of twenty employees shapes portfolio-wide cost discipline. Schouw & Co delegates operational execution to segment managers while centralising treasury and capital allocation. This decentralized structure means that while headquarters maintains strict financial oversight, actual cost-cutting execution relies on local capabilities.

Segment operating leverage mechanisms

4.
Aquaculture feed dynamics: BioMar exhibits unique margin protection but limited direct fixed-cost operating leverage because it operates under a predominantly costplus model. Raw materials represent the vast majority of production costs, which BioMar contractually passes through to farming customers. This pricing power protects earnings from volatile input spikes but ties EPS growth directly to volume expansion.
5.
Electronics manufacturing cyclicality: Electronics manufacturing services provider GPV carries substantial operating leverage during cyclical downturns as demonstrated by the 2024-2025 destocking phase. Revenue fell slightly in 2025, which pressured margins and forced management to execute deep footprint consolidations from 19 to 14 sites. Reaching GPV's 2030 EBITDA target of 10% will require maintaining these leaner structures during the cyclical demand recovery.
6.
Hydraulics wind exposure: Hydraulic systems specialist HydraSpecma faces high operating sensitivity because of renewables customer project postponements and cyclicality in the mobile OEM segments. To safeguard margins in 2025, the company consolidated its footprint by phasing out its Swedish Tranemo site and moving operations to Stargard, Poland. The speed of wind OEM recoveries will dictate how quickly these relocations generate positive operating leverage.

Cost elimination and turnaround feasibility

7.
Remanufacturing overhead pressure: Borg Automotive operates with high fixed labor and overhead costs because of the labor-intensive nature of remanufacturing cores for the European aftermarket. To counter severe price competition and wage inflation in Poland, Borg launched its Refine4Future programme to deliver DKK 100 million in annual savings by 2027. This turn-around requires shifting UK production to lower-cost facilities in Poland and Tunisia, making Polish wage trends a critical variable for EPS.
8.
Nonwovens and outdoor apparel: Spunmelt nonwovens producer Fibertex Personal Care and the newly acquired Spectre segment face distinct cost structures and pass-through limits that affect their contribution to group earnings. Fibertex must actively manage volatile raw material and energy prices through contractual passthrough clauses. Meanwhile, Spectre's premium outdoor apparel manufacturing in Vietnam brings high-margin profiles but relies on scaling brand partnerships to achieve operating efficiencies.

side of the story: Decentralised business units could suffer from uneven execution of restructuring programmes if localized operational challenges offset group-level cost discipline. Delays in footprint optimization or unanticipated restructuring outlays like the DKK 100 million incurred in 2025 can dilute expected EPS benefits. Ultimately, the group's decentralized model limits hands-on operational intervention, meaning cost targets could slip if local market conditions deteriorate further.

Section 3 – GPV and EMS Sector Dynamics during Destocking

Section summary
  1. GPV suffered significant margin contraction as revenue declined during the 2024–2025 global electronics destocking cycle.
  2. Structural footprint consolidations and aggressive head-count reductions successfully stabilized operating margins ahead of a projected demand recovery.

GPV margin compression during destocking

1.
Declining industrial revenue: GPV experienced a severe top-line contraction as global industrial customers aggressively destocked their inventories during 2024 and 2025. According to Schouw & Co's 2025 annual report, GPV's revenue declined 15% from DKK 10,450 million in 2023 to DKK 8,931 million in 2024. The soft demand continued into 2025, dragging sales down by an additional 3% to DKK 8,702 million. This persistent volume pressure means future earnings per share remain highly dependent on a cyclical industrial volume recovery.
2.
Peer margin comparison: Listed electronics manufacturing services peers also experienced severe margin compression during this destocking cycle. For example, Scandinavian competitor Kitron reported a drop in its EBIT margin from 9.12% in 2023 to 7.43% in 2024. Similarly, Nordic competitor Note reported a drop in sales from SEK 3,901 million in 2024 to SEK 3,814 million in 2025 as demand was put under pressure. These peer comparisons demonstrate that GPV's margin compression was part of a systemic sector-wide downturn.

Kitron guidance miss and drivers

3.
Guidance adjustments: Kitron missed its initial 2024 financial guidance by a significant margin due to deteriorating European industrial activity. In December 2023, Kitron originally targeted 2024 revenue of EUR 700 million to EUR 800 million and EBIT of EUR 60 million to EUR 74 million. However, the company subsequently downgraded its 2024 target to revenue of EUR 635 million to EUR 660 million and EBIT of EUR 44 million to EUR 50 million. This substantial adjustment illustrates how sudden customer order postponements can rapidly erase expected operational fixed-cost leverage.
4.
Underlying failure drivers: The primary drivers of Kitron's guidance miss were severe slowdowns in Central and Eastern Europe alongside elevated customer inventories. Kitron's fourth-quarter 2024 report revealed that its Central and Eastern European market collapsed 34% because of weakened electrification demand. To defend its profitability, Kitron was forced to incur EUR 4.8 million in restructuring costs to reset its capacity. This case indicates that rigid manufacturing footprints can cause material operating margin degradation when cyclical markets contract.

Footprint moves and delivered savings

5.
Footprint consolidation actions: GPV aggressively streamlined its manufacturing network by reducing its operational sites from 19 to 14 to eliminate structural excess capacity. These optimization efforts included closing a factory in Malaysia and divesting a small site in Austria. Furthermore, GPV consolidated its Slovakian electronics activities into the new mega site in Piestany and the established site in Nova Dubnica. This footprint migration lowered the group's structural cost base to establish a future-proof operational manufacturing platform.
6.
Realized cost savings: The footprint consolidations are expected to deliver DKK 100 million in annual savings as the restructuring programs reach completion. To achieve these efficiencies, GPV incurred DKK 17 million in one-off footprint and severance costs in 2025, following a massive reduction in its workforce. GPV had previously reduced its employee base by more than 1,000 workers to mitigate the declining top line. These aggressive cost-out actions mean GPV's operating margins should expand as capacity utilization normalizes.

Target validation and mix shifts

7.
Ambitious margin targets: GPV targets a 10% EBITDA margin by 2030, a goal first established during its 2022 Capital Markets Day and recently reconfirmed. While flat near-term revenue of DKK 8.5 billion to DKK 9.0 billion is expected for 2026, GPV expects EBITDA to rise to DKK 690 million to DKK 750 million. Reaching this 10% target from the 7.37% level of 2025 will require systematic operational leverage and scale. This profitability uplift relies heavily on the successful implementation of its group-wide ERP system.
8.
Strategic customer mix: Reaching the 2030 margin target requires a substantial mix shift toward high-margin, structurally growing verticals like defence. GPV's 2026 presentation highlighted defence, semiconductor equipment, and clean-tech as its key growth segments. According to DNB Carnegie's July 2026 note, high-mix electronics manufacturing requires extensive human engineering skill which supports premium value-add pricing. Consequently, shifting the portfolio toward these specialized applications is more critical for margin expansion than pursuing raw commoditized low- margin volume.
9.
Contrary evidence: High-mix EMS peer Scanfil demonstrated that some contract manufacturers managed to expand their profitability despite severe destocking headwinds. Scanfil's adjusted EBITA margin held up at 7.35% in late 2025 due to rapid cost-control actions. These resilient peer results suggest that severe margin compression is not inevitable if supply chains and labor inputs are managed with extreme flexibility. This counter-precedent implies that GPV's historical margin pressure may have been exacerbated by slow integration of its Enics acquisition.
10.
Other side of the story: Prolonged weakness in European industrial markets could prevent GPV from achieving its 10% EBITDA target if low capacity utilization offsets footprint savings. While footprint consolidations have removed DKK 100 million in fixed overhead, a stagnant demand environment will leave GPV with under-utilized mega-factories. If global accounts continue to scale back orders, GPV will fail to capture the volume-driven scale efficiencies required to reach its longterm financial goals. Therefore, the timing of GPV's margin expansion remains highly vulnerable to macroeconomic cycles.

Section 4 – Footprint Moves and Restructuring Durability at Borg and HydraSpecma

Section summary
  1. Persistent restructuring outlays and inflationary headwinds across Schouw & Co's automotive and hydraulics subsidiaries have repeatedly diluted the long-term earnings benefits of their manufacturing footprint relocations.

erosion from Polish minimum-wage increases, while HydraSpecma relies on newly built Polish and Chinese facilities to secure future volume leverage.

Borg Automotive Refine4Future implementation and wage friction

1.
Ambitious cost-reduction targets: Borg Automotive launched its Refine4Future transformation plan in autumn 2025 to counteract a challenging combination of weak market demand, rising production costs, and fierce competitive pressure on brake calipers. Management expects the programme to deliver up to DKK 100 million in annual earnings improvements when fully implemented in 2027. However, achieving these targets is subject to high operational uncertainty as the subsidiary navigates a polarized automotive aftermarket.
2.
Refine4Future savings distribution: The DKK 100 million in expected annual savings is structured across four distinct pillars. Manufacturing footprint optimization is slated to deliver the largest share of up to DKK 50 million. Logistics footprint optimization and commercial excellence are each expected to provide up to DKK 20 million, while SG&A adjustments are projected to yield the final DKK 10 million.
3.
UK production exit completed: A major cornerstone of the footprint optimization was the exit of UK manufacturing, which was finalized ahead of schedule in Q1 2026. Borg Automotive completely closed its large facility in England and shifted those operations to Poland and Tunisia. Production of steering products was integrated into Lublin, Poland, while basic starter and alternator lines were moved to the newly acquired facility in Tunisia.
4.
Tunisian capacity expansion: To facilitate this geographic shift, Borg Automotive acquired a dedicated subcontractor in Tunisia at the end of 2024. The facility operates with approximately 165 employees and possesses over 40 years of starter and alternator assembly experience. Management doubled production output in Tunisia in 2025 to free up capacity at its Polish sites for other high-demand product lines.
5.
Polish wage inflation erosion: The expected savings from relocating to Poland face significant erosion due to severe localized wage inflation. Schouw's President Jens Bjerg Sørensen noted that substantial increases in Polish minimum wages have actively pressured Borg Automotive's operating margins. This wage pressure, combined with lower demand and intense price competition from Chinese imports, brought Borg's consolidated EBITDA down to DKK 0 in 2025.
6.
New commercial organization: Borg Automotive paired its footprint changes with a new commercial organization and a revised go-to-market strategy. This initiative aimed to secure pricing power and counter aggressive market price erosion. While early benefits materialized in Q2 2026 as EBITDA recovered to DKK 38 million, the segment remains highly vulnerable to Polish labor rate dynamics.

Following this relocation, HydraSpecma closed and phased out its old production site in Tranemo, Sweden.

The facility consolidates two existing Chinese production sites into a single zero-emission plant equipped with solar panels and heating pumps. The facility became fully operational in Q2 2026 to support the company's long-term growth ambitions in the Asia-Pacific region.
9.

These one-off costs were slightly lower than the company's initial guidance of DKK 30 to 35 million. Despite these cash outlays, the consolidation of Stargard operations is expected to significantly improve operational efficiency starting in the second half of 2026.
10.

The move was designed to avoid participating in the severe price competition and structural overcapacity impacting the Asian nonwovens market. The temporary reduction in capacity resulted in approximately DKK 15 million in one-off restructuring costs in 2024.
11.
Malaysian specialty upgrade: Following the capacity cuts, Fibertex Personal Care completed a technology upgrade of a spunbond line at its Sendayan facility in Malaysia at the end of 2025. The upgrade enables the production of advanced, higher value-added specialty nonwovens. DNB Carnegie highlights that this transition allows Fibertex to manufacture patented elastic nonwovens, shielding the business from commoditized price erosion.
