Galp (GALP-PT): what the third-quarter results do to the existing case

These results leave the park standing and confirm its central claim, that the price was paying for a restructuring and a refining windfall the company did not control; the one thing that has moved a long way is the price and multiple, which now sit close to where the case put its disappointment branch, before the event that branch was built on has happened.

What changed

  • The Moeve combination is dead. Galp ended the talks on 26 October 2026 because the parties could not align on terms. Management is not seeking a replacement transaction and is in no hurry on a renewables partner. The case held a signed Moeve agreement with a stated distribution as a re-open trigger, put signing at 65 percent, and named "no Moeve signing by mid-2027" as an alarm level. The trigger is gone and the alarm is certain.
  • The quarter beat, on the commodity. Group RCA EBITDA EUR 1,522 million in 3Q26 against a consensus of EUR 1,471 million; RCA net income EUR 682 million against EUR 638 million. Industrial and Midstream EBITDA EUR 639 million against EUR 315 million in 3Q25. The refining indicator margin was USD 34.00 a barrel and the realised margin USD 25.70 after a EUR 172 million hedge drag. The case's test for this print was a realised margin above USD 13; it was roughly double that.
  • Guidance raised again. FY2026 RCA EBITDA to about EUR 5 billion from about EUR 4 billion; operating cash flow to close to EUR 3.9 billion from about EUR 3 billion; the full-year realised margin to USD 25 from USD 13; Brent to USD 90 from USD 80. The case expected the print to be the first test of a lower deck. The deck went up.
  • The windfall is already fading. The spot indicator margin was about USD 10 a barrel in October 2026. Management expects double digits into the first quarter of 2027. The review also carries a broker reading that the full-year guidance implies a fourth quarter near USD 34; that does not sit easily with an October near USD 10, and we flag it rather than resolve it.
  • Cash quality is weaker than the headline. Adjusted operating cash flow EUR 1,223 million was helped by lower cash tax than expected; free cash flow was EUR 271 million against EUR 548 million a year earlier; trade payables rose to EUR 1,510 million at September 2026 from EUR 860 million at December 2025; IFRS net income was EUR 201 million after a EUR 453 million net special charge. Net debt EUR 1,428 million, 0.35 times RCA EBITDA.
  • Hedges are not off in 2027. About 15 percent of 2027 volumes are hedged slightly above USD 15 a barrel. The case counted hedges rolling off in 2027 as a mis-modelled positive.
  • Namibia has slipped and stays on the balance sheet. The first appraisal result the review points to is Mopane-4X in the second quarter of 2027, where the case had a spud in 4Q26. No further farm-down is being considered. The Venus decision has continued to slip.
  • The state has moved. A 33 percent windfall contribution on 2026 profits above the 2024 to 2025 average by more than 20 percent is before parliament. The government is reviewing an increase in its 8 percent stake and has spoken of concentrating it in a refinery-focused company.
  • Distributions unchanged. One third of operating cash flow; EUR 0.70 floor; EUR 230 million of the EUR 250 million buyback done by end-September 2026; anything more is decided with the full-year results.
  • The sell-side response. The shares fell about 10 percent in early trading, from EUR 22.96 to a close of EUR 20.40 on 27 October 2026. Morgan Stanley cut to Equal-weight on 5 November 2026 with a target of EUR 23.00 from EUR 24.00, while raising FY2026 EPS about 5 percent and 2026 and 2027 EBITDA about 2 percent. Jefferies held at Hold, target EUR 21.00. Targets range from EUR 18.40 to EUR 28.00.
  • The price. EUR 19.355 on 13 November 2026, 11.9 percent below the case's reference of EUR 21.97 and 1.8 percent below the 200-day average. The dashboard multiple is 10.9 times, the 25th percentile of the stock's own ten years.

The challenge

First reading: the premium was for the restructuring, not for Namibia, and the case's multiple branches are tied to the wrong event. The case attributes the two-turn premium to Namibia and builds its multiple from three Mopane outcomes: 13.5 times, 12.0 times, and 10.0 times on disappointment, blended to 11.65 times. The shares now trade at 10.9 times with no appraisal result at all. If a failed downstream deal alone took the multiple most of the way to the disappointment branch, then the disappointment branch is largely priced, a poor well has less multiple left to take, and a good well is an option the price no longer pays for. That inverts the case's skew. For this to be right, the de-rating must be specific to the lost catalyst and must hold while brokers still carry Namibia in their targets; the width of the target range, which the review attributes to Namibia assumptions, says some of them do. It fails if the remaining premium to the Southern European integrateds is still Namibia's and the well disappoints.

Second reading: the distribution the case tied to Moeve can arrive without Moeve. The case made a stated distribution conditional on a signed deal. But the framework management reaffirmed is mechanical. One third of close to EUR 3.9 billion is about EUR 1.3 billion; against that the review shows a EUR 253 million interim dividend and a EUR 250 million buyback programme. The gap is undecided until the full-year results. If management honours its own rule on the upgraded cash flow, the windfall year is paid out rather than merely printed, and the bet the case declines, that the company keeps the windfall, is partly won in cash whatever cracks do next. For this to be right, the rule must be applied to the raised figure, and the payables build and the cash-tax timing must not reverse first. Against it: free cash flow of EUR 271 million in the quarter, a broker view that extraordinary returns are unlikely, and a management that has explicitly refused pressure to pay more.

Third reading, and the one that runs against the shares: Sines has become a national asset that cannot be crystallised. The case treated Portuguese sovereignty caution as a reason Moeve might not sign. The stronger form is that the termination, the windfall contribution and the stake review are one event: the state taxes the refinery's best year and narrows who may own it. On that reading the downstream is locked in, standalone capital commitments rise, and the holding-company discount the case names as an archetype is no longer forming but imposed. For this to be right, the contribution must be enacted on 2026 profits and the stake move must proceed. The company's own account is commercial, not political, and nothing in the review tests it.

We do not put up a fourth reading that the walk-away proves operator quality. It is consistent with discipline and equally consistent with a controlling shareholder's preferences, and the evidence does not separate them.

What it does to the case

  • 'TSR/Valuation', pinned D: under clear pressure upward. The store now reads B at 10.9 times, entirely on price; the forward consensus figure beneath it is unchanged.
  • 'Modal return' F: less pressure, same side. On the case's own unchanged arithmetic, its eighteen-month value of EUR 19.93 is about 3 percent above today's price and its three-year value of EUR 21.78 about 12.5 percent above, roughly 2 and 4 percent a year against a 20 percent bar. The case clears below EUR 15.2 on the modal path; the price would have to fall a further 21 percent.
  • 'Re-rating' D and 'Limited multiple risk' D: less pressure. The case's alarm of a multiple below its five-year median of 11.33 times has been crossed on the dashboard's basis; the de-rating risk it named has in good part happened.
  • 'Staircase' B: more pressure. The Moeve steps are gone, the Mopane and Venus steps are later.
  • 'Change Forces' B and the 'Major Corporate Change' setup at C: more pressure. A perimeter force has reversed.
  • 'Financial Outputs' D and 'Sell-side upgrades' B: the near year is stronger and the reason is still the crack; the same. The October margin sits at the case's USD 10 alarm.
  • 'Political, regulatory' D: more pressure.
  • 'No perception shocks' C: more pressure; one occurred.
  • 'Few Key Drivers' C: slightly less; five became four.
  • 'Foundations' C and 'Case Riskiness' D: the same.

What would settle it

  • The second review, four working days on. It carries broker estimates and targets once they have landed: whether FY2027 consensus EPS moves towards the case's EUR 2.2 trigger, whether others follow Morgan Stanley down, and whether targets still carry Namibia. That speaks to the first reading. It does not carry any new company disclosure, and it settles neither the distribution nor the well.
  • Full-year results, expected February 2027. The distribution decision under the one-third framework. This settles the second reading.
  • The fourth-quarter realised margin, against a USD 10 October spot and guidance that needs far more.
  • Mopane-4X, second quarter of 2027 on the review's dating.
  • Parliament's decision on the contribution and the government's on its stake. Neither has a date in the evidence. The company has not committed to say more about why the talks failed, so the third reading may stay open: that is a question nothing scheduled answers.
