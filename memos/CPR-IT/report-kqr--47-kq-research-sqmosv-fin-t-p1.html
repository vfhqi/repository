Section 2 – Campari's Fixed-Cost Base and Operating Leverage

Financial analysts estimate that selling, general, and administrative expenses represent approximately fifty-six percent of total operating costs. The high proportion of structural overheads means that any deceleration in sales volumes immediately impacts profitability because these fixed costs cannot be easily adjusted in the short term.
2.
Negative volume leverage: Campari experienced significant profit dilution during the global pandemic of 2020 due to the lower absorption of fixed costs. The company reported an organic sales decline of eleven percent while its adjusted EBIT fell organically by thirty percent in the first half of 2020. This severe performance contraction demonstrated how negative operating leverage quickly erodes profit margins when sales volumes drop in key channels like the on-premise.
3.
Positive margin expansion: Campari achieved strong organic sales growth of nineteen percent in the first half of 2022. This top-line momentum drove organic adjusted EBIT growth of twenty-eight percent as fixed supply chain costs were better absorbed. The rapid expansion in profitability during this recovery phase proved that the company benefits from significant operating leverage when organic sales growth accelerates above historical averages.
4.
Contrary evidence: Campari experienced organic margin dilution in the first half of 2023 despite reporting healthy double-digit organic sales growth of fourteen percent.
