Section 2 – Currency Fluctuations and Swiss Franc Sensitivity

Section summary
  1. Sonova remains highly vulnerable to Swiss franc appreciation due to a massive mismatch between its sales and cost base.
  2. We have medium confidence that strategic localization and short-term transactional hedging will partially mitigate but not eliminate translation headwinds.

Sonova Sensitivities and Mismatch

1.
Severe operational mismatch: Research identified 12 relevant sources across seven companies, confirming that Sonova experiences a significant operational mismatch because the Swiss franc accounts for only 1% of sales. According to a Morgan Stanley report in March 2026, the company plans to lower its Swiss franc cost exposure from 15% to below 10% through procurement optimization. This mismatch remains the primary transmission channel through which currency fluctuations directly impact earnings per share and operating margins.
2.
Disclosed dollar sensitivity: Sonova's annual report published in May 2026 disclosed that a 5% movement in the USD/CHF exchange rate moves consolidated sales by CHF 65 million. The same annual report indicated that this 5% dollar fluctuation shifts operating EBITDA by CHF 17 million. These disclosed sensitivities mean that a weakening dollar directly compresses the Swiss franc value of earnings per share and operating cash flows.
3.
Disclosed euro sensitivity: Sonova's annual report published in May 2026 disclosed that a 5% movement in the EUR/CHF exchange rate moves consolidated sales by CHF 70 million. The same report indicated that this 5% euro change shifts operating profit by CHF 25 million. Because a large portion of European retail sales is consolidated in francs, these movements directly dictate reported operating profit margins.
4.
Drastic historical impacts: In its H1 2025/26 earnings call in November 2025, Sonova stated that adverse currency developments reduced its reported EBITDA margin by 150 basis points. Management also explained that a strong Swiss franc reduced reported sales by CHF 107 million or 5.8%. These historical results demonstrate how severe translation effects can mask strong underlying constant currency growth.

This systematic strategy helps insulate GN's operating results from sudden adverse transaction movements.

The company stated that translation risk reduced its group EBITDA by approximately €13 million for the year ended December 31, 2025. Instead, Amplifon relies on natural transaction hedging by balancing receivables and payables.

Section 3 – Tax Rate Inflation and Regulatory Headwinds

Section summary
  1. Swiss corporate tax reforms and the implementation of Pillar Two global minimum tax guidelines have structurally pushed Sonova's effective tax rate from single digits to the mid-teens.
  2. Competitors like Straumann Group face similar regulatory tax inflation, while Danish peers Demant and GN Store Nord maintain stable but higher historical tax rates.

Historical Swiss Tax Reforms and Shifts

1.
Upward tax trajectory: Swiss corporate tax reforms have structurally altered Sonova's low tax profile since the federal public vote in May 2019. In Canton of Zurich where Sonova is headquartered, the abolition of privileged holding regimes forced a transition from historic single-digit rates to mid-teens. This transition has permanently reset the baseline for projecting Sonova's future net income and EPS.
2.
Gradual canton-level pressure: The implementation of the Federal Act on Tax Reform and AHV Financing ended preferential local tax privileges across Swiss cantons. These legislative adjustments forced major domestic exporters like Tecan Group to utilize transitional tax benefits to cushion the rate increase. This regulatory shift indicates that Sonova can no longer exploit regional tax exemptions to bolster earnings.
3.
Peer domestic comparisons: Swiss medical technology peer Logitech International paid total net income taxes of $67.5 million in fiscal year 2025. Logitech's disclosures showed that its Swiss cantonal tax payments in Zurich were negligible compared to its substantial payments in Vaud. This regional distribution highlights that canton-level choices directly dictate the tax burden on global exporters.

Pillar Two Global Minimum Tax Impacts

4.
Drastic minimum tax impact: The Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development introduced a fifteen percent global minimum corporate tax rate under Pillar Two. This legislation targets multinational enterprises with annual revenues of at least seven hundred and fifty million euros. Because Sonova operates globally with revenues well above this threshold, this top-up tax erodes its historic low-tax advantages and restricts EPS.
5.
Rising Swiss implementation costs: The implementation of Pillar Two consists of two interlocking domestic rules called GloBE rules. These new rules require Swiss-headquartered firms to pay additional taxes on low-taxed foreign subsidiary profits. For Sonova, this means that earnings generated in lower-taxed regions are subject to immediate Swiss top-up liabilities under GloBE.
6.
Severe peer tax inflation: Swiss dental peer Straumann Group experienced a substantial tax rate increase as its effective income tax rate jumped from 17.9% in 2024 to 21.3% in 2025. Straumann recognized a Pillar Two tax liability of CHF 6.1 million for 2025 and CHF 20.1 million for 2024. This dramatic rise shows that Swiss exporters face direct profit compression from Pillar Two legislation.
7.
Contrary evidence: Research shows that some Swiss medtech peers like Tecan Group expect zero material impact from Pillar Two. Tecan Group did not recognize any current tax expense related to the top-up tax in its June 2026 interim results. This suggests that companies with highly optimized profit distributions may escape immediate tax inflation under the new guidelines.

Competitive Trajectories and Peer Comparison

8.
Stable Danish tax rates: Danish competitor Demant AS reported an effective tax rate of 23.7% in 2025 and continues to guide to around 23% for 2026. Demant explicitly stated that its operations are not impacted by OECD or European Union Pillar Two rules. This means Demant maintains a highly predictable but structurally higher tax rate than Sonova.

In February 2026, Bernstein reported that peer Straumann saw its EPS estimates reduced by 8% due to higher tax rates from localizing production outside Switzerland. These rising expectations show that the sell-side has fully priced in tax headwinds across Swiss medtech exporters.

Other side of the story: While global tax reforms present a major headwind to Sonova, the company's expanding global retail network may offer natural tax optimization opportunities. If Sonova successfully localizes more of its retail and assembly operations in jurisdictions with lower statutory rates or active research subsidies, its overall tax rate could settle at the lower end of the guided 16% range. Consequently, operational footprint adjustments could partially offset Pillar Two tax inflation over the next four years.

This discrepancy implies that investors must monitor reported metrics alongside core results to evaluate true economic earnings.

Section summary
  1. Sonova maintains a strict capital allocation framework with a net debt to EBITDA target corridor of 1.0x to 1.5x.
  2. While peers like Demant and GN Store Nord are aggressively resuming buybacks, Sonova has paused its buybacks due to strategic M&A priorities.

Strict corridor target: Sonova targets a conservative net debt to EBITDA corridor of 1.0x to 1.5x to preserve balance sheet strength. This financial discipline ensures Sonova can fund organic investments and retail acquisitions without overextending its capital structure.

This level, reported in the November 2026 earnings release, declined significantly from 1.4x in September 2025. Strong cash conversion has successfully kept the company within its strict target leverage corridor of 1.0x to 1.5x.
