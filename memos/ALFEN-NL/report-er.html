Section 2 – Sell-side and market focus prior to these recent earnings

Section summary
  1. Alfen is undergoing a fundamental corporate restructuring that replaces long-term leadership and decentralizes operational control.
  2. Operational focus has shifted from hyper-growth to strict working capital management and gross margin protection.
  3. High competitive pressure in EV charging is being offset by expanding energy storage and smart grid volumes.
  4. Structural Dutch grid bottlenecks and transition costs represent critical risks to the company's 2027 recovery goals.

The segment is projected to show negative revenue growth compared to the prior year despite the recent introduction of the new Plus product line. Price adjustments are being implemented to reposition the charging business in key European markets.
3.
Delayed grid expansion: Deutsche Bank analyst Papaccio noted in October 2026 that Alfen's smart grid solutions division faces ongoing bottlenecks that restrict substation shipments. Shortages of grid connections and a lack of skilled workforce continue to delay private transformer substation projects in the Netherlands. Regulators are addressing grid system investments, but any positive financial impacts will take time to materialize for Alfen.
4.
Slashed earnings estimates: Jefferies analyst Kerstens downgraded Alfen to Hold in August 2026 after lowering fiscal year 2026 operating projections. The analyst cut the company's full-year adjusted EBITDA projection by 32% to 18.8 million euros from the previous estimate of 27.8 million euros. Higher personnel expenses of 8.0 million euros related to organizational restructuring are expected to erode near-term operating margins during the transition.
5.
Expected revenue recovery: Jefferies analyst Kerstens projected in October 2026 that Alfen's revenues would grow 7% to 466.8 million euros in fiscal year 2026 compared to 435.6 million euros in fiscal year 2025. Revenue growth is expected to accelerate to 10% in fiscal year 2027 and 12% in subsequent years as the energy storage and smart grid divisions expand. The recovery is projected to drive EBITDA margins to 9.6% by fiscal year 2030, representing substantial long-term operational leverage for investors.

Improved cash conversion: Jefferies analyst Kerstens highlighted in August 2026 that Alfen's operating cash flow rose significantly to 36.5 million euros in the first half of 2026 from 10.8 million euros in the first half of 2025. The positive cash performance was driven by a 23.1 million euro reduction in net working capital. Lower inventory levels allowed Alfen to pivot to a net cash position of 6.2 million euros from a net debt position of 20.7 million euros in fiscal year 2025.
7.
Divergent peer growth: Deutsche Bank research in June 2026 indicated that Alfen's stock valuation had risen alongside its charging peer Fastned due to higher market-wide expectations for regional electrification. Fastned reported strong charging revenue growth exceeding 47% in fiscal year 2025, supported by expanding station utilization. In contrast, Alfen's charging business is unlikely to see immediate benefits, meaning that Alfen's revised earnings forecasts are driven by grid congestion products rather than charging hardware.
8.
Weakening peer momentum: DNB Carnegie analyst Skogman maintained a cautious stance on charging hardware peer Kempower in October 2026, lowering its target price to 8.0 euros. Kempower faces weak order momentum and customer base consolidation, illustrating that charging specialists face widespread demand challenges across Europe. High interest rates and slow decision-making among charging point operators continue to compress hardware volumes.
9.
Elevated valuation risk: Inderes analyst commentary in February 2026 noted that Alfen belongs to a group of energy transition peers with low EV-to-sales multiples but high near-term earnings-based valuations. Earnings-based valuation of turnaround companies is highly sensitive to short-term profitability fluctuations. Turnaround progress remains difficult to value without relying on longer-term performance estimates spanning multiple fiscal years.
10.
Reorganized business structure: Alfen adopted a new decentralized business unit structure on May 1, 2026, to improve customer proximity and accelerate strategic execution. The organizational restructuring aims to align capabilities with revised strategic priorities including digitalization and product excellence. Management expects that decentralization will streamline decisionmaking across the smart grid, energy storage, and EV charging segments.
11.
Abrupt leadership changes: Alfen announced that Chief Financial Officer Onno Krap would step down as of July 1, 2026, following the transition to the business unit model. Investors expressed concern regarding the short notice of the departure because Onno Krap was seen as a safe pair of hands. Bart Meussen was subsequently appointed as the interim Chief Financial Officer to provide stability to the management team under Chief Executive Officer Michael Colijn.
12.
Standardized storage offering: Alfen reiterated its fiscal year 2026 guidance in August 2026, expecting revenue to be between 435 million and 475 million euros. The energy storage division is pivoting toward standardized, utility-scale systems to help protect gross margins. Standardized products are expected to drive robust medium-term growth as European network operators scale up their grid-scale storage installations.
13.
Diversified battery technology: Alfen and CATL established a landmark partnership in July 2026 to deploy advanced sodium-ion battery storage systems across Europe. Sodium-ion technology provides supply-chain diversification and reduces dependence on volatile lithium raw materials. Management does not expect the first commercial sodium-ion project deployment to occur before the second half of 2027.
14.
Key vertical integration: A former Alfen Head of Product Management noted in June 2026 that vertical integration and service excellence are critical to surviving in the competitive European storage market. Purchasing decisions of utility customers are highly sensitive to product safety records and execution timelines. Selecting a larger Tier 1 battery supplier is consistently preferred by utility customers to mitigate operational and technical project risks.
15.
Mixed regional adoption: Alfen's market reports in August 2026 indicated highly divergent electric vehicle adoption rates across its core Western European geographies. Mature charging markets like the Netherlands showed stagnant registration growth, whereas expansion markets like Italy and Spain accelerated by 78% and 37% respectively. Capturing early adopter demand in southern Europe is critical to reviving hardware sales over the next three years.
16.
Other side of the story: Alfen's projected profitability recovery could occur much faster than expected if the Dutch Crisis Act successfully accelerates grid connection permitting. Easing of grid operators' labor constraints and faster nitrogen permitting would allow utility clients to deploy transformer substations rapidly. Rapid deployment of grid infrastructure would accelerate project backlog conversion and immediately boost highmargin smart grid revenues.
