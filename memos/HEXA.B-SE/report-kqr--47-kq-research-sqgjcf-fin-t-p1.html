Section 2 – Hexagon Post-Spin Operating Leverage and Cost Drivers

Section summary
  1. Hexagon post-spin cost base remains highly exposed to Swiss franc salaries and rising central administrative expenses.
  2. Gross margin pressure from sensor mix shifts is offset by early cost restructuring savings and operational volume drop-through.

Post-spin cost structure

1.
High fixed costs: Hexagon retains a relatively high fixed cost base in its continuing operations following the completed spin-off of its higher-margin software business Octave in May 2026. Management expects R&D expenses recognized directly in the income statement to remain within a targeted range of 12% to 15% of sales. This persistent fixed spending pattern implies that future operational leverage remains highly dependent on driving volume growth over fixed overheads.
2.
Persistent currency exposure: Hexagon faces substantial exposure to the Swiss franc because approximately 19% of its salaries and a significant portion of its production costs are denominated in this currency. In accordance with the Group Treasury Policy, the company does not hedge its transaction currency exposure, meaning a strong Swiss franc directly increases its operating expenses. This exposure acts as a structural margin headwind unless the company can accelerate its production relocation plans.
3.
Escalating central expenses: Hexagon reported that central administrative expenses escalated to EUR 94.30 million in the second quarter of 2026 compared to EUR 84.50 million in the prior year. This upward trend is partially driven by transitional corporate duplication and standalone administrative costs following the Octave separation. These rising corporate expenses represent a structural cost drag that will limit near-term operating margin expansion.
4.
Segmented cost profiles: Hexagon Manufacturing Intelligence segment operates with a lower capitalization rate, making its cost structure more variable compared to other segments. This structural difference means that Manufacturing Intelligence margins are less sensitive to changes in R&D capitalization rates. Consequently, analysts at DNB Carnegie expect that this segment will benefit first from operating improvements.

Gross margin pressure

5.
Falling gross margins: Hexagon saw its adjusted gross margin fall to 61.60% in the third quarter of 2026 from 63.70% in the prior year. This contraction occurred even though organic revenue growth accelerated to an impressive 13% during the same period. The decline highlights how strong organic volume gains can fail to translate into gross margin improvements under the post-spin hardware mix.
6.
Dilutive product mix: Hexagon's core gross margin was negatively impacted by a shifting product mix that favored lower-margin hardware and sensors over software. Additional headwinds from tariffs, freight, and semiconductor component shortages further pressured the gross margin in the first half of 2026. These cost headwinds mean that incremental revenue carries a lower gross margin unless the higher-margin software sales mix recovers.
7.
High incremental benchmark: Hexagon has historically maintained highly resilient gross margins of approximately 62% since 2022 despite experiencing raw material and component inflation. Management relies on introducing innovative new products at premium prices to support these high gross margins. However, the post-spin mix makes the group more dependent on maintaining high equipment pricing power.

Restructuring and leverage

8.
Accelerated efficiency gains: Hexagon has executed its EUR 74 million cost restructuring program ahead of schedule, reaching an annualized run-rate of EUR 73.50 million in the second quarter of 2026. This rapid execution delivered cost savings of EUR 14 million in the second quarter and EUR 18.50 million in the third quarter of 2026. These savings are being retained to directly support profitability improvements.
9.
Isolated leverage drivers: Hexagon reported a pro forma EBITAC margin of 25.30% in the third quarter of 2026, which represents a substantial year-on-year improvement. Management isolated the margin drivers to show that currency contributed 130 basis points and the restructuring program added 170 basis points. The remaining 150 basis points of improvement came from drop-through on organic volume growth.
10.
Favorable leverage path: Hexagon expects that its underlying operating leverage will become more visible once the restructuring savings are fully in the baseline by mid-2027. Analysts at DNB Carnegie expect that the completed efficiency program and stronger divisional accountability will support improving earnings quality. This structural leverage will help the group trend toward higher profit margins.
11.
Contrary evidence: Hexagon has previously struggled with operating leverage during sudden market downturns because its fixed cost base was set for growth. During Q1 2025, delayed cost mitigation actions resulted in negative drop-through and adverse impacts on operating profit. This historical precedent demonstrates that the company's operating leverage can quickly turn negative if organic demand reverses.

Capitalized R&D and Robotics

12.
Structural R&D headwinds: Hexagon capitalization of R&D historically exceeded amortization, creating a large positive gap that artificially elevated reported EBIT1 margins. As capitalization rates decline and prior investments amortize, this gap is narrowing and creating a temporary reported margin headwind. However, this transition improves earnings quality because EBITAC increasingly reflects cash flow.
13.
Uncapped robotics spending: Hexagon plans to invest EUR 45 million to EUR 50 million in its Robotics venture during 2026. Management guides that these investments could increase further to EUR 70 million or EUR 80 million in 2027. Because Robotics is excluded from group targets, these rising costs represent a uncapped drag on headline profitability.
14.
Rising capital expenditure: Hexagon net capital investments amounted to EUR 109.20 million in the second quarter of 2026 as the company continues to invest in new product lines. This high capital intensity is necessary to support the commercialization of its next-generation laser trackers. These investments limit cash flow conversion despite favorable underlying operational drop-through.

Future margin trajectory

15.
Divisional profitability focus: Hexagon is reorganizing into 17 fully decentralized divisions that report into three business areas under its new operating model. This structure is intended to improve operational discipline and compress the lag between market signals and commercial response. This increased focus will help the group sustain its long-term growth.
16.
Strong leverage outlook: Hexagon is well positioned to expand its post-spin operating margins through a combination of cost retention and organic growth drop-through. The completed restructuring program and rising R&D amortization will reduce accounting headwinds and support higher earnings quality through 2028. Consequently, the group has a highly credible path to deliver sustainable margin expansion.
17.
Other side of the story: Hexagon entry into industrial robotics carries high upfront investment costs that are excluded from group targets but could weigh heavily on actual returns. Rising investment commitments for the AEON humanoid robot could dilute near-term profitability if commercial adoption lags behind company expectations. Additionally, persistent weakness in core cyclical end-markets like construction could prevent the group from generating sufficient organic volume growth.

Section 3 – Waygate Technologies Integration and Synergies

Section summary
  1. Completing the Waygate acquisition expands metrology capabilities but immediately dilutes Manufacturing Intelligence operating margins by 300 basis points.
  2. Historical centralized cost rigidities under Baker Hughes must be dismantled through Hexagon decentralized operational structure to unlock profitability.

Strategic expansion: Hexagon completed the acquisition of Waygate Technologies on November 2, 2026, for an enterprise value of USD 1.45 billion on a cash and debt-free basis. Management expects this addition to build a strong foundation for long-term profitable growth across industrial markets.

On a pro forma basis, this lowerprofitability profile dilutes Manufacturing Intelligence margins by approximately 300 basis points. Consequently, group-level operating margins are initially diluted by roughly 150 basis points.

Feasibility of margin targets

7.
Synergy targets: Hexagon aims to bring Waygate's margins up to the Manufacturing Intelligence target range of 21% to 24% by fiscal year 2030. Management plans to drive this improvement through localized manufacturing, portfolio optimization, and cross-selling. Connecting Waygate's computed tomography systems with Hexagon's Volume Graphics software represents a significant software integration opportunity.

This consolidation increases estimated Manufacturing Intelligence sales by approximately 33% on a pro forma basis. 10.
Contrary evidence: Sharp cyclical downturns in global industrial markets have historically caused rapid margin contraction when fixed cost structures fail to align with lower volumes. Integrating a highly hardware-heavy asset like Waygate during a downturn increases operational execution risk. Consequently, Hexagon could experience protracted margin compression rather than the planned accretion.

Baker Hughes cost heritage and rigidities

11.
Centralized constraints: Waygate Technologies operated under a highly restrictive capital allocation framework during its long-term ownership by Baker Hughes. The subsidiary did not independently control its own investment budget or prioritize its own spending. Instead, it received a top-down allocated amount from the centralized corporate structure.
12.
Operational rigidities: Waygate local management had to prioritize capital expenditures based on corporate mandates rather than local operational efficiency. While facility expansions like the Hürth and Wunstorf sites were critical to achieving economies of scale, execution was frequently delayed by corporate governance structures. This legacy of centralized control has left the business with structural cost management challenges.
13.
Decentralized remedy: Hexagon plans to dismantle Waygate's legacy centralized rigidities by integrating the asset into its newly launched 17-division operating model. This structure eliminates corporate layers and places full P&L accountability directly onto divisional leadership. Management believes this operational shift will reduce the commercial response lag and accelerate margin expansion.
14.
Other side of the story: While Hexagon's management plans to aggressively expand Waygate's operating margins, the high proportion of low-margin legacy businesses could severely weigh on returns. Retaining the lessattractive Imaging Solutions and Ultrasonic Testing segments under a stability mandate may permanently dilute group-level profitability if disposals fail to materialize. Consequently, the hardware-heavy nature of nondestructive testing could prevent Hexagon from achieving its 24-26% target.
