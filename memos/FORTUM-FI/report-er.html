EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Fortum Oyj has successfully transitioned into a pure-play Nordic clean energy generator following its turbulent exits from Russia and Uniper, positioning itself as the third-cleanest power generator in Europe with 99% carbon dioxide-free generation at just 11g CO2/kWh. The company's Q2/H1 2026 results revealed operational challenges alongside strategic progress, setting a complex backdrop for its ambitious 14% Group comparable RONA target and long-term capital discipline framework.

The Q2 2026 financial delivery materially missed sell-side expectations, with comparable operating profit of €106 million declining 9% year-on-year and falling 13% below Vara consensus of €122 million, while comparable EPS of €0.08 undershot consensus by 27%. First-half comparable operating profit of €627 million grew 8.6% but missed company-compiled consensus of €643 million, and H1 net profit of €478 million covered only 51% of fullyear consensus expectations of €936 million, lagging the historical run-rate. This operational shortfall was driven by three primary factors: lower achieved power prices, nuclear outage headwinds, and optimization premium compression.

The Generation segment's achieved power price dropped 7% to €44.9/MWh in Q2 2026 compared to €48.1/MWh in Q2 2025, failing to meet consensus expectations of €48.2/MWh despite Nordic spot prices doubling to €53.1/MWh, attributed primarily to lower physical value creation from ancillary services and high realized hedge ratios caused by nuclear outages and low spring floods. Management highlighted that auxiliary service income from system balancing was lower than expected due to a rapid influx of batteries on the Nordic system, forcing Deutsche Bank to cut its medium-term optimization premium estimate from €8.0/MWh to €7.5/MWh.

This structural compression represents a critical long-term threat to Fortum's lucrative optimization margins as battery deployment continues to dampen short-term price volatility. Operational volumes reflected mixed trends, with Nordic hydropower generation increasing 19% to 4.4 TWh due to recovery from an exceptionally low base, while nuclear generation declined 2% to 4.8 TWh heavily restricted by extended planned outage days at the Oskarshamn 3 reactor in Sweden, causing Generation segment comparable operating profit to decline 9% to €110 million.

Despite these volume pressures, cash conversion proved robust, with net cash from operating activities surging to €324 million in Q2 2026 and H1 operating cash flow reaching €679 million, while net debt decreased to €1,761 million, beating Bloomberg consensus expectations of €1,970 million and maintaining a low leverage ratio of 1.4x Net Debt to Comparable EBITDA. Management's strategic focus centered on defensive positioning and retail consolidation amid operational headwinds.

CEO Markus Rauramo presented the recommended voluntary cash tender offer for Elmera Group ASA at NOK 47 per share as a pivotal step to strengthen Fortum's Nordic retail footprint and achieve cost synergies in a structurally fragmented low-margin market, with CFO Tiina Tuomela confirming the €450 million cash offer would be funded through balance sheet resources without jeopardizing growth investments or dividends. The company accelerated its decarbonization milestone by deciding to permanently close and dismantle the 0.60 GW Meri-Pori coal plant on March 1, 2027, ending coal-fired power generation in Finland, with plans to redevelop the site into a nationally significant clean transition industrial zone to attract electricity-intensive operations.

The sell-side response revealed sharply divergent views on valuation and strategic trajectory. DNB Carnegie reiterated a Hold rating at EUR 20.40, cutting 2026 EPS forecasts by 4% due to lower achieved prices while raising 2027-2028 estimates by 2% to 3% on higher power forwards and recovering volumes. Morgan Stanley lowered its price target from EUR 23 to EUR 22 due to higher risk-free rates, but raised 2027-2028 EPS forecasts by approximately 10% driven by the Elmera acquisition and 13% to 15% increases in Nordic power forward curves.

Deutsche Bank maintained a highly bearish Sell rating at EUR 15.70, cutting 2026 EPS by 2% while lifting 2027 EPS by 10%, arguing the stock's elevated 22.8x P/E multiple for 2027 is unjustifiable given medium-term downward pressure on Nordic forward curves.

The most transformative strategic development materialized post-earnings. On September 9, 2026, Fortum announced a historic 22-year nuclear Power Purchase Agreement with Google to supply low-carbon electricity covering up to 50% of Loviisa Nuclear Power Plant output through 2050, with CEO Rauramo confirming the contract meets strict internal return requirements and is expected to increase Group comparable RONA by approximately 1.4 percentage points over time once fully contracted. Deutsche Bank calculated this strategic partnership would generate a pre-tax profit uplift of approximately EUR 125 million, representing a 10% increase, and validated Fortum's capacity to sign lucrative deals at terms above forward curve pricing rather than relying purely on merchant exposure. This landmark agreement represents unprecedented long-term revenue certainty and fundamentally validates management's disciplined approach to capital allocation.

Strategic priorities and financial targets remained firmly unchanged despite operational volatility. Fortum's long-term target of achieving a 14% Group comparable RONA remains the cornerstone of capital allocation policy, comparing to a last-twelve-months comparable RONA of 11.0% delivered at Q2 2026, with management emphasizing achievement depends on structural efficiency improvements rather than power price recovery alone.

CFO Tiina Tuomela confirmed the group's committed capital expenditure guidance is completely unchanged at €2.0 billion for 2026-2030, divided into €1.25 billion for maintenance CapEx and €750 million for growth capital, signaling management is not rushing to accelerate unhedged merchant generation growth. This rigid investment discipline preserves balance sheet strength while maintaining strategic optionality for highly attractive opportunities.

Demand dynamics revealed structural contradictions between short-term caution and long-term potential. Fortum's customer discussions confirm industrial buyers are heavily focusing on three to five year short- and midterm electricity contracts with lower volumes, persisting since Q2 2025 and reflecting low near-term risk appetite among commercial clients. Aside from bilateral inquiries, Fortum has stalled on signing new long-term PPAs for major industrial volumes over the past year, raising concerns among sell-side analysts regarding long-term commercialization strategy prior to the Google breakthrough.

Nordic industrial power demand remained broadly flat during H1 2026, with Carnegie warning that massive industrial projects such as green steel in Sweden have been consistently delayed and postponed, threatening to depress long-term electricity prices if renewables supply growth outpaces sluggish demand.

Macroeconomic headwinds intensified valuation pressure through rising discount rates and power curve compression. Rising cost of capital directly pressured valuation models, with Morgan Stanley noting Finland's ten-year government bond yields surging from 2.8% to 3.4% post-results, lifting the group's weighted average cost of capital and making future low-carbon growth projects more expensive. Macroeconomic uncertainty has kept near-term Nordic forward electricity curves depressed, with Morgan Stanley observing that Fortum's current share price implies a long-term Nordic power price of approximately €58/MWh, sitting above the actual Nordic forward curve of €51/MWh and signaling substantial downside revision risk if data center demand fails to materialize rapidly.

The structural composition of Fortum's optimization premium faces direct pressure from rapid utility-scale battery deployment, with Deutsche Bank reporting that ancillary service income from system balancing was lower than expected due to a rapid influx of batteries actively dampening short-term price volatility.

Competitive dynamics remain moderately intensifying but manageable. TVO's first-half 2026 electricity delivery to shareholders rose to 12,237 GWh compared to 9,781 GWh in H1 2025, reflecting higher asset availability and stabilizing Finnish baseload supply before a new rotor arrives at Olkiluoto 3 in late 2027, while TVO Group reported H1 2026 consolidated profit of EUR 19 million, up from EUR 5 million in H1 2025.

Vattenfall's new nuclear company, Videberg Kraft, has selected Rolls-Royce SMR to develop three modular reactors of 470 MW each on the Värö Peninsula, introducing new long-term baseload competition that could eventually challenge Fortum's carbon-free dominance in Sweden starting in the next decade. Wind power capacity growth in the Nordics is slowing, with Axpo Group estimating total 2026 installations will fall below 500 MW compared to the 2 GW annual run-rate seen since 2020, representing temporary relief for Fortum's market-share defense.

Regulatory developments provided mixed signals on medium-term price support. The European Commission's July 17 proposal to revise the EU ETS directive for 2031-2040 deviated from market expectations by loosening the linear reduction factor to 3.7% annually during 2031-2035 and to 1.7% in 2036-2040, extending the emissions cap to 2046 rather than pursuing a rapid path to absolute zero by 2039. Morgan Stanley reported that the EU's €100 billion investment booster fund and ETS proposals are more positive than bearish relative to initial expectations, estimating the new allocation method will result in 140 million tonnes less supply of EUAs in the traded market during 2026-2031, pushing their long-term 2035 carbon price estimate to €270/t.

Finland has progressed on a new "Winter Energy Support Scheme" to support dispatchable bioenergy-based investments and CHP upgrades, but simultaneously removed the reduced electricity tax rate for data centers effective July 2026, raising the electricity tax from €0.5/MWh to €22.4/MWh and aligning with Sweden's 2023 reforms.

The massive 1.4 GW Oskarshamn 3 reactor experienced multiple maintenance delays, with operator OKG delaying the restart to July 10, 2026, due to turbine facility works taking longer than planned, depriving Fortum of high spot prices by escalating the realized hedge ratio and squeezing variable procurement costs. Fortum entered into a strategic agreement with France's Framatome to develop a fully European fuel supply for VVER-type reactors at Loviisa, aiming to eliminate post-Russia supply chain vulnerabilities, with potential regular deliveries slated to commence in the early 2030s utilizing Framatome's French and German fabrication facilities. hedge book progression revealed improved forward positioning despite near-term execution challenges.

Management highlighted its strong hedge book, disclosing that 80% of outright generation is hedged at €40/MWh for the rest of 2026, while demonstrating proactive risk management by advancing its 2027 Nordic power sales hedge ratio to 65% at €41/MWh, up from 60% at €40/MWh in the previous quarter. This hedging discipline provides critical earnings stability and supports the company's proposed dividend of €0.74 per share, representing a 90% payout of comparable EPS at the upper bound of its 60-90% target range.

Leadership and corporate governance remained highly stable with minimal disruption. President and CEO Markus Rauramo's active leadership has been firmly re-established following the Q2 2026 reporting period, reinforced by his personal purchase of Fortum shares on July 22, 2026, where he completed an initial acquisition of shares on exchange following the earnings release. Morgan Stanley highlighted that the market remains highly focused on senior leadership continuity, specifically the latest management and CFO succession timeline given pending retirement planning, the recent appointment of a Deputy CFO, and current CFO Tiina Tuomela taking on the Spanish subsidiary chairmanship role in addition to her ongoing responsibilities.

The investment case now balances near-term operational volatility against transformative long-term contracts. The Google nuclear PPA represents a watershed moment that validates Fortum's strategic discipline and provides unprecedented visibility through 2050, yet the company faces persistent headwinds from battery-driven optimization premium compression, delayed industrial demand recovery, elevated discount rates, and nuclear availability challenges. The maintenance of strict capital discipline at €2.0 billion committed CapEx through 2030 preserves financial flexibility while limiting near-term growth acceleration, creating a tension between prudent balance sheet management and the ambitious 14% RONA target that depends on structural efficiency improvements materializing as planned. The 1.4 percentage point RONA uplift from the Google contract provides a powerful bridge toward this target, but full realization depends on successfully contracting the remaining 50% of Loviisa capacity and executing the broader industrial customer pipeline amid macroeconomic uncertainty.
