[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = C

Thesis. RWE is a self-help case of the 'Major Corporate Change' kind with a demand tailwind behind it: a return-disciplined management has spent 2025 and 2026 turning a merchant-exposed renewables developer into a three-pillar utility whose earnings are mostly contracted or regulated, and the outputs have already arrived in the statements, the guidance and the consensus. At EUR 59.10 the shares have paid for that change in advance: on our modal path the return is 8.1 percent a year over eighteen months and 9.4 percent a year over three years, against the framework's bar of 20 percent. It is the right company at the wrong price, and the price that would make it right is about EUR 45 to 50.

  • The change is internal first and external second, and it is real.
    • The company chose a return discipline and acted on it within eighteen months, which is what separates this from a utility riding a sector tailwind.
    • The hurdle rate was raised to over 8.5 percent and projects sanctioned since early 2025 have achieved 9.9 percent
      • The net investment plan is EUR 42bn for 2026 to 2031, with EUR 7bn for German grids and a third pillar, regulated transmission, added through a 55 percent pro-rata stake in Amprion (an additional 35 percent for EUR 3.6bn at 1.07x 2027 regulated asset base), funded by a EUR 4bn equity raise at EUR 54 a share.
    • What could not earn the hurdle was sold: the US offshore leases for USD 1.22bn, US distributed generation to Goldman Sachs Alternatives, the Swedish and Polish wind positions
      • Lignite closes by 31 March 2030.
    • The external forces are tailwinds on demand (4.8 GW of long-term power purchase agreements signed from January 2024 to July 2026, at prices 20 to 35 percent above historical baselines), on competition (the oil majors and Orsted in retreat, the UK AR7 round cleared 14 percent real above AR6 with RWE taking 6.9 GW of the 8.4 GW awarded) and on regulation (the German capacity act passed, UK contracts extended to 20 years).
      • The one external headwind, solar cannibalisation of merchant capture prices, is being hedged with batteries and contracting rather than escaped.
  • 'Financial Outputs' have arrived at every layer, so the chain holds from the company's resources to the sell-side's estimates.
    • Five of the twelve internal forces are strong and delivered, and the outputs followed within the year.
    • H1 2026 adjusted EBITDA was EUR 3,011m, up 44 percent
      • Adjusted earnings per share EUR 1.77, up 64 percent
      • FY2026 adjusted earnings-per-share guidance was raised 16 percent at the midpoint to EUR 2.95 on 28 July 2026
      • FY2026 consensus is up 24.2 percent in twelve months to EUR 3.06.
    • The same team, extended to June 2031, chose the pillar and the hurdle, acted, and the operating metrics (10.3 GW under construction), the statements and the sell-side all moved.
      • The change has travelled all six layers of the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain.
  • The outputs are not clean, which is why 'Financial Outputs' is a B and not an A.
    • Cash conversion and net debt deteriorate by design through 2028, and the FY2026 base is flattered.
    • Free cash flow is negative in every year to FY2029 on consensus
      • Economic net debt rose to EUR 15.0bn at 30 June 2026 from EUR 10.9bn at end-2025, about 2.6x adjusted EBITDA, with a peak of 3.5 to 3.6x expected around 2029.
    • About EUR 0.40 a share of the FY2026 figure does not recur (EUR 332m of Dutch compensation and about EUR 100m of trading one-offs), so the underlying FY2026 growth is nearer 9 percent than the headline 25 percent, and the FY2027 and FY2028 consensus increments are only 7.7 and 7.9 percent year on year.
  • What we think it earns: adjusted earnings per share of EUR 2.48 in FY2025 rising to EUR 3.65 in FY2028 and EUR 4.65 in 2031, which is 13.7 percent a year to FY2028 and 11.0 percent a year to 2031.
    • Our path sits 1 to 3 percent above consensus to FY2029 and between the company's EUR 4.55 target and the bulls' EUR 5.0 at 2031, because we credit the upgrade habit but do not count data-centre land sales or capacity-auction wins until they are signed.
  • What it costs: EUR 59.10, or 16.83x 24-month-forward earnings of EUR 3.51, the 69th percentile of the stock's own five-year range and the 73rd of two years, after a rise of 53.5 percent in twelve months.
    • Against the stock's ten-year bands (median 14.0x, 75th percentile 17.0x) the shares sit just under the defendable band
      • Against the cohort they are above every peer bar Iberdrola at 18.1x, a 24 percent premium to European utilities against an 18 percent average since 2020.
    • The contracted catalysts (the AR7 awards, the Amprion consolidation, the July guidance raise) are in the price
      • Data-centre site sales and capacity-auction awards are not, and nor is a FY2027 headline stall or a poor transmission-return determination.
  • The return does not clear the bar, and this is where the chain breaks.
    • The earnings leg carries the return, the re-rating leg is slightly negative at the blended multiple of 16.0x we apply, and the cash leg is small.
    • Over eighteen months the value including cash is EUR 66.44, 8.1 percent a year
      • Over three years EUR 77.33, 9.4 percent a year.
      • Both sit at the foot of the framework's D band and within rounding of F.
    • The swing factor is the multiple: held at 17.0x the return is 12.5 and 11.5 percent a year
      • De-rated to the ten-year median of 14.0x it is -0.9 and 4.9 percent.
      • No combination of our path, a sell-side bull path and a multiple inside the stock's own ten-year range reaches 20 percent a year from EUR 59.10.
  • The pattern it fits: 'Major Corporate Change', crisply on breadth, output and maturity
    • But the pattern is at full output rather than at the input-plus-early-output stage where it rewards a buyer most, so the market has read it.

  • What the case therefore is: a compelling business change at a price that already pays for it.

    • The bet is worth making below about EUR 45 to 50 with the path intact, or after a disappointment the business does not deserve
    • A change in the path large enough to clear the bar at today's price would need earnings two years beyond the three-year horizon near EUR 5.75 at 17.0x, which no file supports.

  • Rating = C, because the expected change is material in its forces (B) and in its outputs (B) but not in its return (D), and the three Elements form a chain whose last link binds: a case whose modal return is 8 to 9 percent a year is not a sufficient change for a concentrated investor, whatever the business is doing.

    • Not B because the 20 percent bar is missed by more than the error bands on the modal path and even the bull earnings path at a held multiple does not reach it
    • Not D because the forces and the outputs are real, delivered and dated, and the shortfall is a price, which can change.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The hypothesis at its strongest: the H1 2026 beat was a false friend of one-offs and trading
      • The 2031 path rests on a regulatory review (the RP5 determination of German transmission returns, draft due in Q1 2027) and on merchant power prices, both unresolved
      • And the shares at the 73rd percentile of their own multiple history already discount the bull case and none of this.
    • What we concede: the price, in full
      • And half of the earnings-quality point, because about EUR 0.40 of the FY2026 EUR 3.10 does not recur and FY2027's headline growth could stall while the multiple is fullest.
    • What we do not concede: the business change.
      • Achieved returns on projects sanctioned since early 2025 are 9.9 percent against the hurdle, up rather than down
      • Contracted and regulated earnings are rising toward more than 75 percent of 2031 adjusted earnings per share
      • Sofia and Thor, the two flagship offshore projects, are on schedule
      • And leverage sits at about 2.6x adjusted EBITDA with the rating agencies loosening their thresholds, not tightening them.
    • Where that leaves us: the hypothesis is right about the price and wrong about the direction of the returns.
      • Our conclusion on the shares is the same as its conclusion, for a narrower reason: at EUR 59.10 a good business returns 8 to 9 percent a year.
      • The detailed form of the hypothesis is at the head of the 'Investment Case' Building Block.

Summary: Written Summary of the Investment Case (not rated) (E 3, BB 4)
The case in 8 to 10 sentences (the thesis as tested), kept in full at both ends of the record (Part 1, E 3, BB 4)
  • RWE is a self-help case of the 'Major Corporate Change' kind with a demand tailwind behind it: a German integrated utility that spent 2025 and 2026 turning itself from a merchant-exposed renewables developer into a three-pillar utility whose earnings are mostly contracted or regulated.
  • It did so by choosing a return discipline (an 8.5 percent hurdle, 9.9 percent achieved), by cutting what did not meet it (US offshore, distributed generation, Sweden, Poland), and by buying what did (a 55 percent pro-rata stake in the Amprion transmission grid at 1.07x asset base, funded by a EUR 4bn equity raise).
  • The change has travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain: the same team, extended to 2031, chose the pillar and the hurdle, acted on both, and the outputs followed in the operating metrics (10.3 GW under construction, 4.8 GW of long contracts), in the statements (H1 2026 adjusted EBITDA up 44 percent, adjusted earnings per share up 64 percent, guidance up 16 percent) and in the sell-side (FY2026 consensus up 24.2 percent in twelve months).
  • It is a compelling business change.
  • It is not a compelling investment at EUR 59.10, because the shares have risen 53.5 percent in twelve months to the 73rd percentile of their own multiple history and 24 percent above the sector.
  • On our modal path, 11 percent a year earnings growth to EUR 4.65 in 2031 with the multiple at a blended 16.0x, the return is 8.1 percent a year over eighteen months and 9.4 percent over three years against a 20 percent bar.
  • The case turns on four things, the earnings path (probable), Amprion's regulated return (probable), power-price realisation on the merchant third (plausible) and the multiple holding (probable), and the return at this price fails even if all four hold.
  • The strongest case against is that the H1 2026 beat was a false friend of one-offs and trading
    • We concede the price and the one year of masking, and we do not concede that returns are degrading, because achieved returns are rising.
  • The case is parked on return grounds, with the business kept on the watch list for a price below about EUR 45 to 50.
  • A colleague could repeat it in one line: the right company at the wrong price.
