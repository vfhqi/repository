Section 1 – Brief company description and history 13 findings

Section summary
  1. D'Ieteren enters late 2026 as a diversified holding company pivoting toward pure-play aftermarket compounders, led by its controlling stake in global leader Belron.
1.
Business structure: D'Ieteren Group operates as a family-controlled investment firm, with its 50.3% stake in Belron serving as the primary value driver, accounting for 56% of FY25 operating profit
2.
Segment overview: The portfolio includes D'Ieteren Automotive (VW Group distribution), TVH (industrial parts), PHE (car parts), and Moleskine, covering the entire mobility chain from distribution to aftermarket
3.
Historical performance: Over the last 3 years, the group transitioned from a Belgian-centric distributor to a global aftermarket parts platform, significantly improving its return on capital profile
4.
Sector context: The group operates within the European Distributors and Business Services sectors, outperforming pure automotive peers through its exposure to counter-cyclical repair and maintenance markets
5.
Share price vs market: The stock has performed robustly over 12 months, though it lacked a clear catalyst until the formal confirmation of the Belron strategic review in September 2026
6.
6-month sentiment shift: Sell-side sentiment shifted from "wait and see" on Belron to anticipating a liquidity event, as Bernstein and Jefferies increasingly focused on the valuation gap
7.
Peer relative delivery: Compared to peers like Bunzl or Ferguson, D'Ieteren's Belron stake offers higher organic growth (7%+), though the group trades at a conglomerate discount
8.
Broker ratings: Jefferies' Kerstens and Bernstein's Kirkness have maintained Buy/Outperform ratings heading into results, citing the compounding nature of the aftermarket holdings
9.
VW dependency risks: Historically, the company was defined by its exclusive VW Group distribution rights in Belgium, a segment now facing structural headwinds and market share pressure. [#R]
10.
M&A track record: The company has successfully integrated PHE and TVH over the last three years, diversifying earnings away from the volatile new-car sales cycle
11.
Dividend policy: D'Ieteren has maintained a steady dividend growth trajectory, supported by increasing cash upstreaming from Belron and TVH
12.
Share buyback strategy: The board utilized excess capital for a €100 million buyback program, signaling confidence in the intrinsic value versus the current market price
13.
Other side of the story: The complexity of the holding company structure continues to obscure the true value of the underlying assets, and the reliance on Belron makes the group highly sensitive to any shift in US consumer behavior or glass recalibration trends
