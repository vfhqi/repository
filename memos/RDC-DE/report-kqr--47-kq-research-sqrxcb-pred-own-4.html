Revenue and Deductions Accounting

1.

This policy means that any direct customer bonuses are booked as direct revenue reductions rather than marketing expenses. Consequently, these pricing promotions immediately depress the reported gross margin instead of raising operating expenses.
2.
Consistently recorded: Redcare Pharmacy reported that its consolidated gross margin declined from 23.0% in 2024 to 22.6% in 2025 due to the growing mix of structurally lower-margin Rx products. This long-term trend was further intensified by the introduction of the Rx bonus model in Q3 2025. This development demonstrates how top-line growth can directly dilute reported gross margins under current discount classification rules.
3.
Adjusted pricing: Redcare Pharmacy defines reported sales as gross revenues minus value added taxes and customer discounts. Because statutory prescription co-payments are covered by the company, this promotional discount is subtracted directly from the top line at the transaction level. This accounting approach reduces the average revenue per basket while increasing volume.
4.
Deferred liabilities: Redcare Pharmacy operates a loyalty program called RedPoints that rewards returning DACH customers with redeemable points. A portion of each transaction price is deferred as a contract liability based on relative stand-alone selling prices until final product redemption. This treatment ensures that the financial cost of loyalty bonuses is matched precisely with the timing of subsequent sales.
5.
Contrary evidence: The Social Court of Munich ruled on April 29, 2026, that health insurers cannot refuse separate digital and paper billing, which protects pharmacy revenue flows from administrative disputes. This legal precedent shows that administrative billing barriers are declining in Germany, rendering regulatory collection risks less severe than previously assumed.

Section 7 – If the Answer Differs from the Common View

The consensus narrative among equity analysts assumes that online pharmacies will capitalize on the January 2027 co-payment increase to attract cost-sensitive consumers through aggressive bonus programs, accelerating migration from brick-and-mortar channels despite modest margin pressure. However, if this widely accepted view proves incorrect–whether through regulatory intervention, competitive response, or unfavorable unit economics–the financial implications for Redcare Pharmacy, DocMorris, and the broader European online pharmacy sector would be substantially more severe than current market pricing reflects.

The most consequential alternative scenario involves successful legislative action to ban co-payment waivers entirely. A proposed amendment to German social law Section 43c SGB V seeks to prohibit online pharmacies from offering and advertising co-payment waivers, which would eliminate the primary pricing advantage of foreign mail-order pharmacies if enacted in 2027. Under this scenario, Redcare Pharmacy would immediately lose its principal customer acquisition tool in the German prescription market, forcing management to compete on delivery speed, product selection, and customer service rather than price.

Without this pricing flexibility, new customer additions would likely decline by 40-60% from current run rates, materially delaying the path to sustainable prescription market leadership and compressed earnings per share timelines by multiple years.
