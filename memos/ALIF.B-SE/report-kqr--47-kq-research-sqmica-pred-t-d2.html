Section 4 – Currency Translation Fluctuations and Floating Rate Debt Risks

Section summary
  1. Unhedged currency translation exposures and variable-rate debt structures introduce significant operational and financial volatility for AddLife and its Swedish-listed peer distributors.
  2. High sensitivity to euro-denominated debt and floating interest rates can severely dilute earnings per share during periods of central bank rate hikes and krona appreciation.

Unhedged Translation Sensitivity

1.
Unfavourable exchange rates: Unfavourable exchange rates represent a major operational headwind for AddLife AB due to its significant foreign operations. According to the AddLife 2025 Annual Report published on April 8, 2026, a one percentage point appreciation of the Swedish krona would reduce consolidated net sales by SEK 87 million and EBITA by SEK 13 million. This high currency sensitivity highlights how unhedged translation swings can severely depress reported operating results during periods of krona strength. Consequently, a three to five percent krona appreciation would wipe out up to SEK 435 million of sales, suppressing earnings.

Passive hedging: Decentralized treasury structures expose AddLife AB to substantial currency translation risks because its foreign income remains mostly unhedged. The company’s 2025 annual report, released on April 8, 2026, discloses that translation exposures of foreign subsidiaries are generally not hedged. This passive hedging approach means that any appreciation of the Swedish krona directly translates into lower consolidated earnings per share on the income statement. Although some EUR-denominated subsidiaries are hedged using foreign currency loans, the rest of the group remains fully exposed to exchange rate volatility.
3.
Comparable peer: Comparable peer distributors show similar translation vulnerabilities because they operate heavily across the Nordic region and continental Europe.
