[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = C

Thesis. Hilton Food is an internal-change case in a business whose environment sets a low ceiling: a rebuilt leadership has cleaned up the portfolio, ring-fenced the seafood division that caused two years of falling profit, and contracted two new geographies, and at 692.5p the shares already pay for the modest earnings recovery that produces. The change is real and broad; what it produces is about 8 percent a year of earnings growth from a FY2026 trough, and the return at this price is about 14 percent a year over eighteen months, below the 20 percent bar. The case becomes compelling at about 640p, or on evidence that both the Canada launch and the core's FY2027 growth are delivered, which together we put a little under even.

  • The change is internal, broad and dated.
    • A chief executive's abrupt exit in November 2025 brought in an experienced operator, first as Executive Chair and from 1 July 2026 as Group Chief Executive, and behind him a new Non-Executive Chair, two regional Chief Operating Officers and new committee chairs, all inside ten months.
    • The March 2026 strategic framework put the capital where the business demonstrably earns: core meat, Central European fresh prepared food, and two partner-contracted geographies
      • seafood sits outside the targets under dedicated leadership.
    • The perimeter was cut three times at real prices: Fairfax Meadow for £54.4m gross cash (about 7x EBITDA), 65 percent of Foods Connected for £21.8m cash plus £24.3m of equity, Dalco agreed for £5.4m
      • about £80m of disposal proceeds in all.
    • Eight of the twelve internal forces sit at B or better, three at A, and the framework's screen rule is passed on both limbs.
  • The forces outside the company lean the other way, and they set the ceiling on what the change can produce.
    • Beef supply tightens through 2028, with Australian cattle availability expected to fall sharply from 2027
    • retailers are exercising their power (Tesco moved to Scottish beef for Scottish stores and Hilton ceased supplying it; Lidl Ireland accepted a financial hit to hold a 15 to 20 percent price gap)
    • the United States regulator's restriction on the Greek salmon plant has outlasted every date the company gave for it.
    • None of these stops the internal programme.
      • Together they limit its output to the mid-single-digit growth the company itself targets.
  • The change has travelled the whole input side of the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain and has reached the operating metrics; it has reached the financial statements only in the technical sense.
    • People, choices and actions are all on the record.
    • Group packed-food volumes grew 2.1 percent in H1 FY26 against 0.2 percent for the whole of FY2025
    • adjusted free cash flow turned to +£10.4m from -£30.8m
    • net bank debt fell £8.0m year-on-year while £45.3m of capital was spent.
    • FY2026 guidance was raised on 3 September 2026 to £66m to £71m of adjusted profit before tax from £60m to £65m, but the raise rests on the exclusion of Dalco's losses and on Australian-dollar translation
      • constant-currency continuing profit before tax fell 8.9 percent in the same half.
    • The sell-side's estimates moved on the same day: three named brokers raised outer years by about 5 percent.
      • So the change has reached the sixth layer, just.
  • What it produces is modest.
    • We expect adjusted earnings per share of about 52p in FY2026, 56p in FY2027, 61p in FY2028 and 66p in FY2029, about 8 percent a year from the trough, against 56.0p in FY2025.
    • That is at the four-broker median on the first two years (52.2p, 56.6p) and below it on the third (63.7p), because we carry Canada at its initially higher cost and keep Foppen as a drag at even odds.
    • About six points of the 8 percent come from the components investors extrapolate (revenue about 3.5 points, margin recovery about 2.5 points)
      • about two points come from the gearing of a fixed finance charge, which they do not.
      • Nothing in the path reaches the framework's 12 to 20 percent band on any horizon.
  • The price already assumes the modal path.
    • The shares have risen 42.0 percent in six months from a 52-week low of 447.69p and stand 28.6 percent above their 200-day average.
    • At 692.5p they trade at 11.4x consensus 24-month-forward earnings of 60.63p, the 33rd percentile of the stock's own five-year range, below the 12.03x median.
    • The rise has priced the H1 beat, the guidance raise, the Dalco exit, the cash turn and the on-schedule status of Canada and Saudi Arabia
      • every one of those is in the four brokers' numbers.
    • What is not priced is the cleared branch, in which Canada launches on time and the core delivers mid-single-digit growth in FY2027
      • that business would argue for the stock's own 75th percentile, 13.9x, some 20 percent above today's multiple.
  • The return does not clear the bar at this price.
    • Over eighteen months our path, a 5 percent yield and a multiple moving from 11.6x to a blended 11.9x give about 842p including 45p of dividends, +21.6 percent, about 13.9 percent a year
    • over three years, about 983p, +42.0 percent, about 12.4 percent a year.
    • Against the 20 percent bar and the framework's anchors that is a C on eighteen months and the C/D border on three years.
    • The swing component is the multiple.
      • On the cleared branch the eighteen-month return is about 28 percent a year
      • on the branch where one condition holds, about 10.5 percent
      • where neither holds, negative.
    • The price at which the modal path clears the bar is about 640p on eighteen months and about 569p on three years
      • the stock is 8 percent above the first.
  • The pattern is 'Major Corporate Change', with a 'Major CfC Clearing' behind it that the shares have mostly paid for.
    • The chain holds from the inputs through the operating metrics
    • it is thin at the financial statements, where the profit line has turned only technically
    • and it breaks at the price, where the modal outcome is already paid for.

  • The case therefore is a good change at a fair price: worth keeping live because the decisive output step, the Canada launch, is dated inside four months and the odds on the cleared branch are a little under even, and not worth adding to at 692.5p.

  • Rating = C, because the change is real and broad on the inputs (Change Forces B, with internal forces at A) but what it produces is modest (Financial Outputs C: about 8 percent a year of earnings growth from a FY2026 trough), and the 'Modal return' at the current price is a C (about 14 percent a year over eighteen months, about 12 percent over three years)

    • the materiality test is the return, and at 692.5p the return does not clear the bar.
    • The strong input Element does not carry the Building Block because the two output Elements bind: the change is material to the company (a cleaner, better-led business) and not, at this price, sufficiently material to the shareholder.
    • Not B, because a B would need either the earnings rate inside the band or the price about 8 percent lower
    • not D, because the internal change has already reached the profit line through the disposals and the staircase ahead is dated.

  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.

  • The counter-case is the risks research's "false friend", the winning profile of its own analysis: the guidance raise is technical
    • the underlying profit fell 8.9 percent at constant currency
    • the cash turn is a seasonal working-capital release
    • the revenue growth of 15.3 percent is beef inflation hiding West volumes of 0.6 percent
    • the anchor customer has just shown, inside a renewed partnership, that it can remove a volume stream at will
    • and on this the market has re-rated the shares 42 percent in six months on optics.
  • We concede the first, third and fifth points in full and the second in part.
  • We hold our view, as a neutral at this price rather than a bullish one, because the things that are not optics are on the record: volumes grew 2.1 percent in a half when the prior year grew 0.2 percent for a year
    • net bank debt fell £8.0m year-on-year while £45.3m of capital was spent, which a working-capital release alone does not do
    • the disposals were done at real prices to real buyers (about 7x EBITDA for Fairfax Meadow)
    • West core meat held a 2.8 percent margin through Ireland's price war
    • and the two contracted geographies are dated inside four months with named counterparties.
  • Where the counter-case is right is on the price, and that is where our recommendation sits.

Supply side: input costs, availability, concentration? (KQ 5, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = D
  • Rating = D, because raw-material supply is tightening in beef, white fish and salmon at once, Hilton owns no upstream assets, and its cost-plus contracts pass the price through with a lag that costs margin and volume
    • direction: headwind.
  • Beef: global beef production is projected down 2 percent in 2027 and 3 percent in 2028
    • Australian cattle availability is expected to fall sharply in 2027 and 2028 after two years of heavy cow slaughter (Morgan Stanley)
    • Irish cattle throughput is 16 percent below the prior year
    • European retail beef prices rose over 50 percent year-on-year and United Kingdom cattle over 20 percent.
  • Fish: haddock up to 90 percent
    • Peruvian fishmeal up 90 percent, lifting salmon feed costs.
  • The lag: retailers actively resist real-time price pass-through, and processing costs are fixed annually, so energy inflation is absorbed for up to twelve months.
  • Not C, because the Australian point matters most: Oceania is the most profitable region, and the earnings research names Australian cattle availability as one of four things that would change its view.
