APM Mini-Memo: Schouw & Co

The one-line view. Schouw & Co owns a high-return fish-feed business with a public price and five smaller industrial companies the market values at about four times operating profit; the case is upgraded earnings now and a narrowing discount later, and it depends on BioMar's feed margins holding.

What the company does

Schouw & Co is a Danish industrial holding company listed in Copenhagen, worth about 2.7 billion euros at the price of 827 kroner on 30 October 2026. The group owns seven business-to-business platforms across 35 countries with over 15,000 employees, and in 2025 earned operating profit before depreciation of 2.9 billion kroner on revenue of 34 billion.

BioMar is the engine. BioMar makes fish and shrimp feed at 16 plants, holds about a fifth of global salmon feed volume as the third-largest supplier, earns a return on invested capital of about 24 percent, and provides nearly half of group revenue, over half of group operating profit and roughly three quarters of the group's asset value. Schouw listed BioMar separately in Copenhagen on 28 May 2026, kept 74 percent, and received 2.7 billion kroner.

The rest are European niche leaders. GPV is Europe's second-largest contract electronics manufacturer; HydraSpecma builds hydraulic and cooling systems for wind turbine makers; Borg Automotive remanufactures car parts; two Fibertex companies make hygiene and industrial nonwovens; and Spectre, a maker of technical outdoor apparel bought in September 2026, is the newest platform.

A plausible investment thesis

The story is a discount with a new yardstick. Since BioMar's listing the market can price Schouw's stake directly, and what is left over trades at 4.1 to 4.5 times 2026 operating profit before depreciation, an implied discount of 45 to 50 percent against a normal 10 to 20 percent for holding companies. The market's story is that the discount closes as the parts are marked, and our research files agree both on the quality and on the gap.

Earnings do the work while the discount waits. Second-quarter 2026 revenue grew 5 percent to 9.0 billion kroner, operating profit before depreciation grew 17 percent to 828 million, the margin widened by 0.9 points to 9.2 percent and earnings per share rose 39 percent to 14.19 kroner. Management raised 2026 guidance for operating profit before depreciation to 3.15 to 3.35 billion kroner from 2.9 to 3.2 billion, and at the capital markets day on 8 October set an ambition of 4.5 billion by 2030, about half as much again as 2025.

The step-up has named parts. GPV is cutting its sites from 19 to 14 for about 100 million kroner of savings and targets a 10 percent margin by 2030 from 7.4 percent in 2025; Borg's programme targets another 100 million by 2027; BioMar targets 8 to 10 percent annual profit growth to 2030; and the parent, with leverage down to 0.7 times from 1.9, can buy again and is buying back 410 million kroner of stock in 2026. Consensus earnings growth is 13.6 percent a year.

Drivers to track: BioMar feed volumes against the 3 to 7 percent 2026 target, GPV's order intake and book-to-bill for the industrial restock, the working-capital unwind in the full-year results, and any sign of an actual divestment.

The Target Setup

There is no Investment Analyst memo, so there are no Bottom-up/Stock-driven ratings and the read here is my own. The Target Setup is 'Big Corporate Change-driven EPSU': the BioMar listing changed the structure, released the capital and exposed the discount, and the earnings step-up comes from margin programmes and redeployment. If management keeps buying at the Spectre multiple of 6.5 to 7.3 times, the case also takes the shape of 'Delivering - Serial Acquirer - Mechanical EPS Upgrader', because consensus only models the businesses already owned.

Case Riskiness and simplicity

The case is simple to state and moderately risky to hold. What needs to go right is that BioMar's feed margins hold while the industrial units deliver their cost programmes, and that reported profits turn into cash.

The main risk is a biological shock at BioMar. A disease outbreak or an El Niño spike in marine raw materials would cut feed volumes and could derail group guidance, because BioMar is over half of group profit; the modelled downside floor is about 650 kroner a share under a 2-point BioMar margin contraction and a wider holding discount, about 21 percent below today's price. Against that, BioMar's contracts pass raw material costs through with a one-quarter lag, and a 20 percent rise in its input basket produced margin expansion, not compression, in the second quarter.

The second thing to watch is cash. First-half 2026 operating cash flow fell 43 percent while operating profit set records, as working capital swelled on raw-material buying; net working capital above 6 billion kroner would be the warning. Behind both sits governance: a 28.66 percent anchor shareholder, a chief executive in place since 2000 with no published successor, and no divestment since 2016, which is why the discount may need a seller to close.

Business quality

Quality is high at the core and mixed at the edges. BioMar's cost-plus contracts, 24 percent return on invested capital and 24 days of working capital make it an outlier, and GPV's open-book pricing and Fibertex's indexed prices extend the pass-through habit across the group; group return on invested capital excluding goodwill improved to 14.1 percent in the second quarter of 2026 from 13.1 percent in 2025. Management narrows guidance rather than over-promising, and delivered 2025 inside its range.

The weaker units are real. Borg's profit fell to zero in 2025 under Chinese parts pricing and a 300 million kroner write-down, GPV and HydraSpecma follow industrial capital cycles, and the research files call the Spectre purchase, a lower-return apparel maker bought with proceeds from a high-return feed business, inadequately explained to investors.

Plausible returns and valuation

The shares are cheap on the parts and ordinary on earnings. Schouw trades at 11.7 times current earnings, the 54th percentile of its own history, and at 6.2 times operating profit before depreciation against 10 times for capital goods peers. The weekly TSR/Valuation rating of 30 October is C on the valuation reading alone.

The plausible return clears Richard's bar. The weekly calculation of 29 October puts the plausible return at about 25 percent a year, 29 percent over eighteen months and 22 percent over three years, from consensus earnings growth of 13.6 percent, the multiple moving from 11.7 to 13.7 times, and dividends; on the anchors that is an A, but it is a script reading, not a memo's. Momentum backs it: the weekly Sell-side Momentum rating of 28 October is A and the weekly Technical Momentum rating of 30 October is A, with the shares 3 percent below their 52-week high.

Value chain and thematics: the pros and the cons

Pros. Protein feed demand is defensive and growing 2 to 3 percent a year, BioMar's pass-through engineering has cut marine inputs from 80 percent of feed in 1990 to about 12 percent, and the Industry Digest sees the short-cycle turn reaching electronics first, which would lift GPV's order book.

Cons. The Sector Digest's rule is that a listing merely prices the best part while a trade sale closes the discount, and Schouw's discount has not moved since BioMar listed. The market paid for subtraction this year and nothing for addition, Nordic industrial beats were sold this week, and the weekly Thematic Fit/Momentum rating of 30 October is C, environment only. The stock is thinly traded, with no activist and no short interest on file to force the question.
