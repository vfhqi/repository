Section 3 – Industry Structure + Concentration + Value Chain Economics

Section summary
  1. global precious metals demand is supported by a structural supply deficit and robust industrial expansion
  2. silver mining remains highly fragmented and structurally inelastic due to massive byproduct contributions
  3. successful operators must maintain low cash-cost profiles and tier-1 asset grades to sustain profitability

Global market scale and structural drivers

1.
Massive market valuation: the global silver market reached a valuation of US$5.8 trillion in 2023 compared to gold at US$12 trillion in 2022.
2.
Consistent industrial expansion: the global silver sector is projected to expand at a compound annual growth rate of 6.3% through 2032.
3.
Secular technological drivers: green industrial applications in photovoltaic cells and electric vehicles represent the primary demand growth vectors for silver.
4.
Unprecedented cost pressures: rising silver costs have squeezed solar module margins, representing 22.1% of total module cost in 2026 vs 9.9% in 2025.
5.
Chronic supply deficits: escalating industrial demand has driven a cumulative silver market deficit of 543 million ounces over the last three years.
6.
Neutral monetary reserve: gold continues to act as a neutral reserve asset amid ongoing geopolitical fragmentation and global monetary uncertainty.

Fragmentation and supply inelasticity

7.
Extreme market fragmentation: the thirty largest global silver mines account for just 42% of total supply, with no single mine exceeding 5%
8.
Dominant byproduct sourcing: almost three-quarters of global mined silver supply is extracted as a byproduct of lead, zinc, copper, and gold mining.
9.
Inelastic supply response: byproduct status renders global silver supply stubbornly price-inelastic, as base metal mines ignore standalone silver price signals.
10.
Highly concentrated geography: silver production remains heavily Americas-centric, with Mexico alone representing 84% of North American mined silver supply.
11.
Divergent primary landscape: primary silver mines represented only 28.3% of global silver production, leaving the investible pure-play universe highly restricted.
12.
Unequal exploration spending: global precious metals exploration is gold-biased, averaging just US$505 million for silver versus US$5.5 billion for gold annually.

Competitor conduct and success factors

13.
Sizable peer output: Fresnillo remains a leading silver miner with 52.5 Moz produced annually, compared to Hindustan Zinc at 22.5 Moz.
14.
Diversified base mining: copper giant KGHM produced 1,323 tonnes of silver in fiscal 2025 as an ancillary precious metal credit.
15.
Formidable entry barriers: developing major precious metal assets is prohibitively slow, typically requiring 10-20 years of exploration and permitting.
16.
Declining asset grades: mature mining districts force operators to extract metal from deeper, lower-grade, or technically complex deposits.
17.
Strict quality definition: tier-1 gold assets require a minimum 10-year reserve life and annual production exceeding 500,000 ounces.
18.
Sustained capital discipline: industry consolidation has historically been preferred over high-risk greenfield exploration to replenish declining reserves at cycle peaks.
19.
side of the story: byproduct supply surges from copper expansions could depress silver premiums if industrial demand growth softens.
