EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

AT & S Austria Technologie & Systemtechnik AG (ATS-AT) faces a complex multi-jurisdictional tax landscape spanning Malaysia, China, and Austria, with its group effective tax rate projected to range between 18% and 25% for FY2026/27 to FY2029/30. Tax incentives and effective tax rate optimization are critical drivers of the company's valuation, cash flows, and EPS development, yet regulatory transitions to outcomes-based taxation in Malaysia and Pillar Two minimum tax rules represent primary risks that could limit the benefit of local tax holidays.

In Malaysia, ATS-AT is investing €1.7 billion at the Kulim Hi-Tech Park, with projected spending of €1.5 to €2.0 billion through early FY2028/29. Malaysia has transformed its tax incentive environment by replacing the historical product-based system with the New Incentive Framework, effective from 1 March 2026, which evaluates projects based on measurable contributions including local sourcing, wages, and technical job creation.

Under guidelines from the Malaysian Investment Development Authority released on 15 July 2026, eligible manufacturing companies can access special tax rates ranging from 0% to 10% for up to 10 years, or alternatively choose an Investment Tax Allowance to offset up to 100% of statutory income. However, Malaysia's 15% Global Minimum Tax from 2025 requires assessing incentives against this rate backstop, which means that even if AT&S secures a 0% tax rate in Kulim, the group must pay a top-up tax to reach the 15% minimum threshold, making the domestic tax holiday much smaller than it looks on a consolidated basis.

In China, ATS-AT relies heavily on preferential High and New Technology Enterprise tax status to optimize profitability of its Chongqing operations, with the company's annual financial report published on 10 June 2026 confirming that AT&S China has consistently obtained favorable tax status that reduces the standard corporate income tax rate from 25% down to 15%. Chongqing was granted this status for calendar years 2025 to 2027, with an application for 2026 to 2028 currently in progress, and the status is valid for three years but remains strictly dependent on achieving specific operational and technical criteria each year. Chinese regulators evaluate core technology ownership, R&D staff proportions, and R&D spending as a percentage of total revenues, forcing manufacturers to continuously direct capital into local engineering projects. The principal risk is that geopolitical trade tensions and export controls threaten the utilization and subsequent tax efficiency of foreignowned high-tech plants in China, with peer Nan Ya PCB's Kunshan plant facing a low utilization rate of 50% due to geopolitical factors, meaning idle capacity can inflate local unit costs and undermine the profit base.

AT&S Austria's total group tax loss carryforwards reached €798,651 thousand as of March 31, 2026, up from €749,151 thousand in the prior year, driven by heavy upfront capital expenditure and research spending at the Leoben headquarters. Deferred tax assets were recognized for loss carryforwards in the amount of €21,682 thousand, an increase from €6,634 thousand in the prior year, based on present tax planning showing that domestic entities will generate sufficient taxable income within five years.

Deferred tax assets of €105,369 thousand, corresponding to €702,462 thousand in loss carryforwards, were not recognized because their realization is not probable, with €301,860 thousand carried forward up to five years and €400,602 thousand carried forward between six and ten years. Austrian tax losses can be carried forward indefinitely but can only offset up to 75% of taxable income in any single year, meaning AT&S will always face a minimum cash tax liability on at least 25% of its Austrian taxable profits.

The group's future consolidated effective tax rate will be determined by the profitability balance between high-tax European operations facing a 23% Austrian corporate tax rate and low-tax Asian manufacturing hubs benefiting from a concessionary 15% rate in China, with the speed of Kulim and Leoben ramps shifting the geographic profit mix significantly over the next three years. Deutsche Bank Research models a slow transition, projecting pretax income to improve from negative €62 million in FY26 to positive €65 million in FY27, suggesting that ETR will remain volatile until the Kulim plant reaches optimal capacity. Peer Ibiden Co Ltd reports a statutory effective tax rate of 29.9% for both 2024 and 2025, but Ibiden's actual effective tax rate reached 33.8% in FY25 due to valuation allowance increases, indicating that AT&S Austria's lower tax environment provides a significant structural margin advantage over Japanese peers.

The most significant information gaps concern exact compliance milestones and outcomes-based criteria required to maintain Malaysian tax holidays, as the company's annual report of 10 June 2026 confirms that Malaysia represents its largest manufacturing base but omits specific operational performance indicators, meaning that if the Malaysian Investment Development Authority raises local hiring or wage thresholds, AT&S could face a sudden loss of its concessionary status. Additionally, the application for Chongqing's High and New Technology Enterprise status for calendar years 2026 to 2028 is still in progress, and if Chinese tax authorities reject this renewal, the local tax rate would immediately rise from 15% to 25%. The future trajectory of Pillar Two global minimum tax top-up liabilities remains highly unpredictable, with a top-up tax of €259 thousand levied in FY25/26 because of low effective rates in China, though the report does not clarify if similar top-up taxes will apply to Malaysia's pioneering profits as the Kulim campus scales up.

If regional tax incentives are resolved differently than consensus expectations, the structural earnings consequences would be severe. If Chinese authorities reject the pending high-technology enterprise application, the Chongqing tax rate will jump from 15% to 25%, which would severely compress consolidated net income and drag down future earnings per share unexpectedly. If the Malaysian Investment Development Authority revokes pioneer status because of employment or local sourcing failures, the tax rate will jump to 24%, which would cripple the long-term profitability of Kulim and severely damage future EPS growth projections.

Austria's Minimum Taxation Act enforces a 15% global minimum tax rate, and even if AT&S achieves a 0% concessionary tax rate in Malaysia, the group must pay a top-up tax in Austria to reach the 15% threshold, meaning local tax exemptions are significantly capped and cannot fully optimize consolidated earnings per share.

Overall confidence in the tax structure details is medium for Malaysian incentives and ETR projections, but high for Chinese high-technology status and Austrian loss carry-forwards, based on explicit company disclosures and regulatory documentation. The principal limitation of this analysis is the opacity surrounding specific milestone conditions in Malaysian investment agreements and the unresolved status of Chongqing's pending high-tech renewal application. Future quarterly disclosures from ATS-AT, formal confirmations from the Malaysian Investment Development Authority, and Chinese tax authority approvals will be necessary to close these material information gaps and enable precise modeling of the group's forward effective tax rate trajectory.

This conservative accounting treatment prevents overstatement of assets but means that future tax-free earnings could surprise to the upside if profitability accelerates faster than planned.

European Lithium Ltd's annual report of 30 September 2026 notes that while Austrian tax losses can be carried forward indefinitely, they can only offset up to 75% of taxable income in any single year. This 75% statutory ceiling is also highlighted by ams-OSRAM AG in its annual report of 19 March 2026 as a standard usability constraint under the Austrian Corporate Tax Act. Consequently, AT&S will always face a minimum cash tax liability on at least 25% of its Austrian taxable profits, which limits the near-term conversion of losses into cash flows.
5.
Corporate tax group rules: Operating within an Austrian corporate tax group allows AT&S to consolidate results across its domestic entities, but also introduces regulatory and structural complexities. Andritz AG's financial report of 5 March 2026 highlights that Austrian tax groups under Section 9 of the KStG enable domestic members to offset profits and losses directly. However, changes in the tax group structure can trigger significant retrospective tax adjustments and valuation write-downs. This operational reality means AT&S must carefully manage its domestic entity alignment to preserve consolidated tax group efficiencies and protect earnings.

Leoben investment and tax optimization: Massive research and development spending at the Leoben facility generates substantial tax assets that are directly tied to Austria's premium research incentives.
