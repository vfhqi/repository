Philippine Wage Restructuring and Litigation

1.
Escalating litigation: Teleperformance SE faces an evolving minimum wage framework in its dominant Philippine delivery hub due to ongoing legal disputes over Wage Order NCR-27. This order, published on July 9, 2026, mandated an PHP 85 increase in daily wages for the National Capital Region to be rolled out in two tranches. While the first tranche of PHP 60 took effect in mid-2026, the second step of PHP 25 in January 2027 is subject to ongoing legal challenges by employers in local trial courts. This creates budget uncertainty for offshore operators.
2.
Preemptive regulatory adjustments: The Regional Tripartite Wages and Productivity Board responded to the NCR- 27 court deadlock by issuing Wage Order NCR-28 on September 11, 2026. This emergency directive established a P60 daily minimum wage increase effective September 26, 2026, using the older NCR-26 rates as its baseline. This wage order directly benefits approximately 1.1 million workers in Metro Manila. Consequently, Teleperformance SE's operational cost base in Metro Manila will increase regardless of how the courts resolve the NCR-27 litigation.
3.
Persistent regional inflation: Operational cost pressures in secondary Philippine delivery hubs are intensifying as regional boards approve similar adjustments. Labor Secretary Francis Tolentino announced on November 5, 2026, a PHP 41 wage increase in Central Mindanao, with a PHP 25 tranche in January 2027. Concurrently, Western Visayas approved daily wage hikes of PHP 40 to PHP 45 to support private-sector workers. These regional increases mean Teleperformance SE cannot easily evade Metro Manila inflation by shifting volumes to provincial hubs.
4.
Fragmented legislative threats: Long-term labor costs in the Philippines could face structural escalation if proposed centralized wage legislation succeeds in Congress. Cavite Representative Jolo Revilla ordered the drafting of a substitute bill on November 26, 2025, to institutionalize a national minimum wage system. This legislation intends to abolish the fragmented provincial minimum wage framework. If passed, it would prevent operators from utilizing geographic wage arbitrage within the country.
