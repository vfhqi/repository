EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF) 1 findings

MATERIALITY OF CHANGE

The investment thesis for Mota-Engil centers on a structural transformation from volume-led infrastructure execution to a disciplined, high-margin platform anchored by contract mining, environmental concessions, and selective transport infrastructure. The group transitioned from its Building 26 plan to the Focus 2030 strategy, targeting EUR 9,000 million in revenues by 2030 with sustained EBITDA margins of 18% or higher and net margins of at least 4%.

Operating profitability reached a record 18.5% EBITDA margin in FY25, expanding 270 basis points year-over-year and exceeding the European sector median of approximately 6%. This margin expansion occurred despite an 11% top-line contraction driven by the deliberate exit from low-margin Polish operations and temporary public tender delays in Portugal and Mexico. The order backlog grew to a record EUR 16.2 billion in FY25, shifting toward multi-year concessions and providing over 3.6 years of revenue visibility.

Leadership consolidation under Carlos Mota Santos as both Chairman and CEO reinforces family and CCCC control at 71.32% of voting rights, yet introduces governance complexity through a high-profile US defamation lawsuit initiated by Muddy Waters in January 2026. Capital recycling initiatives included the September 2024 sale of Polish construction and real estate operations for EUR 90 million enterprise value. The strategic pivot prioritizes capital-light growth via partnerships in the Lobito Railway Corridor and selective concession divestments, while sustaining a disciplined 7% capex-to-revenue ratio to self-fund industrial segments.

KEY DRIVERS RANKED BY IMPACT

Industrial Engineering backlog execution represents the dominant growth driver with EUR 3.2 billion in backlog as of early 2026, generating EUR 216 million EBITDA in 2025 at a 30% operating margin. Contract mining project ramp-ups across Kurmuk Gold (EUR 482 million, completion 2029), Amulsar Gold (EUR 578 million), Boto Gold (EUR 308 million through 2029), and Moatize Coal (EUR 279 million through 2027) provide multi-year high-margin revenue streams.

The Lobito Railway Corridor achieved financial close of USD 753 million in July 2026, with USD 553 million from US DFC and USD 200 million from DBSA for corridor rehabilitation. Environmental waste concessions through EGF saw EBITDA grow 35% year-over-year to EUR 147 million in 2025, achieving a 23% division margin. European public infrastructure investments include the EUR 800 million Porto-Oiã high-speed rail contract and the EUR 600 million Violet metro line concession in Lisbon.

Demand drivers are reinforced by Mexico's USD 277 billion Plan 2025-2030 across 2,000 infrastructure projects and the European Union's EUR 150 billion Global Gateway allocation to African infrastructure from 2021 to 2027. Macro hedging strategies utilize natural hedging of exposure values via local currency denominated debt, with African contracts largely denominated in Euros or US Dollars to mitigate currency devaluation.

Risks include dependency on equipment supply availability, commodity demand cycles, multilateral funding execution timing, Portuguese environmental policies, and public tender award schedules.

RECENT EARNINGS DELIVERY HIGHLIGHTS

Net profit rose 31% year-over-year to EUR 35 million in 1Q26, marking the best start to a year, while consolidated turnover grew 2% year-over-year to EUR 1,394 million. EBITDA increased 10% year-over-year to EUR 234 million in 1Q26, driven by strict project selection and backlog quality, with the EBITDA margin expanding 100 basis points year-over-year to 17%. Africa E&C turnover grew 11% year-over-year to EUR 560 million in 1Q26, delivering a 24% EBITDA margin driven by high-margin Industrial Engineering contracts.

Performance diverged regionally as European turnover declined 29% year-over-year to EUR 90 million due to public project awards delays, while Latin America turnover increased 3% year-over-year to EUR 573 million driven by Brazil and Mexico. Balance sheet management extended debt maturity via a EUR 110 million retail sustainability-linked bond issuance maturing in May 2031, increased from EUR 50 million on high retail demand.

Management reaffirmed its FY26 guidance, targeting 10% to 15% turnover growth and a 3% net margin. Historical earnings trajectory demonstrates consistent margin outperformance with EBITDA margins expanding from 14% in FY22 to 18.5% in FY25, though top-line execution remains vulnerable to public budget delays and late-year milestone invoicing volatility.

FORWARD CREDIBILITY AND MANAGEMENT ARCHETYPE

Mota-Engil reached its core 2026 financial targets two years ahead of schedule in FY24, forcing an early revision of its strategic plan, and repeatedly exceeded its annual EBITDA margin guidance, expanding from 14% in FY22 to a record 18% in FY25. Core Net Debt-to-EBITDA was maintained below the 2.0x target ceiling in each of the last four fiscal years. Management operates as an honest tracker that sets achievable low-double-digit revenue hurdles while consistently beating margin expectations through selective bidding and capital discipline. Consolidated turnover declined 11% year-over-year in FY25 to EUR 5,301 million, missing growth expectations due to public tender delays in Portugal and Mexico.

The executive archetype reflects a family-controlled, operationally conservative steward with deep procurement coordination through CCCC partnership, yet facing elevated governance scrutiny from the Muddy Waters defamation lawsuit regarding alleged debt underestimation. Sovereign default risk in Africa is mitigated by the fact that 98% of contracts are signed with Tier 1 private clients or backed by multilateral institutions.

Forward tracking metrics emphasize maintaining a backlog-to-revenue ratio above 2.5 times and ensuring Africa EBITDA margins track at or above 24% to sustain global cash generation. Sensitivity analysis reveals that EBIT margin expansion is ten times more impactful to forward EPS than simple volume shifts, with bottom-line earnings highly vulnerable to variable rate debt exposure representing 72% of total gross debt including leasing and factoring.

Mota-Engil delivered a record 18.5% EBITDA margin in FY25, leading its European construction peer group. Peer portfolios skew heavily toward digital infrastructure and US data centers, with ACS Group backlog containing 22% exposure to data centers and Turner's data center revenues projected to surpass USD 18 billion in FY26, whereas Mota-Engil remains dependent on emerging market infrastructure.

Mota-Engil's Focus 2030 targets a free cash flow to EBITDA conversion of 25% or higher, while management stated working capital represents four days of turnover when excluding the specific Nigerian billing effect.

Pricing power is sustained through customized execution expertise in contract mining and environmental waste services, yet high-margin services remain vulnerable to localized operational volatility in Angola, Ethiopia, and Mozambique.

The primary governance challenge arises from the Muddy Waters civil lawsuit initiated in January 2026 targeting the CEO for alleged defamation and debt underestimation, with management challenging US court jurisdiction in March 2026 and seeking preliminary summary dismissal. MATERIAL RISKS

Unfavorable litigation outcomes in the US lawsuit could validate allegations of debt underestimation, triggering major sell-side rating downgrades. Aggressive tariff escalations and currency devaluations could easily outpace natural hedges, severely compressing net margins. Net Debt including lease, factoring, and confirming liabilities to EBITDA was 2.7x in FY25, which is high compared to typical European peers near 1.5x, while the average cost of debt mix reached 7.1% in FY25, driven by local currency inflation in emerging markets.

Capital intensity risks stem from delayed capital rotation of concessions straining cash availability if interest rates persist at elevated levels, compressing net margins through high financing costs. Execution risks concentrate on severe localized payment delays in African mining contracts that could balloon net working capital days and break the 2.0x leverage target, while unexpected delays in public rail funding and equipment shortages could drag asset turns downward, preventing high-margin backlog from translating into physical cash flow during project mobilization phases.

INVESTMENT-CASE VERDICT: MIXED

The investment thesis for Mota-Engil strengthens on structural margin expansion and backlog quality but weakens on cyclical volume dependency and balance sheet leverage complexity. The structural transformation to high-margin platforms through contract mining and environmental concessions is credible and well-executed, evidenced by record 18.5% EBITDA margins and consistent delivery on leverage targets below 2.0x over the last three years.

The record EUR 16.2 billion backlog provides robust multi-year visibility, with 80% sell-side buy ratings reflecting consensus confidence in the Focus 2030 strategic framework targeting EUR 9,000 million revenues by 2030. However, the 11% top-line contraction in FY25 exposes vulnerability to public budget delays and sovereign tender cycles, while comprehensive Net Debt including off-balance-sheet liabilities at 2.7x EBITDA and 7.1% average borrowing costs create material sensitivity to interest rate persistence and emerging market currency volatility.

The 71.32% concentrated ownership structure eliminates activist catalyst potential but introduces elevated governance risk through the Muddy Waters defamation lawsuit regarding debt disclosure practices. Forward execution depends critically on systematic tracking of backlog conversion timelines, public sector co-investment closures, and equipment supply availability, with bottom-line earnings exhibiting extreme sensitivity to variable rate debt exposure representing 72% of total gross debt. The case strengthens for investors prioritizing margin expansion and defensive infrastructure cash flows, yet weakens for those requiring aggressive top-line growth and balance sheet deleveraging visibility, rendering the overall verdict MIXED pending resolution of litigation risks and demonstration of sustained cash conversion at scale.

Plausible problems to monitor

Mota-Engil's comprehensive net debt including lease, factoring, and confirming liabilities reached EUR 2,612 million in FY25, translating to a Net Debt including LFC-to-EBITDA ratio of 2.7x, which is substantially elevated compared to typical European construction peers near 1.5x. This divergence signals that the group carries materially higher off-balance-sheet financing obligations than its sector comparables, elevating refinancing and rollover risks during periods of credit market stress.

The average cost of debt mix reached 7.1% in FY25, driven by local currency inflation in emerging markets, which is significantly above the European peer median of approximately 3.5% to 4.5%, creating a structural interest expense handicap that compresses net margins relative to developed-market-focused competitors like Vinci and Eiffage. Diverse operations expose net assets to unhedged African and Latin American currencies, introducing persistent translation volatility that is absent among European peers with predominantly Euro-denominated operations.

While treasury utilizes natural hedging of exposure values via local currency denominated debt, this strategy creates a double-edged sword whereby currency devaluations simultaneously erode asset values and maintain high local-currency interest burdens. Local currency debt mix in Africa and LatAm elevates consolidated net financial expenses, further widening the financing cost gap versus sector benchmarks and constraining the pace at which bottom-line profitability can expand.

Operating cash flow in FY25 benefited from EUR 41 million in late-December milestone invoicing for Nigeria, revealing lumpy and discretionary invoicing patterns that complicate quarter-to-quarter cash conversion comparisons. Provisions expanded in FY24 mainly due to warranty costs in Africa and Latin America construction contracts, highlighting elevated execution risk in emerging markets relative to peers operating predominantly in stable regulatory jurisdictions. Construction activity is traditionally weaker in the first half due to delayed public budget executions, and working capital typically absorbs cash in the first six months of the fiscal year, creating pronounced seasonal cash drains that are more severe than peers with diversified concession portfolios that generate steady annuity-like cash flows.

- Earnings Calls - Earnings Calls A significant portion of net earnings de facto leaks to non-controlling interests in Mexico and Nigeria, meaning that reported consolidated net profit overstates the cash flow attributable to Mota-Engil shareholders, a structural headwind that is less pronounced among peers with higher ownership stakes in their operating subsidiaries. Women represent only 13.03% of senior management, despite a 30% recruitment and promotion target for 2026, positioning the group well below sector diversity benchmarks of approximately 25% to 30% female representation in senior leadership, raising governance and talent pipeline concerns that could constrain access to ESG-focused institutional capital.

Outliers worth noting

Mota-Engil's operational and financial profile reveals a constellation of statistical extremes that distinguish the company from its European construction peer set, particularly in margin performance, balance sheet structure, and geographic concentration. The group's underlying EBITDA margin expanded 270 basis points to 18.5% in FY25, exceeding the European sector median of approximately 6%, positioning Mota-Engil in the top decile of global infrastructure contractors.

This extraordinary margin premium is driven by Industrial Engineering delivering a best-in-class 30% EBITDA margin in FY25 on EUR 724 million revenues, a performance level that places the segment materially above typical contract mining peers operating in similar emerging markets. The margin outlier reflects selective contract screening and a pivot toward capital-intensive, high-barrier services, yet also exposes the company to concentrated execution risk in a narrow set of African mining projects.

The company's comprehensive debt profile including off-balance-sheet liabilities represents another significant statistical outlier within the European construction universe. Working capital dynamics present a third notable outlier, particularly the extreme episodic volatility tied to lateyear milestone invoicing.

Operating cash flow in FY25 benefited from EUR 41 million in late-December milestone invoicing for Nigeria, a single event that materially distorted the full-year cash conversion profile.

Management acknowledged that working capital represents four days of turnover when excluding the specific Nigerian billing effect, yet the recurrence and unpredictability of such billing concentrations introduce structural measurement challenges that are atypical among diversified European contractors.
