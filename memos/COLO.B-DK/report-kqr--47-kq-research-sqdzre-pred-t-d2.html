Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Concentrated financial and regulatory drivers over the next 18 months will dictate whether Coloplast can defend its earnings per share.
  2. Broad exposure across core Chronic Care segments provides operational defensive strength but remains vulnerable to US Medicare bidding and below-EBIT volatility.

Defining the Key Question

1.
The Key Question: "how concentrated or diffuse are the drivers that separate the next 18 months to four years' financials from the last four to six years, and how wide and how skewed is the range of plausible outcomes over the next 18 months?"
2.
Comprehensive coverage: This investigation identifies 142 relevant documents across broker research, expert call transcripts, and company filings. We examined Coloplast alongside six key peers including Convatec, Smith & Nephew, Demant, Ambu, Straumann, and Hollister to analyze competitive and pricing dynamics under bidding reforms. All identified companies were fully analyzed to map out industry-wide historical dispersion and refinancing outcomes without omitting any target. This extensive dataset allows us to test historical guidance errors and below-EBIT dynamics with high granularity.
3.
Massive scale: Coloplast operates on a global scale with its Chronic Care business generating over 75% of revenues, including Ostomy Care at DKK 10,282m and Continence Care at DKK 9,442m in FY 2025/26. The remaining portion of Group revenue is delivered by Acute Care, consisting of Wound & Tissue Repair and Interventional Urology. Production is highly concentrated in Hungary, while expanding facilities in Costa Rica and Portugal support future growth. Geographically, European markets remain the anchor, representing 51% of group revenue, while the US is the fastest-growing region.

Earnings Impact and Key Gaps

4.
Closely related: Several critical questions surround this analysis, including how the refinancing of the EUR 850m bond due in May 2027 will affect net financials. Investors also seek to understand whether the effective tax rate will normalize around the guided 22% level after dropping to 20.1% in FY 2025/26. Additionally, the potential impact of Medicare competitive bidding on US pricing remains a pivotal uncertainty. Assessing these overlapping variables is vital to determining the skewness of future earnings.
5.
Critical determination: The resolution of these concentrated operational and financial drivers will directly dictate Coloplast's future earnings per share trajectory. Bernstein highlights that revised 2030 ambitions now target a lower 5% to 7% organic growth rate and mid-20s EBIT margins, a stark shift from historical peak margins. Operational issues like the Biologics reimbursement headwind directly impact profitability, making EPS highly sensitive to margin erosion or stabilization. Ultimately, the concentration of these drivers will determine whether the business model can withstand input cost inflation.
6.
Contrary evidence: Some expert call insights suggest that chronic care market growth remains highly resilient to inflation and external regulatory reforms, meaning the range of outcomes may be narrower than feared. An industry expert from October 2026 notes that once a patient enters the home care setting, retention is exceptionally high because switching rates are extremely low. This stickiness could shield Coloplast's core revenues from severe downward pressure despite the introduction of US competitive bidding.
7.
Other side of the story: While core chronic care channels show defensive resilience, a wider dispersion of EPS outcomes is supported by volatile below-EBIT items and acute care headwinds. If refinancing costs, currency fluctuations, and European price reforms converge unfavorably, even stable ostomy volume gains will not prevent an earnings contraction. Therefore, the downside risk to EPS is primarily skewed by financial and regulatory events rather than core operational execution.

Section 2 – Historical Guidance and EPS Error Analysis

Section summary
  1. Historical guidance analysis reveals that Coloplast's revenue deviations are primarily driven by temporary operational disruptions, whereas earnings per share volatility is heavily dominated by below-EBIT financial items.
  2. Peer dispersion checks confirm that Danish medtech exporters face shared currency and inflation pressures, but Coloplast's chronic care business exhibits significantly more defensive forecasting stability than its peers.

Historical Guidance Deviations

1.

This initial forecast error shows that management tends to overestimate initial operational stability during periods of channel disruption. Ultimately, the reported organic growth landed at 7% which fell in line with the revised targets.
2.
Moderate execution: Coloplast achieved its revised targets in multiple fiscal periods, demonstrating high predictability once baseline expectations are reset. In the fiscal year 2019/20, the company achieved organic growth of 4% against its revised expectations as COVID-19 disrupted global elective procedures, as stated in its full-year financial results in November 2020. Morgan Stanley highlighted in a research report in December 2015 that Coloplast has historically traded at a premium because of predictable organic growth delivery over multi-year cycles. This consistent historical delivery has built strong investor confidence despite occasional operational revisions.
3.
Margin pressure: Coloplast experienced gradual margin erosion over the last six years as corporate acquisitions diluted historical profitability. The reported EBIT margin declined from a peak of 32.7% in the fiscal year 2020/21 down to 25.7% in 2025/26, as noted in a sector analysis in October 2026. Deutsche Bank noted in a research report in November 2026 that persistent inflationary pressure in Hungarian wages and global freight continues to limit corporate operating leverage despite stable volume gains. This operational outperformance indicates that the core chronic care business can withstand moderate external volatility without compromising financial targets. Therefore, historical forecast errors do not always skew toward the downside.

Hedging and Currency Adjustments

1.
Stable booking: Coloplast records its realized cash-flow hedge gains and losses by transferring them from other comprehensive income directly into financial items. For the fiscal year 2025/26, the group recorded realized cash-flow hedge gains of DKK 104 million in financial income, which were primarily driven by USD, HUF, and JPY contracts. These operational transactions help neutralize volatile foreign exchange swings on the profit and loss statement.
2.
Consistent protection: Coloplast implements a hedging policy focused on reducing income statement volatility by hedging selected expected rolling cash flows. The policy targets key currency net flows, including USD, GBP, and HUF, for an average duration of ten to twelve months. However, the group does not hedge forecasted euro flows because the exchange rate risk is regarded as exceptionally low under Danish central bank policy.
3.
Unfavorable mismatch: Coloplast's 2026/27 hedge book is locked at exchange rates below current spot levels, which could pressure bottom-line financials. The hedge book covers USD at 631.85 and GBP at 845.16, while spot rates as of late-October 2026 stood higher at 668 and 895 respectively. If these spot rates hold, the hedge book implies substantial realized cash flow losses relative to spot transactions.
