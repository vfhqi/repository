Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. The Key Question is only partly answered. The evidence identifies the loops at work: a discount-driven buyback loop, a Ferrari proxy/scarcity loop, a negative Stellantis inventory loop, a private-mark credibility loop and a Lingotto loop. It does not yet show which will dominate over the next 18 months to 3 years. Overall confidence is medium, because core listed assets are well disclosed while unlisted operations and turnaround timelines remain opaque.

The discount is a record, so the market is not yet rewarding any loop. EXOR's discount historically averaged 35%, but it widened to an all-time high of 57% in the AVI Global Trust report of November 10, 2026. NAV per share fell 4% in H1 2026 because Stellantis's share price declined. The balance of evidence favors a balancing outcome today: value creation inside EXOR is not being reflected in the share price.

The buyback loop is real and accretive, but self-limiting. EXOR completed a €1 billion buyback in 2025 at a 50% discount to NAV, with a €4.7 per share positive impact on NAV. A new €500 million on-market program began in September 2026. That program reduces the free float while raising NAV per share. The shrinking float could eventually impair trading liquidity and preserve the wider discount.

Peer evidence says buybacks alone do not close discounts. Prosus and Naspers returned $10 billion through buybacks, yet the discount stayed flat at 42%. At Power Corporation, simplification is a primary driver of narrowing, and the 2022 narrowing later retraced. Jefferies indicates that capital returns must exceed 5% of market capitalization annually to compress discounts materially. Against this, Georgia Capital cancelled 11.6% of its shares in 2025 alongside a 61.2% rise in NAV per share. The evidence favors buybacks as necessary for NAV per share but insufficient for the discount. The 5% threshold also rests on a single source, and Section 8 notes it lacks sufficient empirical research.

Ferrari concentration makes EXOR a proxy, but the direction of the effect is disputed. Ferrari stood at €12,250 million, or 34.2% of the €36 billion gross asset value. One reading holds that when Ferrari appreciates, the discount tends to widen as the market lags. Jefferies instead finds that concentrated holding companies see discounts expand in falling markets and compress in risk-on periods. The record 57% discount alongside strong listed assets is consistent with the lag explanation. That gives the first reading modestly more support in the current period. Itaúsa shows proxy trading can be broken, as its discount narrowed from 26% to 19% on tax reform and stronger dividend flows.

Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The resolution of EXOR's reinforcing and balancing feedback loops will directly drive net asset value and earnings per share while defining its systemic risk.
  2. Share buybacks executed at deep discounts generate immediate net asset value accretion for holding companies but risk reducing free-float liquidity.
  3. Peer precedents demonstrate that while massive buybacks increase per-share metrics, corporate simplification is more effective at narrowing persistent discounts.

Section 3 – Listed Concentration and the Ferrari Proxy Effect

Section summary
  1. Asset concentration in Ferrari drives EXOR to trade as a proxy for the luxury stock during market upswings.
  2. Peer precedents reveal that single-stock concentration can anchor holding company discounts or exacerbate volatility unless capital is recycled.

Ferrari Concentration at EXOR

1.
High concentration: EXOR N.V. reported in its H1 2026 interim report on September 22, 2026, that its Ferrari stake reached 12,250 million euros. This extreme concentration represents 34.2% of its total 36 billion euros gross asset value, making EXOR a sector proxy. Consequently, EXOR's public valuation is increasingly tied to the luxury stock rather than its broader industrial holdings.
2.
Consistent appreciation: Ferrari's unique luxury positioning is highlighted by its scarcity model and strong financial metrics. Morgan Stanley reported on November 18, 2026, that Ferrari's highly resilient business model and strong pricing power continue to support high margins. This robust performance elevates the asset's value, which in turn amplifies the holding company proxy effect during positive market cycles.
3.
Persistent undervaluation: EXOR's discount to net asset value widened to an all-time high of 57% in late 2026. AVI Global Trust reported on November 10, 2026, that this extreme discount persists despite several credible routes to narrow the valuation gap. This discount is significantly wider than European holding company peers whose historical average sits around 35%.
4.
Symmetric volatility: Financial analysts argue that when a core asset like Ferrari appreciates, the holding company's discount tends to widen as the market lags the asset's pricing. Morgan Stanley noted on July 28, 2026, that holding company discounts often act as dynamic variables that offset underlying asset volatility. Thus, a falling Ferrari share price can paradoxically narrow the reported discount as the market recalibrates.
5.
Extensive analysis: The research identified 24 relevant sources, covering 8 companies and 4 comparable past cases of extreme concentration. These findings demonstrate that asset concentration is a common structural feature among international investment holding companies. Understanding these precedents helps clarify how holding company discounts respond to proxy trading cycles.

Section 4 – Ferrari's Luxury Scarcity Model

Section summary
  1. Ferrari's disciplined volume restraint protects residual values and supports pricing power, driving high margins.
  2. Structural comparisons with Hermès highlight the strength of Ferrari's mix-driven model, though electrification introduces brand risk.

Mechanics of the Scarcity Loop

1.
Controlled supply: Ferrari deliberately manages its annual production volumes to ensure that demand always outstrips supply by a significant margin. A former dealership principal stated on May 5, 2023, that Ferrari routinely limits shipments to around half of potential market demand. This calculated undersupply prevents residual value depreciation on the secondary market and encourages clients to hold their vehicles indefinitely.
2.
Rising residuals: Strong secondary market performance is a core prerequisite for sustaining Ferrari's primary pricing power. Deutsche Bank reported on February 6, 2025, that Ferrari's average prices are structurally more stable than those of luxury performance peers. This premium residual value reassures ultra-high-net-worth buyers that their vehicles are appreciating assets rather than depreciating expenses.
3.
Order visibility: Ferrari's massive order book provides long-term visibility that shields its manufacturing operations from short-term economic volatility. Morgan Stanley reported on November 3, 2026, that Ferrari's order book now extends into 2028. This long coverage allows management to allocate production and maintain stable profit margins even during volatile market cycles.
4.
Compounding margins: High-margin personalization options allow Ferrari to extract maximum value from each vehicle shipment without expanding total volume. Morgan Stanley reported on November 10, 2026, that personalization options exceeded 21% of Cars and Spare Parts revenue in 3Q26. These complex customizations carry high margins, which supports robust operating profit reinvestment back into capitalintensive research.

Comparative Scarcity with Hermès

5.
Exclusivity mechanisms: Hermès employs a remarkably similar scarcity playbook in the personal luxury goods sector to manage its leather products. An expert from Louis Vuitton explained on February 5, 2026, that Hermès restricts supply and implements strict waitlists for its iconic Birkin and Kelly bags. This intentional restriction regulates long-term revenue growth and creates a highly predictable sales pipeline.
6.
Allocation protocols: Both brands force their clients to establish transactional relationships and history before receiving allocations for restricted products. A former Maserati strategy manager noted on December 10, 2025, that Ferrari's entry-level dealer allocation process closely mirrors the Hermès quota handbag system. This relationship management strengthens long-term customer loyalty by prioritizing repeat collectors over speculative buyers.
7.
Eroding desirability: Investors are actively debating whether Hermès is experiencing a marginal decline in its legendary brand scarcity. Morgan Stanley reported on September 30, 2026, that some luxury investors are focused on reports of a declining pre-spend ratio at Hermès. A sustained drop in this ratio threatens the high valuation premium that the market has historically granted Hermès.
8.
Flexible execution: Ferrari maintains a major structural advantage over Hermès in its ability to drive average selling prices through mix. Morgan Stanley reported on October 19, 2026, that Ferrari can easily offset low volume growth by playing with model mix and personalization. Meanwhile, Hermès' commitment to opening one new factory annually until 2030 creates a rigid volume trajectory that limits its pricing flexibility.
