Why the question matters

2.
Earnings relevance: Data-centre work could materially alter Bravida’s earnings mix over the next 18 months to three years because the company has disclosed a SEK 4.3 billion Green Mountain share, a SEK 2.2 billion atNorth order and a SEK 650 million EcoDataCenter order, while DNB Carnegie estimates that data centres represented approximately 3% of revenue in Q2 2026 and could become 20% to 30% of EBITA by 2030. DNB Carnegie’s estimate is an analyst scenario rather than Bravida guidance, so the evidence supports a potentially important earnings contribution but not yet a precise EPS outcome.
3.
Definition: EBITA means earnings before interest, tax and amortisation, whereas EBIT means earnings before interest and tax after amortisation, and an operating margin normally means EBIT divided by sales. EBITA margin and operating margin therefore differ when amortisation is material, while project margin, country-segment margin and group margin can diverge substantially because project timing, central costs, acquisitions and loss provisions are aggregated at higher levels.
4.
Transmission: A higher project-level margin would lift EBITA first through installation revenue and later through commissioning, retrofit and maintenance work, but EPS would depend on the timing of revenue recognition, tax, financing costs and the share count after repurchases. Bravida’s Q2 2026 report shows the group’s EBITA was SEK 157 million for the first half, with a 5.6% EBITA margin, which provides the relevant group baseline against which any data-centre uplift must be measured.
5.
Cash effects: Profit and cash will not move together because Bravida recognises installation assignments over time using project completion estimates, while invoicing depends on contract milestones and advances. Bravida reported 77% rolling 12-month cash conversion at June 2026 and said larger projects can create substantial period-to-period volatility, although long-run cash conversion has been approximately 100%.
6.
Working capital: Large data-centre contracts can create an initial cash benefit if customer advances precede production, but can later reverse into contract assets and receivables as work is performed ahead of billing. In the first half of 2026, Bravida’s working-capital movement was negative SEK 409 million, mainly because of higher trade receivables and an adverse change in the net balance of contract assets and liabilities, so order value alone cannot be treated as free cash flow.
7.
Provisions: Complex, fixed-price or principal-contractor work can create provisions if estimated costs rise, if commissioning fails, or if defects and disputes become probable. Bravida normally provides for warranty costs over a two-to-five-year period after completion and assesses unresolved disputes individually, making final project profitability potentially different from the margin initially recorded during construction.
8.
Risk profile: The atNorth assignment is economically more consequential than a normal specialist installation package because Bravida says it is the principal contractor for all installation work and manages the construction contract, but the public disclosure does not state whether Bravida bears all campus-level schedule damages or only obligations passed through from its installation scope. Bravida itself identifies project costing, planning, contract administration, time extensions and compensation claims as major risks, which makes contract wording and interface responsibility central to the EPS assessment.
9.
Customer concentration: Bravida reported that its five largest customers represented only 13% of 2025 sales, but the announced data-centre awards create a different form of concentration because several large projects are connected to a small number of operators and construction partners. DNB Carnegie therefore highlights dependence on fewer, larger and more complex projects as a risk even while viewing Bravida’s track record and local footprint as competitive advantages.

Section 2 – Nordic Data-Centre Contractor Margins

YIT’s six-percent benchmark YIT’s 24 September 2026 capital-markets presentation states that data-centre contracting exceeds the company’s strategic adjusted EBIT-margin target for its contracting segments. The target is at least 6% adjusted operating profit margin for both Building Construction and Infrastructure, with negative operative capital employed. YIT does not disclose whether “exceeds” refers to signed projects, the current data-centre order book, a run-rate, or completed-project outcomes, and it gives no quantified data-centre margin.

Confidence in the existence of the claim is high, but confidence that it represents a transferable project-level EBITA benchmark is low. The distinction matters because YIT’s target is an adjusted operating-profit measure, not necessarily EBITA and not necessarily reported EBIT on individual contracts. YIT’s filings define adjusted operating profit by excluding adjusting items, while segment reporting from the first quarter of 2026 uses percentage-of-completion accounting.

Consequently, the strongest defensible interpretation is that YIT expects its aggregate data-centre contracting activity to be above a 6% adjusted EBIT margin, rather than that every MEP, civil, building or principalcontractor package earns more than 6%. Reported YIT results provide a demanding test

YIT’s latest supplied interim data show that neither contracting segment had reached the 6% target in the reported periods through Q2 2026. Building Construction generated a 2.9% adjusted operating margin in H1 2026, while Infrastructure generated 3.0%, compared with their 6% strategic targets. Infrastructure’s strongest listed period was 5.9% in Q3 2025, while Building Construction’s strongest was 4.5%, so the data-centre statement implies either a materially better project mix or a selective margin observation rather than current company-wide performance.

The gap between adjusted and reported profit is also material. In Q1 2026, Building Construction reported adjusted operating profit of EUR3 million but reported operating profit of negative EUR15 million, while Infrastructure reported adjusted profit of EUR2 million and reported operating profit of zero. In H1 2026, Building Construction’s adjusted profit was EUR9 million against reported operating profit of negative EUR14 million, demonstrating why a 6% adjusted target cannot be treated as a 6% statutory EBIT outcome.

YIT’s data-centre exposure is nevertheless becoming significant enough to affect group economics. DNB Carnegie estimated that data-centre revenue represented roughly 17% of YIT’s contracting-segment revenue on an LTM basis in Q2 2026, up from almost zero in Q4 2024. The same broker reported that YIT had announced eight data-centre orders between 2024 and 2026, while its 15 October 2026 note raised 2027–28 EPS estimates by approximately 20% because of stronger contracting-segment growth driven mainly by data centres.

This supports meaningful earnings leverage, but not a project margin that can be isolated from the segment data. 50 51 YIT’s project disclosures show why scope is central to the comparison. YIT’s integrated model can include development, civil and infrastructure, building, MEP installation, commissioning and ICT fit-out, while ICT equipment and some owner-furnished components may remain outside construction cost. A YIT margin above 6% may therefore reflect an integrated principal-contractor proposition with design, procurement, coordination and commissioning value, rather than the margin available to a specialist electrical or HVAC subcontractor. 53 MEP and principal-contractor evidence Caverion and Assemblin Caverion provide the clearest disclosed evidence of technical scope, but not of project profitability.

Their June 2026 Danish data-centre expansion was a SEK160 million TS-MEP assignment, with Caverion Denmark responsible for medium- and low-voltage electrical and fire systems, while Assemblin Sweden supplied cooling, ventilation, security, automation, heating and sanitation. The facility remained fully operational during the work, increasing coordination and reliability requirements, but neither the companies nor the customer disclosed gross margin, EBITA, EBIT or fee levels.

Caverion’s July 2026 Greenergy expansion similarly demonstrates broad technical responsibility without revealing economics. Caverion acted as main contractor for a 22 MW expansion and supplied electrical, cooling, ventilation and fire-protection systems, with completion expected in autumn 2026. The project employed approximately 200 experts and required delivery across Finland, Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania, implying substantial coordination complexity, but there is no disclosed cost-plus fee, target-cost percentage, guaranteed maximum price or project margin. The largest disclosed Caverion precedent is the approximately EUR80 million Mäntsälä agreement with Nebius. The scope included liquid cooling, heat recovery, ventilation, fire alarm, fire extinguishing, smoke extraction, electrical and HVAC installations across an existing facility and two new buildings, with total site capacity planned at 75 MW.

This is a strong technical comparator for Bravida’s installation work, but the public disclosures do not identify whether the contract was fixed-price, reimbursable, target-cost or otherwise fee-based. Assemblin Caverion’s establishment of a dedicated Swedish data-centre business area in August 2026 indicates that management sees sufficient complexity and demand to justify specialist coordination across sales, design, engineering, project management and technical installation.

The group said that it had signed agreements covering approximately 140 MWh of capacity over the preceding twelve months across Sweden, Finland, Denmark and Estonia. This is evidence of strategic positioning and pipeline depth, not evidence that data-centre projects earn more than the group’s reported adjusted EBITA margin. Assemblin Caverion’s company-wide results provide only a broad comparator. Its 2025 annual statement reported revenue of SEK10.842 billion, adjusted EBITA of SEK994 million and an adjusted EBITA margin of 9.2%, with service assignments representing 59% of the Finland and Fidelix segment.

The figure is materially above Bravida’s reported 5.6% EBITA margin in H1 2026, but it is a consolidated or segment-level measure with a larger service component and cannot be used as a data-centre installation margin.

Bravida’s observable margin range Bravida’s management has provided the strongest direct indication that large data-centre contracts can carry a premium to its ordinary work. In the May 2024 earnings call, management said that it does not normally disclose contract margins but that newly won large contracts, including data centres, had “slightly better margins” and “definitely better contracts” than the prior backlog.

This supports a positive margin differential, but “slightly better” is qualitative and does not establish a 10% project EBITA margin. 65 - Earnings Calls DNB Carnegie’s 8 September 2026 Bravida analysis uses 10% EBITA margin for data-centre projects in its base scenario. The broker models data-centre revenue rising from SEK1.0 billion in 2026 to SEK4.8 billion in 2030, with a constant 10% data-centre margin, while ex-data-centre activities carry a 6.3% margin in 2026 and 7.0% thereafter.

This is an analyst assumption, not a disclosed Bravida result, and should be treated as a scenario range marker rather than evidence of realized contract profitability. The same DNB Carnegie analysis assumes that Bravida’s data-centre projects could account for 20% of EBITA by 2030 in its base case and more than 30% in a bullish case. The model produces a group EBITA margin of 6.4% in 2026 and 7.4% in 2030 under the base case, compared with DNB Carnegie’s forecast group EBITA margin of approximately 6.6% in 2026 and 7.1% in 2028. The implication is that a 10% data-centre EBITA margin would be meaningfully accretive, but the result depends on execution, mix and the assumed growth of non-data-centre operations. Bravida’s reported group result establishes the baseline that any project premium must overcome. Bravida reported H1 2026 EBITA of SEK895 million and an EBITA margin of 6.1%, although the period included a net positive effect from the ABEKA divestment and restructuring items. The company separately reported H1 EBITA of SEK157 million and a 5.6% margin in the group-wide financial overview, illustrating that adjusted and reported presentations must be reconciled before estimating EPS impact.

Sector and precedent comparisons Peab’s FIN04A contract provides a useful building-work comparator but points to lower ordinary contractor margins than the data-centre-specific 10% hypothesis. Peab’s EUR130 million award covers a new data-centre building together with related construction and infrastructure work, with completion planned for summer 2027. DNB Carnegie expected Peab’s combined Construction and Civil Engineering EBIT margin to be 3.3% in Q3 2026 and used long-term assumptions of 2.5% for Construction and 4.0% for Civil Engineering.

The comparison suggests that technical installation and scarce specialist coordination may command a premium over shell, building and civil work, but it does not prove that Bravida will earn the full premium. 74 NRC Group’s reported results provide another lower-margin infrastructure benchmark. NRC reported an H1 2026 EBIT margin of 1.1% and Q2 EBIT margin of 3.6%, despite Norway achieving a 10.5% EBIT margin in the quarter after strong project execution and completion.

DNB Carnegie’s October 2026 estimates placed NRC’s 2026 EBIT margin at 2.7% and 2027 at 3.5%, while warning that the company’s history included project losses and missed margin ambitions. These figures reinforce the wide dispersion between specialist or well-executed scopes and ordinary infrastructure contracting.

Skanska’s disclosures show that even a sophisticated data-centre contractor remains exposed to standard construction risk. Skanska states that contract risk varies substantially by mechanism, with fixed-price commitments vulnerable to material inflation, labour shortages, design changes and delays. Its 2024 Construction operating margin was 3.5%, while its annual report separately described data-centre commissioning as continuous throughout construction. The evidence supports a conclusion that data-centre capability can improve pricing and market access, but project-level margin depends heavily on contract form and execution.

Expert evidence supports the scarcity-premium mechanism but not a specific Nordic fee rate. A former senior technical programme manager at Amazon said that specialist MEP work is the key differentiator in data-centre construction and that generator and fibre-optic lead times can extend to 36 months. A separate former Microsoft data-centre manager said large hyperscalers generally reject cost-plus contracting, making fixed-price or tightly controlled commercial structures more likely for major customers. These conditions can support higher contractor pricing, but they also leave contractors carrying procurement, schedule and commissioning risk.

Margin conclusion for Bravida The evidence supports three hypotheses rather than a single verified margin: ordinary building and civil work appears broadly consistent with approximately 2.5% to 4.0% EBIT margins, YIT’s integrated contracting benchmark is above 6% adjusted EBIT, and Bravida’s data-centre installation scenario is 10% EBITA. The confidence in the first range is medium because it is supported by Peab and NRC benchmarks, confidence in the YIT threshold is medium because YIT explicitly states it but does not quantify it, and confidence in Bravida’s 10% figure is low because it is a broker assumption rather than disclosed project data.

1.
Contrary evidence: YIT’s own reported segment results remain below the 6% target through H1 2026, with Building Construction at 2.9% and Infrastructure at 3.0% adjusted operating margin. Caverion, Assemblin Caverion and Bravida disclose substantial data-centre contract values and specialist scopes but no project-level EBITA, EBIT, gross margin, cost-plus fee or target-cost fee. This is the strongest evidence against treating data-centre activity as automatically high-margin. For Bravida EPS, the practical inference is that a sustained data-centre EBITA margin around 8% to 10% would be materially accretive relative to the group’s 5.6% to 6.1% 2026 baseline, while margins nearer 4% to 6% would mainly provide growth without a major mix uplift. The apparent premium may therefore reflect service mix, scale, acquisitions and overhead absorption rather than data-centre contracting alone, so the evidence does not yet justify assuming that Bravida’s large awards will earn 10% EBITA until project provisions, contract assets and completed-project margins are disclosed.

Section 3 – FIN04 Contract Allocation And Liability

Project architecture and separately awarded scopes The public record supports a multi-package delivery structure at atNorth’s FIN04 campus in Myllykoski, Kouvola, rather than a single contract covering the entire site. atNorth originally described FIN04 as a 21-hectare mega-site with an initial 60 MW power supply and a planned path to several hundred megawatts, while later disclosures raised the planned total campus capacity to 430 MW. The campus therefore appears to be a phased development in which individual buildings, substations, infrastructure and technical installations can be awarded to different contractors. YIT’s 21 July 2026 announcement identifies one new data centre in Myllykoski and states that the approximately EUR 300 million award is a comprehensive design-build contract. YIT’s disclosed scope includes design, CSA, MEP and commissioning, with construction beginning immediately and completion expected by the end of 2027.

The evidence therefore places YIT in a broad integrated-delivery role for the facility covered by that award, not merely as a building-shell contractor.

Peab’s 29 June 2026 announcement separately identifies FIN04A and describes its EUR 130 million award as construction of a new data-centre building together with related construction and infrastructure work. Peab states that the project had already begun and was expected to finish in summer 2027, and atNorth is identified as Peab’s customer. Peab’s Q2 report subsequently confirms that FIN04A was order-registered as a EUR 130 million Construction project, which is consistent with a separate building and civil package rather than evidence that Peab holds the whole campus contract.

The apparent overlap between YIT’s EUR 300 million facility and Peab’s EUR 130 million FIN04A award is not resolved by the press releases. YIT’s 21 July disclosure does not name the facility as FIN04A, whereas Peab’s 29 June disclosure does, and YIT later announced a separate FIN04A1 facility on 28 August 2026. The most defensible reading is that FIN04 contains multiple buildings and phases, with Peab linked publicly to FIN04A and YIT linked to another facility plus, later, FIN04A1, but the documents do not establish whether YIT’s EUR 300 million scope includes work that interfaces with or overlaps Peab’s FIN04A package.

Bravida’s role and contract form Bravida’s 9 July 2026 release provides the clearest public description of its FIN04 position. Bravida says it won an approximately EUR 200 million contract for all installation work at the atNorth data centre in Kouvola, acting as principal contractor and managing the construction contract, described as CSA. Bravida also says the project will be led by its PMO Special Projects unit and delivered together with Bravida’s Finnish organisation.

Bravida’s description is materially broader than a normal specialist subcontract. The company says it will manage suppliers on behalf of the end customer and provide support throughout the process, which implies procurement and coordination responsibility within the installation package. Bravida’s own market materials distinguish subcontracting, usually fixed-price installation against client specifications, from design-and-build, typically fixedprice with responsibility from design to delivery, while partnering is generally associated with cost-plus pricing and incentives. evidence does not expressly label the Kouvola contract as cost-plus, target-cost, guaranteed-maximum-price or fixed-price.

Bravida calls it an installation contract and identifies its role as principal contractor for the installation work, but it does not publish the commercial mechanism, change-order rules, fee percentage, procurement mark-up, liability cap or subcontracting terms. It is therefore reasonable to infer a management and integration responsibility larger than that of an electrical or mechanical subcontractor, but not reasonable to infer that Bravida has accepted campus-wide design-build liability on the same basis as YIT.

The amount reported by Bravida also requires care. The July announcement gives approximately EUR 200 million, while Bravida’s Q2 interim report describes the atNorth order as approximately SEK 2.2 billion and says Bravida will perform all installation work as main contractor. These figures are broadly consistent at an indicative exchange rate, but neither document states whether the value includes options, taxes, pass-through procurement, scope additions or the same facility as YIT’s EUR 300 million award.

Allocation against YIT, Peab and NRC YIT appears to carry the broadest publicly disclosed delivery responsibility for its EUR 300 million package because its contract expressly combines design, CSA, MEP and commissioning under a comprehensive designbuild structure. YIT’s subsequent FIN04A1 award is narrower in the public description because it identifies YIT as main contractor for construction, including MEP, but does not disclose value or repeat the full design-andcommissioning wording. The two YIT awards should therefore not automatically be treated as one contract or one liability perimeter.

The common view could be wrong in two ways, namely that data-centre installation earns no meaningful premium over ordinary contracting, or that Bravida’s headline awards carry more execution and liability risk than their descriptions imply. In that case, the principal downside would be delayed EBITA conversion, weaker cash flow and a higher probability of provisions rather than an immediate collapse in reported revenue. Margin compression and earnings transmission Ordinary contracting is a credible downside benchmark because Peab’s contractor operations delivered only a 2.3% combined EBIT margin in 2024, while DNB Carnegie’s long-run scenario used 3.5% for Construction and 4.0% for Civil Engineering. If Bravida’s SEK850 million and SEK650 million awards ultimately earn approximately 2% to 4% EBITA rather than the 10% assumed by DNB Carnegie, the combined SEK1.5 billion order value would

This is a calculated sensitivity, not a reported forecast. The weaker-margin case would not necessarily reduce revenue because Bravida recognises installation work over time using project costs incurred relative to forecast total costs. It would instead reduce the profit recognised on each reporting date and could create negative earnings revisions if estimated costs rise after the initial award announcement.

That distinction matters because DNB Carnegie expects only limited third-quarter data-centre impact and a more significant contribution from the fourth quarter, so a delayed production ramp or low initial margin could affect the timing of EBITA more than the headline order backlog.
