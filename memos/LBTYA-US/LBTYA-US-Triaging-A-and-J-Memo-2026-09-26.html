[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = D

Thesis. Liberty Global is a value-crystallisation case, not a growth case: a deal-driven management is dismantling a holding-company structure whose parts the market values above the whole, while the businesses inside the parts decline. At $9.45 the parent's cash and its Liberty Growth portfolio alone are worth about $13 a share, so a buyer pays less than nothing for half of Virgin Media O2, 90 percent of Ziggo Group and all of Virgin Media Ireland; but that telecom equity is priced at nothing for a reason, and on our modal path the shares return about 11 percent a year against the 20 percent bar.

  • The change is internal, structural and already executed.
    • The group bought out Vodafone's half of VodafoneZiggo for €1.0 billion in cash plus a 10 percent stake in the new holding company (closed 2026-07-31), is combining it with Telenet into Ziggo Group (operating from September 2026), separated Telenet from its network company Wyre after the Belgian approval of 2026-07-23, cut net corporate costs by about 75 percent against 2024, sold about $1.2 billion of assets in 2026 to date, and intends to spin off 90 percent of Ziggo Group on Euronext Amsterdam as early as mid-2027, pulled forward from late 2027.
    • Six of the twelve internal change forces are strong and done, which meets the framework's decision rule with room
      • the two forces the screen weights most (a chief executive change; a guidance increase on the operating lines) are absent.
    • The forces act on the structure and the centre, not on the operating engine.
      • That is the fact the whole case turns on.
  • The external forces run against the assets being crystallised.
    • Competition is tightening in the United Kingdom and the Netherlands, the two markets that carry most of the enterprise value
    • BT Openreach is pricing wholesale discounts specifically into Virgin Media O2's footprint, Sky halved multi-gigabit prices, and Dutch and Belgian entrants are buying share with price.
    • Of the seven external forces, five are headwinds, one is mixed (regulation) and one is a mild tailwind on cash only (the company's own capital cycle cresting).
  • 'Financial Outputs' have not turned; at the operating companies they are going the other way.
    • Consolidated revenue fell 6.0 percent rebased in Q2 2026, an 11.1 percent miss against consensus of $1,318 million
    • Virgin Media O2's EBITDA fell 2.9 percent with consumer fixed ARPU down 4.6 percent to £46.56
    • VodafoneZiggo's EBITDA fell 7.6 percent with its margin down 276 basis points to 41.5 percent.
    • Consensus earnings per share are negative in every year to FY2029 (-1.40, -1.46, -1.02 and -0.755 USD, medians) and consolidated free cash flow is negative until FY2028.
    • The only lines improving are the corporate loss (from about -$200 million in 2024 to about -$50 million targeted for 2026), the parent's cash ($2.4 billion at 2026-06-30) and the capital-expenditure step-down.
      • None of them reaches earnings per share.
  • The change has travelled to layer 5 of the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain at the centre and stalled at layer 4 in the businesses.
    • New leaders, new choices on structure and completed actions on disposals are all in place
    • the financial statements show it only in the corporate cost line and the cash balance
    • the key operating metrics of the two largest assets show decline
    • the sell-side, layer 6, has cut targets since the Q2 print rather than raised them.
  • What the price assumes.
    • The framework's convention (a multiple on the earnings two years beyond the horizon) cannot be applied: the price-to-earnings ratio is -8.3 times at a percentile of 0, and the same is true on a one-year-forward footing.
    • The price is set on the value of the parts.
    • Cash of $2.0 billion (the upgraded year-end target) is $5.43 a share and the Growth portfolio at its $2.9 billion mark is $7.87 a share, together $13.30 a share on 368.4 million diluted shares
    • the $9.45 price therefore implies -$3.85 a share for the telecom equity.
    • Our own modal value of the parts, with $2.00 a share for the telecom equity, is $15.30, so the price is a 38 percent discount to it.
  • What we think it returns.
    • Over eighteen months our modal path is the spin-off completed with the discount only partly closed, worth about $11 a share: +16.6 percent, or 10.8 percent a year.
    • Over three years, about $13 a share, 11.1 percent a year.
    • Both sit at the framework's D anchor, about half the bar.
    • The path that clears the bar (the spin-off completed and the market valuing the telecom equity above zero, the package worth about $16 a share) is possible, at about one chance in three
      • not probable.
    • There is no cash return and no earnings growth
      • the whole return is the narrowing of one discount, resting on one event.
  • What it costs on the valuation.
    • Nothing on earnings, because there is no earnings multiple to pay
    • everything on the balance sheet.
    • Virgin Media O2 carries 5.89 times net total debt to EBITDA including vendor financing against a 4.0 to 5.0 times target, VodafoneZiggo about 6.0 times, and the FactSet enterprise value of $25,133 million is seven times the market capitalisation.
    • The equity is a thin slice of a large enterprise and moves violently with EBITDA, interest and currency.
  • The pattern it fits.
    • 'Major Corporate Change', on one leg only: the input half of the pattern is a textbook example and the output half ("major earnings-per-share trajectory upgrades") is absent, which is why the fit is D, not crisp.
  • Where the chain holds and where it breaks.
    • It holds from the people to the choices to the actions to the centre's financial statements.
    • It breaks at the operating companies' key operating metrics, and until it is repaired there a spin-off changes who holds a levered option, not what the option is worth.

  • What the case therefore is.

    • A one-in-three bet on a re-rating with no cash and no earnings behind it, with a downside bounded near $7.50 to $8.00 a share by the cash and Growth cover.
    • Bounded downside is not what the framework's return test asks for.

  • Rating = D, because the input change is material in shape (a dated spin-off that could move the enterprise value by a large amount) but the financial outputs it must drive are not improving (revenue, EBITDA and cash at the two largest assets are declining and earnings per share are negative on every horizon), and the modal total shareholder return is about 11 percent a year, below the 20 percent bar.

    • Not C, because 'Financial Outputs' at F and 'Modal return' at D bind: a strong input with negative outputs is not a material thesis on the framework's terms.
    • Not F, because the internal forces are real, executed and dated, and the cash and Growth assets cover the market capitalisation, which puts a floor of sorts under the outcome.

  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.

  • The bull case, at its strongest (Deutsche Bank, Buy, $23.00 target, 2026-07-24; the chief executive's $37 to $40 a share "value unlock" ambition on the Q2 2026 call), says: cash of about $2.0 billion and Growth assets of about $2.9 billion are worth about $13 a share against a $9.45 price, so the telecom equity (half of Virgin Media O2, 90 percent of Ziggo Group, all of Virgin Media Ireland) is in the price for less than nothing
    • the Sunrise spin-off of November 2024 shows the mechanism, because Sunrise represented 20 percent of proportionate EBITDA and captured 45 percent of the combined market capitalisation
    • and the Ziggo Group spin-off is dated, funded without external capital and pulled forward.
  • We concede the cover and the mechanism.
  • We do not concede that the telecom equity is worth much more than nothing today: Virgin Media O2's net total debt including vendor financing is 5.89 times EBITDA against a 4.0 to 5.0 times target, VodafoneZiggo's net leverage was nearly 6.0 times at end-2025, Ziggo Group's 2026 free cash flow base is about €120 million against a 2028 target of €500 million that the sell-side calls extremely challenging, and the Sunrise precedent came with 4.5 times leverage funded by a Liberty cash injection and Swiss refinancing below 3 percent.
  • An equity slice this thin on a declining EBITDA is an option, and the market prices it as one.
  • The spin-off can crystallise the option
    • it cannot, by itself, make the underlying assets grow.
  • Where the competing hypothesis would win: if VodafoneZiggo's margin stabilises above 41 percent for two quarters and the first Benelux disposal comes in at or above book, the second condition becomes probable and the case moves to a B-band expected return on our branches.

The case in 8 to 10 sentences (the thesis as tested), kept in full at both ends of the record (Part 1, E 3, BB 4)
  • This is a corporate-change and value-crystallisation case, not a demand inflection, not a recovery and not a compounder.
  • Liberty Global is a Bermuda-domiciled, Nasdaq-listed holding company whose two largest assets (half of Virgin Media O2 in the United Kingdom; since 2026-07-31 all of VodafoneZiggo in the Netherlands, now being combined with Telenet into Ziggo Group) sit outside or at the edge of its consolidated accounts, carry about six times leverage including vendor financing and leases, and are losing revenue and margin to fibre overbuild and price competition.
  • The change is that a management with a long record of separations is dismantling the structure: Sunrise was spun off in November 2024, Wyre was separated from Telenet on 2026-07-23, Vodafone was bought out on 2026-07-31, about $1.2 billion of assets were sold in 2026 to date, corporate costs were cut about 75 percent, and 90 percent of Ziggo Group is to be distributed to shareholders on Euronext Amsterdam as early as mid-2027.

    • at the operating companies revenue fell 6.0 percent rebased in Q2 2026, VMO2's EBITDA fell 2.9 percent with ARPU down 4.6 percent, and VodafoneZiggo's EBITDA fell 7.6 percent.

  • The price is not set on earnings or cash flow, both negative, but on the value of the parts: the parent's cash and Growth assets are worth about $13 a share against a $9.45 price, so the telecom equity is in the price for less than nothing.
  • On our modal path (the spin-off completed with the discount only partly closed) the shares are worth about $11 in eighteen months, a return of about 11 percent a year against the 20 percent bar
    • the path that clears the bar needs the market to value the telecom equity above zero as well, and sits at about one chance in three.
