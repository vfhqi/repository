Section 3 – Landed Cost Analysis under Bulk Import

Section summary
  1. Bulk pre-stocking helps Chinese e-commerce platforms bypass heavy direct-parcel surcharges but introduces higher inventory risk.
  2. European peers like H&M and Next maintain structurally lower fulfillment ratios due to optimized regional sourcing.
  3. Rising ocean freight rates and local fulfillment costs compress the operating margins of platforms transitioning to localized networks.

Surcharges on Direct Parcels

1.
Drastic cost inflation: The European Union implemented a temporary €3 customs duty on low-value e-commerce parcels under €150 on July 1, 2026. Huatai Research reports that this regulatory shift targets direct-shipping models by applying the fee to each distinct item category in a package. This tariff structure significantly inflates the entry cost of cheap goods and forces platforms to re-evaluate direct-to-consumer distribution networks to protect their regional sales volumes.
2.
Severe margin compression: The administrative burden on cross-border shipments increased further when the European Commission introduced an additional €2 customs handling fee. Morgan Stanley estimates that at a typical €10 average selling price, the combined €5 charge represents an unsustainable 50% duty burden on directto-consumer logistics. This regulatory headwind pressures platforms to quickly shift toward bulk-import warehousing models within the European Union.
3.
Disproportionate price impact: Direct-to-consumer small parcel shipments of low-value goods are highly sensitive to these flat fees because their average item values are extremely low. A former Temu senior manager stated that the platform's average item price is below €5, making flat tariffs a major operational bottleneck. These fixed charges completely erode the cost advantage of Chinese factories, making localized warehousing the only viable option to sustain European operations.

Bulk Sourcing Cost Architecture

4.
Favorable duty structures: Bulk importing allows platforms to bypass the per-parcel handling fee and clear products under standard Most Favored Nation tariff rates. Morgan Stanley calculates that the standard bulk duty on apparel is approximately 12.0% of customs value. Assuming customs value represents 35% of the retail price, the bulk import tariff drops to just 4.8% of average selling price, which is far lower than the direct-parcel charges.
