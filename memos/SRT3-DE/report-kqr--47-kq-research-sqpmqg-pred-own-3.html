Section 2 – Definitions and Treatments of Cash Flow Metrics

Section summary
  1. Sartorius AG defines free cash flow as operating cash flow minus investing cash flow, presenting a more comprehensive but less capital-disciplined metric than some peers.
  2. Distinct accounting treatments for leases, interest, and non-operational tariff refunds create significant comparability gaps between Sartorius and its life-science tools competitors.

Sartorius Core Cash Flow Framework

1.
Customized definition: The life-science tools manufacturer Sartorius AG formally defines free cash flow as cash flow from operating activities minus cash flow from investing activities. This specific corporate policy ensures that all capital expenditures, acquisitions, and financial asset transactions are fully deducted from the metric before arriving at the final reported cash figure. By contrast, this all-inclusive definition exposes the final free cash flow figure to significant volatility from inorganic transactions like subsidiary acquisitions.
2.
Financing classification: Sartorius AG records interest paid within the financing activities section of its consolidated statement of cash flows, which totaled €150.9 mm in the first nine months of 2026. This classification choice directly complies with IAS 7 guidelines but effectively inflates reported operating cash flow relative to companies that deduct interest payments from operations. Consequently, any comparison of operating cash generation across the sector must adjust for this financing-activity interest classification.
3.
g Sartorius AG consolidated statement of cash flows for the nine months ended September 30, 2026 Non-cash treatment: Right-of-use asset additions under IFRS 16 are treated as non-cash transactions by Sartorius AG and are excluded from the main cash flow statement. Although lease liabilities repayment is classified under financing activities, the lease payments are never deducted from free cash flow under the company's operating-minus-investing framework. This treatment can lead to an overstatement of available discretionary cash when compared with peers that explicitly subtract lease amortization.
4.
Capitalized development: Internally generated intangible assets and capitalized development costs are capitalized by Sartorius AG when they meet strict technical feasibility criteria. These capitalized R&D expenditures are subsequently classified under investing activities as investments in intangible and tangible assets. Because investing cash flow is subtracted from operating cash flow, capitalized development costs directly reduce reported free cash flow at Sartorius.

Peer Group Comparative Analysis

5.
Narrower definition: Analytical instruments peer Thermo Fisher Scientific defines free cash flow as GAAP operating cash flow less net capital expenditures, which excludes acquisitions. This differs from Sartorius because Thermo Fisher excludes all business acquisition cash from its primary non-GAAP free cash flow metric. Furthermore, Thermo Fisher reports operating lease payments as cash outflows within operating activities, thereby naturally lowering its operating cash flow.
6.
Conservative definition: Diversified peer Merck KGaA is replacing operating cash flow with free cash flow as its key performance indicator from fiscal year 2026. Merck KGaA defines free cash flow as operating cash flow less capex, plus proceeds from disposals, and less lease payments. Unlike Sartorius, Merck KGaA explicitly deducts leasing payments from its free cash flow, representing a more conservative representation of cash generation.
7.
Simplified definition: Molecular diagnostics peer Qiagen NV calculates its reported free cash flow metric as net cash provided by operating activities reduced by purchases of property, plant, and equipment. Qiagen does not deduct intangible asset acquisitions or capitalized development costs from its primary free cash flow metric, unlike Sartorius. This accounting choice drives a higher free cash flow for Qiagen because developmental and intangible investments are left in investing activities.
8.
Reconciled framework: Swiss CDMO peer Lonza Group utilizes a highly customized alternative performance measure called Operational Free Cash Flow, which reconciles directly from EBITDA. Lonza's formula deducts capital expenditures and net working capital changes while adjusting for share-based payments and provisions. This customized EBITDA-start method bypasses traditional cash flow statements entirely, making direct comparison to Sartorius's GAAP-based cash flow highly complex.
