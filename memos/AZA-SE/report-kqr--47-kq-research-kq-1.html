Section 2 – Danish retail savings market structure & TAM 19 findings

Section summary
  1. Denmark represents a structurally lucrative savings market with per-capita net financial assets exceeding twice that of Sweden, unlocking a ~70% addressable TAM expansion for Avanza.
  2. However, retail equity participation severely lags Sweden, and conflicting TAM definitions between Avanza and Nordnet highlight structural friction in converting institutional pension wealth into platform assets.

Substantial wealth depth paired with lagging retail participation

1.
Superior household asset depth: Allianz SE reports that Danish net financial assets per capita reached EUR 191,560, representing more than twice the EUR 86,800 observed in Germany and significantly exceeding Swedish household levels
2.
Underdeveloped retail fund adoption: DNB Carnegie highlights that only 13% of the Danish population directly owns mutual funds compared to 64% in Sweden, revealing a massive structural adoption gap in collective retail investments
3.
Competitive direct share ownership: DNB Carnegie notes direct share ownership in Denmark reaches 21% of the population, which compares closely with Sweden's 22% retail share ownership rate
4.
Underpenetrated tax-advantaged wrapper: Morgan Stanley research shows Denmark's Aktiesparekonto account has reached ~658,000 users or 10% population coverage, trailing far behind Sweden's ISK account penetration of 36%
5.
International investment bias: DNB Carnegie reveals Danish households hold DKK 667bn in foreign securities compared to DKK 549bn in domestic equities, driven by a domestic equity market dominated by a few mega-cap listings
6.
Structural retail participation headroom: DNB Carnegie argues the sharp contrast between Danish high net wealth and low retail fund participation creates a long runway for digital platform conversion

Pension asset domination and tax wrapper architecture

7.
Colossal institutional pension pool: Allianz SE highlights that asset-backed pension arrangements in Denmark correspond to over 200% of national GDP, creating a total market pool exceeding DKK 4tn
8.
Quasi-mandatory occupational pillar: Morgan Stanley notes Danish occupational pension schemes feature mandatory combined employer and employee contributions of 10% to 18% of payroll, achieving 90% workforce coverage
9.
Tax-advantaged contribution bottleneck: Morgan Stanley points out that while 2024 reforms expanded Aktiesparekonto annual limits, the account's lifetime cap of ~EUR 22,000 historically constrained individual wealth accumulation compared to Sweden's uncapped ISK wrapper
10.
Annual tax drag mechanism: Nordea macro research notes Danish pension capital is subject to a 15% annual PAL yield tax, requiring substantial early-year cash outflows that periodically constrain local money market liquidity
11.
High institutional concentration: Expert commentary from a former technology executive indicates Danish banking infrastructure is tightly controlled by incumbent data center combines like BEC, presenting structural distribution hurdles for new platform entrants
12.
Accretive wrapper innovation: DNB Carnegie emphasizes that Nordnet's recent launch of the Livrente pension wrapper illustrates how digital platforms can incrementally tap into Denmark's vast corporate pension flows

Discrepant TAM models and addressable market headroom

13.
Ambitious platform TAM expansion: Avanza management estimates entering Denmark expands its total addressable market by ~70%, adding roughly SEK 9trn in addressable savings capital to its SEK 13trn Swedish base
14.
Conservative incumbent TAM baseline: DNB Carnegie observes a stark discrepancy in market sizing, given Nordnet defines the Danish addressable TAM at SEK 6.2trn while forecasting market expansion of ~8% annually
15.
Unexplained TAM variance: DNB Carnegie notes Avanza has not publicly disclosed its granular asset class assumptions, making the SEK 2.8trn gap between Avanza's SEK 9trn estimate and Nordnet's SEK 6.2trn figure a key analytical uncertainty
16.
Capital mobility vs cash lock-in: Deutsche Bank Research demonstrates Swedish households keep just 12% of financial wealth in bank deposits, whereas Danish retail deposits remain heavily concentrated in traditional mortgage banks
17.
Institutional pension barrier: DNB Carnegie analysis implies Avanza's ~70% TAM expansion assumes capturing broader wealth pools, whereas much of Denmark's wealth remains locked within employermandated pension vehicles inaccessible to basic execution-only platforms
18.
Edge read on wealth concentration: DNB Carnegie ESG research reveals that for investment portfolios above SEK 1m, Danish wealth is heavily concentrated among older male cohorts who demonstrate higher loyalty to established regional banks
19.
Other side of the story: Avanza's headline ~70% TAM expansion overstates near-term addressable opportunity because Danish wealth is overwhelmingly locked in institutional Pillar 2 pensions and traditional bank deposit relationships rather than self-directed retail equity accounts.
