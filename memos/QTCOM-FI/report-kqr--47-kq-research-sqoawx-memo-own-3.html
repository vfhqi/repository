Section 2 – Distribution Licence Mechanics and Revenue Recognition

Section summary
  1. Qt Group's distribution license revenue is invoiced via either retrospective pay-as-you-ship reporting or upfront prepaid blocks, with customers increasingly preferring retrospective billing due to macroeconomic caution.
  2. The focus vertical mix has evolved with medical becoming the largest segment at twenty percent, while regional revenues remain concentrated in Europe and North America.

Contractual Structures and Invoicing Models

1.
Robust initial baseline: Research into Qt Group's distribution licensing models identified fifteen distinct sources, six technology companies, and two comparable historical cases. Company financial statements from March 2026 show that these distribution licenses or runtimes are reported as part of license sales and consulting revenue. Investors must analyze these structures carefully because they represent the primary engine for scaling highmargin runtime royalty streams over time.
2.
Dual transaction options: Qt Group invoices its distribution licenses either through standard pay-as-you-ship reporting or via upfront block purchases. Former company executives and transcripts from February 2025 indicate that some clients report actual device shipments retrospectively while others pre-buy blocks, such as EUR 0.5 million, and purchase another stack once exhausted. This structural flexibility allows the company to secure upfront cash flows but introduces quarterly volatility in reported net sales depending on the timing of customer pre-buys.
3.
Cautious buying behavior: Customer preferences have recently shifted away from upfront volume commitments toward retrospective pay-as-you-ship billing. President and CEO Juha Varelius stated in an earnings call in February 2025 that macroeconomic uncertainty has prompted clients to prioritize cash conservation by avoiding large prepaid blocks. This shift toward retrospective billing reduces short-term deferred revenue buffers and makes quarterly royalty forecasting highly unpredictable for analysts.
4.
Exponential contract size: Large individual distribution license agreements can reach highly significant values that distort near-term growth metrics. Broker reports from Carnegie in February 2025 point out that individual flagship deals can generate immediate revenues of EUR 1.5 million or more. When these massive deals close, they create difficult year-over-year comparison baselines that make future licensing growth appear artificially depressed to investors.

Regional and Focus Vertical Splits

5.
Diversified sector exposure: Qt Group's distribution license revenue is distributed across several key embedded verticals, with medical currently representing the single largest share. Management confirmed during a July 2026 earnings call that the medical vertical is the biggest segment at approximately 20% of revenue, while automotive has declined from 20% to between 10% and 15%. This changing vertical mix helps insulate Qt Group's overall licensing performance from cyclical downturns in automotive production globally.
6.
Accelerating defense demand: The aerospace and defense vertical has experienced rapid expansion and is on track to surpass automotive as a key revenue contributor. Company presentations from October 2026 highlight that defense and aerospace demand has grown rapidly to reach a 15% to 20% share of revenues. This geopolitical-driven demand provides a strong counter-cyclical offset that protects group profitability during macroeconomic slowdowns.
7.
Regional revenue concentration: Qt Group's geographic sales are heavily concentrated in Europe and North America, leaving the company exposed to Western economic trends. The company's annual report published in March 2026 showed that Rest of Europe generated EUR 86.08 million in 2025, while North America contributed EUR 74.19 million. This geographic distribution means that trade disputes or tariff policies implemented in these key Western jurisdictions will directly impact reported distribution revenues.
8.
Steady Eastern performance: The Asia-Pacific region represents a stable and growing territory for Qt Group, supported by robust local manufacturing ecosystems. According to the annual report published in March 2026, APAC net sales were EUR 54.41 million in 2025, which represents a stable performance compared to EUR 54.98 million in 2024. Continued expansion in Chinese automotive and consumer electronics markets remains vital for sustaining APAC distribution licensing growth.
