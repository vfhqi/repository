[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = D

Thesis. Brockhaus Technologies is a sum-of-the-parts and reinvestment case dressed as a corporate change: it has sold the business that was 89 percent of its revenue and is now about EUR140m of net cash, one sub-scale secure-KVM hardware unit and a two-person head office that has promised to hand back about EUR180m in total and to buy the next platform with the rest. At EUR17.60 the shares trade below our mid-point net cash of EUR20.6 a share, so the holder is paid to own what is left; but the modal return from here, a probable second cash return of EUR13.2 a share plus the stub re-rated to about its cash, is 12 percent a year against the 20 percent bar, and the case is not compelling at this price.

  • The internal change is among the strongest in form the framework's screen describes, and it has already happened.
    • The 51.96 percent Bikeleasing stake was sold at EUR525m enterprise value, about 11x trailing EBITDA and 2.5x money, and closed on 30-Jun-26
    • A EUR90m tender at EUR25.00 for up to 3,592,000 shares, about a third of the count, closed on 9-Oct-26
    • IHSE has a new chief executive and finance director
    • A cost programme took IHSE's adjusted EBITDA margin from 10.4 percent to 14.8 percent in H1 2026
    • And the balance sheet went from EUR48.1m of net debt at 31-Dec-25 to EUR243.0m of net cash at 30-Jun-26
    • Six of the framework's twelve internal forces are strong and dated, twice the screen's rule of three
      • The one red flag that applies in form, selling the crown jewel, was a sale above the value the market put on the whole group, which is the opposite of what the flag warns against
  • The external forces are mixed and net down on the revenue line.
    • European defence re-armament is a structural, sector-level tailwind and is already in IHSE's mix at 43 percent of FY2025 revenue, up from about 20 percent a year earlier
    • Against it, commercial KVM demand is being hollowed out by software, German corporate investment is subdued, and US tariff and Middle East disruption took IHSE's Americas revenue from EUR5.17m to EUR1.80m in H1 2026
    • The two roughly cancelled: segment revenue fell 4.7 percent in FY2025 and 7.2 percent in H1 2026
  • 'Financial Outputs' have not improved in the sense the framework means, because the change sold the earnings along with the asset.
    • The balance sheet is transformed, which is the arithmetic of a disposal
    • The operating outputs of the continuing company are falling revenue, a margin improving only from a loss (continuing adjusted EBITDA -EUR1.7m to -EUR0.5m), negative operating cash flow of -EUR1.3m in H1 2026, negative earnings per share, and no sell-side estimate left to upgrade
    • Along the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain the change is complete at the resources, choices and actions, half-lit at the key operating metrics (margin up, revenue down), present in one line of the financial statements (cash) and absent at the sell-side's estimates, which do not exist
  • The price pays for none of the stub.
    • After the tender the market capitalisation is about EUR120m against our mid-point net cash of EUR140m (range EUR124m to EUR153m), so the market values IHSE, the head office and the deployment of the cash at about -EUR20m together
    • The shares sit at 0.86x our mid-point net cash per share and 0.97x the low end
    • The one fact that could thin the cover is a EUR28.7m tax question on the disposal gain, which the research does not settle and which is the first thing to verify
  • The return therefore rests on two things that are not earnings, and together they reach 12 percent a year, not 20.
    • A second cash return of about EUR90m, EUR13.2 a share and 75 percent of the price, is the board's stated intention and we judge it probable within eighteen months
    • The stub moving from -EUR20m to about +EUR7m once the market values IHSE at 7x its FY2028 adjusted EBITDA net of the capitalised head office adds about EUR4 a share
    • Our modal eighteen-month value is EUR20.86 including the cash returned, 12.0 percent a year
      • The three-year value is EUR23.94, 10.8 percent a year
      • The earnings-growth leg is nil because the stub's earnings are negative at both ends
    • The modal path clears the bar only below about EUR15.9, or if the market pays net cash plus a full multiple for IHSE with no charge for the head office, which it has not done for three years
  • The pattern is 'Major Corporate Change', and it fits poorly.
    • The inputs are as broad as the pattern asks
    • The output the pattern is named for, a major upgrade to the earnings trajectory, does not exist, because there is no earnings trajectory and no estimate to upgrade

  • The case therefore is a bounded one: bounded below by cash and a probable return, bounded above by a 12 percent modal return, with the upside owned by a founder whose next decision is undated.

    • What would make it compelling is a price below about EUR15.9, or a first acquisition at a price the market capitalises above cost
    • Neither is on the file today

  • Rating = D, because the Building Block measures the materiality of the expected change as a chain from forces to outputs to return, and the chain breaks at the second link: 'Change Forces' (E 1) is B, 'Financial Outputs' (E 2) is D because what the forces have produced is cash and a smaller company rather than improving earnings, and the 'Modal return' (E 3) is D at 12 percent a year.

    • The Elements bind rather than carry each other: a strong force that produces no earnings improvement is material as an event and immaterial as an investment thesis in the framework's sense, so the rating sits with the weaker Elements, one notch below the forces, not at their average.
    • Not C, because C would need a modal return at or near 13 percent with a plausible earnings path, and the earnings path on the file is negative without an acquisition that has no date
    • Not F, because the cash leg is large, probable and company-delivered, and the price is below the low end of our net cash range.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The strongest case against the D is that the framework's earnings chain is the wrong lens for a liquidation-plus-option case.
    • On that lens the holder at EUR17.60 is buying EUR18.2 to EUR22.5 of net cash, of which EUR13.2 is to be handed back within eighteen months, plus IHSE, a 75 percent gross-margin certified defence supplier in a EUR300m oligopoly niche with new leadership, plus the chief executive's option to buy the next Bikeleasing, all for less than nothing
      • The thesis is highly material, a 75 percent cash return plus a free business, and the outputs that matter, cash per share and the discount, are improving.
    • We concede the lens: it is why we build the return on the sum of the parts rather than on a multiple of earnings, and why E 3 is D rather than F.
    • We hold the D because the framework's bar is 20 percent a year in the modal case, and the cash return plus a modest value for the stub reaches 12 percent
      • The extra 8 to 15 percent a year needs either the market to pay full value for IHSE net of central costs, which it has not done for three years and which this week's environment, with Fragility at Double Underweight, argues against, or a first acquisition at a price the market will capitalise above cost, which has no date
      • Both are possible, neither is modal.
    • The counter-case's own weakest point is on the file: the research's probability-weighted downside was EUR8 to EUR15 a share on the old perimeter, the kvm-tec insolvency came without warning, and the research's own joint probability for its bull case was 33.6 percent
      • A thesis whose own research gives the bull case one-in-three odds is not a B.

Summary: Written Summary of the Investment Case (not rated) (E 3, BB 4)
The case in 8 to 10 sentences (the thesis as tested), kept in full at both ends of the record (Part 1, E 3, BB 4)
  • Brockhaus Technologies is a sum-of-the-parts and reinvestment case dressed as a corporate change: a holding company that has sold the asset that was 89 percent of its revenue (Bikeleasing, at EUR525m of enterprise value, about 11x trailing EBITDA and 2.5x money, closed 30-Jun-26) and now consists of about EUR140m of net cash after the EUR90m tender (our mid; EUR124m to EUR153m), one sub-scale secure-KVM hardware business with EUR13.4m of half-year revenue falling 7.2 percent at a 14.8 percent segment margin, and a two-person head office that has promised to return about EUR180m in total and to buy the next platform with the rest.
  • It is not compelling at EUR17.60, and the reason is arithmetic rather than opinion: the market capitalisation after the tender is about EUR120m, below our mid-point net cash, so the holder is paid to own the stub, and the modal return from here is a probable second cash return of EUR13.2 a share plus a stub worth about EUR7.6 a share once the market stops valuing IHSE and the head office at less than nothing
    • That is EUR20.86 in eighteen months, 12.0 percent a year, and EUR23.94 in three years, 10.8 percent a year, against a 20 percent bar.
  • The change has done to the outputs exactly what a disposal does: the balance sheet went from EUR48.1m of net debt to EUR243.0m of net cash and the profit-and-loss account went to zero
    • The cost programme lifted IHSE's margin 4.4 points and nothing lifted its revenue.
  • Along the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain the change is complete at the resources, choices and actions (new IHSE leadership; the decisions to sell and return; the actions taken), half-lit at the key operating metrics (margins up, revenue down), present in one line of the financial statements (cash) and absent at the sell-side's estimates, which do not exist.
  • The external forces are mixed: European defence re-armament lifts IHSE's largest end market while commercial KVM demand declines structurally, the German economy stagnates, and tariff and export disruption collapsed the Americas in a half.
  • On valuation the case costs nothing on earnings, because there are none, and offers a 14 percent discount to our mid-point net cash
    • What it costs is the risk that a founder with a transactional record and a ten-person head office deploys the retained cash badly, which is the research's "false friend" reading.
  • The drop-through that matters is IHSE's margin: at the 10 percent of a year ago rather than the 15 to 16 percent we assume, the modal return falls from a D to an F, which is the measure of how little the stub can bear and how much the case is the cash.
  • Our side: the second return is probable, the discount closing is possible, an accretive first acquisition is plausible, and the three together do not make a 20 percent modal case at EUR17.60.
  • What would make it compelling is a price below about EUR15.9, or a first acquisition at a price the market capitalises above cost, and neither is on the file today.
