EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Sdiptech AB's pricing power represents the central question for investors as the group transitions from aggressive acquisitions to organic margin stability, particularly in the face of UK minimum-wage rises in labour-intensive service units bound by fixed-price insurance contracts, raw-material inflation on long-lead projects, a strengthening Swedish krona against sterling and the euro, and commoditisation of EV-charger hardware.

This comprehensive investigation finds that Sdiptech's pricing power is real but structurally delayed, operating through a six to nine-month pass-through lag that creates temporary margin compression during inflationary surges before full recovery materializes. The company's mid-term earnings trajectory, with DNB Carnegie projecting adjusted EPS to grow from SEK 13.30 in 2025 to SEK 22 in 2028e, will only materialize if niche pricing power offsets persistent labour and material inflation.

The overall assessment reveals divergent pricing capabilities across Sdiptech's four business segments, with Energy & Electrification demonstrating robust pricing power by growing sales 17% in Q3 2026 while maintaining a high EBITA margin of 24.1%, successfully offsetting component inflation through proactive list-price adjustments, while Supply Chain & Transportation experienced margin compression to 18.5% in Q3 2026 from 21% in Q3 2025 due to higher material costs at Mecno that neutralized strong volume growth.

This bifurcation underscores that Sdiptech's consolidated pricing power is not uniform and depends heavily on individual subsidiary contract structures, customer concentration, and exposure to regulated versus cyclical end-markets. The key findings on price versus cost recovery dynamics indicate that Sdiptech achieved 13% organic sales growth in Q3 2026, but gross margins compressed to 58.1% from 60.4% in the prior year, suggesting the company relies on high-volume deliveries to drive growth rather than raising unit prices.

The group gross margin fell from 61.1% in Q2 2025 to 59.3% in Q2 2026 due to segment mix shifts, with rapid growth in Supply Chain & Transportation diluting overall profitability despite stable pricing elsewhere. This evidence strongly suggests that recent organic growth has been volume-driven rather than pricing-led, raising legitimate concerns about whether the company possesses true forward-looking pricing power or is merely recovering backward-looking costs with a widening lag.

The contract structure analysis confirms that Sdiptech faces a persistent six to nine-month lag between experiencing a cost increase and successfully executing a price increase, representing a structural limitation that causes profitability to temporarily contract during the initial quarters of an inflationary surge. The UK minimum-wage increase on April 1, 2025, negatively impacted profitability in Water & Bioeconomy and Safety & Security business areas, with the statutory wage hike increasing consolidated personnel costs by 5% in early 2025, and this sensitivity will pressure margins again during subsequent UK minimum-wage rises scheduled for April 2026 and April 2027.

The most vulnerable unit remains Auger, which provides pipe and sewage repair services under fixed-price contracts of one to three years with major insurers, relying on these long-term agreements to secure steady volumes despite significant commercial barriers when trying to accelerate price renegotiations with large financial institutions.

Peer comparison analysis reveals that Sdiptech's pricing lags compare unfavourably to best-in-class serial acquirers. Indutrade grew net sales 5% organically in Q3 2026 while expanding EBITA margins by 70 basis points, demonstrating superior pass-through capabilities with minimal commercial friction, whereas Sdiptech faces a persistent six to nine-month lag on its fixed-price service contracts. Halma upgraded its EBIT margin guidance to 23.5%-24% due to favourable mix, commanding exceptional value-based pricing across economic cycles because its life-saving technologies are required by law, preventing buyers from seeking cheaper non-compliant alternatives.

However, Sdiptech's strategic positioning in non-discretionary infrastructure maintenance provides defensive qualities that buffer the group against severe downturns, even if immediate pricing flexibility remains constrained. most critical threats to Sdiptech's margin profile emerge from three distinct vectors. First, Chinese AC charging units import at USD 25 to 40 per unit, forcing Rolec to rely on high-quality modular DC designs and software integration to evade the low-margin B2C segment, meaning Sdiptech's long-term EPS depends heavily on software and service contract recurring revenues.

Second, Mecno's manufacturing operations remain vulnerable to raw material and steel inflation on fixed-price long-lead projects, with higher material costs compressing the Supply Chain & Transportation segment's adjusted EBITA margin to 18.5%, indicating that longlead deliveries without active indexation clauses leave Sdiptech exposed to commodity spikes such as steel safeguards or high Brent crude prices. Third, approximately half of group sales are generated within the United Kingdom, and a strengthening Swedish krona against sterling and the euro severely dilutes consolidated earnings when translating foreign subsidiary results, with a 10 percent change in exchange rates impacting group operating profit by SEK 83 million, meaning a strong Swedish krona directly compresses reported earnings per share.

The macroeconomic and secular dynamics present a mixed picture for forward pricing power. Sdiptech is capturing substantial pricing premiums driven by rapid expansion of power-hungry global data centres, with Phase 3 achieving 17 percent sales growth because of data-centre expansion and European data-centre and power grid expansions driving unprecedented multi-year demand that allows Sdiptech to secure high-margin supply contracts. Safety & Security net sales increased by 9 percent in Q3 2026, supported by an increased threat landscape in critical environments, with Eagle Automation successfully establishing high-margin perimeter security solutions within data centres and airports, and regulatory barriers to entry preventing pricing pressure from low-cost competitors. These secular tailwinds provide genuine margin expansion opportunities in specialized segments where regulatory mandates and mission-critical applications support premium pricing.

The evidence base supporting this assessment demonstrates medium overall confidence, with critical gaps remaining around Sdiptech's precise split between price increases and volume growth in its 13% organic sales growth in Q3 2026, making it difficult to assess true underlying demand trends, so waiting for future quarterly filings or conducting expert interviews with division managers will be necessary to resolve it, and this lack of transparency means forward EPS estimates remain highly sensitive to volume volatility.

Financial markets currently lack direct evidence regarding customer resistance to price increases in Sdiptech's UK service units, with DNB Carnegie warning that prolonged UK labor inflation will keep service margins under pressure unless price increases are accepted, and conducting specific channel checks with major UK insurance clients would close this gap by confirming their willingness to absorb higher costs, because without this data analysts cannot accurately predict whether Auger will experience customer defection which would hurt reported EPS.

The Key Question can be partially resolved with the following conclusion: Sdiptech possesses genuine but imperfect pricing power that operates reactively rather than proactively across its portfolio. The company demonstrates strong pricing capability in regulated, mission-critical niches such as perimeter security, datacentre power infrastructure, and specialized water treatment, where switching costs and regulatory barriers protect margins.

However, pricing power remains structurally constrained in labour-intensive service contracts with large institutional buyers and manufacturing units exposed to commodity volatility on long-lead fixed-price projects. The six to nine-month pass-through lag creates cyclical earnings volatility that will persistently pressure near-term margins during inflationary periods, but historical evidence confirms that Sdiptech eventually achieves full cost recovery over multi-quarter windows.

The forward outlook depends critically on three unresolved factors: whether management can successfully negotiate mid-term contract flexibility to shorten historical pricing lags; whether software and service recurring revenues at Rolec and other digital-enabled units can offset hardware commoditisation; and whether public infrastructure budgets remain resilient despite fiscal constraints across European municipalities.

DNB Carnegie argues that Sdiptech's specialized companies possess strong defensive qualities during public budget constraints because critical repairs cannot be delayed, ensuring steady volumes even during downturns and meaning consolidated earnings remain resilient, thus Sdiptech's structural health may hold up better than a simple bearcase scenario assumes unless macroeconomic headwinds worsen. Investors should monitor consolidated gross margin trends, personnel cost inflation rates, and segment-level EBITA margin divergence as the most reliable early warning signals of deteriorating pricing power that would threaten the projected EPS trajectory to SEK 22 in 2028.

Contract framework diversity: Sdiptech's decentralized subsidiaries utilize diverse contract structures including fixed-price projects, list prices, and multi-year framework agreements. This diverse contract mix means that the group cannot implement immediate price increases across all business units simultaneously, leaving earnings vulnerable to sudden cost spikes.

Currency Headwinds and Hedging Limits

1.
Translational foreign exchange sensitivity: Sdiptech faces significant translation risks because its reporting currency is Swedish krona while its operations are highly international. Sdiptech's prospectus published on October 29, 2026, details that approximately half of group sales are generated within the United Kingdom. DNB Carnegie's report on October 26, 2026, notes that a strengthening Swedish krona against sterling and the euro severely dilutes consolidated earnings when translating foreign subsidiary results. Sdiptech's annual report on September 1, 2026, states that a 10 percent change in exchange rates impacts group operating profit by SEK 83 million. This means a strong Swedish krona directly compresses reported earnings per share.
2.
Short-term derivative hedging constraints: Sdiptech utilizes currency futures to mitigate transactional foreign exchange fluctuations but faces strict duration limits on these contracts. Sdiptech's annual report published on April 14, 2025, reveals that the group enters into currency forward contracts that mature within twelve months to hedge exposure. RedEye's report on October 29, 2026, emphasizes that these short-term hedges cannot shield the group from multi-year structural currency shifts. Sdiptech's Q4 2025 earnings call on February 10, 2026, confirmed that a negative 8 percent currency effect from sterling was a substantial drag on sales. Consequently, prolonged sterling weakness eventually impairs earnings once short-term derivatives expire.
3.
Balance sheet matching strategies: Sdiptech actively manages translation exposure in its equity through balance sheet debt allocation rather than relying solely on derivatives. Sdiptech's annual report on September 1, 2026, explains that some currency hedging takes place by partially distributing group loans in foreign currencies. Sdiptech's CEO Anders Mattson explained during the Q3 2025 earnings call on October 24, 2025, that this balance sheet matching offsets foreign currency asset declines with corresponding loan reductions. DNB Carnegie's sector update on May 27, 2025, reports that this structural hedging reduces net asset volatility but does not protect translation of the operational cash flows. This means that while equity remains balanced, operating EBIT remains highly vulnerable to exchange rate moves.
