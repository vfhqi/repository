Jurisdictional Loss Carryforwards

1.
Significant concentration: Redcare Pharmacy reports total tax loss carryforwards of €345.9 million, with €311.9 million representing unrecognized Dutch tax losses at the end of fiscal year 2025. This concentration of losses in the Netherlands means that the group's future tax shelter is heavily dependent on Dutch taxable profits. Therefore, regional earnings generated in other DACH markets may not be easily offset by these central Dutch losses.
2.
Divergent locations: The remaining €34 million in tax loss carryforwards is split across Germany, Austria, Switzerland, and regional International segments. While Germany represents the largest market, accounting for 82% of sales in 2024, its local losses are relatively small. As a result, the rapid expansion of German revenues will quickly expose the group to German corporate taxes.
3.
Deferred recognition: Under accounting standards, Redcare has not recognized deferred tax assets for the majority of these unused losses. Management noted that a tax group must first demonstrate sustained profitability before recognition is deemed appropriate. Consequently, reported EPS will remain depressed until these assets are recognized on the balance sheet.
4.
Unrecognized potential: Deutsche Bank Research highlighted that the massive tax losses represent a substantial long-term valuation cushion for the company. However, these tax shields will only benefit shareholders once the company transitions into sustainable GAAP profitability. Therefore, near-term EPS forecasts remain highly sensitive to the precise quarter of tax asset recognition.

Jurisdictional disparities: The geographical mismatch between where losses are located and where revenues are growing remains a core challenge. While 82% of sales are generated in the DACH region, €311.9 million of unrecognized tax losses are locked in the Dutch parent company. This mismatch requires complex intercompany structures to prevent double taxation on regional profits while the parent remains in a loss carryforward position.

Dutch Tax Limitation Mechanics

6.
Offset restrictions: The Dutch corporate tax rule restricts the offset of historical losses against current-year taxable profits. Under this legislation, tax losses can be offset fully up to €1 million, but any excess taxable profit can only be offset up to 50% of that remaining amount. This rule applies directly to Redcare's Dutch fiscal unity.
7.
Taxable impact: If Redcare's Dutch operations generate a taxable profit of €10 million, only €5.5 million can be offset by historical losses. The remaining €4.5 million would be taxed at the standard Dutch corporate rate of 25.8%. This means that despite having €311.9 million in losses, Redcare will face immediate cash tax payments upon reaching profitability.
8.
Fiscal coordination: Redcare's Dutch operations are structured as several distinct corporate entities that form a corporate income tax fiscal unity. This fiscal unity includes SA Europe B.V., Shop Apotheke B.V., and EuroService Venlo B.V., among others. Consequently, losses within this unity are consolidated, but the €1 million offset restriction applies to the consolidated group rather than each entity individually.
9.
Temporary variances: The company's deferred tax balances also reflect various temporary differences, including software capitalization and lease liabilities. In fiscal year 2025, deferred tax assets associated with available losses stood at €8.0 million, down from €9.2 million in 2024. These small recognized assets indicate that the vast majority of the tax shield remains excluded from the balance sheet due to profitability uncertainty.
