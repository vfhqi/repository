Section 2 – Long-Term Financial Targets and Capital Markets Day Likelihood

Section summary
  1. AddLife has firmly reconfirmed its long-term financial targets, backed by robust operational run-rates that exceed key efficiency and leverage benchmarks.
  2. Management has signaled no immediate intention to revise its long-term targets or host a 2027 Capital Markets Day, preferring to focus on deleveraging and organic execution.

Reconfirmed Targets and Current Run-Rates

1.
Firm commitment: AddLife reconfirmed its long-term financial targets in both July and October 2026, anchoring its strategic framework on sustainable, profitable expansion. These corporate objectives include an average adjusted EBITA growth rate of 15% per year, a return on working capital (P/WC) above 45%, a net debt to EBITDA leverage ratio below 3.0x, and a dividend payout ratio of 30% to 50% of net profit. This multi-layered target framework is designed to balance aggressive growth with strict balance sheet discipline. Achieving these targets consistently is critical to proving the sustainability of AddLife's decentralized business model.
2.
Strong profitability: The actual return on working capital (P/WC) at AddLife has consistently exceeded the long- term target threshold of 45% by a wide margin. As of September 30, 2026, the rolling 12-month return on working capital reached 63%, up from 62% at the end of fiscal year 2025 and 55% as of September 30, 2025. This high return on working capital reflects substantial operational improvements and tighter inventory controls across its operating subsidiaries. High capital efficiency directly supports the company's self-funded acquisition model by maximizing cash generation.
3.
Leverage cushion: AddLife has successfully reduced its financial net liabilities to EBITDA ratio well below the maximum limit of 3.0x. As of September 30, 2026, the company's leverage ratio stood at 2.5x, down from 2.9x on September 30, 2025, although it rose slightly from 2.2x at the end of fiscal year 2025 due to renewed acquisition payments. The total financial net liabilities amounted to SEK 4,746 mm on September 30, 2026, compared to SEK 4,048 mm on December 31, 2025. This leverage trajectory provides AddLife with the necessary headroom to continue pursuing strategic bolt-on acquisitions without stretching its balance sheet.
4.
EBITA growth: AddLife reported a rolling 12-month adjusted EBITA of SEK 1,311 mm on September 30, 2026, representing a growth of 4% compared to SEK 1,259 mm for the full fiscal year 2025. For the third quarter of 2026, adjusted EBITA grew by 11% year-on-year to SEK 298 mm, driven by organic momentum and recently consolidated operations. Historically, management's long-term objective is for approximately half of this 15% EBITA growth target to be generated organically and the other half through acquisitions.

Capital Markets Day Likelihood and Target Revisions

5.
No formal plans: AddLife has not announced any plans to hold a Capital Markets Day or revise its long-term financial targets during 2027. Management has expressed confidence in the existing targets, which were reconfirmed in consecutive interim reports in 2026. Because the current financial run-rate for return on working capital of 63% is already well above the 45% target, there is little pressure from debt holders or shareholders to alter the efficiency metrics. Instead, management's primary focus remains on consolidating recent acquisitions and keeping leverage firmly below the 3.0x threshold.
6.
M&A capacity: The operational improvements and strengthened balance sheet have enabled AddLife to accelerate its acquisition activity, which had been prepared over an extended period. During the first nine months of 2026, the company completed three acquisitions and finalized an additional one shortly after the Q3 interim report, adding approximately SEK 350 mm in annual net sales. This renewed acquisition pace aligns with the company's long-term EBITA growth targets and helps deploy the cash generated from operations.

Comparative Peer Target Frameworks

7.
Peer targets: To evaluate the strength of AddLife's financial targets, its metrics can be compared with peer frameworks from Swedish compounders Indutrade and Lifco. Lifco operates with a capital efficiency target where return on capital employed (ROCE) excluding goodwill and other intangible assets must exceed 50%. This is conceptually comparable to AddLife's P/WC target of 45%. Indutrade focuses on a return on capital employed of at least 20% on average over a business cycle.
8.
Leverage comparison: Lifco has a balance sheet target to maintain interest-bearing net debt to EBITDA in the range of 2.0x to 3.0x. This target range is in line with AddLife's target of staying below 3.0x net debt to EBITDA. In comparison, Indutrade maintains a net debt to equity ratio target that should normally not exceed 100%.
9.
Payout consistency: All three Swedish serial acquirers target a similar dividend payout ratio of 30% to 50% of net profit. Historically, Lifco has distributed approximately 28% to 33% of its net profit from previous years as dividends, placing its actual payouts at the lower end of its target range to maximize cash reinvestment.

Contrary Evidence and Downside Risks

10.
Contrary evidence: AddLife's rolling 12-month adjusted EBITA growth of 4% as of September 30, 2026, lags far behind its long-term financial target of 15% annual growth. This slower growth rate was driven by negative currency headwinds, rising central operating expenses, and a healthcare labor dispute in Spain that restricted demand. If these macroeconomic and regional operating conditions persist, AddLife may struggle to meet its rolling EBITA growth target without relying on debt-funded acquisitions. Other side of the story: While AddLife’s current return on working capital of 63% and leverage of 2.5x are strong, a rapid acceleration in acquisition pace could quickly erode these buffers if acquired margins underperform. Lifco and Indutrade have demonstrated more consistent long-term EBITA growth rates above 15% through larger scale and more diversified portfolios. If AddLife prioritizes meeting its 15% EBITA growth target through higher M&A volumes, it risks raising its leverage back toward its historical 3.0x ceiling and compressing its return on working capital.
