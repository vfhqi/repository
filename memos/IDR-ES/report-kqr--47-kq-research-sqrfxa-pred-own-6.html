Section 2 – Organic Revenue and Profitability Trends at Hispasat and Hisdesat

  1. The Space division faces severe organic revenue contraction despite substantial inorganic growth from the consolidation of Hispasat and Hisdesat.
  2. Low-orbit satellite constellation operators like Starlink are aggressively disrupting commercial broadband and rural demand segments with superior latency and pricing.

Organic Revenue Dynamics and Consolidation

Declining underlying performance: Indra reports that organic revenue in its newly formed Space segment fell by 17 percent during the first nine months of 2026. This negative trend worsened during the third quarter of 2026 with a reported 20 percent organic decline. This organic weakness contrasts sharply with the division's reported revenue surge of 372 percent which was driven entirely by inorganic consolidation of Hispasat and Hisdesat.
2.
Underlying structural challenges: Morgan Stanley notes that Space organic revenues declined by 14 percent during the first half of 2026. This contraction occurred despite robust organic growth across all other business units. The widening gap between headline growth and underlying performance demonstrates that legacy commercial operations are facing immediate competitive headwinds that public contracts have not yet offset.

9.
Competitive market shift: Major telecommunications operators in Europe are bypassing geostationary capacity by signing direct distribution agreements with low-orbit constellation giants. Telefonica has partnered with Starlink for corporate data and Sateliot for 5G internet services, while Orange and MasOrange cooperate with Eutelsat's OneWeb. These alliances show that traditional distributors no longer view geostationary fleets as viable standalone solutions for regional connectivity needs.

Section 3 – The IRIS2 Programme: Ground Segment and Low-Orbit Commitments

Section summary
  1. The IRIS2 secure connectivity program provides substantial long-term backlog expansion for Hispasat but introduces significant long-term capital expenditure commitments.
  2. Revenue execution will stretch

well into the next decade with most capital spending deferred until the post-2030 commercialization phase.

Substantial Backlog and Revenue Timing

1.
Strong backlog expansion: Indra Sistemas reports that its Space segment achieved an extraordinary book-to-bill ratio of 7.53x in the first nine months of 2026. The company secured 1.926 billion euros of order intake from the IRIS2 program during the third quarter of 2026, which materially improves long-term visibility across the entire division. The massive intake drove the total Space backlog to 4.819 billion euros, providing a secure multi-year revenue pipeline for the group.
2.
Gradual revenue recognition: Hispasat plans to recognize the vast majority of its ground segment and Low LEO revenues over a highly extended horizon. Bernstein Research reports that Indra management expects to recognize approximately 70 percent of the associated program revenues by the end of 2030. The remaining 30 percent of the revenues will be billed during the 2031 to 2035 period, meaning the financial benefits will extend far into the next decade rather than providing an immediate earnings spike.
3.
Lucrative exploitation rights: Hispasat has secured exclusive commercialization rights for related civil and military satellite services from 2031 to 2038 in exchange for its constellation investments. Spanish defense publications report that these rights grant Hispasat valuable commercial access to secure non-geostationary orbits in regions of high strategic and commercial value for dual-use applications. operational exploitation phase will allow the consortium to monetize spare capacity not consumed by the European Union's sovereign security needs.
4.
Elevated peer investments: Hispasat has committed up to 600 million euros to the Low LEO layer, which is lower than its core consortium peers. Eutelsat has committed to investing 2.23 billion euros in the shared infrastructure and an additional 1.16 billion euros in the commercial infrastructure. Meanwhile, SES committed up to 1.8 billion euros, highlighting that Hispasat's commitment remains a highly disciplined capital exposure relative to larger peers.
5.
Substantial order momentum: Indra Group reported that its overall third quarter 2026 bookings surged by 139 percent year-on-year, propelled almost entirely by the massive Space segment bookings. The company's record-high order book of 22.165 billion euros was heavily driven by the Space segment, which added 4.645 billion euros to the total. The massive growth is directly attributed to Hispasat's prime ground segment contract and the Low LEO constellation contract, which together generated 1.926 billion euros of order intake in a single quarter.

Capital Expenditures and Financing Schedules

6.
Phased capital expenditure: Hispasat intends to deploy its 600 million euros capital commitment in a heavily back-loaded manner to protect near-term cash flows. Bernstein Research reports that Indra management has guided to less than 100 million euros of related capital spending before 2030. The vast majority of the capital expenditure, totaling between 500 million and 540 million euros, will ramp up from 2031 onward during the active constellation deployment phase.
7.
Accelerated peer capex: SES is planning a far more front-loaded capital expenditure schedule for its IRIS2 medium Earth orbit constellation segment than Hispasat. Deutsche Bank Research reports that SES expects its capital expenditure envelope to translate into an average annual spend of approximately 400 million euros from 2027 to 2030. This aggressive front-loading contrasts with Hispasat's deferred spending schedule and could strain peer free cash flow over the medium term while Indra protects its balance sheet.
8.
Severe execution delays: Geostationary satellite operators attempting to build and launch massive low Earth orbit constellations from scratch have historically faced severe execution and scheduling delays. Morgan Stanley Research highlights the precedent of OneWeb, which was founded in 2012 but did not complete its constellation of approximately 700 satellites until 2024. This historical precedent suggests that constructing a multi-orbit constellation from scratch is exceptionally challenging, meaning the IRIS2 program's 2030 operational target remains highly susceptible to timeline slippage and cost overruns.
9.
Contrary evidence: Starfish Space chief operating officer expressed extreme skepticism regarding the viability of the IRIS2 procurement structure, predicting the entire program is bound to fail. The expert notes that the European Commission has forced multiple legacy primes to cooperate rather than compete on the same contract, despite their lack of experience deploying proliferated LEO constellations at scale. Such structural coordination issues among legacy operators are expected to lead to severe design blockages and terminal project delays.
10.
Accelerated European funding: European Union authorities have committed over 10 billion euros, including 6 billion euros in direct public funding, to develop the secure communications network. Sivers Semiconductors AB notes that the SpaceRISE consortium will combine this heavy public subsidy with its own capital to accelerate secure satellite deployment across low and medium orbits. The public-private financing structure effectively subsidizes Hispasat and its partners, transforming legacy operators from financially constrained broadcasting companies into critical national security infrastructure providers.

Execution Risks and Return Profiles

11.
Incomplete return profiles: Indra's management has kept the specific profit margins and long-term return metrics for its massive IRIS2 investments entirely undisclosed. Bernstein Research points out that because management did not disclose expected margins or returns, investors cannot yet connect the program's strategic appeal to an eventual cash-return investment profile or return on capital. This lack of visibility contrasts with consortium partner SES, which has already guided to a 55 percent run-rate EBITDA margin and an internal rate of return of at least 10 percent.
12.
Demanding design milestones: Hispasat and the SpaceRISE consortium must satisfy highly complex technological validation milestones before full-scale manufacturing can commence. Satellite news reports indicate that the consortium has only one year to complete and secure approval for its Preliminary Design Review. The consortium must then conclude its crucial Critical Design Review by 2028 if it wants to maintain the aggressive schedule of launching first satellites in 2029 and entering initial service in 2030.
13.
Growing sovereign options: Eutelsat's vice president of sales engineering expresses confidence that the IRIS2 constellation will be successful because multiple European nations view it as their primary secure communications platform. However, alternative LEO constellations such as Telesat Lightspeed and Amazon Kuiper are aggressively scaling up to capture the same high-value government markets. These competing platforms threaten to close the operational gap and limit Hispasat's commercial pricing power by the time IRIS2 becomes fully operational in 2030.
14.
Other side of the story: Although execution and coordination risks within the SpaceRISE consortium are high, Hispasat's role as the prime contractor for the 1.6 billion euros ground segment provides highly secure, riskinsulated sovereign revenue streams that are immune to commercial LEO market volatility. The European Commission's public-private partnership structure guarantees anchor government demand and covers potential launch failures up to in-orbit validation. public protections ensure that Hispasat can build out critical sovereign infrastructure and generate predictable service returns.

Section 4 – Capital Expenditure and Depreciation of the Satellite Fleet

Section summary
  1. Indra Sistemas' consolidated Space division faces a high-depreciation environment that limits near-term operating profit margin conversion despite high EBITDA.
  2. Planned capital spending of 400 million euros over 2025 to 2030 will support fleet renewals and European sovereign satellite programs like SpainSat NG.

Fleet Capital Spending Cycles

1.
Elevated capital intensity: Indra Sistemas' newly consolidated space assets demand significant ongoing investment because satellite infrastructure requires front-loaded development and deployment costs. Morgan Stanley reports on February 4, 2025, that satellite operations are inherently capital-intensive because operators must fund both the initial launches and subsequent multi-year replacement cycles. This heavy investment cycle means that Space division free cash flow conversion is structurally lower than in the group's legacy IT services business, directly impacting long-term earnings per share growth.
2.
Substantial planned spending: Hispasat and Hisdesat have outlined a substantial capital deployment program to support their strategic fleet modernization. Bernstein Research notes on February 4, 2025, that the combined entities plan to spend 400 million euros in capital expenditure over the 2025 to 2030 period. This investment program is driven by the ramp-up of the secure European IRIS2 satellite system and the scheduled retirement of several older geostationary satellites. These capital requirements could restrict nearterm cash returns unless the Spanish government accelerates program billings.
3.
Gradually declining intensity: Hispasat's capital investment rate is projected to moderate over the next several years compared to historical levels. Bernstein Research estimated on February 3, 2025, that Hispasat's capital expenditure-tosales ratio was 49 percent in fiscal year 2023. The broker projected that this ratio would decline slightly to 48 percent in fiscal year 2024 and further to 47 percent in fiscal year 2025. This gradual decline supports nearterm free cash flow generation but also highlights that fleet replacement cycles remain highly demanding relative to the group's broader industrial portfolio.
4.
Key upcoming launches: Hisdesat is actively extending its strategic military satellite capabilities through upcoming launches. Airbus SE announced on August 3, 2026, that the SpainSat NG III secure communications satellite is scheduled for launch in the third quarter of 2030 and will become operational in early 2031. This project completes the SpainSat NG program, which is the largest space project developed by the Spanish aerospace industry. These multi-year deployment timelines mean that depreciation charges will remain deferred until the next decade, temporarily shielding Indra's reported net income.

Depreciation and Amortization Impact

5.
Heavy baseline depreciation: The Space division's capital-heavy structure translates into a massive depreciation headwind that significantly lowers reported operating margins. Morgan Stanley highlights on February 4, 2025, that the implied depreciation and amortization charge for Hispasat and Hisdesat is approximately 140 million euros. This charge represents a substantial 35 percent of consolidated space sales, which is significantly higher than legacy defense divisions. Consequently, while the segment generates high EBITDA, the high depreciation baseline dampens the division's direct contribution to Indra's consolidated earnings per share.
6.
Extended asset lifespans: Hispasat has historically managed its depreciation schedule by extending the estimated useful life of its geostationary satellite fleet. Redeia Corporacion reported on June 8, 2023, that the group re-estimated the useful lives of its core satellite assets prospectively to 16.5 years from the previous 13 to 15 years. This accounting adjustment resulted in a 16 million euros reduction in annual depreciation charges, directly boosting reported net profit. This practice indicates that asset lifespan assumptions are critical for determining Indra's reported space profitability.
7.
Contrary evidence: Hispasat's geostationary fleet useful life of 16.5 years is significantly longer than the lifespans of low-orbit satellites, making the division vulnerable to technology obsolescence. Deutsche Bank Research notes on August 7, 2026, that while geostationary assets are engineered for a 15-to-20-year lifespan, low Earth orbit satellites rely on rapid, iterative hardware cycles of only 3 to 5 years. This structural difference means that if low-orbit competitors capture more market share, Hispasat's long-lived assets may suffer premature economic impairment before their physical lifespans end, requiring sudden write-downs.

Comparative Capital Intensities

8.
Massive peer budgets: Major European satellite operators are undergoing massive capital investment phases that dwarf Indra's space budget. Deutsche Bank Research reported on October 26, 2026, that Eutelsat is maintaining a heavy 4 billion euros capital expenditure envelope out to fiscal year 2029, with annual capital expenditure rising to 1.2 billion euros in fiscal year 2026-27. This aggressive investment program is driven by the replenishment of its OneWeb low-orbit constellation and secure European connectivity programs. This peer comparison indicates that Indra's space capital exposure is conservative, minimizing near-term financing risks relative to its highly leveraged pure-play competitors.
9.
Optimized peer capex: SES has recently adjusted its near-term spending plans to preserve cash flow amidst changing market conditions. Bernstein Research reported on November 5, 2026, that SES optimized its fiscal year 2026 capital expenditure outlook to approximately 600 million euros, down from its prior guidance of 700 million euros. The broker's model projects that SES's capital expenditure-to-revenue ratio will peak at 33.1 percent in 2028 before declining to 28.6 percent by 2030. These metrics demonstrate that even mature operators must allocate substantial revenue to capex to remain competitive, validating the capital-heavy nature of the sector.
10.
Severe in-orbit risks: Satellite operators face severe operational risks that can abruptly truncate asset lifespans and destroy planned depreciation schedules. Bernstein Research highlights on September 25, 2026, that in-orbit malfunctions or launch failures can permanently reduce usable capacity and shorten asset lifetimes. Because repairs in orbit are typically impossible, such technical anomalies can trigger immediate asset write-downs and eliminate expected future cash flows. This operational risk means that Indra's earnings remain sensitive to satellite performance, despite robust insurance coverage.

Based on these sources, the evidence suggests that Indra's space division capital requirements are well-structured to protect group cash, because the majority of its IRIS2 low-orbit commitments are deferred until after 2030. Consequently, the division will not experience a severe near-term cash drain, allowing the group to maintain its broader defense industrialization plans.

Section 5 – The Paz II Programme and Earth Observation Outlook

Section summary
  1. The Paz II radar satellite programme secures Spain's long-term Earth observation sovereignty through a state-funded loan exceeding one billion euros.
  2. The transition to advanced synthetic aperture radar technology will significantly enhance military surveillance resolution and reduce data delivery latency.

Fleet Modernisation and Program Funding

1.
Government funding: The Spanish Council of Ministers approved a modified budget item to grant national satellite operator Hisdesat a repayable loan of 1.011 billion euros. This state backing will finance the manufacture and development of the Paz II dual-use radar satellite mission. The funding will ensure the continuity of Earth observation services after the current Paz satellite reaches its end of life.

2.
Structured disbursement: The government-backed financing for Hisdesat's new radar satellite programme is scheduled to be distributed progressively across several fiscal years. The operator will receive 76 million euros in 2025, increasing to 179 million euros in 2026 and 182 million euros in 2027. The largest allocations of 213 million euros and 272 million euros will be disbursed in 2028 and 2029, respectively.
3.
Strategic backing: Morgan Stanley notes that the PAZ II satellite programme is 100 percent consolidated by Indra through its control of Hisdesat. This program acts as a public financing mechanism under Spain's Special Modernization Programs to build national industrial space capacity. Consequently, Indra's revenue path is insulated by these sovereign, multi-year programmes with guaranteed funding.
4.
Industrial leadership: Airbus Defence and Space Spain was selected in July 2025 as the prime contractor to develop and manufacture the two Paz II satellites. The contract is a major milestone because 65 percent of satellite content will be developed domestically. This high domestic involvement consolidates the leadership of Spain's aerospace sector.
