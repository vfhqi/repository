Section 2 – Cost Base Splits and Fixed vs Variable Leverage

Section summary
  1. Unilever possesses structurally high marginal leverage with incremental volumes yielding contributions far exceeding baseline gross margins.
  2. Competitors like L'Oréal and Reckitt leverage elevated gross margins to maintain investment flexibility and insulate profitability during input cost shocks.
  3. Unilever achieved a significant gross margin recovery over the 2023 to 2025 period, but volume growth remained highly uneven across geographies.
  4. Industry peers demonstrated divergent margin trends during the inflation cycle, with L'Oréal reaching record profitability while food-exposed peers faced persistent raw
  5. Historical precedents confirm that heavy reliance on pricing actions can damage volume momentum, forcing extensive fixed-cost cuts to defend overall operating margins.

Unilever Cost Structure and Drop-Through

1.
High structural contribution: Unilever maintains a pro forma gross margin exceeding 48% and brand marketing investment over 18% of turnover. Within its €20 bn cost of goods sold, surfactants, active chemicals, and fragrances account for approximately 50% of the total spend. This concentrated cost structure means raw material price movements directly dictate baseline gross profitability. Thus, stabilizing chemical input costs remains key to maintaining operational margins.
2.
Elevated incremental profitability: Unilever management asserts that every additional unit of volume generates a marginal contribution upwards of 60% compared to its baseline gross margin. This incremental profitability reaches 62% in Personal Care and ranges between 65% and 70% in the high-margin Beauty segment. Consequently, volume-led expansion delivers dramatic fixed-cost absorption that scales profitability faster than price hikes. This high drop-through means that minor volume accelerations translate directly to significant operating margin expansion.
3.
Operational leverage sensitivity: Bernstein Research highlights that Unilever's margin opportunity remains underpinned by volume growth and mix, driving a 60% marginal contribution. This operating leverage framework is projected to deliver 10 to 20 basis points of annual gross margin expansion. However, the business must sustain positive volume momentum to realize these benefits and offset stranded corporate overheads. Therefore, any slowdown in volume growth directly threatens the bottom line.

Peer Comparisons and Margin Models

4.
Superior margin resilience: L'Oréal reported a strong H1 2026 gross margin of 74.8% and advertising and promotional expenses at 32.6% of sales. The company achieves significant operational flexibility because its high gross margins insulate the business from fixed-cost absorption shocks. Furthermore, selling, general, and administrative expenses declined by 70 basis points to 18.0% of sales. Consequently, L'Oréal can selectively reallocate savings to marketing without diluting its record profitability.
5.
Strong mix benefit: Morgan Stanley notes that L'Oréal should see continued margin expansion driven by sales shifting toward high-margin divisions. Assuming a 75% gross margin drop-through, a 1% incremental revenue increase would generate €330 mm in gross profit. This operating leverage allows L'Oréal to support high marketing intensity while adhering to its margin expansion framework. Thus, the premium beauty business model exhibits superior leverage compared to standard household staples.
6.
Aggressive overhead reduction: Reckitt has established a strict structural target to exit 2027 with a corporate fixed cost base of 19% of net revenue. The company maintains a historically high gross margin of approximately 60%, which provides consistent fuel for marketing reinvestment. Furthermore, Reckitt's Fuel for Growth program focuses on lowering fixed overheads to drive operating profit growth ahead of revenues. This structural cost design aims to maximize bottom-line leverage as volume growth stabilizes.

Emerging Markets and Cost Pressure Precedents

7.
Severe margin pressure: ICICI Direct reports that Hindustan Unilever's gross margin declined by 397 basis points year-over-year to 47.6% due to raw material inflation. To defend its EBITDA margin of 22.4%, the company reduced ad spend by 213 basis points. This trade-off illustrates the challenge of managing operating leverage when input costs spike in emerging markets. Therefore, emerging-market operations remain highly sensitive to local commodity cycles.
8.
Calibrated pricing response: Hindustan Unilever management implemented selective price increases to offset petrochemical and palm oil inflation while protecting volumes. The company continues to pass on absolute inflation and leverage other P&L lines through AI-powered procurement. Structurally, the local subsidiary aims to maintain operating margins within a 22% to 24% range. This strategy confirms that absolute profit protection can occasionally override short-term volume-led leverage goals.
9.
Divergent cost exposure: Morgan Stanley reports that Haleon carries low petroleum cost exposure with only 3% of revenue linked to crude oil. In contrast, Beiersdorf faces margin headwinds from limited pricing power and elevated marketing reinvestments behind its Nivea brand. While Haleon leverages its strong productivity program to expand margins, Beiersdorf remains vulnerable to retailer negotiations. Thus, sector operating leverage depends heavily on brand pricing authority and raw material mix.

Contrary Evidence and Alternatives

10.
Contrary evidence: Jefferies Research reports that L'Oréal demonstrated during the pandemic that margins can be protected through marketing flexibility rather than pure fixed-cost operating leverage. The beauty leader rapidly adjusted its massive advertising and promotional spend to match short-term sales shocks, maintaining stable operating margins. This historical precedent suggests that high gross margin businesses do not rely solely on volume leverage to defend earnings during demand downturns. Thus, variable cost flexibility can be more valuable than fixed-cost leverage in volatile environments.
11.
Other side of the story: While volume-led operating leverage offers attractive margin upside, heavy reliance on this driver exposes consumer companies to severe earnings volatility if market demand weakens. If raw material inflation outpaces pricing power, fixed factory overheads compress margins rapidly as capacity utilization declines. Furthermore, if companies are forced to cut marketing investments to protect short-term margins, they risk damaging long-term brand equity. Therefore, a balanced contribution of price, mix, and structural productivity is required to sustain stable bottom-line growth.

Unilever Margin Expansion and Volume Trends

12.
Sizable progression: Unilever gross margins expanded by over 330 basis points to reach 46.9% in fiscal year 2025. This consecutive multi-year recovery reflects the benefit of portfolio mix and value chain interventions. Management intends to use these gains to continue fueling ongoing reinvestment into our brands during 2026.
13.
Balanced growth: Unilever underlying sales growth of 3.5% in 2025 was driven by a recovery in volume growth of 1.5% and price growth of 2.0%. The company has successfully transitioned to a healthy balance between volume and price after the severe inflation cycle. Maintaining this positive volume momentum is critical for the group to offset its high stranded costs.
14.
Regional variations: Unilever experienced divergent volume performance during 2025, as North American volumes remained resilient while Latin American volumes collapsed. Latin American volumes fell in mid-2025 due to aggressive pricing actions that severely hurt competitiveness in the laundry segment. This pricing pressure forces the group to implement selective pricing resets to restore brand competitiveness in emerging markets.
15.
Concentrated momentum: Unilever Power Brands delivered underlying sales growth of 4.3% in fiscal year 2025, driven by 2.2% volume growth. These core brands achieved structurally superior performance compared to non-power brands which grew by only 0.7%. This divergence confirms that premiumization and focused marketing can drive volume growth in challenging environments even during retail destocking.

The group maintained stable operating margins of 18.6% in 2019 and 2020 despite the initial pandemic shock. This expansion was driven by a gross margin improvement of 50 basis points, rising to 51.1% through supply chain efficiencies and positive mix. The consumer brands division achieved a strong volume rebound of 2.8% in the final quarter of the year.

Section 4 – Unilever Productivity Targets and CMD 2026 Plan

Section summary
  1. Unilever's new 2026 productivity program aims to generate substantial gross savings to offset stranded overheads and support margin expansion.
  2. Structured capital allocation and automated technologies underpin the efficiency drive, although recurring restructuring costs continue to weigh on reported results.

Historical Productivity and Role Reductions

1.
Substantial reductions: Unilever historically addressed structural inefficiencies through its 2024 productivity initiative that targeted office-based operations. The company successfully accelerated this program to deliver €670 mm of cumulative savings by the end of 2025. This early delivery helped the company lower general overheads and offset initial Ice Cream demerger costs ahead of its original timeline. Consequently, these efficiency gains protected short-term operating profit and laid the groundwork for future margin expansion.
2.
Accelerated eliminations: Unilever's prior efficiency drive relied on a massive headcount reduction program targeting 7.5k office roles. The company executed 4.3k of these full-time equivalent cuts by the end of 2024 to capture early overhead benefits. These organizational interventions supported a significant gross margin expansion that provided funding for stepped-up marketing across core divisions. Ultimately, reducing structural headcount represents a repeatable mechanism for management to protect its earnings per share baseline.
3.
Structural consolidation: Unilever's 2024 cost initiative stood out as the largest round of staff reductions in its corporate history. The program eliminated 7.5k jobs and concentrated nearly half of those cuts within historically slow-growth European operations. These aggressive reductions brought the organization closer in line with lean operational structures of US peers. By lowering the fixed employee expense base, the company established a more sustainable cost structure that protects long-term earnings per share.
4.
Complete execution: Unilever achieved the final phase of its €800 mm cost-saving initiative ahead of schedule during 2026. The business expanded its operating margin by 10 basis points in the first half of 2026 by lowering corporate overheads. This operational discipline helped the company maintain stable brand and marketing investment at 16.1% of sales. Thus, completing this program established a leaner operational baseline before initiating the next wave of portfolio restructuring.

The Capital Markets Day 2026 Plan

5.
Ambitious savings: Unilever announced a new €1 bn gross productivity savings program at its Capital Markets Day on November 4, 2026. This initiative is designed to run through the end of 2029 to simplify processes and reorganize business units. Management intends to deliver this program through organizational simplification and process redesign enabled by technology. These efforts aim to protect core margins as the company transitions to a pure-play beauty and home care model.
6.
Dual pillars: Unilever's CMD 2026 plan splits its €1 bn target into structural and technological interventions. The company allocated one-third of the savings target to creating a leaner corporate centre with 30 P&L units. The remaining two-thirds of the savings will come from re-wiring seven key processes and radically reducing non-core IT applications. Executing these dual pillars is critical to expanding operating margins and improving local execution.

Stranded Cost Mitigation and Capital Expenditure

9.
Protracted timelines: Unilever expects to manage its stranded corporate costs through Transitional Service Agreements established with McCormick. These agreement structures will span two to three years and provide essential operational headroom for the business. Management projects that these gross stranded costs will remain between €400 mm and €500 mm. Thus, the transitional timeline allows Unilever to wind down shared corporate expenses without disrupting daily commercial operations.
10.
Persistent requirements: Unilever's historical financial records show that restructuring expenses have remained a recurring feature of its business model. The company guided to €500 mm of restructuring costs over the 2027 to 2029 period to mitigate stranded costs. Furthermore, total guided restructuring spend will reach €1.2 bn over three years, which includes baseline efficiency initiatives. This elevated spending pattern indicates that achieving structural simplification requires continuous capital outlays that limit immediate profit realization.
11.
Targeted deployment: Unilever has raised its capital expenditure budget to 3.6% of turnover for the 2027 to 2029 period. The company plans to allocate over 50% of this capital investment directly to productivity-enhancing initiatives. These focused investments are designed to generate gross margin benefits of 20 to 30 basis points annually. Consequently, the success of this capital program is vital to offsetting persistent input cost inflation and supporting underlying EPS growth.
12.
Contrary evidence: Unilever's ambitious capital payback targets may face regional execution hurdles and extended timelines. High marketing reinvestment needs and local competitive shifts could prevent these savings from reaching the bottom line. Therefore, structural differences in regional supply chains mean that the guided 3.6% productivity capex may not deliver uniform margin benefits across all operating units.

Section 5 – Peer Precedents in Cost Elimination and Simplification

Section summary
  1. Major peer precedents reveal that portfolio simplification and organizational de-matrixing can unlock structural margin expansion when paired with sustained brand reinvestment.
  2. Reckitt and Estée Lauder demonstrate that aggressive overhead reduction must be balanced against market execution risks to prevent long-term volume erosion.
  3. Structural cost elimination programs that fail to fund brand equity ultimately lead to unsustainable profitability margins and subsequent revenue compression.

Procter and Gamble Brand Simplification Model

1.
Decisive rationalization: Procter & Gamble executed a massive portfolio simplification from 2014 to 2019 by divesting, discontinuing, or consolidating over 105 non-core brands. These brands represented approximately 14% of fiscal year 2013 sales but only 6% of earnings, which allowed management to refocus resources on core categories to drive faster organic expansion. This strategic concentration created a structurally higher-margin business model that outperformed the historical diversified portfolio.
2.
Deep organizational savings: Procter & Gamble streamlined operations during this cycle by executing back-to- back $10 bn productivity plans that delivered substantial overhead savings over a decade. By fiscal year 2018, the company had reduced its total employee enrollment by 23% and lowered its count of active manufacturing sites by 20%. These severe cuts improved core profit per employee by 50% and freed up capital to reinvest behind its leading brands.
3.
Structural organizational de-matrixing: Procter & Gamble implemented a major structural redesign in July 2019 to eliminate slow-moving corporate layers and shift operational accountability. The company transitioned from its complex matrix structure to six Sector Business Units reporting directly to the chief executive officer. Shifting 60% of corporate work directly to local business units reduced G&A system expenses from 2.9% in 2015 to a record low of 2.2% by 2019.

Reckitt Fixed Cost Optimisation Programs

4.
Ambitious overhead targets: Reckitt Benckiser launched its Fuel for Growth program targeting a 300 basis point reduction in fixed costs by the end of fiscal year 2027. The company aims to lower its fixed overhead cost base from 22% of net revenue in 2023 to a leaner 19%. Early progress delivered a net 190 basis points of savings in the first half of 2025 by reducing management layers from five to three and consolidating legacy corporate roles.
5.
Reinvestment-led efficiency: Reckitt utilized its overhead savings to expand its brand equity investments rather than allowing the benefits to simply drop to the bottom line. The company expanded its adjusted operating profit margin by 110 basis points in 2025 to reach 24.6% while increasing marketing support by 120 basis points. This balanced strategy supports long-term volume momentum by ensuring that administrative cuts do not compromise consumer-facing brand health.
6.
Contrary evidence: Reckitt experienced near-term operating profit margin compression during its structural transformation due to high upfront execution costs and building headwinds. For example, Core Reckitt gross margins fell by 110 basis points year-over-year in the first half of 2026 as supply chain inflation outpaced pricing. This margin deterioration suggests that fixed-cost programs can fail to protect short-term profitability if input inflation accelerates before structural efficiencies materialize.

Kraft Heinz Cost Elimination Precedents

7.
Drastic cost extraction: Kraft Heinz implemented aggressive zero-based budgeting in the years following its 3G Capital-led merger to maximize short-term operating margins at any cost. The company successfully expanded its EBITDA margins from 18.7% in 2013 to a peak of 30.2% in fiscal year 2017. However, this expansion was achieved by severely cutting advertising and R&D spend, which caused absolute sales to contract by nearly 10% over the same period.
8.
Severe structural underinvestment: Kraft Heinz is currently forced to maintain a massive $700 mm reinvestment base in fiscal year 2027 to repair a decade of underinvestment in its core brands. This necessary spending will pressure near-term operating profit and lead to an adjusted operating income decline of low single digits in 2027. The precedent confirms that extracting overheads without reinvestment destroys brand equity and requires expensive, multi-year recovery plans.

Regional Reorganizations and Execution Risks

9.
Disruptive structural overhauls: Haleon initiated a major operational re-organization in January 2026 by restructuring its global reporting segments into six distinct operating units. The company established a new International segment combining Latin America, the Middle East, Africa, and the Indian subcontinent. Although this restructuring aims to improve agility, management admitted that the transition has taken longer and been more disruptive to execution than initially anticipated.
10.
Highly structured turnarounds: Estée Lauder is executing its Profit Recovery and Growth Plan to generate $1.2 bn of annual gross savings by the end of fiscal year 2027. The program targets a net reduction of approximately 10,000 positions globally while optimizing manufacturing and procurement networks. This comprehensive transformation helped expand first-quarter adjusted gross margins by 240 basis points to 75.7% while funding an 8% increase in brand advertising.

Cuttable Versus Uncuttable Cost Lines

12.
Scalable administrative savings: Industry peers have demonstrated that procurement, logistics, and administrative headcount are highly cuttable corporate overhead lines when modernized. For example, Nestlé is utilizing automated AI negotiation agents and expanded e-auctions to drive up to 10% incremental procurement savings across its CHF 60 bn cost base. This focus on site-level productivity and footprint optimization helps streamline back-office infrastructure without affecting consumer-facing growth.
13.
Uncuttable marketing support: Brand marketing and consumer-facing advertising have proven to be uncuttable cost lines because implementing aggressive cuts directly increases the risk of market share losses. Cutting these investments to protect short-term margins directly compromises long-term volume growth under intense competitive threat. Consequently, consumer goods leaders must maintain highly competitive promotional support to defend uncuttable cost lines under threat from private labels.
14.
Other side of the story: Although simplifying brand portfolios and de-layering regions is designed to expand margins, the resulting organizational changes can create severe near-term execution disruption that stalls sales growth. For instance, Procter & Gamble experienced negative organic sales growth in key regions and EPS dilution in the initial quarters following its massive Beauty segment divestiture. Furthermore, these dissynergies can depress core earnings per share before long-term productivity benefits are fully captured.

  2. Retailer consolidation and rising private label penetration in developed markets threaten Unilever's pricing power and volume recovery efforts.

Underfunded brands: Reducing marketing investments to protect short-term profitability risks causing a permanent loss of market share. Morgan Stanley notes that Procter and Gamble boosted its advertising spending by eleven percent in fiscal year 2026. Value Chain Squeezes and Structural Friction

5.
Inflationary pressure: Raw material cost inflation in key chemical feedstocks could overwhelm Unilever's procurement savings. Deutsche Bank Research notes that simple average inflation across crude, palm oil, and aluminium is running high, with surfactant costs rising steeply. If these input costs remain elevated, gross margin improvements will fail to materialize unless volume growth accelerates.
6.
Retail consolidation: Branded suppliers face an increasingly concentrated retail channel in Western Europe that limits their pricing power. Morgan Stanley reports that grocery buying alliances are highly concentrated relative to food suppliers, with private label penetration reaching forty percent in the United Kingdom. This structural shift allows retailers to reject price increase requests from branded manufacturers.
7.
Intense competition: Private label alternatives continue to gain market share in commoditized home care and personal care categories. Morgan Stanley highlights that private label penetration has reached sixteen percent in beauty and personal care across Western Europe. This growing threat means that Unilever cannot easily raise prices without experiencing immediate volume declines.

Early Indicators of Strategy Deviation

8.
Decelerating search: Search interest for high-margin wellness acquisitions has started to show worrying signs of multi-quarter declines. Deutsche Bank Research reports a year-on-year decline in combined Liquid I.V. and Nutrafol online searches. Because these high-gross-margin brands generate essential funding for group marketing reinvestment, their underperformance threatens the wider portfolio.
9.
Weak volumes: Negative volume growth in developed markets indicates that consumers are pushing back against premium pricing. Deutsche Bank Research highlights that developed markets declined by nearly one percent, with Europe particularly weak at negative one point seven percent. If this volume contraction persists, positive operating leverage will not work in key regions.
10.
Contrary evidence: Some industry analysts argue that the planned portfolio simplification will provide sufficient margin insulation regardless of volume trends. Deutsche Bank Research indicates that the corporate cost savings of one billion euros are highly supportive and equivalent to three percent of home and personal care costs. This baseline means that productivity gains can defend earnings even during temporary regional downturns.
11.
Slowing innovation: Long-term brand equity relies heavily on fast innovation pipelines rather than cost elimination. Former Insights Manager at Unilever believes that focusing on core high-margin brands is essential to protect market share. However, if cost-cutting slows the pace of new product development, competitors will capture younger consumers who favor agile, digital-native brands.
12.
Channel shift: Rapid migration towards e-commerce and discount clubs alters the traditional margin profile of personal care products. Former President at Unilever PLC notes that scale with club retailers provides velocity but compresses individual brand margins. If Unilever fails to optimize its promotional strategy in these channels, reinvested cost savings will be entirely absorbed by retailers.
13.
Other side of the story: Although regional reorganizations are intended to improve local execution, shifting back to a regional-first model risks creating decision-making friction. Jefferies Research notes that this structural change represents a complete U-turn on the divisional operating model employed since 2022. This constant structural shifting could disrupt local commercial execution and delay the realization of guided synergies.
