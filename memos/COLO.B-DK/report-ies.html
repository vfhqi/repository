Coloplast (COLO.B-DK): what the 3 November 2026 results do to the existing case

These results leave our park decision where it was but weaken, moderately, the one claim that made it a park and not a drop: that the problem is timing, not quality.

1. What changed

The event the case was waiting for has happened. The new chief executive delivered a numbers-attached strategy update alongside the fourth quarter and full year 2025/26 results (fiscal year ended 30 September 2026).

  • Targets to 2030 were cut. Organic growth of 5% to 7% a year, from 7% to 8%. An adjusted EBIT margin flat in the mid-20% range, replacing the pledge that EBIT would grow at least as fast as sales. Return on invested capital of around 20% for 2029/30, from more than 20%.
  • Guidance for 2026/27: 5% to 7% organic growth and a 25% adjusted operating margin, back-end weighted, with a slow first quarter flagged.
  • A savings programme of DKK 500 million a year, full effect in 2027/28, about 75% landing in 2026/27. About 80% is to be reinvested; about 20% offsets gross margin pressure.
  • The quarter itself was soft. Revenue DKK 7,459 million, organic growth 5%, EBIT before special items DKK 1,848 million, margin 24.8%, gross margin 65.5%. Biologics declined 12% organically. Continence Care grew 5.0%, Voice and Respiratory 9.0%. Organic growth excluding China was 7.0%. Chronic Care grew 6% for the full year. Reported EBIT margin for 2025/26 was 26.1%.
  • One caution on the evidence. The figures given for the size of the miss (8% below a DKK 2,009 million consensus) and for Kerecis's 1% EBIT margin are sourced to broker notes dated February 2026. We treat them as indicative of the pattern, not as confirmed fourth-quarter facts.
  • New headwinds were named: price reforms in France and Germany from January 2027 (low single digit), high-single-digit Hungarian wage rises from January 2027, raw material and freight inflation of 3% to 4% in 2026/27, and neutral-to-negative pricing across the strategy period. US competitive bidding is said to be inside the 2030 model, but the company declined to quantify it.
  • The sell-side split. Jefferies raised its target to DKK 600 (Buy) and calls it a clearing event. DNB Carnegie went to DKK 540 (Hold), Deutsche Bank to DKK 505 (Hold). Redburn cut to DKK 490, Bernstein sits at DKK 465. Morgan Stanley reads the margin target as 8% downside to prior consensus EBIT; its target appears as both DKK 454 and DKK 418 and we cannot say which is current.

Against what the case assumed: the case had core Ostomy and Continence growing 7% to 8% and treated the 7% to 8% group target as the standing ambition. Both are now lower.

2. The challenge

Reading one: this was a reclassification, not a clearing event. The case holds that quality is "not in dispute" and that the open question is timing. The strongest form of the opposite view is that management has now told us the quality is lower, permanently. Margin is to stay flat in the mid-20s to 2030 against a history near 30%. Four-fifths of the savings must be reinvested simply to hold 5% to 7% growth. The innovation spend is aimed beyond 2030 and Deutsche Bank expects no material sales benefit before then. Pricing is neutral to negative for the whole period. The growth target now sits below Convatec's 6% to 8% with a similar margin target. If that is right, the case's best setup, change-from-current clearing in a high-quality compounder, is describing a business that no longer exists, and the comparison is with a solid-quality franchise on a peer multiple. For this to be right, the reinvestment has to be maintenance, not growth: organic growth stays inside 5% to 7% despite the spend, and the margin does not lift once the factory ramp and Kerecis washout pass.

Reading two: the case is now too cautious, and the price is already saying so. The case marked the "huge clearing event" setup as incomplete because guidance had not been kitchen-sinked. It now plausibly has been: targets cut to levels every broker cited calls achievable, the DKK 3.0 billion impairment taken, the two integrations and the China right-sizing declared complete, a chief executive in place with a stated conservative guidance philosophy. Excluding China the group grew 7.0% in the quarter, and the direct US Chronic Care business is growing double digits. The shares stand at DKK 510.2 (16 November 2026), 17.3% above the 200-day average, against the case's reading of DKK 435.5 and 9.5% below it. The case has no route to act before its remaining three reassessment criteria are met, and those may take until mid-2027. For this to be right, 2026/27 guidance has to prove beatable and the false-friend mechanisms benign. The counterweight is real: earnings estimates fell 4% over the last month while the price rose, so the move is multiple, not earnings. Current P/E is 18.0x against the 14.2x to 15x the case recorded, and the consensus target of DKK 482.8 is below the price.

Reading three: the level is conservative but the shape is the old one. The case tests investor relations by whether guidance arrives with numbers. It did. But the year is back-end weighted again, the pattern that preceded the earlier cuts. The second half must carry the Kerecis annualisation, easing factory costs, three-quarters of the savings, and a wound margin above 10%, while wage rises and European price cuts begin in January 2027. The full-year margin guide of 25% is only fractionally above the 24.8% just delivered. For this to be right, the headwinds arrive on time and the offsets slip by a quarter or two. That would be a fourth miss under a new name.

We do not put up a reading that the false friend has been disproved. It has not. R&D spend rose 13% in 2025/26, which eases the R&D-decay mechanism, but a former head of R&D calls recent products incremental. On channel economics the update was silent, and the China destocking shows inventory did sit in the channel. The case said silence here would be a soft negative.

3. What it does to the case

  • Reassessment criteria. The first (a numbers-attached update) is met. The second (Kerecis inpatient growth in double digits, outpatient above the 0% threshold) is not: biologics fell 12%, and the 85% inpatient share is at least partly the arithmetic of outpatient collapse. The third (Luja penetration against the 15% threshold) cannot be tested; no penetration figure was given. The fourth (a fresher risks report) is outside these results.
  • E8.RA4, change-from-current clearing in a high-quality compounder : more pressure, downward. This is where reading one lands.
  • E8.RA5, huge clearing event in solid quality : pressure upward. The missing kitchen-sink is plausibly supplied.
  • E8.RA8, false friend : the same. Not addressed.
  • E7.RA5, conservative investor relations : pressure upward on level, held back by the phasing.
  • E7.RA4, handful of change-from-current items : the same. Three items closed; a savings programme, a two-unit reorganisation, an interim head of Wound and a possible disposal review opened.
  • E7.RA3 and E11.RA4 (both F): the same. More European headwinds; against that, brokers put direct exposure to competitive bidding at about 6% of group sales and a 30 basis point margin drag, well below the scale the case recorded, and a bill proposing a five-year pause is before Congress with uncertain passage.
  • E11.RA2, competitive : more pressure. E11.RA1, demand : slightly more pressure; Continence at 5.0% is below the 8% the case recorded.
  • E1.RA2.CQ4, change-from-current magnitude : more pressure. The de-rating the case measured is partly reversed.
  • MR1, technical momentum : price and relative strength (percentile 58, from 14) have improved; the pinned letter is still D. MR2, sell-side earnings momentum: the memo locked this at F; the pinned letter now reads D.

4. What would settle it

  • The second review, four working days on. It should carry settled broker estimates and targets, including which Morgan Stanley target is right and where 2026/27 consensus EBIT lands against the 25% margin guide. It is unlikely to carry anything on the false-friend mechanisms, Luja penetration or competitive bidding.
  • First-quarter results, 9 February 2027. The test of reading three: is the slow start only as slow as flagged, and is guidance held. Also the first read on Kerecis inpatient recovery.
  • Interim results, May 2027. Gross margin back above 67% would favour reading two; flat near 65.5% favours readings one and three.
  • Convatec's organic growth, each quarter, against Coloplast's.
  • Not committed to by the company: the competitive bidding impact, Luja penetration, receivables ageing and distributor inventory. These are open questions that nothing scheduled answers.

VERDICT

  • Direction: negative
  • Grade: material
  • Moves: MR2 pinned letter F to D (up); price crossed above the 200-day average (9.5% below to 17.3% above); E8.RA4 under downward pressure; E8.RA5 and E7.RA5 under upward pressure
  • Reason: The grade is material only because MR2 is locked to a pinned letter that now reads D against the memo's F, an upward move driven mostly by old revisions rolling off, while the net direction is negative because the reset rebased margin and returns permanently and so weakens the "timing, not quality" claim more than the cleared guidance strengthens it, with no Element or Building Block moving a full grade on judgement.
