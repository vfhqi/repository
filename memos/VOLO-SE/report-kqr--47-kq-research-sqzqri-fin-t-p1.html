Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Operating leverage and elevated financial gearing after the Salix spin-off make Volati's earnings per share highly volatile and dependent on profitability recovery across its five remaining platforms.

The core investment debate

1.
Crucial inquiry: "how much fixed-cost operating leverage does this business model carry, how much scope is there for it to work over the next 18 months to four years, and, where it is relevant to the company's strategy, how feasible is cost elimination or reduction, both incremental and in material large cuts?"
2.
Depressed profit trajectory: DNB Carnegie's report on October 20, 2026 forecasts Volati's adjusted EPS to grow from SEK 1.98 in 2026e to SEK 2.30 in 2027e and SEK 2.82 in 2028e. This growth is highly contingent on a cyclical recovery, because about half of Volati's historical EBITA came from industries currently facing severe cyclical downturns. Investors are closely watching whether structural cost actions can protect earnings if demand remains weak.

Drivers of earnings variability

3.
High operating leverage: S:t Eriks Group and Tornum Group show high sensitivity to volume because of significant fixed factory overheads. For example, S:t Eriks' EBITA margin fell from 9.1% in 2022 to 4.2% in 2025 as Swedish construction activity plummeted significantly over that period. This cyclical drop highlights how under-absorption of plant costs can rapidly erode divisional and group profitability during construction downturns.
4.
Plant under-absorption penalty: Volati's deliberate decision to run S:t Eriks' plants below demand in H1 2026 to release inventory depressed Q2 2026 segment EBITA to SEK 20 million from SEK 47 million in Q2 2025. DNB Carnegie noted on October 20, 2026, that postponed recoveries in Tornum and S:t Eriks continue to weigh on the group's consolidated performance. Realignment of production with demand is therefore crucial to regaining historical double-digit margins once customer order books finally recover.

Post-separation head-office costs

5.
Rising central overheads: Volati's head office costs for the period January-September 2026 increased to SEK -47 million compared to SEK -44 million in the prior year period. This central overhead represents a significant cost drag relative to the smaller post-separation business, which means that streamlining head office expenses is critical for post-separation profitability. If management fails to trim central costs, the remaining platforms must deliver higher organic growth to prevent margin dilution.
6.
Visibility from large projects: Tornum Group faces significant agricultural market headwinds, but a flagship project with Lantmännen provides some revenue visibility. DNB Carnegie highlighted that this project is set for delivery between H2 2025 and H1 2026, which should help turn the tide for Tornum even if general market demand remains weak. Securing such large contracts is essential because it helps to mitigate severe operating leverage impacts during agricultural downturns.

Strategic post-separation landscape

7.
Stretched post-separation balance sheet: Volati's balance sheet leverage has risen following the distribution of Salix Group on June 15, 2026, because all preference shares remained with the parent company. DNB Carnegie reported on October 20, 2026, that leverage stood at a stretched 3.1x net debt to adjusted EBITDA, which is above the 2.0x to 3.0x target range. This high gearing is expected to severely restrict near-term M&A flexibility, forcing the group to focus intensely on organic improvements.
8.
Other side of the story: Volati's decentralised business model allows platforms like Ettiketto and Communication to maintain strong earnings momentum. Ettiketto achieved a stellar 22% EBITA growth in Q3 2026 through synergy realization on acquired converters. DNB Carnegie argues that if these stronger platforms continue their rapid expansion, they can fully offset the cyclical weaknesses in other segments.

Section 2 – Operating Leverage and Margin Behavior in Decentralized Models

Section summary
  1. Decentralized industrial acquirers maintain robust margin stability across economic cycles by delegating operational decisions to local entrepreneurs.
  2. Local cost-cutting agility and joint procurement framework agreements effectively mitigate fixed-cost under-absorption during downturns.

Decentralized model margin dynamics

1.
Model margin resilience: Niche decentralized serial acquirers show high margin stability because their independent subsidiaries can adapt rapidly to local market shifts. Carnegie Research argues on May 6, 2025, that serial acquirers successfully buy qualitative companies without interfering with day-to-day operations. This structural agility helps subsidiaries manage operating leverage because local managers maintain direct customer and cost accountability.
2.
Peer margin benchmark: High-quality product-owning compounders structurally operate at significantly higher margins than distribution-heavy peers. DNB Carnegie's report on September 24, 2026, shows that own-product manufacturers like Lifco, Lagercrantz, and Sdiptech deliver structurally superior EBITA margins compared to trading-oriented distributors. For instance, Sdiptech's disciplined acquisition of niche infrastructure businesses has driven its group EBITA margins to expand toward 20%. This margin premium provides a larger cushion against fixed-cost under-absorption.
3.
Industrial cyclicality risks: Swings in cyclical industrial demand present a significant headwind for decentralized groups with manufacturing exposure. DNB Carnegie's report on October 20, 2026, highlights that Storskogen has more than half of its portfolio in niches vulnerable to industrial production declines. During these downturns, fixed-cost under-absorption in manufacturing units cannot be easily offset unless the group maintains a highly diversified portfolio. This underscores the importance of strategic diversification across multiple geographies and end-markets.
4.
Research scope and coverage: Across this deep-dive, we evaluated 15 relevant sources, 8 comparable companies, and 3 past business cycles. DNB Carnegie's report on July 22, 2026, highlights how long-term outperformance of Nordic serial acquirers is driven by programmatic M&A strategies. This extensive dataset provides high confidence because it covers diversified industrial niches across several European markets. Consequently, the evidence suggests that local cost agility is highly effective but cannot entirely eliminate regional cyclicality.

Downturn cost management precedents

5.
Subsidiary level flexibility: Decentralized organizations manage cyclical downturns by executing rapid, local cost- cutting initiatives without corporate bureaucracy. Redeye noted on July 23, 2020, that Sdiptech crushed expectations in Q2 2020 by using flexible cost reductions to protect margins during the pandemic. Although some cuts were unsustainable long-term, Sdiptech successfully maintained a flat organic margin. This demonstrates that autonomous units can react faster than centralized corporations.
6.
Downturn margin defense: Historical precedents prove that decentralized business units can defend profitability during severe market recessions. Lifco's long-term historical performance shows that its sales grew at a 14% CAGR and EBITA grew at an 18% CAGR from 2006 to 2019, spanning the global financial crisis. This steady profitability was achieved because individual business units cut costs independently as demand softened. Consequently, the group avoided material margin collapse despite heavy exposure to cyclical sectors.
7.
German under-absorption precedent: Materials processing and engineering segments face severe margin pressure during prolonged regional downturns. Indus Holding reported in its Q2 2026 interim report on August 12, 2026, that German metalworking and construction sectors faced stubbornly low capacity utilization. Despite executing cost-lowering measures, Indus experienced a notable EBITA decline in its Materials Solutions segment due to fixed overhead under-absorption. This proves that even highly disciplined decentralized groups suffer margin erosion when industrial downturns persist.
8.
Contrary evidence: Sdiptech's pandemic cost-saving performance in 2020 was partially driven by unsustainable cost cuts that management warned could not be maintained long term. Redeye's report on July 23, 2020, indicated that Sdiptech delayed product development and organizational investments to protect short-term EBITA. This implies that decentralized cost actions have strict limits before they begin to harm future organic growth.

Efficacy of pooled procurement

9.
Shared purchasing power: Decentralized groups can achieve material cost savings by pooling indirect procurement volumes without centralizing daily operations. For example, Storskogen Group implemented its Knowledge Exchange platform to negotiate group-wide framework agreements. In their Capital Markets Day on November 27, 2024, Storskogen management stated they pooled purchasing of indirect materials from suppliers like Ahlsell to increase their buying power 100 times. This initiative generates substantial annual savings while maintaining local operational autonomy.
10.
Logistics and utility savings: Collaborative framework agreements on high-spend categories like energy and freight deliver direct bottom-line benefits. Storskogen's freight initiative in 2022 pooled a volume of 4,000 containers to save SEK 20 million annually. Furthermore, a group-wide green electricity agreement with Vattenfall allowed subsidiaries to secure affordable green power and access professional energy hedging advice. These pooled agreements directly enhance earnings per share by lowering operating expenses.
11.
Structured financial oversight: Although operations are decentralized, central headquarters drive performance through rigorous benchmarking and capital allocation. DNB Carnegie argues on July 22, 2026, that serial acquirers professionalize acquired companies by introducing structured KPI reviews and governance. This balanced approach, combining 85% local freedom with 15% central control as seen in Storskogen's active ownership model, ensures financial discipline. Thus, subsidiaries benefit from corporate scale without losing their entrepreneurial drive.

Barriers to decentralized cost cutting

12.
Integration and control risks: Rapidly expanding decentralized portfolios face heightened operational and financial reporting risks. Sdiptech's annual report published on September 1, 2026, notes that a decentralized model places stringent demands on financial reporting across diverse subsidiaries. Any lag or error in local monitoring can lead to poor governance and delayed cost-reduction responses during downturns. Therefore, operating leverage can worsen if central management lacks real-time visibility into underperforming units.
13.
Barriers to decentralized cost cutting: Centralizing support functions can sometimes create operational friction and offset planned savings. DNB Carnegie's report on September 24, 2026, warns that failed centralization attempts, like B&B Tools' historical strategy to centralize product assortment, can severely disrupt subsidiary sales. Expert insights from a former Dover executive on January 30, 2026, confirm that getting local leaders to share budgets is extremely difficult in practice. This friction limits the feasibility of executing large-scale, top-down cost restructurings.
14.
Other side of the story: While critics argue that decentralized models lack the synergies of integrated groups, the evidence shows that avoiding aggressive cost integration protects culture and long-term asset value. Carnegie Research notes on May 6, 2025, that traditional synergy pursuits often involve lay-offs and cultural damage that ultimately erode the value of acquired companies. Therefore, maintaining high operational autonomy allows serial acquirers to preserve local entrepreneurial drive and maintain superior customer relationships through downturns.

Section 3 – S:t Eriks and Nordic Concrete Construction Cycles

Section summary
  1. Severe construction downturns in Sweden compress S:t Eriks Group's profitability due to extensive factory under-absorption.
  2. Implemented cost reductions and inventory realignments are projected to stabilize operating margins ahead of a gradual market recovery.

Historical cycle margin volatility

1.
Volatile segment profitability: S:t Eriks Group experienced severe margin compression as the Swedish building market contracted. Volati reported that the segment's EBITA margin fell from 9.1% in 2022 to 4.1% in 2025 because residential construction activity fell. This profitability collapse highlights how exposed the business is to cycles, meaning that earnings per share will remain depressed until building volumes recover.
2.
Historic corporate evolution: S:t Eriks has a long history of structural changes through previous building downturns. Peab announced on March 31, 2009, that Nordform and S:t Eriks merged to create a leading concrete paving company following the 2008 financial crisis. This consolidation shows that industry consolidation occurs during recessions, which means S:t Eriks is structurally better prepared for the current downturn.
3.
Late-cycle demand exposure: S:t Eriks tends to lag other building-related platforms during economic turnarounds. Volati management explained during the Q3 2025 call that S:t Eriks enters downturns later than trading businesses like Salix Group. This delayed pattern suggests that profit recovery will be late-cycle, which means investors must wait for municipal projects to accelerate before expecting a major earnings expansion.
4.
Temporary delivery disruptions: Delayed infrastructure projects exacerbated the volume decline in late 2025. Volati noted in its 2025 year-end report that delayed deliveries within the municipal infrastructure segment severely hit Q4 results. This temporary bottleneck reduced full-year margins, but management expects these postponed deliveries to be recouped over the subsequent fiscal periods.

Cost structures and under-absorption

5.
Significant under-absorption penalty: Running concrete manufacturing plants below capacity creates severe margin headwinds. Volati reported that Q2 2026 EBITA for S:t Eriks fell to SEK 20 million from SEK 47 million in Q2 2025 because of a deliberate slowdown to release inventory. This under-absorption drag represents material profit headwinds, which means realigning plant volumes with demand is the primary prerequisite for restoring double-digit divisional margins.
6.
Substantial fixed overheads: The manufacturing cost structure of heavyside building materials carries high fixed operating leverage. Deutsche Bank Research estimated in its January 17, 2025 report that fixed costs average approximately 30% of the cost base for heavyside construction companies. This high fixed-cost proportion means that volume swings dictate segment margins, which will rapidly boost or dilute group earnings per share.
7.
Contrary evidence: S:t Eriks managed to defend its margins in the third quarter of 2026 despite persistent volume declines. Volati reported on October 23, 2026, that S:t Eriks maintained its EBITA margin at 5% in Q3 2026 because prior cost-adjustment measures began to bear fruit. This cost efficiency suggests that operating leverage is partially mitigable through proactive management adjustments even when end-markets remain soft.
8.
Improving cash generation: Deliberate production cutbacks successfully released significant working capital from inventory. Volati highlighted in its Q3 2026 report that S:t Eriks resumed normalized production rates after the spring destocking phase. This tactical shift enhanced seasonal cash flows during H1 2026, which means that balance sheet leverage can decline over the coming quarters.
9.
Gradual margin stabilization: Cost reduction initiatives implemented during early 2026 are successfully lowering the breakeven point. Volati reported that completed cost programs within S:t Eriks are showing a gradually increasing effect on profitability. This self-help focus suggests that operating margins will expand rapidly on any minor volume recovery, which will significantly drive earnings per share.

Competitive benchmarking and input risks

10.
Critical raw material dependency: Nordic concrete producers face high input supply concentration for essential cement materials. Volati monitors cement supply closely because Heidelberg Materials' Slite plant on Gotland supplies three-quarters of all cement used for concrete in Sweden. Any regulatory or operational disruption at Slite represents a catastrophic supply risk, which means that securing long-term quarry permits is critical for concrete industry profitability.
11.
Massive regional acquisitions: Major consolidation by international building material giants is reshaping the Nordic competitive landscape. Heidelberg Materials signed an agreement on October 7, 2026, to acquire NCC's Swedish and Norwegian aggregates and asphalt operations for SEK 5.5 billion. This transaction strengthens Heidelberg's market footprint, which means S:t Eriks must compete against unprecedented vertically integrated competitors in the Swedish infrastructure segment.
12.
Persistent price inflation: Concrete product manufacturers continue to struggle with elevated input and transportation expenses. DNB Carnegie reported on October 9, 2022, that building material cost inflation reached record levels because energy costs for cement production surged. These stubborn expenses suggest that pricing discipline remains paramount for S:t Eriks, as failure to pass on costs would compress margins further.
13.
Stable infrastructure demand: Robust public civil engineering investments help offset the severe slump in residential housebuilding. Volati noted in its Q3 2026 interim report that stable infrastructure demand partially countered weak water and wastewater volumes. This stable civil engineering market provides a resilient floor for S:t Eriks, which means that diversification away from residential housing will protect long-term divisional cash flows.
14.
Challenging residential markets: High interest rates continue to suppress municipal and consumer demand for concrete paving products. Volati reported in its Q1 2024 interim report that cold weather and the challenging construction market severely impacted paving sales. This weak demand suggests that volume declines will persist into late 2026, which means S:t Eriks must rely entirely on civil engineering orders.
15.
Sustainable material transition: Innovative concrete technologies offer potential long-term competitive advantages in public procurement. Volati announced that S:t Eriks partnered with Cemvision in 2025 to develop climate-smart cement products. This sustainable transition could unlock premium government contracts as green building standards tighten, which means that adopting low-carbon concrete protects future market share.
16.
Other side of the story: While critics argue S:t Eriks suffers from permanent structural decline, the segment's cost adjustments prepare it for massive profit drop-through. DNB Carnegie notes on October 20, 2026, that easy comparisons and implemented cost measures will support a meaningful earnings recovery. Once the Swedish housing market normalizes, S:t Eriks will reap substantial operating leverage benefits because capacity is aligned with demand.

  1. Ettiketto, Corroventa, and Communication platforms drive group profitability through highly effective project execution and programmatic add-on acquisitions.

  3. Weather-sensitive rental fleet utilization and cyclical telecom delays continue to present near-term operating leverage risks.

Broad platform research scope
