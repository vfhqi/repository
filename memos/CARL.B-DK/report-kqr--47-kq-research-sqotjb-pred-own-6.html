Transition to in-house production

1.
State-of-the-art facility: Carlsberg Kazakhstan officially opened its new $344 million beverage production plant in Boraldai on 1 October 2026 to produce PepsiCo drinks. The facility can produce up to 1 billion liters of beverages annually at full scale, which vastly expands regional production capacity and strengthens the local portfolio. This commercial launch is expected to drive high volume growth and support mid-term earnings per share.
2.
Insourcing margin benefits: Carlsberg expects to complete the insourcing of all regional PepsiCo volumes by the end of 2026, which replaces the zero-margin copackers used previously. Historically, using third-party toll fillers was a 40 basis points drag on H1 2026 gross margins, meaning that moving production in-house will eliminate this dilution. Consequently, eliminating toll-filling fees will immediately lift local operating profitability and expand Central Asian operating margins from 2027 onward.

High revenue expectations: Rothschild & Co Redburn's report on 30 September 2026 estimates that the Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan PepsiCo bottling franchise will generate 2.5 million hectoliters in volume during 2026. Section 5 – Franchise Swap Precedents and Coca-Cola Licensing Outlook

Section summary
  1. Historical bottling franchise swaps show that major brand owners consistently consolidate territories under dedicated, single-system partners to maximize scale.
  2. Coca-Cola Europacific Partners is the primary candidate to secure the expiring Danish and Finnish Coca-Cola licenses by 2029.
  3. Royal Unibrew's active discussions with Coca-Cola represent a key alternative scenario that could prevent industry overcapacity in the Nordics.

The research incorporates company filings, press announcements, and detailed broker evaluations. These historical precedents offer critical insights into the financial and operational mechanics of franchise reshuffling.

German in-housing precedent: PepsiCo decided to transition its German bottling operations in-house starting in 2024 to simplify its distribution network. Therefore, historical Nordic setups suggest that a unified beverage model creates significant route-to-market advantages.

Nordic market share downside

1.
Suboptimal market penetration: Carlsberg's assumption that it can easily replicate its Norwegian Pepsi success across Denmark and Finland faces severe downside risks if consumer preferences remain highly defensive. Bernstein Research warns that Pepsi remains a distant number two brand behind Coca-Cola in several key European markets, which means capturing market share will be highly challenging. If Nordic consumers refuse to switch from established cola brands, Carlsberg will struggle to generate the regional route density needed to expand its operating margins. Consequently, this slow market adoption would result in sub-scale soft drinks volume growth, depressing Western European earnings.
2.
Stiff retail resistance: Large supermarket chains in Northern Europe hold immense pricing power and can restrict shelf space if Carlsberg attempts to push combined beer and soft drink portfolios. Sourcing experts note that long-term contracts are uncommon in the beverage industry, meaning retail customers can easily shift their purchasing patterns to favor cheaper private labels or rival brands. If major Danish and Finnish retailers resist Carlsberg's dual-beverage distribution model, the company will face severe pricing pressure. This lack of retail cooperation would directly impede Carlsberg's local route density and delay target cost synergies.
3.
Plant underutilization pressure: Retooling legacy production lines to accommodate the massive Nordic switch in 2029 will trigger severe fixed-cost absorption challenges if volume targets are missed. Jefferies Research warns that the Pepsi license transition will likely bring DKK 300 million in transition costs, which represents a heavy near-term headwind. Because the incoming PepsiCo contracts represent a volume that is significantly smaller than the expiring Coca-Cola contracts, Carlsberg must prepare for near-term operational deleveraging. Unless the company secures alternative co-packing agreements, this manufacturing imbalance will dilute Western European operating margins.

Regulatory and sugar tax shocks

4.
Emerging health levies: Governments in Central Asia are actively pursuing fiscal policies to curb sugar consumption, which directly threatens Carlsberg's regional volume growth. Kyrgyzstan's Ministry of Health is currently reviewing a sugar-content-based excise tax system due to rising concerns over adolescent obesity. These proposed policy changes could double the price of sugar-sweetened beverages, which means local consumption would experience a sharp contraction. If these taxes are implemented, Carlsberg's newly built Almaty plant will suffer from lower capacity utilization rates, dragging down Central Asian earnings.
5.
Escalating European taxes: Stricter European health legislation represents a persistent threat that could derail the economics of Carlsberg's soft drinks portfolio. Morgan Stanley reports that several of Carlsberg's core markets, including Finland and Latvia, already impose high excise taxes on sugar-sweetened soft drinks. The broker notes that while manufacturers can reformulate recipes, more stringent taxes in new countries still pose a major long-term risk. Consequently, any unexpected tax increases will squeeze beverage operating profitability unless offset by premium pricing. Packaging inflation dilution: Persistent packaging cost inflation represents a critical risk that could erode the margin benefits of Carlsberg's in-house production. Global geopolitical conflicts have driven transport, energy, and crude oil prices higher, which directly inflates the cost of aluminum cans, glass bottles, and cardboard. Because these packing material increases cannot be easily passed on to price-sensitive consumers, Carlsberg's margins must absorb the impact. This means that persistent commodity inflation could dilute expected Almaty plant margins below management's targets.
