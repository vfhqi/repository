Section 5 – Contract Structures, Pass-Through Lags, and Margin Behaviors

Section summary
  1. Hexatronic utilizes indexed price adjustment clauses to pass through resin and material cost fluctuations to customers.
  2. A structural lag in pricing adjustments creates temporary gross margin volatility during acute raw material spikes.
  3. Consolidated adjusted gross margins recovered strongly to 39.8% in late 2026 as capacity utilization improved.

Indexation and pricing lag mechanics

1.
Lagged pricing pass-through: Hexatronic manages commodity price volatility by incorporating indexed price adjustment clauses into its long-term customer contracts. CEO John Rikard Fröberg explained at the Redeye Investor Forum on 21 May 2026 that duct contracts tie pricing directly to public plastic and resin indexes. Because there is a structural delay in waiting for these indexes to be officially published, some customer accounts experience a short-term pricing lag as confirmed in the Q2 2026 earnings call on 15 July 2026. This means that sudden spikes in polymer costs temporarily squeeze operating margins before pricing adjustments take effect.
2.
Divergent material lead times: Hexatronic experiences different procurement contract structures for its primary manufacturing inputs of resin and optical glass as noted in the Q1 2026 earnings call on 29 April 2026. Management explained in the same 29 April 2026 earnings call that resin operates on spot market dynamics where inventory levels determine immediate cost impacts. Conversely, the glass fiber market relies on longer-term supplier purchase agreements to secure volumes. This purchasing split implies that polymer inflation hits cost of goods sold faster than fiber inflation, which enables better gross margin planning for cabling.
3.
Peers margin comparison: Integrated industry competitors like Prysmian also utilize pricing pass-through mechanisms but benefit from greater upstream vertical integration. While peers experience similar multi-month delays in repricing contract backlogs, they successfully pass through rising raw material costs over time as noted in Hexatronic's Q3 2026 interim report of 22 October 2026. This coordinated industry pricing behavior reduces competitive friction during raw material spikes because all major duct suppliers lift prices in parallel as stated by management on 21 May 2026. This standard industry practice helps Hexatronic maintain its relative market positioning without suffering high customer churn.

Margin behavior through cost spikes

Profitability subsequently rebounded to 39.8% in Q3 2026 as demand inflected and resin costs stabilized at lower levels. This recovery demonstrates that operating leverage from higher factory volumes remains the primary driver of gross margin expansion.
5.
Historical margin volatility: Hexatronic experienced significant margin shifts during the prior fiber rollout cycles and the post-pandemic supply constraints.
