APM Mini-Memo: Azelis

Written by the Assistant Portfolio Manager, 1 October 2026. Ideas Bench rank 6 in the lists drawn from the Pool of Stocks Eligible for Middle Innings Portfolio Selection. Evidence: the Investment Analyst's Triaging memo of 29 September (re-rated 1 October), the ratings store as at 29 and 30 September, FactSet valuation of 30 September, the research digest and the list-maker's notes of 1 October.

The one-line view. Neutral, working hypothesis: a real cause-for-concern clearing in a solid but over-borrowed specialty chemicals distributor, where the market has already paid for the end of customer destocking and the rest of the return waits on leverage falling back inside three times.

What the company does

Azelis is the second-largest pure specialty chemicals and food ingredients distributor after IMCD: the outsourced sales and technical arm for over 2,800 manufacturers ("principals") and more than 65,000 customers in 64 countries, with over 70 application laboratories. 2025 revenue was €4,111m and adjusted operating profit (earnings before interest, tax and amortisation) €411m, a 10.0% margin. The mix is about 63% Life Sciences and 37% Industrial Chemicals, and market value is €2,927m at €12.09 on 30 September.

A plausible investment thesis

Recovery, not transformation. Three concerns hit at once: organic revenue fell for three years, leverage overshot to 3.4 times net debt to cash earnings against a 2.5 to 3.0 times target and stopped an acquisition engine that had done 64 deals in eight and a half years, and 2025 was the third year of downgrades. Now destocking has ended (second-quarter organic growth +3.9% against 1.8% expected; IMCD +7.5%), supplier price rises lifted gross margin 100 basis points to 24.7%, €20m of cost is out and guidance rose on 30 July. The memo of 29 September rated Thesis's Change Forces' Strength C, because the forces are mostly external and partly temporary.

The mechanism to earnings per share is modest. The Investment Analyst's path runs from €0.830 in 2025 to €0.90, €1.01 and €1.08 by 2028, 9.2% a year, below the framework's 12 to 20% band. Revenue growth of 3.7% and margin gain of 1.3% give only 5% a year of operating profit growth; the largest contributor is net financial expense falling from €124m toward €103m. Track the late-October third-quarter update (volume or price), year-end leverage (toward 3.1 times or above 3.4 times), the conversion margin against its 47.6% average and any top-twenty principal termination.

The Target Setup

The Investment Analyst's memo of 29 September says the Target Setup is 'Major CfC Clearing in MQ non-cyclical or better business on Q1 multiple, leading to reversion to normal', with 'CfC in quality cyclical at trough-on-trough, leading to reversion to normal' as a secondary reading of the same facts. It rated Setups' Fit/Crispness C: all five conditions pass, two only at the boundary. The multiple sat at the 25th to 29th percentile rather than clearly in the bottom quarter, and capitulation showed in the price but never in the sell-side's ratings. I agree: this is the setup seen from the middle, nine months and 77% off the low.

Case Riskiness and simplicity

The memo rated Case Riskiness C (Investment Analyst, 29 September; its wording re-formed by me on 1 October), from Riskiness at C and Simplicity at D. Simplicity is poor: seven company-specific causes for concern against a threshold of three, qualitative guidance, 64 countries and an earnings line the market reads on two bases. What holds it at C is that the case turns on three dated things, and a 4.5 times covenant against 3.4 times bounds the fundamental downside. Downside to a trough multiple is 18 to 36% with earnings unchanged.

Business quality

Solid, not strong. Foundations' Robustness is C (Investment Analyst, 29 September). The supplier franchise is exceptional: a thirty-year average tenure with the top ten principals, zero attrition among the top twenty in ten years, and at least 85% of top-ten revenue exclusive. Cash conversion averaged 93% from 2019 to 2025. Against that, return on invested capital is 7.3% against IMCD's 10.4%, leverage is the highest in the peer set, and organic growth has lagged IMCD in each of the last six quarters cited. Management is competent and defensive rather than value-creating.

Plausible returns and valuation

The memo's modal return, rebased to €12.09 on 30 September, is 20.2% a year over eighteen months and 14.5% over three years, an average of 17.3%, between the C and B anchors; TSR/Valuation is C. Re-rating supplies 17.0 points of the 31.8% eighteen-month total, at a blended 13.1 times with 45% odds of the balance sheet clearing by March 2028. On the consensus median earnings path it drops to 7.9% a year. The shares trade on 13.1 times 24-month-forward earnings of €0.9254, the 28th percentile of history (FactSet, 30 September), against a ten-year median of 15.1 times and IMCD at 14.9 times. The memo puts the price that clears both horizons at about €10.4. The script's Sell-side Momentum reading of 29 September is A and its Technical Momentum reading of 30 September is B.

Value chain and thematics: the pros and the cons

Pros. Producers send only 20% of volume through distributors, who serve 85% of customers; the market grows about 3.5% a year to 2030, the top ten hold under 15%, and downcycles push more accounts to distributors. Cons. Non-exclusive Chinese supply is pressing the semi-specialty end (Latin American gross profit fell 15% in 2025), the European small-customer tail is shrinking, and pricing rests partly on Middle East disruption, where Azelis has 6 to 7% direct exposure against IMCD's under 1%. The script's Thematic Fit/Momentum reading of 30 September is C, provisional.

The strongest counter-case

This is a false friend: a pricing-led re-rating without volume, in Morgan Stanley's phrase. Estimates were cut by a fifth, then raised on one price-assisted quarter; management expects the Middle East pricing and cheap inventory to ease in the second half; cash conversion was flattered by inventory liquidation and reverses as stock is rebuilt; and leverage rose to 3.4 times in June. The broker table splits into two earnings clusters, and on the lower one the stock is on about 16 times with the re-rating already spent. The price rose 39% in six months while estimates rose 6%. On this reading the call is to drop it, not park it, and Morgan Stanley's bear case is €6.80.
