Appendix

Outliers worth noting

The Americas region achieved an all-time high of SEK 105 million in Q2 2026, representing a 62% organic growth rate that places CellaVision in the top percentile of regional performance among Nordic medtech peers. This exceptional growth trajectory significantly diverges from both historical patterns and peer benchmarks, driven by severe clinical laboratory staff shortages in North America accelerating automated platform procurement.

The magnitude of this regional surge masks underlying structural vulnerabilities in other geographies and raises questions about sustainability once labor market dynamics normalize. [Cross-ref: Section 6] APAC regional sales collapsed by 59% reported to SEK 19 million in Q2 2026 against large one-off instrument shipments to establish local manufacturing in China during the prior year comparable period. This represents a bottom-decile performance relative to the company's historical quarterly variance distribution and reflects an extreme comparative base effect rather than fundamental demand deterioration.

The severity of this contraction, compounded by Chinese domestic healthcare cost-containment measures depressing capital equipment tenders, creates statistical noise that obscures underlying clinical demand trends and complicates forward modeling accuracy. [Cross-ref: Section 6]

The group gross margin inflected upward to 70.4% in Q2 2026, positioning CellaVision in the top 5% of Nordic medtech companies for profitability metrics. This 32.70% return on capital employed significantly outperforms the Nordic peer median, suggesting exceptional operational efficiency that remains unexplained by standard software licensing economics alone. The persistence of these outlier margins across multiple reporting periods, despite reagent mix dilution concerns, indicates either sustainable competitive advantages in pricing power or temporary favorable product mix effects that warrant deeper investigation. [Cross-ref: Section 4, Section 7] Over 60% market penetration has been achieved in high-volume laboratory networks for automated workflow transitions, while low-volume clinical laboratories present limited penetration of roughly 1%, offering structural long-term growth.

This extreme penetration bifurcation between large and small laboratory segments represents a statistical anomaly in capital equipment diffusion patterns, where adoption rates typically follow more gradual S-curves across customer segments. The 59-percentage-point gap between large and small lab penetration rates suggests either significant unaddressed barriers to adoption in smaller facilities or substantial untapped market opportunity that management has yet to unlock through appropriate commercial strategies.
