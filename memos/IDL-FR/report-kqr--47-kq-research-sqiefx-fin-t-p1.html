Contract Structures and Mix

1.
Open-book framework: ID Logistics utilizes a highly collaborative partnership model where customers are notified of all assets deployed and operational expenses incurred. This transparent arrangement operates as an openbook contract structure, although actual invoicing is rarely based on a simple cost-plus-margin mechanism. The shared visibility ensures that both parties agree on the underlying cost base, which ultimately helps protect the operator's mid-single-digit EBIT margins during periods of operational volatility.
2.
Dedicated contract terms: ID Logistics formalizes agreements that link warehouse pricing to specific customer operating conditions including order mix, seasonality, and volume. These dedicated agreements, ranging from three to ten years, incorporate billing structures based on the exact quantity of parcels or pallets processed. Aligning pricing terms with customer assumptions guarantees that revenues scale with actual warehouse activity while minimizing the risk of unrecovered capital investments.
3.
Peer portfolio comparison: GXO Logistics significantly enhanced its contractual stability and margin insulation through the strategic acquisition of British logistics operator Wincanton in 2024. Wincanton brought a stable contract portfolio characterized by an eighty percent open-book and twenty percent closed-book contract mix across its warehousing and transportation operations. This high open-book exposure protects GXO because all operational costs are passed through to the client with an agreed mark-up.

Stable margin profile: GXO Logistics targets defensive growth because open-book contracts ensure that margins remain broadly fixed regardless of macroeconomic headwinds.
