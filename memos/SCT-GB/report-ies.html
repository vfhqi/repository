Softcat (SCT-GB): what the FY2026 results do to the case

These results weaken the existing case materially, and in a way the case did not anticipate: the company we rated as an unlevered, UK-only organic compounder has, between the memo and these results, agreed a $1.05bn debt-and-equity-funded US acquisition, while the share price has fallen back inside the range the memo said it had left.

What changed

The year to 31 July 2026, reported on 14 October 2026, beat on every profit line. Gross profit grew 24.7% to £616.3m against Deutsche Bank's £585.4m (18.4% growth). Underlying operating profit grew 19.4% to £215.1m against £211.8m. Underlying basic earnings per share were 83.2p against 69.5p a year earlier. The case had FY2026 consensus operating profit at £205-208m, so the beat completes the sandbag-and-raise pattern the memo graded A: mid-teens guidance, 19.4% delivered. The ordinary dividend rose 7.5% to 31.5p and the £45m buyback completed.

Underneath, every quality-of-growth metric the case watches moved the wrong way:

  • Underlying operating profit fell to 35% of gross profit from 36%. Management attributes a low-teens-millions headwind to the ten-year free share award and performance kickers.
  • Gross profit fell to 13.1% of gross invoiced income from 13.7%; in the second half alone it was 12.8%.
  • Non-underlying costs were £18.7m (£15.6m for IT and sales systems, £3.1m Oakland), so statutory operating profit grew only 13.6% to £196.4m. Cash conversion was 93.6% against 95.6%.
  • A single direct customer accounted for £502.2m, 25.4% of revenue, up from 22% the prior year, at a gross margin the company calls considerably lower.

Standalone FY2027 guidance is high-single-digit underlying operating profit growth and mid-single-digit gross profit growth, weighted to the first half, which management attributes to customers pulling planned 2027 orders into FY2026 to beat memory shortages. Jefferies calls it one of the slowest growth guides in ten years; Deutsche Bank reads it as simple conservatism and pencils in 8% gross profit growth and 3% EBIT growth before the acquisition.

The acquisition, signed in September 2026 and therefore after the memo: General Datatech, $1.05bn enterprise value for $80m of EBITDA, about 15x 2026e EBITDA, funded by £100m of cash, a £354m equity placing and £550m of new debt facilities. Net debt goes to about 1.3x EBITDA at completion, targeted below 1.0x by July 2028, with a long-term range of 0.5x to 1.0x; returns beyond the progressive ordinary dividend are now subject to that range. Completion needs Hart-Scott-Rodino and CFIUS clearance. GDT's 30% gross profit growth in 2026 was heavily neocloud-driven and over 85% of its revenue is networking and hybrid data centre. GDT's management stays in place and it brings a 230-person delivery platform in Bengaluru.

Sell-side: Jefferies kept Hold and moved its target to 1,750p from 1,700p, arguing that the guided double-digit EPS accretion will probably be offset by multiple compression, and that Computacenter spent $500m on US deals now generating $350m of EBIT; it raised FY2027 and FY2028 organic estimates by 3% and 14% to fold GDT in. Deutsche Bank kept Hold at 2,140p and expects 8-10% EPS accretion in FY2028 and FY2029. Morgan Stanley discontinued coverage in April 2026. The review also carries Jefferies' estimate that Microsoft is about 50% of group gross profit, and that Microsoft has removed the 12% volume discount on Enterprise Agreements.

Price: the shares closed at 1,719p on 14 October, down 10.7% on the day from 1,924p, about 16% below the 2,052p at which the memo found the entry point closed, and below both live broker targets. The dashboard's pinned letters stand at B for technical momentum and A for sell-side momentum as of 14 October, before post-results revisions have landed; its current P/E is 18.1x, at the 13th percentile of the stock's own ten-year history.

The challenge

Three readings, none of which the case holds.

The compounder has been sold, and FY2026 was the top of a cycle rather than a step on a staircase. The memo rated Softcat as a narrow-perimeter, zero-debt UK business whose change forces were internal and incremental: an £8m consultancy, a systems rebuild, a wallet-share ambition. A board that believed that runway sufficient would not pay about 15x EBITDA, in new equity and new debt, for a US networking reseller whose growth came from neoclouds. Read this way the deal is management's own verdict that the standalone model cannot carry the premium, and the FY2027 guide is the first honest number: mid-single-digit gross profit growth after a year in which invoiced income grew faster than gross profit, take-rate fell 60 basis points, conversion fell a point, and a quarter of revenue came from one low-margin account. The memo's false-friend finding said the first-half beat was pull-forward dressed as compounding; this reading says the whole year was, and that the strategic response confirms it. For it to be right: standalone gross profit growth excluding the large customer in the low single digits through the first half of FY2027; GDT's gross profit growth falling below the 10% a year the review sets as the test of whether the neocloud boom was structural; and underlying operating profit continuing toward the 33% of gross profit the review names as its sustained-dilution line. The case cannot absorb this reading because its primary setup, strong-corporate-change-driven earnings upgrade, treats an acquisition step-up as a positive delta, and its riskiness grade rests on a balance sheet that will not exist at completion.

The change-force case just got bigger, the price just got cheaper, and the sell-side is anchored on the wrong thing. Gross profit beat the best-informed broker by about £31m on a year management guided upward three times, extending a run of twenty consecutive years of double-digit gross profit growth. Guidance has been raised inside the year in each of the last four years, and the FY2027 guide is set against a comparative management itself calls exceptional; Deutsche Bank's own read is conservatism. GDT puts Softcat into the geography where US chief information officers have raised calendar 2026 budget growth to 4.3% against 3.1% in Europe, and where Computacenter now earns over 60% of its adjusted operating profit; the retained GDT management, the Bengaluru platform and the new multinational wins the chief executive cites are the early evidence the memo would want for a strong-corporate-change setup. And the entry the memo called closed has reopened: 1,719p is below Jefferies' 1,750p and far below Deutsche Bank's 2,140p, at a P/E in the bottom fifth of its own ten-year range. For this to be right: regulatory clearance and completion on the announced terms; GDT gross profit growth staying above 10% through 2027 and 2028; standalone FY2027 beating the high-single-digit guide the way FY2025 and FY2026 beat theirs; net debt back under 1.0x EBITDA by July 2028. The case does not hold this reading either. It put valuation out of scope at Triaging, held the robust base at C partly on a closed entry, and never considered a setup in which the corporate change is a billion-dollar deal arriving with a 16% lower price. Where it is weakest on the evidence: both covering brokers looked at the same facts and stayed at Hold, and nothing in the results answers Jefferies' price comparison with Computacenter.

One customer is the story, and neither side is pricing it. £502.2m from one direct customer is 25.4% of revenue, up from 22%, at a considerably lower gross margin. The memo carried "no client exceeds 2.00% of gross profit" and a £42.4m single-customer advance payment inflating first-half cash conversion; the second was the clue, the first is a different denominator, and revenue concentration at this scale was simply not in the case. If this customer is the memory-shortage pull-forward, then most of the FY2027 normalisation is one account and the other three-quarters of the business is plausibly growing as the case assumed, which rescues the compounder reading. If instead it is a framework-style mega-contract won on price, it is the competition headwind the case graded C arriving at scale, and the review's own red line is 30% of revenue or termination. For either version to be shown right we would need the customer's gross profit contribution and the growth of the business excluding it, and neither is a disclosure the company has made or committed to make. The case's narrow-perimeter A and demand-side B both assumed a customer base with no single dependency; this reading presses on both, whichever way it resolves.

We considered and set aside a fourth reading, that Microsoft's incentive changes are the hidden driver of the take-rate fall. The company attributes the 13.1% to lower-margin hardware mix, and the 50%-of-gross-profit figure is a broker estimate on a different denominator from the memo's roughly 23% of invoiced income. It is a live question for the supplier-side attribute, not a reading of these results.

What it does to the case

Under more pressure:

  • Group 2 Case simplicity [C], and within it E7.RA2 Narrow perimeter [A]. The A rested on trading being almost entirely UK and Ireland, five towers in one business, drop-ship logistics. A $1.05bn US acquisition run as a standalone entity under its own management, with an Indian delivery centre, is a second perimeter. It is probable that E7.RA2 would not hold at A, and E7.RA1 Focused bet [C] and E7.RA4 Handful of change-from-current items [C] move the same way.
  • Group 4 Case riskiness [C]. The memo's stated reason for C rather than D was that zero debt and £206.0m of net cash made permanent impairment improbable, so this was a de-rating risk only. Net debt of 1.3x EBITDA at completion removes that reason. Computacenter's memo grades Group 4 at D with a US business already built. Likely to move.
  • E1.RA2 Robust base [C]. The unlevered, well-invested base the memo graded B inside this attribute is now a levered one. Partly offset below.
  • E7.RA3 Zero headwinds [D] and E8.RA8 Fit with false friend setups [D]: the same grades, but the evidence has moved from suspected to reported. Take-rate did not stabilise, which was half of the case's own condition for turning bullish; the other half, organic growth excluding large deals above 10%, is not disclosed. The case's bearish trigger was first-half FY2027 gross profit growth below 10%; the company has now guided to mid-single digits for the year. Note the tension this creates: the case cannot lean on E7.RA5 Conservative investor relations [A] to discount the guide and at the same time treat the guide as confirmation of the false friend. One of those two attributes is doing less work than the memo thought.
  • E5.RA1 Suitability for bear market [D] and E11.RA1 Demand side [B]: leverage plus a 25.4% customer at low margin is a worse downturn profile than the memo described.
  • E11.RA5 Supportive supplier side [B]: if Microsoft is about half of gross profit rather than roughly 23% of invoiced income, and Microsoft has removed the 12% Enterprise Agreement discount, the "no supplier above roughly 23%" leg of the B is weaker than stated. The denominators differ, so the pressure is real but unquantified.

Under less pressure:

  • E1.RA2 Core Question 4, the entry point, graded D. At 1,719p the price is below both live broker targets, having been 24% above consensus at 2,052p. The memo's own re-look condition if parked, the price back inside the target range, has been met. This is the one unambiguous improvement.
  • E8.RA3 Strong corporate change driven earnings upgrade [B]: the corporate change is now larger by an order of magnitude and the sell-side has started modelling it, Jefferies up 14% on FY2028. Whether that is a better fit or a different case is exactly the open question.
  • The memo's second condition for progression, striking E3 against a live multiple, now has a number: 18.1x at the 13th percentile. It was ungradeable in August.

The same:

  • E8.RA1 Mis-modelled high-quality upgrader [B]: a £31m gross profit beat is another miss by the models, as the memo predicted.
  • E7.RA5 Conservative investor relations [A]: the pattern completed on FY2026; whether it holds on FY2027 is the question above.
  • MR1 Technical momentum and MR2 Sell-side earnings momentum are pinned elsewhere; both pinned letters stand as of 14 October, before revisions land.
  • MR3 Thematic fit [C]: unchanged on balance, though the US exposure plausibly helps the AI-thematic side and the leverage hurts the bear-market side.

Net: Pillar II (MR5 and C.2, both C) carries two Building Blocks that would probably move. Pillar I (MR4 and C.1, both B) has denser change forces on a weaker base with a better entry, and we see it as the same.

What would settle it

The second Earnings Review, four working days on, will carry the post-results broker estimates and targets, the reset consensus, and the revision figures the dashboard's sell-side letter is pinned to. It will show whether Deutsche Bank's 2,140p survives the print and whether consensus FY2027 settles nearer Deutsche Bank's 3% standalone EBIT growth or Jefferies' GDT-inclusive upgrade. That answers the price question and the sell-side momentum letter. It will not carry the customer's identity or gross profit contribution, organic growth excluding large deals, gross profit per customer or per employee (the datapoint the memo named as most useful, which the review does not carry for FY2026), a quantification of Microsoft's incentive changes, or services margins. None of those is a committed disclosure.

The readings separate at these points:

  • First-half FY2027 results, expected March 2027: gross profit growth against the case's 10% line; take-rate against 13.1%; and, if disclosed, growth excluding the largest customer. First-half weighting means a soft first half cannot be excused by phasing.
  • GDT completion, which needs Hart-Scott-Rodino and CFIUS clearance with no date given. Until then the leverage, the perimeter break and the accretion are all contingent, and the £354m placing is the only part of the funding that cannot be undone.
  • GDT's calendar 2027 gross profit growth against the review's 10% threshold, which tests the neocloud question. Softcat has not said it will report GDT separately.
  • Underlying operating profit as a share of gross profit: 35% now, 33% is the review's sustained-dilution line. Two halves would tell.
  • Net debt below 1.0x EBITDA by July 2028, management's own commitment.

The question that nothing scheduled will answer is the customer. Until the company says who it is and what gross profit it carries, the first and third readings cannot be told apart from the results alone, and that is a reason to treat the FY2027 guide as unresolved rather than as either conservatism or confession.
