Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The effective tax rate for Kainos directly determines the conversion of record operating profits into shareholder earnings during a period of rapid digital transformation growth.
1.
Key Question: "What is the effective tax rate outlook for Kainos Group plc (KNOS-GB) on adjusted profit for FY2027 to FY2030, given its reported effective rate of 24% in H1 FY2027 against 27% a year earlier (attributed by the company to a higher deferred tax credit on share options arising from the share-price increase), management's expectation of 26% for FY2027, the UK corporation tax rate of 25%, higher US tax rates on its North American Workday business, non-deductible Davis Pier acquisition costs in Canada, R&D expenditure credits (£6.0m in FY2026) and any Patent Box eligibility; and how does the deferred tax on share options behave when the share price falls between balance-sheet dates, as it has since 30 September 2026, using precedents from UKlisted peers such as Bytes Technology, Softcat and Computacenter?"

Impact on Shareholder Returns

2.
Kainos Group plc reported record contracted backlog of £853 mm in H1 FY2027, indicating that the scale of future profits will be significantly larger than historical levels. Because the effective tax rate determines how much of this record profit reaches the bottom line, even a 1% shift in the tax rate has a material impact on adjusted earnings per share (EPS). Management guidance of 26% for FY2027 suggests a degree of stability, but the 24% reported in H1 shows how accounting adjustments can temporarily mask the underlying tax burden. Investors must distinguish between these non-cash accounting credits and the cash tax reality of the 25% UK rate and higher North American levies.
3.
The company's expansion into North America, specifically through the acquisition of Davis Pier, has shifted the geographic mix of earnings toward higher-tax jurisdictions. This transition is critical because it creates a permanent floor for the effective tax rate that sits above the UK's statutory 25%. While the Digital Services division grew by 57% in H1 FY2027, the associated tax leakage from international work must be modeled correctly to avoid overestimating the profit-to-cash conversion. Understanding the durability of R&D credits, which reached £6.03 mm in FY2026, is essential for judging the sustainability of current margin levels.

Risks from Share Price Volatility

4.
Kainos’ share price has historically been a driver of tax-related earnings volatility due to the treatment of sharebased compensation (SBC). When the share price increases, the company recognizes a deferred tax credit, effectively reducing the reported tax rate as seen in the H1 FY2027 result of 24%. However, the fall in share price since September 30, 2026, presents a downside risk where these credits must be reversed, potentially leading to an earnings "miss" at the EPS level even if operating profit remains strong. This accounting nuance is a recurring theme for UK-listed technology peers like Softcat and Bytes, who also manage significant SBC programs.
5.
The question of Patent Box eligibility remains a potential upside that could provide a meaningful long-term hedge against rising global corporate tax rates. While Kainos invests heavily in product development, with R&D spend rising 33% to £11.4 mm in H1 FY2027, it has primarily relied on RDEC rather than the Patent Box regime for tax relief. If the company successfully migrates more of its software IP into a Patent Box-eligible structure, the effective tax rate for the Workday Products division could drop significantly. This would provide a tailwind for EPS growth through FY2030 as Workday Products targets £200 mm in annual recurring revenue.
6.
Other side of the story: While geographic expansion and share price volatility present risks, the core UK business remains highly tax-efficient due to the stable 25% statutory rate and consistent R&D credits. If Kainos achieves its margin improvement targets by replacing contractors with full-time staff, the absolute value of R&D credits could increase, potentially neutralizing the impact of higher US tax rates. Investors might overstate the impact of share price swings on long-term value, as these are non-cash accounting movements that do not affect the underlying cash-generating capability of the three divisions.

RDEC as a Core Tax Offset

In H1 FY2027, the company reported another £4.09 mm in R&D expenditure credits, suggesting that the full-year FY2027 benefit will likely exceed the FY2026 level. The scale of this relief is tied to the company's commitment to innovation, with R&D spend rising 33% year-on-year.
2.

Because the company expenses all R&D costs in the period they are incurred, the tax credit provides an immediate P&L benefit that buffers adjusted profit. This consistent stream of tax relief is the primary reason Kainos can maintain a 26% effective rate despite high-tax US profits.

Section 4 – Share Price Impact on Deferred Tax

Section summary
  1. The decline in Kainos’ share price since September 2026 will likely trigger a reversal of H1 tax credits, creating a headwind for full-year FY2027 adjusted EPS.

Mechanics of Share-Based Compensation Tax

Kainos Group plc reported that its H1 FY2027 tax rate of 24% was flattered by a deferred tax credit arising from a share price increase. In UK accounting, the tax deduction available upon the exercise of share options is based on the market price at the time of exercise. Companies recognize a deferred tax asset (DTA) for the

1.
future tax deduction they expect to receive, and the value of this asset is re-measured at each balance sheet date based on the current share price. When the price rises, the expected deduction increases, creating a non-cash credit that lowers the effective tax rate.
2.
Conversely, when the share price falls, as it has for Kainos since September 30, 2026, the deferred tax asset must be written down. This write-down is recognized as a tax expense in the income statement, which increases the effective tax rate for the period. Morgan Stanley research explains that in a sustained market correction, the tax benefits that materially lowered rates during a bull market will "dissipate and potentially reverse," increasing corporate tax rates. This suggests that the 2% to 3% tax benefit Kainos enjoyed in H1 FY2027 is at risk of being completely erased in the H2 FY2027 results.

Precedents from UK Tech Peers

3.
Bytes Technology Group plc also manages significant share-based compensation and has faced similar earnings volatility from non-cash accounting items. Jefferies analysts note that while Bytes has "reinvigorated growth," its valuation is often impacted by the "right-shifting" of recoveries and accounting adjustments. For a company like Kainos, which just returned £56.2 mm to shareholders through buybacks, the cash tax rate is often a better measure of health than the reported rate, as the share price-driven tax adjustments are purely accounting-driven.
4.
Softcat PLC provides a further example where SBC is treated as a theoretical wage cost in broker models to normalize earnings. Jefferies analysts base their price targets on peer group free cash flow (FCF) yield, explicitly treating SBC as a cost to bypass the noise of deferred tax adjustments. This precedent suggests that sophisticated investors in Kainos should look past the 24% H1 rate and focus on the 26% management guidance, which effectively "averages out" the expected share price volatility over the full fiscal year.
5.
Contrary evidence: Share price movements only affect the deferred tax asset on unexercised options, not the cash tax paid on options that have already been exercised. If a large tranche of options was exercised during the H1 price peak, the tax benefit would be "locked in" as a cash saving, even if the share price subsequently fell. However, Kainos’ H1 presentation specifically highlights a "deferred tax credit," which implies the benefit is primarily unrealized and thus subject to future reversal.

Sensitivity to Future Share Performance

6.
Kainos’ share price reaction to its H1 results, where the stock fell more than 12% on the day, directly impacts the FY2027 tax outlook. Financial Times reports suggest that investors were disappointed by the unchanged guidance despite strong H1 results. This sell-off will likely force a revaluation of the DTA at the next reporting period. If the share price remains at depressed levels through March 31, 2027, the company will face a "tax headwind" in its full-year results that could offset the operating profit outperformance reported in August.
7.
The behavior of deferred tax assets is a ubiquitous issue for technology firms with high SBC, as noted by Jefferies in its analysis of Rolls-Royce. While the specific industry differs, the logic of deferred tax assets "disappearing" when they are no longer certain to be used or when their value drops is a standard accounting principle. For Kainos, this means the FY2027 to FY2030 tax outlook is inherently "noisy," and the company will likely continue to reconcile its adjusted profit using a more stable tax rate to help investors see through these share price-induced swings.
8.
Other side of the story: The impact of share price falls on the tax rate might be mitigated by the company’s share buyback program. By reducing the number of shares in issue, Kainos can potentially support the share price and limit the downside to its deferred tax assets. Furthermore, if the company issues fewer new options in response to a lower share price, the total pool of deferred tax assets would shrink, reducing the magnitude of future volatility in the reported effective tax rate.

However, if the company is unexpectedly successful in claiming Patent Box status for its Workday Products, the effective rate could structurally shift downward to 22% or 23% by FY2030. Such a shift would lead to a persistent "EPS multiplier" effect, where every 10% of revenue
