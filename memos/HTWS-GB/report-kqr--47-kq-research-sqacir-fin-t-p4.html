Section 2 – Effective Tax Rate and Structured Tax Drivers 14 findings

Section summary
  1. Structural tax inefficiencies will keep Helios Towers' effective tax rate elevated above statutory levels due to non-deductible parent costs and withholding taxes.
  2. Operating entities face local minimum tax burdens and upstreaming friction that prevent cash tax expense from aligning with reported accounting losses.

Helios Towers ETR Drivers

1.
High accounting tax: Helios Towers experienced a sharp increase in its tax rate as statutory earnings grew. The company reported a total tax expense of USD 96.6 million in FY2025 compared to USD 17.2 million in FY2024. This drove the effective tax rate to 71.0% in FY2025 on FactSet data compared to 38.9% in FY2024. Because cash tax paid was only USD 45.5 million, unrealized deferred tax adjustments heavily distorted the reported earnings per share
2.
Minimum tax drag: Operating subsidiaries face localized tax obligations that apply regardless of profitability. Helios Towers' operating entity in the DRC made losses for tax purposes in FY2025. However, minimum income taxes were levied as stipulated by local DRC law. These minimum tax rules did not apply to other loss-making holding companies, meaning local operational tax expenses continued to depress earnings
3.
Non-deductible parent costs: Helios Towers suffers from structural tax friction due to its international corporate setup. A high tax charge was reported because holding company losses in Mauritius and the United Kingdom cannot be offset against profitable operating subsidiaries. These holding company losses are not able to be group relieved under local tax jurisdictions. Unless the corporate structure is simplified, non-deductible interest expenses will continue to inflate the consolidated effective tax rate
4.
Unwinding tax assets: Non-cash deferred tax movements create significant volatility in the group's reported effective tax rate. The high tax charge in FY2025 was exacerbated because deferred tax assets in the DRC unwound during the year. This unwinding stood in contrast to FY2024, when a large deferred tax credit reduced the overall tax expense. Such non-cash tax adjustments make year-on-year EPS projections highly unpredictable
