Section 2 – Hedging Strategy and 2027 Gas Cap Dynamics

Section summary
  1. Ithaca Energy's hedging structures provide robust floor protection but severely limit upside from gas price spikes.
  2. Competitors like Harbour Energy and Serica Energy maintain higher collar ceilings that allow better capture of commodity rallies.
  3. Broker analysis confirms that Ithaca's tight collar caps drag on earnings per share during geopolitical supply disruptions.

H2 2026 Hedge Book Costs

1.
Substantial drag: Ithaca Energy experienced a heavy financial penalty from its defensive commodity hedges during the recent pricing upswing. According to its half-year report published on 19 August 2026, the producer realised average prices of 83 dollars per boe before hedging but only 69 dollars after accounting for hedge settlements. This hedging impact resulted in a direct cash outflow of 203.8 million dollars on oil contracts and 32 million dollars on gas derivative contracts. Consequently, these structures reduced the company's hedged revenue and cash flows during strong market conditions.
2.
Extensive downside: Ithaca Energy maintained a highly hedged position across its portfolio to protect cash flows at the expense of market upside. Management stated in its 19 August 2026 press release that the group expanded its hedges to 58.8 million boe covering the period through 2028. The company designed this proactive program to protect capital spending and dividend cover against price collapses. However, this strategy means its earnings per share remain insulated from physical commodity price movements.
3.
Secure floors: Ithaca Energy locked in solid minimum pricing protections that establish a resilient baseline cash flow. Its half-year report of 19 August 2026 detailed H2 2026 hedges of 10.2 million boe of oil at a 59 dollars per barrel floor and 6.9 million boe of gas at an 82 pence per therm floor. These floor protections adjust in 2027 to 60 dollars per barrel for oil and 78 pence per therm for gas. This defensive positioning underpins essential capital programs but limits near-term profit growth.
4.
Rigid ceilings: Ithaca Energy limited its near-term financial upside due to narrow trading bands on its primary collar instruments. DNB Carnegie reported on 16 September 2026 that Ithaca's average 2026 collar floors of 58 to 61 dollars per barrel and ceilings of 68 to 70 dollars restricted oil gains. For gas, standard collar floors of 80 to 81 pence per therm were paired with ceilings of 100 to 102 pence. These restrictive collars act as a key constraint on share price performance when commodity prices spike.

Gas Cap and Collar Ceiling Dynamics

5.
Capped upside: Ithaca Energy faces significant gas ceiling limitations into 2027 that prevent it from fully capturing high spot prices. Jefferies reported on 18 March 2026 that Ithaca's rapid hedging build capped over 42% of its 2027 gas volumes under a 100 pence per therm collar ceiling. These collar structures were implemented as a base cash flow anchor for capital distributions. However, this hedging ceiling mutes potential earnings per share expansion when regional gas markets experience supply shocks.
6.
Limited participation: Ithaca Energy cannot exploit elevated European gas curves because forward prices sit far above its hedge caps. DNB Carnegie highlighted on 16 September 2026 that forward curves were already trading above Ithaca's 100 to 102 pence per therm collar ceilings. Although some zero-cost collars offer wider ceilings up to 124 pence, standard collars cover the largest share of the hedge book. This high hedge ratio caps potential cash generation during periods of extreme gas undersupply.
7.
Vulnerable exposure: Ithaca Energy faced substantial revenue constraints during the 2026 Strait of Hormuz supply disruptions. Redburn reported on 24 March 2026 that the closure of the Strait halted 20% of global liquefied natural gas supplies, triggering a 48% spike in TTF gas futures. Redburn noted on 18 March 2026 that these disruptions pushed European gas prices to peak levels of 19 dollars per MMBtu. This rapid commodity surge outpaced Ithaca's rigid collar ceilings and limited its immediate cash windfalls.
8.
Divergent pricing: Ithaca Energy is expected to face a different commodity environment as global supply dynamics shift. Morgan Stanley observed on 30 October 2026 that European gas prices soared to their highest levels since 2022 because of Middle East tensions. However, the broker forecasts that global gas markets will return to oversupply from 2028 onwards as new liquefied natural gas projects in the United States and Qatar commence operations. This supply wave will depress future regional gas pricing just as Ithaca's restrictive hedges roll off.

Peer Hedging Strategy Comparisons

9.

Morgan Stanley noted on 3 September 2026 that while Ithaca is highly gas-exposed, it offers less direct commodity upside than peers due to its extensive hedging program. For example, Energean operates under long-duration gas contracts with fixed or lower prices in Mediterranean markets rather than volatile spot pricing. This contractual difference makes Energean a natural defensive hedge against price weakness but eliminates spot market windfalls.

This ceiling translates to approximately 230 pence per therm, which is more than double Ithaca's 100 pence limit. Consequently, Harbour enjoys superior financial leverage to gas price shocks.

Defensive positioning: Serica Energy utilized a combination of swaps and zero-cost collars to protect its cash flows while maintaining selective exposure. Serica Energy reported in its 2025 annual accounts that it entered 2026 with oil collars featuring ceilings up to 85 dollars per barrel and gas collar ceilings up to 139 pence per therm. For 2027, Serica's gas collar ceilings adjust to 121 pence per therm, which provides slightly better upside than Ithaca's tight caps. EnQuest reported in its half-year results on 3 September 2026 that it hedged 3.6 million barrels of 2027 oil production at an average swap price of 64.4 dollars per barrel. Section 3 – UK Fiscal Regime, Cash Tax Gaps, and Post-2030 Position

Section summary
  1. Ithaca Energy's massive legacy loss pools shield it from the underlying forty percent tax rate, keeping cash tax focused only on the windfall levy.
  2. The timing of tax payments creates near-term cash flow distortions, with elevated earnings from commodity spikes trailing into deferred tax liabilities.
  3. Expiration of the Energy Profits Levy in March 2030 will trigger a substantial boost to earnings per share as marginal rates drop.

Tax Divergence and Loss Pools

1.
Tax divergence: Ithaca Energy exhibits a stark contrast between its headline accounting tax rate and its actual cash tax outlays. DNB Carnegie reported on 16 September 2026 that the company faces a marginal tax rate of 78 percent under the current UK upstream fiscal framework. However, the group is sheltered from the base ringfence corporation tax and supplementary charge due to its substantial historical tax losses. This separation keeps its immediate cash tax payments limited to the 38 percent Energy Profits Levy.
2.
Loss shelter: Ithaca Energy maintains a massive pool of carried-forward tax allowances that shields its core operations from the standard 40 percent corporate tax. DNB Carnegie confirmed on 16 September 2026 that Ithaca carried approximately 5.1 billion dollars of ring-fence corporation tax losses and 4.2 billion dollars of supplementary charge losses at the end of 2025. The company also holds a brought forward investment allowance of 2.3 billion dollars to offset future liabilities. These massive tax shields will effectively protect its underlying earnings through the end of the decade.
3.
Floor insulation: Ithaca Energy possesses a secondary layer of tax protection if oil and gas prices experience a severe downturn. DNB Carnegie reported on 16 September 2026 that the Energy Profits Levy falls away under the Energy Security Investment Mechanism if prices stay below 76.12 dollars per barrel and 59 pence per therm. This mechanism would immediately reduce Ithaca's marginal tax rate from 78 percent to 40 percent. Therefore, fiscal relief is structured to arrive precisely when commodity prices collapse and cash flows weaken.
4.
Payment timing: Ithaca Energy experiences cash flow distortions because UK upstream tax payments lag its actual physical earnings. DNB Carnegie noted on 16 September 2026 that the ring-fence tax and Energy Profits Levy are paid in three equal installments across July, October, and the following January. Because of this structure, the cash-flow impact of elevated commodity prices during 2026 will not fully hit the balance sheet until late 2027. This lag means reported free cash flow looks temporarily stronger during price spikes.

Payment Timing and Successor Levy

5.
Future liabilities: Ithaca Energy expects its cash tax burden to rise as legacy loss pools are gradually drawn down. DNB Carnegie projected on 16 September 2026 that higher oil and gas prices in 2026 will drive elevated tax payments into 2027. Although the company remains shielded from the standard 40 percent tax, the 38 percent levy cannot be offset by these historical losses. Consequently, these payment cycles will create a short-term cash drag before the levy expires.
6.
Fiscal transition: Ithaca Energy will transition to a more predictable tax regime when the current Energy Profits Levy expires. Mark Wilson of Jefferies reported on 28 October 2026 that the UK government is legislating for a successor Oil and Gas Revenue Levy in the Finance Bill. This successor mechanism is scheduled to take effect on 1 April 2030 or earlier if the price floor is triggered. This change represents a significant long-term policy reset for North Sea operators.
7.
Price thresholds: The proposed post-2030 levy introduces a revenue-based windfall tax that applies only during unusually high pricing environments. DNB Carnegie explained on 16 September 2026 that the new mechanism applies a 35 percent tax rate on revenues above 90 dollars per barrel for oil and 90 pence per therm for gas. Because this tax is calculated on revenue rather than net profit, it does not adjust for escalating development or operating costs. This regressive nature could penalise high-cost development projects if commodity prices spike.
8.
Earnings acceleration: Ithaca Energy stands to generate substantial earnings per share growth once the windfall levy officially terminates. DNB Carnegie forecast on 16 September 2026 that the expiration of the levy will reduce Ithaca's marginal tax rate from 78 percent back to 40 percent. Since Ithaca's massive loss pools are expected to shelter it from the remaining 40 percent rate, its cash tax burden will fall precipitously. This post-2030 shift will directly boost its future dividend coverage and reinvestment capacity.

Section summary

1.
Strategic expansion: Ithaca Energy announced its inaugural international acquisition by purchasing Suncor Energy's offshore East Coast Canada portfolio for 860 million dollars in upfront cash. This acquisition provides the group with a key entry platform to diversify its production beyond the UK North Sea.

Management estimates that these assets will add an average of 30 thousand barrels of oil equivalent per day between 2027 and 2031. This additional output upgrades the group's medium-term production outlook to between 140 and 150 thousand barrels per day.
3.
Financial returns: Suncor Energy structured the transaction with an upfront payment and a Brent-linked contingent consideration of up to 250 million dollars. This contingent mechanism shares cash flows if Brent oil prices exceed specified annual thresholds over a 27-month period. Ithaca Energy expects the transaction to be immediately accretive to free cash flow and dividends upon completion.
4.
Asset overview: Suncor Energy is transferring interests in both mature producing fields and near-term development extension projects. The Terra Nova field is an operated asset supported by a recently completed floating production vessel life extension project. The White Rose area includes a 40 percent nonoperated interest in existing lands and a 38.6 percent interest in the West White Rose extension project.

Suncor Energy reported that this extension project is designed to access substantial additional resources to the west of the main field. This near-term development reduces investment risk because significant capital has already been spent on the platform.

Strategic entry: Ithaca Energy became the fifth largest operator in the offshore East Coast Canada basin through this transaction.
