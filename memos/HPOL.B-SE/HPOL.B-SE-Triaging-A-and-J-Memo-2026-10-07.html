[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = C

Thesis. Hexpol is a change-of-regime case at a good, slow business: a board that dismissed its chief executive for inaction has installed one with a mandate and a balance sheet to deploy, and a cyclical turn in volumes has arrived at the same moment. At SEK 97.05 the shares already pay for both: the modal return from here is about 14 percent a year, a C against the 20 percent bar, and the case clears the bar only about 12 percent lower, or if the acquisition engine proves itself at twice the pace we assume.

  • The change is internal first, and it is funded rather than announced.
    • In May 2026 the board dismissed Klas Dahlberg for "stagnant operational progress" and limited acquisition activity
    • Henrik Elmin, a director since 2023 and nineteen years at Atlas Copco, took office on 1-Aug-26.
    • The Capital Markets Day of 04-Nov-25 had already raised the leverage ceiling from a history below 1.0x to 2.5x net debt to EBITDA, set 2030 targets (EPS growth above 10 percent a year; EBIT margin 14 to 16 percent), and carved Thermoplastic Compounding out as a third segment from 1-Jan-26 to show the faster-growing part.
    • The first large deal of the new strategy, Vipa Group at EUR 143.5 million, was signed on 31-Jul-26 under the acting chief executive.
    • Four of the twelve internal forces are strong, which meets the framework's decision rule twice over.
    • What is still missing is the new chief executive's own record: he has signed nothing yet, holds 5,000 shares and bought none in the open market in twelve months, and the sell-side calls his appointment a rushed succession.
  • The external change is a turn in the company's own cycle, not a demand inflection.
    • Twelve quarters of negative organic sales ended in Q2 2026 (reported 20-Jul-26) with +8 percent organic growth, a 4 percent sales beat and a 9 percent adjusted EBIT beat.
    • The next two comparables, Q3 2025 (EBIT SEK 688 million) and Q4 2025 (EBIT SEK 508 million), are the two weakest quarters in three years.
    • Against it: order books are weeks long and shortening, automotive (38 to 42 percent of volumes) was flat in rubber, the Americas grew 3 percent, and the only active broker says customers may have bought ahead of mid-single-digit price rises.
      • The wire-and-cable niche that grows 10 to 15 percent a year is about 12 percent of the business.
      • So the external force is a moderate cyclical tailwind with a plausible destock behind it, and the supply side (Middle East raw materials) and the krona are live headwinds.
  • 'Financial Outputs' have turned up, one quarter deep.
    • Rubber Compounding grew 7 percent organically and Thermoplastic Compounding 12 percent
    • Thermoplastic Compounding's EBIT margin rose 280 basis points to 11.9 percent while Rubber Compounding's fell 160 basis points to 15.2 percent on operating-cost inflation.
    • All four named brokers raised FY2026 and FY2027 EPS after the quarter (consensus FY2026 +7.6 percent and FY2027 +10.6 percent over three months to 2026-10-02)
    • The twelve-month revisions are still negative (FY2026 −12.9 percent).
    • The outputs add up to modal EPS growth of about 8 percent a year from FY2025 to FY2028, below the framework's 12 to 20 percent band, because the margin is flat on our path: the growing segment is the lower-margin one and Rubber Compounding's cost inflation offsets automation and utilisation.
      • Cash conversion went the wrong way in Q2 2026 as safety stocks lifted net working capital to 15.3 percent of trailing sales.
  • The change has travelled from resources and choices through actions into one quarter of outputs along the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain.
    • The chief executive (resources), the 2030 targets and the leverage ceiling (choices), Vipa, the price rises and the captive-conversion wins (actions), one quarter of key operating metrics, and one round of sell-side estimates: input plus early output.
    • The second quarter of evidence and the second deal do not yet exist, which is where the chain is untested rather than broken.
  • What we think it earns: EPS of about SEK 6.05 in FY2026, 6.70 in FY2027 and 7.25 in FY2028 on the brokers' basis, against SEK 5.77 in FY2025, about 8 percent a year, with a dividend of SEK 4.20 a share (4.3 percent of the price) on top.
    • Our path sits level with consensus for FY2026 (median 6.01) and a shade below it for FY2027 (6.84), because we haircut the Americas and H2 2026 volumes for a destock and carry more interest for Vipa than the brokers do.
  • What it costs: SEK 97.05, or 13.8x the 24-month-forward consensus EPS of SEK 7.04 (2026-10-06).
    • That is the 40th percentile of the stock's own ten-year range, the 62nd of five years and the 93rd of two years
    • The shares are up 33.5 percent in three months and 22.9 percent above the 200-day average.
    • The re-rating that an oversold entry offered in July 2026 at SEK 63 to 80 has been taken.
  • The return against the bar does not clear it.
    • Three components, each counted once: the cash return of 4.3 percent a year (predictable), earnings growth of about 8 percent a year on the 24-month-forward figure (the carrier), and a re-rating of about +3.7 percent in total from 13.8x to a blended 14.3x (the swing factor, and small).
    • The modal return is 13.9 percent a year over eighteen months and 12.7 percent a year over three years, a C against the framework's anchors.
    • The same path at 15.5x is a B and at 13.5x a D
    • A plausibly worse drop-through from EBITDA to EPS (tax 28 percent, a financial net of SEK 200 million, a 5 percent stronger krona) takes about 6 percent off the EPS path and moves both horizons to D.
    • The case clears the 20 percent bar on the modal path at about SEK 90 over eighteen months and about SEK 80 over three years
      • We take about SEK 85 as the price at which we would progress it on the modal path alone.
  • The pattern it fits is 'Major Corporate Change', with medium-high crispness.
    • A board-mandated chief executive, a reorganised structure, a raised leverage ceiling and an acquisition step-up that is now actual, entered at the input-plus-early-output stage, which is where this pattern is meant to be entered.
    • The secondary, 'OSHQC CfC Clearing', described the entry in July 2026 and no longer fits at SEK 97, because the oversold condition has gone.
  • Where the chain holds and where it breaks.
    • It holds from the board's decision through the first deal and the first quarter, and it holds in the foundations: the number one position in rubber compounding, procurement scale that lifts acquired units' margins on day one, a recipe database that locks in customers, and a balance sheet at 1.15x against a 2.5x ceiling.
    • It breaks at the price, because the market has read one quarter of early output as a trend, and it is untested at the two things the return turns on: whether volumes stay positive after the pre-buy, and whether the new chief executive finds and prices the second and third deals.

  • The case therefore is the right story at the wrong price, by about 12 percent: a good company at a full multiple, with a C return in the modal case and a B only in the branch (about 40 percent odds) where organic growth proves durable and the acquisition engine proves itself at once.

  • Rating = C, because the change forces are broad and real ('Change Forces' B) but the financial outputs they have produced are one quarter old and add up to EPS growth of about 8 percent a year ('Financial Outputs' C), and from SEK 97.05 the modal return of 13 to 14 percent a year does not clear the 20 percent bar ('Modal return' C).

    • Not B, because the one thing that would make it B, a 20 percent modal return, needs either an entry about 12 percent lower or the cleared acquisition branch, which we put at about 40 percent odds.
    • Not D, because the forces are funded (SEK 1.6 billion spent on Vipa; about SEK 4.5 billion of headroom to the ceiling on our estimate) and the first output quarter is strong against the two weakest comparables in three years.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The case against, stated at its strongest: the Q2 2026 quarter was the profile the risks report calls a 'false friend', price rises and pre-buying masking flat volumes.
      • Mid-single-digit price rises and customer pre-buying ahead of them, triggered by Middle East raw-material volatility, lifted organic sales 8 percent while underlying volumes stayed on the twelve-quarter trend
      • The risks report's own analysis of competing hypotheses found this profile had the fewest hard inconsistencies with the evidence.
      • Rubber Compounding, 68 percent of sales, saw EBIT fall 5 percent and its margin fall 160 basis points in the same quarter.
        • Automotive, 38 to 42 percent of volumes, was flat and faces European OEM profit warnings (Stellantis, Volkswagen) and a 2.6 percent cut to 2026 light-vehicle production (S&P Global, June 2026).
        • The Americas grew 3 percent.
        • A destock follows in H2 2026 as raw materials stabilise.
      • The new chief executive is a board insider hired within days of the dismissal, with no transaction record and no personal share purchase, at a company on its third chief executive in 36 months that loses two divisional presidents by the end of 2027.
        • The acquisition pace has slowed since mid-2024 and the North American rubber pipeline is depleted and limited by antitrust.
      • The krona is forecast to strengthen (Nordea: USD/SEK 9.23 at end-2027), and a 10 percent move takes about 9 percent off EBIT.
      • The stock has re-rated 30 percent in three months to the 93rd percentile of its two-year multiple range on one quarter.
        • A trough multiple of 11.6x on SEK 7 of EPS is about SEK 82
        • The sell-side's bear case is 11x on about SEK 5, SEK 55 to 65.
        • The risks report's pre-mortem puts the joint failure probability at 45 percent if a macro downturn overlaps a feedstock spike.
    • What we concede.
      • That the pre-buy is plausible and unproven either way until Q3 2026 (23-Oct-26)
      • That rubber's margin fell on operating costs, which are harder to price through than raw materials
      • That the multiple is high against two years and the re-rating has happened before the evidence
      • That Elmin is unproven and the senior bench is thinning
      • That the krona is a real, unhedged drag on reported figures
      • And that the modal return at SEK 97 is a C, not a B.
      • We make no claim that any segment covers the enterprise value: Rubber Compounding's run-rate adjusted EBIT of about SEK 2.2 billion after tax at 26.8 percent is about SEK 1.6 billion of post-tax earnings, worth about SEK 10 billion at the comparison set's lowest multiple (6.2x) and about SEK 22 billion at Hexpol's own 13.8x, 30 and 67 percent of the market capitalisation
      • Neither is a cover.
      • The bounded-downside argument rests on the dividend, the balance sheet and the margin history, not on a sum of parts.
    • Why we still hold our view.
      • Because the internal change does not depend on Q2's volumes being durable: the mandate, the ceiling and Vipa stand whether or not customers pre-bought, and the acquisition half of the thesis has fifteen years of record behind it (45 companies bought, Piedmont integrated within 90 days, Kabkom above group margin).
      • Because the next two comparables are the two weakest quarters in three years, so even a destocked H2 2026 is likely to show growth, and the sell-side skew is four of four raising.
      • Because the pass-through model has held through 2009, 2020, 2021 and 2022, and the risks report's own history finds margin-collapse bear cases "consistently overly pessimistic".
      • Because the downside is bounded in the sense that matters: a 4.3 percent yield covered by cash, a 1.15x balance sheet, a 61 percent equity ratio and a margin that held at 10 percent in the worst year on record.
    • Where the case against would win: if Q3 2026 organic growth comes in below 2 percent with a Rubber Compounding margin below 14 percent.
      • That is the reading on which we would drop the case.
      • The competing hypothesis is already in our rating: it is why the 'Modal return' is C, why 'Limited multiple risk' is D, why the recommendation is neutral, and why the price we would progress at is about 12 percent lower.
