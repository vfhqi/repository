Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Billerud faces high operating leverage where structurally elevated Swedish wood costs and North American packaging transitions dictate its earnings trajectory over the next four years.
  2. Profitability remains sensitive to raw material cost fluctuations and the execution of the moderate Evolution investment program across its North American mill system.

Structural Pressures and

1.
Fiber Costs Key Question: "how much fixed-cost operating leverage does this business model carry, how much scope is there for it to work over the next 18 months to four years, and, where it is relevant to the company's strategy, how feasible is cost elimination or reduction, both incremental and in material large cuts?"
2.
Significant operating leverage: Billerud experiences high earnings sensitivity where marginal volume and pricing shifts across its European and North American mill networks dictate net margins. Jefferies Research indicates that the company is a value-recovery story where profitability hinges on managing its extensive asset base and recovering from cyclical troughs. With a targeted mid-cycle EBITDA of over SEK 6 billion, understanding cost structures is vital because even small volume improvements can dramatically accelerate earnings per share recovery. Consequently, the business model requires high capacity utilization to absorb substantial fixed costs and protect historical margins.
3.
Severe fiber inflation: Billerud faces intense cost pressure in Sweden where structurally high wood prices directly squeeze European margins. Morgan Stanley Research highlights that Swedish pulpwood prices surged by 160% trough-to-peak over five years, structurally altering regional cost-competitiveness. This inflation has been driven by the Russian import ban which removed 10% of regional pulpwood supply, creating a persistent fiber deficit. Wood raw material accounts for a third of operating costs, so these elevated costs depress the company's earnings potential unless pricing power can fully offset them.
4.
Substantial earnings sensitivity: Billerud's business model exhibits high raw material exposure because every 10% lower Nordic pulpwood price adds SEK 0.9 billion to group EBITDA. Jefferies Research indicates that declining pulpwood costs represent a major tailwind for EBITDA recovery over the next two years. Sourcing 70.6% of its European wood from Swedish forests means the company benefits significantly more from wood cost relief than peers with higher forest self-sufficiency. Consequently, the projected decline in wood costs is expected to drive substantial EBITDA recovery back toward mid-cycle levels.
5.
Contrary evidence: Billerud may not fully retain the benefits of declining pulpwood costs because oversupplied European board markets could force the company to give back cost savings through lower end-product pricing. Jefferies Research warns that overcapacity in cartonboard and folding boxboard segments may delay margin recovery as industry players compete on price. Furthermore, higher diesel and logistic costs to the mill gate could offset up to half of the anticipated raw material relief. Therefore, the wood cost decline might not translate into the projected EPS improvement.

North American Transition and

6.
Savings Moderate strategic investment: Billerud's strategic pivot to North American packaging materials is defined by its focused Evolution program. After scrapping the capitalintensive conversion of the Escanaba mill in 2024, the company committed to a leaner SEK 1.3 billion investment path. DNB Carnegie notes that this program aims to gradually migrate 30% of its 1 million tons of graphic paper capacity into various packaging grades. This shift enables the company to mitigate secular graphic paper declines while utilizing attractively priced local fiber.
7.
Improving earnings expectations: Billerud's dual-region strategy and structural cost-out programs are expected to support gradual earnings recovery over the next two years. DNB Carnegie projects adjusted earnings per share to recover from SEK 1.55 in 2026 to SEK 5.12 in 2027, and reach SEK 6.21 by 2028. This robust earnings trajectory relies heavily on lower Scandinavian wood raw material costs and fixed-cost savings. Ultimately, the company's ability to restore stable earnings growth will determine whether its valuation can recover from historic lows.
8.
Other side of the story: Billerud's high exposure to structurally elevated Nordic wood costs and oversupplied packaging markets could restrict group margin expansion. Jefferies Research warns that overcapacity in cartonboard and folding boxboard segments may delay margin recovery as industry players compete on price. If European demand remains soft and wood costs stabilize at historically high levels, Billerud may be forced to implement costly capacity closures to protect its remaining asset margins.

Section 2 – Fixed and Variable Cost Splits

Section summary
  1. Billerud's divergent regional cost structures establish highly distinct operational profiles where Region Europe operates as a margin-driven business and North America functions as a high-leverage volume play. Net read: Peer comparisons with Nordic producers reveal that Billerud's low forest integration increases its variable cost volatility, making its European operating margins highly sensitive to raw material price swings.

Regional Cost Disparities

1.
Divergent regional structures: Billerud's cost configurations reveal a sharp structural contrast between its European and North American manufacturing footprints. The company reported at its Capital Markets Day on 3 December 2024 that its European operations are highly variable-cost intensive while North American operations carry much higher fixed costs. Management stated that variable expenses make up 75% of European costs compared to just 56% in North America. This structural disparity means that European margins depend on fiber spreads whereas North American earnings require high utilization to absorb heavy fixed overheads.
2.
Variable European expenses: Billerud's European mill system remains highly sensitive to volatile input costs due to its massive variable cost component. According to company presentations on 3 December 2024, the 75% variable cost base in Europe is dominated by fiber, chemicals, energy, and logistics. This high proportion of variable costs means the region must focus heavily on improving gross margins through pricing. Consequently, any downward movement in Nordic wood costs directly translates into rapid margin expansion rather than requiring volume gains.
3.
Fixed North American costs: Billerud's North American mills carry a substantial fixed cost burden that creates aggressive operating leverage. The company disclosed on 3 December 2024 that fixed costs account for 44% of the North American cost base. Because of this heavy structural overhead, the business model in North America relies primarily on keeping machines filled. Therefore, raising capacity utilization is the primary lever for improving unit costs and restoring profitable operations in the region.
4.
Contrary evidence: Billerud's European operations might experience higher fixed-cost operating leverage than the reported 25% split suggests when production volumes drop severely. DNB Carnegie reported on 27 April 2023 that weak volumes in Europe during early 2023 had a major negative impact on fixed-cost absorption. Furthermore, the company was forced to take significant production curtailments in its European mills to manage high inventories. This indicates that despite a high variable proportion, European mills still face severe margin compression from underabsorption during cyclical downturns.

Peer Comparative Cost Structures

5.
Variable peer structures: Metsä Board exhibits a highly variable cost structure that closely resembles Billerud's European operations but features lower forest integration. DNB Carnegie published a cost breakdown showing that Metsä Board's cost base in 2025 was 79% variable and 21% fixed. This variable portion was led by wood at 20%, pulp at 16%, logistics at 15%, chemicals at 15%, and energy at 11%. Because of this high variable exposure, Metsä Board is structurally vulnerable to pulpwood inflation and must aggressively implement rationalization measures to protect its net margins.
6.
Integrated peer models: Stora Enso's cost structure reflects a different balance due to its extensive raw material integration. DNB Carnegie reported that Stora Enso's major cost items in 2025 consisted of fiber at 39%, personnel at 14%, logistics at 11%, chemicals at 7%, and energy at 5%. This high fiber component represents both a risk and an opportunity depending on raw material sourcing. However, Stora Enso's cost reduction actions are designed to offset this persistent variable cost inflation over the next four years.
7.
Fixed peer sensitivity: Holmen's industrial operations exhibit a highly stable cost structure but face severe pressure in non-integrated segments. Morgan Stanley reported that Holmen's southern Swedish sawmills are under pressure because logs represent 70% of total input costs. Furthermore, the broker highlighted that Holmen faces structural wood cost inflation across its sawmill operations. This exposure means that Holmen's sawmill margins are highly vulnerable to persistent raw material inflation.
8.
Backward integrated advantages: Holmen's integrated business model provides a competitive cost advantage over the long term. DNB Carnegie highlighted that Holmen's large forest and renewable energy assets provide a strong foundation for long-term value creation. The broker noted that this high backward integration helps Holmen keep overall industrial input costs down in high-inflation environments. This structural advantage mitigates the raw material volatility that non-integrated Nordic peers face.

Section 3 – Margin Sensitivity in Past Cycles

Section summary
  1. Historical packaging cycles show that Billerud's margins react violently to delivery volume shifts due to heavy fixed cost structures.
  2. A modest one to three percent volume swing in Europe triggers substantial adjustments to operating profitability under the current cost base.

Historical Downturns

1.
Severely depressed margins: Billerud experienced substantial margin contraction during the 2008-2009 global financial crisis due to volume-related downtime. The company reported that operating margin plummeted to 4% in 2008 from 8% in 2007. While total deliveries in 2009 rose 3% to 1,312,000 tonnes, high input costs continued to restrict margin recovery. Consequently, these early downturns demonstrated that fixed costs quickly erode earnings during periods of low capacity utilization.
2.
Declining transitional profitability: Billerud faced severe margin compression in the 2012-2013 downturn which coincided with its major merger with Korsnäs. The company reported that its operating margin halved from 10% in 2011 to 5% in 2012 as selling prices fell. Higher fixed costs of SEK 454 million during 2012 further depressed operating profits. This performance highlighted the vulnerability of the expanded mill system during demand contractions.
3.
Sluggish operational performance: Billerud experienced low profitability during the 2019-2020 downturn due to heavy start-up costs for the KM7 machine at Gruvön. The company reported that EBITDA margin fell from 12% in 2019 to 11% in 2020. The commercial ramp-up was slowed by pandemic-related demand shocks which created major earnings headwinds. Therefore, the combination of cyclical weakness and heavy asset investments intensified group margin erosion.
4.
Highly challenging conditions: Billerud's EBITDA margins contracted severely during the 2023 downturn as extreme destocking forced major production curtailments. The company reported that group EBITDA margin dropped from 19% in 2022 to just 8% in 2023. Deliveries fell to 3.5 million tons as the company voluntarily took extensive downtime. This recent down-cycle confirmed that fixed-cost under-absorption remains a critical risk.
5.
Contrary evidence: Billerud managed to protect some product lines during the 2023 downturn because of highly stable contract structures. The company reported that liquid packaging board demand remained stable at normalized levels throughout the year despite severe destocking. Furthermore, pricing negotiations concluded in late 2023 provided meaningful insulation against pricing pressure. Thus, certain highly specialized packaging grades do not suffer from the same severe cyclical volatility as graphic paper.
6.
Severe peer margin compression: Stora Enso and Mondi also suffered from high operating leverage and severe margin compression during the 2023 downturn. Morgan Stanley reported that oversupplied packaging markets and elevated fiber inflation forced sector-wide margin declines.
7.

Historical Upturns

8.
Strong volume recovery: Billerud demonstrated significant operating leverage during the 2010 upturn as demand rebounded. The company reported that operating margin reached 12% for the full year, surpassing its 10% mid-cycle target. Operating profit jumped by SEK 737 million, driven by improved pricing and mix. This recovery proved that the asset base generates rapid margin expansion when demand returns.

The company reported that adjusted EBITDA margin peaked at 19% in 2022 on net sales of SEK 42,590 million. The company reported an incremental EBITDA impact of SEK 230 million in the fourth quarter of 2024. Section 5 – Maintenance, OEE, and North American Mill Transitions

Section summary
  1. Scheduled maintenance shutdowns represent a major cost barrier for Billerud's margins, with recent overruns highlighting operational risks in North America.
  2. The strategic evolution of North American mills from graphic paper to packaging materials supports higher capacity utilization and mitigates secular volume declines.

Shutdown Costs and Overrun Risks

1.
Significant seasonal costs: Billerud faces heavy earnings impacts from scheduled maintenance shutdowns across its Nordic and North American mills. The company reported that total maintenance shutdown costs for the first nine months of 2026 reached SEK 1,029 million, representing a substantial increase from SEK 782 million in the prior year. Standard maintenance procedures generally require stopping production for approximately one week, causing substantial delivery volume losses. Consequently, managing these recurring operational stops is critical to protect group EBITDA margins from seasonal deterioration.
2.
Q p Estimated cost impact and planned schedule of maintenance shutdowns by Billerud production unit Elevated operational risks: Billerud's bi-annual maintenance shutdown at the Quinnesec mill in the second quarter of 2026 suffered from material start-up challenges. The company reported that the actual cost reached SEK 171 million, which was SEK 50 million higher than originally planned. This overrun was driven by a combination of higher fixed costs and a delayed restart of the mill assets. This incident illustrates how operational delays during planned outages can severely impact regional profitability and disrupt delivery schedules.
3.
Elevated maintenance expenses: Billerud successfully executed its scheduled maintenance shutdown at the Escanaba mill during the third quarter of 2026. The company reported that the outage had a total cost impact of SEK 104 million, compared to SEK 100 million in the third quarter of 2025. The shutdown included strategic upgrades to modernized winders and roll-handling systems to support packaging board formats. However, minor operational challenges during the shutdown partially depressed North American margins during the quarter.
4.
Contrary evidence: Billerud demonstrated strong operational execution during its second-quarter 2025 maintenance campaign when all European shutdowns were completed on budget. The company reported that shutdowns at Gruvön, Skärblacka, and Rockhammar had a total cost impact of SEK 380 million, which was slightly below the SEK 396 million recorded in the prior year. This shows that Billerud can successfully control outage costs under stable operating conditions when no major technical complications occur during the startup phase.
5.
Volatile historical headwinds: Billerud's quarterly earnings exhibit severe volatility due to the uneven distribution of planned maintenance shutdowns. Jefferies Research highlighted that in the third quarter of 2022, the company faced a substantial SEK 550 million quarter-on-quarter EBITDA headwind from scheduled mill maintenance. Furthermore, seasonal variations mean that the second quarter typically serves as the heaviest European maintenance period, impacting earnings by approximately SEK 380 million. Consequently, these large cost fluctuations make it difficult for investors to assess underlying operational profitability without adjusting for maintenance timing.
6.
Significant capacity upgrades: Billerud's investments at the Quinnesec mill are designed to enhance long-term asset productivity and increase premium grade output. The company successfully executed a major upgrade at Quinnesec to expand its annual market pulp capacity of 210,000 tons and paper capacity of 430,000 tons. This strategic improvement delivered a positive annualized EBITDA contribution of SEK 70 million to SEK 100 million. Therefore, these capital projects help the North American division offset recurring shutdown costs by improving structural mill efficiency.
7.
Modernized woodyard operations: Billerud completed a major strategic upgrade at the Escanaba mill in late 2025 to improve fiber preparation capabilities. The company reported that the woodyard modernization was completed in October 2025, introducing advanced debarking technology and enhanced conveyor systems. This project provides enhanced chip quality, higher operational safety, and increased raw material capacity. Consequently, this foundational investment is expected to lower variable manufacturing costs and support the subsequent packaging board qualifications.

Equipment Effectiveness and Efficiency Metrics

8.
Substantial efficiency leverage: Billerud's Capital Markets Day presentations highlighted the massive financial sensitivity associated with its internal operational improvements. The company presented a claim that every single percentage point increase in overall equipment effectiveness adds approximately SEK 100 million to annual EBITDA. This relationship forms a core pillar of the Billerud Production System, which targets a continuous improvement of 2 percentage points per year. Therefore, enhancing machine reliability represents a highmargin self-help lever that does not rely on positive market pricing.
9.
Positive broker outlook: Jefferies Research highlighted that Billerud's focus on mill operating efficiency remains a crucial driver of its medium-term earnings recovery. The broker noted that the company's Billerud Production System targets systematic gains to help achieve its target of over SEK 6 billion in EBITDA. In their upside scenario, the broker assumes that improving overall equipment effectiveness will successfully boost margins and reduce waste. This operational improvement is expected to offset structural fiber inflation across the European mill network.
10.
