EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Business Model + Market Position

1.
Givaudan operates as the clear market leader in global specialty ingredients, commanding a 17% share of the EUR 46.80 billion global flavors and fragrances market with a highly defensive, naturally-hedged business model backed by high switching costs embedded in proprietary formulations.
2.
Fragrance market dominance positions the group with a leading 25% share in global fragrance ingredients, outpacing dsm-firmenich at 20% and IFF at 15%, while the flavor market remains more fragmented with Givaudan holding a 10% share alongside Kerry.
3.
Divisional performance divergence reveals Taste & Wellbeing underperforming with negative -1.10% growth in 4Q2025 and -0.40% in 1Q2026, contrasting sharply against Fragrance & Beauty's robust 7.90% like-for-like expansion driven by exceptional fine fragrance momentum.
4.
Revenue growth consistency demonstrates a highly reliable 5% average organic sales growth rate over the past decade, keeping consolidated operating margins bounded within a predictable 19% to 24% corridor through multiple commodity cycles.
5.
Geographic diversification provides natural operational hedging, with high-growth markets representing 49% of total sales, balanced against Europe/Africa/Middle East at 40%, North America at 23%, and Latin America at 12%.

Financial Profile + Peer Benchmarking

6.
Industry-leading profitability establishes Givaudan as maintaining the highest EBITDA margin among all major F&F producers at 24.20% in FY2025, with margin premiums widening versus peers whose profitability was damaged by raw material inflation.
7.
Gross margin compression reflects a 60-basis-point decline from 44.10% to 43.50% driven by inflation-driven price increases that protect absolute earnings but mechanically dilute reported margins due to the sector's structural 12-month contract lag.
8.
Capital return superiority demonstrates best-in-class ROCE of 20% to 25%, vastly outperforming peers who face massive capital-employed penalties from merger-related goodwill and intangibles carried on balance sheets.
9.
Unmatched cash conversion efficiency achieved 63% of EBITDA converted to free cash flow over ten years, outclassing Symrise at 44% and IFF at 40%, delivering CHF 1,053 million or 14.10% of sales in FY2025.
10.
Shareholder return discipline maintained an unbroken 25-year dividend growth track record with a proposed CHF 72 per share dividend for FY2025, representing the 25th consecutive annual increase since the group's 2000 spin-off.

Value chain concentration positions Givaudan in the highly profitable compounding stage, capturing elite 40% to 45% gross margins and 15% to 20% EBIT margins through proprietary formulation intellectual property and massive switching costs.

Supply chain complexity management spans over 10,000 raw materials sourced from 2,800 suppliers across 100 countries, with procurement split roughly 50/50 between natural and synthetic ingredients providing natural operational hedges.

Appendix

Plausible problems to monitor

Consolidated gross profit margins compressed by 60 basis points, declining from 44.10% in FY2024 to 43.50% in FY2025, driven by inflationary input costs and Givaudan's structural 12-month contract lag in passing raw material price increases through to CPG customers. While this pricing mechanism protects absolute EBITDA dollars, it mechanically dilutes reported gross and EBITDA margins, creating optical weakness relative to competitors like Croda who negotiate shorter-term quarterly contracts.

The margin compression is particularly acute given Givaudan's historical premium positioning, and persistent petrochemical inflation could extend this pressure beyond the typical recovery timeline. [Cross-ref: Section 2 + Section 5]

Givaudan's post-tax return on invested capital has eroded substantially, contracting from its historical peak of 17.50% in FY2015 to approximately 11% to 12% in recent years. This return contraction was primarily driven by large-scale acquisitions including Naturex in 2018 and Ungerer in 2020, which diluted capital efficiency through elevated goodwill and intangible assets. While Givaudan maintains best-in-class ROCE of 20% to 25% relative to merger-penalized peers, the persistent ROIC gap versus historical performance raises concerns about the group's ability to generate value-accretive growth through continued bolt-on M&A.

The strategic importance is amplified by Givaudan's strong 55% historical correlation between equity valuation and post-tax ROIC progression, suggesting further return erosion could materially impact shareholder value creation. [Cross-ref: Section 2 + Section 9]

Outliers worth noting

Givaudan's proposed dividend of CHF 72 per share for FY2025 represents its 25th consecutive annual dividend increase since its public spin-off in 2000, establishing an extraordinary shareholder return track record that stands virtually unmatched in the global specialty chemicals and ingredients sector. This unbroken compounding record reflects not only the group's exceptional free cash flow generation capability but also management's unwavering commitment to capital discipline through multiple economic cycles, commodity inflation waves, and competitive disruptions.

The cumulative shareholder value creation has been massive, with Givaudan returning over CHF 9 billion to shareholders through dividends and buybacks since 2000, demonstrating that this dividend growth trajectory is backed by genuine operational cash generation rather than financial engineering. The consistency of this record becomes even more remarkable when benchmarked against peer ingredient companies that have suspended or cut dividends during periods of merger integration, raw material inflation shocks, or demand volatility in downstream consumer markets.

Givaudan recorded a comparable group EBITDA margin of 24.20% in FY2025, representing the highest and most resilient operating margin among all tier-1 flavors and fragrances compounding peers, establishing the company as a clear profitability outlier in an industry facing severe input cost inflation and CPG customer pricing pressure. This margin leadership position has actually widened versus peers as high raw material inflation damaged competitor profitability, with Givaudan maintaining its margin premium despite industry-wide petrochemical cost shocks.

The structural margin advantage is particularly striking when benchmarked against peers operating with significantly lower profitability corridors. Givaudan's mid-term comparable EBITDA margin is projected to normalize around 22.90% to 23.40% through FY2028, while Symrise targets only 21% to 23% and dsm-firmenich targets 22% to 23%. This superior profitability is primarily driven by Givaudan's highly optimized total operating cost structure, with operating costs as a percentage of sales running 200 basis points ahead of Symrise and employee productivity generating €65,000 of EBIT per employee versus €58,000 at Firmenich and €46,000 at Symrise.

The durability of this margin outlier status becomes especially remarkable given that Givaudan faces the same contractual 12-month pricing lags, petrochemical cost exposure, and CPG procurement pressure as its competitors, yet consistently delivers 200 to 500 basis points of structural margin premium.
