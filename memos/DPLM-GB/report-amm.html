The one-line view. Neutral, medium conviction: one of the best-run compounders in the Pool, but at 7,725 pence the shares already pay for a peak year the company says will not repeat.

What the company does

Diploma distributes cheap parts whose failure is expensive: seals, gaskets, aerospace fasteners, wire and cable, clinical consumables. It runs about 17 businesses in three divisions, Controls (55 percent of revenue), Seals (29 percent) and Life Sciences (16 percent), with 53 percent of revenue in North America. It grows by buying small founder-owned distributors and leaving them to run themselves.

A plausible investment thesis

Real, but already paid for. The adjusted operating margin rose from 22.5 percent in financial year 2025 to a guided 26.5 percent, and organic growth guidance was raised three times in 2026, from 6 to 14 percent. The Investment Analyst's memo of 16 September rated Thesis's Change Forces' Strength D because the change has already reached the estimates and the price: consensus 2026 earnings per share rose 37 percent in twelve months. Next comes mid-single-digit growth in 2027 as Peerless, the aerospace fastener unit, gives back a supernormal margin, then 9 to 11 percent a year.

The memo judged the Target Setup to be 'Delivering - Serial Acquirer - Mechanical EPS Upgrader', with Setups' Fit/Crispness B, no secondary: about £300 million a year goes into acquisitions at about eight times operating profit, brokers model only announced deals, and the company closes that gap each year. Case Riskiness and simplicity

Simple to follow, medium risk at this price. The memo rated Case Riskiness C. Three drivers carry the case: the Peerless margin (about 100 basis points of group margin), the acquisition engine, and whether the market keeps paying about 25 times earnings through a year of 4 to 6 percent consensus growth. The risk sits in the price: published bear cases are 4,500 pence (Jefferies) and 3,600 pence (Morgan Stanley).

Business quality

High, with one flattering component. The memo rated Foundations' Robustness B and the operator A. Customers are locked in by design-in and certification: 99 percent retention at Windy City Wire, no customer above 1.5 percent of revenue. Cash conversion has averaged 96 percent over eighteen years. The flattering part is Peerless: without it the group margin would be 19.9 percent, and adjusted operating profit ran £43.8 million above statutory in the first half.

Value chain and thematics: the pros and the cons

Pros: structural niches and local sourcing. About 75 to 85 percent of United States products are sourced locally, a tariff advantage, and about half of revenue sits in aerospace (order books of 10 to 12 years), defence (about 7 percent) and data centres. The Pool readings are firm: twelve-month relative strength of 85, sector 72, analysts raising sales estimates with none cutting.

Cons: the rates regime and the weaker half. Last Friday's Portfolio Fitness for Fighting reads a rates-led late cycle, and higher-for-longer rates is a strengthening thematic, the direct enemy of a 28-times multiple. Industrials and the United Kingdom sit at Neutral, and the script's Thematic Fit/Momentum reading of 30 September is C (provisional), below the memo's thematic momentum B. International Seals and Life Sciences are flat to weak, and sterling strength cuts reported earnings.

The strongest counter-case

The sell-side's "better for longer". In Rothschild & Co Redburn's strongest form: Peerless settles at 40 to 45 percent margins rather than 35, the rest of the group grows 10 percent, £600 million of acquisition capacity adds 10 to 14 percent to earnings per share, 2027 earnings grow 12 to 15 percent, and the multiple holds at 25 to 26 times: 20 to 25 percent a year. It needs a Peerless margin of 45 percent or more and growth elsewhere of 8 percent or more together, about 30 percent likely in the memo. The test is 2027 guidance at the 17 November results: organic growth of 8 percent or better and a margin of 26 percent or better.
