Section 2 – The Wood-Relief and Forestry Dynamics Loop

Section summary
  1. Nordic wood costs are experiencing a temporary balancing loop as storm-felled timber increases short- term supply, but structural deficits from the Russian import ban will keep long-term stumpage prices elevated. Net read: Non-integrated producers like Billerud enjoy the highest earnings sensitivity to falling pulpwood costs, whereas highly integrated peers like SCA and Holmen experience limited relative margin expansion as wood prices unwind.

Wood Cost Pass-Through and Grade Pricing

1.
Declining raw material: Declining raw material inputs threaten to trigger a balancing loop where lower wood costs are passed directly to packaging customers. An expert and former Vice President at Stora Enso explained in December 2025 that while a ten percent decrease in wood costs goes directly to margins, overcapacity and historically low industry returns have historically prevented manufacturers from lowering product prices. Consequently, this price rigidity suggests that the wood cost relief may not be conceded to customers immediately unless oversupply intensifies.
2.
Oversupplied grades: Oversupplied packaging grades face intense price pressure as European chemical pulpwood costs retreat from their historical peaks. Morgan Stanley reported in April 2026 that Nordic pulpwood prices had fallen thirty-five to forty percent from their highs, which provides significant margin relief to downstream packaging mills. However, because packaging paper continues to suffer from overcapacity, brokers warn that these lower costs could eventually erode pricing power in oversupplied board categories.
3.
Elevated damage: Heavy storm damage in late 2025 created a temporary oversupply of timber that depressed Swedish stumpage prices throughout 2026. Jefferies reported in February 2026 that Storm Johannes damaged approximately ten million cubic meters of timber, which represents ten percent of Sweden's annual harvest. This sudden volume influx temporarily lowered Nordic pulpwood costs during the first half of the year before salvage operations began to wind down.
4.
Stabilizing forest: Stabilizing forest dynamics are expected to reverse the storm-driven wood cost relief as salvage felling ends and normal harvesting resumes. Jefferies highlighted in October 2026 that Billerud expects Nordic wood costs to remain flat sequentially in the fourth quarter of 2026 and stable on average throughout 2027. This flat outlook indicates that the temporary stumpage price relief will halt as Swedish forest operators reduce felling rates to restore market balance.

Virgin Premium Dynamics and Substitution Risks

1.
Elevated pricing: European virgin containerboard benchmark prices reached one thousand and five euros per ton in late 2026, maintaining a strong position compared to recycled testliner. Jefferies reported in October 2026 that recycled testliner prices stood at seven hundred and seventy-five euros per ton, leaving a wide premium. This pricing gap creates a substantial incentive for packaging converters to substitute virgin grades with recycled board wherever technically feasible. This dynamic directly threatens the premium realizations of virgin-reliant producers like Billerud over the 2027 to 2028 period.
2.
Growing spread: The premium of virgin kraftliner over recycled testliner expanded to two hundred and thirty euros per ton in late 2026. Jefferies highlighted in October 2026 that this current premium is significantly above the historical ten-year average of eighty euros per ton. High virgin premiums encourage box makers to maximize cheaper recycled fiber content to reduce raw material input costs. Consequently, this wide price gap activates a balancing feedback loop where overearning in virgin grades accelerates demand destruction.
3.
Upcoming capacity: Upcoming recycled containerboard capacity additions are poised to flood the European market in early 2027. Jefferies reported in August 2026 that Eren Holding's Shotton mill and Palm's King's Lynn mill will both start up in 1H27. These two mega-projects will add a combined one million four hundred and fifty thousand tons of recycled containerboard capacity. Analysts warn this massive supply wave will severely depress recycled testliner pricing across the European continent.
4.
Converted machines: Germany-based Palm is converting its newsprint machine at the King's Lynn mill in the United Kingdom to recycled containerboard. EUWID reported in July 2026 that Palm plans to rebuild paper machine seven in the first quarter of 2027. Jefferies noted in July 2026 that this single conversion adds seven hundred thousand tons of recycled containerboard capacity. This project represents a negative supply surprise that will disrupt European containerboard supply-demand balances.
5.
Environmental push: Brand owners and major retailers are driving a structural shift toward paper packaging due to environmental regulations. An expert from Heinzel Holding reported in February 2026 that European markets utilize recycled grades for eighty-five percent of containerboard. This high utilization rate is supported by mandatory requirements for recycled content in commercial packaging within key nations. However, this regulatory push can create intense competition for recovered fiber, driving up waste paper costs.

Section 4 – The European Board Capacity Closures and Market Returns Loop

Section summary
  1. Persistent overcapacity and low operating rates in European board have severely depressed industry returns, but a prisoner's dilemma has stalled necessary permanent mill closures.
  2. Large Nordic peers Stora Enso and Metsa Board continue to ramp up competitive new capacity rather than close lines, shifting the pricing burden onto smaller, higher-cost producers.

European Operating Rates and the Closure Barrier

1.
Depressed utilization: European consumerboard markets suffer from severe structural oversupply that continues to keep regional operating rates in the high seventies to low eighties. Broker research from Jefferies in August 2026 indicates that the continent's thirteen million ton boxboard market remains deeply unbalanced. Analysts estimate that the industry needs to permanently rationalize twelve percent of total capacity to restore healthy market conditions. Achieving this structural rebalancing would significantly lift Billerud's European margins and long-term earnings.
2.
Delayed rationalization: Strategic capacity rationalization in Europe has been stalled because individual manufacturers refuse to shut down their underperforming assets. Billerud and other regional producers face a classic prisoner's dilemma where smaller high-cost players wait for competitors to close capacity first. Jefferies reported in April 2026 that after more than two years of depressed margins, large-scale closures have still not materialized across the European landscape. This lack of market discipline directly threatens Billerud's medium-term earnings per share by prolonging the pricing downcycle.
3.
Contrary evidence: European packaging markets are showing tentative signs of price consolidation as high energy and input costs force temporary economic downtime. Jefferies reported in September 2026 that more than twenty-five percent of the European recycled containerboard cost curve was actively making cash losses. This high cost pressure has driven extensive commercial curtailments, which keep regional inventory levels unexpectedly low. Consequently, these operational adjustments might temporarily support packaging paper prices even if permanent capacity closures fail to materialize immediately.
4.
Unviable operations: Fragmented market structures in continental Europe further prevent the rapid execution of permanent paper mill shutdowns. Morgan Stanley highlighted in September 2026 that European paper and packaging companies have historically failed to stop expanding capacity. This persistent expansion speaks to the difficulty of maintaining disciplined pricing power in fragmented converting industries. As a result, Morgan Stanley expects any potential European earnings recovery to remain quite shallow over the next three years.

While this cost-competitive line strengthens Stora Enso's longterm position, Morgan Stanley warns it will add severe pressure to an already weak packaging market.

During their third-quarter 2025 earnings call, executive leadership noted that Europe holds one million tons of consumer board capacity with higher operating costs than Stora Enso's most expensive line. Consequently, Stora Enso has no plans to close capacity because its own lines represent the most efficient assets in the region. This low-cost focus means Stora Enso will continue to run its mills full, leaving smaller peers to bear the burden of any price competition.

Morgan Stanley reported in November 2024 that the combined ramp-up of Oulu and Husum will add significant consumer board supply. Because Europe remains a net exporter, these capacity additions will force intense commercial competition across the continent. This impending volume battle will likely depress European board prices, presenting a major headwind for Billerud's European segment earnings.

North American Closure Precedents

9.
Aggressive rationalization: North American packaging producers successfully restored pricing power by executing unprecedented capacity closures throughout 2025. Deutsche Bank reported in September 2026 that major domestic manufacturers permanently retired nearly four million short tons of containerboard capacity. This aggressive supply-side response removed ten percent of industry capacity and rapidly tightened domestic market balances. This successful precedent demonstrates that substantial, permanent closures represent the primary mechanism to reverse a prolonged industry downturn.
10.
Massive retirements: International Paper led the North American supply correction by permanently shutting down multiple high-cost containerboard facilities. Jefferies reported in August 2025 that the company closed its unbleached kraft linerboard mills in Savannah and Riceboro. Together with the earlier closure of the Red River mill, International Paper permanently removed over two million tons of manufacturing capacity. These actions significantly reduced system high-cost tons and restored the company's operating leverage as demand stabilized.
11.
