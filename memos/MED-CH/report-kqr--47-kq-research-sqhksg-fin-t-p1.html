Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The feasibility of cost reduction and the realization of fixed-cost operating leverage will determine if Medartis can scale its core EBITDA margin toward historical peer averages or if persistent reinvestment will limit profitability.

Operating Leverage and Earnings Power

1.
The Key Question: "How much fixed-cost operating leverage does this business model carry, how much scope is there for it to work over the next 18 months to four years, and, where it is relevant to the company's strategy, how feasible is cost elimination or reduction, both incremental and in material large cuts?"
2.
High margin sensitivity: Medartis Holding AG is highly sensitive to operating leverage because its future earnings per share depend directly on the structural expansion of operating margins from the H1 2026 core EBITDA level of 18.0%. If the company successfully scales its premium and value manufacturing footprint, incremental revenues will drop straight to the bottom line. Conversely, should fixed overheads and distributor investments continue to outpace sales growth, the valuation multiple could face downward pressure. This operating dynamic makes the leverage debate the primary driver of future EPS and investor returns.

Capital Allocation and Strategic Execution

3.
Elevated capital intensity: Cash generation is constrained by capital expenditure because cash outflows for surgical set investments and plant expansions led to a negative free cash flow of CHF -10.1 million in H1 2026. The company is currently deploying significant capital into Warsaw, Switzerland, France, and Brazil to support anticipated growth. A successful transition to a leveraged model would allow Medartis to reduce capitalexpenditure intensity over time and improve cash conversion. Failing to achieve this operating scale would mean persistent capital dilution for shareholders.
4.
Intense selling costs: Geographic expansion represents a major risk as Medartis continues to expand its sales channels and transition to direct models in regions like the United States and Japan. While these transitions aim to establish exclusive relationships in operating theatres, they require substantial upfront investments in training and personnel. The company must balance these commercial investments against the goal of expanding operating margins. Managing this balance will decide if geographic expansion creates self- sustaining profit centers or dilutes corporate margin targets.

Integration Challenges and Valuation Risks

5.
Critical integration success: Latin America expansion is tied to the 'Cold Fusion' program, which consolidated the head offices of Medartis Brazil and NeoOrtho in Curitiba. Management expects this project to generate operational synergies starting in 2027 to support its regional expansion. However, integrating a value brand like NeoOrtho with premium Medartis products presents severe brand-dilution and pricing risks if execution falters. If these synergies fail to materialize, Latin America could remain a low-margin drag on group results.
6.
Substantial tariff mitigation: Shifted production through Project Flash is another closely related strategic pillar as Medartis aims to relocate manufacturing. The company plans to manufacture 70% of its US sales locally in Warsaw by end-2026 and 80% by mid-2027 to bypass the US tariffs that penalized H1 2026 gross margins by 0.6 percentage points. Resolving these supply-chain frictions is crucial to unboxing the true gross-margin potential of the US portfolio. Any delays in this relocation would prolong tariff-related margin dilution and risk depressing EPS.

Other side of the story: Medartis might choose to intentionally reinvest any achieved operating leverage back into salesforce expansion and medical education rather than allowing it to flow to core EBITDA margin. Management continues to prioritize aggressive market share gains over short-term profitability, particularly as it rolls out Keri Medical's TOUCH prosthesis in the US and Australia. Section 2 – Medartis's Cost Base Structure: Fixed vs. Variable Analysis

Section summary
  1. Medartis's cost base is characterized by a high proportion of fixed costs across manufacturing facilities, R&D, and administrative layers.
  2. The apparent operational improvement in selling and distribution expenses during 2025 was primarily driven by a restated cost classification rather than organic efficiency gains.

Manufacturing Footprint and Plant Overhead

1.
Significant fixed overheads: Medartis operates six production plants globally that create a high fixed-cost burden. Management reported in March 2026 that the group has manufacturing sites in Basel, Warsaw, Curitiba, Archamps, Besançon, and Gent. These multi-country facilities require substantial ongoing investments in machinery and building upkeep that expand the fixed manufacturing cost base ahead of sales. This heavy footprint increases operating leverage because low initial utilization across new sites dilutes group gross margins.
2.
Capital expenditure growth: Medartis's massive capital deployment across several production hubs locks in high future operational costs before revenues scale. Management reported that capital expenditure increased by CHF 14.4 million in 2025 to support investments in machinery and buildings across all production facilities. These heavy investments went directly into Basel, Warsaw, Curitiba, Besançon, and Archamps to expand physical manufacturing capacity. This rising capital intensity elevates future depreciation headwinds on core operating margins.
3.
Inefficient plant utilization: Low utilization rates at the Medartis US plant highlight the high level of fixed capacity built ahead of demand. Management disclosed during an earnings call in March 2025 that Warsaw was running at less than 20% of its total manufacturing capacity. Project Flash was launched in November 2025 to relocate high-precision lathes and milling systems from Basel to Warsaw to absorb this idle capacity. Raising local production to 70% by the end of 2026 is critical to reduce unit costs and expand margins in the crucial North American market.
4.
Expanding physical capacity: Heavy expansion investments by Medartis in France and Brazil add substantial lease and depreciation charges to the group's fixed cost ledger. Management confirmed during a conference call in June 2026 that KeriMedical is doubling its production space in Archamps and Besançon to meet growing demand for its TOUCH thumb prosthesis. At the same time, NeoOrtho built a new modular production plant in Curitiba spanning 6,800 square meters that was completed in early 2026. These capacity additions lock in higher long-term depreciation expenses regardless of near-term volume fluctuations.

Personnel Costs and R&D Accounting

5.
Rising manufacturing personnel: Personnel expenses within the Medartis cost of goods sold act as a growing fixed manufacturing cost component. The group's annual report published in March 2026 showed that personnel expenses allocated to cost of goods sold grew from CHF 11.198 million in 2024 to CHF 14.014 million in 2025. This increase reflects the expansion of headcount to 1,141 employees in 2025, driven by the acquisitions of NeoOrtho and KeriMedical. Total group payroll costs now represent a massive fixed commitment of CHF 127.559 million.

Fixed corporate overhead: Centralized support costs at Medartis represent a rigid corporate layer that fails to scale dynamically with local revenue growth. The Medartis annual report published in March 2026 indicates that central headquarters support functions, such as finance, IT, and human resources, do not generate separate revenues. These essential administrative expenses are grouped under non-operating non-allocated items. Contrary evidence: The Medartis headquarters in Basel utilizes highly automated, continuous manufacturing processes that keep the variable labor component of production exceptionally low. Stated in its annual report in March 2025, Medartis relies on robotics and lean manufacturing to enable highly automated 24/7 manufacturing processes under one roof. This advanced level of automation allows the Basel facility to achieve efficiency gains that protect gross margin even during periods of rising supplier costs. Therefore, some manufacturing costs are more flexible than standard manual labor frameworks suggest.
8.
Substantial research capitalization: Capitalizing development costs allows Medartis to temporarily defer high R&D payroll expenses from its core operating profit statement. The Medartis annual report published in March 2026 reported that capitalized development projects reached CHF 12.5 million at the end of 2025 compared to CHF 9.0 million in 2024. The group's intangible asset roll-forward showed additions of CHF 4.323 million to capitalized development in 2025, while amortizing only CHF 0.821 million of existing projects. This accounting method boosts near-term core EBITDA margins by shifting current spending into long-term intangible assets.

Selling Cost Shifts and Commission Analysis

9.
Ambiguous selling cost reduction: The dramatic decline in Medartis selling and distribution expenses as a percentage of sales is partly due to accounting classification adjustments rather than pure operational improvements. Company reports from March 2026 show that selling and distribution expenses fell from 46.4% of sales in 2024 to 37.7% in 2025. However, this shift is heavily influenced by the full consolidation of KeriMedical and NeoOrtho, which altered the blended distribution model of the group. The reduction does not reflect pure productivity gains since direct sales investments and medical education costs continue to rise in focus markets like the United States.
10.
Sales commission dynamics: The lack of clear disclosure surrounding Medartis distributor commission accounting makes it difficult to verify organic efficiency gains. The Medartis annual report published in March 2026 does not explicitly state its distributor commission policies or whether sales are reported net of these commissions. If commissions are reclassified from distribution costs directly to a reduction in reported revenue, the selling cost ratio would artificially improve. This lack of transparency masks the true underlying selling pressure in direct versus indirect markets.
11.
Increasing commercial investments: Field sales education and commercial infrastructure investments at Medartis remain highly fixed, limiting immediate selling cost leverage. Management explained in March 2026 that it invested a mid-single-digit million amount to recruit 10 exclusive field experts and build training infrastructure in the US. These field experts do not function as pure commission-based sales reps but rather as salaried product educators who must stand in surgery rooms with clinicians. Such commercial infrastructure represents a structural overhead burden that requires significant sales volume to dilute.
12.
Rigid distribution cost: Logistic and transportation costs at Medartis remain highly correlated with shipment volumes rather than operational efficiencies. Management reported in March 2026 that logistics costs to sales ratio was 9.8% in 2025 compared with 9.5% in 2024. This cost category has risen due to more complex supply chain processes when integrating the newly acquired KeriMedical product lines. These integration frictions constrain distribution cost leverage despite group volume expansion.
13.
Segment margin divergence: Medartis segment cost allocations show that regional profitability is heavily distorted by localized manufacturing and distribution costs. The Medartis annual financial report published in March 2026 shows that EMEA generated a gross profit of CHF 122.051 million on CHF 155.732 million in revenue, while North America posted a gross profit of CHF 43.477 million on CHF 52.213 million in sales. In contrast, LATAM achieved a gross profit of only CHF 21.373 million on CHF 30.512 million in revenue, reflecting the dilutive nature of NeoOrtho's lower-priced value segment. These regional cost variations demonstrate that operating leverage is not uniform across the group.
