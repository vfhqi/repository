Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. . The evidence supports a strategically compelling but financially uneven integration of Coca-Cola Beverages Africa (CCBA) into Coca-Cola HBC. The deal looks accretive over the long run but is dilutive to margins, funding costs and risk in the near term. The thinly modelled position ahead of the 18 February 2027 results means that guidance, not the transaction itself, is the key event. The findings below are written as separate paragraphs, one per finding. Transaction status. Coca-Cola HBC is acquiring 75% of Coca-Cola Beverages Africa, with completion on 27 November 2026. The company confirmed this timing in a 16 November 2026 regulatory filing. However, the transaction will not be implemented in Tanzania because approval from the Fair Competition Commission of Tanzania has not been received. Completion also brings the issuance of 21,027,676 new shares to Gutsche Family Investments, which dilutes existing holders.

Strategic logic. Deutsche Bank Research notes that the deal gives direct access to 14 fast-growing African geographies with expanding populations and very low per capita soft drink consumption. Jefferies expects CCBA to lift the embedded organic sales growth algorithm by up to one percentage point. The brokers largely agree on the top-line case.

Their disagreement lies in how quickly it translates into earnings. Thin and uneven broker modelling. Only three brokers published detailed consolidation models, while eight others deferred official updates. Morgan Stanley, Barclays, UBS, Goldman Sachs, Evercore ISI, Goodbody, Eurobank Equities and Rothschild & Co Redburn had no models incorporating the 16 November announcement and are treated as deferring consolidation until completion.

The scale of revenue forecasts differs sharply across the published models, which signals inconsistent consolidation treatment. Deutsche Bank has FY2027 revenue of EUR 14,347 million, while Eurobank Equities has EUR 17,705 million. Comparisons between brokers should be read with that caution.

Margin dilution is the consensus direction. Bernstein expects CCBA to represent 27% of pro-forma volume but only 17% of consolidated operating profit. Jefferies forecasts the FY2027 EBIT margin falling to 11.0%, against a consensus of 11.7%. Absolute profit still grows, as Deutsche Bank has operating profit rising from EUR 1,515 million in FY2026 to EUR 1,905 million in FY2027.

Accretion estimates diverge. Deutsche Bank estimates greater than 5% EPS accretion in the first full year, net of new debt costs. Jefferies projects only low single-digit accretion. The gap comes mainly from below-the-line assumptions.

Jefferies estimates FY2027 net finance costs well above consensus. It also models a tax rate near 30%. Morgan Stanley points to leverage rising towards 1.8 times net debt to EBITDA. Guidance expectations for 18 February 2027 are conservative.

Morgan Stanley expects organic revenue growth to normalise within the company's six to seven percent long-term target. Jefferies argues management is unlikely to change its formal guidance algorithm immediately. It also sees the operating margin diluting by about 100 basis points. Morgan Stanley expects the first half of 2027 to face tougher comparatives after exceptional early-2026 volume tailwinds from warm weather and extra selling days.

The balance of evidence points to a cautious, margin-dilutive guide with accretion emphasised over a longer horizon. Because no broker's guidance expectations could be fully compared across all metrics, this is a directional read rather than a precise forecast. Rating and target price moves reflect sector pressure, not deal rejection. Morgan Stanley trimmed its target to reflect broader European beverage sector de-rating, and Bernstein lowered its target citing macroeconomic considerations and sector multiple contraction rather than operational shortcomings.

Brokers with published views retained their stances, so sentiment toward the company has not turned against the deal itself. Precedents show a repeating pattern. CCEP traded at a deep discount to European Staples after Amatil, yet its first consolidated half-year results, with strong organic revenue and operating profit growth, drove positive consensus revisions. Carlsberg's initial guidance after Britvic beat buy-side expectations and de-risked the consensus EPS trajectory, even though the deal initially depressed its multiple.

Heineken's Distell integration was expected to be accretive, but Nigerian devaluations and APAC headwinds capped the re-rating. CCEP's Philippines deal also showed that gains can be temporarily offset by a mature-market miss. CCH's own Egypt precedent was overwhelmed by the Russia shock rather than by integration failure. Historically, then, initial de-rating, a conservative first guide and a first consolidated half-year as the confirmation point is the typical sequence, with exogenous macro shocks as the main disruptor.

Which broker views align best with precedent. Jefferies and Morgan Stanley are the most aligned. Jefferies' view combines margin dilution, a conservative guidance algorithm, higher financing costs and currency vulnerability. Morgan Stanley emphasises tough first-half comparatives, geopolitical exposure and de-rating risk if integration disappoints. Both match the CCEP, Carlsberg, Heineken and CCH sequences of early compression and conservative guidance. Deutsche Bank 's view of greater than 5% accretion fits only the later, recovery leg of those precedents, as it itself observes that CCEP faced initial skepticism before delivering organic profit growth that narrowed its discount. Bernstein 's base case assumes no cost synergies, because of restricted initial disclosures, which is conservative and consistent with precedent. Limitations and what would change the read. Confidence is lower on broker consolidation status than on financial metrics and precedents, where the report's confidence matrix rates Section 2 as medium and Section 3 to Section 5 as high.

That rating is generous given the thin model coverage and the unresolved Tanzania timing. Open gaps include the Tanzanian regulatory decision, unmodelled synergies and the post-completion net debt position, which the audited statements on 18 February 2027 should clarify. Early warning signs of divergence from consensus include consumers trading down to cheaper local brands and weakening volume or price-mix, along with further devaluation of the South African Rand or Nigerian Naira.

Future work should revisit the conclusions once the eight deferring brokers publish post-completion models and the first consolidated half-year results arrive.
