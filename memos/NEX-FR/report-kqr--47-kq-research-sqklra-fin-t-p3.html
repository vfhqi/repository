Section 2 – The Down-Payment and Grid Capacity Loops

Section summary
  1. Subsea transmission cash conversion relies heavily on upfront down-payments, which create a temporary negative working capital benefit that reverses as backlogs stabilize or projects reach completion.
  2. While NKT and Siemens Energy fund capital projects using customer prepayments, Prysmian operates on a positive working capital model, and Nexans faces cash conversion volatility as down-payment momentum slows.
  3. Grid accessories capacity expansions are designed to shorten product lead times, helping manufacturers secure critical utility frameworks and rapidly fill new production lines.

The down-payment feedback loop

1.
Down-payment cash dependency: The subsea cable transmission business model exhibits a highly cyclical cash flow profile due to its reliance on upfront customer down-payments. Jefferies Research notes that major highvoltage order wins typically secure upfront payments of 10% to 15%, creating a helpful temporary negative working capital balance that funds early project phases. However, this dynamics introduces high volatility to cash generation metrics. In H1 2025, Nexans reported an exceptionally high cash conversion ratio of 83.1% driven by a surge in PWR-Transmission down-payments. By H1 2026, as these down-payments normalized and capital expenditures progressed, Nexans's cash conversion ratio dropped significantly to 42.7%, representing a free cash flow of €165.5 million compared to €308.8 million in the prior year period.
2.
Reversal of working capital: When transmission backlog growth slows or halts, the working capital benefits of the down-payment loop rapidly unwind. NKT's historical reports show that as large projects approach commercial completion, upfront cash is fully consumed, and final pending milestone payments require substantial working capital funding. In its Q4 2013 earnings call, NKT Cables highlighted that a substantial increase in working capital occurred because multiple major contracts were finishing, leaving the company to fund the final stages before receiving final payments. If Nexans's transmission backlog–which sat at €7.7bn at end-2025 and €7.8bn in September 2026–stalls, the consumption of existing down-payments during project execution will mechanically drain cash reserves unless offset by a constant stream of massive new awards.
3.
Sustaining milestone payments: To prevent working capital reversals, manufacturers actively structure intermediate milestone payments to remain cash-positive. Carnegie Research points out that the cash flow profile of a cable project can remain positive during its entire execution phase if milestone payments are structured to match or exceed ongoing outflows. NKT has successfully utilized this prepayment-funded model to maintain a deeply negative working capital position, which stood at negative €1.24bn in H1 2026. In its Q1 2026 earnings call, NKT management emphasized that adjusting prepayment terms on new awards is one of the primary strategic levers used to manage stable cash flows during intense investment periods.

Comparable peer working capital structures

4.
NKT negative working capital model: NKT represents the peer most exposed to the down-payment loop, relying on prepayments to fund its massive asset base. Carnegie Research cautions that NKT's highly favorable negative working capital position is elevated due to an exceptionally large backlog relative to its active capacity and will eventually normalize over time. According to NKT's H1 2026 financial report, its negative working capital of €1.24bn was supported by contract liabilities of €1.86bn, which offset inventories of €517 million and trade receivables of €419 million. This structure allows NKT to maintain a net cash position of €591 million while carrying out a €1 billion investment program. 54 g p NKT working capital and net debt breakdown as of June 30, 2026 Prysmian positive working capital model: Unlike NKT, Prysmian operates on a positive working capital framework where down-payments are balanced by operational inventory and receivable requirements. Bernstein Research explains that Prysmian expects working capital to provide a neutral cumulative benefit from 2025 to 2028, as rising contract liabilities from down-payments are fully offset by the higher inventories and trade receivables needed to support sales growth. Prysmian's Q1 2026 balance sheet reports net operating working capital of positive €1.32bn, representing 6.3% of annualized revenues. During its 2025 Capital Markets Day, Prysmian explained that deploying its new capacity will temporarily burden cash flow by €150 million annually in 2026 and 2027 due to grid-related working capital build-ups, before reaching a sustainable balance in 2028.
5.
Siemens Energy grid prepayments: Siemens Energy provides a clear sector parallel, utilizing substantial grid customer prepayments to support its backlog execution. Jefferies Research highlights that Siemens Energy delivered a significant pre-tax free cash flow beat of €1.98bn in Q2 2026, driven directly by strong customer down-payments within its Gas Services division. Siemens Energy's Q1 2026 earnings release confirmed that cash flow improvements in its Grid Technologies and Gas Services segments were heavily supported by customer advance payments, including reservation fees. This prepayment influx provides the company with capital allocation flexibility, enabling accelerated share buybacks.

The grid capacity loop

6.
Grid capacity and lead times: In the medium- and low-voltage Grid segment, adding capacity serves as a reinforcing loop by shortening lead times and securing customer framework contracts. Jefferies Research reports that Nexans is actively adding capacity in grid accessories on top of medium-voltage cables to capture growth, driving a 14.8% organic revenue beat in its Grid segment during Q3 2026. Nexans expects US grid accessories lead times to fall by up to approximately 60% following its latest capacity additions, allowing it to win major utility tenders. This shorter lead time creates a competitive edge over smaller peers, rapidly filling the newly built capacity with high-margin backlog.
7.
Contrary evidence: However, the down-payment loop can turn negative if capital expenditures outpace customer cash inflows. Jefferies Research downgraded NKT to Hold, noting that accelerated capital expenditures would drive free cash flow into negative territory as transmission down-payments start to slow down before new capacity comes online in 2027. This is visible in NKT's H1 2026 results, where free cash flow fell to negative €249 million due to capital investments and working capital outflows.
8.
Other side of the story: While a stabilization of the transmission backlog poses a material threat to working capital, the transition of Julien Hueber to CEO of Nexans signals a strategic pivot toward the less cash-volatile PWR-Grid and PWR-Connect businesses. Deutsche Bank Research notes that Grid and Connect segments are showing accelerated growth and margin expansion, which reduces Nexans's overall operational dependency on high-risk, lumpier subsea transmission down-payments. By allocating a larger share of future capital expenditures to grid accessories and low-voltage solutions, Nexans can establish a more stable, self-sustaining cash conversion cycle that is less vulnerable to the sudden unwinding of project prepayments.
