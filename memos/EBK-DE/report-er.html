Executive Summary 1 finding

Section summary
  1. EnBW's uniquely integrated infrastructure portfolio provides highly predictable earnings and stable public backing, though massive transition capex will temporarily compress credit metrics, while the strategic pivot toward regulated grids and disciplined offshore exits strengthens earnings predictability despite catastrophic blade failure at He Dreiht exposing severe offshore construction execution liabilities. EnBW's first half 2026 results met expectations, confirming the resilience of its regulated grids and e-mobility divisions against falling thermal generation margins. The company delivered adjusted EBITDA of €2.27 billion in 6M 2026, representing a 5.46% decline compared to €2.40 billion in 6M 2025 but matching the consensus whisper figures and keeping the group on track to meet its confirmed full-year 2026 guidance range of €4.60 billion to €5.10 billion. The earnings composition reveals a fundamental shift toward low-risk activities, with low-risk earnings contribution reaching 79.1% of adjusted EBITDA in the first half of 2026

3

The most material consensus deviation centers on EnBW's strategic retreat from uneconomic offshore wind expansion. EnBW decided to walk away from the 3 GW Mona and Morgan offshore wind projects in the UK Irish Sea because the projects failed to secure Contracts for Difference in the AR7 auction round, highlighting strict adherence to economic criteria over unhedged volume growth. The termination led to a massive write-down of €1.20 billion in the FY 2025 financial statements, treated as a non-operating item that did not affect adjusted EBITDA, indicating how supply chain cost inflation and high interest rates have eroded offshore profitability.

This disciplined capital allocation contrasts sharply with peers pursuing growth at any cost, demonstrating management's unwavering commitment to return thresholds.

7

A significant operational surprise emerged at the flagship He Dreiht offshore wind project. EnBW faces heightened offshore execution risks following a rotor blade failure in July 2026 on a Vestas V236-15.0 MW turbine at the 960 MW He Dreiht project, prompting BNetzA to temporarily prohibit work on the affected turbine. Despite this technical setback, Vestas has successfully installed all 64 turbines, and the wind farm remains on track for full commercial operations by late summer, representing minimal deviation from the original schedule. The project benefits from extensive commercial de-risking, with management having already contracted a substantial majority of He Dreiht's generation capacity through corporate PPAs, which is critical to insulate project returns from volatile merchant power prices.

The earnings trajectory has fundamentally shifted toward grid-dominated infrastructure. Following the massive capital increase in July 2025, EnBW revised its 2030 Adjusted EBITDA target upward to a range of €5.80 billion to €6.60 billion, representing a major step up from the previously communicated 2030 goal of €5.50 billion to €6.30 billion, demonstrating that management is aiming for much higher profitable growth than originally anticipated by the market.

Under the updated 2030 targets, the System Critical Infrastructure segment is expected to deliver Adjusted EBITDA of €3.40 billion to €3.70 billion, representing a solid increase compared to the previously communicated segment target of €2.70 billion to €3 billion, as management intentionally prioritizes highly predictable regulated revenues to cushion the balance sheet against volatile merchant markets. However, this ambitious growth trajectory carries significant execution risks.

Management continues to promote its unprecedented €50 billion gross investment program scheduled between 2024 and 2030 to transform the energy infrastructure, representing a major transition step compared to earlier €40 billion mid-term budgets. Standard & Poor's revised its rating outlook on EnBW to negative while keeping the credit rating at A- on July 28, 2026, warning that the massive €50 billion gross investment program will inevitably compress intermediate credit metrics until assets generate returns.

In contrast, Moody's Investors Service reaffirmed its Baa1 stable rating on July 30, 2026, highlighting that partnership co-financing and the 2025 capital increase effectively mitigate short-term debt pressures.

The Smart Infrastructure for Customers segment delivered a positive surprise, with adjusted EBITDA rising to €309 million in 6M 2026, up 32.62% compared to €233 million in 6M 2025, beating consensus expectations of a flat performance due to booming electric vehicle charging volumes and improved commodity sales. EnBW has successfully expanded its fast-charging network to operate over 9,000 high-power charging points in Germany, with its HyperNet platform providing customers with access to approximately 1 million charging points across Europe, allowing EnBW to capture every third charging session and establish undisputed fast-charging market leadership in Germany.

The regulatory framework remains broadly supportive despite near-term uncertainty. BNetzA is modernizing the grid framework through its Networks, Efficient, Safe, Transforming (NEST) process, with final decisions expected for gas grids by the end of 2026 and electricity grid determinations following in 2027, aiming to facilitate investment in grid transformation during the energy transition.

Morgan Stanley's Robert Pulleyn argued that the initial negative market reaction to the draft gas return was misplaced because gas and electricity frameworks differ materially, expecting German power networks to achieve a 6.50% allowed return on equity under the fifth regulatory period, reflecting that electricity networks must attract substantial capital to support aggressive grid expansion capital expenditures.

Kusterer confirmed that EnBW enforces strict hurdle rates that typically run 150 to 300 bps above the base WACC, ensuring that capital allocation remains highly selective, especially in merchant-exposed generation markets, with management's commitment to these hurdle rates validated by their selective exit from offshore wind projects that fell short of financial metrics. - Earnings Calls The single most important change to the investment case is the permanent reorientation toward low-risk regulated infrastructure, sacrificing merchant upside for balance sheet stability during the unprecedented capital deployment cycle.

The outlier call is that EnBW's SENEC home storage exit signals that residential electrification will remain structurally unprofitable for utilities, concentrating future value exclusively in transmission networks and commercial e-mobility infrastructure.
