EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

CEO Quality and Operator Archetype Match

Tom Greenwood represents a pure-play internal succession archetype with deep execution continuity across 16 years at Helios Towers, contrasting sharply with peers who recruited external turnaround CEOs during periods of strategic crisis. Greenwood launched IMPACT 2030, targeting more than 42,000 tenancies with organic focus aligned to high-returning lease-up rather than risky debt-fueled acquisitions, restricting growth capital to projects delivering around 35% incremental ROIC.

His career progression from Finance Director in 2012 through CFO and COO roles to CEO in 2022 demonstrates exceptional functional mastery across finance, operations, and capital allocation. During his CFO tenure from 2015 to 2020, Group Adjusted EBITDA scaled from $54 million to $227 million while margins expanded from 30% to 55%, establishing rigorous financial discipline. As Group COO from 2020 to 2022, the tower portfolio doubled from 7,000 to over 14,000 sites while he elevated group power uptime to a record 99.99% in 2021, proving deep technical and operational expertise. The CEO's operator archetype match is exceptionally strong, reflecting continuous sector dedication rather than generalist career jumps seen at peers like Rai Way's Roberto Cecatto.

Delivery Record and TSR Performance

Helios shares returned 83% over the past 12 months, leading all global tower peers, while European tower peers Inwit fell 10% and Cellnex rose only 5.8% over the same year-to-date period. Greenwood achieved the group's previous 2.2x tenancy ratio target a full year ahead of schedule in 2025, demonstrating consistent operational over-delivery against conservative guidance. Revenue increased 7.8% year-on-year to $854.1 million in 2025, outperforming the European tower sector median revenue growth of approximately 4%, while Adjusted EBITDA grew to $471.1 million in 2025 from $283 million in 2022.

Net leverage was reduced progressively from 5.1x in December 2022 to 3.4x by December 2025, enabling the inaugural $75 million buyback and $25 million fiscal 2026 dividend under the IMPACT 2030 strategy. Average power uptime increased from 99.96% in 2022 to 99.99% in 2025, reducing downtime to 1.1 minutes, while average delivery time for a new colocation decreased 80% from 10 days to 2 days, reflecting world-class execution standards.

The strategic pivot from capital-intensive M&A to organic lease-up and cash compounding has successfully expanded returns above the cost of capital.

Leadership Style and Capital Allocation Discipline

Greenwood exhibits a disciplined, operator-led owner mindset focused on continuous process optimization, restricting growth expenditure to projects delivering around 35% incremental ROIC, far exceeding the 11% corporate WACC. Greenwood holds a massive equity stake representing 1,461% of his base salary, while employees are aligned through the HT SharingPlan, making every single worker a shareholder. Management maintains a track record of setting intentionally conservative initial guidance and upgrading, with 2026 tenancy additions guidance upgraded by 1,000 points of service.

Greenwood altered the operating structure to empower local teams, reducing build-to-suit delivery times by almost 40% to 102 days and enabling standard colocation delivery within an average of just two days. Remuneration and hiring frameworks emphasize building teams locally, resulting in 94% local staff, with 79% of the leadership team promoted from within and 65% of the workforce trained in Lean Six Sigma. The CEO's conservative guidance posture and owner-mindset contrast sharply with generalist executives who lack personal capital at risk.

Governance Robustness Verdict

The board comprises 10 experienced directors, maintaining a 50% independence ratio with five independent nonexecutive directors, which is lower than American Tower's 92% and Inwit's 85%, and below Cellnex's 67% independent majority. Female directors represent 40% of the total board, meeting the FTSE Women Leaders goal and outclassing Crown Castle's 16.7%, while directors from ethnically diverse backgrounds constitute 40% of the board. Helios introduced a 1x base fee shareholding requirement for independent non-executive directors, with a five-year compliance window, while CEO Tom Greenwood owns equity representing 1,461% of base salary. Helios share capital comprises a single class of ordinary shares carrying equal voting rights, avoiding dualclass structures that entrench management. Deloitte LLP has delivered clean, unqualified statutory audit opinions for Helios covering the 2025 financial year, though Deloitte's tenure covers 7 uninterrupted years for the parent. However, Helios carries US$50.1 million in outstanding related-party receivables, which remain unsecured, interest-free, and repayable on demand, presenting a material governance red flag. The governance structure is generally robust but requires watchful monitoring of related-party terms.

CFO and Senior Leadership Team Stability

Group CFO Manjit Dhillon brings a strong background in corporate finance and TMT transaction advisory from Deloitte and Goldman Sachs, joined Helios in 2016, progressing to CFO in January 2021, and led over $5.0 billion in capital raisings. Dhillon successfully drove net leverage down from 5.1x in 2022 to 3.4x in 2025 and executed a refinancing of $500 million in senior notes in April 2026, cutting interest costs by 40bps. The Group's executive leadership team boasts over 450 years of combined telecom and emerging market experience, with operational delivery managed locally by dedicated Country Managing Directors and succession planning heavily favoring internal promotes. Helios avoids the severe corporate restructuring and headcount reductions recently observed at Crown Castle, unlike American Tower which experienced total leadership changes across its senior executive suite in 2024. The exceptional leadership stability outclasses highly volatile peer environments and mitigates key-person risk.

The combination of currency volatility, pricing compression risk, and elevated near-term capex requirements represents the most significant threat to the investment thesis.
