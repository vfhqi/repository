Outliers worth noting

The aggregate analysis across all three parts of this financial report reveals three extreme statistical outliers that represent unexplained anomalies demanding investor scrutiny. First, ChemoMetec exhibits a severe capitalized development cost outlier where the balance sheet carrying value ballooned from DKK 35.2 million to DKK 141.8 million between fiscal years 2017/18 and 2024/25, representing a 303% surge over seven years.

This capitalization trajectory is alarming because the asset growth occurred despite the commercial failure of the Xcyto 5 and Xcyto 10 systems, yet amortization did not rise proportionally to reflect the impaired platform economics. When combined with the DKK 15.1 million accounting estimate adjustment executed in May 2026 that artificially inflated the third-quarter EBITDA margin from an underlying 55% to a reported 59%, these accounting signals suggest management is systematically deferring the recognition of product transition costs through aggressive balance sheet capitalization policies.

No comparable peer data establishes whether this capitalization intensity exceeds sector norms, but the pattern of rising intangible assets concurrent with delayed write-downs and one-off estimate shifts raises questions about the true quality of reported operating cash generation. [Cross-ref: Section 4, Section 11]

Third, ChemoMetec operates as an extreme currency mismatch outlier among its peer group, exhibiting a structural imbalance where approximately 59% of revenues are denominated in USD while only 5% of cost of goods sold and 15% of operating expenses are naturally hedged in USD. This creates a severe asymmetric transactional exposure where the vast majority of manufacturing costs remain anchored in Danish kroner and euros at the Allerød headquarters and European supply chain, leaving reported earnings extremely vulnerable to dollar depreciation against the krone.

When benchmarked against Swedish peer Biotage's 44% Americas revenue share and German peer Sartorius's 35% Americas segment contribution, ChemoMetec's 59% North American concentration emerges as a material outlier. The currency risk is further amplified by ChemoMetec's explicit policy of zero derivative hedging and reliance solely on natural currency matching, contrasting sharply with active hedging programs deployed by Tecan (forward contracts up to 18 months) and Sartorius (rolling 12-month forward coverage of approximately 50% of USD net exposure).

Recent operational evidence confirms this exposure is not theoretical: the 10% USD depreciation in late 2025 directly depressed nine-month fiscal 2025/26 revenues, creating a 500 basis point wedge between the 4% reported revenue growth and 9% constant-currency growth.
