[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = D

Thesis. Aker BP is a capex-cycle-rollover case: a very good operator finishing a very large programme, in a market that has already paid for the finish. At 353.7 NOK (2026-10-09) the shares offer the dividend yield of about 7.6 percent a year and little else, because the 35 percent step in barrels that two projects deliver in 2027 and 2028 is already inside every broker's model and does not grow earnings per share.

  • The change is internal and sits in the layer of actions.
    • A roughly USD 20 billion pre-tax development programme sanctioned in December 2022 is in its last nine months of construction, and the people who chose it are unchanged.
    • Projects: Yggdrasil and Valhall PWP-Fenris are to start up in 2027, lifting net production from a 2026 trough of 380 to 400 mboepd to about 525 mboepd in 2028, 35 percent more barrels, at the shelf's lowest unit cost of about USD 8 per boe.
    • Delivery so far: Skarv Satellites on stream in August 2026, ten months early
      • Symra nine months early
      • The Hugin B topside installed in July 2026
      • Yggdrasil power-from-shore commissioned in June 2026
      • The Valhall PWP topside sail-away on track for late August 2026.
    • Cost so far: budgets raised in three of the last five quarters, a USD 1.1 billion pre-tax raise in July 2026, and the October 2026 government budget data showing Yggdrasil up 9.8 percent to NOK 195 billion and Valhall-Fenris up 23 percent to NOK 93.4 billion.
  • The outputs that follow are cash and barrels, not earnings per share.
    • That is the shape the market has to pay for, and it is the shape that decides the return.
    • Capital expenditure: consensus USD 7,069 million in FY2026 falling to USD 2,188 million in FY2028.
    • Free cash flow: USD -1,885 million in FY2025 to a consensus USD 2,187 million in FY2026 and USD 3,772 million in FY2028, a 16.5 percent yield on the USD 22,877 million market value.
    • Dividend: USD 0.6615 a quarter, USD 2.646 for 2026, committed to grow at least 5 percent a year through 2028.
    • Earnings per share: consensus 3.56 USD in FY2026, 3.00 in FY2027, 3.02 in FY2028 and 2.88 in FY2029, because the sell-side's oil deck normalises from above USD 100 per barrel to about USD 75 to 80, depreciation rises from USD 2,145 million to USD 3,240 million as the new fields are written off, and 78 percent of what remains is tax.
  • 'Financial Outputs' have turned up on price, not yet on volume.
    • Record operating cash flow of USD 3.10 billion in Q2 2026 came with realised liquids at USD 107.90 per barrel against USD 82.20 in Q1 2026, while production fell to 383.6 mboepd
    • The profit-and-loss account has not yet shown a barrel of the inflection.
  • The change has reached the sell-side's estimates ahead of the operating metrics, and that is where the chain breaks for the return.
    • Along the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain the change has travelled from resources through choices and actions straight into the sell-side's models: FY2027 consensus earnings per share are up 18.3 percent in six months, and the consensus FY2028 revenue of USD 13,751 million and free cash flow of USD 3,772 million are the delivered case.
    • The market has paid for layer six before layer four has reported.
  • What we think it earns: adjusted earnings per share of about USD 3.7 in FY2026, 3.0 in FY2027, 3.1 in FY2028 and 3.0 in FY2029 (the Investment Analyst's own estimate; Viewforth has no model for this stock), about -7 percent a year from FY2026 to FY2029 and about -1.6 percent a year across the plateau years FY2028 to FY2031.
    • Why flat: 35 percent more barrels are absorbed by a price per barrel about a fifth lower, depreciation up by about half and the 78 percent tax rate.
  • What it costs: 353.7 NOK is 12.4 times consensus 24-month-forward earnings (2026-10-09), exactly the stock's ten-year median of 12.10 times and the 84th percentile of its five-year range, whose median is 9.33 times.
    • Against peers: above the sector median of about 10.0 times
      • Level with the Norwegian cohort, Equinor at 11.6 and Vår Energi at 12.8 times, the fair set because of the 78 percent tax rate.
    • Against the sell-side: 5.5 percent above the consensus target of 334.26096 in the price's currency
      • A buy share of 24 percent, down 5 points over three months
      • A twelve-month return of +46.6 percent.
  • The return does not clear the bar.
    • On the modal path, delivery on schedule and oil normalising to the deck, the shares at a held 12.0 times plus dividends return 5.1 percent a year over eighteen months and 5.9 percent a year over three years
    • The cash return is the whole of it.
    • The bull branch: Brent averaging USD 95 or more through 2028, which we put at possible, about one in four, gives a B or better
      • It is a commodity call we do not make.
    • The entry: the price below which the modal path clears 20 percent a year is about 290 NOK over eighteen months and about 245 NOK over three years.
  • The pattern it fits: 'OSHQC CfC Clearing', an investment-phase cause for concern clearing on hard, dated evidence in an operator that passes the quality gate at B
    • The fit is a C, because the entry is absent: the stock is not oversold.

  • What the case therefore is: compelling as a story and not as a return from this price.

    • We are not betting at 353.7 NOK
    • The bet on offer is that Brent stays above the sell-side's deck through 2028 while two projects land on time, at a price that already pays for both.

  • Rating = D, because the expected change is material to cash and to volumes but not to earnings per share, and the modal total shareholder return it produces from 353.7 NOK is about 5 to 6 percent a year against the 20 percent bar.

    • Not C: C would need a modal return near 13 to 17 percent a year, which from this price needs either oil held above the consensus deck through 2028 or a re-rating above the ten-year 75th percentile, and neither is modal.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The case against, stated at its strongest: the sell-side's oil deck is wrong, not the company.
      • Brent is near USD 104 per barrel (2026-10-09) and the environment's own judgement is bullish on energy as the hedge
      • The company runs no oil hedges, is 86 percent liquids and realises a 4 percent premium to Dated Brent, so it is the purest exposure in the cohort.
      • The arithmetic: at a sustained USD 100 Brent and 525 mboepd, FY2028 earnings are about USD 4.3 a share on the same depreciation and tax (revenue about USD 17.5 billion, EBITDA about USD 15.5 billion, less USD 3.0 billion of depreciation and USD 0.2 billion of interest, 22 percent retained)
        • The stock stands at about 8 times that
        • Free cash flow is near USD 6 billion, a 26 percent yield.
      • The re-rating: to the ten-year median on those earnings gives about +45 percent, plus the dividend, more than 30 percent a year.
      • The skew: Bernstein sees more upside at USD 90 than downside at USD 50, which is the right way to hold a two-way price.
    • What we concede.
      • At a sustained USD 95 or above the return is a B or better and this company is the cleanest way to own it
      • The dividend cushions the wait
      • The delivery odds are good
      • And if the environment's hedge judgement is right for two more years, the deck is conservative.
    • Why we still hold our view.
      • That is a commodity call, and we have no commodity input the environment reading does not already weigh as two-way.
      • The environment's own caution: a case that needs the price carries the price risk
        • Two months into energy leadership 'is where reversals begin'
        • A record is not a run rate.
      • The multiple: already at the 84th percentile of its five-year range, so the re-rating in the bull case is a second bet laid on top of the first.
      • The sell-side's behaviour: analysts who raised FY2027 estimates 18.3 percent in six months cut their buy share to 24 percent and set the consensus target below the price, which is the behaviour of people who have run the bull arithmetic and declined it at this price.
      • The expected return: the modal case at the deck returns 5 to 6 percent a year, so the bull branch at about one in four odds has to carry the whole expected return.
    • Where the case against would win.
      • We would rather own the same case at about 290 NOK, or after a project slip that re-creates the entry without breaking the operator
      • If Brent averages USD 95 or more through 2028 from here, the case against wins and we have missed it on price.
    • The second competing hypothesis: near-term strength masks terminal decline after 2028.
      • This argues against the 'Foundations' B rather than against our neutral view
      • We partly concede it (resource replenishment 23 percent; no growth leg after 2028 in the research; a seven-year 1P reserve life) and hold the B, because the thesis runs to 2028 and a plateau above 500 mboepd into the 2030s is the company's stated plan rather than our assumption.
