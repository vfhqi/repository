EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Chief-Executive Quality

Matteo Liberali maintains zero external executive experience, spending his entire thirty-year career at LU-VE following a brief two-year audit apprenticeship at Arthur Andersen starting in 1992. He advanced through internal positions including Head of Sales in 2003 and General Manager in 2008, driving organic development and sales integration before assuming the CEO role in July 2014. Eligio Macchi serves as both Group Chief Financial and Legal Officer and Financial Reporting Manager, maintaining over nine years of financial leadership and signing off on group accounts since at least 2017. LU-VE is a relatively new entrant in the United States, completing its Jacksonville, Texas facility in March 2026, competing directly against established players including Kelvion, Alfa Laval, Modine, and Güntner. Liberali holds the dual role of Chairman and CEO to send a strong message of continuity after the founder's death, with the board adopting zero formal succession plans for the CEO or other executive directors.

Archetype Match and Delivery Record

Liberali exhibits an operator-track specialist profile with a 30-year tenure at the company, ensuring leadership continuity following the founder's passing. The CEO delivered resilient compounding growth and margin expansion through highly disciplined execution, strategic M&A integration, and global manufacturing footprint scaling. EBIT margin expanded to 9.3% in 2025 from 8.6% in 2024, while TSR delivered a 19.2% IRR from the 2015 IPO to September 2022, compounding capital at 2.9x cash-on-cash.

The company secured Refrion at 7.35x adjusted EBITDA, below the double-digit multiples of global refrigeration peers, and divested Tecnair LV in 2022 for €12.9 million, generating an exceptional capital gain of approximately €9.5 million. Management allocated €9.5 million of CapEx to the US plant in 2025, representing over 55% of period investments, and reduced leverage to 0.8x Net Debt/EBITDA, well below the conservative medium-term limit of 2.0x.

However, over-dependence on cyclical datacenter capex could expose the expanded US manufacturing footprint to severe capacity underutilisation if hyperscaler demand cools.

Governance Robustness

The board is heavily dominated by founder-family interests with an average tenure of 11.2 years, exceeding European peer governance norms, with three family members holding board seats comprising Chairman Matteo Liberali, Fabio Liberali, and CSDO Michele Faggioli. Independent directors constitute only 40% of the board, below the 55.56% ratio of competitor Carel, and non-independent directors sit on key committees including Laura Oliva on Audit and Roberta Pierantoni on Remuneration. Finami S.p.A. controls 55.97% of voting rights against 44.32% of capital, while G4 S.r.l. controls 15.79% of voting rights against 12.50% of capital, creating a combined 71.76% family voting block. Shareholders approved a 10-vote-per-share progressive loyalty system on 1 July 2025 to cement long-term control, with zero dilutive equity-based plans, preventing any equity dilution for family or minority shareholders. LU-VE resolved its 2019 Italian tax audit in March 2026 for a total of €312 thousand in taxes, interest, and penalties, with the tax authority waiving penalties for 2021 because transfer pricing documentation was deemed fully eligible.

Insider Signal

G4 S.r.l. and Finami S.p.A. completed an accelerated bookbuilding in February 2026 to institutional investors to increase the free float and liquidity of LU-VE shares on the market, allowing a rebalancing of stakes by nonstrategic shareholders. Finami S.p.A. maintains 55.97% of the voting rights while G4 S.r.l. retains 15.79% of the voting rights, strengthening the combined family block despite the liquidity enhancement transaction. LU-VE holds just 28,027 treasury shares, representing a thin 0.08% of voting capital compared to peers. In contrast, Systemair founder Gerald Engström purchased 1,500,000 shares in March 2026 at SEK 72.5, raising his stake to 43.5%, while Carel major shareholder Athena FH S.p.A. disposed of 5,600,000 ordinary shares via accelerated bookbuilding in May 2026. Modine CEO Eric Ashleman proposed the sale of 15,000 shares in

June 2026, indicating profit-taking at peak valuation. The February 2026 accelerated bookbuilding may signal long-term family divestment if operational cash flows face headwinds in the US.

Expert Sentiment Against Management Narrative

Public messaging highlights green transitions and datacenter megatrends to position the brand, yet expert insights reveal concerning divergences. Expert testimony suggests air-cooled datacenter systems face growing commoditization from integrated turnkey vendors, directly contradicting the company's high-tech positioning. Hyperscaler clients are prioritizing immediate production capacity over historical supplier relationships to secure tight project timelines, with the primary driver for winning large accounts being lead time and local capacity rather than technical differentiation or price. Large integrators like Vertiv package complete turnkey systems that bypass component suppliers, threatening LU-VE's component-based business model. Hyperscalers may easily drop LU-VE once industry-wide capacity constraints ease, as customer loyalty remains secondary to pure lead-time availability. The air-cooled datacenter segment is increasingly commoditized compared to highmargin on-chip liquid cooling systems. Rapid US capacity scaling could trigger margin compression if hyperscalers delay projects, causing severe industry-wide overcapacity.

Single Largest Credibility Risk

The absence of a formal succession plan concentrates key-person risk in Matteo Liberali's dual Chairman-CEO role, with the executive team compressed from eight to only three Key Management Personnel in February 2026, heavily relying on Group COO and General Manager Riccardo Quattrini. The extreme compression of key management personnel could leave the company vulnerable to severe operational disruption if CFO Eligio Macchi departs, with prior KMPs including the Chief Technical Officer and several regional Cluster Managing Directors removed from KMP status. If a forced transition disrupts current plans, the newly expanded Texas facility faces execution headwinds, potentially endangering the newly signed EUR 100 million hyperscaler framework agreement of April 2026 and threatening the 25% Q1 2026 growth in the cooling systems unit. The absence of external leadership experience may limit strategic adaptability when navigating unprecedented global macroeconomic disruptions. The transition to a 10-vote progressive loyalty system may insulate management from any accountability if capital allocation returns deteriorate, with the owner-manager voting control of 55.97% leading to strategic insularity and the dismissal of independent board challenges.

Appendix

Plausible problems to monitor

The company utilizes zero share-based incentive plans, preventing any equity dilution but potentially impairing the recruitment of top-tier non-family international managers who expect equity upside, with CEO total compensation of EUR 1.24 million sitting at approximately the 20th percentile compared to US peers. Modine CEO Neil Brinker received USD 12.71 million in FY25, representing over nine times Liberali's compensation, while the absence of equity-based long-term incentives diverges sharply from standard industrial peer practice where 40% to 60% of total executive compensation typically derives from equity grants.

This compensation structure threatens the company's ability to attract and retain world-class external talent needed to compete against well-resourced global competitors such as Alfa Laval, Vertiv, and Schneider Electric in high-growth datacenter and nuclear cooling segments. Female representation remained flat at 30% in 2025 compared to 31% in 2024, below the peer rate of 35% targeted by comparable European industrials, while the company increased non-employee workers to 923 in 2025, representing 23.3% of the total workforce.

Management blocked staff turnover in Italian facilities as a restructuring measure to secure EUR 13.5 million in operating savings, a strategy that could, if sustained too long, impair employee morale and trigger industrial action by Italian metalworking unions. The growing reliance on temporary contracts and structural hiring freezes contrasts unfavorably with peer Alfa Laval's proactive gender diversity targets and Systemair's flat entrepreneurial organizational model, signaling potential cultural rigidity that may hinder the attraction of top engineering talent needed for next-generation liquid cooling technology development and advanced datacenter system integration capabilities.
