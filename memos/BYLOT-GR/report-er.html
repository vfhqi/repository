Section 15 – Value chain: supply side

1.
Unified equipment leasing: Ballys Intralot utilizes a combined model of direct sales and operating leases to supply specialized lottery terminals and central computer systems to state lottery organizations. In fiscal year 2025, the group reclassified twenty-one point four million euros of equipment leasing income to optimize its primary revenue reporting. All supplied terminal hardware and software are integrated into a unified operating system platform to ensure a consistent player experience across international networks. This vertical technology supply structure helps insulate the company's long-term B2B segment profit margins.
2.
Internal technology supply: Ballys Intralot relies on its proprietary Athens technology hub and its subsidiary Intralot Tech to act as the primary internal developer of global software assets. Established in 2019, Intralot Tech operates as a Greek development center that directly supports the hardware and software pipeline of U.S. and Canadian lottery contracts. Sourcing software updates from these dedicated internal research centers reduces the group's dependence on expensive third-party technology providers. This cost-saving internal supply structure is projected to bolster the group's long-term earnings per share growth.
3.
Proprietary software integration: Ballys Intralot utilizes its advanced proprietary LotosX Omni and PlayerX technology solutions to supply next-generation lottery platforms to key regional partners. A recent twelve- year contract with Hellenic Lotteries S.A. validates the continued demand for the group's central gaming system technologies. Implementing these in-house software solutions allows the group to secure highly visible recurring revenues while avoiding external licensing fees. This technology ownership strengthens the company's operating leverage as it expands its international footprint.
4.
Elevated supply risks: Ballys Intralot maintains substantial proprietary boundaries that prevent public disclosure of its third-party hardware manufacturers and cloud infrastructure partners. While the company outlines its B2B and B2C operational segments, specific supplier contract terms and supplier concentration metrics remain undisclosed. This lack of visibility introduces potential supply chain bottleneck risks that could delay terminal deployments for newly won contracts like Ontario Lottery and Gaming Corporation.
5.
Other side of the story: Concerns over undisclosed third-party supplier risks could be completely overblown if Ballys Intralot's long-term technology partnerships are highly secure. Operating a unified hardware and software platform across all terminal networks minimizes custom manufacturing needs and significantly simplifies the supplier onboarding process. Furthermore, the group's established Athens research hub provides sufficient software development capacity to bypass external platform dependencies entirely.
