[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = D

Thesis. Randstad is a cyclical recovery in temporary-staffing volumes at a generalist staffer with a slow self-help overlay, and at EUR 35.42 (2026-10-09) it is not a compelling case: the shares have already risen 55 percent in six months to the stock's own ten-year median multiple on consensus earnings that assume a gross-margin recovery we rate as possible, not probable. From here the modal return is about 10 percent a year over eighteen months and about 8 percent over three years, half the 20 percent bar. The compelling version of this case existed at EUR 21 to EUR 25 in the first half of 2026 and has been taken.

  • The change is external and cyclical, and it is real.
    • Temporary-staffing volumes have turned after more than twelve quarters of market decline, and the turn is broadening
    • Organic revenue per working day went from -2.5 percent in FY2025 to +0.4 percent in Q1 2026 and +1.9 percent in Q2 2026 (results of 22 July 2026)
      • Over 60 percent of regional markets were back in growth in Q2 2026, up from 50 percent in Q1
    • The United States operational business grew 13 percent and Germany returned to +4 percent
      • June exit rates ran ahead of the quarter and continued into July
    • Peers confirm the turn: Adecco grew 5.6 percent organically in Q2 2026 and ManpowerGroup 6 percent in constant currency
    • The turn is in the lowest-margin third of the business.
      • Permanent-placement fees, the high-margin line, still fell 5 percent in Q2 2026, and the sell-side expects only 2.7 to 3.3 percent organic growth in Q3 2026
  • The change inside the company is a credible programme, not a step-change.
    • Three internal forces are real (specialisation and marketplaces, cost discipline, perimeter reduction), and three that the framework weights most are absent (no chief-executive change, no guidance increase, no capital-return step-up)
    • More than EUR 100 million of structural indirect cost came out in FY2025
      • Headcount fell from 48,000 to 38,000
      • The underlying EBITA margin was held at 3.1 percent in each of FY2023, FY2024 and FY2025 while revenue fell 16 percent from FY2022
    • The digital marketplaces run at EUR 4 billion of annualised revenue but 15 percent of volume after about EUR 500 million of technology capital over four years
      • The sub-scale IT consulting business was sold for EUR 160 million at about 10x EV/EBITA
    • The chief executive was reappointed in March 2026, the company gives no numeric guidance, the EUR 1.62 floor dividend was held not raised, and no buyback has run since 2024
  • 'Financial Outputs' have turned in volume and cost, and not in margin.
    • The cost base is doing what the thesis needs
    • The gross margin is doing the opposite
    • Q2 2026 underlying EBITA of EUR 182 million beat consensus of EUR 175 million
      • Operating expenses fell 3 percent year on year to EUR 889 million
      • The recovery ratio was 71 percent in FY2025 and 82 percent on a four-quarter basis at Q2 2026
    • The gross margin has fallen for thirteen consecutive quarters, to 18.2 percent in Q2 2026 (down 70 basis points year on year), because the recovery is arriving in low-margin large accounts while permanent fees fall
      • The company guides it modestly lower again for Q3 2026, and the sell-side expects about 17.8 to 17.9 percent
  • The change has travelled the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain as far as the financial statements for volume and cost, and no further.
    • It has not reached the gross-margin line and it has not moved the sell-side's estimates
    • Consensus EPS has changed 0 percent over one month, +1 percent over three and -2 percent over six (2026-10-11), after cuts of 17 to 19 percent over twelve months
      • Price targets rose 21 percent in three months and the buy share fell 6 points to 11 percent.
      • The sell-side has re-rated the stock without raising its earnings
  • What we think it earns.
    • Earnings per share on the brokers' adjusted basis of EUR 2.52 in FY2025 rising to about EUR 3.00 in FY2028, 6.0 percent a year, against consensus of EUR 3.21 (8.4 percent a year)
    • The whole gap is gross margin: our path holds it at about 18.1 to 18.2 percent through FY2028 and lets a 45 percent drop-through of incremental gross profit do the work
      • Consensus (four brokers, a thin sample) has it back to 18.6 percent
    • Modal EPS growth is below the framework's 12 to 20 percent band at every horizon, and even consensus is below it over three years
  • What it costs.
    • EUR 35.42 is 11.2x 24-month-forward consensus earnings, the stock's ten-year median (11.20x band)
    • On our earnings the same price is 12.1x, the ten-year 75th-percentile band
    • The shares rose 38.8 percent in the three months to September 2026 and 55.3 percent in the six months to 2026-10-09 on a sector re-rating that Morgan Stanley itself calls "overdone and primarily driven by multiple expansion"
      • The cyclical turn is in the price, the gross-margin recovery that consensus carries is in the price, and a structural failure of the margin is not
    • Adecco trades at 8.6x (33rd percentile of its own history)
      • Randstad's premium is explained by leverage of 1.8x against 2.7x and the absence of scrip dilution, not by growth
  • The return does not clear the bar, and the shortfall is structural to the price, not to the business.
    • On the modal path the shares are worth EUR 40.89 including EUR 3.24 of dividends at the eighteen-month horizon (2028-04-09), 10.0 percent a year, and EUR 44.76 including EUR 4.86 of dividends at three years (2029-10-09), 8.1 percent a year
    • The cash return of about 4.6 percent a year is the reliable leg
      • Earnings growth adds about 4 percent a year on the 24-month-forward figure
      • The re-rating leg is zero, because the multiple is already at its median
    • The bull branch, which needs volume at 3 percent or more a year (probable) and the gross margin recovering by 50 basis points or more (possible) at the same time, returns about 28.5 percent a year at a 12.00x multiple
      • We put its odds at about one in four
    • The modal path clears 20 percent a year from a price of about EUR 31 on eighteen months and about EUR 26 on three years
      • The 52-week low was EUR 21.24
  • The pattern it fits.
    • 'Major Corporate Change' at C as the primary 'Target Setup', and 'Trough on Trough' at D as the secondary
    • The fundamentals describe a trough-on-trough turn (demand down twelve-plus quarters, company and peers right-sized, estimates cut 17 to 19 percent), but three of that pattern's four mandatory conditions fail on price: the multiple is at the median, guidance was never numeric so was never rebased, and there is no capitulation after a 55 percent rally

  • Where the chain holds and where it breaks.

    • It holds at the input layers (volume turn, cost base) and at the operating metrics
    • It breaks at the gross-margin line, which is exactly where consensus and the bull case put the earnings.
    • What would make this compelling is the gross margin above 18.5 percent for two consecutive quarters with permanent fees back in year-on-year growth, or a price near EUR 26 to EUR 31.
    • The case is therefore a trackable cyclical whose crisp entry has passed, to be parked and watched rather than progressed

  • Rating = D, because the change is not material enough: a genuine but modest cyclical volume turn and a slow self-help programme produce modal EPS growth of 6.0 percent a year, and from a multiple already at the ten-year median the modal return is about 10 percent a year over eighteen months, half the bar.

    • The parts combine as a chain that holds at the input and cost layers and breaks at the gross-margin layer
    • 'Change Forces' and 'Financial Outputs' are both C and the 'Modal return' is D, and the return is the Element that decides materiality.
    • Not C, because a C was live only if the gross margin were already stabilising, and the Q3 2026 guide says it is not.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The strongest case against our D is the bull case in its own words (ING, Bernstein, and the closing "other side" of the earnings review): the cost base is now structurally lower
      • A four-quarter recovery ratio of 82 percent and an annualised conversion run-rate of 70 to 80 percent against a historical 40 to 50 percent mean incremental gross profit drops through at unprecedented rates
      • The marketplaces are three to four times as profitable as legacy delivery
      • And if digital volume goes from 15 to 60 percent over 24 to 36 months the group EBITA margin reaches 5 to 6 percent by 2028 even with the gross margin at 18 to 19 percent, which would put FY2028 EPS near EUR 4.50 and the shares toward EUR 60.
    • We concede the cost discipline, which three years of flat 3.1 percent margins on falling revenue have shown, and we concede the marketplace unit economics at Zorgwerk, Torc and the United States app.
    • We do not concede the timing or the group effect.
    • Digital volume has reached 15 percent after EUR 500 million and four years
      • The gross margin has fallen in every quarter since the strategy was launched
      • The 5 to 6 percent target has been missed in every year since 2002, including the 2021 to 2022 boom at 4.2 to 4.7 percent
      • And a 70 to 80 percent conversion run-rate measured off a trough quarter is a cyclical artefact until the gross margin stops falling.
    • The bull branch is possible, at about one in four, and it is not the modal path.
    • Where the case against would win is a gross margin above 18.5 percent for two consecutive quarters
      • That is the series that settles this case, and it is quarterly.

  • Rating = C, because the external forces are a genuine but early and modest cyclical turn in temporary volumes set against tightening competition and a strengthening disruption force, and the internal forces are a credible but slow self-help programme with no guidance raise and no capital-return step-up
    • Neither family is strong enough to carry a 20 percent return on its own.
  • Direction by force: the company's own cycle and end-market demand are tailwinds (early)
    • Competition, disruption and the macro environment are headwinds
    • Regulation is mixed and a net headwind in the Netherlands
    • The supply side is a mild tailwind.
  • Propagation: the internal forces have reached the operating metrics (operating expenses -3 percent year on year to EUR 889 million in Q2 2026; recovery ratio 71 percent in FY2025 and 82 percent over four quarters at Q2 2026; talent placed per FTE +5 percent) and the EBITA line (EUR 182 million against EUR 175 million consensus in Q2 2026), and not yet the gross margin or the sell-side's estimates.
  • Sector reading: the Assistant Portfolio Manager's sector note of 2 October 2026 reads the 2026 rebound as a relief rally off a -59 percent twelve-month return across every labour-service name rather than a staffing-cycle turn, and says a genuine turn shows first in net fee growth, upgrades and vacancies
    • The company's own Q2 2026 data (volumes up, fees down, estimates flat) agrees with that description more than it contradicts it.

Revenue and demand cycle: where is the company's own cycle, and is it turning? (KQ 6, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because the company's own cycle has turned after what the Annual General Meeting transcript calls over twelve quarters of market volume decline: revenue fell from EUR 27,568 million in FY2022 to EUR 23,077 million in FY2025, quarterly growth went from -4.4 percent in Q4 2025 to +1.8 percent in Q2 2026 (FactSet basis), and the sequential organic path (+0.4 percent in Q1 2026, +1.9 percent in Q2, exit rates higher in June and July) is consistent.
  • Breadth: over 60 percent of regional markets were growing in Q2 2026, up from 50 percent in Q1.
  • Mix of the turn: the growth is in the lowest-margin third of the business (Operational +4 percent) while permanent fees, the high-margin line, still fall (-5 percent in Q2 2026, from -10 percent in Q1).
  • Not A, because the risks research's own signal for a true turn is a gross margin inflecting by more than 50 basis points and permanent volumes moving by more than 5 percent, and neither has been seen.
Demand: is end-market demand changing, structurally or cyclically (a swing with two-plus years of visibility)? (KQ 1, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because end-market demand for temporary labour is turning up cyclically (organic growth +1.9 percent in Q2 2026 from +0.4 percent in Q1 and -2.5 percent in FY2025), but the turn is early, concentrated in blue-collar volumes at large accounts, and carries no two-year visibility.
  • Peer confirmation: Adecco +5.6 percent organic in Q2 2026
    • ManpowerGroup +6 percent constant currency, with the Manpower brand in the United States up 16 percent.
  • Against: permanent fees fell 5 percent in Q2 2026
    • Professional was -2 percent and Digital -4 percent
    • Belgian temporary hours fell 7.6 percent in August 2026 and Dutch hours 3 percent
    • The United States hiring rate at 3.1 percent is its lowest since the pandemic, and only 30 percent of surveyed clients plan to add internal staff.
  • Not B, because the sell-side's Q3 2026 organic growth expectations are 2.7 to 3.3 percent (Morgan Stanley, ING, Jefferies; company-compiled consensus 2.8 percent), a modest turn, and the environment reading of the week of 2026-10-09 has labour softening (United Kingdom unemployment heading toward 5.2 percent).
Competition: is competitive intensity easing or tightening? (KQ 2, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = D
  • Rating = D, because competitive intensity is tightening at both ends: Adecco overtook Randstad as Europe's largest staffer in 2026 and gained 160 basis points of share in Q2 2026, and lean AI-native platforms are quoted at 10 percent markups against Randstad's 35 to 65 percent.
  • Share loss: Adecco has gained share in 14 of the past 16 quarters
    • Adecco North America grew 12 percent against Randstad's 4 percent in Q2 2026
    • In France Adecco was -1 percent against Randstad -2 percent.
  • Pricing conduct: still rational.
    • Adecco and ManpowerGroup report firm pricing and positive bill-wage spreads, and Randstad walked away from two large Belgian accounts in early 2026 rather than cut price.
  • Not C, because the share loss is to the closest peer in Randstad's core markets and has been continuous, and pricing discipline alone does not explain it: Adecco's gross margin of 18.6 percent fell only 30 basis points in Q2 2026 against Randstad's 70.
  • The share loss matters for the thesis because the drop-through that the case relies on is a drop-through of gross profit Randstad has to win first.
Disruption: is a disruption force strengthening or being disproved? (KQ 3, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = D
  • Rating = D, because the disruption force is strengthening, not being disproved: Indeed has partnered with OpenAI, Indeed's United States revenue rose 30 percent in Q1 FY2026 despite lower job volumes, Adecco has agentic AI enabling 50 percent of its revenue, and mid-market clients are expected to in-house regular hiring with AI screening by 2027.
  • Against: digital-native platforms hold about 5 percent of market share (Morgan Stanley)
    • A former Indeed senior director and a former Randstad USA manager both say platforms lack compliance, payroll and show-up accountability
    • Blue-collar roles are the least exposed to automation, and Randstad is 66 percent Operational.
  • Randstad's answer: its own marketplaces (1.7 million self-scheduled shifts in Q2 2026, up double digits sequentially; Torc cutting fill time by 50 percent) are the company's response and are the thesis's internal force (RA 2, E 1, BB 1).
  • Not C, because the sell-side's own valuation work now prices the threat (Morgan Stanley's bear multiple of 6.5x EV/EBITA; Deutsche Bank's 40 percent discount narrowed to 20 percent), and the Industry Digest of 9 October 2026 says the AI de-rating is clearing "from the highest-quality names outward", naming data owners, not staffers.

Political and regulatory: is a change moving demand, pricing or cost? (KQ 4, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because the regulatory picture is mixed: Dutch collective-labour-agreement changes in January 2026 raised wages 10 to 11 percent and took 10 basis points off the group gross margin in Q2 2026 with Dutch revenue -1 percent, while the United States Department of Labor's February 2026 proposal eases contractor classification and the German minimum wage rises to EUR 14.60 from EUR 13.90 in January 2027, lifting bill-rate baselines.
  • Compliance moat: the EU Pay Transparency Directive and Dutch freelancer tightening favour scaled compliant providers, a modest advantage.
  • Dutch pricing: turnover +3 to +7 percent on hours -3 to -6 percent is bill-rate inflation passed through from regulated wages, which the risks research flags as a potential false friend for revenue growth.
