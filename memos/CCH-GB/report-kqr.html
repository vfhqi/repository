Section 2 – Russia Contribution, Local Cash and Peer Exits

Section summary
  1. Trapped Russian operations contribute a disproportionate share of Coca-Cola HBC's profitability but remain heavily discounted by the market.
  2. Precedents from Carlsberg, Danone, and Heineken show that a formal exit would trigger massive non- cash balance sheet reclassifications without affecting group cash flows.
  3. Local cash balances of over one billion euros are restricted from repatriation and yield rates below prevailing market benchmarks.
  4. The scheduled acquisition of Coca-Cola Beverages Africa will expand Coca-Cola HBC's geographic footprint but initially dilute consolidated operating margins.
  5. The transaction is expected to deliver low-single-digit earnings per share accretion in its first full year of consolidation.
  6. Peer precedents demonstrate that initial margin pressure from large-scale bottling acquisitions can be overcome through disciplined integration and synergy capture.

Operational and Financial Exposure

1.
Significant contribution: Bernstein Research notes on 6 November 2026 that the Russian business of Coca-Cola HBC contributes approximately 13% of group revenue and a low-to-mid 20s percentage of its operating profit. This high-margin performance is run under a self-sufficient model with local brands since the suspension of the core Coca-Cola brand trademark. This exposure is expected to dilute to approximately 10% of revenue and 20% of operating profit once the acquisition of Coca-Cola Beverages Africa is finalized.
2.
Severe impact: Jefferies Research reports on 11 November 2026 that a complete loss of the Russian business would reduce Coca-Cola HBC's fiscal 2027 operating profit by about 21% and lower earnings per share by about 26%. This estimated downside includes the loss of interest income on cash balances and would push pro-forma net debt to EBITDA from 1.6x to 2.2x. Operating margins in Russia and Ukraine reached 22% in fiscal 2025 compared to only 9.5% for the rest of the group.

Local Cash Yields and Restrictions

5.
Restricted returns: Coca-Cola HBC reported in its half-year 2026 earnings call that its Russian cash balance of over 1.04bn EUR is held 85% in local currency and 15% in foreign currencies. Management notes that the interest yield is coming in lower than market strength because of local investment restrictions. This capital remains trapped within the local operating structure and cannot be easily mobilized for corporate debt reduction.
6.
Contrary evidence: Morgan Stanley reported on 10 January 2024 that peer Carlsberg managed to resolve its CIS brand licenses and dispose of its remaining Russian business assets for a cash consideration of 2bn DKK. This indicates that despite extreme capital controls, some international beverage firms have successfully extracted hard currency from the country. This precedent suggests that Coca-Cola HBC might eventually repatriate a portion of its trapped wealth rather than suffering a total asset write-down.
7.
Balance sheet stress: Jefferies Research notes on 4 November 2026 that losing the trapped Russian cash would increase Coca-Cola HBC's pro-forma leverage to 2.0x EBITDA. If both the operating profit and the cash are lost, the pro-forma net debt to EBITDA ratio would increase further to 2.7x. This level is significantly higher than the 1.47x leverage ratio reported by peer Carlsberg or the historical cash levels of other emerging market bottlers.
8.
Emerging comparisons: Coca-Cola FEMSA and Coca-Cola Icecek also navigate severe currency devaluations and capital restrictions in emerging territories. However, these peers do not face the immediate threat of nationalization or asset seizure that hangs over Coca-Cola HBC's Russian juice operations. OTP Bank also reported that deconsolidating its Russian entity would impact its capital ratio by only 11 basis points, demonstrating a much lower relative exposure.

Accounting Precedents for Asset Loss

9.
Carlsberg precedent: Carlsberg reported in its fiscal 2023 annual report that the temporary transfer of Baltika Breweries to Russian federal management forced a full deconsolidation from July 2023. This event triggered a non-cash reclassification of 40.9bn DKK in accumulated currency translation losses and 0.5bn DKK in hedging losses from equity to the income statement. The total net loss from the discontinued Russian operations reached 47.7bn DKK, though it had no immediate impact on group cash flows.
10.
Danone precedent: Danone announced in March 2024 that the loss of control over its Essential Dairy and Plant- based business in Russia resulted in a total cumulative loss of 1.2bn EUR. The company deconsolidated the business in July 2023 and recycled 487m EUR of cumulative translation adjustments through its income statement. The transaction completed in May 2024 with a net disposal gain of 227m EUR recognized in other operating income.
11.
Heineken precedent: Heineken reported in its fiscal 2023 annual report that it completed the sale of its Russian operations to Arnest Group for a purchase price of 1 EUR. This transaction incurred non-cash exceptional losses of 307m EUR, which included cumulative foreign exchange losses previously recognized in equity. The buyer committed to repaying historical intercompany debt of 103m EUR in installments, which minimized the net cash impact.
12.
Peer comparisons: Heineken experienced a net loss on disposal of 219m EUR for its Russian exit, which represented a small fraction of its global asset base. By contrast, Carlsberg's massive write-down of 47.7bn DKK significantly impacted its reported net profit, forcing a reported net loss of 40.8bn DKK for fiscal 2023. These variations highlight that the severity of a Russian exit on earnings per share depends heavily on the historical accumulation of foreign exchange translation losses within equity.
13.
Other side of the story: Optimistic broker estimates suggest that any seizure of Coca-Cola HBC's Russian business could be resolved with compensation or cash repatriation based on historical precedents. Jefferies Research indicates that the market has already fully priced in a total loss of the Russian operations. Therefore, a formal exit would remove a major valuation overhang and allow the group to trade at multiples closer to its peer Coca-Cola Europacific Partners.

The company will also buy a thirty-three point forty-eight percent stake from Gutsche Family Investments for three hundred and eight million dollars and twenty-one million newly issued shares. This dual-funding mechanism implies a total enterprise valuation of approximately three point four billion dollars for the entire African operations.

Consolidating these liabilities will increase the pro-forma net debt and financing obligations of the combined entity.
