  1. PGE's third-quarter 2026 results remain unpublished because the reporting date is scheduled for late November, leaving historical second-quarter baselines and peer estimates as primary indicators.
  2. Strong peer performance from Tauron in the third quarter of 2026 suggests resilient distribution margins across the Polish utility sector.

Pending performance updates

1.
Pending figures: PGE's third-quarter 2026 results remain unpublished because the publication date is scheduled for late November. Consequently, specific segment results for recurring EBITDA, 9M capex, and final onerouscontract provisions are unconfirmed. This delay means that investors must wait for late November to confirm PGE's near-term trajectory.
2.
Stable baselines: PGE reported a strong recurring EBITDA of PLN 3.96 bn in the second quarter of 2026, representing a 21% increase year-on-year. This performance was driven by record quarterly results in both the gas-fired generation and district heating segments. However, the upcoming third-quarter release will test whether these generation margins can be sustained over 2027 amid fluctuating fuel costs.
3.
Comparative spending: PGE's capital expenditures of PLN 4.92 bn in the first half of 2026 were significantly higher than its peer group. For comparison, peer utility Tauron reported property, plant, and equipment purchases of only PLN 2.21 bn over the same six-month period. This aggressive spend reflects PGE's faster transition pace, but it also places a much heavier near-term cash burden on the parent group.

Leverage and balance sheet

4.
Low leverage: PGE maintained its net debt at 0.03x LTM recurring EBITDA in the second quarter of 2026, demonstrating strong short-term liquidity. However, the group's economic net debt, which incorporates future carbon commitments, stood much higher at PLN 14.20 bn or 1.06x LTM recurring EBITDA. This gap indicates that future carbon payment obligations represent a major hidden threat to long-term earnings per share.
5.
Contrary evidence: PGE's capital program faces substantial balance sheet pressure, as future capital commitments reached PLN 22.34 bn as of June 30, 2026. This massive backlog is primarily directed at offshore wind, energy storage, and gas-fired units. Although current leverage is low, executing this PLN 210 bn long-term plan will inevitably drive leverage higher and pressure future dividend payouts.
6.
Substantial liabilities: PGE's balance sheet carries regulatory risks, particularly from the onerous-contract provisions at the retail supply and conventional generation subsidiaries. If these provisions expand beyond their historical Q2 levels of PLN 218m and PLN 697m, the group's net income will face direct negative adjustments. Consequently, the upcoming Q3 report is crucial for identifying margin squeeze in the retail sector.

Peer results read-across

7.
Strong peerage: Tauron reported preliminary third-quarter 2026 EBITDA of PLN 1.82 bn, which comfortably beat the Pap Biznes consensus of PLN 1.57 bn. The group's nine-month EBITDA reached PLN 5.46 bn, down 11% year-on-year but reflecting stable underlying operations. This strong showing suggests that Polish power utilities continue to benefit from robust distribution margins despite weaker merchant generation segments.
8.
Comparative margins: Tauron's nine-month EBITDA margin of 21.10% was resilient, though down slightly from 25.31% in the prior year period. This peer baseline highlights that while Polish network operators face WACC normalization, their cash generation remains highly defensive and predictable. PGE's upcoming results should mirror this trend, provided its distribution tariffs are not cut excessively.
9.
Past precedents: Tauron's historical performance in 2024 and 2025 was boosted by high WACC levels of 10.5% and 10.8% respectively. As the regulator normalizes WACC to 9.5% for 2026 and an average of 8% for 2027- 2030, distribution EBITDA is expected to fall. This regulatory adjustment shows that peak grid earnings are shifting to a lower but more stable plateau.

Strategic outlook

10.
Transition timelines: PGE's updated 2035 strategy targets PLN 15 bn in EBITDA by 2030, which aligns with broker expectations. This long-term target assumes that networks and renewables will contribute approximately 63% of EBITDA by 2030, reducing merchant exposure. However, achieving these targets depends on successfully transitioning away from coal, which management expects to generate negative EBITDA by 2030.
11.
Other side of the story: PGE's delayed Q3 2026 reporting prevents a definitive assessment of its current-year earnings trajectory, making near-term forecasts highly speculative. While peer results from Tauron indicate a healthy distribution environment, PGE's larger exposure to unprofitable coal assets could drag down its overall profitability. If the pending report reveals expanded onerous-contract provisions or slower grid monetization, PGE's target of returning to dividends in fiscal year 2026 will face significant downward revision.

Section 3 – Corporate Income Tax and Draft Bill UD459 Impact

Section summary
  1. The Polish corporate income tax rate hike to 22% is currently pending executive action by President Karol Nawrocki, presenting potential headwinds for PGE's 2027 net earnings.
  2. Draft Bill UD459 introduces temporary windfall tax rates for energy groups, but PGE's core coal operations are legally excluded from fossil fuel extraction penalties.

Standard CIT Rate Changes

1.
Pending decision: The Polish Senate on November 6, 2026, passed Sejm Paper No. 3293 to increase the corporate income tax rate for large companies with revenues over EUR 50 million from 19% to 22%. The legislation was officially submitted to President Karol Nawrocki on November 10, 2026, starting a 21-day constitutional review period. If signed, this tax hike will become effective on January 1, 2027, and directly reduce PGE's future earnings per share unless vetoed.
2.
Presidential veto: President Karol Nawrocki has historically vetoed nearly 40 bills since taking office in August 2025, consistently opposing tax increases. Fitch Ratings reported on September 8, 2025, that these political standoffs reduce room for fiscal consolidation and create budget implementation risks. If the President vetoes the tax hike, PGE will continue to enjoy the standard 19% CIT rate, preserving cash flows for capital programs.

Distorted tax: Under the proposed CIT amendments, the 22% rate will apply to the remaining part of the tax base of a tax capital group. This distinction is critical for large, state-backed conglomerates that manage diverse financial and infrastructure assets under one tax umbrella.

Consequently, the utility sector's tax environment remains highly politicized and volatile ahead of the 2027 parliamentary elections.

Draft Bill UD459 windfall tax

6.
Temporary windfall: Draft Bill UD459 proposes a temporary corporate income tax rate increase targeting large energy and fuel companies with revenues above EUR 50 million. Under this draft bill, the CIT rate will rise to 30% in 2027, 26% in 2028, and 23% in 2029 before returning to 19% in 2030. These temporary rates will squeeze utility margins unless companies can optimize their internal corporate structures.

KPMG reported in September 2026 that this levy mimics the historical banking sector tax model. Consequently, the policy will divert cash flows away from transition projects toward state-directed consumer support programs.

Contrary evidence: The fuel-sector lobby POPiHN warned on August 31, 2026, that Draft Bill UD459 raises serious constitutional concerns and creates uneven burdens. This indicates that the proposed tax might face legal challenges or spark inflation, potentially forcing the government to dilute the final bill.
9.
Subsequent fuel: The Polish government has simultaneously worked on a separate fuel windfall tax targeting extraordinary profits from imported fuels. President Karol Nawrocki signed this fuel windfall tax on October 1, 2026, but referred it to the Constitutional Tribunal for preventive review due to concerns over retroactiveness. This legislative pattern shows how the Polish government continues to seek creative tax channels to fund energy subsidies.
10.
Targeted activities: Draft Bill UD459 limits its scope to specific activities including natural gas and crude oil extraction, fuel trading, and non-integrated electricity networks. Taxpayers with revenues over EUR 750 million are subject to the higher rates regardless of group membership conditions. This narrow scope protects diversified energy companies while hitting pure-play oil and gas companies harder.

Consequently, PGE's mining operations are shielded from the 30% temporary tax rate, preserving the cash flows of its conventional fleet.

Section 4 – Energy Regulatory Office Tariff Approvals for 2027

Section summary
  1. Polish distribution network margins remain under pressure from regulatory adjustments to the allowed cost of capital.
  2. Final retail tariff determinations for 2027 are pending regulatory approval, leaving utilities exposed to potential G-tariff margin squeezes.

Regulated distribution return profiles

1.
Decreasing returns: Tauron reported a contraction in its distribution segment profitability during the first half of 2026 due to regulatory changes. The Group's network operations were negatively affected by a decrease in the WACC of 1.34 percentage points. This adjustment demonstrates that even with a growing Regulatory Asset Base, regulatory adjustments directly impact operating profitability. Consequently, any further reduction in the reinvestment premium or allowed return for 2027 will continue to weigh on Polish grid operators.
2.
Regulatory account: Enea and Tauron rely on specific regulatory mechanisms to buffer their distribution revenues over time. The impact of WACC cuts is partially mitigated by the regulatory account mechanism, which stabilises distribution segment revenues over the medium term. This framework allows operators to defer offsetting differences between actual sales volumes and regulatory assumptions. However, the implementation of new IFRS standards for regulatory assets will alter how these timing differences are recognized in future financial reports.
3.
Stable baselines: The Polish energy regulator URE historically maintains distribution fees within tight boundaries to protect consumers. For the 2025 fiscal year, the regulator approved distribution tariffs that kept average annual household fees unchanged. Under this regulatory decision, PGE Dystrybucja experienced a minor tariff increase of only 0.40%. For comparison, peer distributor Tauron Dystrybucja was allowed a 4.10% increase, while Enea Operator saw a 3.50% rise.
4.
Comparable systems: Polish grid operators look to international regulatory frameworks to assess return stability. Canadian Utilities reported that higher revenues were offset by regulatory refunds in its distribution divisions during 2025 and 2026. This precedent highlights that regulatory re-opener decisions can quickly alter allowed returns and claw back profits. Thus, PGE Dystrybucja remains exposed to retroactive tariff adjustments that could unexpected disrupt its multi-year capital expenditure plans.

Household retail tariff baselines

5.
Regulated limits: The Polish energy regulator URE set the benchmark for household electricity prices at a level that restricted supplier margins. For the 2026 fiscal year, the regulator approved the household G-tariff at an average of PLN 495.16 per MWh. This tariff was designed to apply to all major retail suppliers including Tauron Sprzedaż and Enea. This restricted pricing meant that retail divisions could not fully recover their actual energy procurement costs.
6.
Contrary evidence: Deutsche Bank Research reported in February 2026 that the G-tariff level of PLN 495 per MWh was actually favorable. This approved rate sat approximately one percent below the cap of PLN 500 per MWh. The broker reasoned that this narrow gap allowed the state-mandated price shield to expire without causing a severe spike in consumer inflation. This suggests that the regulatory tariff was not as punitive to suppliers as some utility management boards claimed.
7.
Subsequent filings: Polish energy companies have initiated the formal process to secure new household pricing structures for 2027. Energa-Obrót submitted its official application for the 2027 electricity tariff approval on September 28, 2026. The utility also applied to amend its previous tariff to incorporate new component fees into the pricing model. These early moves represent a coordinated industry effort to lift the margin cap imposed during the energy crisis.
8.
High procurement: The structure of the Polish electricity trading market prevents retail suppliers from easily passing through volatile wholesale costs. Enea Operator and other trading subsidiaries purchase energy at volatile market prices while selling at fixed retail tariffs approved by the regulator. This misalignment creates a structural risk where sudden wholesale price spikes cannot be recovered. Consequently, the timing of tariff approvals is the main factor determining retail segment cash flows.

Onerous contract provisions history

9.
Financial burdens: The gap between wholesale procurement prices and regulated retail tariffs forced Polish utilities to recognize massive balance sheet liabilities. Tauron recognized an onerous-contract provision of PLN 146 million in 2025 to cover expected household segment losses. Similarly, peer utility Enea established a G-tariff provision of PLN 173.27 million. These substantial provisions directly reduced the reported net income of the respective retail segments.
10.
Expanded provisions: Energa adopted a revised calculation methodology that significantly increased its recorded retail liabilities. The utility established a PLN 304 million provision for onerous G-tariff contracts in December 2025. This expanded calculation incorporated indirect costs including net-metering prosumer distribution fees, contract servicing, and invoicing expenses. Energa gradually utilized PLN 161 million of this reserve during the first nine months of 2026 to offset retail losses.
11.
Severe exposure: PGE faced the largest absolute exposure to retail margin compression among all Polish state-controlled utilities. The group established provisions for onerous contracts at PGE Obrót totaling PLN 436 million in 2025. PGE's generation unit PGE GiEK carried an additional provision of PLN 766 million. If the upcoming 2027 tariff approvals fail to fully cover procurement costs, PGE will be forced to expand these provisions and drag down its 2027 EPS.
12.
Severe crisis: Polish power companies have historically operated under strict state-mandated pricing caps that created massive retail sector imbalances. Energa reported that statutory interventions severely depressed its EBITDA during 2024 and 2025 due to frozen household tariffs. The gradual unfreezing of these prices in late 2025 finally allowed the retail division to begin restoring its operating margin. However, the legacy of these price caps is a highly cautious provisioning policy across all state-backed utilities.

Unresolved regulatory timelines

13.
Pending decisions: The final regulatory determinations for the 2027 fiscal year remain outstanding as of November 15, 2026. Morgan Stanley reported on October 7, 2026, that the pending URE decisions add significant upside risk to Poland's inflation outlook. The central bank continues to monitor these regulatory pricing steps as critical inputs for its near-term monetary policy stance. Until URE issues its official 2027 approvals, the final financial impact on utility earnings remains highly speculative.
14.
Macroeconomic factors: The Polish central bank's rate decisions are closely tied to the upcoming regulatory tariff announcements. Bank Pekao reported that household electricity prices remained frozen until late 2025, which helped contain consumer price index growth. If the URE approves sharp tariff increases for 2027, inflation is expected to accelerate in early 2027. This macroeconomic feedback loop puts immense political pressure on the regulator to restrict the 2027 tariff levels.
15.
Other side of the story: Polish utilities could avoid massive onerous-contract provisions if the regulator approves more generous retail G-tariffs for 2027. While historical decisions forced companies to absorb significant losses, the unfreezing of retail energy prices allows for a normalization of supplier margins. If the URE aligns its 2027 approved tariffs with current market wholesale rates, the need for new balance sheet provisions will be minimized, allowing PGE and its peers to experience a strong earnings recovery.
