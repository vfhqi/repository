The eleven ratings for TT Electronics

TT Electronics: a repair 40% complete with profit beats ahead; the shares have priced the first step of the re-rating and wait for the second at the 10 March 2027 results

Written 25 October 2026 for Richard. Share price 186p (22 October 2026); market capitalisation £347m; enterprise value £402m; about $1m of shares traded a day; DBAY Advisors holds 29.04%. Sources, bases and definitions are in the end notes.

0Summary: the case in one page

The bottom line

The risk/reward on TT Electronics is skewed to the positive: my central case returns +37% to December 2027, the probability-weighted return is about +34% (an expected value of about 246p against 186p), and the -34% downside case carries less than a fifth of the probability.

  • Why the upside is credible: the next two profit prints are more likely to beat consensus than miss it, and the return comes from earnings growth rather than from a re-rating.
  • Where the risk sits: in the entry point, not the business. The shares have nearly doubled in a year, sit at the 93rd percentile of their own five-year forward P/E range and trade only about $1m a day.
  • The decisive date: the FY2026 results on 10 March 2027 test cash conversion, the dividend and the tax rate. A clean print earns the second step of the re-rating, from my 12.5x anchor toward the 13.3x guideline multiple.
  • The likely end state: a sale of the company, because sector precedent, a balance sheet heading to net cash and a 29% holder that has already blocked one bid all point that way.

What TT is now. TT Electronics is a £480m-revenue engineered-electronics group that has spent eighteen months repairing itself and is about 40% of the way through a recovery in margins and cash; the share price has already rewarded the first part of that recovery. The group has three product divisions. Power is the profit engine: £206m of revenue at about a 16% margin, two thirds of it aerospace and defence. EMS (electronics manufacturing services) is the repair project: £196m of revenue at a 5% margin rising to 8%. Components is for sale: £79m of revenue at a 2% to 3% margin. The group's adjusted operating margin was 7.7% in FY2025 and 8.1% in H1 2026, and the model takes it to 9.3% in FY2027 and about 10% by FY2029.

The case in five points.

  1. The next two profit prints are more likely to beat consensus than miss it. FY2026 operating profit is half banked, and the second half rests on dated self-help items. A £550m order book covers almost all of FY2027 revenue. Our EBIT forecasts of £41.5m for FY2026 and £47.0m for FY2027 sit 4% above consensus, and the range of likely outcomes around the FY2026 forecast is narrow.
  2. EPS grows much faster than profit, from 6.9p in FY2025 to 15.5p in FY2027 and 17.9p in FY2028, because interest costs fall and the adjusted tax rate drops from 57% through 36% toward 29% as North America turns profitable. The falling tax rate is the largest single EPS lever, worth 15% to 20% of earnings, and it is the one line on which consensus is already ahead of the company.
  3. The share price already reflects the first step of the recovery. At 186p the shares sit on the consensus price target, at the 93rd percentile of their own five-year forward P/E range and at roughly their ten-year median. The near-term risk is therefore a de-rating triggered by a sentiment event, not an earnings downgrade triggered by a fundamental one.
  4. The return is earnings-led. Viewforth's guideline fair multiple for a stock in the Broadening phase of its lifecycle is 13.3x two-year-forward earnings, 33% above today's 10x. At 13.3x the model returns +46% to December 2027 and +62% to June 2029, mostly from earnings growth. My own anchor is 12.5x until the March 2027 results prove clean margins and cash, and 12.5x still returns about +37%.
  5. The most likely end state for the shares is a sale of the company, not a long compounding path. Three facts point that way: the sector's precedent, a repaired balance sheet heading to net cash, and a 29% holder (DBAY) that blocked one bid at 150p and has bought more shares since.

Where I differ from the market. I am above consensus on operating profit, below consensus on FY2027 EPS, and in line with consensus on revenue and cash. I am above on FY2026 and FY2027 operating profit because no broker models the site-level operating leverage at the Cleveland and Kuantan factories. I am below on FY2027 EPS because consensus embeds a 26% to 27% tax rate that the company has not guided. On valuation, I am more cautious than Viewforth's lifecycle guideline on the multiple for the next twelve months, and more constructive than Jefferies' Hold rating on the earnings path.

The skew: fundamentals are positive over twelve months, sentiment at the entry point is negative, and the March 2027 print resolves both. The fundamental positives are profit beats, a reinstated dividend, a disposal and a possible step down in the tax rate. The sentiment negative is that TT is a re-rated, thinly traded small cap in a market regime that punishes exactly that profile. I weight the outcomes as follows: a base case at 55% to 60%; a correlated execution downside at 15% to 20% (margin back toward 7.5%, FY2027 EPS of 11p to 12.5p, shares at 120p to 130p); and an upside at 20% to 25% (Components sold for £30m or more, recognition of the US tax losses, a 17% Power margin, shares toward 300p).

1Tailwinds in the operating environment, and the absence of headwinds

Defence is the one tailwind that is both structural and already in the order book; restocking is a pulse that fades by mid-2027; and the headwind that matters most hits the share price multiple, not profits.

Evidence. Three demand forces are live and additive over the next twelve months.

Aerospace and defence: a funded structural upswing
A third of group sales and two thirds of Power. The UK is raising defence spending to 2.5% of GDP by 2027, France has committed EUR 36bn to 2030, and European defence book-to-bill was 135% in 2025. TT is designed into platforms (Boxer, CV-90, Challenger 3, Typhoon) that run for 15 to 30 years. Since the period end TT has signed a lifetime agreement with Rolls-Royce, received a letter of intent from MBDA and won a $10.3m classified programme from Collins.
Electronics restocking: real and already in TT's orders
Group book-to-bill is 1.12x. Components order intake rose 26% in H1 2026 while Components revenue rose 5.8%. Peer book-to-bills are above 1.0, distributor inventory is back at its ten-year median of 56 days, and lead times are extending (multilayer ceramic capacitors at 30 weeks against a normal 12).
Healthcare: stabilised
Healthcare (22% of sales) has stopped falling.

Three headwinds offset these forces: the macro regime (a US ten-year yield near 5.1% and a risk-off market that "refuses re-ratings"); tightening input costs (memory chips, printed circuit boards booked into 2028, and mature-node integrated circuits on 40 to 52-week lead times); and US compliance costs (Executive Order 14415 from 1 January 2027, and cybersecurity scoring under National Institute of Standards and Technology rules).

Inference. The tailwinds are broad but individually modest, and they differ sharply in how long they last. Defence has more than two years of visibility and is the only force that is both structural and already in the order book.

Restocking is a pulse of six to eighteen months. The sector expects restocking to plateau between late 2026 and mid-2027 (mid-2027 is the component maker Yageo's own estimate of when inventories saturate). XP Power's update on 20 October has already shown how the pulse ends: orders up 93% including £18.7m of pull-forward, reported revenue down, and the shares down. The gap between TT's Components orders and Components revenue is the same signature on a smaller scale.

Two smaller external factors matter less. Tariffs are neutral at the profit line because TT's contracts pass duties on to customers. Sterling drifting to $1.31 to $1.34 is a mild tailwind to reported profit, at £0.1m per cent.

So what. The external forces add roughly 3 to 5 points of organic growth across H2 2026 and FY2027, and 2 to 4 points in FY2028, which matches the model's 5.0% and 4.0% growth. Because restocking is front-loaded, the model front-loads growth too (H1 2027 at +6.5%). The external force that bites hardest does not touch the income statement at all: it bites the multiple, because small, thinly traded, recently re-rated stocks are fragile in today's market regime.

Strongest counter-case. The restocking pulse may reflect distributor positioning rather than end demand, and defence revenue may follow the timing of awards rather than budgets. On restocking, 42% of distributors are pre-buying (up from 29%), and the pulse could reverse in H2 2027 as it did in 2024, when distribution revenue fell 27% organically and TT's North American profit fell 62%. On defence, Solid State's revenue swung from £163m to £125m to £154m, Kitron's Q3 defence order intake was 30% below consensus, and the US continuing resolution funds the government only to 11 December.

Where I differ from the market. The sell-side cites the 26% growth in Components orders approvingly as momentum, but no broker has stress-tested it as pull-forward. I treat the pull-forward as a FY2028 risk worth about one point of group revenue. I hold that risk as a skew rather than changing the base case, because a Components disposal (55% probability within 18 months) would remove it from the group. On defence I share the market's enthusiasm, but I allow for a half-year phasing risk of £5m to £10m of revenue in either direction.

What would change my view. Components order intake falling below Components revenue before mid-2027, which would mean the air pocket is arriving early; discoverIE's December interims disappointing on orders; or, positively, MBDA's letter of intent converting into dated production orders.

Score: 58. A reliable structural tailwind (defence) and a time-limited cyclical one (restocking) arrive together, offset by a macro regime that punishes the equity rather than the business.

2Internally driven corporate change forces, and the absence of problems

Corporate change is the strongest part of the case and the part the model can rely on most: the margin has already risen 230bp. Its limit is that it is a repair programme with no growth leg.

Evidence. Seven of the eight markers of corporate change are present and dated.

People
CEO Eric Lakin (acting from April 2025, permanent from August 2025); CFO Ian Ashton (from June 2026, formerly at SIG); Chair Phil Swash (from May 2026, formerly at BAE Systems, Airbus and GKN), who bought 100,000 shares at 119p; and Mary Waldner as Senior Independent Director and Audit Chair (from August 2026).
Structure
Regional management replaced by three product divisions (April 2026).
Footprint
The Plano site closed within two months of the decision, removing an annual loss of £5.7m to £6m. Mexicali closed. Suzhou's work moved to Kuantan on plan, at a temporary revenue cost of £10m.
Factory repair
The Cleveland site has been consistently profitable in H1 2026 and has won three new customers and six new products after a three-year drought.
Cost
A net saving of £3m in FY2026 and more than £6m annualised from FY2027, self-funded and "substantially complete".
Incentives
The short-term incentive plan pays 50% on operating profit and 25% on free cash flow. The long-term incentive plan (LTIP) pays on EPS growth and on 95% cash conversion.
Balance sheet
Net debt and covenant leverage have fallen sharply (tiles below), and the going-concern uncertainty has been removed.
Portfolio
The review of Components has drawn "an encouraging number of indications of interest".
Guidance
Raised in September 2026, for the first time in three years.

Inference. The first £5m of margin improvement is banked before any volume recovery, because the adjusted operating margin has already risen from 5.8% in H1 2025 to 8.1% in H1 2026. The programme is a repair, not a growth plan: capex is 1.8% of sales against peers near 3.5%, R&D is 3.8% of sales against XP Power's 12%, acquisitions are parked, and no new markets are targeted. The repair should lift the margin from 7.7% toward 9.5% with high confidence; it supports growth above 4% only with low confidence.

So what. The internal items alone explain the EBIT path from £37m in FY2025 to £45m to £47m by FY2027, without any help from demand: Plano gone, Cleveland profitable, £6m of savings, Kuantan restored and Power's delayed contracts landing, helped further at the EPS level by a lower interest charge. Most of the pay-off lands in two half-years (H2 2026 and H1 2027), which makes the forecast both testable and fragile.

The problems inside the programme. Six weaknesses remain.

  • H1 2026 cash conversion was 42%, on a £12.7m build in working capital, so the full-year target of 70% to 80% depends on H2.
  • Power's margin fell 120bp to 14.2% because customer agreements were delayed.
  • Kuantan is "yet to achieve full-rate production" (Jefferies, 1 October).
  • The new CEO, CFO and Chair have not yet worked together through a difficult half.
  • Deloitte still runs a fully substantive audit with no reliance on internal controls, a legacy of the £5.7m restatement in 2023.
  • DBAY, with 29%, holds an effective veto over any transaction.

Strongest counter-case. Sector precedent says that rework and yield crises re-emerge in demand upswings, and TT's own 2024 failure was triggered by a volume step-up at Cleveland and Kansas City. The thesis therefore needs TT to maintain an existing capability under load, not to build a new one, and the first test of that comes in H2 2026.

Where I differ from the market. The sell-side has fully captured the turnaround headline, the Power phasing and the disposal. It has not modelled how much extra revenue at Cleveland and Kuantan drops through to profit: consensus's 23% incremental margin is a residual, not a thesis. That site-level leverage is where I sit above consensus on profit. I agree with the market that the business lacks a growth engine.

What would change my view. A trading update before March 2027 (each of the last three profit warnings came in an unscheduled September or November update); an H2 2026 EMS margin below 7.5% on rising revenue, which would repeat the 2024 pattern of volume arriving with execution cost; or Cleveland productivity flattening below 81%.

Score: 72. Unusually broad, dated and already-delivering corporate change for a company of this size, held back from the top decile because it is a repair programme with no growth leg.

3Value chain, business model, financial profile and management

TT's position is protected but not privileged: qualification keeps its customers, but a sub-scale place in the weak middle of the value chain caps its margins and growth, and makes TT a takeover target rather than a consolidator.

Evidence. TT holds no number-one or number-two position in any market it reports. In military and aerospace power it is a tier-two supplier behind XP Power, Advanced Energy and the defence primes' in-house capacity. In European electronics manufacturing services it is a mid-tier player among more than 2,000 providers; Cicor became the number four partly by buying TT's own former sites. In passive components it is a sub-scale maker that customers routinely use as a second source, with allocations below 20%.

The industry is shaped like an hourglass, and TT sits in its narrow, weak middle. Suppliers upstream are concentrated and earn high margins: the top ten connector makers have grown their share from 38% to 61%, and printed-circuit-board and semiconductor makers earn gross margins of 35% to 40%. Customers downstream (the defence primes) are also concentrated. The middle, where TT operates, is fragmented and prices are soft.

Consolidation favours scale and low-cost footprints, and TT is the target, not the consolidator. Volex has reached $1.24bn of revenue through twelve acquisitions bought at 5.2x EBITDA; Cicor is growing 19% to 28% a year; and discoverIE is deploying £95m at 9x EBIT. Volex and Cicor have both tried to buy TT, and TT's former Chair wrote that "insufficient scale has structurally restricted" the company.

Qualification, not market share, protects TT's customer base. TT co-engineers its products into platforms at the design stage, and a customer needs 18 to 24 months to qualify a replacement supplier. TT's sites hold the accreditations that aerospace (AS9100D), medical (FDA and ISO 13485) and US defence export (ITAR, the International Traffic in Arms Regulations) work requires. Customer churn has fallen from 30% to about 10%; every top-ten customer relationship is more than ten years old (Ultra PCS at 35 years); no customer exceeds 10% of sales; and TT lost no major customer through the 2024 factory crisis. New sovereign-supply and critical-minerals rules raise the qualification bar further. The moat kept TT's customers in 2024, but it did not protect TT's margins: North America fell into loss because its fixed costs were under-absorbed.

TT's financial profile is below its peers' on every measure, though improving. TT's EBITDA margin is 10% to 12% against discoverIE's 17% and Volex's 13% to 14%, and its capex is about 2% of sales against 3% to 4% for peers (chart below). TT's return on invested capital is 13.3% (company figure) against Volex's return on capital employed of 21%, and free cash flow is 0.6x to 0.9x net income on consensus against discoverIE's 0.9x. Returns on tangible capital are plausibly 25% to 35%, because £93.6m of North American goodwill has been written off; that figure is my hypothesis, not a disclosure.

TT's earnings base is not yet clean, on three counts: adjusting items of more than £180m over three years (falling to about £10m to £12m in FY2026); an adjusted tax rate of 36% to 57% against a 25% underlying rate; and non-recurring profit inside adjusted figures (£3.5m of last-time-buy profit at Plano in H2 2025).

Management has a strong recent record on top of a weak institutional one. Eric Lakin has delivered every milestone on the date he gave and has beaten expectations three times. He is a finance-and-restructuring executive (formerly at Smiths Interconnect and CFO of Ceres Power) who had not previously run a standalone business. He holds shares worth 21% of his salary against a 200% guideline, and has made no purchase in the open market. Management's ambition is deliberately the smallest in the peer set: 4% to 6% growth and an undated 12% margin target, against Volex's $2bn revenue target and discoverIE's tenth upgrade of its margin target. The sell-side calls TT's management "honest trackers", which is both the compliment and the ceiling.

Inference. The value-chain structure holds Power's margin in the mid-teens, caps EMS in the high single digits and leaves Components exposed to the distribution cycle. The group margin is therefore a question of mix and execution, not of structure. TT cannot build a flywheel in which scale lowers costs and lower costs win share, so its growth comes one design win at a time, which caps organic growth near the market's 4% to 6%.

So what. TT's competitive position supports a 9% to 10% margin once self-help completes and the mix shifts, and supports 4% growth; it supports neither a premium multiple nor growth faster than the market. The assets have proved one important thing: they were under-exploited. Cicor recovered 84% of what it paid for TT's three European sites within nine months and doubled their margins. That shows how much headroom TT's remaining sites have under disciplined ownership, and it shows why a buyer would want them.

Strongest counter-case. The bull case is that TT after the Components disposal would be a far simpler and better business. Power would be half the revenue and most of the profit, EMS would be the fix, 70% to 80% of sales would sit behind qualification barriers, and a technology roadmap (silicon carbide, silver sintering, AX-FORCE) would keep TT designed into the next generation of platforms. I accept the direction of that argument; I do not accept that it earns a higher multiple before it is delivered.

Where I differ from the market. The market sees TT as the cheap turnaround with a blockholder, which is accurate. I would add that the gap to Volex and discoverIE in scale and returns widens each year TT spends repairing. The standalone case therefore works only over the next two years; after that, the sale thesis takes over.

What would change my view. A dated 12% margin target with a bridge, set out at a capital-markets event in 2027; a Cleveland programme win large enough to be named; or a second footprint exit, which would show that management will accept disruption to fix the structure.

Score: 42. Real but narrow moats inside an unfavourable value-chain position, a below-peer financial profile that is improving, and competent rather than outstanding management.

4Conservative guidance, expectation management and the delivery record

Management guides low and raises only after a half-year is banked, so consensus EBIT is a floor for FY2026 and FY2027. The exception is tax, where consensus is ahead of the company.

Evidence. TT's guidance is specific about building blocks and silent about outputs.

FY2026
Adjusted operating profit "ahead of market expectations" (2 September 2026), raised in words after H1 profit beat consensus by 12%. The company-compiled consensus range was £32.6m to £38.5m; consensus has since moved to £40.0m, the top of what the wording implies.
FY2027
No guidance. The £45.0m consensus rests on brokers extrapolating the savings and the order book.
Medium term
A 12% margin (undated, after the original "by 2026" was withdrawn), 4% to 6% growth, cash conversion of 90% or more, leverage below 1.5x, and a dividend decision in March 2027.
Building blocks given
Savings of £3m and then £6m; cash conversion of 70% to 80%; a tax rate in the "mid-to-high 30s"; £0.1m of profit per cent of movement in the sterling-dollar rate; capex of £10m to £13m.
Outputs not given
Revenue, margin and divisional guidance.

The delivery record is short and good under the current CEO, and long and poor before him. The long-term incentive plan reveals management's own ceiling: full vesting requires EPS growth of 5% to 13% a year from a base of 12.0p (adjusted for deferred tax), which puts management's maximum stretch at about 17.3p by FY2028, at or below both our forecast and consensus.

Inference. Management's pattern is to set guidance at consensus, hold it, deliver, and raise it in words once a half-year is banked. That pattern is why consensus keeps lagging delivery in an upturn, and why TT's surprises follow the direction of the cycle: beats in the 2021 to 2022 restocking and misses in the 2023 to 2025 destocking, both amplified by operating leverage and site execution. The cycle is now turning up.

Tax is the one line on which the company's guidance is less conservative than the street's forecasts. The company guides a high tax rate with no date for it to normalise, while consensus FY2027 EPS of 15.9p on £39.0m of pre-tax profit implies a rate of 26% to 27%.

So what. Consensus EBIT is a floor for FY2026 and FY2027. The H2 building blocks point to £41m to £43m of FY2026 EBIT against consensus of £40m, and £46m to £47m for FY2027 against £45m. The most likely sequence of guidance over the five company events to April 2028 is hold, meet-and-beat, hold, raise, meet (timeline below). I put the probability of at least one further raise within 18 months at about 60%, and of a cut at about 15%.

A comparator trap will make H2 2026 look weaker than it is. H2 2025 profit included £3.5m of non-repeating last-time-buy profit from Plano. My H2 2026 profit forecast of £23.0m will therefore read as a 3% fall against the reported £23.7m, but a 14% rise against the clean £20.2m. The analysts covering TT know this; investors reading the headline will not.

Strongest counter-case. A short clean record sits on top of a long poor one. TT's own history says that a volume shortfall combined with a site failure produces a 15% to 30% downgrade within a quarter, and the two sites carrying the extra volume (Cleveland and Kuantan) are the two whose utilisation TT does not disclose.

Where I differ from the market. I give full weight to the recent record and put FY2026 and FY2027 profit 4% above consensus; the two brokers with Hold ratings (Jefferies and RBC) give the record less weight. For the out-years I side with Jefferies rather than the rest of the street: management's own EPS stretch target is no higher than consensus, so the FY2029 consensus (from two brokers) is not conservative.

What would change my view. A numeric FY2027 profit range below £45m in March 2027; a dated 12% margin target, which would signal that the Chair wants a higher bar; or a November update that stays silent while the profit run rate is visibly above £42m, which would mean management is holding back more than I assume.

Score: 60. Honest, mechanism-rich, number-light guidance that lags delivery by design, a strong short record on top of a poor long one, and one quiet line (tax) where the street is ahead of the company.

5Pricing power, operating leverage, financial leverage, cost-out scope and feedback loops

High operating leverage and a falling tax rate make EPS grow far faster than revenue for about three years. Pricing power is near zero and the cost programme is spent, so the reinforcing loops run out after FY2028.

Evidence. Six mechanisms set how revenue converts into earnings.

Cost structure
Materials are about 55% to 60% of revenue (variable); direct labour and factory overhead 18% to 20% (semi-fixed within a half-year); SG&A 14% to 16% (fixed). Central costs are £7.5m to £7.7m.
Operating leverage
A marginal pound of revenue drops 30% to 40% through to profit at a site with spare capacity and 15% to 20% at new capacity; the model uses a blended 25% to 30%. TT has shown the leverage in both directions. Deutsche Bank calculated an 88% drop-through on the 2024 loss of North American revenue (a ceiling, because it included scrap and write-downs). The 230bp margin gain in H1 2026 came on falling revenue, from removing loss-making fixed capacity.
Pricing power: near zero
80% to 90% of contracts pass costs through on an index, with a lag of three to six months. TT recovered £40m of inflation in 2021 to 2023, but £28m of it at zero margin. Real price rises of 1 to 2 points a year come only in Power, at contract renewal; customers demand annual price cuts of 1% to 2% in standard EMS and Components. Over four years, price added a net 20bp to 40bp to margin, which is inside the noise.
Financial leverage
£75m of 3.65% notes maturing in 2028 and 2031, and a £105m revolving credit facility with £8.3m drawn; 84% of debt is at fixed rates. Interest falls from about £8m in FY2025 to £5.5m in FY2026 and £3.2m in FY2030, worth about 1p of EPS by FY2028.
Cost-out scope
The £6m programme is the last announced saving, and £3m to £4m a year of wage, compliance and energy inflation will consume it within two years. Further site closures are possible (TT has eight UK sites, with revenue per site in the bottom quartile), but each costs £4m to £7m in cash and is "extremely difficult" because customers must re-approve the products.
Tax
TT has £282.4m of gross unrecognised tax losses (£225.1m in the US, with no expiry). Cash tax therefore runs £3m to £5m a year below the income-statement charge, and the adjusted tax rate falls mechanically from 36% toward the high 20s as the losses shelter US profits, before any decision to recognise them as an asset. Formal recognition (a credit of up to £18.7m plus £7.9m, outside adjusted profit) would settle the rate near 25%.

Inference. Four reinforcing loops dominate the next 18 months, because they are mechanical, dated and already visible. The site-utilisation loop is the core of my above-consensus profit forecast. The US tax loop is the largest EPS loop, but it depends entirely on North American profit recurring.

Five balancing loops take over from month 18 to month 48. The most important is that growth absorbs working capital, at 16p for every £1 of revenue growth, which caps cash conversion at 85% to 95% in growth years.

The net result has three parts: operating profit compounds at 10% to 12% a year to FY2028 and then slows to 5% to 6%; EPS compounds faster, at 17% a year to FY2028, because two of the loops act below operating profit; and cash lags both.

So what. For about three years, revenue growth produces outsized profit growth and even faster EPS growth; then the loops run out and the business flattens into a 10% margin growing at 3.5% a year. That is the shape of a repair, not of a compounder, and the model carries it honestly. Operating leverage also widens the range of outcomes: a 5% swing in FY2027 revenue moves profit by £6m to £8m and EPS by about 3p, and drop-through is higher on the way down than on the way up. Selling Components would narrow the range, because Components has the shortest visibility and the highest gearing of the three divisions.

Strongest counter-case. TT's last growth year (FY2022: revenue +29.6%, profit +19%) showed weak drop-through on the way up, because cost pass-through and price lags diluted it; the high drop-through TT has demonstrated was on the way down. The mitigant is that the volume for the next two half-years is already in the order book rather than in a demand forecast.

Where I differ from the market. No broker models the drop-through per pound at Cleveland and Kuantan, or quantifies how the US losses shelter profit before formal recognition. The market treats the tax rate as an accounting footnote rather than a forecast line. I think the tax rate is the second-largest driver of the share price, after the H2 2026 margin.

What would change my view. An H2 2026 EMS margin above 8.5% would confirm the operating-leverage mechanism; a margin below 7.5% would show execution costs absorbing it. The FY2026 annual report's statement on the five-year plan and deferred tax will decide when the tax loop pays out.

Score: 64. High operating leverage, now working upward, plus a discontinuous tax lever, offset by near-zero pricing power and a cost programme that is already spent.

9Riskiness and simplicity of the forecasts and expectations

The case is more concentrated than it looks: North America sits behind three of the five drivers and about half of the EPS uplift, and at today's entry point the sentiment risk is larger than the fundamental risk.

Simplicity of the case. Five drivers explain about 80% of the gap between the forecast and the past: volume absorption at Cleveland and Kuantan, the £6m cost programme, Power phasing and mix, the tax rate, and the interest line. The five drivers look diffuse but are concentrated. Cleveland volume, North American profitability and the tax rate are one driver showing up on three lines, and that one driver (North America) carries about half of the 11p of EPS uplift to FY2028. The cost programme and interest are near-mechanical and independent of demand; Power and Kuantan depend on customer timing and are independent of each other.

Almost all of the gap lands in two half-years (H2 2026 and H1 2027), which makes the case testable by September 2027 but fragile to a slip of one quarter.

Fragilities and requirements. The forecast needs six things to go right (figure below), and the evidence on each is mixed.

  1. Kuantan reaching full-rate production without disrupting the customer whose work moved there. XP Power's Malaysian commissioning took twelve months, and the +6.5% growth forecast for H1 2027 is the single most Kuantan-dependent number.
  2. Cleveland holding 81% productivity as volume rises. Rising volume is exactly what triggered the 2024 failure.
  3. Two to three years of US profitability to deliver the tax path. The auditor has twice declined to underwrite it (derecognising £16.0m and then £2.7m of deferred tax assets), and precedents (Weir, Elastic, Vernalis) suggest it takes about three years to emerge from a cumulative-loss position.
  4. Cash conversion while growing, which TT has never shown.
  5. Power's delayed contracts landing in H2 2026 at a margin of about 16%. The former CFO warned in March 2026 that Power margins would soften in 2026 because of mix.
  6. A Components sale struck at fair value and structured so that the US loss pool stays with the continuing group. An asset sale keeps the losses; a share sale of BI Technologies and Optek would carry Plano's historic losses away. A further, unquantified tail risk is that cumulative ownership change approaches the 50% threshold in Section 382 of the US tax code, which would restrict use of the losses; DBAY's 12.5-point stake build counts toward that threshold.

How suddenly the price risks would arrive, and how well they can be tracked. Two kinds of risk threaten the share price, and over twelve months the sentiment risk is the larger (figure below).

  • Fundamental risk is a correlated failure of site execution (Kuantan, Cleveland and a Power slip together). It would arrive as an unscheduled trading update between September and November, the pattern of all three previous warnings.
  • Sentiment risk is a de-rating of 20% to 35% with no change to earnings (section 11 sizes the trough). Its triggers are dated: discoverIE in December 2026, and in March 2027 a cash print below 70%, no dividend, or the H2 comparator optics.
  • DBAY can trigger a sentiment move at any time. A holding above 30% would force DBAY to bid for the company; selling part of the stake would swamp a stock that trades $1m a day; and a bid rejected by DBAY would cap the shares, as the Cicor episode did.
  • Trackability between results is poor. TT has no quarterly reporting and no site-level disclosure, and the November trading update exists by precedent, not policy. The company's silence is the most informative signal it gives.
  • Prevalence: flows out of UK small caps and fiscal risk-off episodes add a channel of risk that has nothing to do with TT.

Sector and cyclical precedents. TT amplifies the cycle through operating leverage and site execution. In the 2021 to 2022 restocking, revenue rose 10% and then 30% and TT beat forecasts. In the destocking that followed, revenue fell 15% and then 8%, TT issued three warnings, consensus EPS was cut 57%, and the forward P/E fell to 3.5x in March 2025. Peers one year into a site-led recovery (XP Power in 2025 to 2026, Volex in 2017 to 2019, and Kitron) more often than not met or beat operating consensus and missed on cash and timing, with the misses coming in the second year. An August 2026 sector note argued that strong cash conversion on falling volume is the signature of a weak business; that applies to TT's 150% conversion in FY2025, and the reverse test, conversion on rising volume, is now running.

Inference and so what. TT's forecasts are most predictable where they matter first (FY2026 EBIT, within about 5% either way) and least predictable where they matter most to the share price (tax, cash and the multiple), which is the inverse of what a P/E-based price assumes. The case is simpler than TT's own history (consensus FY2026 EPS has ranged from 8.7p to 23.4p in 18 months) and simpler than Solid State or XP Power at the same stage, but less simple than discoverIE or Volex. The disposal would make it simpler on every count except TT's concentration in Power.

Strongest counter-case (to my caution). The shares have risen 99% over two years on TT's own news, the drivers are observable twice a year in the divisional figures, and the failure mode has a known early indicator. A case with a single point of failure that can be watched is less risky than one with many failure points that cannot.

Where I differ from the market. The market has fully priced the doubts about cash and Power phasing. It has not priced the Components pull-forward, or the tax rate that it is itself assuming. I think the sentiment risk exceeds the fundamental risk over twelve months, and both resolve at the same print.

What would change my view. A quantified disclosure of Kuantan and Cleveland utilisation in March, which would narrow the range of outcomes materially; a dated margin target; or DBAY's intentions becoming known.

Score: 40. A moderately concentrated case that depends on two half-years and on site execution, with a wide range of outcomes below operating profit and an entry point exposed to sentiment; mitigated by observable drivers and a known failure pattern.

★The five judgements that move the shares most, with the arithmetic

Judgements 1, 2 and 5 move EPS; judgements 3 and 4 move the multiple. A plausible miss on any one EPS judgement costs 5% to 8% of EPS, but the correlated failure of all three costs 25% to 30% of EPS and 35% to 45% of the share price.

Each judgement is sized as a change to FY2027 adjusted EPS and to the share price at 12.5x the resulting two-year-forward earnings. The FY2027 EPS base is 15.5p, built on £47.0m of operating profit, £5.2m of interest, a 32% tax rate and 183.5m shares; each 1p is about 6.5% of EPS.

1. The site-level drop-through at Cleveland and Kuantan, tested by the H2 2026 EMS margin. The model assumes 25% to 30% drop-through on about £24m of FY2027 revenue growth. If the upward drop-through is only 15% (the FY2022 pattern), FY2027 operating profit is about £3m lower, at £44.0m (an 8.7% margin, close to Jefferies' £44.6m). EPS then falls by £3m × 0.68 / 183.5m, about 1.1p, to 14.4p (-7%), and at 12.5x the lower earnings path values the shares at about 235p rather than 254p. In a harsher case, with revenue 5% below the model (£479m) and the 30% drop-through applying on the way down, profit falls £7.5m to £39.5m (an 8.2% margin), EPS falls 2.8p to 12.7p (-18%), and the multiple would not hold at 12.5x. Indicator: an H2 2026 EMS margin above 8.5% confirms the judgement; a margin below 7.5% refutes it.

2. The adjusted tax rate, which is North American profitability on one line. The model's 32% rate for FY2027 is the midpoint between the direction of the company's guidance and consensus. At 35%, the extra 3 points on £41.8m of pre-tax profit cost £1.25m, about 0.7p, taking EPS to 14.8p (-4.5%). At the 27% that consensus implies, EPS gains £2.1m, about 1.1p, to 16.6p (+7%). If recognition of the US losses settles the rate at 26%, EPS is 17.0p (+10%). The FY2028 gap is wider: 35% against the model's 29% on £46.7m of pre-tax profit is £2.8m, about 1.5p, taking EPS to 16.4p against 17.9p (-8%).

Judgements 1 and 2 fail together, because a relapse in US profitability would also remove the Cleveland absorption in judgement 1. That correlated downside means FY2027 operating profit of about £36m to £41m, EPS of 11p to 12.5p and a multiple nearer 8.5x, giving about 120p to 130p a share. Indicator: the FY2026 annual report's statement on the five-year plan, and the FY2027 tax guidance in March.

3. Cash conversion and working capital, which decide how the March 2027 results are received. Each point of working capital as a share of revenue is about £5m of cash. If FY2026 working capital ends at 17% of revenue rather than 15%, free cash flow is about -£2m rather than +£8m, net debt about £47m rather than £36.5m, covenant leverage about 1.1x rather than 0.8x, and cash conversion about 50% rather than 73%. The EPS effect is trivial: £10m of extra debt at the blended interest rate, after tax, costs about 0.15p. But the dividend would probably be deferred again, and the multiple would take the hit: a fall from 12.5x to 9.8x on unchanged earnings is -22%, about 55p a share. Indicator: FY2026 cash conversion against 70%.

4. The Components disposal, which changes quality and the multiple, not EPS. At £30m net, FY2028 revenue is £444m and operating profit £47.5m to £48.5m, a 10.7% to 10.9% margin (up 90bp to 110bp). The £2.5m to £3m of lost profit is offset by £1.2m of interest saved, after £1m of stranded cost, so EPS is roughly unchanged at 17.5p to 18.0p. Net cash arrives by the end of FY2027, and the dividend steps up to 3p to 4p. The value lies in the multiple: the Senior precedent (a disposal, then a re-rating from 18.6x to 25.5x) and the removal of the highest-gearing, shortest-visibility division argue for at least one extra turn of P/E, worth about 20p a share on 20p of forward earnings (+11%). The risks are a cheap sale into a restocking upturn (Jefferies values Components at £11.5m on 5x EBITA) and a share-sale structure that carries the US losses away. Indicator: a regulatory announcement setting out the perimeter, price and structure.

5. Power's margin and phasing, which show whether the profit engine is steady. Power is £220m of FY2027 revenue at a 16.0% margin. Each point of Power margin is £2.2m of profit, about 0.8p of EPS (5%): a 15.0% margin gives 14.7p, and 17.0% gives 16.3p. A half-year slip of £5m to £10m of awards, at a mid-teens incremental margin, moves a half-year's profit by £1m to £2m and the annual figure by less. It moves the narrative by more, because Power is the anchor of the thesis. Indicator: the H2 2026 Power margin against 16%, and the Rolls-Royce and MBDA frameworks converting into dated production orders.

Taken together. A plausible miss on judgement 1, 2 or 5 alone costs 5% to 8% of EPS. Their correlated failure costs 25% to 30% of EPS and 35% to 45% of the share price. Their joint success (30% drop-through, a 27% tax rate and a 17% Power margin) gives about 17.5p to 18p of FY2027 EPS (+15%) and, with the disposal in judgement 4, the upside column of the return table.

13Summary conclusion

TT Electronics is a business whose repair is about 40% complete, and whose next two profit prints are more likely to beat than miss. Its EPS should compound at about 25% a year from a trough to FY2030, for reasons that are mostly internal, dated and already visible. The shares have re-rated once on the first of those reasons and now wait, at the consensus price target and the top of their own five-year range, for the second.

The case rests on three things the company controls (the H2 2026 margin, the FY2026 cash print and the Components disposal), one thing it half controls (the tax rate, which is North American profitability by another name), and one thing it does not control (the multiple a risk-off market will grant a thinly traded small cap).

Over twelve months the fundamentals are skewed positive and sentiment is skewed negative, and the results on 10 March 2027 resolve both. The model's +46% at the guideline multiple becomes +37% at my 12.5x anchor and +34% probability-weighted after an honest downside. In all three cases the return is earnings-led, which is the right kind of return.

Beyond September 2027 the reinforcing loops slow, the balance sheet turns to net cash, and the story becomes what a 29% holder and the sector's precedent suggest it will become: a sale. The window for the standalone case is the next two years, and its first and biggest test is five months away.
