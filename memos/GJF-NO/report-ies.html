Gjensidige Forsikring (GJF-NO): what the Q3 2026 results do to the existing case

These results weaken the existing case modestly: the margin delivery is better than the case assumed, but the growth differential that carried the case's strongest rating has gone and Norwegian retention now reads below the level the case itself set as its tripwire.

1. What changed

Reported on 23 October 2026, for Q3 2026:

  • Underwriting far ahead. Combined ratio 73.6% against 79.7% in Q3 2025 and a consensus of 78.5%. Insurance service result NOK 3,003.6m against a consensus of NOK 2,530m, an 18.72% beat. The group underlying frequency loss ratio improved 5.7 percentage points to 60.1%, 2.9 percentage points better than consensus. Large losses were NOK 235.6m against NOK 387.7m a year earlier, and the company acknowledged benign weather and no major catastrophe events.
  • Growth sharply lower. Gross written premiums grew about 1% (0.90% currency-adjusted). Insurance revenue of NOK 11,367.4m missed consensus of NOK 11,799m by 3.66%; adjusted growth was 5.3% against roughly 10% in the first half. Commercial gross written premiums fell 3.7% reported and 1.7% currency-adjusted. Private Norway revenue still grew 12.1%. Sampo grew 5.9% in the same quarter.
  • Retention drifting. Private Norway retention was 89.1%, against 89.3% the quarter before. Norwegian commercial retention was 90.3%, against 90.7%. Danish retention fell; the review gives no figure. The company's target is above 90% in Norway through 2028.
  • Below the line. Investment result negative NOK 192.9m against a consensus of negative NOK 68m. Pre-tax profit NOK 2,781m, 7.75% ahead. Earnings per share NOK 4.08 against NOK 4.00. Sweden's combined ratio was 92.0% against 78.9%. Solvency II ratio 190.3%.
  • Sell-side response was muted. Jefferies stayed at Hold, target NOK 279 from NOK 272, 2026 earnings per share unchanged at NOK 15.68, 2027 up 4%. DNB Carnegie stayed at Hold, target NOK 265, 2026 adjusted earnings per share up 3% to NOK 14.90. Norne stayed at Buy, NOK 315. Morgan Stanley stayed Underweight, target NOK 276.20 on 10 November 2026. On 6 November 2026 the count was eight Buy, nine Hold, two Sell.

Against this, the case assumed premium growth of 10.6% against a peer median near 4.5%, Norwegian retention at 91%, and ten Buy ratings of seventeen. It also carried a Danish workers' compensation charge of DKK 500-800m as its key risk; the review carries no such charge, and shows a Q2 2026 combined ratio of 78.9% and pre-tax profit of NOK 2,790m.

2. The challenge

Reading one: the margin is being harvested, and the pricing driver is finished. The case treats Norwegian pricing power as one force that delivers growth, margin and retention together. These results split them. Margin rose because past rate increases are earning through; growth fell because new rate increases are now deliberately smaller and the company is letting commercial customers leave. On this reading Q3 is the high point of the margin, because the earn-through ends within about a year while claims inflation continues at the guided 3% to 6% for motor and 4% to 6% for property. The case's own first fulcrum driver, which it weights at about 40% of forward earnings growth, then stops contributing. For this to be right, two things would have to be true: the underlying frequency loss ratio stops improving once pricing merely matches claims inflation, and the new distribution agreements, two of which only start in early 2027, do not replace the lost volume. Private Norway revenue growth of 12.1% is the evidence against it, though that figure is itself mostly earned-through price.

Reading two: the inertia the case counts as the moat is the exposed asset. The case rates disruption as a distant vehicle-maker threat and says there is no disintermediation risk. The market spent September and October pricing a different threat: the shares fell 12% from the end of August on fears that AI shopping agents automate renewal re-shopping. The strongest form of the argument is that a book with roughly 90% retention, where only about 26% of customers negotiate or switch each year, has the most to lose if switching friction falls, and has no distribution cost to cut in compensation. One broker puts the effect on 2028 earnings per share at 3% to 15%, with a 49% fall if Nordic combined ratios move to European levels. For this to be right, agents would have to clear the Norwegian authorised-adviser requirement and customers would have to use them. The company and two brokers call the fear overblown. The case has no view on it at all, which is the gap.

Reading three, in the other direction: the margin step is real and the case's "largely priced" judgement is now too harsh. The underlying loss ratio, not just large losses, beat consensus by 2.9 percentage points. Brokers barely moved 2026 estimates on an 18.72% underwriting beat, so they are treating most of it as non-recurring. The share price is NOK 282.4 (13 November 2026) against NOK 283.40 when the case was written, so none of it has been paid for. If the underlying ratio holds through a normal winter, the company's target of a combined ratio below 81% for 2027 and 2028 is far too cautious, and the possible capital release from internal-model approval (the review carries two estimates, about NOK 3.8bn and about NOK 7bn) sits on top. For this to be right, the 5.7 point improvement must be mostly price and risk selection, not weather. Tryg and Alm. Brand both posted record results in benign weather in the same quarter, which is why we cannot yet tell.

Readings one and three cannot both be right, and the same number decides between them: what the underlying loss ratio does in a normal-weather quarter.

4. What would settle it

  • The second review, four working days on. It will carry settled broker estimates and targets. Much of that response is already visible, so we expect limited change. It will carry no new company disclosure, and so cannot settle any of the three readings.
  • Q4 2026 results, probably late January on last year's timing. Three numbers: Private Norway retention against the case's 90% level and the 87% level one broker names as thesis-breaking; the Private Norway underlying frequency loss ratio in a winter quarter; premium growth once the earn-through fades.
  • The retention basis. A like-for-like history of the 89.1% series would tell us whether the tripwire was crossed or was never comparable. That is answerable now from company reports.
  • The Danish charge. Whether it was booked, where and how large. Also answerable from company reports.
  • Internal-model approval. The company has committed to no date. This is an open question nothing scheduled will answer.
  • AI agents. No disclosure settles it. Retention is the only observable proxy, and it lags.
