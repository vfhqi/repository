Bear Case Credibility Assessment The bear case against MONY Group plc demonstrates moderate empirical credibility rooted primarily in factbased operational and regulatory evidence rather than speculative market concerns. The most concrete structural threat emerges from the ongoing HMRC VAT dispute, where rejection of the Partial Exemption Special Method has created GBP 4 million in provisions and persistent cash flow drag without an agreed recovery basis.

This represents a documented balance sheet liability verified through audited financial statements. The secondary empirical pillar rests on verifiable competitive traffic erosion, with SimilarWeb analytics confirming that Compare the Market has expanded its monthly traffic advantage to 1.6x over MONY, signaling genuine market share pressure. The third fact-based concern centers on persistent gross margin compression, falling from 80% in FY15 to 64.4% in FY25, driven by escalating Google pay-per-click search marketing costs that grew 21% year-onyear during recent periods.

However, the widely cited agentic AI disintermediation threat remains highly speculative and opinion-based, lacking immediate empirical proof despite Morgan Stanley's December 2025 downgrade to Equal-weight. Multiple brokers, including Jefferies, contest this narrative by highlighting massive regulatory and data barriers that position price comparison platforms as essential partners rather than casualties of AI evolution.

The bear case therefore exhibits a bifurcated quality profile, with concrete operational and regulatory risks carrying substantial weight while existential technology disruption fears remain low-plausibility near term. Sell-side downgrades by Deutsche Bank, Morgan Stanley, and Jefferies reflect elevated structural skepticism, with revised price targets ranging from 130p to 205p representing 29% to 42% downside from recent trading levels.

Net Currency Exposure Verdict The net currency position for MONY Group is essentially neutral, representing neither a material tailwind nor a meaningful structural headwind to investment returns.

The company maintains no active currency derivatives and instead relies on natural sterling matching, where the domestic cost base provides a 95% natural hedge against sterling-denominated revenues. The currency exposure integrates into the pre-mortem framework through the failure narrative of margin dilution, where accelerated technology infrastructure spending correlates with unhedged dollar SaaS licensing inflation. However, this risk remains materially subordinate to the dominant structural threats of pay-per-click search cost inflation and regulatory pricing intervention, which operate at far larger economic scale.

The lead referral business model prevents direct pricing pass-through of technology cost increases to consumers, limiting management's ability to offset dollar strength through revenue adjustments. Peer comparison reveals that MONY shares a highly insulated sterling profile with domestic UK classifieds platforms Rightmove and Auto Trader, while continental marketplace peers like Scout24 deploy active hedging programs to manage 83.6% hedge ratios over 24-month horizons.

The verdict therefore concludes that foreign exchange exposure poses low structural threat to MONY's earnings trajectory, with the dominant 100% domestic revenue stream preserving core business cash flows regardless of global currency volatility.

Outliers worth noting

Across the comprehensive bear case assessment, pre-mortem analysis, and currency exposure research, three extreme statistical outliers and structural anomalies emerge that materially diverge from established peer base rates and historical norms for digital comparison platforms. The first outlier is the persistent gross margin compression trajectory that has driven MONY's gross margin from 80% in FY15 to 64.4% in FY25, representing a 1,560 basis point contraction over the decade.

This sustained erosion pattern stands in sharp contrast to online classified peers that typically maintain stable or expanding gross margins as operational leverage builds with scale. The underlying driver– Google pay-per-click search cost inflation growing 21% year-on-year –represents an external dependency relationship that compresses the gross margin from historic peaks of 80% down to current levels. The magnitude and persistence of this contraction cannot be explained by temporary cyclical factors and instead signals a fundamental structural shift in the competitive economics of customer acquisition for price comparison platforms.

The second outlier centers on the HMRC VAT dispute and the Special Override Notice, where VAT provisions reached GBP 4 million in 2025, representing a 47% increase compared to GBP 3 million in 2024, with an additional GBP 2 million provision recognized during the first half of 2026. The regulatory withdrawal of a previously sanctioned methodology represents an exceptional administrative enforcement action that creates material balance sheet uncertainty absent from peer financial comparison platforms.
