Appendix

Plausible problems to monitor

Outliers worth noting

The investigation of Altarea uncovered several statistical anomalies and unusual corporate events that place the company in the extreme tails of sector distribution. The exceptional scrip dividend subscription rate of 78.5% achieved for the FY25 dividend represents an unusually high level of shareholder commitment to equity retention, substantially exceeding the typical scrip uptake rates observed across European real estate companies, which rarely surpass 50% without major financial distress.

This outlier is directly attributable to the coordinated commitment from the Taravella family and Crédit Agricole Assurances to take 100% of their dividends in shares, preserving €110 million in corporate equity during a cyclical downturn. The magnitude of this shareholder alignment is highly atypical in the sector and provides Altarea with an extraordinary capital preservation mechanism unavailable to peers. [Cross-ref: Section 4] The operating margin expansion from 9.8% in FY24 to 14.5% in FY25 during a period of severe revenue contraction represents a counter-cyclical anomaly that diverges sharply from peer behavior.

While diversified developer peers like Nexity saw margins compress to 0.8% and Icade's development margin collapsed to 1.9% in H1 2026, Altarea achieved margin expansion exceeding 470 basis points despite a 25% revenue decline. This performance places Altarea in the top percentile of European real estate developers for margin resilience during downcycles, driven by the accelerated shift toward higher-margin new-generation low-carbon residential projects that reached 72% of housing revenue by H1 2026.

The structural margin improvement contradicts typical sector cyclicality patterns and suggests a fundamental product repositioning rather than temporary operational efficiency gains. [Cross-ref: Section 4, Section 7]

The 400 MW Citadel data center campus partnership with Vantage Data Centers represents an extreme scale outlier for property developers entering digital infrastructure. The secured power grid connection of 400 MW substantially exceeds typical colocation data center projects in the European market, which typically range between 10-50 MW, and positions Altarea at the forefront of hyperscale campus development alongside specialized infrastructure investors.

This strategic pivot into hyperscale digital infrastructure is highly unusual for a traditional residential and retail property developer and introduces operational complexity, execution risk, and capital deployment patterns that diverge fundamentally from Altarea's historical business model. The dependency on securing final user contracts to monetize the massive grid connection investment creates binary outcome scenarios rarely observed in conventional real estate development. [Cross-ref: Section 2, Section 9] The consolidated LTV ratio of 29.5% achieved in H1 2026 places Altarea in the bottom quintile of leverage among European diversified real estate companies, representing an exceptionally conservative capital structure relative to peers.

Commercial REIT peer Gecina operates at 38.5% LTV while maintaining investment-grade ratings, indicating that Altarea maintains leverage approximately 900 basis points below comparable institutional property companies. Combined with the exceptional Interest Cover Ratio of 9.9x that provides a 395 basis point cushion over the 2x covenant minimum, Altarea's financial conservatism represents a statistical outlier that suggests either significant balance sheet inefficiency or strategic preparation for counter-cyclical acquisition opportunities. This extreme financial positioning is highly atypical for developers operating through severe residential market contractions and contradicts the sector norm of elevated leverage during distressed cycles. [Cross-ref: Section 4] The massive inventory write-down of €448.8 million in FY23 represents an extreme tail event that placed Altarea among the most severely impacted European property developers during the 2023-2024 interest rate shock.

This impairment magnitude exceeded 16% of the FY23 revenue base and drove reported net income to negative €20.85 per share, representing one of the largest single-period asset devaluations among French diversified real estate companies. While peers like Nexity and Kaufman & Broad experienced margin compression and volume declines, the scale of Altarea's inventory revaluation suggests either unusually aggressive prior-cycle land acquisitions or exceptionally conservative revaluation methodologies that diverge from sector norms.

The writedown created a statistical reset point that makes historical earnings comparisons unreliable and positions subsequent FFO recovery trajectories as potentially misleading measures of operational improvement versus accounting base effects. [Cross-ref: Section 5, Section 11]
