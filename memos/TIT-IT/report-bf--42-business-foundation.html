Section 1 – Business Model + Organisation 26 findings

Section summary
  1. Telecom Italia has pivoted to a lean, service-oriented architecture (ServCo) post-NetCo separation, leveraging high-growth enterprise ICT and profitable Brazilian operations while relying on a strategic distribution partnership with Poste Italiane to stabilize consumer market share.

Core Business Units and post-NetCo Structure

1.
Corporate perimeter definition: Following the landmark €14.20 billion debt-reducing sale of the NetCo fixed- line network to KKR on July 1, 2024, Telecom Italia (TIM) structurally transitioned into "ServCo," a horizontal service provider completely stripped of primary and secondary wireline network assets
2.
Segment asset ownership: The remaining corporate structure is organized around three independent, self- sufficient operating business units consisting of TIM Consumer, TIM Enterprise, and TIM Brasil
3.
TIM Consumer core operations: TIM Consumer serves Italian households, private individuals, and small-to- medium enterprises (SMEs) with fixed and mobile voice and data services
4.
TIM Enterprise market focus: TIM Enterprise operates as a digital sovereignty champion dedicated to large corporations and public administrations, integrating connectivity with high-margin IT solutions such as cloud, cybersecurity, and IoT
5.
TIM Brasil strategic engine: TIM Brasil (67.00% owned) operates as a highly profitable emerging market mobile powerhouse, leveraging consolidated three-player dynamics to drive sequential cash flow and EBITDA expansion
6.
Post-split operational mechanics: Post-separation, the relationship between TIM and the independent network owner (FiberCop/NetCo) is strictly governed by a 15-year Master Service Agreement (MSA), renewable for an additional 15 years, operating at market prices with no minimum volume purchase commitments
7.
Structural and margin realities: Deutsche Bank's Keval Khiroya notes that ServCo's post-NetCo transition

results in a lower EBITDA After Lease (EBITDAaL) margin profile because the company now rents wholesale capacity, leaving margins highly sensitive to opex and high-margin revenue fluctuations.

Revenue Model, Deferral Accounting and IFRS 5 Classifications

8.
Geographical and segment revenue split: For FY2025, Telecom Italia reported total group revenues of €13.73 billion, with the Domestic segment contributing €9.54 billion (69.48% of total) and Brazil contributing €4.22 billion (30.73% of total)
9.
Domestic revenue mix: Within the domestic perimeter, TIM Consumer recorded revenues of €4.50 billion, while TIM Enterprise rose to €3.50 billion, demonstrating a structural mix shift toward Enterprise ICT services
10.
Sparkle IFRS 5 classification: Telecom Italia Sparkle, the international submarine cable business, was officially classified under IFRS 5 as "Discontinued Operations/Assets Held for Sale" starting in 2025 following a €700.00 million binding offer from the Italian Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF) and Retelit
11.
Wireline cost deferral transition: At year-end 2025, TIM implemented a key accounting policy change, reducing the amortization/deferral period for wireline contract acquisition and subscriber provisioning costs from 8 years to 4 years. - Earnings Calls Amortization adjustment impact: This shift was prompted by the sale of the fixed network, meaning customers are no longer tied to physical lines but to a service ecosystem, resulting in a non-cash prospective P&L charge of €710.00 million (€628 million at Domestic BU level) in 2025
12.
Cyclicality and predictability: While TIM's B2C retail revenues are highly predictable due to monthly recurring subscription structures, the domestic segment faces moderate cyclicality in enterprise IT spending, offset by secular digital transformation waves
13.
Accounting change evaluation: Deutsche Bank's Keval Khiroya highlighted that the transition from 8 to 4 years for wireline contract acquisition costs will trigger a €600.00 million drag on 2025 reported EBITDA but has zero cash impact, ultimately improving the alignment between EBITDA and cash generation

Strategic Partnerships and the Poste Italiane Transaction

14.

15.
Poste Italiane's ownership entry: Following an extraordinary board resolution on December 21, 2025, and subsequent shareholder approval on January 28, 2026, TIM converted its savings shares into ordinary shares, which resulted in Poste Italiane becoming the largest shareholder with a 20.10% stake
16.
Capital increase mechanics: To serve the exchange offer, Poste Italiane’s Board of Directors resolved on July 7, 2026, to increase its share capital by €371.99 million via the issuance of 371,986,879 new ordinary shares
17.
Synergy and platform integration: The combination of the two groups targets €0.70 billion in annual run-rate pre-tax synergies (€0.50 billion in cost and €0.20 billion in revenue), merging TIM’s telecom services with Poste Italiane's 13,000 post offices and "Super App" digital platform
18.

Integration feasibility assessment: Analysts at Jefferies noted that the €500.00 million cost synergy target, representing approximately 5.00% of TIM’s cost base, is highly feasible and could find further upside if Poste Italiane leverages its capillary branch infrastructure to optimize the network footprint

Plausible problems to monitor

Telecom Italia's standalone domestic EBITDA After Lease margin of approximately 21.00% sits materially below the European peer median, with Orange delivering 31.00% and Telefonica achieving 34.00%, revealing that the post-NetCo ServCo transition has structurally compressed profitability relative to vertically integrated incumbents. This margin weakness stems from the wholesale-dependent business model that converts historical network capex into high, variable operating lease payments to FiberCop, leaving quarterly earnings extremely sensitive to minor retail revenue fluctuations.

The competitive positioning is further undermined by an estimated domestic Return on Invested Capital of only 3.70%, which lags the European sector average and turns negative when incorporating multi-billion-euro spectrum costs, as detailed in Section 9.

The impending December 2029 spectrum renewal deadline represents a material cash flow cliff, with 62.00% of Telecom Italia's total spectrum holdings expiring simultaneously alongside 70.00% of all active Italian spectrum. Historical Italian spectrum auctions have been among the most expensive in Europe, with TIM spending €2.40 billion in 2018 at inflationary pricing of €0.035/MHz/pop/year for the 700MHz band, and under a single-upfrontpayment structure, the company faces a potential one-time cash outflow of €2.60 billion for a 15-year license, which would entirely erase the domestic free cash flow turnaround trajectory outlined in Section 2. FiberCop's operational performance reveals concerning structural headwinds, with legacy ADSL and voice lines projected to decline from 2.30 million in 2025 to 1.60 million in 2026, representing annual line losses of 0.70 million connections.

Critically, FiberCop opted against paying dividends to KKR and the Italian government in late 2025, retaining cash internally to support capital-intensive fiber buildout before the 2027 completion goal, signaling that the upstream wholesale infrastructure partner prioritizes its own balance sheet over shareholder distributions. This dividend withholding contradicts management's narrative of stable, cash-generative wholesale relationships and exposes ServCo to elevated counterparty risk, as analyzed in Section 7. Cash flow generation quality remains severely impaired, with group Operating Free Cash Flow collapsing to a minimal €14.00 million in Q1 2026 from €144.00 million in Q1 2025, while Equity Free Cash Flow deteriorated to negative €276.00 million from negative €89.00 million.

Stripping out the highly cash-generative TIM Brasil segment reveals an Italian domestic business with elevated lease-adjusted leverage of 3.4x net debt-to- EBITDAaL and a domestic free cash flow profile not expected to turn structurally positive until 2027, leaving the standalone entity dangerously exposed to working capital volatility and seasonal payment cycles as detailed in Section 8.

Outliers worth noting

The €14.20 billion NetCo deleveraging transaction executed on July 1, 2024, represents an unprecedented corporate restructuring across European telecommunications, comprising €1.50 billion of transferred FiberCop bank debt, €5.50 billion of eliminated consolidated bonds, and €7.20 billion in cash proceeds. This transaction scale dwarfs the revenue base of comparable European peers, as TIM's total revenues of €13.73 billion sit below Telefonica's €35.12 billion and Orange's €40.40 billion, meaning the deleveraging represents more than

100% of annual group revenues –a financial engineering feat with no direct parallel among integrated European incumbents. The capital restructuring positioned the company to achieve a net debt-to-EBITDAaL ratio of approximately 1.50x by year-end 2026, transforming TIM from one of Europe's most leveraged telecommunications operators into a relatively ungeared entity. However, as explored in sections two and four, this apparent financial stability came at the strategic cost of surrendering the physical infrastructure moat that historically protected retail margins, leaving the group structurally exposed to monopolistic wholesale pricing power from the newly independent FiberCop.

Telecom Italia's TIM Enterprise division has achieved an exceptional 50.00% year-on-year organic growth in revenues from the National Strategic Hub (Polo Strategico Nazionale), serving over 650 public customers and establishing an unassailable sovereign cloud position that contrasts sharply with the commoditized consumer retail segment. While the group's domestic ServCo EBITDAaL margin of approximately 21.00% lags significantly behind Orange's 31.00% and Telefonica's 31.20% to 37.70%, TIM Enterprise's high-margin B2B ICT services now represent 65.00% of Enterprise service revenues, creating a structural profitability outlier within the group's portfolio.

The €500.00 million digital sovereignty and AI capital expenditure plan scheduled between 2026 and 2028 supports 16 integrated data centers managing over 100MW of deployed capacity, positioning TIM as Italy's de facto digital sovereignty champion. These data centers are effectively non-replicable without spending €1.00 billion to €2.00 billion in greenfield capital expenditure, creating formidable barriers to entry that do not exist in the deflating consumer mobile and fixed broadband markets analyzed in sections three and five.

This binary quality split –between the high-growth, high-margin Enterprise cloud business and the structurally challenged consumer retail operations–represents the defining strategic tension within Telecom Italia's post-NetCo corporate structure.
