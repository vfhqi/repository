HMS Networks (HMS-SE): what the Q3 2026 results, reported 21 October 2026, do to the existing case

These results weaken the existing case moderately: the business-quality view survives, but the case's own Q3 gross-margin gate came back deferred rather than answered, the false-friend pattern the memo called its most important finding repeated, and the largest division, which carried the case's data-centre demand evidence, stopped growing.

What changed

What the company reported, set against what the case assumed:

  • Q3 2026 revenue SEK1,024m, up 15% reported and 6% organic, against SEK1,032m expected by DNB Carnegie; organic growth slowed from 12% in Q2. Adjusted EBITA SEK280m at a 27.4% margin (27.1% a year earlier), against SEK284m expected.
  • Order intake SEK1,104m, up 29% reported and 22% organic, against SEK1,034m and 15% expected. Management estimated roughly SEK70m of orders were placed early ahead of longer lead times and price increases effective 1 January 2027; DNB Carnegie puts underlying organic order growth near 14%. Book-to-bill 1.09x in constant currency.
  • Gross margin 62.7%, against 64.1% a year earlier; 62.4% excluding a SEK3m US tariff refund. The case's gate was a Q3 margin above 62.5% with the memory cost already absorbed to raise conviction, below 61.5% to signal that framework agreements block pass-through, below 61% in Q3 or Q4 2026 to park. The reported figure clears 62.5% by 0.2 points, the underlying figure sits 0.1 points below it, and the condition fails: management says cost pressure has spread from memory to printed circuit boards, passive components and a broader part of the portfolio, revised tariff rules widened exposure over the summer, and pressure continues through Q4 and potentially into 2027. DNB Carnegie models a trough near 62.0% in Q1 2027 before the January price increases, which management has not sized, take effect mainly in Q2 2027.
  • Adjusted basic EPS SEK3.82 against SEK3.83 a year earlier: flat.
  • Industrial Data Solutions, roughly half of sales on DNB Carnegie's estimate: orders SEK404m, down 3% organic; revenue SEK448m, up 2% organic; management says larger project orders are still missing. The case recorded this division at plus 23% organic in Q2 on North American data-centre build-outs. Industrial Network Technology: orders SEK468m, up 70% organic, EBITA SEK108m at 31.7%, but almost all of the SEK70m pre-orders sit here. New Industries: orders up 14% to SEK232m, EBITA margin 24.7% against 26.4%.
  • Geography broadened: organic orders up roughly 40% in China, roughly 20% in Europe, just under 10% in North America.
  • Operating cash flow SEK333m (SEK258m a year earlier); DNB Carnegie estimates free cash flow of SEK291m, 55% above its forecast, with about SEK49m from higher payables that management calls temporary. Net debt to adjusted EBITDA excluding IFRS 16 of 1.52x at 30 September 2026, from 2.77x a year earlier; unused facilities SEK1,191m. Robot offer at roughly EUR21m enterprise value, settlement expected around 1 December 2026.
  • Sell-side: DNB Carnegie, the only post-results note found, cut 2026 adjusted EPS from SEK15.7 to SEK15.3 (3%) and 2027 from SEK19.4 to SEK19.2 (1%), left 2028 at SEK21.9, cut its target from SEK661 to SEK654 and kept Buy on 27.5x 2027 EV/EBIT, a multiple it had already lowered from 30x in its September preview. The case carried this house at SEK704 and 30x. The shares fell 5% on the day in the broker's account.
  • Price against the case: SEK500.0 at 21 October 2026 against a previous close of SEK541.0, so 2.3% above the SEK488.6 the case says Element E3 must be written against; below the 50-day (SEK529.85) and 150-day (SEK509.94) averages, 3.8% above the 200-day (SEK481.58). P/E 28.0x at the 48th percentile of the stock's own ten-year history; the case's multiple test was a 64th-percentile reading against the universe, a different measure, so we do not read that test as moved. The pinned letters, technical momentum A and sell-side earnings momentum B, were measured before the print and are context only.

The challenge

Three readings of these results that the case does not hold, each in its strongest form.

The gate was answered with a deferral, and the park window is still open. The case made Q3 the test of cost pass-through so that the Early-Stage Assessment would be written on a known margin rather than an assumed one. Q3 did not test pass-through; it tested the cost before the price. The price response starts on 1 January 2027, lands mainly in Q2 2027, and has not been sized. Meanwhile the cost the case had in view, memory alone at 100 to 200 basis points of gross margin, has become memory, boards, passives and re-scoped tariffs, with management having said in September that memory increases could reach tenfold. In this reading the 62.4% underlying margin is a point on a descending path, not a floor, and the 62.0% trough comes from a broker that underestimated the breadth of the problem before the release. The park trigger names Q3 or Q4 2026 below 61%; Q4 is guided as a pressured quarter and remains live. What would have to be true: that framework agreements and backlog delay pass-through, as the case itself feared below 61.5%, and that component inflation does not stabilise by year-end. Plausible on the evidence. The case cannot accommodate this because its progression logic assumes the margin answer exists at the gate; it does not, and the first clean read of price against cost is the Q1 2027 report on 15 April 2027.

The demand engine has moved to a different division, and part of it is a supply-driven wave. The case's data-centre evidence sat in Industrial Data Solutions, growing 23% organically in Q2. In Q3 that division's orders fell 3% organically and its sales grew 2%; the strength is in Industrial Network Technology, up 70% organically, where almost all of the SEK70m pre-buying sits and where the CFO says pre-ordering could continue for one or two more quarters. The short-cycle, distributor-led division that reads end demand most directly went flat; the long-cycle design-win division that reads allocation anxiety most directly surged. In this reading the headline breadth, China up roughly 40%, Europe up roughly 20%, Japanese customers resuming after restrained purchasing, is restocking and pre-buying ahead of prices and lead times, in a Europe whose industrial output remains 3% below its 2021 level and a China whose automation growth slowed to 18% in the quarter from 28% in Q2. The destocking cycle the case declared cleared has restarted in the other direction: pre-orders inside customers, safety stock inside HMS. What would have to be true: that Industrial Network Technology orders fall back in Q4 and Q1 as the pull-forward unwinds, and that the missing larger Industrial Data Solutions projects do not arrive by the 28 January 2027 report. If both hold, the demand-side reading the case graded B rests on the wrong division, and the book-to-bill warning at 0.95x, remote today at 1.09x (about 1.01x with the pull-forward stripped out, on arithmetic), comes into view in the first half of 2027. The case does not hold this because its clean test, Q2 with no pull-forward and a 1.0x book-to-bill, has been superseded by a quarter with a new pull-forward of a different kind.

The clearing the case said was behind it may be ahead of it. The memo graded the change-from-current clearing setups D because the quality was confirmed but the clear had already happened, and the trough-on-trough setup D because the operational preconditions were met in 2024 and 2025 while valuation and sentiment read the opposite way. This quarter supplies the first ingredients of a new clear: the only covering broker cut near-term estimates and its target again while leaving 2028 earnings at SEK21.9; the market sold an in-line quarter on an "as good as it gets" question; a margin trough is guided into Q1 2027; and the price has given back its run-up into the print. In this reading the next two quarters are a clearing event the case should wait for rather than a risk it should price around, and the entry point the memo was neutral on could become acceptable on the case's own terms during the first half of 2027. What would have to be true: that the trough is shallow and temporary, that underlying gross margin holds above 62%, that Q4 orders remain solid after pre-ordering, and that the share price de-rates further into the trough rather than looking through it. It is probable that the business-quality half of this is right; the timing half depends on the market, which one day's reaction cannot settle. The case does not hold it because its setup logic says the opportunity was in late 2024 and is gone.

We have not found a fourth. The obvious alternatives, that the quarter breaks the structural case or that it is simply quiet, both fail on the evidence: 2028 earnings are unchanged at the only house publishing, and the case's own gate and its own false-friend guardrail were both tested and both came back ambiguous rather than clean.

What would settle it

  • The second review, four working days on, carries what lands between now and then: any further broker notes, since only one post-results note exists today, consensus EPS and target revisions, and where the price settles against SEK488.6 and the 200-day average. It does not carry Q4 orders, Q4 gross margin, the size or acceptance of the January price increases, Industrial Data Solutions project orders, a design-win count or a recurring-revenue figure. On the two readings that matter most, it can only report that the question is still open.
  • The year-end report on 28 January 2027 answers the park trigger (Q4 gross margin against 61%), the first quarter of the pre-order unwind, and whether the larger Industrial Data Solutions projects arrived. It carries the size of the January price increases only if management chooses to quantify them, which it has not committed to.
  • The Q1 2027 report on 15 April 2027 is the first quarter with the new prices in force and the modelled trough; it is the first clean read of price against cost. Full effect is guided for Q2 2027, whose report date the evidence does not give.
  • Customer acceptance of the price increases, the tariff charge, distributor inventory and sell-through: no disclosure is committed. These are questions the company has not undertaken to answer.
  • The active design-win count (last known 1,945 at end-2023) and a recurring-revenue metric: not disclosed in Q3, no commitment. The case's designated early-warning instrument for standardisation remains unread, and the third reading's reliance on business quality rests partly on it.
  • The bolt-on deployment test (at least SEK200m during 2027) is untouched: Robot settles in 2026.
