[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = D

Thesis. WH Smith is a cause-for-concern case driven by internal change, not a recovery in demand: a high-return UK travel-concession franchise is priced at a trough multiple because its North American division, its accounts, its balance sheet and its chief-executive seat failed together in the twelve months to June 2026. At 377p the market already values North America and the Rest of the World at about nothing, which bounds the downside on an audited UK profit; what the price does not yet pay for is the clean-up, because every visible output was still deteriorating at the last reading. The modal return clears the framework's 20 percent bar at eighteen months by a hair and misses it at three years, so this is a case to park and re-examine at the FY2026 results on 12 November 2026, not one to progress.

  • The change is internal, dated and real.
    • Leo Quinn became Executive Chair on 7 April 2026 with a record of resetting Qinetiq (2009) and Balfour Beatty (2015) within a year of arrival, and his first five months produced the actions a reset needs.
    • Exits: Norway exited and four Dusseldorf stores closed in early 2026
      • about 65 store closures targeted in FY2026
      • InMotion to shrink to below 100 stores
      • 26 loss-making Las Vegas clothing stores closing
      • a franchise model outside the UK and North America.
    • Capital: a capex ceiling near GBP 90m against GBP 110m in FY2025
      • hurdle rates of a payback under three years and an IRR above 20 percent
      • the dividend suspended in April 2026
      • a GBP 106m placing at 410p on 10 June 2026
      • a leverage target below 2.0x.
    • Breadth: four of the framework's twelve internal forces are strong (chief-executive change, strategy change, perimeter reduction, standards improvement), which meets the screen's rule of three
      • the screen's red flags for a caretaker chief executive and for dangerous leverage both partly fire.
  • The external forces run the other way, and they are why the outputs have not turned.
    • Five of the seven external categories are headwinds in 2026.
    • Passengers: UK Air like-for-like sales went from +2 percent in H1 FY2026 to -1 percent in the 14 weeks to 6 June 2026 and -2 percent in the last seven of them
      • Las Vegas resort sales fell 11 percent in those seven weeks.
    • Landlords: concession fees rose 100 basis points to 22.5 percent of sales in H1 FY2026, and Avolta won a ten-year Orlando contract and a fifteen-year JFK Terminal 4 contract in 2026.
    • Costs: wage and National Insurance legislation adds about GBP 20m a year
      • Brent was above USD 120 a barrel after the early-2026 Middle East escalation.
  • 'Financial Outputs' have not turned; they were still moving against the case at the last reading.
    • FY2026 profit-before-tax guidance is GBP 75 to 90m against GBP 100 to 115m in December 2025, the North American margin target is 5 percent against 7.5 percent, and revenue growth is guided at 3 to 5 percent against 4 to 6 percent.
    • Consensus: FY2027 EPS has fallen 53 percent in twelve months to 0.386 GBP (38.6p)
      • the one-month change of +0.3 to +0.6 percent across FY2026 to FY2028 is the first uptick in the series and says only that the cutting has stopped.
    • Cash: H1 FY2026 free cash flow was GBP -61m, with a GBP 54m working-capital outflow.
    • Share count: 25.8m new shares took the count from 126.45m to about 152.3m, a 20 percent larger base on twice-cut profits.
  • The change has travelled three of the six layers of the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain.
    • It shows in the company's people, its choices and its actions
    • it does not yet show in the key operating metrics, the financial statements or the sell-side's estimates.
    • First test: the FY2026 results on 12 November 2026 are the first print that can show a North American margin at or above 5 percent, year-end net debt at or below GBP 327m and no charge beyond the flagged GBP 150m impairment.
  • What we think it earns: EPS of 38.5p in FY2026 (against 43.4p in FY2025), about 40p in FY2027 and about 50.5p in FY2029, a rate of about 9.5 percent a year that sits below the framework's 12 to 20 percent band.
    • Components: revenue adds about 3 percent a year, margin (mostly the removal of North American losses) about 3.7 percent, the lines below EBITDA (interest falling with the placing and deleveraging) about 7.4 percent, and the share count subtracts about 4.7 percent a year
      • the components investors extrapolate are the weaker two.
    • Against consensus: our FY2028 EPS of 46p is 6.5 percent above the consensus 43.2p, because the interest saving and the closure benefits are arithmetic
      • it is not further above, because the concession-fee and wage headwinds are arithmetic too and the record is five misses in a row.
  • What it costs: 377p on 2026-09-11 is 8.68 times 24-month-forward consensus earnings of 43.4p, the 9th percentile of the stock's five-year range (7.93x to 20.40x, median 12.27x) and the 22nd percentile of the last two years (median 9.75x).
    • What the price assumes: a UK-only company with no dividend and another cut possible
      • the enterprise value of about GBP 900m is about what UK Travel alone is worth at 10x its trading profit after central costs.
    • The discount is company-driven: both 2026 sell-offs were company-specific and the 20 percent discount to SSP and Avolta pre-dates the June warning, so company delivery can close it
      • the cohort floor (Lagardere at 7.9x) is where the stock already sits.
  • The return clears the bar at eighteen months and misses it at three years.
    • Valuing the shares at each horizon on a multiple of the earnings two years beyond it, 9.5x FY2030's modal 53p gives 504p at March 2028, +33.6 percent, about 21 percent a year
    • 10.0x FY2031's modal 57p plus about 15p of dividends gives 585p at September 2029, +55 percent, about 16 percent a year.
    • One-year footing beside it: 10.5x FY2029's 50.5p gives 530p at eighteen months, about 25.5 percent a year
      • 11x FY2030's 53p plus dividends gives 598p at three years, about 16.6 percent a year.
    • The sensitivity that governs: a plausibly worse drop-through from profit before tax to EPS (a ratio of about 0.60 rather than 0.67, on a 27 percent tax rate, larger minorities and GBP 4m a year more interest) takes FY2030 EPS to 44p and the return to about 7 percent a year at eighteen months and about 9 percent at three years, an F against the bar.
      • The case does not survive a worse drop-through.
  • The pattern it fits is 'Major CfC Clearing', at a D.
    • Three of the five conditions pass strongly (a bottom-decile multiple; estimates cut about half across the whole roster; guidance rebased three times with an impairment flagged)
    • the fourth fails, because the disruption to revenue was accelerating rather than abating at the last reading
    • the fifth is partial, because the price has capitulated and the guidance has not.
  • Where the chain holds and where it breaks.
    • It holds from the new leadership through its choices and its actions, and in the arithmetic of the interest saving and the closed formats.
    • It breaks at the operating metrics: like-for-like sales, the North American margin and the UK Travel margin were all falling at the last reading, and the framework's guidance is to assume a concern is more persistent than it looks.

  • The case is therefore a parked working hypothesis with a bounded downside.

    • It becomes compelling only if the November print shows the North American margin at or above 5 percent, leverage at or below 2.0x and no reset beyond the flagged impairment
    • it becomes bullish at a price below about 340p, where the three-year modal return clears the bar.

  • Rating = D, because the input change is large but the outputs are still moving against it, so the change is material in intent and not yet in evidence, and the modal return clears the 20 percent bar at eighteen months only by a hair and misses it at three years.

    • Not C, which was live on the strength of the internal forces and the trough multiple, because the framework rates this Building Block on the materiality of the whole chain and the three output layers are negative.

Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.

  • The case against, at its strongest, is the risks digest's "false friend": UK Travel's resilience is a false confirmation that masks structural decay in the US concession economics, supplier-income acceleration and working-capital timing flattered both profit and cash, and the June 2026 placing is the first rescue rather than the last.
    • UK margin: the UK Travel margin itself fell to 8.7 percent from 10.4 percent in H1 FY2026 and its full-year expectation was cut to 13 to 14 percent, so the UK is not immune.
    • Cash: trade payables fell GBP 89m and free cash flow was GBP -61m in H1 FY2026
      • GBP 33m of accrued un-invoiced supplier income sat on the balance sheet before the correction.
    • Capital starvation: capex is capped near GBP 90m with GBP 30m absorbed by three refits, which the digest reads as storing up competitive decay in the concessions.
    • Scale: the group is sub-scale against Hudson with a two-to-three-point rebate deficit
      • Deutsche Bank calls its 20 percent discount to peers "fully justified" and puts the pro forma multiple at 16x if North America is worth nothing.
    • Record: five misses in a row, from FY2025 to Q3 FY2026.
  • What we concede.
    • The April 2026 kitchen-sink analysis's hypothesis that the reset was complete was refuted by the June update
    • the resorts and InMotion declines were accelerating, not abating, at the last reading
    • the April value-chain analysis's 7 percent North American margin for FY2026 was refuted within six weeks
    • the leverage ratio was bought with equity
    • our own drop-through sensitivity fails the bar
    • and the case's weaknesses cluster rather than stand alone.
    • We agree with the digest's progression verdict: park.
  • Why we hold a D rather than the F the "false friend" implies.
    • The UK Travel profit is audited and un-restated (the Deloitte review confirmed the integrity of UK accounting), its returns rose from 35 to 38 percent through FY2025, its largest estate (Heathrow Terminals 3, 4 and 5) is contracted for ten-plus years, and the enterprise value at 377p is about the value of that division alone at 10x trading profit after central costs, so the price already carries the bear's valuation of North America.
    • The rescue has GBP 550m of liquidity behind it, and every failure mode the bear names is a dated series on the monitoring table.
  • Where the case against would win: a FY2026 North American margin printed below 4 percent, Travel Essentials growth below 10 percent, or a second equity raise.
    • The "false friend" is the right hypothesis to hold until 12 November 2026
    • it is not yet the verdict, because its diagnostic test (the restated North American margin) has already been applied and the guidance now rests on 5 percent rather than 13 percent.

Summary: Written Summary of the Investment Case (not rated) (E 3, BB 4)
The case in 8 to 10 sentences (the thesis as tested), kept in full at both ends of the record (Part 1, E 3, BB 4)
  • This is a cause-for-concern case driven by internal change: a turnaround of a group whose core is a high-return, incumbent UK travel-concession franchise and whose periphery (a sub-scale North American division, a restated set of accounts, a geared balance sheet, a vacant chief-executive seat) has cut guidance three times in six months and diluted the equity by a fifth.
  • The change is the arrival of an Executive Chair with a record of resetting damaged companies, and his first five months have produced the actions a reset needs: exits, a capex ceiling, hurdle rates, a dividend suspension, a placing and a leverage target.
  • What the change has done to the outputs so far is nothing visible: like-for-like sales were still falling at the last reading, margins were guided down again on 10 June 2026, consensus EPS has fallen about half in a year, and the only positive output is a one-month uptick in estimates of less than 1 percent.
  • On the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain the change has travelled through people, choices and actions and stopped at the operating metrics.
  • Our modal path has EPS recovering from 38.5p in FY2026 to about 50.5p in FY2029, about 9.5 percent a year, and the multiple moving from 8.7x toward 9.5 to 10.5x, which gives about 21 percent a year over eighteen months and about 16 percent a year over three years against the framework's 20 percent bar, with nothing from cash for at least eighteen months and a plausibly worse drop-through that takes both figures below 10 percent.
  • It is not yet compelling.
  • It becomes compelling only if the FY2026 results on 12 November 2026 show the North American margin at or above 5 percent, the balance sheet at or below 2.0x and no reset beyond the flagged GBP 150m impairment, and the framework's guidance is to assume a concern is more persistent than it looks.
  • The price, at 8.7x 24-month-forward earnings and the 9th percentile of five years, already values North America and the Rest of the World at about nothing, which bounds the downside on the UK profit and is the reason this is a case to park and watch rather than to reject.
