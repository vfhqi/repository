Section 5 – 5G densification and technological disruption 13 findings

Section summary
  1. Pervasive 5G densification requirements support long-term tenancy growth as micro- infrastructure expansion offsets slow macro-tower rollouts and operator sharing initiatives.

Densification demand and micro-infrastructure

1.
Accelerating indoor densification: Cellnex has significantly expanded its Distributed Antenna System footprint to support 5G rollout, deploying fifteen thousand five hundred antenna nodes across high-density European venues
2.
Lagging network capacity: Deutsche Bank Research highlights that Italy's mobile network requires substantial densification because the country currently lags in 5G, necessitating seven to twelve thousand new sites in the coming years
3.
Growing guest ratios: Inwit is leveraging densification to increase its co-location density, targeting a rise from its current two point three six guests per site to two point six guests by thirty
4.
Rising power demands: Highly efficient 5G equipment remains materially more energy-hungry than legacy technologies, forcing operators like Vantage Towers to deploy on-site solar systems across four hundred ground-based tower sites to manage grid costs

Open RAN and operator bargaining shifts

5.
Standardized software orchestration: The emergence of Open RAN technology is redefining mobile architecture by separating hardware from software, allowing operators to run modular vendor-agnostic wireless networks at scale
6.
Concentrated baseband intelligence: Morgan Stanley highlights that Open RAN shifts the network intelligence away from physical tower antennas to centralized data centers, potentially altering the traditional passive hosting perimeter over the long term
7.
Gradual commercial maturity: Industry specialists at Rakuten Symphony note that while Open RAN provides long-term cost flexibility, its near-term deployment remains constrained by integration complexity and site readiness delays with key tower partners
8.
Concentrated vendor partnerships: AT&T is testing Open RAN viability through a massive fourteen billion dollar agreement with Ericsson, aiming to run seventy percent of network traffic on open-capable hardware by late twenty-six

Non-consensus deployment and street bypass

9.
Municipal street bypass: Outside the traditional tower network, mobile network operators are successfully bypassing macro towers by mounting small cells directly on publicly owned municipal street furniture such as lamp posts and bus shelters
10.
Integrated smart infrastructure: Technology partners like Far EasTone are developing multifunctional 5G smart poles that combine LED streetlights with integrated base stations, deploying nearly one hundred thousand units in Taoyuan City
11.
Shared active networks: Traditional tower landlords face emerging headwinds from active network sharing agreements, which allow operators to share active radio access networks and construct only half of the physical infrastructure
12.
Fragmented urban deployment: Industrial operators like Suyog Telematics are building dense, low-power networks closer to end-users, managing four thousand operational small-cell tenancies supported by six thousand kilometers of fiber
13.
Other side of the story: Slow consumer 5G monetization and capital expenditure constraints threaten to delay carrier densification investments, as telecom operators delay hardware upgrades until low-latency use cases generate returns
