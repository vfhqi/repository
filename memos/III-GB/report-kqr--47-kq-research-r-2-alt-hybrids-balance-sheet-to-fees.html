Section 2 – The fee-transition mechanism and valuation repricing

Section summary
  1. Structural balance sheet transition shifts asset managers from volatile investment returns to highly recurring fees, driving multiple expansion from NAV-based metrics to capital-light multiples.
  2. Transitioning platforms capture premium multiples as stable fee-related earnings scale, though mid-market players face persistent holding company discounts.

Step-by-step capital transition mechanics

1.
Revenue mix shift: In Eurazeo's 23 July 2026 interim report, balance sheet management fees fell 14% to €50m as the group reduced balance sheet commitments in its funds.
2.
Operational cost leverage: Tikehau Capital's 30 June 2026 half-year report shows management fees rose 13% to €190m, helping expand core FRE margins to reach a record 42%
3.
De-emphasizing balance sheets: Jefferies' 6 March 2026 report notes that Intermediate Capital Group's balance sheet is becoming less central as it focuses on recurring fee income.
4.
Structural separation: Jefferies' 16 July 2026 report highlights Tikehau Capital's transition to a selective capital framework creating a clearer separation between investing and asset-management functions.
5.
Premium multiple rerating: Morgan Stanley's 6 October 2026 report highlights that alternative asset managers trade on an average forward P/E of 21.7x, confirming substantial upside from feetransition structures.

Financial transition size and precedents

6.
Precedent spin-out model: Morgan Stanley's 23 January 2025 report notes Brookfield Asset Management spun out 25% of its business in December 2022 to isolate highly stable management fees.
7.
Capital-light execution: Jefferies' 26 August 2026 note explains HMC Capital's capital-light model aims to grow AUM to $50bn by recycling owned balance sheet assets into high-yielding co-investments.
8.
Cash-backed milestones: Morgan Stanley's 25 August 2026 report emphasizes HMC Capital's transition to cash-backed earnings for FY27 to eliminate volatile unrealized gains.
9.
Aggressive consolidation scale: Wendel's 26 February 2026 full-year report confirms its asset management platform reached €31bn in fee-paying AUM, supporting annualized growth targets to exceed €200m in FRE by 2026.

Section 6 – Data gaps

Section summary
  1. Opacity in distinguishing proprietary capital fees from third-party earnings and highly inconsistent valuation assumptions prevent absolute resolution of the hybrid transition thesis.

Proprietary versus third-party disclosure gaps

1.
Muddled fee structures: Evaluating Eurazeo is difficult because a significant portion of fee-related earnings stems from internal balance sheet investments rather than pure third-party capital, as Jefferies' 8 March 2023 note cautions. Analysts can resolve this opacity by monitoring segmental reporting in future interim reports to isolate pure third-party management fees from proprietary capital allocations.
2.
Divergent margin dynamics: Assessing whether smaller hybrid platforms can match the operating leverage of larger peers is challenging due to limited disclosure on fixed cost bases, as Deutsche Bank's 1 May 2026 report suggests. Conducting structured expert interviews with former chief financial officers can help isolate core fee-related margins from volatile investment income.
3.
Opaque fund holdings: Accessing granular asset-level performance data for private funds remains extremely difficult for non-investors, as a former Partners Group valuations expert explained in an April 2026 interview. Resolving this information deficit requires utilizing specialized third-party fund data aggregators or conducting direct expert interviews with former investment committee members.

Valuation and realization path uncertainty

4.
Inconsistent valuation metrics: Disclosures regarding fair value assumptions vary widely between litigation funding platforms, making direct risk comparisons difficult, as Zeus's 3 June 2024 initiation report highlights. Investors can reconcile these differences by adjusting idiosyncratic asset-class risk premiums to a standardized discount rate during peer analysis.

Unclear monetization timelines: Tracking the exact realization path for legacy assets remains highly speculative because market volatility delays exits, as Morgan Stanley's 22 July 2025 note observes. 6.
Complex leverage profiles: Disclosures on intermediate holding company debts without recourse to the parent can blur the true risk profile, as Euroland Corporate's 5 October 2026 note explains.
