[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = D

Thesis. Bravida is a demand inflection in a low-margin business, layered on a self-help recovery that is already complete and already paid for. Two external forces at once, the rate-driven Nordic construction turn and more than SEK 7 billion of data-centre contracts, have taken organic growth from -7.7% in Q2 2025 to +9.0% in Q2 2026 and consensus FY2027 earnings per share up 14.1% in six months; but at 148.0 SEK the share price has travelled further than the change, so on our own earnings path the return is negative over eighteen months and about 4% a year over three years, against the framework's 20% bar.

  • The change is external, and it is two forces acting together.
    • The cyclical force is the Riksbank's policy rate falling from 4.00% in April 2024 to 1.75% in March 2026, which has begun to restore private construction demand
    • The structural force is the Nordic data-centre build-out, which in two months put three contracts summing to over SEK 7 billion into a record order backlog of SEK 20,372 million, +21% year on year (13 July 2026).
    • Data centres were about 1% to 3% of revenue before Q2 2026
      • The three contracts run to 2028 and 2029 and are worth about 7% to 9% of revenue at the peak of delivery.
    • The cyclical turn is broad: organic growth in Q2 2026 was Finland +18%, Denmark +11%, Sweden +8%, Norway +5%, and the cohort peer Instalco printed +8.4% against -2.8% a year earlier.
  • The internal change is real, modest, and mostly in the base rather than ahead of it.
    • Denmark went from a 0.1% EBITA margin in Q2 2024 to 5.1% in Q2 2026
    • Three Swedish divisions were merged for SEK 65 million of annual savings
    • ABEKA was sold
    • A new CFO, a new Head of Sweden and a new Head of M&A arrived
    • The first buybacks began.
    • There is no chief executive change and no guidance increase, so the internal forces are a C and the external forces carry the Building Block.
  • 'Financial Outputs' have inflected on every line except cash.
    • Adjusted EBITA rose 20% year on year in Q2 2026 (SEK 452 million against SEK 378 million)
    • The adjusted margin was 5.9% against 5.4% in the quarter and flat at 5.9% on a twelve-month basis
    • Cash conversion fell to 77% on a last-twelve-month basis against a decade's average of 99%, because milestone contracts hold working capital.
  • The change has travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain.
    • It shows in management's stated pivot from a defensive stance to growth (the Q4 2025 call, 18 February 2026), in the central project office that won the contracts, in organic growth and backlog, in the financial statements, and in the sell-side's estimates, whose earnings momentum is pinned A.
    • Nothing in the chain is missing
    • What is missing is room in the price.
  • The business converts volume into volume, not into margin, so the earnings growth is good rather than major.
    • The adjusted margin has stayed inside a 5.2% to 6.9% envelope for ten years across a 20% swing in revenue
    • 9% organic growth in Q2 2026 bought 50 basis points of margin in the quarter and none over twelve months.
    • Our modal path is earnings per share of SEK 5.88 (FY2025, brokers' basis) to SEK 8.70 (FY2028), 14% a year, inside the framework's 12 to 20% band and about 5% below consensus at FY2028 (SEK 9.19), because we take the margin to 6.8% and consensus takes it to 7.1%, above anything the company has delivered.
  • What it costs is the problem.
    • The shares have risen 70.3% in twelve months to 148.0 SEK (2026-09-28), 97% above the 52-week low of 75.17 SEK, and trade at 16.5x consensus earnings 24 months forward: the 74th percentile of the stock's own ten years, the 78th of five and the 99th of two, and 23% above Instalco's 13.4x on similar growth and a lower margin.
    • The premium is company-earned (the data-centre narrative, the estimate momentum, the balance sheet), which means it is the company's to lose on a single miss.
  • The return therefore fails the bar on every combination in the evidence short of a mania multiple on earnings above consensus.
    • Valuing the shares at the stock's ten-year median multiple of 14.5x on earnings two years beyond each horizon, the modal return is -2.4% a year over eighteen months and +3.7% a year over three years
    • With the multiple held at 16.5x it is +6.1% and +7.9% a year
    • On consensus earnings extended at 7% a year after FY2028 at the held multiple it is about 9.7% a year.
    • All of these are F or D on the framework's anchors.
    • The bar clears on our path only at or below about 108 SEK over eighteen months.
  • The pattern is 'Demand Inflection', and the fit is loose for a structural reason.
    • The demand condition and the dominant-position condition hold
    • The three conversion conditions (one-to-many scalability, an oligopoly, fixed-cost leverage) are absent in a labour-and-subcontractor installer with a 15.2% gross margin.
    • The same absence is why the margin component of the earnings path is the one we trust least.

  • The case is therefore a good business story whose price has consumed it.

    • The change is material
    • The return at this price is not.
    • We decline the bet at 148 SEK, and we would take the earnings-only bet at about 108 SEK.

  • Rating = D, because the Building Block's question runs from the forces to the outputs to a 20%-plus modal return, and the chain holds at the first two links ('Change Forces' B, 'Financial Outputs' B) and breaks at the third ('Modal return' F) for a reason that is a weakness of the price rather than of the change: the market has already paid for the inflection.

    • The parts combine as a chain, so the weakest link sets the level.
    • Not F, because the same change at a price about 27% lower (about 108 SEK) clears the bar over eighteen months on our own path
    • Not C, because the modal return is negative over eighteen months, not merely short of the bar.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The strongest case against our view is the bull case in its strongest form: that Bravida is an early-stage Nordic AI-infrastructure enabler, that the SEK 7 billion is the first tranche of a pipeline hyperscalers keep filling because the Nordics have the power, the cooling and the land, that consensus FY2028 earnings per share of SEK 9.19 is too low (DNB Carnegie at SEK 9.47 with a 170 SEK target; Nordea at SEK 9.93), that the margin reaches the 7% target by 2027 as DNB Carnegie's path has it, that the multiple should sit at the stock's 'mania' 19.2x because earnings upgrades are scarce in European services and this is Deutsche Bank's "rare idiosyncratic winner", and that a balance sheet at 1.3x net debt to EBITDA funds buybacks on top.
    • The evidence for it: the Q2 2026 print itself
      • Three awards in the two months to July 2026 (the atNorth win came within a month of Green Mountain; EcoDataCenter is a repeat customer eight times over)
      • Relative strength at the 95th percentile
      • An earnings momentum pin of A.
    • What we concede: the pipeline may refill
      • The momentum is real
      • We cannot see past the three contracts any better than the bulls can.
    • Why we hold our view: even on consensus earnings extended at 7% a year after FY2028 and with the multiple held at 16.5x, the three-year return is about +32% in total (24-month-forward earnings of about SEK 11.08 at September 2029 × 16.5 = 182.8 SEK, plus SEK 12.65 of dividends, 195.5 SEK on 148.0 SEK), about 9.7% a year, still below the framework's D band
      • At the same earnings and the 19.2x mania multiple it is about 15% a year, a C.
      • The bar at this price needs both the top-decile multiple and earnings above consensus.
      • The framework tells us to treat an inflection to high growth as cyclical until proven structural, and to be sceptical about how far multiples can fall.
    • Where the bull case would win: a fourth data-centre award of SEK 1 billion or more in 2027, or the FY2028 earnings base re-set materially above SEK 9.19 by evidence of the pipeline refilling.
      • Either would re-open the case at any price.
Change Forces: are the input change forces sufficiently strong? (E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because the external forces are strong, new or intensifying and mostly tailwinds, with the data-centre demand sector-level and structural and the construction recovery cyclical with two-plus years' visibility from contracts in hand, while the internal forces are real but modest and consolidation-shaped rather than transformational.
  • External set: carries the Element.
    • The demand inflection is evidenced by the company's own organic growth (+9.0% in Q2 2026 against a consensus expectation of about 2%), by Instalco's Q2 2026 organic growth of +8.4% against -2.8% a year earlier, by policy (the Riksbank at 1.75%; Swedish defence appropriations of SEK 175 billion in 2026, up SEK 27 billion; the National Plan for Transport Infrastructure 2026 to 2037 at SEK 1,171 billion) and by contracts that run to 2028 and 2029.
  • Internal set: a C.
    • A new CFO (May 2025), a new Head of Sweden (January 2026), a new Head of M&A (February 2026), a first-ever buyback (SEK 100 million completed 9 July 2026, a second running to 19 October 2026), the ABEKA sale, and a stated pivot from defence to growth
    • No chief executive change, no guidance increase, and the largest internal improvement (Denmark) is already in the FY2025 base.
  • Propagation: to the sixth layer.
    • The sell-side has raised FY2027 earnings per share by 14.1% in six months (2026-09-25) and price targets by 31% in six months (2026-09-29).
  • Not A, because the internal forces do not reach the screen's "chief executive change plus guidance increase" shape, and the external demand has a cyclical half that the framework says to treat as cyclical until proven structural.
    • Not C, because the demand force passes the 'Demand Inflection' test at the sector level, with industry data, order books and policy behind it.

Demand: is end-market demand changing, structurally or cyclically (a swing with two-plus years of visibility)? (KQ 1, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because end-market demand is changing on both counts the framework names: structurally in data centres and defence, and cyclically in Nordic construction, with visibility beyond two years from a backlog that has grown 21% to SEK 20,372 million (13 July 2026).
  • Data-centre contracts: data centres were about 1% to 3% of the revenue mix before Q2 2026 (DNB Carnegie).
    • Since then three contracts sum to over SEK 7 billion: the SEK 4.3 billion Green Mountain joint venture in Norway running to 2029, the EUR 200 million atNorth principal-contractor assignment in Finland over two years, and the SEK 650 million EcoDataCenter contract in Sweden to Q1 2028.
    • Against revenue of about SEK 29,020 million over the four quarters to Q2 2026, that is about a quarter of a year's sales to be delivered over roughly three years
      • At a run rate of SEK 2.3 to 2.8 billion a year it is 7% to 9% of revenue at the peak of delivery.
  • Cyclical breadth: organic growth in Q2 2026 was Finland +18%, Denmark +11%, Sweden +8%, Norway +5%
    • Swedish order intake was +16% year on year in Q1 2026 and Finnish +33%
    • Building permits and construction confidence stabilised across the Nordics in late 2025 (Deutsche Bank).
  • Sector-level evidence: equipment lead times of about three years for switchgear and transformers, and 87% of data-centre operators reporting sourcing problems, describe demand outrunning supply, which is the tailwind and the headwind in one fact.
  • Not A, because residential construction (7% to 10% of sales) and southern Sweden are still weak, and the data-centre pipeline beyond the three contracts is not evidenced.

Supply side: input costs, availability, concentration? (KQ 5, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = D
  • Rating = D, because the supply side is a headwind on cost, availability and concentration at once: copper demand from data centres estimated at 760 kilotons in 2026 with power distribution 75% of data-centre copper use (Morgan Stanley)
    • Copper rising to up to 6% of data-centre capital expenditure from about 4% (Bernstein)
    • Switchgear and transformer lead times of about three years (PwC)
    • Skilled electricians and commissioning specialists scarce
    • A few Nordic wholesalers passing raw-material spikes straight through, with Lindab's Q2 2026 margin decline and Systemair's price rises showing suppliers pushing cost down the chain (DNB Carnegie, 29 July 2026).
  • Mitigants: index-linked clauses and cost-plus models on complex work
    • Central frame agreements
    • Input-price volatility moderating as inflation normalises (DNB Carnegie).
    • The lag of index adjustments behind spot prices is the mechanism by which this headwind reaches a 15.2% gross margin (FY2025).
  • Not C, because the three-year lead times land on exactly the contracts that carry the case.
Competition: is competitive intensity easing or tightening? (KQ 2, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because competitive intensity is easing at the top of the market and unchanged at the bottom: the three largest (Bravida, Instalco, Assemblin) prioritise profit over volume (ABG Sundal Collier), Instalco is running its "Instalco 2.0" margin programme (EBITA margin 7.1% in Q2 2026 from 6.4%), and the merged Assemblin Caverion Group (about SEK 41 billion of FY2025 revenue) is prioritising integration over bidding (Bernstein), while the tail of small firms still submits zero-margin bids in traditional construction.
  • M&A competition: InstallatørGruppen, private-equity backed, has made 48 acquisitions since 2023, and DNB Carnegie says it could bid up bolt-on multiples.
  • Not B, because price pressure in private commercial construction is present now (Deutsche Bank, 11 May 2026) and would intensify if the recovery stalled (DNB Carnegie's own counter-point).
Disruption: is a disruption force strengthening or being disproved? (KQ 3, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because the disruption forces cut both ways and neither is yet strong: building automation, 5D BIM and prefabrication favour scale installers, while hyperscalers' direct procurement from equipment makers is an attempted disintermediation that Bravida answers with its Specialist Package Contractor model
    • The physical trade is insulated from software disruption.

The case in 8 to 10 sentences (the thesis as tested), kept in full at both ends of the record (Part 1, E 3, BB 4)
  • Bravida is a demand inflection in a low-margin business, layered on a self-help recovery that has already been completed and paid for.
  • The number-two Nordic multi-technical installer has come out of a two-year construction trough with organic growth of +9.0% in Q2 2026 against -7.7% a year earlier, and has at the same time won more than SEK 7 billion of data-centre contracts that lift its record backlog by 21% to SEK 20,372 million and turn a segment that was 1% to 3% of revenue into something like 7% to 9% of it for 2027 and 2028.
