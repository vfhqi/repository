Section 2 – European Cigar and Cigarillo Volumes, Prices, and Market Shares

Section summary
  1. European machine-rolled cigar and cigarillo volumes experienced mid-single-digit declines in 2026, with major markets exhibiting persistent structural contraction.
  2. Competitors like Imperial Brands and local players intensified price competition, compounding the market share losses Scandinavian Tobacco Group suffered during raw material disruptions.

Market Volume Dynamics

1.
Persistent contraction: European machine-rolled cigar markets faced ongoing pressure in 2026 as volumes fell across major consumer countries. Analytical reports from Deutsche Bank on November 26, 2026, confirmed that cigar volumes across seven key European nations declined by three point six percent during the first nine months of 2026. This ongoing volume reduction highlights the severe headwinds facing manufacturers attempting to sustain top-line growth in highly regulated European territories.
2.
Moderate deceleration: Scandinavian Tobacco Group reported slightly slower rates of market contraction during the first half of 2026. According to the company's interim report published on August 27, 2026, preliminary data showed that European machine-rolled cigar volumes fell by four percent in the first six months. This shows that although the market is still contracting, the rate of decline has marginally improved from previous periods.
3.
Long-term erosion: Investment analysts expect the downward volume trend to persist indefinitely across Western Europe. Industry estimates published by DNB Carnegie on November 12, 2026, projected that European cigar and smoking tobacco volumes will remain in structural decline of three to five percent annually. This rate of decline suggests that manufacturers must rely heavily on price increases to protect profitability, though such hikes are becoming more difficult to execute.
4.
Parallel contraction: Imperial Brands also experienced substantial volume pressure across its broader European tobacco portfolio. In its full-year results presentation on November 17, 2026, the company reported that its European market volume declined by four point one percent. This rate of decline closely mirrors the four percent market contraction reported by Scandinavian Tobacco Group, demonstrating that volume pressure is a sector-wide issue.

Regional Share Shifts

5.
Gradual contraction: Scandinavian Tobacco Group experienced a noticeable reduction in its European market share during 2026. The company's interim report on November 11, 2026, showed that its rolling twelve-month volume share fell to twenty-five point nine percent compared to twenty-seven percent in 2025. This represents a significant loss of market presence that management attributed to temporary supply disruptions.
6.
Severe disruption: Supply chain bottlenecks in regional manufacturing hubs significantly damaged Scandinavian Tobacco Group's market share in major European countries. Deutsche Bank reported on August 27, 2026, that a rare raw tobacco quality issue affecting Signature cigars dragged down overall market share by fifty basis points to twenty-six point seven percent. This disruption enabled competitors to capture premium shelf space, particularly in France where the brand has its largest presence.
7.
Historic precedent: Previous ERP system implementations provide a relevant precedent for how operational issues cause long-lasting market share erosion. Scandinavian Tobacco Group reported in its 2025 annual report that its European machine-rolled cigar share declined due to inventory availability issues from SAP implementation. This historic precedent suggests that operational disruptions often have multi-quarter negative impacts on retail shelf presence before volumes can stabilize.
8.
Contrasting performance: Competitive dynamics in the United Kingdom highlighted the divergence in market share performance between major players. Nielsen retail data cited by Jefferies on October 19, 2026, revealed that Imperial Brands lost seventeen basis points of market share in the United Kingdom, while Japan Tobacco gained thirty-four basis points year-on-year. This shows that even in declining markets, agile competitors can capture share from larger incumbents through disciplined execution.

Price Elasticity Trends

9.
Subdued pricing: Price increases are becoming less effective at offsetting volume declines as consumers migrate to cheaper alternatives. DNB Carnegie reported on November 12, 2026, that Scandinavian Tobacco Group's previous strategy of mitigating volume declines with price hikes has barely been enough to mitigate recent losses. This indicates that the price elasticity of cigars has increased as economic pressures mount on consumers.
10.
High fragmentation: The competitive structure of the European cigar market creates additional pricing pressure compared to other tobacco categories. DNB Carnegie noted on November 12, 2026, that unlike the oligopoly structure of cigarette markets, the cigar market tends to be quite fragmented. This fragmentation means that cost-conscious consumers can easily downtrade to cheaper peer brands when premium prices rise.
11.
Regional pricing: Retail scan data from the United Kingdom illustrates the balance between pricing and volume contraction in Western Europe. Jefferies reported on October 19, 2026, that the Great Britain cigarette and cigar market saw a nine point five percent volume decline partially offset by six point three percent pricing and mix. This pricing power is relatively weaker than that of cigarettes, which can pass through larger hikes.

Sector Performance Comparison

12.
Stronger combustibles: Traditional cigarettes continue to exhibit superior pricing power and volume resilience compared to the machine-rolled cigar category. Philip Morris International reported in its third-quarter results on October 21, 2026, that its international combustibles segment achieved nine point one percent pricing growth year-to-date. This compares favorably to the weaker pricing dynamics in cigars, where high absolute unit prices limit pricing flexibility.
13.
Contrary evidence: Recent retail performance shows that some premium machine-rolled cigar segments can achieve significant short-term margin expansion despite volume headwinds. Scandinavian Tobacco Group reported on November 11, 2026, that Europe Branded gross margins increased to fifty point one percent in the third quarter. This demonstrates that temporary price mix improvements and regional consolidation can temporarily boost profitability.
14.
Moderate outlook: Analysis of the 2026 data suggests that the European machine-rolled cigar segment will remain a cash-generative but structurally declining business. Scandinavian Tobacco Group's Europe Branded organic sales growth of negative three percent in the first nine months of 2026 indicates that volume declines remain hard to offset through pricing. Profitability should stabilize under Focus2030, but any expansion will depend on recovering lost market share.
15.
Other side of the story: Structural volume declines may be mitigated more successfully if premium brand loyalty and specialized distribution channels insulate manufacturers from general tobacco downtrading. Deutsche Bank Research noted on November 26, 2026, that Scandinavian Tobacco Group's portfolio should mitigate near-term headwinds in the premium segment. If premium consumers maintain their purchasing patterns, the division could deliver more resilient operating margins than the broader combustibles market.

Consequently, premium brands must continuously defend their positions against these lower-priced peer alternatives to protect long-term earnings.

Section 5 – Germany's Tobacco Tax Act Amendment and Excise Outlook

Section summary
  1. Germany's new Tobacco Tax Act amendment imposes steeper excise taxes from 2027 to 2030, accelerating volume declines across major product categories.
  2. Strategic divestments of fine-cut assets insulate Scandinavian Tobacco Group from direct tax shocks while competitors face significant profit headwinds.

Legislative Shift and Tax Structures

1.
Legislative escalation: The German Bundestag approved a revised tobacco excise schedule on October 15, 2026, introducing annual tax increases over the 2027-2030 period. Specific cigarette excise taxes will rise by approximately 17% in 2027, followed by roughly 9% annually over 2028-2030. This approved schedule is projected to increase cigarette retail prices by up to 14% in 2027. Consequently, these front-loaded tax shocks will create severe volume headwinds for premium manufacturers.
2.
Favorable deviations: Germany's new legislation treats alternative tobacco products differently, providing lower tax rates for certain categories compared to traditional cigarettes. Social Democratic MP Ingo Vogel confirmed that taxes on cigars and cigarillos would not rise as sharply as originally planned in previous drafts. Conversely, water pipe tobacco and fine-cut alternatives face sharply escalating minimum tax floors under the new framework. For example, water pipe tobacco taxes are projected to rise to approximately one hundred and eighty-eight euros per kilogram.
3.
163 166 F1 Technical error: A major drafting blunder by the German Ministry of Finance prematurely enacted the Tobacco Tax Act amendment on October 21, 2026, instead of the planned January 1, 2027, date. The premature enactment created legal uncertainty because the required tax stamps at the new higher rates were not yet available. Although prices to consumers remained unchanged initially, the tobacco industry warned of significant production and delivery difficulties during the transition. The Ministry quickly initiated a corrective procedure to resolve the effective date discrepancy.

Scandinavian Tobacco Group Gearing

4.
Strategic isolation: Scandinavian Tobacco Group completed the disposal of its German fine-cut tobacco business, including the BREAK and Moro brands, to Japan Tobacco on October 1, 2026. This divested business represented approximately 4% of group revenue and 6% of earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization in 2025. By divesting these assets, Scandinavian Tobacco Group successfully eliminated its direct exposure to Germany's pending fine-cut tax hikes. Furthermore, the cash proceeds of 1.3 billion Danish kroner will help reduce company leverage to around 2.0x by year-end.
5.
Financial normalization: The completion of the German fine-cut divestment significantly improves Scandinavian Tobacco Group's leverage profile heading into 2027. Net debt to EBITDA is expected to decline to around 2.0x, bringing the company back within its long-term financial target. This cash injection could open the way for management to resume share buybacks of approximately 0.5 billion Danish kroner in 2027. Consequently, this transaction supports overall earnings per share growth through capital allocation rather than organic European volume expansion.

Competitor Views and Market Impacts

6.
Negative outlook: Japan Tobacco expects the steep German tobacco tax hikes to trigger severe market disruption across all combustibles segments starting in 2027. The company anticipates a double-digit volume contraction for the German tobacco industry in 2027 as consumers adjust to higher retail prices. Furthermore, the tax changes are expected to accelerate consumer downtrading and fuel illicit cross-border smuggling from neighboring low-tax countries. These factors will severely limit the industry's ability to grow profitability in Germany through 2030.
7.
Manageable headwind: Imperial Brands anticipates that the front-loaded January 2027 excise tax increase will create a short-term volume impact in Germany. Management expects a profit shortfall of up to 20 million pounds on adjusted operating profit in fiscal year 2027 compared to its original plan. However, the company maintains that the German market remains highly attractive and sustainable over the long term due to its large adult smoker base. The company plan balances responsible pricing against volume protection across its key premium and discount brand tiers.

The broker noted that Germany possesses high relative affordability for cigarettes, which should mitigate severe volume shocks over the multi-year tax schedule. This perspective suggests that the industry-wide volume contraction may prove less disruptive than competitors fear.

However, Scandinavian Tobacco Group's strategic exit from the German fine-cut market will protect its core operating margins from the severe tax-driven downtrading affecting peers. Earnings per share should remain supported by cost efficiencies under the Focus2030 program as the market stabilizes.
