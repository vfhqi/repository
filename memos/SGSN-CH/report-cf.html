EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

SGS has executed a wholesale governance refresh bringing proven turnaround experts to key roles, with CEO Géraldine Picaud and CFO Marta Vlatchkova implementing fully aligned executive incentives with Strategy 2027 targets, while simultaneously dismantling the complex matrix structure in favor of local accountability and delivering immediate financial gains through headquarters relocation to Baar. The company is on track to achieve Strategy 2027 targets one year ahead of schedule, driven by CHF 150 million of cost savings delivered through corporate simplification and procurement efficiencies, with CHF 115 million since 2024 already flowing through the P&L.

The acquisition of Applied Technical Services for USD 1.325 billion marks the largest transaction in SGS history, expected to deliver at least USD 30 million EBITDA synergies within three years, while sustainability revenues are targeted to reach CHF 1 billion by 2027 from CHF 400 million in 2023, with environmental services growing 17.7% in first half of 2026 and outperforming historical group organic growth rates.

SGS delivered 5.6% organic sales growth in H1 2026 aligning with medium-term targets, with adjusted operating income margin improving to 16.0% in FY25 from 15.3% in FY24 and tracking one year ahead of the original Strategy 2027 plan. The company reported net debt leverage of 1.7x adjusted EBITDA in FY25 providing ample financial flexibility, while achieving 57% free cash flow conversion surpassing its 50% strategic target.

However, leverage rose to 2.5x in H1 2026 following the historic acquisition of Applied Technical Services, and sharp appreciation of the Swiss franc reduced adjusted operating income by 6.4% in FY25 and created a forex headwind on revenues of 5.8% in H1 2026. The most recent H1 2026 earnings delivered sales of CHF 3,683 million up 7.6% year over year with organic growth reaching 5.6% and adjusted operating income margin improving by 20 bps to 15.1%, with adjusted EBITA exceeding compiled consensus by 1%, though adjusted EPS of CHF 1.58 fell short of the CHF 1.69 consensus.

High tariffs on direct US-China trade routes pose minimal risk to total group sales, while potential US tariffs incentivize brands to reconfigure supply chains to alternative countries, creating additional testing demand. North American industrial testing is boosted by construction and infrastructure projects totaling USD 8 billion, though structural shifts in major mining capital expenditure plans limit near-term exploration volumes, partially offset by double-digit growth from critical metals and battery metals exploration.

Strategy 2027 self-help savings programs deliver immediate earnings benefits offsetting global wage pressures, with management targeting at least 150 bps margin improvement chasing Intertek expected margins of 18.2% in FY26, achieving a CHF 150 million savings run rate by end-2025 with CHF 115 million savings already accounted in the consolidated P&L since 2024. Sustainability certification demand drives unprecedented organic growth acceleration with environmental testing and ESG audits under IMPACT NOW platform targeting at least CHF 600 million incremental sales by 2027, growing 17.7% in H1 2026 and outperforming historical group average growth rates, secured by European CSRD and CBAM standards mandating independent third-party sustainability audits for industrial exporters.

Management has established a highly conservative forecasting archetype that consistently de-risks execution, with the group on track to exceed cash conversion goals driven by tight working capital discipline. Plausible problems to monitor

The comprehensive analysis of SGS's operational and financial performance reveals a constellation of structural vulnerabilities that consistently underperform peer medians and sector base rates, despite the narrative momentum surrounding Strategy 2027 execution. These plausible problem to monitor cluster around three critical domains: organic growth dilution relative to specialized peers, persistent below-the-line margin erosion, and cyclical regional headwinds that materially constrain divisional performance.

SGS organic growth remains below the peer-leading rate of Bureau Veritas over the historical period, exposing a fundamental structural challenge in the group's diversified portfolio strategy. While management defends this diversification as defensive resilience, the growth gap reveals that commoditization in standard inspection services leads to price-based competition, particularly in mature European markets where SGS lacks the specialized life sciences exposure that drives Eurofins' premium pricing power.

This growth dilution is further compounded by slower high-margin division growth that may offset some of the corporate self-help initiatives, creating a structural headwind to operating leverage expansion despite the CHF 150 million cost savings program. The most insidious plausible problem to monitor manifests in persistent below-the-line margin dilution that systematically undermines reported earnings quality. Below-the-line costs remain a feature that dampens reported EPS, with restructuring charges standing at CHF 18 million in H1 2026, driven by Middle East slowdown.

This structural earnings quality issue is exacerbated by adjusted EPS of CHF 1.58 falling short of the CHF 1.69 consensus in the most recent reporting period, despite operational EBITA beats. The magnitude of this dilution is striking when compared to peer base rates: below-the-line costs inflate reported P/E, creating a persistent valuation discount mechanism that high dividend payout ratios absorb over 90% of free cash, rendering leverage semi-permanent and constraining financial flexibility for counter-cyclical capital deployment. 295 296 & Co Redburn Regional cyclical headwinds constitute the third critical plausible problem to monitor cluster, with Industries & Environment growth limited to 4.7% by Middle East conflict materially underperforming group organic growth rates.

This geographic concentration risk is amplified by delayed starts of client projects in Europe affecting clinical research and cosmetics testing, while softer trade flows in Europe offset Connectivity and products organic growth. The disposed US Consulting business further validates management's recognition of a cyclical asset with no synergies, though the disposal itself confirms historical capital allocation inefficiency and portfolio construction errors that destroyed shareholder value over multiple years.

Currency translation represents a structural rather than cyclical plausible problem to monitor that distinguishes SGS from lower- CHF-exposure peers. Sharp appreciation of the Swiss franc reduced adjusted operating income by 6.4% in FY25 and created a forex headwind on revenues of 5.8% in H1 2026, a sensitivity ratio that exceeds Bureau Veritas which maintains lower leverage at 1.1x in FY25 and operates with greater natural currency hedging through its portfolio construction.

This translation sensitivity creates a persistent reported earnings drag that requires substantially higher constant-currency operational performance simply to match peer reported growth rates, effectively penalizing SGS's Swiss domicile in a strengthening franc environment.

Operational quality signals reveal concerning patterns in human capital management and service delivery standards. Unrealistic quotas drive high turnover, risking professional service delivery quality according to expert testimony from a former lead auditor, while missing leadership and weak internal communication could weaken local market positions per expert assessment from a former SGS head. These qualitative signals suggest that aggressive cost-cutting and structural simplification initiatives may be inadvertently compromising the embedded service quality and technical expertise that constitute SGS's core competitive moat in accredited testing markets.

The leverage profile following the Applied Technical Services acquisition represents a temporary but material plausible problem to monitor relative to peer financial positioning. Leverage rose to 2.5x in H1 2026 following the historic acquisition and reached 2.5x adjusted EBITDA after the ATS acquisition, substantially above Bureau Veritas which maintains lower leverage at 1.1x in FY25 and constraining near-term flexibility for opportunistic bolt-on acquisitions or counter-cyclical margin investments.

While management targets deleveraging to 2.2x by year-end, high dividend payout ratios absorbing over 90% of free cash make debt semi-permanent, creating a structural constraint on balance sheet optimization that differentiates SGS from peers with more conservative capital allocation frameworks.
