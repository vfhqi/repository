Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Operating leverage in electrical distribution dictates that volume and pricing changes directly amplify Rexel's mid-term earnings per share expansion.

Defining the Research Scope

1.
Key Question: "how much fixed-cost operating leverage does this business model carry, how much scope is there for it to work over the next 18 months to four years, and, where it is relevant to the company's strategy, how feasible is cost elimination or reduction, both incremental and in material large cuts?"
2.
Structural operating leverage: Electrical distribution models feature high structural fixed costs in personnel and branch infrastructure, meaning that small volume fluctuations trigger amplified profitability swings. Historically, Rexel has carried high leverage on volume adjustments, which means operating margins can collapse quickly during industrial slowdowns unless management executes rapid cost-containment programs. This operating cost structure dictates that volume recovery directly determines whether Rexel can expand its earnings per share or must pivot entirely to defensive restructuring.
3.
Earnings per share growth: Rexel has demonstrated robust historical execution on its transformation plan, raising its adjusted EBITA margins from 5.0% in 2019 to a peak of 7.3% in 2022. Morgan Stanley's update on October 6, 2026, models a 13% earnings per share CAGR over the 2025 to 2028 period, driven by footprint optimization and €40 million of productivity savings. This projected growth remains highly sensitive to macro recovery, which means that any structural failure to expand margins will directly threaten consensus earnings expectations.

EPS Impacts and Value Creation Levers

4.
Pricing power resilience: Distribution margins benefit significantly from an inflationary environment where distributors successfully pass through supplier price hikes to preserve gross profit. Bernstein's report on August 13, 2026, notes that better supplier pricing and elevated copper prices support the distribution model, defending margins through economic downturns. This pricing dynamic implies that sustained productprice inflation is highly accretive to earnings per share, whereas deflation in core product categories would compress operating leverage.
5.
Productivity optimization potential: Management has prioritized self-help cost actions, particularly AI-driven call- center automation and targeted regional turnarounds, to achieve its medium-term targets. Morgan Stanley's analysis on July 28, 2026, highlights that productivity improvements could add over 100 basis points to group margins over four years, while country turnarounds could add another 100 basis points. These self-help initiatives should bolster earnings per share even in a stagnant volume environment, but slow execution could delay margin recovery.
6.
Secular demand support: Rising investment in data centers and electrification represents a crucial offset to weak residential construction markets, acting as an external driver of operating leverage. Bernstein's report on August 13, 2026, details that strong data center demand and industrial automation support positive pricing dynamics and margin expansion. This secular exposure means that specialized growth will cushion earnings per share from broader construction downturns, unless utility power constraints or regulatory delays halt project execution.
7.
Other side of the story: Serious downside risks persist if prolonged product deflation and volume contractions occur across European construction markets. Bernstein's report on October 22, 2026, warns that persisting underlying product deflation would directly impair organic sales forecasts, especially if volume growth fails to recover from past declines. In my judgment, this risk means that if deflationary pressures accelerate, self-help cost programs will merely offset organic declines rather than driving the targeted earnings per share expansion.

Section 2 – Split of Fixed and Variable Costs

Section summary
  1. Rexel's opex structure is highly fixed in the short term, with personnel and delivery representing 68% of operating expenses.
  2. Benchmarking against industry peers reveals that branch-based distributors carry structural fixed costs of 70% to 85% over short horizons.
  3. Over a 12-month period, customer service, branch staff, and occupancy costs are rigid, whereas delivery and commissions scale with volume.

Rexel Cost Structure and Claims

1.
Rigid cost structure: In conducting this comprehensive cost structure analysis across the electrical distribution sector, I identified and analyzed 18 relevant sources, covering nine major peer companies and tracking historical cost precedents. Rexel's 2025 Universal Registration Document confirms that fixed opex represents 72% of the company's total operating cost base. This high baseline means only 28% of opex is strictly variable with immediate volume changes, leaving the company heavily exposed to negative operating leverage during market downturns.
2.
Personnel cost dominance: Rexel's 2025 Universal Registration Document reveals that personnel and delivery represent 68% of total group distribution and administrative expenses. In fiscal year 2025, personnel costs amounted to €2,227.9 million or 59% of the cost base, while delivery costs stood at €349.8 million or 9% of the total. This concentration implies that any material opex reduction is highly dependent on labor adjustments, which typically face regulatory and structural friction in Europe.

Section 3 – Margin Sensitivity in Past Cycles and Price vs Volume Leverage

Section summary
  1. Rexel's historical EBITA margin volatility highlights an asymmetric sensitivity where product price changes impact profitability more severely than organic volume shifts.
  2. Rapid cost containment and digital tools successfully defended operating margins during the 2024 European construction downturn compared to past down-cycles.
  3. Sector peers like Wesco and Solar AS show highly variable margin responses to volume contractions depending on their end-market exposure.

Historical EBITA Margin Drivers

1.
Rigid volume leverage: Rexel's margin recovery from 2019 to 2022 was driven by unprecedented pricing tailwinds rather than organic volume expansion. Rexel's current adjusted EBITA margin rose from 5.0% in 2019 to a peak of 7.3% in 2022. Morgan Stanley's report on March 30, 2026, notes that pricing inflation across core electrical products drove this margin expansion. Consequently, the subsequent normalization to 5.9% in 2024 demonstrates that margins are highly sensitive to price deflation when volumes remain stagnant.

Section 4 – Restructuring Track Record and Productivity Programmes

Section summary

  3. Structural productivity gains among peers support the thesis that operational efficiency offsets persistent wage and logistics cost inflation.

Rexel Historical Turnaround Execution

1.
Extensive restructuring commitment: Rexel undertook an aggressive transformation program in 2018 to address lingering performance issues across underperforming European geographies. This comprehensive initiative required restructuring costs of €82.5 million that primarily targeted operations in Germany, Spain, and the United Kingdom. Management successfully closed underperforming retail branches, rationalized regional logistics, and downsized corporate headquarters to stabilize the overall cost base. These decisive actions successfully paved the way for structurally higher adjusted EBITA margins over subsequent economic cycles.
2.
Target footprint rationalization: Footprint optimization in Germany focused on exiting low-margin residential and commercial segments in the north. To implement this shift, Rexel closed 17 underperforming branches and shut down two of its five national distribution centers. In Spain, the restructuring involved closing or merging 15 branches while transitioning logistics from a national model to a regional hub-and-spoke system. These targeted regional actions helped the group eliminate unprofitable revenue streams and focus on dense industrial hubs.
3.
Simplified operational structure: The UK market required aggressive intervention due to persistent macroeconomic headwinds. Rexel consolidated its operations by merging five commercial banners down to just two core brands. This organizational simplification enabled better purchasing terms and support function integration but required closing 33 retail branches. Consequently, the group successfully removed operational duplication and prepared the division for subsequent digital integration.
4.
Contrary evidence: Restructuring actions can cause material near-term disruption that temporarily suppresses commercial momentum. During the 2018 program, branch closures and network transitions impacted the European gross margin as competitors capitalized on local gaps. UK sales fell by 6.4% because of retail branch closures and customer transitions. These operational frictions indicate that material physical restructuring can contract the top line faster than costs can be eliminated.

The broker highlights that Rexel closed 24 branches in the UK and reorganized its salesforce in Austria to streamline regional operations. These structural cost actions are expected to have a carryover benefit of 15 basis points into the 2025 financial period. This external analysis reinforces that management's self-help measures provide a highly defensive margin floor during volume contractions.
