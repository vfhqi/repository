Post-Tax Joint Venture Math

1.
Elevated tax burden: Friedrich Vorwerk reported exceptionally high effective tax rates of 34.9% in the financial year 2024 and 32.6% in the financial year 2025. The company explained in its annual report published on March 26, 2026, that these rates reflect Germany's strict corporate tax environment. Consequently, these baseline rates put considerable pressure on the group's net income before adjusting for structural distortions. This high tax environment significantly impacts the net profitability of domestic operations.
2.
Structural distortion: Friedrich Vorwerk's equity-method joint venture income is recognized after local taxes have already been deducted at the project level. Because this JV income enters the consolidated income statement below the tax line, it mathematically suppresses the reported consolidated tax rate when compared to earnings before taxes. Analysts modeling the company's future earnings must separate this post-tax contribution to avoid overestimating core profitability. Thus, the reported tax rate fails to reflect the true operating tax burden on wholly-owned segments.
3.
Calculated normalization: Friedrich Vorwerk generated an earnings before tax of 55.3 million euros and equity investment income of 6.0 million euros in the financial year 2024. By subtracting the post-tax equity income from earnings before taxes, the pre-tax income of wholly-owned operations drops to 49.3 million euros (calculated). Applying the reported tax expense of 19.1 million euros yields a normalized tax rate of 38.74% (calculated) on the operating profit. This normalized rate highlights that the core operational segments face a much higher tax burden than the consolidated figures suggest.
4.
Pronounced disparity: Friedrich Vorwerk reported earnings before taxes of 128.0 million euros and equity investment income of 21.0 million euros in the financial year 2025. Excluding the post-tax JV income reduces the core taxable pre-tax income to 107.0 million euros (calculated) for the period. With a reported tax expense of approximately 41.7 million euros, the core business faced a normalized tax rate of 38.97% (calculated). This calculation proves that the operating segments sustain a tax rate nearly six percentage points higher than the reported 32.6% rate.
