EEG 2027 Reform Details

1.
Regulatory overhaul: Germany is planning its largest renewable policy overhaul since the introduction of the Renewable Energy Sources Act, known as the EEG 2027 reform. The draft package proposes to end fixed feed-in tariffs for new rooftop solar and shift installations toward market exposure. This proposed policy shift is expected to introduce heightened uncertainty in European distribution channels. Ultimately, this regulatory headwind is projected to constrain short-term residential installation volumes and affect SMA Solar's market recovery.
2.
Drastic reduction: The German government's draft reform sharply reduces the compensation structure for smaller rooftop installations under 25 kilowatts. These small residential systems will see their fixed tariff cut to 5.20 cents per kilowatt-hour from the previous 7.70 cents. Furthermore, the duration of this support is proposed to shrink to only three years instead of the historical twenty years. This dramatic reduction in support duration represents a direct threat to the payback calculations of German homeowners.
3.
Tighter integration: The draft EEG 2027 reform also targets larger systems above 100 kilowatts by moving them to contracts for difference. This mechanism includes a strict clawback of high-price revenue during periods of elevated market pricing. Additionally, the policy aims to enforce tighter links between solar deployment and grid availability to manage grid congestion. These structural changes will require commercial developers to adapt their business models or face lower project returns.
4.
Budget pressure: The German government justified the planned cuts to solar support by pointing to the heavy burden on the national budget. Economy minister Katherina Reiche argued that many renewable energy projects are now economically viable without subsidies. However, the country's Renewable Energy Federation head Ursula Heinen-Esser warned that these measures could throttle the expansion pace and jeopardize long-term capacity targets. This budget-driven legislative push is forced to meet new European Union state aid rules by the end of 2026.
