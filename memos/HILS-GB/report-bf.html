EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Hill & Smith operates a highly decentralized, entrepreneurial structure that maximizes local agility while pivoting rapidly toward higher-margin US infrastructure assets, underpinning a resilient earnings profile that delivers toptier financial output highlighted by exceptional return on capital, robust cash conversion, and sustained margin expansion. The business is led by high-quality management executing disciplined capital allocation focused on expanding high-return US platform capacity, achieving an exceptional 26.70% ROIC in FY25 while maintaining stringent financial frameworks.

The group's competitive advantage rests on profound regional scale advantages in hot-dip galvanizing and specialized composite engineering, where prohibitive transport logistics and stringent regulatory barriers create immense customer lock-in and high switching costs.

Core investment thesis and strategic positioning

The group has structurally tilted toward the United States, which generated 63% of revenues and 79% of underlying operating profits in FY25, concentrating exposure in the world's most favorable infrastructure end markets. US Engineered Solutions accounts for 48% of revenue and 49% of operating profits, while the highly profitable Galvanizing Services division contributes 24% of revenue but 37% of operating profit, offsetting the subdued UK & India segment at 28% of revenue and only 14% of operating profit. Geographic margin disparity is striking, with US galvanizing operational margins exceeding 30% versus mid-teens in the UK, illustrating the profit quality embedded in the American platform.

Customer-centric operating businesses win contracts by delivering highly customized, low-to-medium capitalintensive products directly to end customers, with highly engineered niche solutions such as composite utility poles insulating the business from commoditized competition. Agility at the operating level enables successful inflation pass-through of rising steel and zinc input costs without triggering material volume declines in US platforms, demonstrating utilized pricing power. Multi-year public investment frameworks, particularly the US Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act (IIJA), supply durable demand tailwinds amplified by ageing grid infrastructure and accelerating data center expansion driving demand for substation structural steel and composites.

Financial profile and peer-anchored performance metrics

Group revenues expanded to £868.80m in FY25, representing 2% reported growth, with 3% organic constant currency revenue growth accelerating to 4% in the second half driven by robust US platform momentum. Divisional organic performance highlights the geographic split, with US Engineered Solutions posting 6% organic growth, US Galvanizing delivering stellar 13% organic growth, while UK Engineered Solutions experienced a 6% organic revenue decline reflecting delayed transport spending.

Underlying operating margins expanded by 60 basis points to 17.40% in FY25, with underlying operating profit reaching £151.30m, and management upgrading its mid-term through-the-cycle operating profit margin target to 18%+ from a previous 15% baseline. Operating margin expansion is fueled by an improved portfolio mix toward US assets where business operating margins frequently exceed 20%, while persistent UK infrastructure weakness drags down margins due to flat-to-declining domestic activity.

Balance sheet strength is exceptional, with covenant net debt to EBITDA reaching an extremely low 0.10x in FY25 as net debt excluding lease liabilities fell to £50.80m, remaining comfortably below the group's target range of 1x to 2x leverage. Prudent liquidity management includes a £300m revolving credit facility extended to 2029, providing £346.50m in total facility headroom to fund strategic acquisitions while maintaining financial flexibility.

Return on Invested Capital expanded by 190 basis points to 26.70% in FY25, with management raising its midterm through-the-cycle ROIC target to 22%+, significantly outpacing historical levels of 24.80% in FY24, 22% in FY23, and 19.20% in FY22. Adjusted operating cash flow conversion was 91% in FY25, ahead of the 80%+ target, with historical cash conversion reaching 99% in FY24 and 115% in FY23, generating robust free cash flow of £106.90m in FY25.

Hill & Smith trades at a 13% EV/EBITA discount to US listed peers AZZ and Valmont, with an FY27F EV/EBITA multiple of 13.40x, representing a persistent valuation discount despite comparable operating performance. The group delivers comparable operating margins to AZZ Metals Coatings and Valmont Infrastructure, with US peer AZZ targeting Metal Coatings segment EBITDA margins of 27% to 32%, Valmont reporting NA Utilities revenue growth of 27% in Q1 2026, and AZZ delivering record Metal Coatings sales up 25.70% year-on-year.

Competitive moats and structural value chain positioning

Hot-dip galvanizing benefits from profound regional scale advantages as steel is expensive to transport long distances, with high freight costs limiting competition within a 150-to-200 mile radius, while Voigt & Schweitzer's network of nine strategically located US plants secures a dominant northeastern footprint. Creative Composites Group secures high switching costs through specialized product qualification and custom pultrusion engineering, with customer adoption of composite utility poles relying on brand recognition and longterm safety validation requiring extended approval cycles.

The value chain mapping reveals that Hill & Smith occupies the most profit-favored stages, with galvanizing capturing 35% to 45% gross margins and 25% to 30% EBIT margins while composites achieve 30% to 40% gross margins and 18% to 25% EBIT margins, substantially outperforming upstream raw inputs at 5% to 8% EBIT margins and downstream installation at 8% to 10.30% EBIT margins.

Galvanizing Services generated 37% of group operating profit in FY25, with the US galvanizing business achieving EBIT margins exceeding 30%, supported by V&S Galvanizing's dominant northeastern footprint through nine plants and management investing £35m to expand high-return US galvanizing capacity.

Hill & Smith's customer base is highly diversified, with operating companies maintaining an active strategy of diversifying their regional customer bases to avoid single-account dependency, ensuring no individual customer dominates total divisional revenue. Roadside safety systems require strict regulatory testing to secure mandatory state department approvals, with crash cushions and attenuators passing rigorous MASH TL-3 crash criteria, creating high substitution barriers because qualifying competing safety products requires several years. Creative Composites Group's utility customers value extreme weather resilience and fire resistance, with proprietary multi-layer composite pole designs eliminating the need for traditional guy wires and providing an estimated 80-year lifespan compared to decay-prone wooden alternatives.

Most operating units sell highly engineered solutions directly to utility and transport customers, allowing the group to retain strong pricing power and customer relationships, with specialized product certifications making distributor disintermediation highly unlikely due to strict regulatory barriers.

North American after-fabrication hot-dip galvanizing is led by AZZ Inc. commanding a 27% market share, with Valmont Industries holding an estimated 13% US market share, while the remaining 60% consists of highly fragmented private regional operators providing consolidation opportunities. US utilities accelerate capital spending on transmission networks to resolve severe data center power bottlenecks, with strong demand extending manufacturer backlogs to 42 to 44 weeks at Valmont, while robust volumes and efficiency gains expanded AZZ Metal Coatings adjusted EBITDA margin to 31% in FY26. - Conference 32 8K Peers report unprecedented demand in infrastructure and utility end markets driving double-digit volume expansion, with AZZ delivering a 25.70% year-on-year sales increase in its metal coatings segment during late fiscal 2026, while Hill & Smith matched this strong US trajectory with US Galvanizing volumes expanding 13%.

Scaled after-fabrication hot-dip galvanizers maintain firm pricing discipline despite softening raw material steel and zinc indexes, with Hill & Smith successfully passing domestic steel inflation directly through to public and private infrastructure customers without volume destruction.

Operator quality and capital allocation discipline

CEO Rutger Helbing took office in September 2024 bringing a proven public market track record running international manufacturing firms, with his experience managing US-skewed manufacturing operations directly benefiting the group's current portfolio to assure public market investors. Management eliminated the corporate Chief Operating Officer role to flatten reporting lines, with divisional managing directors now reporting directly through the highly scalable Group President structure, keeping central headcount exceptionally lean at just 20 people.

The group prioritizes organic capital expenditure to expand high-return US platform capacity, with management guiding to £50m total capex in FY26 excluding M&A, targeting US transmission, distribution, and galvanizing capacity growth investments. Highly disciplined bolt-on M&A strategy reinvests £50m to £70m annually on value-enhancing acquisitions, with acquired businesses required to achieve returns exceeding the group's cost of capital within three years, with most transactions sourced outside competitive public auctions to secure attractive entry multiples.

Hill & Smith acquired 80% of Freeberg Fabrication for an initial consideration of c.£27m, implying a highly reasonable 8.50x trailing EV/EBITA multiple, with the acquisition immediately bolstering high-margin data center fabrication exposure in the rapidly growing Arizona market. A £100m share buyback program was initiated in August 2025 triggered because covenant leverage remains low at 0.10x, while the group raised the full-year dividend by 8% in FY25, demonstrating balanced capital deployment between growth investment and shareholder returns.

Margin gains are driven by structural shift toward US infrastructure, with higher-margin US operations generating 79% of group underlying operating profits in FY25, representing portfolio-driven rather than heroic improvement scope. Management aggressively divests low-margin, non-core assets to structurally enhance profitability, with the UK permanent steel barrier business VRS divested for £5.25m in May 2026, eliminating breakeven revenue and adding an estimated 50 basis points to margins, while loss-making Australian road barriers and UK security gates were successfully sold in early 2025.

Long-term growth adjacencies and secular alignment

Power utilities structurally accelerate the replacement of decay-prone wood poles with high-strength composite alternatives, offering an 80-year lifespan double that of wood, drastically lowering total cost of ownership while severe weather events, wildfires, and woodpecker damage drive utility adoption of weather-resistant composite materials. Polyurethane resin systems require tight thermal process controls and advanced chemical formulation, with high process complexity and specialized equipment requirements preventing low-end competitors from replicating CCG's profiles, while utilities enforce rigorous multi-year onboarding processes requiring extensive physical field testing creating high switching barriers. The $36m acquisition of an 80% stake in Freeberg provides critical data center exposure sourced at c.8.5x trailing EV/EBITA, with Freeberg constructing a 160k square foot leased facility in Arizona heavily geared toward genset packaging to capture surging regional data center buildouts. Data centers require c.60 tonnes of galvanized steel per megawatt of capacity, with rapid hyperscaler rollouts potentially driving US data center capacity to 150GW by 2030, providing massive structural tailwinds to the group's galvanizing platform. Surging load demand from AI workloads and electrification of vehicles triggers a multi-decade grid modernization cycle, with approximately 59% of federal IIJA funding remaining unspent guaranteeing long-term visibility, while US spending on manufacturing construction surpassed $200b annualized in 2025 from $75b in 2020 reflecting domestic onshoring resurgence protected by Buy America compliance mandates. CCG sits as the largest manufacturer of FRP composite structures in North America, leveraging regional density and specialized R&D to maintain operating margins near 18%, while industry consolidation accelerates as utilities favor scaled suppliers capable of executing national tenders.

Plausible problems to monitor

Hill & Smith's UK & India Engineered Solutions division represents the most persistent structural headwind, with the UK business experiencing a 6% organic constant currency revenue decline in FY25 and contributing only 14% of operating profit despite accounting for 28% of revenue. This underperformance stems from delayed UK transport spending, particularly the flat nominal funding allocation under Road Investment Strategy 3 (RIS 3), which has prolonged sales cycles for UK-centric highway customers and created subdued domestic market conditions.

The persistent weakness in UK infrastructure threatens to dilute the financial gains achieved by US operations, representing a material drag on group-level margin progression despite active portfolio pruning efforts.

Executive-level disruption poses governance continuity risks, with the sudden departure of CFO Hannah Nichols in March 2025 to join Coats Group plc being particularly notable given her instrumental role in driving margin expansion. Multiple top-tier management departures, including both the CEO and CFO within a compressed timeframe, could disrupt strategic consistency and integration execution at a critical juncture when the group is actively pursuing bolt-on acquisitions and organic capacity expansions.

While interim leadership arrangements have maintained reporting stability, the succession planning around CFO transitions requires careful monitoring to preserve the group's active capital allocation momentum.
