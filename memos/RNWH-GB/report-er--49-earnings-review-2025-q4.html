Section 1 – Brief company description and history

Section summary
  1. Renew Holdings entered its fiscal 2025 results with a defensive opex-focused infrastructure exposure despite experiencing sector-specific headwinds from a slow start to the rail control period.
1.
Defensive engineering focus: Renew Holdings operates as a holding company providing critical maintenance, repair, and renewal services for owners of critical UK infrastructure. The group maintains an asset-light model that delivers non-discretionary support services under regulated spending cycles. Management targets high-barrier markets that require highly skilled, directly employed personnel.

2.
Segmented revenue structure: The group derives approximately 40% of its revenues from the rail sector, which historically targets a stable operating margin corridor of 7% to 8%. The remaining segments, including infrastructure, energy, and environmental services, each contribute between 13% and 15% of total group revenues. These three divisions consistently target a slightly lower but highly predictable operating margin corridor of 6% to 7%.
3.
Steady revenue compounding: In fiscal 2024, the group delivered a record financial performance with revenue increasing by 5.6% to £1,116.1m. This performance compared descriptionally against the restated fiscal 2023 revenue of £887.6m following the strategic disposal of the non-core Walter Lilly specialist building business. The strategic shift to pure-play engineering has successfully removed exposure to lumpy, higher-risk building contracts.
4.
Resilient operating profitability: Renew Holdings reported adjusted operating profit of £72.1m in fiscal 2025, representing a modest increase from £70.9m in fiscal 2024. Adjusted operating margins contracted slightly by 20 basis points to 6.5% in fiscal 2025 from 6.7% in fiscal 2024, reflecting operational headwinds. However, this delivery remained aligned with management's long-term financial framework targeting stable margins between 6% and 7%.
5.
Excellent cash generation: The company reported free cash flow conversion of 63% in fiscal 2024, compared with 68% in fiscal 2023. This cash-generative model supported a 5.3% dividend increase to 20p per share in fiscal 2025, up from 19p in fiscal 2024. Additionally, the group's long-term five-year average return on capital employed stood at a highly attractive 27% return on capital.
6.
Subdued share price performance: In the 12 months leading up to the fiscal 2025 earnings release on 25 November 2025, the share price declined by 13.4%. This negative performance followed a temporary rerating earlier in the year where the shares had gained 27% by June 2024. In the final three months before the preliminary results, the share price recovered some ground to deliver a positive absolute return of 8.4%.
7.
Underperforming relative metrics: Renew's 12-month share price decline of 13.4% represents a material underperformance against the broader UK market, with the FTSE All Share Index gaining 13.9% over the same period. Earlier in the year, the group had achieved absolute gains of 20.3% over a single month, significantly outperforming the market's 8.5% gain. However, subsequent macro concerns and sectorspecific challenges ultimately eroded these relative gains before the year-end.
8.
Stable broker support: Analysts at Deutsche Bank maintained a consistent 'Buy' rating on Renew Holdings in the six months leading up to the results. The broker's price target was raised slightly from 1,200p in September 2025 to 1,220p post-results in November 2025. This target revision reflected improved confidence in the group's net cash position and stable long-term infrastructure funding.
9.
Peer outperformance at Balfour: Peer Balfour Beatty underwent a structural transformation under long-term leadership, transitioning its portfolio toward low-risk target-cost and cost-plus contracts. Analysts at Jefferies noted that Balfour's UK portfolio was 85% target cost or cost plus and 95% public or regulated by mid-2024. This contract transition supported stable earnings growth and allowed the peer to achieve higher valuation multiples than historical averages.
10.
Peer deleveraging at Kier: Kier Group successfully executed a dramatic transformation plan since 2019, focused on deleveraging and rationalizing its operations. Analysts at Deutsche Bank highlighted that Kier achieved a milestone average net cash position in fiscal 2026 for the first time in over a decade. This deleveraging profile contrasted with Renew's historically strong, debt-free balance sheet that instead utilized cash flow for consistent acquisitions.
11.
Strong framework market share: Renew Holdings holds an estimated low double-digit market share within its largest addressable rail market under the Network Rail CP7 framework. While this represents a solid competitive position, the group holds a much lower market share of less than 10% across its other core infrastructure markets. This fragmented market structure provides a significant runway for market share expansion and consolidated organic growth.
12.
Other side of the story: The group remains highly exposed to customer budget pressures and delayed public spending, which could severely disrupt its compounding growth model. This risk was clearly illustrated by the slow start to the Rail CP7 cycle, where renewals deferments led to a 9% downgrade in operating profit forecasts earlier in the year. While management asserts that these volumes will normalize over the five-year cycle, any persistent capital deferrals by cash-constrained public clients would directly impact Renew's medium-term EPS trajectory.

Section 3 – Key moving parts and changes

Section summary
  1. The strategic transition to pure-play engineering services represents the most critical driver of Renew's future earnings trajectory, insulating the group from historical contracting risks.
1.
Incoming board chair: Pete Raby was appointed as independent Non-executive Director and Chair designate with effect from 5 October 2026. He will succeed David Brown as Chair at the conclusion of the Annual General Meeting on 27 January 2027 after Brown's nine-year tenure. Raby previously served as the Chief Executive Officer of Morgan Advanced Materials and brings deep corporate leadership experience. This transition marks a critical change in senior board leadership as the company enters its next growth phase.
2.
Executive director retirement: Having served on the board as Executive Director for Rail for over eight years, Andries Liebenberg retired from the group effective 31 January 2025. Following his retirement, the board structure was streamlined to comprise just two Executive Directors and four Non-executive Directors. This change represents a consolidation of executive board duties under the remaining leadership team.
3.
Structured executive board: During 2024, management established a new Executive Board to address the escalating size and operational complexity of the group. This new tier is comprised of the Chief Executive Officer, Chief Financial Officer, Group Commercial Director, and individual Sector Directors. The creation of this management layer provides enhanced operational oversight across divisions to support compounding growth.
4.
Increased executive compensation: The Remuneration Committee approved substantial base salary increases for the remaining Executive Directors starting 1 October 2024, setting the Chief Executive Officer's salary at £404,000 to £489,000 and the Chief Financial Officer's at £310,000 to £355,000. These salaries were planned to increase further to £574,000 and £400,000 respectively from 1 October 2025. This upward adjustment reflects the growing complexity of the group's pure-play engineering operations.
5.
Divestment of building business: Renew executed its strategic exit from the Specialist Building sector by disposing of Walter Lilly in October 2024. The disposal allows the company to transition fully into a pureplay engineering services provider. This structural shift removes volatile fixed-price contract risks that historically burdened group earnings.
6.
Material renewables expansion: The group acquired Full Circle on 7 October 2024 for a total cash consideration of €59m, equivalent to £49.3m. Full Circle specializes in onshore wind turbine repair, maintenance, and monitoring services across the UK and continental Europe. This milestone transaction provides an immediate entry into renewable energy markets.
7.
Expanded power capabilities: Following the acquisition of Excalon in June 2024, the group further strengthened its position in October 2025 by acquiring Emerald Power for an initial cash consideration of £7.8m. This bolt-on deal adds a dedicated overhead line capability that complements Excalon's underground power expertise. The combination provides a highly complementary transmission and distribution service offering.
8.
Specialized marine acquisition: On 30 April 2026, Renew's subsidiary, Envolve Infrastructure, acquired Edwards Diving Services for an initial consideration of £10m. Edwards Diving Services provides highly technical marine and civil engineering solutions to the water utility sector. This acquisition is expected to be immediately earnings enhancing for the water division.
9.
Regional operational underperformance: While Full Circle has successfully won new turbine service agreements, its near-term performance was hindered by operational difficulties in its French subsidiary. Management has placed this specific European business unit under a formal restructuring review. This represents a temporary operational drag on margin expectations for the onshore wind segment.
10.
Refinanced credit facility: Renew successfully refinanced its existing Revolving Credit Facility in October 2025, increasing the committed limit from £120m to £140m until October 2029. The refinancing secured highly favorable terms, reducing the interest margin from 180 basis points to 150 basis points. This expanded facility provides significant additional acquisition firepower to execute the M&A pipeline.
11.
Conservative leverage profile: The group maintained a pre-IFRS 16 net cash position of £6.2m at 30 September 2025, which recovered to £10.6m by 31 March 2026. While covenant terms permit leverage up to 2x adjusted EBITDA, management operates under a self-imposed soft limit of 1x net debt to EBITDA. Maintaining this conservative profile avoids any urgent balance sheet constraints.

Section 5 – The company's focus at the most recent earnings

Section summary
  1. Management positioned the company as a resilient pure-play engineering compounder after successfully exiting the specialist building sector.
  2. The company prioritized its massive regulatory growth opportunities in the water and energy transition markets to offset temporary rail headwinds.
  3. Executives emphasized a strong self-funded acquisition pipeline supported by excellent cash generation and a newly expanded credit facility.

1.
Completed strategic transition: Management focused heavily on its evolution into a pure-play engineering services provider following the disposal of the Walter Lilly specialist building business in October 2024. This structural shift was highlighted as essential to removing high-risk contracting liabilities and aligning the group with opex-led regulated cycles.
2.
Resilient profit delivery: Executives showcased a solid financial performance, pointing to group revenues increasing by 5.6% to £1,116.1m and adjusted operating profit rising by 1.7% to £72.1m. To cushion the transitional slowdown, the company reallocated resources toward maintenance and secured new framework success across all five Network Rail regions.

Strengthened framework pipeline: The company prioritized its record-high order book of £915m to demonstrate strong visibility heading into the new financial year. Management emphasized that these non-discretionary frameworks underpin long-term revenue visibility across its core infrastructure, environmental, and energy sectors.

Growing talent investment: Addressing the critical operational challenge of skilled labor retention, management highlighted that employee development enrollment reached 378 individuals. This expanded training pipeline is designed to resource future organic growth opportunities and secure technical delivery across high-barrier sectors.
