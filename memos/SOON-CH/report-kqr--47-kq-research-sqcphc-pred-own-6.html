Section 3 – Swiss Pillar Two Tax Implications

Section summary
  1. Sonova experienced a substantial increase in its effective tax rate following the initial implementation of the Swiss Pillar Two global minimum tax framework.
  2. Safe harbor provisions protect the group from immediate material financial exposure, whereas peers report higher baseline tax rates or no direct impact.

Trajectory of Tax Rate Guidance

1.
Decreasing tax benefits: Sonova Holding AG previously benefited from significant tax reforms that kept its tax expenses exceptionally low. In the FY2023/24 financial year, the group reported total income taxes of only CHF 37.8 million, which was reduced by CHF 39.1 million due to favorable Swiss tax reforms. This resulted in an exceptionally low weighted average effective tax rate of 5.8% for that period. This low baseline set an unsustainable expectation for future earnings.
2.
Rising tax rates: Sonova Holding AG saw its tax expenses surge during the FY2024/25 period due to the onset of the new minimum tax regime. The group reported total income taxes of CHF 105.0 million on pre-tax income of CHF 652.0 million. This drove the effective tax rate up to 16.1% for the year. This tax increase was a direct consequence of Switzerland enacting its Minimum Tax Ordinance in December 2023.
3.
Increasing tax drag: Sonova Holding AG warned investors in its FY2024/25 disclosures that the global minimum tax framework would create a persistent drag on its net income. The implementation of the global minimum tax laws depressed basic earnings per share to CHF 9.07, representing a 10.0% decline in Swiss francs. Management expects that the reported effective tax rate will continue to hover around these elevated levels. This shift directly dampens the group's mid-term EPS expansion prospects.
