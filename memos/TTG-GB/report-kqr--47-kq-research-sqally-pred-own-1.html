Section 2 – IAS 12 Recognition Conditions and US Tax Loss Precedents

Section summary
  1. Under IAS 12, companies must demonstrate probable future taxable profits to recognize deferred tax assets.
  2. UK industrial peers apply different forecast horizons ranging from three to ten years to support asset recognition.
  3. Successful recognition precedents require a clear transition out of cumulative regional loss positions.

Core IAS 12 Recognition Criteria

1.
Strict recognition threshold: Under the IAS 12 accounting framework, a deferred tax asset can only be recognized in the balance sheet if future utilization is deemed probable. This probability standard is internationally interpreted as a more likely than not threshold, requiring a probability of recovery greater than 50% under standard corporate policies. For companies with recent loss histories, this creates an extremely high bar for recognizing net operating losses. Consequently, management teams must exercise significant judgement before altering their balance sheet recognition status.
2.
Convincing evidence requirement: When a business jurisdiction has a history of recent cumulative losses, IAS 12 demands strong, objective evidence of future profitability before any asset recognition can occur. Under these conditions, general management optimism is insufficient, and companies must present convincing evidence of a regional turnaround to satisfy auditors. In the absence of such clear evidence, corporate policy dictates that deferred tax assets on carry-forward losses must remain unrecognized as a matter of accounting prudence. This strict standard directly prevents companies from inflating their book earnings during operational turnarounds.
3.
Regional profitability requirement: The formal utilization of deferred tax assets remains strictly conditional upon the return to taxable profitability in the specific geographical region where the net operating losses were originally generated. For global industrial companies, tax losses in one region cannot be offset against profits in another, meaning that a regional operational turnaround is a non-negotiable prerequisite. Furthermore, the speed of asset utilization depends on whether the local tax laws place any expiration limits on tax loss carry-forwards. Without sustained regional profits, these balance sheet assets remain entirely locked.

Peer DTA Recognition Precedents

4.
Peer tax losses: Comparing peer balance sheets reveals that several UK-listed industrial companies maintain substantial unrecognized tax assets due to regional profitability challenges. For instance, engineering group Kier Group PLC reported £521.7 million of unused tax losses in 2026, but restricted DTA recognition to £362.0 million because of local profit limitations. Similarly, Central Asia Metals PLC chose not to recognise potential deferred tax assets on $32.5 million of cumulative losses in 2024 because of insufficient profit evidence. These cases show that carrying large unrecognized tax pools is common among complex global operations.
5.
Divergent peer treatment: A comparative look at Morgan Advanced Materials PLC highlights how another peer manages massive unrecognized tax pools during a turnaround. In 2025, Morgan Advanced Materials reported gross unrecognized tax losses of £205.8 million, compared to £169.5 million in 2024, citing ongoing uncertainty surrounding future regional utilization. While they did recognize a small portion of £12.6 million in 2025, the bulk remains off the balance sheet. This cautious stance matches the defensive reporting seen across UKlisted industrial peer groups facing volatile end markets.
6.
Positive profit signals: In contrast, peer Vesuvius PLC demonstrated how improving business forecasts can unlock deferred tax asset recognition in loss-making jurisdictions. During 2025, Vesuvius recognized tax loss carry-forwards with a tax value of £17.0 million (up from £5.6 million in 2024) based on board-approved subsidiary plans. However, they maintained a high level of unrecognized tax assets at £155.0 million in 2025 because the future economic benefits of those specific pools were not yet probable. This shows that even profitable peers must apply highly targeted probability tests to each tax pool individually.

This precedent suggests that exiting a three-year cumulative loss position is the vital trigger for full tax recognition.
8.

In their analysis of utility turnarounds, Morgan Stanley noted that while companies accumulated massive tax assets, there was extremely limited visibility on the timing and probability of their actual recovery. This indicates that even when a company establishes regional profits, auditor caution can delay recognition for several years. Therefore, assuming an immediate earnings benefit from tax asset recognition after a turnaround remains highly uncertain.

Such transitions create a substantial one-time earnings boost.

Forecasting and Planning Horizons

10.
Multi-year plan test: Under IAS 12, companies must support their tax asset recoverability assessments by utilizing board-approved multi-year financial forecasts. The forecasting horizons chosen by companies vary, but typically align with their standard corporate planning cycles. For example, Vesuvius PLC bases its viability assessments and deferred tax recoverability modeling on a board-approved three-year budget and strategic plan. Similarly, Alumasc Group PLC structures its commercial operations and legislative compliance around defined three-year plans. These shorter horizons reflect the volatile nature of global industrial end markets.
11.
Extended forecast models: While some industrial companies rely on three-year cycles, others utilize more extended horizons to establish deferred tax asset recoverability. For instance, financial institutions like FinecoBank SpA apply a ten-year forward-looking time horizon in their IAS 12 recoverability tests. They build these models by taking the board-approved annual budget and projecting cash flows out to ten years using long-term growth convergence. In contrast, some UK entities use a five-year plan to justify their long-term deferred tax asset valuations of £158.9 million.
12.
Divergent planning metrics: The choice between a three-year and five-year planning horizon is critical because a longer horizon allows a company to capture more future taxable income to justify tax asset recognition. However, longer projection periods naturally introduce greater estimation uncertainty and invite intense audit scrutiny from regulators. For instance, Banca del Mezzogiorno bases its probability tests on a four-year business plan, but applies conservative haircuts and assumes zero growth beyond the explicit period to satisfy auditors. This shows that longer horizons must be balanced with cautious assumptions.
13.
Audit compliance standards: Ultimately, auditors like Deloitte require that any forward-looking estimates used for tax asset recognition are grounded in highly reliable operational budgets. Because forecasting is inherently subjective, auditors closely examine the underlying assumptions of customer initiatives and cost efficiencies. If a company's actual performance falls short of its budgets, auditors will force immediate asset write-downs to comply with IAS 12. This reality means that management cannot easily use long-term forecasts to manipulate their short-term earnings.
14.
Other side of the story: While auditors demand strict adherence to historical profitability and conservative multiyear forecasts, this rigid approach can lead to a significant understatement of a company's true economic assets. Under IAS 12, a company in the early stages of a genuine operational turnaround may be blocked from recognizing its tax assets simply because it cannot yet show a long track record of regional profits. Once profitability is firmly established, however, the sudden reversal of tax asset write-downs will deliver a massive, backward-looking step-change in reported book earnings. Consequently, the accounting standard often hides valuable tax shields from the balance sheet until the very last moment.
