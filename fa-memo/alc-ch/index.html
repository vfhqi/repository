The reasoning behind the inputs

Basis of every input. Revenue growth is reported (USD) growth on the company's basis (FY2025 $10,319 million). FactSet's standardised revenue runs $20 to $80 million a year above the company's (FY2025 $10,401 million); applied to that base, our growth rates give levels about 0.8% higher than ours. "EBITDA" is core EBITDA: core operating income (which excludes amortisation of acquired intangibles, restructuring, impairments and other items above a $10 million threshold) plus depreciation and software amortisation. It is not FactSet's "operating EBITDA" history line. Core EBIT margin is therefore EBITDA margin less about 5.5 points: 21.0%, 21.6%, 22.6%, 23.3%, 23.9%. The tax rate is the core effective rate. The share-count change line is shares issued to employees under equity plans (net of shares bought back is entered separately); grants exceeded vesting by 0.4 million shares in Q1 2026 alone. Lease payments are an estimate: the files do not disclose them; FactSet's net debt including leases ($3.53 billion) against the company's measure excluding them ($3.25 billion at 30 June 2026) and the guided lease interest within non-operating expenses point to a lease liability of a few hundred million dollars and payments of about $100 million a year. Acquisitions: 2026 is Neurotech ($550 million upfront, closed 20 October 2026) plus small items; 2027 to 2030 are an assumption of continued bolt-ons at the bottom of the $300 to $600 million a year that the company's record suggests, including milestones (RxSight up to $140 million; Neurotech).

How to read these figures

  • Amounts are in millions of the reporting currency; per-share figures are in units of that currency. Negative figures are shown in brackets.
  • Historical figures use FactSet's standardised lines, which can differ from the company's own presentation.
  • Earnings per share appears on four bases, which must not be mixed: reported basic; FactSet's adjusted diluted, before unusual items; the Viewforth definition, which is the model's standing convention; and the brokers' adjusted basis, on which consensus is based.
  • Consensus is FactSet's median of the broker estimates it holds. Where FactSet's line-by-line consensus differs from the broker-by-broker detail, the line-by-line table takes precedence.
  • Viewforth's forecasts are the Excel model's base case. There are no bull or bear cases.
