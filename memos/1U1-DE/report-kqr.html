Section 1 – Key question and brief 10 findings

Section summary
  1. The Federal Cartel Office investigation into Vantage Towers and Vodafone regarding alleged 5G site obstruction remains procedurally stalled in early 2026 following Higher Regional Court filings, directly impacting 1&1's long-term infrastructure trajectory.

Scope and higher intent of the investigation

1.
Obstructed market entry: The primary Key Question examines the exact procedural status and enforceable remedies stemming from the Federal Cartel Office's provisional finding that Vantage Towers AG and parent Vodafone Group plc committed an abusive obstruction under Sections 19 and 20 of the German Act against Restraints of Competition
2.
High equity stakes: For a concentrated equity investor evaluating 1&1 AG, the higher intent of this inquiry is determining whether legal remedies can force rapid site delivery or deliver material financial disgorgement to underwrite 1&1's 18-month to 3-year earnings per share trajectory
3.
Sequential legal dependencies: Resolving this question determines whether 1&1 can eliminate costly national roaming cash drains and build an independent Open RAN network or face prolonged capital expenditure inefficiencies

Procedural timeline and legal challenges

4.
Initiating antitrust complaint: The dispute originated from 1&1's formal complaint in March 2023 alleging that Vantage Towers failed to fulfill its December 2021 Master Services Agreement target of sharing 3,800 antenna locations
5.
Formal objections issued: The Federal Cartel Office issued its initial Statement of Objections on 11 April 2025 and follow-up statements on 22-23 September 2025, finding that Vantage Towers and Vodafone acted as a single economic unit to delay site delivery
6.
Contemplated mandatory orders: The antitrust authority's provisionally considered remedies include a mandatory order to supply all contractually promised tower sites within three years alongside potential disgorgement of financial advantages derived from delayed market entry
7.
Judicial injunction delay: In November 2025, Vodafone filed an application for interim legal protection before the Higher Regional Court of Düsseldorf citing procedural irregularities, effectively delaying a final administrative decision into 2026

Adjacent 5G obligations and edge reads

8.
Achieved coverage threshold: Despite passive infrastructure delays, 1&1 successfully reached 27% household coverage in December 2025, surpassing the Federal Network Agency's 25% competitive independence mandate and mitigating immediate regulatory fine risks
9.
Non-consensus strategic signal: Market consensus treats antitrust proceedings as a pure operational catalyst, but edge signals indicate 1&1 could leverage binding Cartel Office findings to negotiate an advantageous market exit or structural network integration
10.
Other side of the story: Vantage Towers maintains that supply chain disruptions from the war in Ukraine, contractor shortages, complex urban rooftop permitting, and strict electromagnetic field requirements created valid operational delays, arguing that ongoing Düsseldorf court proceedings and contractual arbitration could suspend binding remedies indefinitely

Section 2 – Regulatory & Legal Dynamics: FCO Proceedings & Düsseldorf Injunctions 16 findings

Section summary
  1. The Bundeskartellamt's abuse of dominance case faces a prolonged procedural stall following Vodafone's November 2025 injunction application before the Higher Regional Court of Düsseldorf.

Statutory framework and relative power allegations

1.
Statutory abuse threshold: Under Sections 19 and 20 of the German Act against Restraints of Competition, the Federal Cartel Office established that Vantage Towers and Vodafone hold relative market power over 1&1 due to strict customer dependency on passive infrastructure
2.
Relative market dependency: The competition authority determined that 1&1's status as an entering fourth mobile network operator created an asymmetric reliance on Vantage's existing 20,000 German tower sites, making nondelivery a structural barrier to entry
3.
Avoidable execution failure: In its preliminary legal assessment, the regulator concluded that Vodafone and Vantage possessed numerous operational alternatives to prevent rollout delays, including shifting internal expansion resources away from Vodafone's own 5G network upgrades
4.
Preserved incumbent advantage: The antitrust watchdog highlighted that slow contract fulfillment directly benefited Vodafone by delaying the commercial market entry of a fourth operator while weakening 1&1's position in Federal Network Agency spectrum allocation proceedings

Statements of Objections and single economic unit

5.
Single economic entity: In its formal Statement of Objections issued on 11 April 2025, the Federal Cartel Office treated Vodafone Group, Vodafone Germany, and Vantage Towers as a single economic unit jointly responsible for anti-competitive obstruction
6.
Expanded preliminary charges: The antitrust authority issued supplementary statements of objections on 22 and 23 September 2025, solidifying its preliminary legal classification of abusive hindrance under German and European competition law
7.
Rejection of corporate claims: Vantage Towers and Vodafone formally rejected the preliminary findings, arguing in disclosures that supply chain bottlenecks, labor shortages, and municipal permitting delays created unavoidable operational defaults under the 2021 Master Services Agreement
8.
Disgorgement risk notification: The regulator formally notified Vodafone that any binding infringement decision would likely incorporate a mandatory order for disgorgement of economic advantages obtained through delayed market entry

Düsseldorf judicial challenge and procedural injunctions

9.
Injunction application filed: Vodafone escalated the dispute in November 2025 by filing an application for preliminary injunctive relief before the Higher Regional Court of Düsseldorf to block an immediate infringement decision
10.
Procedural bias claims: In its court application, Vodafone challenged alleged procedural irregularities and administrative bias, asserting that the Federal Cartel Office inappropriately favored 1&1 during the investigative process
11.
Enforceable decision suspended: The pending emergency application before the Higher Regional Court of Düsseldorf effectively prevents the Federal Cartel Office from issuing a final binding infringement order until judicial review resolves
12.
Formal defense submitted: The Federal Cartel Office submitted its formal defense to the Düsseldorf court, with corporate filings from 1&1 confirming that a judicial decision on interim legal protection is expected during 2026

Judicial appeal timelines and enforcement delays

13.
Extended litigation horizon: If the Düsseldorf court denies interim relief, Vodafone retains the right to appeal any subsequent administrative infringement ruling, creating an extended judicial timeline spanning 18 to 36 months
14.
Medium confidence anchor: High confidence attaches to the occurrence of Düsseldorf filings based on 1&1 and Vodafone annual report disclosures, but the ultimate judicial timeline remains medium confidence pending the court's preliminary ruling in 2026
15.
Unimpaired operational targets: Corporate management at 1&1 disclosed in its 2025 annual report that pending court proceedings present no material risk to its underlying 5G network construction trajectory
16.
Other side of the story: Vodafone and Vantage Towers contend that the Federal Cartel Office's administrative process was procedurally flawed and biased toward 1&1, asserting that a favorable Düsseldorf court ruling on procedural irregularities could permanently void the regulator's preliminary findings

Section 3 – Enforcement Remedies: Site Orders, Disgorgement & Commitment 15 findings

Section summary
  1. The Federal Cartel Office targets mandatory site delivery within three years alongside unprecedented economic advantage disgorgement after rejection of earlier commitment proposals.

Negotiations

Mandatory supply orders and behavioral acceleration

1.
Mandatory supply mandate: The Federal Cartel Office's primary behavioral remedy provisionally mandates that Vantage Towers supply all contractually promised tower sites to 1&1 within a strict three-year execution window
2.
Behavioral site acceleration: Mandating site delivery forces Vantage Towers to reallocate physical construction resources, site planning teams, and urban rooftop upgrade capacity directly toward 1&1's Open RAN deployment
3.
Operational capacity reallocation: In its preliminary assessment, the competition authority emphasized that Vantage Towers and Vodafone possessed sufficient operational flexibility to prioritize 1&1's co-location requests over internal network upgrades
4.
Contractual target restoration: The three-year mandatory supply order aims to restore the original 2021 Master Services Agreement trajectory, which initially required sharing 3,800 macro sites by the end of 2025
5.
Enforcement oversight mechanisms: To ensure compliance with the behavioral order, the regulator is evaluating administrative coercion tools including recurring periodic penalty payments for missed intermediate site milestone targets

Economic advantage disgorgement and fine mechanics

6.
Disgorgement notification issued: The Federal Cartel Office formally notified Vodafone and Vantage Towers that any binding infringement ruling will likely incorporate a mandatory order for disgorgement of economic advantages
7.
Statutory disgorgement framework: Under Section 34 of the Act against Restraints of Competition, economic advantage disgorgement ( Vorteilsabschöpfung ) extracts excess profits earned by delaying rival market entry
8.
Quantifying economic benefit: Regulator calculations for disgorgement focus on financial gains accrued by Vodafone through preserved subscriber market share and delayed retail price competition from 1&1
9.
Administrative fine exposure: Beyond disgorgement of profits, Section 81 of the Act against Restraints of Competition empowers the competition authority to impose administrative fines reaching up to 10% of global group revenue
10.
Unprovisioned balance sheet: Annual report filings reveal that Vantage Towers and Vodafone Group have recognized zero financial provisions on their balance sheets, classifying potential monetary outflows as contingent liabilities

Failed commitment negotiations and remedy contrast

11.
Rejected commitment offers: Formal negotiations regarding voluntary commitments under Section 32b of the Act against Restraints of Competition took place between the regulator and Vodafone from late 2023
12.
Inadequate voluntary proposals: The competition authority formally rejected Vodafone's proposed commitments after determining that offered site delivery schedules fell significantly short of contractual supply obligations
13.
Behavioral versus financial: Deutsche Bank Research highlights that behavioral site orders accelerate physical network expansion, whereas financial disgorgement serves as a retrospective penalty without directly building tower infrastructure
14.
Complementary remedy structure: The antitrust authority intends to combine behavioral supply orders with monetary disgorgement, creating both operational acceleration and severe financial deterrence against market obstruction
15.
Other side of the story: Vantage Towers and Vodafone argue that supply chain bottlenecks, municipal permitting delays, and technical rooftop restrictions caused performance defaults, contending that coerced three-year supply orders could prove physically impossible to execute without relaxing urban planning regulations

Section 4 – Market Structure & Infrastructure Economics: Tower Sharing & Co- 19 findings

Section summary
  1. Dual-control governance within Oak Holdings and lagging co-location economics at Vantage Towers create inherent structural conflicts that obstruct neutral host access for 1&1's 5G rollout.

Location Conflict

German market structure and tenancy ratio economics

1.
Dominant market position: Deutsche Telekom's tower unit GD Towers leads the German mobile infrastructure market with approximately 40,000 macro sites across Germany and Austria and an industry-leading tenancy ratio of 2.30x
2.
Lagging co-location density: Vantage Towers Germany manages 20,436 macro sites as of March 2026 but exhibits a persistently low tenancy ratio of 1.33x, underperforming independent European benchmarks
3.
Independent operator benchmark: Independent European tower companies achieve an average co-location ratio of 2.40x across ground-based and rooftop assets, driven by pure neutral host commercial incentives
4.
Commercial floor exposure: Urban rooftop sites account for the majority of Vantage's German portfolio, where physical weight constraints and structural wind load limits restrict multi-tenant co-location additions
5.
Concentrated infrastructure ownership: The German tower landscape remains highly concentrated across GD Towers, Vantage Towers, and American Tower Corporation, leaving 1&1 with few alternative macro site providers
6.
Edge market signal: Morgan Stanley research indicates that captive tower operators controlled by incumbent mobile carriers systematically underperform independent TowerCos on secondary tenant lease-up rates by 30 to 50 basis points

The 2021 Master Services Agreement and co-location dynamics

7.
Landmark long-term contract: In December 2021, 1&1 signed a binding Master Services Agreement with Vantage Towers securing access to at least 3,800 and up to 5,000 existing macro sites through 2040
8.
Targeted tenancy expansion: The Master Services Agreement was designed to underpin Vantage's medium-term commercial targets by raising its overall German tenancy ratio above 1.50x
9.
Severe delivery shortfall: Vantage contractually committed to delivering 1,000 shared sites by the end of 2022 and 3,800 by 2025, but actual physical handovers fell dramatically short of contractual milestones
10.
Stranded capital allocation: Disclosures from 1&1 management confirm that the company constructed fiber optic backhaul connections to hundreds of designated Vantage towers that were subsequently never made available
11.
Non-consensus contract interpretation: 1&1 executive commentary asserts that contractual commitments require Vantage to build replacement build-to-suit sites at its own expense if existing anchor slots are occupied

Governance conflicts, site blocking, and technical bottlenecks

12.
Captive ownership structure: Oak Holdings GmbH controls 89.32% of Vantage Towers AG, operating as a 50/50 joint venture between Vodafone Group and private equity consortium partners GIP and KKR
13.
Direct management control: A formal Domination and Profit Loss Transfer Agreement empowers Oak Holdings to issue legally binding operational instructions to Vantage Towers' management board under Section 308 AktG
14.
Structural governance conflict: Vodafone acts simultaneously as Vantage's primary anchor tenant generating 83% of revenues and as its co-controlling shareholder, creating direct alignment against facilitating rival entry
15.
Discriminatory resource prioritization: Federal Cartel Office investigations concluded that Vantage and Vodafone possessed sufficient operational capacity but prioritized Vodafone's internal 5G upgrades over 1&1's co-location handovers
16.
Neutral host credibility deficit: Industry expert insights from former colocation directors highlight that MNO- controlled TowerCos face irreconcilable trust barriers when attempting to host competing tier-one MNOs on strategic urban sites
17.
Technical obstruction defense: Vantage Towers management attributes delivery delays to external execution bottlenecks, including municipal rooftop permitting delays, local civil engineering shortages, and complex electromagnetic field emissions regulations
18.
Single critical bottleneck: Equity analysts at Deutsche Bank Research conclude that governance ties to Vodafone fundamentally limit Vantage's ability to act as a true neutral host in the German telecom market
19.
Other side of the story: Vantage Towers and Vodafone maintain that severe supply chain disruptions and strict municipal structural engineering rules prevented timely site handovers, arguing that coerced co-location acceleration could destabilize existing network equipment and breach local building safety codes

Section 5 – Commercial & Operational Impact on 1&1's 5G Rollout 43 findings

Section summary
  1. 1&1 successfully eliminated immediate regulatory fine risks by surpassing its 25% coverage mandate in late 2025, but site handoff shortages continue to constrain physical network autonomy.
  2. Completing the migration of 12.5 million subscribers to Vodafone national roaming secures 5G continuity but introduces capacity-share wholesale cost volatility into short-term operating margins.
  3. Equity markets increasingly price a strategic pivot toward capex reduction or market consolidation, which would transform 1&1's cash flow profile despite ongoing infrastructure friction.
  4. Identifying key empirical data gaps surrounding antitrust disgorgement formulas, court dockets, sub- contracts, and spectrum discretion provides a roadmap to fully resolve the 1&1 regulatory landscape.
  5. Binding Federal Cartel Office remedies against Vantage Towers and Vodafone will establish a European antitrust precedent on self-preference, unlocking long-term equity upside for United Internet while exposing incumbent TowerCo contracts to sector-wide repricing.

Network rollout trajectory and coverage milestone achievement

1.
Accelerated coverage expansion: 1&1's Open RAN 5G network achieved 27% household coverage by late December 2025, surpassing the Federal Network Agency's 25% competitive threshold
2.
Sustained deployment momentum: Network coverage expanded further to exceed 34% of German households by H1 2026, driven exclusively by mid-band and high-band frequency deployments
3.
Virtualized core architecture: The operational foundation relies on a cloud-native architecture supported by 4 core data centers and over 330 operational decentralized far-edge data centers connected to 1&1 Versatel's 70,000 km fiber network
4.
Lagging physical footprint: Despite coverage percentage gains, 1&1 operated only approximately 1,500 active cell sites by early 2026, representing a severe deficit against incumbent MNO grids that range between 27,000 and 33,000 locations
5.
Incremental site handovers: Vantage Towers' delivery shortfalls restricted 1&1's quarterly site additions to approximately 200 to 300 locations, slowing the transition of physical traffic onto fully owned infrastructure

Customer migration and national roaming cost economics

6.
Historic customer transfer: 1&1 completed the migration of its entire mobile customer base of over 12 million subscribers to its own core network in November 2025
7.
Exclusive roaming partner: Following the phase-out of legacy Telefónica arrangements, 1&1 transitioned all national roaming services exclusively to Vodafone starting 1 January 2026 under a long-term 18-year agreement
8.
Capacity-share cost structure: The Vodafone roaming model requires 1&1 to reimburse network expenses based on its relative share of total data volume across Vodafone's German mobile infrastructure
9.
Margin squeeze from roaming: Slower-than-expected network expansion by Vodafone elevated 1&1's calculated data usage share, driving higher wholesale roaming payments and reducing H1 2026 Consumer segment EBITDA to 391.3 million euros
10.
Contractual financial floor: The roaming agreement enforces a minimum annual remuneration of 50 million euros, creating a cumulative 175 million euro baseline commitment through the remainder of the initial five-year term

Regulatory fine elimination and non-consensus build capping

11.
Discontinued regulatory fines: The Federal Administrative Court affirmed Federal Network Agency independence, leading regulatory authorities to formally discontinue fine proceedings against 1&1 regarding initial 2019 rollout target delays
12.
Low-band spectrum bottleneck: Operational reach remains constrained as 1&1 failed to secure a market-aligned agreement for shared access to low-band sub-1GHz spectrum during mandated negotiations with incumbent operators
13.
Non-consensus build capping: UBS equity research suggests that 1&1 will cap its physical network build at 25% coverage, which could lift free cash flow above 350 million euros as capital expenditure normalizes
14.
Strategy shift upside: Deutsche Bank Research argues that persistent rollout hurdles could prompt 1&1 to pursue market consolidation or a switch back to wholesale, creating potential target valuation upside between 34.50 and 46.70 euros per share
15.
Commercial churn pressure: Bernstein Research notes that technical migration churn and aggressive value- segment pricing by competitors like Telefónica Deutschland dampened subscriber growth and service revenue expansion through 2025
16.
side of the story: While financial markets view capped capital expenditure and wholesale reliance as cashgenerative, 1&1's long-term competitive independence remains fundamentally vulnerable without proprietary lowband spectrum and an autonomous physical site footprint, meaning ongoing site handoff delays from Vantage Towers could leave 1&1 permanently dependent on rival national roaming grids
