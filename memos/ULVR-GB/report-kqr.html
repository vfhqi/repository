Section 2 – The Magnum Ice Cream Demerger Precedent

Section summary
  1. The demerger of The Magnum Ice Cream Company was executed with an eight-for-nine share consolidation to maintain post-transaction financial comparability.
  2. Unilever distributed shares with a fair value of six point seven five billion euros, representing approximately eighty percent of the demerged entity. Net read: The share consolidation successfully defended Unilever's per-share metrics by reducing the outstanding share count by eleven point one percent.

Demerger and Market Values

1.
Historic separation: Unilever completed the separation of its Ice Cream business on sixth December 2025, listing the unit as The Magnum Ice Cream Company N.V. on Euronext Amsterdam, London, and New York. According to Unilever's 2025 full-year results published on twelfth February 2026, the company distributed eighty point one five percent of the shares to its shareholders and retained a nineteen point eight five percent stake. This transaction represented a significant milestone that allowed the company to sharpen its strategic focus on higher-growth personal care segments.
2.
Initial valuation: The Magnum Ice Cream Company issued six hundred and twelve point two five million shares at its debut in December 2025. Based on the first day of trading, the demerged entity's total issued shares had an estimated fair value of seven billion nine hundred and forty-one million euros, as reported in its 2025 annual report. This implies the eighty point one five percent distributed stake had an estimated market value of six billion three hundred and sixty-four point seven one million euros (calculated) at admission.
3.
Substantial distribution: Unilever recorded a non-cash dividend of six billion seven hundred and fifty-two million euros in its equity statement for the distribution of the Ice Cream business. This transaction was executed via an interim in specie dividend, according to a takeover document published on sixteenth February 2026. The distribution of this substantial asset is expected to improve group operating margins over the long term because continuing operations exclude the lower-margin Ice Cream business.
4.
Relative sizing: Unilever maintained a massive market capitalization of one hundred and thirty-seven billion seven hundred and thirty-five million euros on twelfth December 2025. This valuation was recorded by Morgan Stanley in a broker note published on fifteenth December 2025, which noted that the company trades on seventeen point zero times its forecasted 2026 earnings. Therefore, the distributed Magnum business represented roughly five percent of Unilever's total group equity value at the time of demerger.

Consolidation Ratio Mechanics

5.
Defensive consolidation: Unilever implemented an eight-for-nine share consolidation on eighth December 2025 to reduce its outstanding share count. The consolidation, which became effective on ninth December 2025, resulted in shareholders receiving eight new ordinary shares for every nine previously held, as confirmed in an RNS announcement. This corporate action successfully reduced the total ordinary shares in issue from approximately two point five billion to two point two billion shares.
6.
56 20F Mechanical adjustment: Unilever determined the share consolidation ratio by comparing post-demerger share prices against pre-demerger prices. According to a current report filed with the SEC on second October 2025, the Board planned to set the ratio to maintain comparability of per-share metrics including earnings and dividends. Consequently, the eleven point eleven percent share reduction was designed to reflect the value distributed rather than the actual historic earnings contribution.
7.
Contrary evidence: Deutsche Bank Research highlighted on eighth December 2025 that the share consolidation was intended to offset a projected four percent to six percent decline in Unilever's day-one share price. The broker argued that the consolidation ratio of eight-for-nine represented an eleven point eleven percent share count reduction, which was larger than the value drop of the demerged assets. This indicates the consolidation ratio was set conservatively to defend the absolute share price rather than perfectly mirroring the immediate market value given up.
8.
Comparative reporting: Unilever restated its prior-period average share counts retrospectively to ensure historical comparability after the demerger. The company's annual report on Form 20-F noted that the demerged Ice Cream segment was treated as a discontinued operation, leaving continuing operations with an underlying earnings per share of three point zero eight euros for 2025. While the consolidation did not constitute a share repurchase at fair value, it successfully prevented immediate dilution in reported EPS.

The company's 2025 annual results revealed that shares outstanding net of treasury shares fell to two thousand one hundred and seventy-nine point five million by the end of 2025. This reduction helped protect the dividend per share comparability as the company declared a three percent increase in its quarterly dividend in early 2026.

Section 3 – The Foods-McCormick Combination and Expected Share Consolidation

Section summary
  1. Unilever expects to complete the Reverse Morris Trust transaction combining its Foods business with McCormick by mid-2027.
  2. The deal terms include a fifteen point seven billion dollar cash payment and a sixty-five percent combined equity stake.
  3. Sell-side analysts expect Unilever to implement a major share consolidation upon transaction closing to neutralize substantial EPS dilution.

Strategic Transaction Terms

1.
Transformational combination: Unilever announced an agreement on thirty-first March 2026 to merge its global Foods business with McCormick & Company through a Reverse Morris Trust. The deal excludes Unilever's food operations in India and certain other assets, creating a combined flavor giant with twenty billion dollars in annual revenue. This represents a pivotal step that will accelerate Unilever's transition to HPC categories.
2.
Substantial cash consideration: Unilever will receive a massive one-time cash payment of fifteen point seven billion dollars upon closing of the transaction. The company expects to allocate approximately five billion euros of these proceeds to cover tax, separation, and restructuring costs. These cash inflows are crucial to fund accelerated capital return programs over the medium term.
3.
Structured equity ownership: Unilever and its shareholders will receive sixty-five percent of the fully diluted equity of the combined company at closing. Specifically, Unilever's shareholders will directly receive fifty-five point one percent of the stock, while Unilever retains a nine point nine percent stake. This structured equity distribution will allow shareholders to participate directly in future combined growth.

Expected Share Consolidation Dynamics

4.
Explicit consolidation guidance: Unilever confirmed in its November 2026 investor event that a share consolidation will be executed on or around closing. The consolidation is designed to protect key per-share metrics as the Foods business is demerged. This corporate action will reduce the total shares in issue to reflect the carved-out asset value.
5.
Serious dilution threat: Rothschild & Co Redburn estimated on twenty-fifth March 2026 that the Foods demerger would cause a thirty-two percent decline in pro forma EPS. This severe drop reduces to twenty-one percent when excluding estimated stranded costs. Consequently, the broker expects Unilever to address this earnings gap retrospectively through a comprehensive share consolidation.
6.
Moderate dilution modeling: Jefferies Research modeled the transaction on eighteenth May 2026 and highlighted a four percent day-one dilution to continuing earnings. However, the broker notes that the transaction can turn seven percent accretive once six hundred million dollars in cost synergies are realized. This indicates that the ultimate consolidation ratio depends heavily on the synergy capture timeline.

Post-Closing Financial Sizing

7.
Contrary evidence: Deutsche Bank Research argued on thirty-first March 2026 that the high leverage of the combined entity could depress its opening market value. The broker notes that pro forma net debt to EBITDA will stand at approximately four times at closing, which is significantly higher than standalone levels. This leverage overhang could limit the initial market capitalization and disrupt the intended consolidation valuation.
8.
Robust cash utilization: Unilever plans to spend six billion euros of the remaining proceeds on debt reduction. This debt retirement will allow the company to maintain leverage below two times net debt to EBITDA by the end of 2027. These balance sheet measures will fully offset the loss of cash flow from the divested Foods assets.
9.
Other side of the story: Bernstein Research warned on thirty-first March 2026 that European investors might sell off Unilever shares due to the primary US listing of the combined company. This potential selling pressure could depress Unilever's share price in the weeks surrounding the closing. Such downward pressure would distort the market value comparison used to finalize the share consolidation ratio.
