Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Nexans' complete electrification pure-play transition renders its mid-term earnings trajectory highly sensitive to Halden plant capacity utilization and deep-water subsea interconnector awards.
  2. The July 2026 preferred-bidder Mediterranean mass-impregnated subsea cable order secures critical production loading at Nexans' Halden plant, mitigating under-utilization risks from the postponed Great Sea Interconnector.

Refocused Pure Play Electrification Strategy

1.
Key Question: "Nexans (NEX-FR), a French-listed cable maker that completed its exit from non-electrification businesses in July 2026; it reports PWR-Transmission (high-voltage subsea and land cable systems with installation: the Halden plant in Norway with its mass-impregnated and extruded lines, Charleston in the United States, Charleroi in Belgium and three cable-laying vessels including Electra), PWR-Grid (medium-voltage cables and accessories for distribution utilities), PWR-Connect (low-voltage cables for buildings, industry and data centres, including Republic Wire in the United States since June 2026) and Other Activities (metallurgy and copper rod). Nexans (NEX-FR) announced in July 2026 that it was preferred bidder for a replacement mass-impregnated subsea cable order intended to load the mass-impregnated line at its Halden plant from the end of 2026 to mid- 2028 after the Great Sea Interconnector was postponed, and the Mallorca-Menorca 3 preferred-supplier award it announced with Red Eléctrica on 22 October 2026 uses extruded XLPE alternating-current cable, so it is not that order. Which project is the July 2026 mass-impregnated order (customer, route, voltage, length, value, delivery schedule and technology), what have the customer, regulators, governments and the press in Italy, Greece, Egypt, Morocco, Tunisia, Cyprus and Spain said about it since July 2026, has the EPCI contract been signed and on what terms (down-payments, reservation fees, liquidated damages), and which further deep-water or mass-impregnated interconnector projects (for example the Greece-Egypt GREGY link, Terna's Tyrrhenian and Sardinia-Corsica- Italy links, the Italy-Tunisia ELMED link, Morocco links and the Great Sea Interconnector, whose cable production Nexans's chief executive said in October 2026 is targeted to restart in 2028) could load the Halden massimpregnated line after mid-2028, with each project's status, tender timetable, competing bidders and the technology chosen?"
2.
Strategic refocussing: Nexans successfully completed its transformation into a pure-play electrification player by completing the sale of its automotive wire harnesses unit Autoelectric to Motherson on July 3, 2026. This portfolio rotation allows management to direct focus and capital entirely toward the high-growth electrification value chain, which drove a 12.9% pro forma growth in standard sales for the first nine months of 2026. This strategic transition increases exposure to secular transmission and grid demands, which will insulate earnings per share from cyclical automotive segments.
3.
Nexans 9M 2026 revenue and organic growth breakdown by segment Critical manufacturing: The Halden plant in Norway represents a critical asset for Nexans, housing specialized mass-impregnated production lines that are uniquely designed for high-tension, deep-water interconnector cables. Since the €1.2 billion Great Sea Interconnector project was postponed, the plant faced immediate fixed-cost under-absorption risks that threatened segment profitability. Resolving the preferred-bidder replacement mass-impregnated order is vital because it secures plant loading and protects the segment's high-teens margin trajectory.

Earnings Outlook and Risk Safeguards

4.
Strong EPS: Financial models highlight that Nexans is positioned for significant earnings growth, with Deutsche Bank estimating that earnings per share will grow by 35.2% in fiscal 2026 to reach €6.64. This projection is further supported by estimated earnings per share expanding to €8.42 in fiscal 2027 and reaching €10.39 in fiscal 2028. Securing the July 2026 mass-impregnated order by year-end is crucial to de-risk these projections, as any manufacturing delay would disrupt near-term transmission capacity utilization rates.

Contrary evidence: Bernstein Research argued in August 2026 that the replacement Mediterranean project could suffer from reduced bargaining power because the postponement of the Great Sea Interconnector put Nexans under immediate timing pressure. This compressed timeline might force the company to accept lower margin terms than initially planned, which would impair PWR-Transmission earnings margin progression.
6.
Other side of the story: While the sudden postponement of the Great Sea Interconnector threatened capacity utilization at the Norwegian plant, the successful integration of Republic Wire in the low-voltage PWR-Connect segment provides a substantial offset to transmission-related volatility. The expanded United States footprint and strong short-cycle grid accessory demand can stabilize group margins even if deep-water interconnector contracts face geopolitical delays. This structural diversification demonstrates that Nexans has a solid foundation to capture booming grid and data center opportunities.

The CFO Vincent Piquet confirmed in October 2026 that this replacement order will start production at the end of 2026 and fully occupy the mass-impregnated line until mid-2028. This secured workload removes the near-term fixed-cost under-absorption threat from the Norwegian plant.

Jefferies reported in October 2026 that the tender included a collaborative engineering phase to set the grounds for signature. This proactive engineering phase allows the group to align its three cable-laying vessels, including the newly commissioned Electra.

Optimized vessel fleet: Nexans' newly operational cable-laying vessel, Electra, will enhance transmission execution efficiency by reducing reliance on expensive third-party charters. The vessel entered service in the second quarter of 2026 on time and within its €380 million budget. Deutsche Bank reported in June 2026 that Electra was fully loaded with installation contracts through the end of 2028. This high vessel utilization helps protect the segment's profitability even during periods of manufacturing re-phasing.

This complex installation established a recordbreaking depth of 2,150 meters for a 500 kV mass-impregnated direct current subsea cable. This historical success demonstrates that Nexans possesses the specialized maritime engineering expertise required to execute deep Mediterranean installations.

TECHNICAL DIFFERENTIATION AND APPLICATION LIMITS

12.
Specialized subsea technology: Mass-impregnated cables utilize layers of high-density paper tape impregnated with a high-viscosity compound to survive extreme underwater pressures. Deutsche Bank explained in October 2025 that this non-flowing insulation compound is the only qualified solution for water depths exceeding 1,500 meters. This technical restriction makes mass-impregnated technology irreplaceable for deep-sea routes across the Mediterranean.
13.
Severe physical pressures: Extruded XLPE designs face extreme engineering challenges at depths below 1,500 meters due to intense water pressure. A major risk at 3,000 meters deep is that water can easily penetrate tens of kilometers into the conductor if the sheath is damaged. This vulnerability requires complex waterblocking layers that make extruded systems highly expensive compared to mass-impregnated alternatives.
14.
Standard insulation contrast: The Mallorca-Menorca 3 preferred-supplier award announced on October 22, 2026, contrasts sharply with the Mediterranean order by utilizing extruded cross-linked polyethylene technology. This 132 kV HVAC project with Red Eléctrica relies on solid synthetic polymer insulation designed for shallower Balearic island waters. While extruded cables are cheaper, they are not yet certified for the extreme depths that require paper-insulated mass-impregnated systems.
15.
Challenging depth precedents: The SaPeI interconnector, which links Sardinia to mainland Italy, represents a historical precedent for deep-water mass-impregnated cable technology. This cable system was successfully installed at a depth of 1,640 meters below sea level to cross the Tyrrhenian Sea. This historical project confirms that mass-impregnated insulation remains highly reliable in extreme deep-water environments over long operating lifespans.

Section 3 – Mediterranean Government and Regulatory Stances

Section summary
  1. Geopolitical maritime disputes and regulatory revisions create persistent bottlenecks for Eastern Mediterranean subsea interconnectors, contrasting with the smoother administrative pathways of Western Mediterranean links.
  2. Diplomatic tensions between Greece and Turkey over exclusive economic zones highlight the administrative hurdles facing Nexans as it prepares to resume critical deep-water seabed surveys.

Eastern Mediterranean Geopolitical Friction

1.
Sovereign boundary conflicts: Turkish maritime claims in the Aegean Sea present the primary geopolitical obstacle for subsea cable route planning. The Turkish government asserts that the planned route of the Great Sea Interconnector infringes on its sovereign continental shelf. Unresolved territorial disputes could easily delay required seabed surveys or halt construction if naval vessels block cable-laying operations again.
2.
Bilateral pact expansion: The Libyan parliament unanimously ratified a controversial maritime boundary agreement with Turkey in October 2026. This legislative step formally establishes a joint exclusive economic zone that directly intersects the proposed subsea cable corridor southeast of Crete. The Greek government has rejected this agreement because it ignores the exclusive economic zones of Greek islands.
3.
Diplomatic alternative proposed: Turkey has leveraged its territorial claims to propose that regional nations bypass the subsea cable project altogether. Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan announced that Turkey could directly supply Cyprus with electricity, gas, and water. European partners remain highly cautious about this option because it would increase energy dependence on a historically uncooperative regional supplier.
4.
Contrary evidence: Turkish diplomats indicated in October 2026 that Ankara will not actively block the construction of the Eastern Mediterranean interconnector. Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan stated that Turkey would allow the cable installation to proceed if the French and Greek governments provided prior written notification. This diplomatic opening suggests that the geopolitical threat of naval intervention might be mitigated through bilateral communication.

Administrative Reforms and Local Backing

5.
National execution strategy: The Greek government maintains that the regional transmission interconnection is proceeding in accordance with international law. Energy Minister Stavros Papastavrou confirmed in October 2026 that Athens is executing the project according to a systematic plan. This state support is crucial to lift the electrical isolation of Cyprus.
6.
Regulatory timeline revisions: The Cyprus Energy Regulatory Authority officially revised the expected completion date for the Great Sea Interconnector. The regulator pushed the targeted commercial operation date back by one year to January 2031. This regulatory delay reflects the ongoing uncertainty surrounding the final project costs and administrative approvals.
7.
Seabed survey requests: Nexans submitted a formal application to the Greek government in October 2026 to resume offshore technical activities. The cable manufacturer requested official permission to restart the remaining seabed surveys for the Cyprus link. Securing this permit is a critical step to move the delayed power link forward.
8.
Discrete political handling: Cyprus President Nikos Christodoulides announced that interconnector negotiations are being managed quietly. The government is purposely avoiding public statements regarding the exact schedule for upcoming seabed surveys. This cautious approach aims to resolve diplomatic friction away from the public eye.

Western Mediterranean Regulatory Progress

9.
Stronger Western progress: Subsea transmission projects in the Western Mediterranean face fewer political hurdles than their Eastern counterparts. The Italian and Tunisian governments have provided clear regulatory backing for the ELMED interconnector project. This stable political environment allowed the developer to issue a final notice to proceed in June 2026.
10.
Italian legislative support: The Italian Senate passed a comprehensive framework in September 2026 to reintroduce nuclear energy. This regulatory shift is accompanied by Terna expanding its five-year investment plan to €17.7 billion. These state-backed grid upgrades will facilitate the integration of deep-water subsea cables.
11.
Spanish funding amendments: The Spanish government approved Royal Decree 641/2026 in July 2026 to adjust transmission network grants. The administration set the maximum funding allocation at €762 million for strategic decarbonization projects. This clear financial framework supports local connections like the Mallorca-Menorca 3 project to ensure long-term energy security.
12.
Other side of the story: While Western Mediterranean governments offer highly supportive regulatory frameworks, local project execution still faces significant administrative delays. Permitting complexity and lengthy environmental approval processes remain major bottlenecks across Italy and Spain. These bureaucratic delays can extend development timelines by several years and disrupt planned transmission manufacturing schedules.

Section 4 – EPCI Contract Status and Commercial Terms

Section summary

  2. Structural risk management frameworks, including metal price pass-throughs and labor indexation, protect Nexans' long-term transmission margins from raw material volatility and cost escalation.

Proactive engineering: Nexans has already initiated early engineering work on the Mediterranean project in anticipation of the final award. Analysts expect the official contract award to occur before the end of 2026. This preemptive execution secures the manufacturing schedule at the Halden plant.

Contrary evidence: Bernstein Research highlighted on October 22, 2026, that the specific customer, exact route, and total financial value of the replacement project remain entirely undisclosed to the market. This lack of contractual visibility prevents independent verification of the project's ultimate margin contribution. The ongoing non-disclosure leaves the company vulnerable to late-stage changes or pricing revisions.

Metal and Labor Cost Protections

4.
Secured protection: Nexans mitigates raw material risk through standard contract terms that pass copper and aluminum price variations fully to the end customer. Management confirmed in February 2026 that metal price adjustments occur with zero time lag, insulating segment EBITDA. This hedging framework protects the company's margins against severe commodity volatility.
5.
Standardized risk: Peer manufacturer NKT A/S operates under similar contract structures that pass raw material commodity risks entirely to its customers. This standard protection protects the peer's transmission margins from volatile metals pricing. These contract safeguards qualify European cable peers for resilient valuation premium.
6.
Indexed safeguards: Nexans incorporates labor cost indexation or escalation clauses into its long-term project contracts to cover potential wage inflation. Labor expenses represent approximately 20% of the overall cable manufacturing cost base. These contractual protections insulate the group's transmission margins from sudden labor cost shocks.

Advance Payments and Financial Guarantees

7.
Favorable cash: Nexans relies heavily on customer down-payments and advance cash receipts to manage its working capital on large-scale transmission projects. These partial payments are recorded as contract liabilities before the corresponding work is performed. The majority of these advance commitments are typically recognized as revenue within two years.
8.
Upfront liquidity: Strategic down-payments from major interconnector tenders provide Nexans with the upfront capital needed to expand its industrial footprint. These upfront cash injections reduce the group's net debt and lower overall project financing risks. This funding structure allows Nexans to commit resources to deepwater projects.
9.
Substantial exposure: Nexans provides extensive parent company guarantees and performance bonds to its customers to secure advance payments. The group's outstanding parent guarantees stood at €5.50 billion at the end of December 2025. These bonds expose the group to potential cash drains if quality disputes arise.

Risk Reviews and Liquidated Damages

10.
Systematic vetting: Nexans subjects all high-voltage subsea bids exceeding €25 million to the Group Tender Review Committee. This committee reviews project parameters to manage the risk of liquidated damages for delivery delays. This systematic process helps prevent margin erosion on complex subsea turnkey installations.
11.
Rigid scheduling: A past subsea installation contract with Equinor ASA highlights that Nexans operates primarily under fixed lump-sum turnkey contracts. These agreements enforce strict penalties and liquidated damages if cable deliveries are delayed. In this historical case, Nexans and Equinor shared alternative logistics costs to preserve the installation schedule.
12.
Other side of the story: While Nexans' risk management policies and metal hedging agreements provide robust baseline protections, the company cannot guarantee that its commercial insurance policies will fully cover major liability claims. Insurance coverage remains capped at annual levels and typically excludes latedelivery penalties and product replacement costs. Consequently, severe execution issues can still lead to substantial unhedged losses.

Severe Financial Downside Scenarios

1.
Severe revenue contraction: If the July 2026 mass-impregnated replacement order fails to finalize, Nexans faces immediate under-utilization at the Halden plant. Bernstein Research warned in July 2026 that the GSI postponement already created a risk of under-absorption of fixed costs. This failure would directly hit the PWR-Transmission adjusted EBITDA margins. Management's high-teens margin target by 2028 would be highly compromised as a result.
2.
Decreased segment margins: The cancellation of GSI without a rapid replacement represents a significant risk to Nexans' financial targets. Deutsche Bank Research estimated in January 2026 that failing to mitigate GSI revenues would lead to a 7% to 17% cut in Transmission EBITDA. Idle capacity on the paper-insulated massimpregnated line at Halden cannot be easily repurposed for extruded XLPE cable systems. This technical constraint directly threatens the segment's overall margin expansion trajectory.
3.
Idle vessel capacity: Underutilized marine logistics assets pose another major threat to Nexans' long-term earnings progression. The newly commissioned cable-laying vessel, Electra, cost approximately €380 million to build and enter service. While Deutsche Bank noted that Electra is booked through 2028, this relies on executing major subsea installation contracts. Project delays would force costly under-utilization of marine assets and dent transmission earnings.
4.
Contrary evidence: Deutsche Bank Research suggests that even in a worst-case scenario, the immediate financial damage is well-contained. Deutsche Bank Research calculated in August 2025 that a complete GSI cancellation with zero mitigation through 2029 would only represent €5 per share of downside. The firm can partially offset idle capacity by bringing forward lucrative subsea repair and maintenance contracts. This flexibility helps insulate near-term adjusted EBITDA from collapse.

Competitive Imbalances and Market Shifts

5.
Lost market share: Any prolonged delay in finalizing Nexans' subsea orders allows competitors to capture dominant positions in the European market. Prysmian boasts approximately double the mass-impregnated manufacturing capacity of Nexans through its Arco Felice facility near Naples. These massive rival capacity advantages present immediate headwinds to Nexans' long-term subsea growth plans. Any execution gap would lead to persistent subsea market share erosion for Nexans.
6.
Expanding peer capacity: Peer companies are executing extensive capacity upgrades that threaten Nexans' competitive standing. NKT is investing €2 billion in cumulative capital expenditures through 2028 to expand highvoltage manufacturing. This aggressive investment program supports a massive transmission backlog that exceeds €13 billion. This scale allows peer groups to offer more competitive project delivery schedules to key utility customers.
7.
Rising Asian competition: Emerging Asian cable manufacturers pose a growing threat to the established European subsea triopoly. Morgan Stanley Research projected that Asian players will expand their market share by nine percentage points by 2030. These competitors are rapidly expanding manufacturing capacity to capture global grid and interconnector demand. This intensifying global competition would permanently depress pricing power in subsea tenders.
8.
Restricted client flexibility: Transmission operators have strong incentives to diversify their supplier base but face severe capacity bottlenecks. A consultant at an electricity transmission operator reported that procurement activity increased significantly in late 2026. Because Prysmian, Nexans, and NKT operate at nearly full capacity, clients cannot easily switch vendors. This tight supply landscape prevents rapid shifts in near-term supplier allocation.
