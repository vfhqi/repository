Section 1 – Brief company description and history

Section summary
  1. INDUS entered Q1 2026 as a diversified, long-duration industrial holding company with improving order visibility and a credible acquisition strategy, but the earnings bar was still modest because 2025 profitability had fallen and the German industrial cycle remained weak.
1.
Long-term industrial investor: Founded in 1989 and listed since 1995, INDUS acquires majority stakes in owner-managed German-speaking Mittelstand companies, retains their management teams and develops them without the exit timetable typical of private-equity owners. The model meant investors entered Q1 expecting gradual portfolio compounding rather than a sharp cyclical earnings rebound, although management’s 2030 plan raised the potential for faster growth through acquisitions, internationalisation and engineering competence.
2.
Three balanced operating pillars: Engineering covers automation, machinery, measurement, process and media technology; Infrastructure covers construction, building, telecommunications and energy infrastructure; and Materials Solutions covers metal technology, materials processing, recycling and medical or rehabilitation products. At year-end 2025, the segments were similar in revenue scale, with Engineering at €583.0 million, Infrastructure at €597.2 million and Materials Solutions at €554.5 million, reducing single-segment concentration relative to a conventional industrial specialist. 19 Conference feedback: Focus on tungsten, growth drivers and M&A INDUS segment revenue mix and projected sales growth Broad portfolio, concentrated economics: The portfolio contained 43 companies at December 31, 2025, comprising 16 Engineering businesses, 14 Infrastructure businesses and 13 Materials Solutions businesses. The diversification reduced dependence on any one customer, product or end market because no individual product group or customer generated more than 10% of group revenue, but the later tungsten windfall showed that one portfolio company, BETEK, could still dominate incremental earnings.
3.
Deep ownership supports resilience: Twenty-five companies, or 58% of the portfolio, had been owned for more than 15 years at the end of 2025, while approximately 75% had been held for more than 10 years according to NuWays’ company profile. That longevity differentiated INDUS from buy-and-sell private equity and supported the market’s expectation that management could preserve capacity and customer relationships through a weak industrial cycle. International exposure offsets Germany, imperfectly: Germany accounted for 47% of 2025 revenue, while foreign markets represented 53%, including 20% from the European Union and 12% from the United States. The mix was more internationally diversified than a purely domestic German industrial company, although NuWays still identified dependence on European markets and limited geographic diversification as structural weaknesses entering the results. Three-year revenue record was weak before the rebound: Group revenue was €1.802 billion in 2023, declined to €1.722 billion in 2024 and recovered only to €1.735 billion in 2025, leaving 2025 approximately 3.7% below 2023 calculated. Against that history, the pre-Q1 NuWays forecast of €1.921 billion for 2026 implied a much stronger 10.7% increase, but depended partly on acquisitions and the unusual tungsten-related uplift rather than a broad industrial recovery. Profitability had not yet recovered: Adjusted earnings before interest, tax and amortisation of purchase-price allocation effects and impairments fell from €195.4 million in 2023 to €162.7 million in 2024 and €147.8 million in 2025, while the adjusted EBITA margin declined from 10.8% to 8.5% across those years. The market therefore expected 2026 improvement from a depressed base, not a return to a proven new margin regime, making the subsequent Materials Solutions surprise particularly important but also unusually difficult to extrapolate. Engineering was the main recovery option: Engineering revenue declined 2.3% in 2025 to €583.0 million and its adjusted EBITA fell to €53.7 million from €57.7 million, but order intake rose 26.9% to €667.3 million and year-end backlog increased 14.4% to €401.2 million. The expectation entering Q1 was therefore for delayed revenue conversion in 2026 to 2028, particularly from long-term plant-engineering projects, rather than immediate margin expansion.
4.
Infrastructure supplied the steadier base: Infrastructure revenue increased 6.7% in 2025 to €597.2 million, including 4.3% organic growth, while adjusted EBITA was €62.1 million against €63.6 million in 2024. Expectations entering Q1 were more constructive than for Engineering because public infrastructure, digital networks, construction activity and energy-related investment offered structural support, although the company still guided only to moderate revenue growth.
5.
Materials Solutions carried the largest risk: Materials Solutions revenue fell 1.8% in 2025 to €554.5 million, but adjusted EBITA increased 4.0% to €51.9 million and the margin rose to 9.4% from 8.8%. Before Q1, management expected higher material costs, Chinese export controls, United States tariffs and increased working capital to pressure the segment, with guidance calling for sharply lower earnings, so the subsequent tungsten-driven earnings upside deviated most dramatically from the original operating case. 2025 delivery met the published bar but not a growth bar: INDUS reported 2025 revenue of €1.735 billion within its €1.70 billion to €1.85 billion guidance range and adjusted EBITA of €147.8 million within the €130 million to €165 million range. NuWays described revenue as broadly in line with its €1.74 billion estimate, while adjusted EBITA was ahead of expectations, but the market still entered 2026 focused on margin recovery, cash generation and portfolio execution rather than assuming a sharp earnings acceleration.
6.
Cash generation had been a valuation anchor: Free cash flow reached €124.0 million in 2025 versus €135.4 million in 2024, and the company proposed a €1.30 dividend after paying €1.20 previously. This supported the pre-results investment case because INDUS could fund dividends and acquisitions from internally generated cash, but the company’s initial 2026 free-cash-flow target of above €70 million already acknowledged that higher raw-material prices could absorb working capital. Balance-sheet expectations were disciplined: INDUS ended 2025 with a 38.4% equity ratio and a net-debt repayment period of 2.5 years, compared with 2.4 years in 2024. Management’s stated target was to keep leverage around 2 to 2.5 times earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation, so the market expected acquisition-led growth to remain compatible with balance-sheet discipline rather than require a material gearing step-up. - Conference The 2030 strategy raised the long-term ceiling: INDUS planned to invest more than €100 million annually in acquisitions by 2030 and more than €500 million in total, with the objective of adding over €600 million of annual revenue. NuWays’ February view assumed a €100 million 2026 acquisition budget, at least three additional deals and approximately €25 million to €30 million of related revenue, so expectations entering Q1 included an M&A contribution but still required execution to convert the strategy into EPS growth. Broker sentiment improved before Q1: NuWays analyst Christian Sandherr maintained a Buy rating while raising the target from €34 in December 2025 to €34.50 in February and €35.50 later that month, as preliminary 2025 EBITA and the PRO VIDEO and AMIRA transactions improved the 2026 earnings case. After the April tungsten-related guidance revision, NuWays analyst Sarah Hellemann raised the target again to €37 while maintaining Buy and lifted 2026 revenue estimates from €1.83 billion to €1.92 billion and adjusted EBITA from €167 million to €179 million. Share-price momentum was positive but volatile: NuWays recorded relative share-price performance of 17.7% over three months, 35.7% over six months and 40.1% over 12 months as of February 13, 2026, with the shares at €30.25 and the rating at Buy. By July 29, after the market had absorbed the tungsten-driven guidance revision, NuWays showed three-month relative performance of approximately negative 0.7%, sixmonth relative performance of negative 7.6% and 12-month relative performance of positive 26.2%, indicating that the pre-results setup was not a straightforward momentum story. Section summary
  1. Q1 improved 2026 EPS, not normalized EPS, because the earnings surprise raised the near- term operating outlook through tungsten pricing and stronger orders, while cash conversion, leverage and the company’s own disclosures argue against carrying the exceptional margin into 2027 to 2029.
