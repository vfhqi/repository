Section 3 – Unit cost dynamics and contract structures

Section summary
  1. The massive cost advantages of edge gas projects, combined with flexible contract structures, insulate operators from global benchmark price volatility while maintaining superior margins.

Low-Cost Edge Versus Legacy Portfolios

1.
Competitive development: The joint operators of Neptun Deep are advancing on budget with a EUR 4 billion CAPEX development plan scheduled for first gas in 1H 2027.
2.
Unrivalled unit opex: OMV Petrom highlights that the Neptun Deep deepwater gas field will achieve an average plateau production cost of ~3 USD/boe.
3.
Carbon profile excellence: The Neptun Deep project expects a green footprint of 2.2 kg CO₂/boe at plateau, which sits significantly below the global industry average.
4.
Structural cost reduction: OMV Petrom expects Neptun Deep volumes to reduce average group unit production costs from USD 17.8/boe in 2025 to ~USD 8/boe by 2030.
5.
Standardized subsea execution: Var Energi targets a low-cost volume capture by utilizing a standardized "project factory" tie-back approach to achieve a long-term unit opex of ~USD 10/boe.
6.
Outperforming near-term opex: Morgan Stanley notes that key Norwegian startups like Balder X and Johan Castberg can achieve peak operating costs of USD 3-5/boe.
7.
Robust cost discipline: Energean successfully maintained its cost of operations excluding royalties at USD 6/boe year-on-year through tight control of cash general and administrative expenses.

Contractual Pricing Floors and Regulatory Regimes

8.
Market liberalization shift: OMV Petrom reported that the proportion of its Romanian gas sales subject to regulated prices decreased to 28% in Q2 2026.
9.
Export revenue security: Romgaz has achieved an agreement in principle to acquire Azomureș, securing a robust domestic industrial outlet for its share of Black Sea gas volumes.
10.
Defensive pricing insulation: Energean has protected its cash flow by securing long-term domestic gas contracts with robust floor pricing over twenty years.

Storage Mandates and Pricing Power Windows

11.
Flexible inventory mandates: Regulation EU 2025/1733 extends storage filling targets to 90% before winter but allows member states to achieve this flexibly between October and December.
12.
Disincentivized commercial storing: Bernstein Research argues that high summer LNG prices and an inverted seasonal spread removed the financial incentive for commercial operators to store gas.
13.
Regulated filling premiums: Italy’s ARERA Resolution 112/2026 introduced a storage premium mechanism to compensate bookers and support the filling of inventories despite unfavorable market spreads.

Fiscal Pressures and Realized Price Limitations

14.
Persistent fiscal headwinds: OMV Petrom warns that domestic E&P activities are subject to rising concession charges including a potential 40% increase in state royalty rates.
15.
Other side of the story: Fitch warns that Energean realizes lower average prices in Israel than European peer Romgaz, leaving its credit profile vulnerable to domestic demand caps until the Nitzana export pipeline is completed in late 2028.
