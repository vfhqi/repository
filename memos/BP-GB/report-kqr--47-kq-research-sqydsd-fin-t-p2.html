Section 2 – Downstream Refining Margin Capture and Complexity

Section summary
  1. BP achieved leading European refining margin capture of 64% in H1 2026 due to its complex portfolio configuration.
  2. Realized margins frequently diverge from indicator markers due to volatile feedstock discounts and freight rate cycles.

Testing the Jefferies Capture Claims

1.
Superior performance: Jefferies reported in September 2026 that BP captured 64% of global refining margin markers during the first half of 2026. This benchmark outperformed Shell's capture rate of 55% and TotalEnergies' capture rate of 35% over the same period. Analysts attributed this outperformance to the high complexity of BP's refining footprint compared to European peers. This capture capability supports stronger earnings per share during periods of elevated mid-cycle refining cracks.

Structural vs Cyclical Margin Gap

2.
Indicator divergence: Jefferies reported in May 2026 that BP's realized margins fell over $5/bbl below its refining indicator margin. This discount was driven by feedstock constraints, product yield volatility, and elevated shipping freight rates. While some portion of the freight impact is cyclical, feedstock availability represents a structural headwind for European coastal refineries processing non-standard crudes. Persistent freight volatility will likely continue to depress realized margins.

Operational Refinery Drivers

3.
Midwest anchor: BP's Whiting refinery has a crude distillation capacity of 440 kbpd and can process up to 400 kbpd of heavy Canadian crude. This heavy crude processing capability serves as a significant earnings multiplier for the group when Western Canadian Select discounts widen. However, earnings remain sensitive to operational issues, as shown by the plant-wide power outage that reduced availability in 2024. Whiting's reliability is critical because it represents BP's largest refining asset.

Management stated that at least two percentage points of this average gain are generated by its structural global asset portfolio.

This exceptional trading execution is estimated to represent roughly one-third of group earnings during underperforming operational periods. But because trading results are not reported as a standalone segment, investors struggle to model the sustainability of these earnings.

Contrary evidence: Morgan Stanley reported that BP's quarterly earnings delivery has faced extreme volatility since 2023 due to weaker-than-expected trading contributions. This underperformance was highlighted by eight consecutive quarters of below-consensus earnings between mid-2023 and early 2025. These unexpected results show that the proprietary trading premium is highly cyclical and can quickly evaporate in calm market environments.

Accelerated returns: Jefferies reported that the historical return on average capital employed contribution from BP trading was only two percentage points in previous cycles. The recent double-digit volatility driven by geopolitical tensions has structurally increased the trading capture rate. While management plans to maintain this four percentage point performance target, the normalization of global energy flows could compress future trading margins.

Section 4 – Upstream Liquids Realisations and Contract Structures

Section summary

  3. Confidence is high that BP's upstream realisations are structurally constrained by pricing lags and production-sharing contracts, backed by detailed disclosures in 2026 interim reports.

Price Realisations and Benchmark Tracking

1.
Average realisations: BP reported a global average liquids realisation of USD 85.14 per barrel in the second quarter of 2026. This average price fell significantly below the dated Brent oil marker price of USD 103.85 per barrel recorded in the same period. The discount highlights that BP's crude portfolio faces substantial realisation discounts during market spikes due to regional pricing dynamics. This variance directly impacts upstream replacement cost operating profit when benchmarks rally.

Regional pricing: BP experienced wide regional variations in its crude pricing during the second quarter of 2026. The company captured realisations of USD 76.03 per barrel in the United States and USD 97.49 per barrel in Europe. Meanwhile, realisations for the rest of the world averaged USD 95.39 per barrel during this period. These regional gaps show how North American heavy crude discounts weigh on average group realisations.
3.

Realisation Lags and Segment Impacts

5.
GoM lag: BP's operations in the Gulf of Mexico experience a structural pricing lag of approximately one month. This means that crude sales in the Gulf are priced using lagged benchmarks where March prices are captured in second quarter earnings. This mechanism prevents the company from immediately capturing rapid price increases in its upstream reporting. Consequently, quarterly earnings often surprise the market when spot prices pivot.
6.
UAE lag: BP's crude production in the United Arab Emirates is priced on a two-month lagged basis. Despite this pricing lag, approximately 90% of BP's Abu Dhabi volumes are transported safely by pipeline to Fujairah. This export route helps bypass the highly sensitive Strait of Hormuz during geopolitical disruptions. However, the delayed pricing structure means that geopolitical risk premiums are deferred in BP's reported financial results.

Production Sharing Contract Entitlement Effects

8.
PSC dilution: BP's production-sharing agreements dictate that the company recovers costs through variable physical hydrocarbon volumes. When commodity prices spike, the monetary cost recovery requires fewer physical barrels, which reduces BP's entitlement volumes. This entitlement effect creates an inverse relationship between prices and volumes in key developing regions. This volume dilution limits BP's overall upstream upside during periods of exceptional market volatility.
9.
Egypt contracts: Egypt represents a key area where production-sharing contract terms are undergoing structural modernization. Industry players are actively lobbying the Egyptian General Petroleum Corporation to align cost recovery and profit share incentives. For instance, Pharos Energy agreed to new concession terms in 2025 that consolidated its acreage and improved its cost recovery ceiling. This contractual shift provides stronger incentives for upstream investment in mature Egyptian basins.
10.
Angola Azerbaijan: BP's complex upstream agreements in Angola, Azerbaijan, and Oman remain highly sensitive to local price caps. The company's reported production can fluctuate significantly based on how these local formulas allocate host government shares. In volatile environments, the differences between marker prices and realisations become highly dislocated. This friction suggests that entitlement effects act as caps on international pricing power.

Iraq Rumaila and Service Terms

11.
Rumaila contract: BP has operated the super-giant Rumaila oil field in Iraq under technical service terms since 2009. Under these service agreements, the contractor receives a capped flat fee per barrel instead of capturing high spot prices. This contractual structure means that the Government of Iraq captures all the upside benefits of elevated oil prices. This service model restricts BP's direct upstream pricing power in the Middle East.
12.
Iraq pressure: The technical service contracts in Iraq created severe financial pressure on the host government during the 2014 to 2016 oil price collapse. Because contract payments to international oil companies are made in physical barrels, a lower oil price requires increasingly higher volume payments. This dynamic severely impacted the Iraqi national budget as the government's remaining share declined. This imbalance forces host governments to seek activity reductions when markets crash.

Stable volumes: BP maintains a substantial global LNG supply book that anchors its gas segment cash flow. The integrated major plans to expand its LNG portfolio to deliver 25 million tonnes per annum by 2025. This strategic growth is supported by major project expansions including the Tangguh expansion in Indonesia and Coral. Delivering on these targets is critical to improving group return on average capital employed.
3.
Contract indexation: BP utilizes a mix of long-term and spot volumes to optimize its overall pricing capture. The company's offtake book is heavily weighted toward defensive long-term gas contracts that protect against declining spot benchmarks. These long-term agreements typically feature oil-indexed slopes with pricing lags that average nine months, helping smooth out short-term commodity price drops. This contract mix supports cash flow predictability during market downcycles.

Middle East exposure: BP retains a modest operational reliance on Middle Eastern supply networks that can introduce localized pricing bottlenecks. Around five to ten percent of BP's global LNG portfolio is sourced through Middle East assets. This concentration exposes a portion of BP's volumes to transit disruptions.
