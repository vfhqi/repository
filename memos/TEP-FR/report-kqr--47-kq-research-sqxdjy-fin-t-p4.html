Section 2 – Operating Drivers: Recurring EBITA Margin and Currency Dynamics

Section summary
  1. Teleperformance faces operational pressure in reaching its 15.5% EBITA target by 2028 due to accelerating Trust and Safety declines and structural offshoring.
  2. Currency fluctuations present significant translation risks as dollar sensitivity and cost exposure in offshore hubs pressure margins despite hedging programs.

3.

Their forecasts show adjusted EBITA margins remaining stagnant at 14.6% in FY2028 compared to 14.4% in FY2026. These projections reflect expectations of low-to-mid single-digit downgrades to consensus figures as organic revenue declines persist.
4.
Unchanged expectations: Rothschild & Co Redburn maintains a flat projection for Teleperformance's margin progression. Their summary financials show adjusted EBITA margins holding steady at 14.5% through FY2028 from the FY2026 expectation. This stagnant forecast reflects ongoing structural challenges as automation from artificial intelligence begins to erode traditional customer care volumes.
5.
Contrary evidence: Offshore volume migration could accelerate margin expansion faster than consensus expects because offshoring is structurally margin accretive. For example, Concentrix management noted that as work shifts offshore, the operating margin percentage actually increases even as absolute revenue compresses. This structural tailwind could allow Teleperformance to defend its profitability if offshoring accelerates.

Currency and Translation Sensitivities

6.
Significant volatility: Teleperformance remains highly vulnerable to currency translation risks because its functional operations span multiple global currencies. For instance, the company suffered a negative currency translation impact of EUR 165 million on reported revenue during the first half of FY2026. To mitigate these exposures, management implements hedging operations using forward contracts that typically run up to twelve months.
7.
Offshore concentration: Teleperformance utilizes massive labor-arbitrage advantages in low-cost offshore hubs to support its operating margins. The Philippine peso represents 25% of the company's cost of goods sold, while the Indian rupee represents 15%. Because wages in India are c. 90% below the United States, offshoring remains highly margin accretive despite wage inflation.
