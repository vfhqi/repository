Plausible problems to monitor

Approximately 30% of group EBITDA remains exposed to industrial activity and macroeconomic downturns, with cyclical components including commercial and industrial waste and construction segments that directly track industrial production cycles, introducing material earnings volatility compared to pure-play regulated water utilities that exhibit near-zero economic cyclicality [Cross-ref: Section 1]. This cyclical exposure creates periodic margin compression risks during industrial recessions that cannot be fully mitigated through contract indexation or pricing discipline alone.

Roughly 70% of municipal contracts suffer a six to twelve month lag to fully pass through rising costs, with indexation formulas typically tied to historical inflation baskets, creating temporary but recurring margin pressure during inflationary spikes that exceeds the three-month lags observed at best-in-class regulated utilities with more frequent tariff adjustment mechanisms [Cross-ref: Section 5, Section 6]. This structural timing mismatch caused a €60 million EBITDA headwind during first-half 2026 operations, demonstrating the material financial impact of imperfect inflation protection despite contractual indexation clauses.

Net financial debt increased to €24,548 million in mid-2026, driven by €2,778 million in Clean Earth acquisition payments, pushing leverage toward 3.0x EBITDA levels that sit at the upper end of the self-imposed ceiling and meaningfully above the sub-2.5x leverage profiles maintained by investment-grade environmental services peers [Cross-ref: Section 2]. While management targets deleveraging below this threshold by end-2027, the elevated leverage position temporarily constrains financial flexibility and increases refinancing risks compared to more conservatively capitalized competitors during periods of rising interest rates.
