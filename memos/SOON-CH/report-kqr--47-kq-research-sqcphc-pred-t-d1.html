It does not yet support the strengths Sonova would need to acquire: retail integration at scale, Swiss-franc cost localisation and leadership delivery on footprint savings. The financial stakes are high because Sonova raised constant currency revenue growth guidance to 6% to 8% and core EBIT growth to 10% to 13%, so the plausibility of that raised guidance carries real weight for the EPS trajectory. Section 2 – Foreign Exchange and Swiss-Franc Earnings Conversion

Section summary
  1. Sonova has consistently failed to convert robust local-currency growth into reported Swiss-franc earnings per share since financial year 2019/20 because of relentless translation headwinds and a rising effective tax rate.
  2. Swiss exporters are actively localizing production to reduce their expensive Swiss-franc cost base below ten percent, but execution remains gradual and capital-intensive.

Currency Headwinds and

1.
Reported Growth Severe translation: Sonova reported that the appreciation of the Swiss franc has severely diluted its financial performance by shaving off substantial revenues during translation. According to the Sonova half-year earnings call on November 14, 2025, strong appreciation of the Swiss franc reduced reported sales by CHF 107 million or 5.8% during the first half of the year. This persistent translation pressure makes it extremely difficult for the group to convert organic top-line momentum into reported Swiss-franc earnings.
2.
Unbalanced costs: Sonova faces structural margin pressure because its headquarters and core research operations remain heavily anchored in Switzerland. Management noted in the interim earnings call on November 14, 2025, that while foreign exchange movements reduced operating expenses in Swiss francs by 3.8%, this relief was less pronounced than the impact on sales. This mismatch occurs because key functions such as research and development are largely located in Switzerland, creating an unfavorable currency mismatch for operating expenses.
3.
Detracting factors: Sonova has struggled to show clean earnings per share growth since financial year 2019/20 due to various structural and transactional items. A Jefferies broker note on May 9, 2025, indicated that reported basic earnings per share of CHF 5.57 was heavily impacted by rising tax rates and higher financing costs. These headwind items collectively drag down reported earnings, proving that reported Swiss-franc profitability remains highly vulnerable to non-operational volatility.
4.
Contrary evidence: Sonova actually delivered reported earnings per share expansion in some earlier periods when currency movements stabilized. According to the Sonova annual report on June 6, 2025, adjusted earnings per share rose by 7.4% in reported Swiss francs to CHF 10.81 during the 2024/25 financial year. This proves that when local currency growth is exceptionally strong, the company can overcome translation headwinds and deliver positive reported growth.
5.
Historical comparisons: Sonova experienced similar foreign exchange challenges in previous product cycles where currency movements diluted strong operational performance. In the earnings call on November 19, 2019, management reported a top-line foreign exchange headwind of nearly CHF 33 million and an adjusted operating profit headwind of CHF 12 million. This historical precedent highlights that reported earnings remain structurally capped by the strength of the Swiss currency.

  3. Soft private-pay consumer demand and capped reimbursement across Europe and the United States force upgrade deferrals and dilute wholesale average selling prices.
