Section 1 – Brief company description and history 14 findings

Section summary
  1. NN Group enters H1 2026 with resilient core capital generation despite mounting competitive asymmetries in its Japanese COLI business.
1.
Corporate heritage – ING divestment: NN Group was established from the legacy European insurance and Japanese operations of ING Group, listing via an initial public offering in July 2014 before ING fully exited its holding by April 2016. This structural history created a robust foundation, allowing the group to establish top market positions in group pensions, individual life, and non-life insurance
2.
Domestic dominance – Dutch leadership: The group maintains a leading Dutch market footprint, holding a c. 40% market share in group pensions and a 23% market share in individual life lines. This domestic strength anchors the business model while management executes an explicit strategy to grow its international protection footprint away from traditional life books
3.
Segmental breadth – diversified operations: NN Group operates across five core business segments, including Netherlands Life, Netherlands Non-Life, Insurance Europe, Japan Life, and Banking. Management expects its high-growth international businesses and Dutch P&C lines to contribute over 55% of total OCG by 2028, reducing its historic reliance on the mature domestic life run-off portfolio
4.
Capital generation – absolute growth: On an absolute basis, NN Group has delivered stable fundamental progress, growing its annual Operating Capital Generation from EUR 1,902 million in FY23 to EUR 1,922 million in FY24. This metric reached EUR 2.1 billion in FY25, beating the company's initial 2025 target of EUR 1.9 billion on the back of rising interest rates
5.
Cash conversion – progressive remittances: The group's free cash flow grew from EUR 1,410 million in FY22 to EUR 1,519 million in FY24, and ultimately reached EUR 1.62 billion in FY25. This robust cash generation supported a progressive payout, allowing NN to deliver an interim 2026 dividend of EUR 1.55, up 12% year-on-year
6.
Peer dynamics – Aegon transformation: Unlike NN Group's focus on organic consolidation, peer competitor Aegon completed a radical restructuring by relocating its head office to the US and selling its Dutch operations to ASR in July 2023. This structural move left NN Group and ASR Nederland as the dominant players in the domestic Dutch pension consolidation market
7.
Competitive comparison – capital returns: In FY25, Aegon delivered an Operating Capital Generation of EUR 1,287 million, which beat compiled consensus of EUR 1,230 million by 4.6%. In comparison, NN Group reported a stronger FY25 OCG of EUR 2.1 billion, beating its company-compiled consensus of EUR 2.0 billion by 5% and proving its superior absolute scale
8.
Consolidation race – ASR integration: Peer competitor ASR Nederland completed the integration of Aegon NL within three years, reporting record organic capital creation in H1 2026. However, NN Group remains highly resilient, leveraging its dominant 40% pension market share to capture massive defined contribution transition flows under the ongoing Dutch pension reform
9.
Share performance – substantial outperformance: NN Group's share price closed at EUR 81.20 as of August 2026, marking a robust recovery within a 52-week range of EUR 56.48 to EUR 81.88. This represents a c. 40% year-to-date gain, outperforming the European Insurance sector by c. 20%, which contrasts with the 20% underperformance seen in 2023
