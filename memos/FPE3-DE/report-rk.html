Section 10 – Pre-Mortem: Executive Summary

The pre-mortem framework assumes that Fuchs SE has comprehensively failed as an investment over the next twelve to twenty-four months, requiring investors to reverse-engineer which combination of structural vulnerabilities and operational missteps proved fatal. This analysis evaluates three competing hypothesis profiles: the YES profile assumes Fuchs sustains its specialty niche and successfully scales new electric vehicle solutions, the NO profile assumes rapid volume destruction from electrification and permanent margin squeeze, while the FALSE FRIEND profile assumes headline growth masks core decay and massive working capital cash drain.

The winning hypothesis is determined by having the absolute fewest hard inconsistencies rather than supporting facts. The YES profile emerges as the most coherent framework with only two hard inconsistencies relating to cash flow and working capital expansion, while the NO profile contains six hard inconsistencies as record organic sales volumes and margin expansion contradict failure, and the FALSE FRIEND profile contains four hard inconsistencies as industrial and specialty trends continue showing robust underlying demand.

The diagnostic evidence reveals that H1 sales rose 11% led by temporarily higher customer demand which contradicts the NO profile, while engine oil representing only 10% of revenue insulates the group from rapid EV volume destruction. However, the EUR 139 million NOWC outflow represents a severe cash conversion drag that challenges the YES profile, and FCF of EUR 61 million indicates underlying cash flow pressure despite strong reported sales.

The aggregated failure probability is low compared to the specialty chemicals peer average of medium. The clustering analysis demonstrates that geopolitical supply disruptions in the Middle East represent the primary margin failure root cause, with surging base oil costs coinciding with net working capital outflows of €139 million in H1 2026, compounded by a three-to-six-month pricing pass-through lag.
