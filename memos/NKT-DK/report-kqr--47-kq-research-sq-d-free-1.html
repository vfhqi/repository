Section 2 – Transmission Backlog Price-Vintage Composition

Section summary
  1. NKT's record EUR 13.0bn Transmission backlog is rapidly shifting toward high-margin vintages as older legacy projects are completed.
  2. Commercial delivery schedules stretch to 2033 with substantial

execution front-loaded after 2027 when major factory expansions commence commercial operations.

Backlog pricing eras and composition

1.
Granular vintage split: DNB Carnegie calculates that the average contract value for 525kV high-voltage direct current projects has increased from EUR 0.62m per kilometre in the 2019-20 legacy era to transitional pricing of EUR 1.17m for contracts signed in 2022-23.
2.
Unprecedented pricing growth: DNB Carnegie reports that average revenue per kilometre for 525kV high- voltage direct current projects reached EUR 1.44m in the 2024-26 era, representing a twofold pricing increase since 2019-20 due to extreme supplier capacity constraints and expanded project scopes.
3.
Rapid backlog expansion: NKT managed to more than double its Transmission order backlog, which reached EUR 13.0bn in H1 2026, up from EUR 4.7bn at year-end 2022.
4.

5.
Shifting application mix: NKT commercial presentations indicate that the backlog is heavily weighted toward interconnectors, which represent around 70 percent of orders while offshore wind projects comprise 30 percent.
6.
Significant revenue capacity: DNB Carnegie estimates that this improved project pricing, combined with organic factory capacity expansions in Karlskrona, Sweden, and Cologne, Germany, will drive Transmission revenue capacity to EUR 4.0bn by 2030, presenting massive consensus upgrade potential.

Backlog execution and delivery schedules

7.
Front-loaded execution profile: NKT corporate disclosures show that 33 to 35 percent of the high- voltage backlog is scheduled for execution in 2026-27.

11.
Execution bottleneck risks: Jefferies cautions that NKT's significant backlog execution depends heavily on the successful, timely commissioning of the Karlskrona factory expansion in Q2 2027.
12.
Other side of the story: Although the record backlog provides long-term visibility, DNB Carnegie warns that flat to lower Transmission revenues in 2026 due to the Champlain Hudson project ramp-down will delay market appreciation of the multi-year high-margin backlog inflection.

Section 3 – Contractual Protections: Indexation, Re-openers, and Penalties

Section summary
  1. NKT shields its Transmission profitability through robust commodity indexing while project-level negotiations secure critical capacity reservation fees.
  2. Turnkey contract terms pass base metal inflation directly to clients, but labor and civil works present unhedged execution risks.

Project-level terms and prepayments

1.
Customary prepayments: NKT Chief Financial Officer Michael Yong confirmed that prepayments for the massive Eastern Green Link 3 project remain within a customary commercial range to help manage stable corporate cash flow.
2.
Milestone payments: Standard high-voltage turnkey projects utilize a structured cash profile starting with upfront prepayments at contract inception, followed by progress payments linked to key project engineering milestones.
3.
Scope expansion: SSEN Transmission awarded NKT a combined €2bn final contract for the Western Isles and Spittal to Peterhead links in January 2026, increasing from the 2023 estimate due to inclusion of full installation scope.
4.
Capacity reservation: The initial capacity reservation for the Scottish SSEN projects was secured in August 2023, reflecting a tight supplier capacity environment where clients must secure manufacturing slots early.
5.
Framework call-offs: TenneT framework projects including LanWin7 and NordOstLink, valued at approximately €1bn, are structured as firm commitments called off between 2026 and 2027 to align with long-term commissioning.
6.
Alliander and Dutch links: Turnkey grid connection contracts with European partners typically require structured payment guarantees to minimize counterparty credit risk during the multi-year manufacturing cycle.

Indexation, penalties, and peer comparisons

7.
Delay liabilities: General sales terms at NKT dictate that if the company is culpably in default, the customer can claim liquidated damages of 0.5% per week of delay capped at 5% of delayed supply.
8.
Raw material indexing: Turnkey transmission contracts integrate automatic metal pass-through clauses, ensuring that fluctuations in copper and aluminum prices do not erode project margins.
9.
Unhedged cost pressure: While metal is hedged, inflation in labor, specialist installation crews, and subcontracted marine vessel charters are harder to recover and create direct near-term margin pressure.
10.
Tighter contract terms: A European utility procurement expert noted that modern high-voltage contract awards increasingly feature tighter metal indexation and capacity reservation fees to protect cable manufacturer profitability.
11.
Competitive peer terms: Core peer Nexans maintains a selective value-over-volume strategy, utilizing daily indexation formulas and long-term copper contracts to insulate operations from supply disruptions.
12.
Prysmian pass-through lag: Peer leader Prysmian passes through raw material inflation with a time lag of one month to two quarters, which can temporarily pressure margins during periods of rapid commodity escalation.
13.
side of the story: While robust raw material indexation protects the headline contract value, extreme shortages of specialist insulation materials like high-density polyethylene can trigger severe delay penalties if manufacturing fails to meet strict delivery schedules.

2.
Steady profitability improvement: Segment operational EBITDA grew from EUR 83m in FY2021 to EUR 182m in FY2023, representing a standard-metal margin rise to 15.80% in FY2023.

Transitional margin deceleration: Operational EBITDA in FY2025 fell to EUR 251m because of a temporary margin drop to 15.80% from 17.20% in FY2024 as capacity ramp-up costs began to scale.

Subsea project progression: The subsea segment was driven by massive offshore projects such as Dogger Bank A, B, and C and BorWin5, which dominated the manufacturing pipeline in Sweden during 2022-25.

Underground corridor progression: Onshore underground projects such as SuedLink and SuedOstLink began generating revenues, providing multi-year baseline factory loading in Germany.

Subsea installation acceleration: The rapid jump in profitability in Q2 2026 was supported by high vessel utilization as subsea execution shifted toward high-margin offshore wind and interconnector scopes.

Section 8 – Second and third-order implications

Section summary
  1. A rapid pricing normalization would disrupt mid-term transmission margins, although industry leaders remain insulated by expanding grid infrastructure and data center demand

Sector-wide impacts of pricing normalization

1.
Accelerating overcapacity: Morgan Stanley notes that European grid spend risks peaking in 2030, which could accelerate a margin normalization back to the historical high-voltage margin range of 5-15%
2.
Diverging peer paths: Jefferies argues that Prysmian is better positioned than Nexans to weather a downturn due to its significant US power distribution market share and expanding high-value optical fiber business.
3.
Concentrated backlog vulnerability: Bernstein notes that Nexans' transmission business is exposed to underutilized capacity at Halden and shallower pipeline visibility than Prysmian due to Great Sea Interconnector delays.
4.
Strained funding capability: ING Wholesale Banking reports that network operators face severe cash shortfalls, with capital expenditure reaching 164% of EBITDA on average, requiring heavy debt issuance.
5.
Interdependent supply chains: RedEye notes that Hexatronic's major investment to triple its submarine cable capacity relies heavily on its strategic communication cable partnership with NKT.
6.
Declining offshore interest: DNB Carnegie warns that waning interest from developers in European offshore wind auctions could release scarce cable production capacity elsewhere, distorting the favorable supply-demand balance.

Multi-party strategic behaviors and non-consensus signals

7.
Motive to renegotiate: Morgan Stanley argues that project affordability is becoming a major political issue, giving TSOs strong motive to prioritize cheaper overhead lines over expensive underground cables.
8.
Opportunity for renegotiation: An industry expert notes that German TSOs are legally required to hold highly competitive open bidding processes to ensure they do not overcompensate equipment providers.
9.
Outlier: Alternative DC standards: Bernstein Research warns that a sudden data center shift to 800VDC could shrink the addressable market for traditional power cables and optical solutions.
10.
Outlier: Far East penetration: An industry expert reports that Far East competitors Sumitomo and LS Cable are overcoming execution barriers by building local European manufacturing track records.
11.
Other side of the story: : DNB Carnegie argues that despite medium-term political volatility, European transmission investments will continue growing after 2030, meaning high-voltage cables remain a structural bottleneck.
