TotalEnergies (TTE-FR) — what the Q3 2026 results do to the existing case

These results leave the park where it was and move the case both ways beneath it: the balance sheet and the distribution evidence are stronger than the case assumed, the earnings drop-through is weaker than the case's modal path, and the price has moved further from the level at which the case re-looks.

What changed

The company reported third-quarter 2026 results on 29 October 2026. Adjusted net income was 9.10 billion dollars against a company-collated consensus of 8.90 billion dollars, a 2 percent beat attributed to lower corporate and financial costs. Adjusted EBIT was 9.62 billion dollars against 9.50 billion dollars. Segment adjusted net operating income was 9.71 billion dollars, in line with 9.70 billion dollars. Cash flow from operations excluding working capital was 12.26 billion dollars against 12.10 billion dollars.

Beneath the line the segments split. Exploration and Production earned 3.16 billion dollars against 3.30 billion dollars, a 4 percent miss on lower liquids prices and a 49.2 percent quarterly tax rate. Integrated LNG earned 1.89 billion dollars against 1.80 billion dollars, a 5 percent beat on LNG prices up 20 percent quarter on quarter to 12.28 dollars per million Btu and strong trading. Refining and Chemicals was in line at 3.60 billion dollars and Integrated Power in line at 0.60 billion dollars. Marketing and Services beat at 0.47 billion dollars against 0.40 billion dollars.

The balance sheet moved most. Net debt excluding leases fell 5.92 billion dollars quarter on quarter to 13.79 billion dollars and gearing finished the quarter at 9.1 percent, against the 20 percent ceiling set at the 2025 investor day. Net debt was nonetheless 1.86 billion dollars above the Visible Alpha consensus of 11.93 billion dollars. Organic capital expenditure was 4.79 billion dollars, 14 percent above the Visible Alpha consensus of 4.20 billion dollars, with full-year net investments held at about 15 billion dollars. Asset sales were 3,702 million dollars in the quarter against 1,565 million dollars a year earlier; acquisitions fell to 773 million dollars from 3,416 million dollars. Non-operational impairments of 1.90 billion dollars on offshore wind and petrochemical assets were taken outside adjusted earnings.

Distributions: a third interim dividend of 0.90 euros per share, up 5.9 percent year on year; buybacks of 2.50 billion dollars for the fourth quarter of 2026 and 2.50 billion dollars for the first quarter of 2027, the top of the 2.00 to 2.50 billion dollar guided range; the payout framework of more than 40 percent of cash flow reiterated; full-year 2026 cash flow guided to 42 to 43 billion dollars. Management reaffirmed 3 percent a year production growth to 2030, zero decline to 2035, the Middle East production impact at 5 percent in the quarter and below 5 percent expected in the fourth quarter, Integrated Power net capital expenditure of 3 billion dollars balanced by the segment's cash flow in 2026 and free cash flow positive in 2027. Net power production was 17.9 TWh in the quarter, up 42 percent year on year. LNG sales fell 9 percent quarter on quarter to 9.8 million tons and petroleum product sales fell 4 percent year on year to 1,218 thousand barrels per day.

The sell-side response the review carries is a single house. Rothschild & Co Redburn raised 2026 adjusted EBITDA 5 percent to 57,904 million dollars and 2026 EBIT 4 percent to 42,887 million dollars, yet trimmed 2026 adjusted EPS 2 percent to 12.75 dollars from 12.97 dollars, left 2027 adjusted net income virtually unchanged at 25,260 million dollars and trimmed 2028 by 1 percent to 21,435 million dollars; it kept a Buy and a 97.00 euro target and expects a final dividend top-up of 0.50 euros per share to reach the 40 percent payout, which would take its 2026 dividend estimate to 4.15 euros from 3.65 euros. Post-results notes from Goldman Sachs, JP Morgan, Morgan Stanley, Barclays, Kepler Cheuvreux, Jefferies and UBS were not available to the review. Since the print, Brent fell more than 20 dollars per barrel during November from 117 dollars on one-month forwards in late October as Middle East export corridors recovered; Morgan Stanley expects dated Brent to normalise to 75 dollars in late 2027.

The challenge

The quarter was quiet on the headline and not quiet underneath it. Two readings press on the case, and a third that looks like a challenge does not survive the evidence.

First reading: the drop-through the case carried as a sensitivity is the modal path. The case's 'Modal return' of about 11.0 percent a year over eighteen months and 9.5 percent over three years rests on its own EPS path; the case then ran a worse drop-through (adjusted tax rate three to four points higher as production-sharing entitlements shrink at high prices, minorities taking more, depreciation outgrowing EBITDA) and got 5.4 and 6.9 percent a year, an F, with the three-year clearing price falling to about 52 euros. This quarter is that sensitivity in the statements: Exploration and Production missed on a 49.2 percent tax rate while the group beat on corporate costs, and the one broker with a post-results model raised EBITDA 5 percent and cut EPS 2 percent in the same note, with 2028 trimmed. The 1.90 billion dollar impairment on offshore wind and petrochemicals sits beneath the adjusted line too, pressing toward the case's alarm of an adjusted-to-statutory gap above 4 billion dollars in a year. For this reading to be right, the Redburn pattern has to repeat across the broker set when their revisions land, the upstream tax rate has to stay elevated through the fourth quarter, and FY2027 EPS has to be trimmed while EBITDA holds. If it is right, the case's 'Modal return' is F not D, and the price at which the case re-looks is about 52 euros, not about 62 euros.

Second reading: the balance sheet has arrived and the distribution leg is larger than the case modelled. The case had gearing at 13.1 percent heading to a 10 percent target "possibly by end-2026", a buyback of 1.5 billion dollars a quarter (6 billion dollars a year, retiring about 3 percent of shares) and a 2026 band of 3 to 6 billion dollars at 60 to 70 dollar Brent. The company is at 9.1 percent a quarter early, has committed 2.50 billion dollars a quarter for two consecutive quarters (10 billion dollars a year annualised), and one broker expects a 0.50 euro top-up on top of the 3.60 euros the case counted. The case's re-rating odds are the product of two things, Integrated Power demonstrated and Brent at or above 70 dollars, about 30 percent; this reading says a third route exists that the case did not price, a sub-10 percent geared major whose distribution capacity rises because every dollar not needed for debt is available for buybacks, and that the market may pay for it without waiting for Power. The 52-week high has moved to 85.94 from the 80.33 the case recorded, and technical momentum pinned B against the case's D, which is consistent with the market paying for something since the memo. For this reading to be right, buybacks have to hold at or above 2.0 billion dollars a quarter with Brent in the 70s and 80s rather than at 117, gearing has to stay below 10 percent as Brent normalises, and the top-up has to be declared. The evidence against is real: net debt came in 1.86 billion dollars above consensus, organic capex 14 percent above, and the quarter's debt reduction was helped by 3,702 million dollars of asset sales against 773 million dollars of acquisitions, which is the 'FALSE FRIEND' pattern the case already watches. On the company's own sensitivity a 10 dollar per barrel move is 2.80 billion dollars of annual operating cash flow, so November's fall of more than 20 dollars is, if sustained, more than 5 billion dollars a year off the cash that funds the 2.50 billion dollar quarters.

The third candidate, and why it is not a challenge. The Integrated LNG beat after the second-quarter miss invites the reading that gas trading was a one-off lapse and the case's 'cockroach present' red flag should be withdrawn. The case already held this as a dated step ("gas trading promised back to over-performance") and as a Key Risk of volatility, not of direction; one quarter up after one quarter down is the volatility the case named, not its refutation. Likewise the de-escalation reading, Brent lower and the Middle East impact under 5 percent, is the case's own catch-all external force at D; the evidence is moving toward it but has not reached the case's alarm of Brent below 70 dollars for a quarter. We find two readings the case does not accommodate, not more, and we are not confident the second survives a 75 dollar Brent.

What would settle it

The second review, four working days on, will carry what this one lacks: the broker set's post-results revisions rather than one house. That settles the first reading, because the question is whether EBITDA-up, EPS-down generalises and whether FY2027 moves, and it settles whether the sell-side momentum pin at B has company-driven content or is the oil price again. It will not carry the top-up decision, the fourth-quarter gearing with Brent lower, the buyback band at normalised prices or the upstream tax rate for another quarter; those arrive with the fourth-quarter results and 2027 guidance in February 2027.

The second reading is settled by February 2027 and by the oil price between now and then: a buyback held at or above 2.0 billion dollars for the second quarter of 2027 with Brent in the 70s, gearing still below 10 percent at year-end, the 0.50 euro top-up declared. Brent below 70 dollars for a quarter is the case's alarm, and Morgan Stanley's 75 dollar path for late 2027 says the test is probable within the case's eighteen-month horizon.

One question nothing scheduled will answer. The case's re-open trigger is two consecutive quarters of organic positive Integrated Power cash before farm-downs in the Q3 2026 to Q2 2027 prints. The company reports the segment balancing 3 billion dollars of net capital expenditure with its cash flow, and the review's own words are that this is "supported by fourth-quarter farm-downs"; the number before farm-downs is not disclosed and the company has not committed to disclosing it. Unless it does, that trigger can only be inferred, and the case should say so when it is next rebuilt.
