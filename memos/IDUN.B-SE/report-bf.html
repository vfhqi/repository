Plausible problems to monitor

1.
Elevated financial leverage: Net debt to EBITDA stood at 2.50x in Q1 2026, or 2.90x on 2026 estimates [Cross-ref: Section 2 + Section 9].
2.
Peer leverage comparison: This debt level is higher than the sector median of 1.80x ND/EBITDA [Cross-ref: Section 2 + Section 9].
3.
M&A capacity constraints: Elevated leverage could hamper its ability to maintain a meaningful pace of acquisitions [Cross-ref: Section 2 + Section 9].
4.
Slower capital returns: Return on capital employed remains below the 20%+ ROCE benchmark achieved by Lagercrantz [Cross-ref: Section 2 + Section 9].
5.
Returns peer discount: Idun's ROCE of 8.40% in 2025 is structurally weaker than its proven peers [Cross-ref: Section 2 + Section 9].
6.
Substantial minority leakage: Idun owns only ~85% of consolidated EBITA, creating cash transfer complications [Cross-ref: Section 2 + Section 9].
7.
Peer ownership levels: Larger compounders typically own between 95% and 100% of subsidiary EBITA [Cross-ref: Section 2 + Section 9].
8.
Cash conversion drag: This co-ownership structure results in partly weaker cash conversion than peers [Cross-ref: Section 2 + Section 9].
9.
High key person dependence: Capital allocation depends heavily on a lean seven-person headquarters investment team [Cross-ref: Section 1 + Section 9].
10.
Scale peer disadvantage: Idun is more dependent on fewer people compared with larger peers [Cross-ref: Section 1 + Section 9].
11.
Service segment sluggishness: Several subsidiaries have struggled with flat EBITA growth due to process industry headwinds [Cross-ref: Section 1 + Section 8].
12.
Subsidiary operational bottlenecks: Subsidiaries like ILEMA and P&L Nordic saw a decline in profitability [Cross-ref: Section 8].
