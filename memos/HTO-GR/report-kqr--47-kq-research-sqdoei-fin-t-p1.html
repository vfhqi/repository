Section 2 – Operating Leverage and the Fixed-Variable Cost Split

Section summary
  1. Hellenic Telecom's rigid cost structure yields high core fixed-network operating leverage.

Fixed vs Variable Cost Structures

1.
Fixed cost intensity: Hellenic Telecom operates a business model where network, personnel, and lease costs represent a highly rigid operating structure. According to OTE's 2025 financial report, total operating expenses excluding restructuring grew 5.2% to EUR 2,043.2 million while personnel costs fell 4.0% due to voluntary leave schemes. This rigidity means that core telecom service revenue drops directly to the bottom line, which heavily influences long-term earnings per share expansion unless competitive pressures disrupt retail pricing.
2.
Variable cost expansion: Hellenic Telecom experiences significant variable cost inflation from its rapidly expanding ICT and handset sales segments. Management reported on 25 February 2026 that Q4 2025 operating expenses rose by EUR 65.4 million because of exceptionally strong 57.8% growth in System Solutions revenue. This shift toward variable third-party integrations compresses the consolidated margin, meaning that earnings per share become increasingly sensitive to low-margin public sector bidding.
3.
Margin mix dilution: Hellenic Telecom's transition toward digital services has diluted its structural operating leverage because of the lower margins inherent in corporate IT. OTE's adjusted EBITDAaL margin fell from 40.4% in 2024 to 39.6% in 2025 due to low-margin System Solutions. In comparison, Proximus reported a domestic EBITDA margin of 39.8% in 2020, demonstrating that European incumbents face similar dilution when enterprise IT revenues outpace core telecom.
4.
Sector cost structure: Telecommunication operators across Europe maintain highly structured fixed costs that insulate cash flows from short-term volume contractions. A cost study by Jefferies on 13 July 2026 showed that fixed costs contribute 61% to 66% of total cash operating expenses for typical telecom business models. This high fixed proportion accelerates EBITDA growth during economic expansions, meaning that any sustainable retail price increases will strongly boost group earnings per share.
5.
EBITDAaL growth driver: Hellenic Telecom's structural profit growth remains dependent on its core Greek telecom segment rather than volatile digital integration projects. Deutsche Bank reported on 20 October 2025 that OTE's Greek core segment earned 105% of group operating free cash flow in FY24, highlighting the minimal cash contribution of international operations. This core dominance means that structural operating leverage on the fixed network must be protected, as low-margin System Solutions cannot support the group's dividend commitments alone.

Voluntary Leave Scheme Dynamics

1.
Decreasing personnel expenses: Hellenic Telecom has consistently utilized voluntary leave schemes to optimize its operational cost structure. In the first half of 2026, the company recorded group-level VLS expenses of EUR 28.3 million, compared to EUR 23.6 million in the prior year period. In the full year 2025, group restructuring costs reached EUR 28.0 million, helping personnel costs decline by 4.0% year-on-year. These structural schemes generate high annualized savings, such as the 2019 scheme which achieved savings representing roughly one-third of its EUR 52.0 million cost.
2.
Restructuring expense treatment: The accounting treatment of Hellenic Telecom's voluntary exit schemes separates these non-recurring restructuring charges from underlying operational profitability. Company financial statements show that VLS expenses are excluded from adjusted EBITDAaL to facilitate comparability with industry peers. However, these cash outflows are recorded in the cash flow statements under restructuring payments, where Hellenic Telecom paid EUR 23.0 million at the group level in the first half of 2026. Treating these charges as one-off adjustments prevents structural margin distortion while highlighting the cash-intensive nature of workforce transformations.
3.
High historical savings: Historical precedents at Hellenic Telecom confirm that large-scale retirement schemes deliver substantial, permanent cost relief. For example, the voluntary retirement program implemented at the end of 2020 targeted 1,100 departures and yielded annualized savings of approximately EUR 50 million. Similarly, the company's major 2012 program cost EUR 153.0 million but delivered EUR 60.0 million in annualized payroll savings. These historical cases illustrate that up-front cash outlays systematically translate into multi-year operational leverage once the payroll reductions solidify.
4.
Consistent peer restructuring: Peer operators across European telecommunications markets execute comparable workforce adjustments to protect operational margins under inflationary pressure. Orange Polska signed a social agreement for the 2026-2027 period that targets voluntary departures of 1,000 employees, representing 12% of its total workforce. This social plan resulted in a PLN 151.0 million provision in 2025, illustrating how incumbents front-load restructuring charges to achieve future labor efficiencies. These initiatives highlight that structural headcount reduction remains a universal strategic necessity across the European telecom sector.

Rising labor inflation: Hellenic Telecom faces upward cost pressure from mandatory Greek minimum wage hikes and scheduled collective labor agreements. In past cycles, OTE management noted that scheduled salary increases under collective negotiations partially offset the immediate benefits of voluntary departures. For the 2026 to 2028 period, OTE must navigate these escalating domestic labor rates while attempting to maintain its target of flat or declining personnel costs. If wage inflation outpaces the pace of voluntary attrition, the company's structural operating leverage will face downward pressure.
6.
Negligible strike risks: Despite persistent wage indexation across Greece, the risk of disruptive labor opposition at Hellenic Telecom remains low. Deutsche Bank argued on 3 December 2024 that voluntary exit schemes will remain the company's primary workforce reduction mechanism, making the risk of strike action insignificant. This allows management to execute headcount optimizations smoothly without facing the prolonged union disputes that historical restructuring efforts faced.

Deutsche Bank noted on 21 June 2024 that rising customer take-up of FTTH allows OTE to employ significantly fewer network maintenance engineers. 11.
Contrary evidence: Although headcount reduction remains OTE's primary margin driver, actual personnel expense savings can be entirely consumed by volatile non-labor opex. Deutsche Bank reported on 3 March 2025 that OTE Greek staff cost savings of EUR 20 million in FY24 were consumed by rising power costs returning to spot rates. This offset demonstrates that external cost headwinds can neutralize labor efficiencies, meaning that personnel cuts do not always guarantee group EBITDAaL expansion.
12.
Strategic operational transformation: The combination of structural headcount reductions and advanced digitalization establishes a highly efficient operating framework for Hellenic Telecom. Peer benchmarks suggest that OTE can sustainably expand core profit margins by migrating high-cost physical transactions to automated digital platforms. Consequently, successful execution of these efficiency levers will remain a central driver of group earnings per share growth over the next four years.
13.
Other side of the story: While aggressive voluntary exit schemes and digital transformation support immediate margin relief, over-reliance on attrition can introduce execution risks. Rapidly shrinking the workforce may deplete critical technical skills required for complex FTTH rollouts, forcing OTE to rely on higher-cost external subcontractors. Furthermore, peer precedents such as Orange Polska show that salary increases often partially dilute the savings gained from headcount optimization. Therefore, OTE's capacity to expand earnings per share through personnel cuts depends on carefully balancing digital self-service adoption with core engineering capacity.
