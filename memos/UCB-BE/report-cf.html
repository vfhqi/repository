Appendix

Plausible problems to monitor

UCB's H1 2026 earnings beat was characterized as low quality by Deutsche Bank and Morgan Stanley, driven primarily by legacy epilepsy products rather than the anticipated strong performance from key launch assets. Rystiggo net sales of EUR 192 million and Zilbrysq net sales of EUR 139 million both missed consensus expectations, suggesting that access barriers continue to constrain rare disease commercial execution relative to street models. While the group delivered robust headline growth, the underlying composition reveals execution challenges in strategically critical novel franchises.

Free cash flow generation remains materially constrained at only EUR 83 million in H1 2026, falling substantially below the European mid-cap pharma peer median. This weakness stems from EUR 1,672 million in outstanding CMO commitments and pressure from a EUR 2 billion greenfield US biologics facility investment, creating a capital intensity profile that diverges unfavorably from specialty pharmaceutical peers who typically convert 20- 30% of EBITDA to free cash flow during commercial inflection phases.

US average net pricing fell 1.7% across UCB's product portfolio in FY2025 despite list price increases, underperforming the European specialty pharma median of flat to slightly positive net pricing. Jefferies highlighted that Bimzelx pricing remains under pressure as the commercial payer mix expands, with gross-to-net discount rates rising toward levels that historically compress long-term margin trajectories in competitive immunology markets.

Outliers worth noting

UCB recorded an unquantified prior-year gross-to-net rebate adjustment exceeding €50 million in H1 2026, which flattered gross margins and contributed to a 25.4% EBITDA beat against consensus. This one-time accounting benefit is unusual relative to peer-group reporting norms, where such material adjustments are typically disclosed explicitly. The magnitude of this adjustment raises questions about the sustainability of the elevated 82% adjusted gross margin reported for the period, particularly as Bimzelx pricing remains under pressure in the US as the payer mix continues to expand. [Cross-ref: Section 6] Deutsche Bank characterized the H1 2026 earnings beat as "low quality" because key launch assets missed or merely met expectations, leaving legacy epilepsy products to drive the upside. This represents an unusual revenue composition for a company in the midst of a major product cycle transition, where Bimzelx, Evenity, Fintepla, Rystiggo, and Zilbrysq now account for over 50% of UCB's total net sales.

The reliance on mature franchises like Briviact to deliver consensus beats is statistically inconsistent with the forward growth narrative and suggests potential underlying weakness in new product execution. [Cross-ref: Section 6, Section 7] UCB committed US$ 2 billion upfront to acquire Candid Therapeutics and its early-stage BCMA x CD3 T-cell engager cizutamig, representing one of the largest biotechnology acquisition premiums relative to clinical maturity in recent years.

Cizutamig represents a massive financial outlay for an early-stage asset with limited clinical data, with Phase 2 clinical trials in myasthenia gravis planned to initiate by late 2026. The valuationto-risk ratio appears to be a statistical outlier when benchmarked against peer transactions for Phase 1/2 assets, raising questions about capital allocation discipline during a period of elevated leverage. [Cross-ref: Section 2]

H1 2026 free cash flow was restricted to only EUR 83 million due to intensive capital spending, representing an extreme compression relative to the EUR 2,291 million in cash flow from operating activities achieved in FY25. This negative cash conversion is highly unusual for a specialty pharmaceutical company experiencing double-digit revenue growth and expanding margins, and reflects a EUR 2 billion investment in a greenfield US biologics manufacturing facility. The magnitude of this working capital and capex burden creates a statistical divergence from peer cash generation profiles. [Cross-ref: Section 4, Section 7]
