Terms used throughout. EBITA: adjusted earnings before interest, tax and amortisation of acquired intangibles, the company's main profit measure. EBITDA: EBITA before depreciation. EPS: the memo's model earnings per share (adjusted EBITA less net financial items, taxed at 24%, over ordinary shares, before amortisation of acquired intangibles), unless another basis is named. PPA amortisation: amortisation of intangibles from purchase-price allocation on acquisitions. Organic: growth in units owned in both periods, at constant currency. Leverage: net debt including earn-outs to adjusted EBITDA, on the company's definition, against its 3.0x ceiling. Earn-outs: deferred payments to sellers that depend on the acquired unit's later profit; write-backs are gains booked when the expected payment is cut. Cash conversion: operating cash flow over EBITDA, on the company's measure (target 70% to 90%). ROCE: return on capital employed. STIBOR: the Stockholm interbank rate on which the bonds and bank debt are priced. The Riksbank: Sweden's central bank. The four segments: Supply Chain & Transportation, Energy & Electrification, Water & Bioeconomy, Safety & Security. Units named: Hilltip (road maintenance vehicles, with truck-chassis pass-through sales), Mecno (rail, with fixed-price projects), Certus (a business with service and lumpy project income), GAH (transport refrigeration). FY2026: the financial year to December 2026; H1: first half. bp: basis points.

A first point for Richard before the questions. Sdiptech is held as a Broadening / Early S2 Core Investment at 5.64% of the fund, so the questions are weighted to whether to add, hold or cut, and to what would break the case. The memo (31-Oct-2026) calls it "good niche infrastructure businesses whose group-level record was spoiled by what it paid and how it financed itself, and which a new management has cleaned up quickly and well": EPS flat at SEK 11 to 13 for FY2022 to FY2025 while revenue grew 48%, and now compounding at 15% to 17% (SEK 15.27 to 27.96 over FY2026 to FY2030) because dilution, a seven-fold rise in interest and 9x entry prices have gone. About a third of FY2027's EPS increase is already contracted. Its verdict on sizing: size the position for the multiple's volatility, and add on the fourth-quarter proof point, not before it; that proof point is fourth-quarter cash conversion and year-end leverage on 10-Feb-2027. Two other things: the third-quarter earnings summary found the two drivers the case needed (organic growth, deals) well ahead and the margin, cash and Water & Bioeconomy alarm going the wrong way; and the Industry's relative strength fired Sell Criterion 1 for Sdiptech with four other holdings on 10-Nov-2026 (an Industry-wide reading, not a stock-specific one). The Rater's four ratings: Thesis's Change Forces' Strength 75, Foundations' Robustness 55, Setups' Fit/Crispness 74, Case Riskiness 48.

A second point: what this call can and cannot give. A chief financial officer can go further than investor relations on capital allocation, the cash and leverage path, the finance line, earn-outs and the steer within the building blocks, but will not give numbers outside them: the company gives no annual guidance, only building blocks (organic EBITA growth of 5% to 10% a year, SEK 100m of acquired run-rate EBITA a year, cash conversion of 70% to 90%, leverage below 3.0x, ROCE above 15%, more than 15% EBITA growth a year and SEK 2,000m of EBITA by 2030). Frame questions as "how should we model this" or "is our reading of what you published right". He was Chief Executive until mid-2025, so he can speak to the reset from both seats. What the call should settle, and why

The case in one line: Sdiptech's EPS now compounds mechanically because the drags have gone, and two numbers decide whether it keeps doing so, the conversion of growth into profit and cash, and the price and pace of acquisitions inside a 3.0x ceiling; the share price, meanwhile, answers to the Riksbank and the cohort. Six judgements decide whether to add, hold or cut.

  1. Judgement 1, the fourth-quarter cash release and year-end leverage. Third-quarter conversion was 62% and leverage 2.90x to 2.95x against the 3.0x ceiling; we have 2.6x to 2.7x at year end after the seasonal release (134% in the fourth quarter of 2025). Above 100% and 2.6x to 2.7x on 10-Feb-2027 unlocks the engine at SEK 100m a year; below 90% and 2.8x pauses deals for two quarters, and the memo's proof point for adding fails.
  2. Judgement 2, the drop-through in H1 2027. Organic sales +7%, +11%, +13% in 2026 against organic EBITA +4%, +5%, +6%. We read most of the gap as about SEK 50m a quarter of near-zero-margin chassis pass-through at Hilltip and the Mecno overrun, both ending in the fourth quarter of 2026. If H1 2027 shows organic EBITA growing faster than organic sales, RedEye's reading that part of the gross-margin fall is lasting is disproved; if Supply Chain & Transportation stays at 18% to 19%, organic sales of 4% and a 20.2% margin take FY2027 EPS to about SEK 17.1, 7% below the model.
  3. Judgement 3, the acquisition engine's price and pace. Base SEK 90m of EBITA in 2027 and SEK 100m a year after, at about 6.5x upfront and 8x total; each SEK 10m closed adds about 0.7% to EPS and consensus models none. A year of SEK 50m costs 2.3% of FY2027 EPS and 3.3% of FY2028, and stops the upgrade stream; deals at 8x upfront instead of 6.5x add about 25% less EPS; a deal at 9x total is the memo's alarm.
  4. Judgement 4, the earn-out write-backs. About SEK 90m of gains in nine months of 2026 against SEK 48m in 2025, the 2026 figure unattributed. If they trace to units bought in 2024 and 2025 missing their plans, the quality of reported profit and the deal record both weaken; if timing, as in 2025, no change.
  5. Judgement 5, sterling and the finance line together. 49% of sales in the United Kingdom, hedges within twelve months; 40% to 45% of credit swapped. A 10% move in foreign currencies against the krona moves operating profit by SEK 83m (the annual report), SEK 0.9 to 1.7 of EPS; 100bp on STIBOR adds SEK 11m to 33m of interest (SEK 0.2 to 0.7); together, 5% and 100bp take FY2027 EPS to about SEK 17.3 (6% lower).
  6. Judgement 6, the margin's floors: Water & Bioeconomy and Safety & Security. Water & Bioeconomy at 20.3% has triggered the earnings summary's alarm (below 21.8%); Safety & Security at 36.2% is called unsustainable by the Chief Executive. Water & Bioeconomy still below 22% at the fourth-quarter report meets the case's invalidation level (the third-quarter earnings summary); a pause in data-centre spending costs Safety & Security and Energy & Electrification SEK 30m to 50m of EBITA together (SEK 0.6 to 1.0 of EPS).

Range A: scope for guidance and sell-side upgrades or cuts over the next four quarters (the FY2026 report on 10-Feb-2027, the first-quarter report in late April 2027, the second-quarter report in mid-July 2027 and the third-quarter report in late October 2027)

A1 ★ The fourth-quarter cash release and leverage at year end. "Third-quarter conversion was 62%, with SEK 230 million spent on deals and SEK 172 million on earn-outs. How much of the working-capital build unwinds in the fourth quarter, where should leverage land at the year end, and how much room does the 3.0x ceiling then leave for deals in 2027?"
- Why: fourth-quarter 2025 conversion was 134%. Leverage was 2.90x in the report (2.95x said on the call); we have 2.6x to 2.7x at end-2026 and DNB Carnegie has 2.0x by end-2027. Counting earn-outs there is almost no headroom under the ceiling; without them (2.39x) about 0.6x.
- Tell: "a normal seasonal release, below 2.8x at year end" = Judgement 1 on track and the proof point likely met; "the ceiling excludes earn-outs" = more room than we assumed, and a faster 2027; "growth keeps absorbing working capital" = deals slow in H1 2027, and adding should wait.

A2 ★ The drop-through in 2027. "Is our reading right that the gap between 13% organic sales and 6% organic EBITA is mostly Hilltip's truck-chassis pass-through and Mecno's unindexed raw materials, both ending with the fourth quarter, so the first half of 2027 shows organic EBITA growing faster than organic sales?"
- Why: each 50bp of EBITA margin is about SEK 0.58 of EPS. About SEK 50m a quarter of near-zero-margin chassis revenue explains the gross-margin fall from 60.4% to 58.1%; RedEye has cut its long-run gross margin to 59%, reading part of it as lasting.
- Tell: "yes, both end in the fourth quarter, indexation in place" = the debate ends in two reports and Judgement 2 passes; "Hilltip's larger road segment continues" or "cost pressure broader than Mecno" = RedEye's 59% is right, and FY2027 EPS is nearer SEK 17.

A3 Supply Chain & Transportation's margin path. "What margin should we expect from Supply Chain & Transportation through 2027, and how much of its fixed-price work now carries indexation?"
- Why: this is the largest segment (38% of 2025 EBITA). The memo's test is the margin back above 20% by the second quarter of 2027; DNB Carnegie expects pressure into the fourth quarter.
- Tell: "back to 20% to 21% by mid-2027" = our base; "18% to 19% is the level with this mix" = a lower margin path.

A4 ★ Organic growth in 2027. "How should we think about organic growth next year, given comparisons of 7%, 11% and 13%, the refrigeration orders moved from 2025 into 2026, the chassis sales leaving the base, and the backlogs at GAH, Certus and Hilltip?"
- Why: each point of organic growth is about SEK 0.23 of EPS; organic sales below 3% for two quarters would be a warning. We model about 7% for FY2027; RedEye has 6%, fading to about 3% by FY2029.
- Tell: "mid-to-high single digits; the backlogs carry the first half" = our base, and the slowdown is normalisation; "low single digits once the timing effects go" = RedEye's fade comes sooner.

A5 ★ The acquisition pace. "The company has said four to six deals this year would be a good outcome and that 2027 is the full ramp-up. With about SEK 75 million of run-rate EBITA bought by October against SEK 100 million, will you reach it by year end? Anders told us in August that cash flow could fund SEK 125 million to 130 million of acquired EBITA next year: is that the pace to assume for 2027, inside the ceiling?"
- Why: DNB Carnegie builds the 15% EBITA growth target from about 4% organic and 8% acquired; consensus models only announced deals, so each SEK 10m closed lifts EPS about 0.7%. We assume SEK 95m bought in 2026 and SEK 90m in 2027. The 2025 target was halved.
- Tell: "close to 100 this year, more next year" = the upgrade stream runs at 1.5% to 2% a quarter; "we will wait for the cash release" = Judgement 3's downside for H1 2027.

A6 ★ What the deals cost. "The company has spoken of around six times EBITA, up to seven for faster growers, and a 20% return hurdle. What did the five deals of the last twelve months cost on average, including earn-outs?"
- Why: the past cycle paid about 9x, which is why ROCE is 13.6% against 18% to 22% at the leaders; Indutrade paid 7.9x on average in 2026. Deals at 8x upfront in place of 6.5x add about 25% less EPS. Our "deal above 9x" alarm cannot be read from what is published.
- Tell: "about 6x upfront, 7x to 8x total" = the discipline the case relies on; "higher for the UK platforms" = returns close more slowly.

A7 ★ The earn-out write-backs. "Contingent consideration was SEK 720 million at the end of September after SEK 90 million of positive revaluations in nine months, against SEK 48 million in all of 2025. Which acquisitions do they come from, and are they timing, as GAH was last year, or units below their cash flow targets?"
- Why: the write-backs flatter reported profit while they last. Earn-outs are tied to the acquired units' cash-flow targets, so a write-back can mean a unit is running below the plan its price was set on.
- Tell: "timing at one or two units" = no change; "several 2024 to 2025 deals below plan" = Judgement 4 bites, and the deal record weakens.

A8 The earn-out schedule. "How should we model payments on the SEK 720 million over 2027 to 2029?"
- Why: the memo's earn-out schedule shows SEK 159m to 162m in 2027 and SEK 392m to 436m in 2028 to 2029, 20% to 35% of free cash flow at the peak; its model of record carries SEK 182m in 2027. No schedule is published.
- Tell: a lower schedule = more room for deals in 2028; a higher one = the 2028 to 2029 squeeze.

A9 ★ The finance line and the swaps. "After the refinancing at STIBOR plus 2.00%, what net financial items should we model for 2027? What share of the debt do the swaps cover, when do they end, and does the cost then fall or rise?"
- Why: we have SEK (262)m for FY2027 against SEK (275)m at DNB Carnegie and RedEye (SEK 0.3 of EPS). Each 100bp on STIBOR is SEK 0.2 to 0.7 of EPS; the memo has 40% to 45% of utilised credit swapped.
- Tell: "about SEK 260m to 270m, the swap premium falls away" = our base or better; "unhedged into a rate rise" = the rates leg of Judgement 5 arrives in 2028.

A10 Sterling. "With about half of sales in the United Kingdom and currency hedged within twelve months, what is the right sensitivity to the pound, and does sterling debt offset it?"
- Why: the annual report gives SEK 83m of operating profit for a 10% move in foreign currencies against the krona (about 8% of core EBITA), SEK 0.9 to 1.7 of EPS; about half of sales are in sterling.
- Tell: "translation only, debt matched" = an EPS risk but not a balance-sheet one; "debt mostly in kronor" = a weaker pound also lifts leverage toward the ceiling.

A11 ★ The targets in February. "Is the 15% total EBITA growth target now the single measure, or do you still hold a separate SEK 100 million acquired-EBITA goal for 2027, and will the February report revisit either, or the path to SEK 2 billion of EBITA by 2030?"
- Why: the company has never raised a target. Our FY2030 EBITA of SEK 1,695m is 15% short of the 2030 ambition; DNB Carnegie's sector note of 12-Nov-2026 reported positive revisions of about 3% to EBITA.
- Tell: "15% is the measure, deals as they come" = upgrades show as delivery, not raises; "a higher deal target for 2027" = a formal raise, and the memo would mark guidance up.

A12 ★ Water & Bioeconomy under its new head. "In July you guided Water & Bioeconomy at about 21% to 22%; the third quarter was 20.3%. What does Anders Petré plan, and with the April 2027 UK wage rise and fixed-price insurer contracts, what margin should we expect next year?"
- Why: the margin fell from 23.8% (FY2024) to 20.3% (20.1% on the interim report's basis). The earnings summary's alarm (below 21.8%) is triggered, and still below 22% at the fourth-quarter report meets its invalidation level; the memo models 21.3% by FY2028.
- Tell: "about 22% from the fourth quarter, repricing at renewal" = the invalidation test is passed; "20% to 21% for now" = the test fails at the February report and Foundations' Robustness (55) comes down.

A13 Safety & Security's margin. "Safety & Security earned 36.2% in the third quarter, which the Chief Executive called unsustainable, and you have said data centres drive one company there. What margin is sustainable once that company's projects normalise?"
- Why: DNB Carnegie has 32.9% for FY2026 and 31% for FY2027; we take 31% (SEK (20)m to (30)m of EBITA as it normalises). The shift to software and secure communications lifted the margin from 30%.
- Tell: "low thirties" = our base; "high twenties" = a further SEK 0.2 to 0.4 of EPS at risk.

A14 The working-capital programme. "Working capital has moved between 19% and 22% of sales against your 20% target. How much cash should the programme release over 2027 and 2028?"
- Why: about SEK 200m of one-off cash if the 20% target is met; it is one of the two routes to a 14% ROCE.
- Tell: "20% by end-2027" = ROCE near 14% by the FY2027 report, and more room under the ceiling; "it moves with growth" = no release to count on.

A15 ★ The road to a 15% return on capital. "In February the company said 15% ROCE could take one or two years, with the divestments worth 1 to 1.5 points. It was 13.6% in the third quarter. Is it 2027 or 2028, and what carries it: organic profit, working capital, or the deals' returns?"
- Why: we model about 14% by the FY2027 report and 15% only by 2030, because deals at 6.5x upfront and 8x total yield about 12.5% before tax. The discount to the compounders (10.8x against a peer median of 14.9x 2027 EV/EBITA, RedEye) closes only as returns close.
- Tell: "2027, from organic profit and working capital" = ahead of our path and a case for a higher fair multiple; "2028, as the deals mature" = our path.

The holistic questions: the general pattern, set against what Anders Mattson told Richard on 11-Aug-2026

H1 ★ How the building blocks are set. "You give building blocks in place of guidance: organic EBITA growth of 5% to 10%, SEK 100 million of acquired EBITA, cash conversion of 70% to 90%. This year organic EBITA grew 4%, 5% and 6% by quarter, at or below the bottom of the band, while organic sales ran at 7%, 11% and 13%. When you set a block, do you aim for the middle of it, or to beat it?"
- Why: the company has never raised a target. The memo puts the blocks met and reaffirmed over the next five reports at 65%, something raised at 15% and a block missed at 20%.
- Tell: "set to be met comfortably, we would rather beat" = the 15% chance of a raise is too low and each report can lift the Street; "they are stretching" = delivery in line is the best case, and upgrades come only from deals.

H2 ★ Whether the sell-side is too low. "DNB Carnegie has SEK 1,169 million of EBITA for 2027, about 10% above its SEK 1,060 million for this year, on announced deals only. Some investors say that must be too low for a company that plans to buy SEK 100 million of EBITA a year. Is that fair, and which analyst is closest to how you see 2027?"
- Why: each SEK 10m of acquired EBITA is about 0.7% on EPS; the memo puts 60% on the Street's 2027 EPS rising 3% or more by February 2028. RedEye, which pencils in future deals, has SEK 1,207m; DNB Carnegie says 2027 "could be a strong year with 15% EBITA growth including recent acquisitions".
- Tell: "fair: analysts cannot model deals we have not announced" = the upgrade stream is intact, about 1.5% to 2% a quarter at the planned pace; "analysts already assume our ramp-up" or a caution about the margin = less to come than the memo's 60% implies.

H3 ★ More or less positive than in August. "In August Anders told us end-market demand was stable and trending upwards, and that his concerns were in acquisitions, not in the existing companies. Since then the margin has missed consensus again and cash conversion was 62%. Is that still the picture, or has anything in the existing companies moved onto the worry list?"
- Why: Richard's notes of 11-Aug-2026: "Not concerned ATM re. existing companies"; "M&A is where the CEO's concerns are". The margin has missed consensus four quarters running (20.0% against 20.9% in the third). The memo's lean for 2027 is "sales up, margin short".
- Tell: "the same picture; the misses are Hilltip and Mecno and both end" = tone unchanged, and Judgement 2 likely passes; a new worry named in the existing companies (wages in the United Kingdom, a large unit) = less positive than August, and the margin shortfall carries into 2027.

H4 Concerns one to two years forward: cash in the weaker companies. "Anders told us about a fifth of the companies, the larger ones among them, are weaker on margins, cash and returns, and he would not promise that group cash conversion improves in the medium term. How much of this year's working-capital build sits in that fifth, and what worries you most for 2027 and 2028?"
- Why: his notes: "80% of the companies are pretty outstanding at EBIT margins + CC + ROCE"; "there is 20% that are not so good. Larger companies, etc."; "CEO wouldn't say the impact will improve the Group FCF conversion though in mid-term". Cash conversion gates the deal pace (Judgement 1).
- Tell: "the build is growth, spread across the group, and it unwinds" = the fourth-quarter proof point is likely; "a few large companies, and it takes time" = conversion stays at the low end of 70% to 90% and the engine runs below SEK 100m a year.

H5 What sceptical investors say. "In August the sceptics' challenge was earnings volatility: prove 15% growth and stable margins over three years. RedEye now reads part of the margin fall as lasting. What do the sceptics say to you today, and which number in the February report answers them?"
- Why: his notes: "Challenge from tough investors is 'you need to prove' you are stable/high quality"; "Prove you are 15% growth and stable margins. Show it over 3Y". Three large holders reduced or sold during 2026.
- Tell: a number he names himself (the fourth-quarter margin, cash conversion, leverage) = where the company expects to deliver or beat in February, and a test we can hold it to; "it takes three years" = estimates may rise without the multiple following.

H6 Options that are in no forecast. "Which of these is live for 2027: a first platform in Germany, further disposals, a first dividend or a buyback? And is new equity ruled out, whatever the pipeline looks like?"
- Why: none is in our numbers. In August a buyback was "not realistic at all ATM", and a rights issue was on Richard's list of questions. The share count rose 25% over FY2019 to FY2025; eleven units plus Metus were sold within twelve months, and leverage came down from 3.39x to 2.80x.
- Tell: "no equity, deals from cash flow, disposals finished" = the EPS compounding holds as modelled; "all options open" on equity = a dilution risk we do not model; a further disposal = more headroom just as the ceiling binds.

H7 The final, difficult question. "If one building block is missed in 2027, which is it most likely to be, and by how much?"
- Why: the memo puts a missed block at 20%. His answer shows where he himself sees the least margin for error.
- Tell: "cash conversion" or "the acquired EBITA" = the upgrade engine is the risk; "organic profit" = the margin is; "none" with no reasons = no information.

Range B: the key risks and drivers of the case (six questions; each could move a group forecast line)

B1 ★ Data-centre exposure. "Anders has said data centres drive only one Safety & Security company. How much of that company's sales are data-centre projects, and with hyperscalers' credit spreads wider since October, have you seen any project timing change?"
- Why: it is where the peak margin sits; a pause costs SEK 30m to 50m of EBITA with Energy & Electrification (SEK 0.6 to 1.0 of EPS). Europe has about 94 gigawatts of data-centre capacity in planning (Jefferies), 2.6 times the operating base. Most units hold about two months of orders.
- Tell: "one company, projects on schedule" = the risk is small and dated; "some timing slips" = Judgement 6's downside.

B2 ★ Price rises in 2027. "The pricing project in four companies, disclosed in the first quarter, had shown no effect yet. How much price are you putting through for 2027, and how much of the group now has indexation in its contracts?"
- Why: pricing is real in the product niches (GAH's EBITA +76% after acquisition) and lagged six to nine months in service and project units; price adds 2 to 3 points of revenue a year and about nothing to the group margin. Component inflation is flagged for 2027.
- Tell: "3% to 4% with indexation spreading, the project showing in 2027" = the lag shortens and the margin holds; "we absorb part of it" = a margin drag in H1 2027 on top of the UK wage rise.

B3 United Kingdom demand. "In August Anders told us the group is not exposed to United Kingdom construction or automotive, and that demand was trending upwards. With half of sales in the United Kingdom, what organic growth should we use for the United Kingdom businesses in 2027?"
- Why: the old company missed consensus in five of six quarters in the last soft period in the United Kingdom (2024 to mid-2025). Each point of group organic growth is about SEK 0.23 of EPS.
- Tell: "regulated niches, growth in line with this year" = the 2027 organic base holds and momentum is intact; "softer order intake" = organic growth toward 3% to 5%.

B4 Order visibility. "Most units hold about two months of orders. How has group order intake moved through the autumn against sales?"
- Why: only about SEK 210m of orders run beyond twelve months; visibility is short, so the first-quarter 2027 report is where a slowdown would first show.
- Tell: "intake ahead of sales into the winter" = the 2027 organic base holds; "slower since September" = toward the lower end of the 5% to 7% path.

B5 Energy & Electrification's margin. "Energy & Electrification grew 17% at 24.2%, against the 25% to 26% you guided. How much of the growth is grid and electrification demand, and what margin should we use for 2027?"
- Why: with Safety & Security, the structural tailwinds reach about 37% of EBITA. Each 50bp of group EBITA margin is about SEK 0.58 of EPS.
- Tell: "orders ahead of sales, back to 25%" = the structural leg holds into 2027; "24% is the level with this mix" = a small margin drag.

B6 One EPS figure, and coverage. "You show three EPS figures, and analysts' 2027 EPS runs from 15.85 to 19.5, largely on definition. Which one should we hold you to, would you give one adjusted EPS, and do you expect more brokers to publish?"
- Why: the FactSet consensus is a median of two definitions (DNB Carnegie adds back amortisation of acquired intangibles, SEK 19.5 for FY2027; Nordea appears not to). Detailed models were found from DNB Carnegie and RedEye only.
- Tell: "one adjusted EPS from 2027" = the market sees the upgrades the deals create, and the median rises as houses converge; "no change" = the stock keeps trading a hybrid consensus; a named new broker = a wider base for revisions.
