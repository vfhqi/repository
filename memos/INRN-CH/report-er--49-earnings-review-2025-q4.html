Section 1 – Brief company description and history

Section summary
  1. Market analysts expected a robust recovery in Interroll order momentum driven by e-commerce demand stabilization.
  2. High executive leadership turnover created short-term noise but was viewed as a strategic transition toward customer execution.
  3. Broader automated material handling peers displayed improving underlying demand despite ongoing industrial and macroeconomic headwinds in Europe.
1.
Established global provider: Interroll Holding AG operates as a Swiss-based multinational manufacturer in the intralogistics and material handling industry. The company provides key platform-based products such as rollers, drives, conveyors, and sorters to system integrators and original equipment manufacturers. Interroll products serve critical end markets including e-commerce, express postal services, airports, and food processing.
2.
Leading market share: Interroll operates in the internal logistics solutions niche of the material handling equipment market. The global relevant market is estimated to be worth between CHF 6 bn and CHF 8 bn annually. Interroll maintains a relevant market share of approximately 8% to 11% within this competitive landscape.
3.
Challenging financial performance: Interroll experienced a significant contraction in financial performance during fiscal year 2023 due to difficult macroeconomic conditions. Net sales declined by 16.3% to CHF 556.3 mm in 2023 from CHF 664.4 mm in fiscal year 2022. Earnings before interest and taxes fell by 20.2% to CHF 83.9 mm in 2023 compared to CHF 105.2 mm in 2022.
4.
Sustained volume pressure: Interroll continued to face a lack of large-scale project orders in fiscal year 2024 as customers delayed capital investments. Consolidated sales fell by 5.3% to CHF 527.1 mm in 2024 compared to CHF 556.3 mm in fiscal year 2023. Earnings before interest and taxes declined by 7.2% to CHF 77.8 mm in 2024 from CHF 83.9 mm in 2023.
5.
Prolonged top-line decline: Interroll reported a third consecutive year of declining sales in fiscal year 2025 due to a weak opening backlog. Consolidated sales decreased by 2.5% to CHF 514.2 mm in 2025 from CHF 527.1 mm in fiscal year 2024. Operating earnings before interest and taxes declined by 7.8% to CHF 71.8 mm in 2025 compared to CHF 77.8 mm in 2024.
6.
Relative market divergence: Interroll underperformed some of its closest automation peers during the cyclical downturn between 2023 and 2025. System integrators and component specialists experienced divergent growth trajectories during this period. Peer competitor Kardex Holding AG successfully gained market share and continued to accelerate order bookings in a flat industry environment.
7.
Significant equity correction: Interroll experienced a notable share price correction in the months leading up to its early 2026 financial updates. The company faced investor skepticism after three consecutive years of flat order growth. Executive management changes including the replacement of the chief technology officer created an attractive entry point for longterm investors.
8.
Optimistic analyst outlook: Jefferies analyst Constantin Hesse reiterated a Buy rating on Interroll shares on January 20, 2026. The broker highlighted an anticipated mid-to-high teens earnings compound annual growth rate from 2025 to 2028. The analyst maintained a high pre-earnings price target of CHF 2,700 based on an expected recovery in e-commerce orders.

Section 3 – Key moving parts and changes

Section summary

  2. Strong currency headwinds and a depleted opening order backlog dragged consolidated sales down, but product business recovery supported robust second-half operating margins.
  3. Normalized working capital movements reduced free cash flow generation, while the group maintained a strong cash position and a stable dividend.
  4. Analyst projections indicate a gradual earnings per share recovery over the next eighteen months driven by an expanding opening order book.

The board of directors also appointed Ulrich Engenhardt as Chief Technology Officer in January 2026 to accelerate platform development. The simultaneous transitions coincided with the departure of Chief Operating Officer Alp Ayhan Demirel and Chief Financial Officer Heinz Hössli. Strong capital discipline supports the view that payouts will remain tightly controlled over the next three years.

Conservative cash deployment: Interroll increased net financial assets to CHF 205.8 million at the end of fiscal year 2025, compared with CHF 194.8 million in fiscal year 2024.
