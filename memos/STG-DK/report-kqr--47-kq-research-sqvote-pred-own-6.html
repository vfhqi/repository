Section 3 – Senior Notes Refinancing and Lease Liabilities

Section summary
  1. The EUR 300 million senior notes maturing in September 2029 represent the primary long-term debt obligation for Scandinavian Tobacco Group.
  2. Strong free cash flows enhanced by the BREAK and Moro divestment provide the group with substantial flexibility to retire or refinance these notes.
  3. Under IFRS 16 lease liabilities of DKK 396 million are fully integrated into reported net debt, adding to interest obligations but remaining highly manageable.

Senior Notes and Refinancing Paths

1.
Stable obligations: Scandinavian Tobacco Group manages its primary long-term debt through the EUR 300 million senior notes issued at a fixed rate. Deutsche Bank reported on April 3, 2025, that the company issued these fiveyear senior notes at 4.875% interest. The maturity of these notes in September 2029 gives the group ample time to plan its refinancing strategy. This long-term maturity ensures that near-term interest expense remains highly predictable for earnings per share projections.
2.
Refinancing flexibility: Scandinavian Tobacco Group has several options to address the senior notes before their maturity in September 2029. Deutsche Bank highlighted on November 26, 2026, that the group is highly focused on cash generation to optimize its capital structure. The company can choose to replace this debt with bank loans or issue new notes depending on market conditions. Utilizing these options is expected to protect the adjusted earnings per share from sudden refinancing shocks.
3.
Debt retirement: Scandinavian Tobacco Group can leverage its post-divestment cash reserves to retire a portion of the senior notes early. DNB Carnegie reported on November 12, 2026, that the group's net debt will decrease significantly over the next two years. Specifically, net interest-bearing debt is projected to fall to DKK 2,171 million in fiscal year 2027. This rapid deleveraging allows management to retire outstanding senior notes using excess cash instead of executing a full refinancing.

Post-Divestment Cash Flow Dynamics

4.
Strong cashflows: Scandinavian Tobacco Group benefits from exceptionally robust cash generation that supports its debt management strategies. DNB Carnegie estimated on November 12, 2026, that the group will generate free cash flow of DKK 1,125 million in fiscal year 2027. This high cash generation is further supported by the DKK 1.0 billion in post-tax proceeds received from the BREAK and Moro divestment in October 2026. These combined inflows will reduce reliance on external debt facilities and support earnings per share expansion.
5.
Liquidity cushion: Scandinavian Tobacco Group maintains substantial liquidity through its credit facilities to backstop its long-term obligations. Deutsche Bank noted on May 21, 2024, that the company's revolving credit facility was split equally among providers to ensure diversified support. The company's subsequent expansion of this facility to EUR 500 million provides a massive buffer. Having this committed facility ensures that repayment of senior notes remains fully backed by alternative liquidity sources.
6.
Contrary evidence: DNB Carnegie cautioned on October 18, 2024, that the issuance of the EUR 300 million senior notes at 4.875% actually increased the company's financial expenses. This coupon rate was higher than the historical cost of the company's fixed-rate term loans, which had been fixed through swaps. The company's 2024 annual report showed that financial liabilities carried effective interest expenses of DKK 200.9 million. This step-up in funding costs serves as a reminder that refinancing in high-rate environments compresses margins and restricts earnings per share growth.

The total lease liability of DKK 396.0 million (calculated) is integrated into the reported net interest- bearing debt. This lease inclusion modestly inflates the reported gearing ratios but does not affect bank covenant calculations.
