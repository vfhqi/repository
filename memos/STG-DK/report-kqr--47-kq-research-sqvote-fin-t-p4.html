Section 2 – US Tariffs, Duty Drawbacks, and One-Off Items

Section summary
  1. Evaluating the split of tariff refunds and duty drawbacks shows that a major portion represents non- recurring reversals of historical costs.

Tariff and Duty Drawback Refunds

1.
Significant temporary benefit: Scandinavian Tobacco Group reported a massive DKK 116 million boost to its EBITDA before special items during the first nine months of 2026. This positive impact was recognized under other income and was primarily driven by duty refunds. This temporary influx of capital helped inflate the group's reported EBITDA margin before special items to 21.8%. Investors must isolate these refunds to understand the underlying operating run-rate.
2.
Elevated refund expectations: Scandinavian Tobacco Group expects to recognize a further DKK 50 million of tariff and duty refunds during the fourth quarter of 2026. This addition will bring the estimated full-year tariff and duty refunds to approximately DKK 166 million. However, these refunds are expected to reverse or decline in subsequent periods, which will negatively impact the group's near-term margin trajectory. This makes the DKK 166 million figure a key risk when projecting sustainable margins.
3.
Favorable drawback split: Scandinavian Tobacco Group recorded DKK 79 million of duty drawback refunds in other income during the first half of 2026. This specific drawback benefit was driven by refunds of tariffs paid in the previous year. The group had faced significant margin pressure from those tariff-induced costs because passing them on to retail consumers was highly challenging. This refund effectively reverses prior year headwinds rather than indicating a recurring operational improvement.
4.
Temporary offset mechanism: Scandinavian Tobacco Group reported that its second-quarter tariff refund more or less offset a write-down of DKK 35 million for obsolete mass-market cigars. The write-down of obsolete products was related to a raw tobacco quality issue in Europe. While the refund was primarily recognized in the online and retail gross profit, the underlying quality issues are expected to persist. This shows how transient tariff adjustments disguised underlying product losses during the first half.
5.
Contrary evidence: Scandinavian Tobacco Group was able to grow its handmade cigars organic sales by 4% in the first nine months of 2026 despite the tariff challenges. This indicates that the consumer demand for premium cigars remained relatively resilient even as the group navigated supply chain disruptions. This strong performance suggests that underlying organic demand was much stronger than the headline margins implied.

Legal Status of US Import Tariffs

6.
Unlawful tariff ruling: Scandinavian Tobacco Group was impacted by a landmark legal shift when the US Supreme Court ruled in February 2026 that certain executive-imposed tariffs were unlawful. This judicial ruling established that previously paid tariffs could be legally subject to full or partial refund. Following this decision, the US Customs and Border Protection opened a formal refund process. This legal development is what unlocked the substantial refund inflows recorded in 2026.
7.
Persistent barrier uncertainty: Scandinavian Tobacco Group continues to face trade policy risks because the United States imposed new tariffs ranging from 10% to 12.5% on 66 countries in July 2025. These measures specifically targeted nations like the Dominican Republic under allegations of insufficient measures against forced labor. Because the US represents almost half of Dominican export value, these tariffs threaten to permanently raise costs. These new trade barriers could offset judicial refund gains in the future.
8.
Complex quota tracking: Scandinavian Tobacco Group's regional suppliers face intricate administrative hurdles under existing US agricultural policies. For instance, Honduras monitors its tariff-rate quotas using a strict import licensing system that the US government tracks. These bureaucratic requirements complicate the regional supply chain for handmade cigars. Such operational complexities make it difficult to optimize the import cost structure over the long term.

Analysis of Historical One-Off Items

Special items for the first half of 2026 rose to negative DKK 135 million compared to negative DKK 105 million in the prior year period. These costs were primarily driven by the Focus2030 reorganization, global SAP implementation, and Mac Baren integration. These substantial cash outlays continue to weigh heavily on reported net profit.
10.
Historical special costs: Scandinavian Tobacco Group recorded DKK 35 million of special items in the fourth quarter of 2025, which was lower than the DKK 148 million recognized in the fourth quarter of 2024. These historical adjustments highlight the volatility in the company's non-operational expenses. Over the full year, these non-recurring special items totaled DKK 181 million. This volatility makes it difficult for investors to establish a clean core earnings base.

Section 3 – Contract Manufacturing, Divestment, and Capital Allocation

Divestment of Fine-Cut Brands

1.
Positive divestment step: Scandinavian Tobacco Group completed the sale of its fine-cut tobacco brands BREAK and Moro to Japan Tobacco on 1 October 2026. The transaction was valued at DKK 1.3 billion before tax, which yields approximately DKK 1.0 billion in post-tax proceeds. Management intends to use these proceeds to restore strategic and financial flexibility by paying down debt. This cash infusion should support future capital returns.
2.
Reduced operating exposure: Scandinavian Tobacco Group noted that BREAK and Moro represented about 4% of group net sales in 2025. These brands also accounted for almost 4.5% of group gross profit and slightly more than 6% of EBITDA before special items. Although the divestment removes a profitable business, it sharpens focus on core categories with higher long-term potential. This strategic trade-off shifts the margin profile of the remaining business.
3.
Temporary operating agreement: Scandinavian Tobacco Group signed a contract manufacturing agreement with Japan Tobacco that will run for up to three years. Under these terms, the group continues to manufacture BREAK and Moro with a six-month termination notice period. This agreement helps optimize the group's manufacturing network during a transitional phase. It provides near-term volume security for the factories before efficiency programs take full effect.
4.
Diluted margin expectations: Scandinavian Tobacco Group expects the fine-cut brand divestment to have a dilutive impact on reported net sales growth and profit margins. However, the return of the SAIL cigar trademark in Canada is expected to partially offset these negative impacts. Gaining control of the SAIL brand provides a constructive counterweight to divestment dilution in the North American region. This helps stabilize the consolidated margin trajectory over the next 18 months.

Positive transaction multiples: Deutsche Bank Research highlighted that the divestment of BREAK and Moro was executed at highly attractive transaction multiples of 3.60 times sales and approximately 12 times EBITDA. These multiples are materially ahead of Scandinavian Tobacco Group's own trading valuation. However, the ongoing contract manufacturing agreement is expected to be lower-margin, which will modestly drag on the group's outeryear profitability. Gearing had escalated to 2.70 times net debt to EBITDA at the end of the third quarter of 2026. Debt Structure and Divestment Proceeds

Substantial debt reduction: Scandinavian Tobacco Group has successfully reduced its leverage by utilizing the proceeds from the German fine-cut divestment. This substantial cash injection helped bring net interest-bearing debt down from DKK 5,461 million at the end of 2025. Consequently, the group expects its leverage ratio to decline below its long-term target of 2.5 times by the end of 2026.
2.
Highly optimized debt mix: Scandinavian Tobacco Group maintains a structured debt profile comprising both fixed and floating interest rate liabilities to manage interest rate volatility. At the end of 2025, the group reported that approximately 46% of its total debt was held at fixed interest rates, compared to 62% in the prior year. The primary fixed-rate component is a senior unsecured notes issuance of EUR 300 million bearing a coupon rate of 4.875% maturing in September 2029. This fixed-rate exposure provides stability, although floating-rate credit facilities remain sensitive to central bank interest rate changes.
3.
Growing lease obligations: Scandinavian Tobacco Group relies on lease agreements for its retail superstores and production facilities, which represent significant off-balance-sheet commitments. As of 30 September 2026, the group reported non-current lease liabilities of DKK 329.4 million and current lease liabilities of DKK 66.6 million. These lease liabilities total DKK 396.0 million (calculated) and represent a steady cash outflow that impacts adjusted free cash flow. These obligations are treated as debt-like items when calculating the group's total economic leverage.
4.

Tax Rate Drivers and Pillar Two

This stable tax expectation is driven by a balanced geographical mix of profit contributions between North American and European divisions.

Management confirmed that all legal entities within the group have fully met the transitional Safe Harbour requirements under the Pillar Two framework. Consequently, the group did not recognize any top-up tax liabilities for the financial year ended 31 December 2024. This safe harbor compliance minimizes the risk of unexpected tax expenses over the next three years.

Diverse peer tax profiles: Imperial Brands faces a rising tax rate due to changes in its geographic profit mix. Section 5 – Currency Sensitivity and Share Dilution

Section summary
  1. Unhedged US dollar translation exposure remains the primary foreign exchange risk for Scandinavian Tobacco Group while share dilution from employee incentives is negligible.
  2. Our confidence in these currency sensitivities and share structures is high because they are based on official annual and interim regulatory filings.

Foreign Exchange Sensitivity Analysis

1.
Significant translation risk: Scandinavian Tobacco Group faces substantial volatility because it leaves its foreign currency earnings completely unhedged. The company reported that 46.8% of its net sales in 2025 were denominated in US dollars. This unhedged translation exposure means fluctuations in the dollar will directly impact reported consolidated earnings per share.
2.
Elevated dollar sensitivity: Scandinavian Tobacco Group has detailed that a 5% shift in the US dollar exchange rate would impact its financials significantly. According to the 2025 annual report, this 5% move creates a DKK 224.9 million sales impact and a DKK 45.7 million EBITDA impact. Such sensitivities mean that a weakening dollar will significantly drag on profitability unless offset by pricing.
3.
Slightly higher sensitivity: Scandinavian Tobacco Group reported even higher sensitivity levels in its prior annual reporting periods. During 2024, a 5% currency movement was estimated to alter net sales by DKK 231.1 million and EBITDA by DKK 55.1 million. These historical levels suggest that while the overall dollar exposure has diminished slightly, it remains a dominant driver of earnings volatility.
4.
Unhedged transaction risk: Scandinavian Tobacco Group also chooses not to hedge its transaction risks using financial contracts. Management stated that transaction risk is considered to be within the group's risk appetite and is therefore left unmanaged. This lack of hedging exposes operating margins to volatile raw tobacco import costs if exchange rates deteriorate.
5.
Similar translation risk: Haypp Group faces comparable foreign exchange translation exposures because it operates several international online subsidiaries. The Swedish peer reported that currency risks primarily arise when translating the earnings of these entities into Swedish kronor. Much like its Danish peer, Haypp Group relies on unhedged balance sheet exposures to manage its global capital.
6.
Negative translation headwind: Haypp Group experienced a major drag on its reported growth due to currency movements in late 2025. The online retailer suffered a translation impact of four percentage points in the fourth quarter. This translation headwind was primarily driven by the relative weakness of the US dollar against the Swedish kronor.

Declining treasury shares: Scandinavian Tobacco Group has slowly reduced its holding of treasury shares over the past year. The company reported holding 1.238 million treasury shares as of September 2026 compared to 1.266 million in September 2025. These treasury holdings are critical because they mitigate dilution from employee equity and performance share plans.
8.
Stable outstanding shares: Scandinavian Tobacco Group has kept its total outstanding share count relatively stable over the medium term. The number of outstanding shares stood at 78.937 million in late September 2026 versus 78.875 million a year earlier. This leaves a very small dilution risk because the outstanding share count increased by under 0.1% year over year.

Significant peer dilution: Haypp Group has introduced significantly more dilutive equity incentive plans compared to Scandinavian Tobacco Group. The peer approved a long-term incentive program in May 2026 authorizing up to 982,000 new C2 shares. Assuming a share price of SEK 300, this program alone would trigger dilution of 1.3% for existing shareholders.

Contrary evidence: Scandinavian Tobacco Group saw its third-quarter 2026 net sales growth positively impacted by currency movements despite lacking hedges. The group reported a positive exchange rate benefit of 1% during the quarter, which helped offset a 2% headwind from earlier in the year. This demonstrates that unhedged exposure can sometimes act as a positive tailwind for reported results.
11.
Significant structural risk: Dissecting the currency and dilution inputs indicates that dollar movements represent a far larger risk to earnings than share dilution. While the stable outstanding share count and treasury holdings limit dilution, the unhedged dollar sensitivity creates major translation swings. Therefore, investors must monitor foreign exchange rates as the primary driver of reported margin volatility over the next 18 months.
12.
Other side of the story: Scandinavian Tobacco Group can effectively neutralize translation risks if it aligns its borrowing currencies with its operating assets. Management noted that the group uses borrowings in currencies other than Danish krone to partly mitigate translation risk. Consequently, the actual economic impact of dollar fluctuations might be much lower than raw sensitivity models suggest.

This portfolio change means the underlying 2026 earnings base is permanently smaller but structurally less leveraged going forward.

This section grades each analytical block of the report by how firmly the evidence supports it. Confidence is stated in prose rather than in a table. The ratings are an analytical judgement; only Section 2 and Section 5 state a confidence level themselves. Section 2 (US tariffs, duty drawbacks and one-offs) is Medium confidence. The report itself rates it medium because legal resolutions are clear, although peer duty drawback comparisons show highly variable timing. The firm evidence is the size of the items: DKK 116 million of EBITDA benefit in the first nine months, a further DKK 50 million expected in the fourth quarter, and DKK 79 million of duty drawback in the first half. The legal trigger is also documented: the US Supreme Court ruled in February 2026 that certain executive-imposed tariffs were unlawful. The weaker part is the split. The first-half drawback was driven by refunds of tariffs paid in the previous year, but the evidence does not separate 2025 reversals, 2026 refunds and genuinely recurring drawback across the full DKK 166 million.

Section 3 (contract manufacturing, divestment and capital allocation) is Medium confidence. The deal facts are solid.

The sale of BREAK and Moro to Japan Tobacco completed on 1 October 2026, and the brands were about 4% of group net sales in 2025. The dividend framework is a 40% to 60% payout of adjusted earnings per share. The buyback is softer, because the DKK 0.5 billion figure is a broker estimate (resumed buybacks of around DKK 0.50 billion ) rather than a company commitment.

Section 4 (capital structure, tax and special items) is Medium confidence. The anchors are the EUR 300 million senior notes at a 4.875% coupon maturing in September 2029, lease liabilities of DKK 396.0 million and guidance for a 23% to 24% effective tax rate. The forward special items (DKK -100 million in 2027 and DKK -90 million in 2028) are broker estimates.

Section 5 (currency and dilution) is High confidence. The report states this rating because the data come from official filings. Key facts are that 46.8% of 2025 net sales were in US dollars, that a 5% move creates a DKK 224.9 million sales impact and a DKK 45.7 million EBITDA impact, and that 1.238 million treasury shares were held in September 2026.

Section 6 (2026 earnings base and EPS bridge) is Low confidence. It rests on inference rather than disclosure. The report strips DKK 166 million of refunds from DKK 1,827 million of EBITDA guidance. It then compares broker figures of DKK 11.12 and DKK 11.43 with DKK 11.30 and DKK 11.60. No company-published bridge or independent model fixes the underlying 2026 base.

Major gaps. First, the contract manufacturing margin is unknown. The evidence gives only the term, up to three years, with a six-month termination notice. The agreement is described as expected to be lower-margin, with no figure.

Second, there is no tariff litigation update beyond the ruling and the formal refund process opened by US Customs and Border Protection. Third, the debt maturity and floating-rate profile is dated. The 46% fixed share is an end-2025 figure, before the divestment proceeds.

What would resolve them. The full-year 2026 annual report should provide the debt maturity ladder, the floatingrate share, the performance share unit table, a Pillar Two update and the final special items. Customs counsel or trade-law expert calls, plus the CBP refund schedule and court dockets, would clarify refund timing and tariff exposure.

A call with investor relations or the CFO could address the Japan Tobacco contract margin, buyback size and timing, and the dividend within the 40% to 60% range.
