Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Regulatory threats and premium tier adoption represent the twin forces determining the duration of double-digit revenue growth for European online classifieds.
  2. A certified antitrust claim against Rightmove could trigger sector-wide regulatory pressure, while slow tier migration poses downside risks to consensus earnings estimates.

Defining the Key Question

1.
Key question: "How far can the national portals keep raising revenue per agent and dealer before regulators or customers stop them? First, what do brokers, lawyers and experts expect from the Competition Appeal Tribunal certification hearing in November 2026 on the GBP 1.56 billion claim against Rightmove, including any fee-cap remedy or a Competition and Markets Authority market study, and do they see a read-across to Autotrader, Scout24, Hemnet or Swiss Marketplace? Second, how fast are customers moving to the top tiers since mid-2026, given Scout24 Gold at about 10 percent of agencies, Hemnet Max at about 2 percent and the Swiss Marketplace Expert tier at 7 percent of real estate revenue?."
2.
Declining pricing power: Rightmove's upcoming Competition Appeal Tribunal certification hearing in November 2026 represents a critical milestone that could disrupt the portal's historical pricing power. Real estate agents and developers have accused the platform of abusing its market dominance to enforce unfair fee increases. If the tribunal certifies the GBP 1.56 billion class action, it could introduce a fee-cap remedy that severely curtails future average revenue per user growth.
3.
Slower growth trajectories: Scout24's professional subscription segment remains a core driver of top-line expansion through customer migration to premium tiers. Currently, 60% of B2B customers remain on the entry-level Bronze tier, while only 10% have adopted the premium Gold tier. Morgan Stanley estimates that faster adoption of these higher-priced packages represents a key upside risk to the company's mid-term financial targets.
4.
Subdued package adoption: Hemnet's strategic pivot to free listings in late 2026 aims to rebuild platform network effects after losing listings share to competitors like Booli. The Swedish portal previously struggled with a double-digit decline in published listings and stalled adoption of its premium Max package, which stood at just 2% penetration. DNB Carnegie cautions that if the new initiatives fail to encourage free-to-paid conversion, Hemnet faces significant downside risks to its long-term pricing power.
5.
Contrary evidence: Deutsche Bank Research suggests that the regulatory and legal risks surrounding Rightmove's November court case are actually low. Category-leading portals with proprietary datasets maintain exceptionally strong moats that preserve their value proposition in an AI-driven environment. This indicates that national portals can continue to exercise pricing power, as their essential gatekeeper status shields them from significant agent churn.

Section 2 – Rightmove and the Competition Appeal Tribunal November 2026 Hearing

Section summary
  1. The upcoming Competition Appeal Tribunal certification hearing in November 2026 represents a pivotal legal challenge to Rightmove's long-term pricing model.
  2. While litigation funder Innsworth Capital backs the massive class action, brokers remain divided on the ultimate financial risk and potential for fee-cap remedies.

Details of the Legal Claim

1.
Substantial litigation risk: The class action claim brought against Rightmove has escalated to a scheduled certification hearing in November 2026. The proposed collective proceedings seek damages of up to £1.56 billion on behalf of UK real estate agents. Claimants argue that Rightmove consistently abuses its dominant market position to extract excessive fees. If certified, the case could curtail future average revenue per user growth significantly.
2.
Heavyweight legal backers: The litigation is spearheaded by former Competition and Markets Authority panel member Jeremy Newman. Newman has engaged antitrust experts Scott+Scott and barrister Kieron Beal to represent the class. The case is funded by Innsworth Capital, which supports similar opt-out actions. This backing represents a formidable legal threat to the portal going forward.
3.
Disclosure ruling details: The Competition Appeal Tribunal handed down a key disclosure ruling in July 2026. Rightmove successfully argued that it must be allowed to test the credibility of the evidence. The tribunal ordered the disclosure of communications regarding fears of commercial retaliation. This ruling allows the company to identify specific class members raising complaints before November.
4.
Procedural timeline delays: Bernstein Research suggests that collective proceedings order applications typically face significant procedural delays before going to trial. Their historical analysis of UK competition class actions shows the certification process takes over a year on average. Because most of these competition class action cases settle outside of court, the portal could potentially avoid a prolonged trial through settlement to resolve the overhang.
5.
Rising litigation costs: Rightmove expects related litigation costs of £4 million to £7 million in 2026. The company recorded £2.3 million of these legal expenses during the first half of 2026. Management expects these expenses to be temporary while they defend the claim vigorously in court because they believe it lacks merit.
