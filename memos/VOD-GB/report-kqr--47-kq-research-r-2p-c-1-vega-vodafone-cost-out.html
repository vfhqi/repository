Section 2 – Vega's expected change at Vodafone

Section summary
  1. Vega's strategic stake is poised to accelerate opex savings once regulatory clearances unlock direct governance influence at Vodafone.
  2. Pricing headwinds in Germany offset near-term operational gains despite significant synergy upgrades from the UK consolidation.

Board seats and regulatory timelines

1.
Accretive transaction: On 10 July 2026, Xavier Niel's investment vehicle Vega agreed to buy a 16.2% voting stake in Vodafone from Emirates Telecommunications Group at a premium price of 112.5 pence per share, immediately terminating the previous relationship agreement.
2.
Creeping stake: In a report dated 16 July 2026, Morgan Stanley noted that Xavier Niel quickly increased his equity holding in Vodafone to 19.9% through Vega, cementing his position as the company's largest single shareholder.
3.
Prolonged regulatory review: Morgan Stanley's 2 September 2026 analysis indicates that UK National Security and Investment Act approvals for the stake transfer could delay execution until the summer of 2027.
4.
Anticipated board seats: In a report dated 20 July 2026, Deutsche Bank noted that financial institutions are holding the shares in block trades pending regulatory approvals and eventual board seat representation.

Cost saving targets and capital returns

5.
Doubled opex targets: Morgan Stanley's 2 September 2026 report argues that Vega's lean DNA could push Vodafone to double its current European cost targets to include emerging markets.
6.

Section 3 – Tele2 and the Freya Playbook

Section summary
  1. Freya's active ownership has successfully accelerated Tele2's margin expansion through aggressive headcount and IT modernization cuts.

Operational restructuring and labor cuts

1.
Workforce reduction: In January 2025, Tele2 announced a major transformation plan aiming to reduce its total workforce by 15% or 600-700 full-time equivalents to eliminate organizational complexity.
2.
Rapid execution: By the end of 2025, Tele2 successfully completed the workforce reduction, canceling approximately 650 positions and exceeding initial cost-saving expectations in record time.
3.
IT platform modernization: A core pillar of the playbook involved consolidating Tele2's legacy IT systems, migrating from six IT stacks down to just two to streamline go-to-market execution.
4.
Digital store migration: Management under CEO Jean-Marc Harion shifted distribution away from expensive third-party retail, closing external contracts and opening new company-owned digital channels to lower churn.
5.
Network modernization savings: Tele2 achieved substantial operational efficiencies by shutting down its 2G and 3G networks in Sweden during December 2025, significantly lowering legacy energy costs.
6.
Vigorous contract renegotiation: Alongside personnel cuts, Tele2 re-negotiated over 350 supplier contracts and downsized its corporate headquarters footprint by 1,800 square meters to lower fixed administrative costs.

Capital efficiency and financial ceilings

7.
Spectacular margin expansion: Driven by these aggressive cost measures, Tele2 delivered a spectacular 11.4% organic EBITDAaL growth in 2025, outpacing flat end-user service revenue growth of 2.3%.
8.
Tele2 (Hold): A supportive main owner – Q3 preview Tele2 organic EBITDAaL growth versus end-user service revenue growth trend Capex intensity reduction: Tele2's capital expenditure declined to 10.8% of sales in 2025, coming in well below historical investment levels due to strict prioritization.
9.
Aggressive payout acceleration: Supported by robust equity free cash flow of SEK 6.2 billion, the board increased the 2025 dividend by 65% to SEK 10.50 per share.
10.
Slowing growth momentum: In July 2026, Bernstein noted that Tele2's organic EBITDAaL growth slowed to 4% in Q2 2026, suggesting that headcount savings have run their course.
11.
Approaching peak efficiency: Rothschild & Co Redburn argued in October 2026 that Tele2 is finding it increasingly difficult to deliver further cost savings, indicating that operational limits have been reached.
12.
Credit rating constraints: DNB Carnegie warned that while leverage sits at 1.7x post-Baltic tower sale, the board is unlikely to raise leverage beyond 2.2x to preserve credit metrics.
13.
Other side of the story: Bernstein cautions that without structural top-line acceleration, aggressive cost cutting creates severe operational disruption risks that threaten long-term customer service quality and churn.
