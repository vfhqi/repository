1.
Sequential expense acceleration: Banca Generali experienced a significant expansion in its cost base during the latter part of 2025. Net operating expenses reached €196.84 million in the second half of 2025, showing a sharp increase of 19.7% compared to the €164.39 million reported in the first half. This sequential increase of €32.45 million demonstrates that absolute cost pressures intensified late in the fiscal year. This surge directly impacted operating leverage prior to the subsequent year's performance.
2.

Although first-half 2026 total operating costs reached €181.80 million to mark a 10.6% increase, core costs of €164.30 million were up a more moderate 8.5%. These figures confirm that elevated expenditures persisted into 2026 rather than being an isolated event. Section 4 – Cost Methodology and Adjusted Cost-to-Income Definitions

Section summary
  1. Banca Generali's adjusted cost-to-income ratio provides a normalized view of operating efficiency by removing highly volatile performance fees and temporary non-recurring items.
  2. The bank's cost reclassification methodology restates historical figures to integrate acquisitions like Intermonte, presenting a consistent baseline for core expense growth.
  3. Banca Generali's reporting is moderately conservative, aligning with Banca Mediolanum's asset-based approach while differing structurally from Fineco's digital scalability.
  4. We have high confidence in this methodology analysis because the accounting reconciliation is explicitly detailed in the bank's consolidated interim reports.

Core Operating Cost Definition

1.
Excluding volatile elements: Banca Generali defines its core operating costs as net operating expenses adjusted to isolate structural overheads from non-recurring project costs and specific sales personnel expenses. According to the bank's first-half 2026 report, total operating expenses of EUR 181.80 million were adjusted by deducting EUR 5.40 million in non-recurring items and EUR 12 million in sales personnel costs to derive core operating costs of EUR 164.30 million. This methodology ensures that strategic tech investments and banker compensation changes do not distort the underlying cost baseline.
2.
Integrating strategic acquisitions: The bank simplifies its financial representation by restating historical series to reflect its changing corporate perimeter. Management noted in the first-quarter 2026 earnings call that historical cost series were restated to include the Intermonte Group and BG Aequitum. This reclassification prevents artificial cost spikes when acquired entities are consolidated, allowing investors to evaluate organic cost growth of 8.5% on a like-for-like basis in the first half of 2026.

Adjusted Cost-to-Income Calculation

3.
Adjusting for volatile fees: The adjusted cost-to-income ratio is a key metric utilized by management to measure operational efficiency net of market-driven distortions. Banca Generali calculates this ratio by dividing operating expenses, net of non-recurring items, by net operating income that excludes highly volatile performance fees. In the first half of 2026, this adjusted cost-to-income ratio stood at 36.7%, marking an improvement from 39.0% at year-end 2025.
4.
Systemic charge reclassification: To further refine its efficiency presentation, Banca Generali isolates mandatory banking system contributions from its core operating structure. Under this methodology, ordinary and extraordinary contributions paid to the Italian Interbank Deposit Protection Fund and the European Single Resolution Fund are reclassified from administrative expenses. This adjustment aligns the bank's disclosures with widespread market practices and highlights the efficiency of its direct operating structure.

Banca Mediolanum targeted a cost-to-income ratio of approximately 38% for 2026, which is comparable to Banca Generali's adjusted range.
