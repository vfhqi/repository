The power price and hedging trajectories of Fortum and Polish utilities are highly resolvable through forward curves and disclosed hedging books, offering visible 18-month EPS pathways, although regulatory price caps and squeeze-out actions disrupt Polish utility valuations despite supportive capacity market revenues through 2028.

The investment landscape for Nordic and Polish power utilities targeting 2027-2028 forward exposures presents a bifurcated opportunity set where structural resolvability varies sharply across geographies and regulatory frameworks. The key question focuses on Nordic and Polish forward power prices for 2027 and 2028, companyspecific hedge ratios, and verifying the 24 September Energa print of 0.37, with the ultimate objective of identifying predictable 18-month to 3-year EPS trajectories capable of delivering 25%+ internal rate of return potential within a concentrated long-only portfolio framework.

The Nordic power market structure exhibits strong fundamental support for elevated forward curves, driven by a 15% year-on-year reservoir deficit in Sweden that is driving Nordic forward prices higher and Nordic baseload contracts averaging €66/MWh in 2027 before softening to €53/MWh in 2028. Meanwhile, Polish 2027 base power forwards rose to PLN 590 per MWh in mid-September due to worsening regional energy outlooks, reflecting the acute supply-demand imbalances plaguing the Polish system.

Critical grid infrastructure developments including the 1.4 GW Viking Link trading at maximum capacity and Finnish datacenter estimates revised upward to 2,000 MW, meaning computing will consume 10% of national electricity by 2030 are fundamentally reshaping Nordic demand profiles and creating unprecedented structural tightness. Fortum's operational dynamics present a highly attractive near-term earnings pathway, supported by comparable operating profit that increased by 10% to EUR 613 million in H1 2026 and a transformational commercial milestone in which Fortum secured a 22-year nuclear power purchase agreement with Google priced approximately EUR 30/MWh above the forward curve. The company maintains 80% of its remaining 2026 generation hedged at EUR 40/MWh and 65% of 2027 generation at EUR 41/MWh, with an expected 2028 hedge ratio of 45% at an estimated price of EUR 44/MWh. This structured hedging framework provides exceptional near-term cash flow visibility, although Fortum's achieved power price of EUR 44.90/MWh in Q2 2026 fell below the blended spot market price of EUR 53.10/MWh due to a high hedge ratio and nuclear outages, highlighting the trade-off between hedging stability and spot market upside capture. 7 state utility landscape demonstrates fundamentally different risk-return characteristics, dominated by aggressive regulatory interventions that create profound valuation uncertainty.

Energa shares skyrocketed by approximately 37% following the announcement of a compulsory buyout at PLN 27.60, validating the 24 September print as an extraordinary single-day equity performance event rather than a valuation multiple. PGE outsmarted peers by securing coal capacity market revenues until 2028, whereas competitors' contracts expire in 2026, and PGE plans to invest PLN 37 billion in distribution during 2025-2030, which drives highquality regulated EBITDA growth.

However, underapproved retail G-tariffs of PLN 495.16/MWh for 2026 forced PGE to recognize a PLN 560 million loss, demonstrating how arbitrary state pricing mandates can instantaneously destroy shareholder value.

Critical information gaps surrounding Fortum's undisclosed explicit 2028 hedge ratios, requiring a wait-andmonitor strategy until the company releases its Q3 results and pending Polish G-tariff levels or retail price cap decisions for 2027, requiring wait-and-monitor tracking of official bulletins published in late December introduce moderate near-term analytical uncertainty. Yet these gaps are systematically addressable through structured monitoring protocols and targeted expert consultations, unlike the fundamentally unresolvable political risk embedded in Polish utilities where information gaps are inherently unresolvable because they stem from arbitrary state political interventions, and any wait-and-monitor approach on Polish utilities might fail to protect capital from unpredictable regulatory value destruction.

Second-order consequences of sustained power price elevation include aggressive windfall levies on statebacked utilities to fund retail energy relief programmes during acute price spikes and pending regulatory energy tariff decisions fueling Polish inflation, forcing the central bank to delay rate cuts through late 2027. The analysis reveals a profound outlier scenario in which extreme spot price volatility and localized grid bottlenecks in Finland threaten commercial feasibility closures for highly energy-intensive industrial pilot plants before achieving final investment decisions, representing a tail risk to structural demand assumptions underpinning bullish Nordic forward curves. (Poland) The aggregate investment verdict positions Fortum as a Stage 2 Probing Bet with exceptional near-term earnings visibility enhanced by transformational datacenter partnerships, whereas Polish state utilities represent Stage 1 Speculative Bets fundamentally constrained by unpredictable regulatory interference.

The key question is partially resolvable for Nordic utilities given transparent hedging disclosures and observable forward curves, but remains fundamentally indeterminate for Polish utilities where state intervention risk systematically overwhelms conventional fundamental analysis frameworks. Equity investors seeking predictable 18-month EPS trajectories will find substantially higher confidence in Nordic exposures, while Polish positions demand significantly wider margin-of-safety requirements to compensate for inherent regulatory unpredictability and arbitrary wealth redistribution risk.

Section 6 – Data gaps

Section summary
  1. Key information gaps around 2028 hedge disclosures and Polish regulatory interventions obscure mid-term utility cash flows, requiring structured monitoring of upcoming Q3 filings.

Commercial Hedging and Operational Gaps

1.
Opaque hedging disclosures: Fortum has not officially disclosed its explicit 2028 hedge ratios, leaving the market to rely on broker expectations of a 45% hedge ratio. Resolving this requires a wait-and-monitor strategy until the company releases its Q3 results.
2.
Opaque PPA pricing: Stated pricing terms for major data center power purchase agreements remain closely guarded secrets, creating models based on indirect return assumptions. Analysts must utilize expert calls with transaction originators or consult subscription services like Pexapark to verify these contract premiums.
3.
Unconfirmed grid connections: There is highly limited visibility regarding the absolute volumes and timelines of PGE's 2 GW data center grid connection requests. This critical gap can be resolved through field visits to regional distribution centers and scheduled management interviews.
4.
Incomplete retail provisions: Energa has established a massive PLN 304 million provision for onerous retail contracts, but its future utilization remains highly dynamic. This exposure can be resolved by a detailed wait-and-monitor approach of the upcoming G-tariff decisions.

Regulatory and Structural Policy Gaps

5.
Pending tariff approvals: The Polish energy regulator has not yet finalized G-tariff levels or retail price cap decisions for 2027. Resolving this requires wait-and-monitor tracking of official bulletins published in late December.
6.
Unresolved coal restructuring: State plans to spin off legacy coal units to a single state entity have been repealed due to a massive PLN 53.8 billion funding gap. Analysts should conduct expert calls with industry advisors to evaluate the alternative restructuring models.
7.
Stalled datacenter legislation: Proposed Finnish laws requiring new datacenters to construct matching generation capacity remain completely unpassed. The risk can be clarified via wait-and-monitor tracking of legislative votes or expert calls with regional policy groups.
