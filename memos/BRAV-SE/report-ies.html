Bravida (BRAV-SE): what the Q3 2026 results do to the existing case

The Q3 2026 results modestly weaken the case's cautious stance: the quarter beat on margin and cash, not only on the volume the case expected, and the margin beat came from the core business, where the case had not looked for upside; the hinge the case rests on, the data-centre contract margin, is no better evidenced than before.

What changed

  • Margin, not just volume. Net sales were SEK 7,027m for Q3 2026 (reported 23 October 2026), organic growth 9%, against a consensus of SEK 6,945m. EBITA was SEK 460m (6.5% margin; 5.3% a year earlier) against a consensus of SEK 417m and 6.0%. Before SEK 8m of Swedish restructuring cost, adjusted EBITA was SEK 468m, a 6.7% margin. The case's own marker for the quarter was adjusted EBITA of about SEK 420m and a margin of about 6.0%. The volume was as the case expected; the margin was about 70 basis points above it.
  • Denmark. The Danish EBITA margin rose 200 basis points to 7.5% on net sales up only 1% to SEK 1,929m, against DNB Carnegie's 5.8%. Management attributed it to mix and the turnaround, not to one-off items. The case carries Denmark at 5.1% rising to 5.8% by FY2028 and treats the Danish recovery as already in the base.
  • Cash. Operating cash flow was SEK 800m (negative SEK 111m a year earlier), helped by the settlement of overdue receivables. Rolling twelve-month cash conversion reached 110%, from 77% at June 2026. Net debt was SEK 2,564m, 1.0x EBITDA. The case expected conversion back above 100% at the Q4 print; it arrived a quarter early, for the reasons the case named.
  • Orders. Order intake was SEK 10,317m (+72%) and the backlog SEK 24,216m (+48%), above the case's SEK 22bn test. The Mälardalen award (SEK 850m), which the case held at half weight as probable, is signed. Data-centre projects were about one third of absolute sales growth.
  • Two things said on the call. Profit on the large data-centre contracts is recognised cautiously in their early stages, under the same rules as other large projects. A second SEK 100m buyback runs from 2 November 2026 to 5 February 2027: the same size as the first, not the larger programme the case listed as possible.
  • Sell-side. DNB Carnegie raised its target to SEK 177 from SEK 173 (Buy) and its adjusted EPS to SEK 7.3 for 2026e (+5%), SEK 9.2 for 2027e and SEK 9.9 for 2028e (+3% each). Deutsche Bank called the quarter strong. No other broker update is in hand. The case expected FY2027 estimates held or up 0% to 3%; the one visible revision is at the top of that range.
  • Not in hand. The Finnish and Swedish segment margins, which two of the case's four tests need, are not given. Finland's sales were SEK 701m; Sweden's SEK 3,047m (+9% organic).

The challenge

Reading one: the core margin is moving, and the case has the wrong half of the gap under watch. The case splits its gap to consensus into two parts: the data-centre contract margin (5% to 6% against DNB Carnegie's 10%) and the core margin (the case's 6.6% against a 7.0% it describes as never reached in ten years). It treats the first as the hinge and the second as a ceiling. This quarter says the ceiling may be giving way. A 6.7% adjusted margin arrived in a quarter where data-centre work was a third of the growth and was booked cautiously, so the rest of the business was probably earning at or above 6.7%. Denmark's 200 basis points on SEK 1,929m is only about SEK 39m of a SEK 118m rise in group EBITA, so roughly two thirds of the improvement came from the other three countries, which is not a one-country story. If selective bidding is producing real price and mix in the core, consensus can be right on FY2027 without the data-centre contracts earning anything above the case's 5% to 6%, because the case sizes the core half of the gap at about SEK 125m of EBITA, close to the contract half at about SEK 157m.

What would have to be true: Denmark's 7.5% holds when Denmark grows again, since it was reached on flat sales and the case's own test of a turnaround is margin held with growth; the Swedish margin is up by more than the SEK 16m a quarter the savings explain; and the group margin does not fade from Q1 2027 as idle capacity fills. One quarter does not prove this, and DNB Carnegie itself had expected Danish gains to slow. But the case gives this reading no probability, and it should have some.

Reading two: cautious early profit recognition breaks the case's evidence calendar, in both directions. The case names the Finnish and Norwegian segment margins in February and May 2027 as the first reads on the hinge, with 8% or more opening the evidence route. If little profit is booked in a contract's early stages, those reads are biased low by construction. A soft Finnish margin in February would then say nothing about whether atNorth earns 5% or 10%. Three things follow that the case does not hold. The evidence route probably cannot open on the dates the case names. The margin fade the case expects from Q1 2027 could appear for accounting reasons, and whether the market reads that as a miss decides whether the case's price route to SEK 115 to 120 opens. And profit held back early is released late, in 2028 to 2029, the years where the case's return arithmetic depends on forward EPS standing still at SEK 8.80. Held-back margin is also a cushion against the SEK 100m to 400m write-down the case gives a 20% to 30% chance.

What would have to be true: the caution is material in size, which the company has not quantified, and it applies to the contracts that matter. On the case's own account Green Mountain is cost-plus, where margin accrues as cost is incurred, so the effect is plausibly confined to atNorth and the Swedish jobs. If the statement is boilerplate, this reading falls away.

A reading we do not put up. The record backlog as proof of a structural change is not an alternative the case lacks. Every large award was known to the case, the capacity ceiling it describes is untested by anything reported, and the backlog says nothing about margin.

What it does to the case

  • Internally-driven corporate change forces (60, D): more pressure, upward. Its reasoning cites Denmark at 5.1% and 77% cash conversion as an offset. Both figures are overtaken.
  • Setups' Fit/Crispness (72, B): slightly more pressure, upward. One of the two named blemishes, 77% cash conversion, has gone. The two main reasons it is held down, the capacity ceiling and unproven margin conversion on the new work, stand.
  • Scope for forecasts to lead to upgrades (35, D): slightly more pressure, upward. The first revision is at the top of the case's range, and reading one is a route to upgrades the case weights at nil. The dated negatives in 2027 are untouched.
  • Riskiness and simplicity (50, C+) and Case Riskiness (50, C): the same. The hinge is as unobservable as before; reading two makes it harder to observe on the dates the case names.
  • Foundations' Robustness (56, C) and the conservative-guidance topic (45, D): the same. A margin beat in an upswing is the pattern the case describes, and the ten-year record is not changed by one quarter.
  • The fair multiple (34, D) and total shareholder return (25, D): the same. The share price was SEK 147.4 on the day of the report and SEK 143.7 on 9 November 2026. That is at the case's base value of SEK 145 and far above its SEK 115 to 120 entry zone.
  • The advice to lighten on the prints: less pressure on the direction, more on the mechanics. The case said a close below the 50-day average after a volume beat would say momentum is exhausted. The price is now below its 50-day average of SEK 146.5, though above the SEK 141 level the case wrote. The print gave no strength to lighten into.

Of the case's four tests for this quarter, the order book passed; the group margin rose 120 basis points year on year, which probably passes the core-margin test of 40 basis points; Finland and Sweden cannot be scored from what we have.

What would settle it

  • The second review, four working days on. It should carry the remaining brokers' estimate and target changes, so it can show whether FY2027 consensus moved by more than the case's 0% to 3%. It can also carry the Finnish and Swedish segment margins, which the company has already published and which close the two unscored tests. It will not carry anything on the contract margin or on how much profit is being held back.
  • The Q4 2026 report, on the case's date of 10 February 2027. A second Danish margin, the Swedish margin against the savings, and the first Finnish and Norwegian margins with data-centre work inside them. For reading one, Denmark at or near 7% with growth would be strong support; a return toward 5.8% would probably end it.
  • The Q1 2027 report in May. The case's fade test. A group margin held with data-centre revenue rising supports reading one; a fade leaves reading two and the case's own view indistinguishable.
  • What nothing scheduled answers. The company has not committed to disclose contract-level margins or to quantify its recognition policy. Reading two can therefore be resolved only indirectly and late, as contracts approach completion in 2028. That is a different kind of open question from the others, and it is the one the case's evidence route depends on.
