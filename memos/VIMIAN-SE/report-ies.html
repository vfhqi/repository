Vimian (VIMIAN-SE): what the Q3 2026 results do to the existing case

These results strengthen the operating half of our case moderately, because the cash test the case set was passed and MedTech grew at about twice the case's modal rate, and they leave its conclusion (good change, fully priced, park) where it was and further from a purchase, because the shares at SEK 42.1 (9 November 2026) now stand above the case's own eighteen-month value of SEK 38.63.

1. What changed

Reported on 22 October 2026 for the quarter to 30 September 2026. Group figures unless a segment is named.

  • Growth. Revenue EUR 120.3m, up 15%. Organic growth 11% against a consensus of 8%. The case's modal for the second half of 2026 was 9% to 10%.
  • Profit. Adjusted EBITA EUR 29.2m, 4% above a consensus of EUR 28.1m. Margin 24.3% against 24.5% a year earlier. Across the three quarters of 2026 adjusted EBITA was EUR 29.2m, EUR 29.7m and EUR 29.2m, and the margin 25.2%, 24.4% and 24.3%.
  • MedTech. Organic growth 15%, from 11% in Q2 2026. Segment margin 24.4% against 23.7% a year earlier. North American orthopaedics grew at a low-single-digit rate, with management describing surgical market conditions as unchanged. The case's modal was 7% to 9% segment growth and a 26% to 27% margin, and its alarm level on that margin is below 24%.
  • Specialty Pharma. Organic growth 7% as reported and 11% on management's adjustment for the prior-year US campaign. Margin 30.3% against 29.5%. The case's alarm is underlying growth below 8% for one quarter.
  • Veterinary Services and Diagnostics. Organic growth 10% and 13%. Margins 30.5% (management expects about 28.5% in Q4 2026) and 7.2% (13.8% in Q1 2026).
  • Cash. Operating cash flow EUR 22.6m against EUR 10.8m a year earlier, which is 66.7% of adjusted EBITDA against 37.1%, and about 77% of adjusted EBITA, the basis the case uses (our arithmetic). The case's test was above 60%. Working capital released EUR 0.3m in the quarter. Net debt EUR 262.4m at 30 September 2026, 2.0x pro-forma adjusted EBITDA, from 2.1x at 30 June 2026.
  • Deal and targets. Norfolk Medical Products adds about USD 6.9m of trailing revenue at about 9x EBITDA, which management called the group's historical average; the case carries 7.5x as that average. The ambition of adjusted EBITA above EUR 300m by 2030 was reaffirmed, and a first Capital Markets Day is set for 24 November 2026.
  • End market. US clinic visits fell 2.2% year on year in Q3 2026, the eighteenth consecutive quarterly decline. Swedencare grew 2% organically with North America down 4%. Zoetis reported a 1% organic decline.
  • Sell-side response. DNB Carnegie kept Buy, raised its target to SEK 45 from SEK 40, raised adjusted EBITA by 1% for 2026 and 3% for 2027 and 2028, and lifted its anchor multiple to about 17x from 16x 2027e EV/EBITA. Jefferies (28 October 2026) kept Hold, raised its target to SEK 40 from SEK 35 on a lower risk premium, and holds a 2027 margin estimate of 24.9%, 50 basis points below consensus. Both say the debate has moved from growth to margin conversion.

2. The challenge

One obvious alternative fails, and we say so first. The reading that Specialty Pharma's 7% is the hangover of a campaign-flattered first half does not hold, because the same adjustment that took the Q2 2026 figure down from 14% to about 9% in the case takes Q3 up to 11%, and the segment's year-to-date organic growth is 11%. The case called the campaign effect contained, and it was.

Three readings survive. The first and the third pull in opposite directions, and that is the finding: the same print supports a lower margin path and a higher multiple.

Reading one: the margin is not lagging the growth, it is the price of the growth. The case treats the flat group margin as an output that has not yet arrived, and builds in about 40 basis points a year of expansion from mix. This quarter was the cleanest test of that so far, and it points the other way. Every segment's margin rose year on year and the group's still fell, because central costs rose and the fastest-growing segment, Diagnostics, carries the lowest margin. MedTech had its strongest growth of the recovery and a margin of 24.4%, only 40 basis points above the case's alarm level; the year-on-year gain the case recorded at Q2 2026 as 210 basis points was 70 basis points in Q3. Group adjusted EBITA has been about EUR 29m for three quarters running while revenue grew 15%. In its strongest form: a field sales force, the training of 1,300 veterinarians in a quarter, new country launches and a larger centre are the recurring cost of holding double-digit growth in a market where visits fall 2% a year, so the group margin settles near 24% rather than climbing to the case's 26% by FY2028. For this to be right, central costs have to keep rising with revenue, MedTech's margin has to stay below the case's 26% to 27% while it grows above 10%, and Diagnostics has to miss management's recovery from 2027. If it is right, the case loses its margin contribution of about 1.6 points a year of earnings growth, and the Jefferies margin path becomes the modal one rather than the cautious one.

Reading two: the cash test was passed on the flow and not on the stock. The case accepted that the Q2 2026 receivables build would reverse in Q3 and set a 60% conversion test. Conversion cleared the test, but working capital released only EUR 0.3m, against the EUR 21.3m the case recorded as built in Q2. The build stopped; it did not come back. In its strongest form: moving from distributors to direct sales and from an annual order programme to shipping on demand permanently raises the receivables and stock the group carries, so on the case's own 26% ratio each euro of growth absorbs about a quarter of a euro of working capital, and Q3 shows what normal conversion looks like rather than a staging post to the case's full-year modal of above 80%. For this to be right, Q4 2026 has to show no release and year-end working capital has to sit above the case's invalidation level of 24% of revenue. The Q3 ratio is not in the evidence we have. The case's invalidation needs a failed conversion together with a high ratio; a passed conversion with a high ratio is an outcome the case does not classify, and it bears directly on the self-funding Key Driver.

Reading three: the re-rating the case gave 20% odds is under way, on less evidence than the case required. The case said both tests passing would lift the re-rating branch from 20% toward 35%. One passed and one did not: North American orthopaedics is at low single digit, not mid. Yet broker targets rose 12% to 14% while their profit estimates rose 1% to 3%, DNB Carnegie added a turn to its multiple, Jefferies cited a lower risk premium while keeping its margin below consensus, and the price moved from the case's SEK 35.7 to SEK 42.1. In its strongest form: the five-year median multiple the case uses as its modal belongs to a company with a dismissed chief executive, an abandoned target and two equity raises; with the leadership layer complete, cash conversion re-established and leverage at 2.0x, the fair multiple sits above that median, and the market is paying for the durability of growth without waiting for margin. For this to be right, organic growth has to hold near double digits through 2027, conversion has to hold, and the Nordic compounder set must not de-rate. If it is right, the case's re-entry price of about SEK 29 is a level the market is unlikely to offer, and the park has no working trigger. If it is wrong, it is the case's de-rating branch starting from a higher price.

That bears on reading three and on the pattern's named output. It carries no new company disclosure, so it does not answer readings one or two.

A guidance increase would bear on 'Guidance raised' (KQ 4, RA 2, E 1, BB 1), D. A reaffirmed 2030 figure without a bridge settles nothing.
