Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. AQ Group's decentralized operating model faces tax headwinds in its key low-tax manufacturing jurisdictions due to Pillar Two compliance.

Defining the Key Question

1.
Testing the premise: "For AQ Group (AQ-SE), how much additional tax will the OECD Pillar Two global minimum tax impose on its profits in Bulgaria, Estonia and Lithuania (named in its 2025 annual report as failing the transitional safe harbours), has the company been accruing top-up tax in its income statement since Pillar Two began applying to it on 1 January 2025 or will the effect appear only when cash is first paid in 2027, how large are the substance-based income exclusions for its payroll and tangible assets in those countries and how fast do they decline, have those countries adopted qualified domestic minimum top-up taxes or other safe harbours (such as Bulgaria's 2025 amendments), and what effective and cash tax rates do brokers model and comparable Nordic manufacturers with Baltic or Bulgarian plants (Kitron, HANZA, Scanfil) report?" According to AQ Group's 2025 annual report, this central inquiry evaluates how the OECD global minimum tax rules will affect the long-term profitability of AQ Group across its European footprint. Understanding these tax dynamics is essential for investors because DNB Carnegie's note of 24 June 2026 suggests any unmitigated top-up taxes will directly reduce the group's future earnings per share.

Decentralized operations and segments

2.
Segmented business structure: AQ Group operates through two core business segments named Component and System, as detailed in its 2025 annual report. The same report shows the larger Component segment produces specialized items like transformers, generating SEK 8,295 million in net sales during 2025. Conversely, the System segment focuses on electrical cabinets, delivering SEK 1,470 million in net sales in that same year. This segment mix means that the asset-heavy Component business represents the bulk of group sales and earnings.
3.
Decentralized global footprint: AQ Group maintains a highly decentralized operational model with approximately 8,000 employees working across 17 countries, as stated in its press release of 22 September 2026. In its 2025 annual report, the company claims this model allows for rapid decision-making and proximity to demanding industrial OEMs. However, its interim report of 13 July 2026 shows a large portion of this workforce is concentrated in low-cost manufacturing hubs, including 1,308 employees in Bulgaria. Consequently, maintaining competitive pricing depends on the cost advantages of these Baltic and Bulgarian operations.

Pillar Two compliance threats

4.
Operational risk profiles: AQ Group's decentralized model relies on moving production between independent subsidiaries to optimize customer service, according to its year-end report of 12 February 2026. While this operational agility helps manage geopolitical disruptions, its interim report of 14 July 2026 notes that local tax entities must still calculate separate Pillar Two liabilities. This means that localized top-up taxes could disincentivize internal manufacturing shifts to historically low-tax Baltic plants. Ultimately, these regulatory frictions threaten to complicate decentralized capital allocation decisions and raise cash tax rates.
5.
Increasing tax burdens: Pillar Two global minimum tax regulations threaten to disrupt AQ Group's historical cost advantages in low-tax jurisdictions, as highlighted in its interim report of 14 July 2026. The company's 2025 annual report indicates the group exceeded the EUR 750 million threshold in 2024, subjecting its low-tax profits to a 15% minimum rate starting 1 January 2025. These rules are expected to increase the group's tax burden going forward, as noted in its 2024 annual report. Investors must watch this regulatory headwind because DNB Carnegie's note of 24 June 2026 argues it could compress consolidated net profit margins and lower EPS.
6.
Contrary evidence: AQ Group's 2025 annual report indicates that the average tax expense of 19% was in line with the prior year despite Pillar Two provisions. Furthermore, DNB Carnegie's report of 14 July 2026 indicates that strong organic demand in data centers and defense segments will support robust EPS growth. This suggests that robust revenue-driven earnings growth may completely overshadow any near-term minimum tax headwinds, according to DNB Carnegie's note of 9 October 2026.
7.
Other side of the story: Although compliance with Pillar Two introduces administrative complexities, AQ Group's interim report of 15 October 2025 notes that a reassessment of calculations will be made as regulations evolve. Its 2024 annual report suggests that substance-based income exclusions for payroll and tangible assets will likely reduce potential top-up tax exposures. Therefore, the eventual cash tax drain starting in 2027 might be substantially lower than feared, as projected in its interim report of 15 October 2026.

Section 2 – Pillar Two Top-Up Tax Accruals and Balance Sheet Timing

Section summary
  1. AQ Group has been actively accruing Pillar Two top-up tax liabilities in its income statement since 1 January 2025.
  2. The financial statement impact of these provisions is immediate, whereas the actual cash tax payments are deferred until 2027.
  3. We have high confidence in this timing because both annual reports and interim reports confirm the ongoing accounting accruals.

Timing of Pillar Two accruals

1.
Active tax provisioning: AQ Group actively recognizes its Pillar Two tax exposures directly in its consolidated income statement rather than waiting for cash settlement. The company's annual report published on 25 March 2026 notes that its accounting under OECD rules started on 1 January 2025. This means that provisions for additional top-up taxes are accrued immediately as part of current tax expenses. Consequently, quarterly net income and earnings per share reflect these regulatory costs well before the actual payments are made.
2.
Delayed cash obligations: A significant gap exists between the accounting accrual of top-up taxes and the actual cash outflow. The group's interim report from 15 October 2026 reiterates that the first cash payment of these taxes is deferred until 2027. This delayed timing allows the company to maintain its near-term liquidity despite the immediate negative impact on recorded profits. However, the accruing balance sheet liabilities will eventually require substantial cash outlays.
3.
Elevating tax expenses: The early implementation of these top-up tax provisions has already begun pushing up the group's average tax rate. According to the company's third-quarter interim report from 15 October 2026, the average tax expense for the first nine months of 2026 increased to 20%. This is higher than the 18% reported for the same period in the prior year. Thus, the ongoing accrual of these tax liabilities is actively diluting current net income margins.
4.
Cumulative provisioning impact: The cumulative balance sheet provisions are growing as the group continues to report profitable quarters. The group's second-quarter interim report on 14 July 2026 states that the average tax expense of 20% includes provisions for additional tax. Because these provisions are recorded under current tax liabilities, they steadily accumulate on the balance sheet. This ongoing buildup means that accrued liabilities will peak just before the first cash payments commence in 2027.

IAS 12 accounting treatment

5.
IAS 12 compliance: AQ Group adheres strictly to the temporary exception under IAS 12 amendments when preparing its financial statements. The company's annual report from 24 March 2025 confirms that the group applied these international tax reform guidelines to manage its reporting. While these rules prevent the recognition of deferred taxes related to Pillar Two, they mandate full disclosure of current tax impact. This ensures that the group's balance sheet reflects only the immediate liabilities.
6.
Current tax integration: Under the provisions of Note 15 in the 2025 annual report, top-up tax provisions are blended into the overall current tax lines. The report dated 25 March 2026 shows that current tax for 2025 was SEK 139 million, which was unchanged from 2024. The additional tax is explicitly included in the current tax figures listed within the tax specification table. This integration prevents the top-up tax from being isolated as a separate line item on the face of the income statement.
7.
Regulatory reporting demands: Expert opinions highlight that multinational companies face immense pressure to automate their complex Pillar Two tax calculations. A global consulting expert specializing in financial reporting stated on 20 October 2026 that demand for regulatory tax compliance solutions is up 20% due to global minimum tax rules. This indicates that managing multi-entity tax data foundations requires significant administrative software investments. For decentralized firms like AQ Group, this regulatory burden adds ongoing compliance overhead.
8.
Reporting transition mechanics: AQ Group's transition to Pillar Two reporting required a baseline assessment of all international operations. The company's 2024 annual report published on 24 March 2025 outlines that the group had to analyze its potential exposure based on transitional safe harbour rules. Since key hubs like Bulgaria and Estonia failed these tests, the group had to set up full calculation models for 2025. Consequently, the accounting team has been running dual-reporting tracks to ensure compliance.

Section 3 – Substance-Based Income Exclusions and Decline Trajectories

Section summary
  1. Substance-Based Income Exclusions provide significant near-term tax relief for AQ Group in its Baltic and Bulgarian operations.
  2. These exclusions will steadily decline through 2033 under the OECD transition schedule, exposing more profits to top-up taxes.
  3. We have medium confidence because while the OECD rules are defined, AQ's exact local asset and payroll breakdowns are combined.

OECD Transition Carve-Out Schedule

1.
Regulated carve-out rates: The global minimum tax framework includes transition rules that offer temporary relief to companies like AQ Group. According to the OECD model rules, the substance-based income exclusion allows companies to exclude a percentage of their payroll costs and tangible assets from the top-up tax base. This transitional relief is designed to reduce the immediate tax burden on substantive economic activities in local jurisdictions.
2.
Step-down transition percentages: The transition percentages for both payroll and assets are scheduled to decline annually from 2025 to 2033. In 2025, the payroll carve-out is set at 9.6% while the eligible tangible assets carve-out is 7.6%. These step-down percentages will shrink gradually each year until reaching a permanent threshold of 5.0% for both categories in 2033.
3.
Formula-driven profit exclusion: Under the OECD rules, the top-up tax is applied only to profits that exceed the calculated substance exclusions. The exclusion is calculated as the sum of the applicable payroll and asset percentages. This means that jurisdictions with high capital and labor presence can report significant profits without triggering tax.
4.
Accelerating tax exposure: The annual reduction in exclusion rates will expose a larger share of low-tax profits to the 15% global minimum tax. For example, the payroll rate drops from 9.4% in 2026 to 9.2% in 2027, and the asset rate drops from 7.4% to 7.2% in the same period. This means that unless AQ Group increases its local headcount or capital investments, the taxable top-up base will expand.

Country-Specific Exclusion Estimations

5.
Bulgarian substance exclusions: Bulgaria represents one of the largest potential exclusion pools for AQ Group due to its substantial manufacturing presence. The company had 1,480 employees in Bulgaria in 2025 and reported combined regional assets of SEK 292 million. Applying the 2025 carve-out percentages to these asset bases provides a substantial shield against local top-up taxes.
6.
Baltic substance exclusions: Operations in Lithuania and Estonia also qualify for exclusions, though their asset and payroll profiles differ. In 2025, Lithuania had 1,091 employees and assets of SEK 236 million, while Estonia had 611 employees and SEK 286 million in assets. These figures mean that Lithuania's exclusion is heavily weighted toward payroll, whereas Estonia's exclusion benefits from recent high investments in production equipment.
7.
Regional wage rate factors: Low wage rates in Bulgaria and the Baltic states limit the absolute monetary value of payroll exclusions. Although employee headcounts are high in Bulgaria, average salaries are much lower than in Sweden. Consequently, the monetary value of payroll exclusions is lower than headcount figures might initially suggest.
8.
Contrary evidence: The company's country-level asset disclosures in its 2025 annual report merge both tangible and intangible assets, which may lead to an overestimation of eligible exclusions. Under OECD GloBE rules, only tangible assets qualify for the substance-based exclusion, while intangible assets must be completely excluded. This means that the actual eligible asset carve-out is likely lower than calculations based on total non-current regional assets suggest.
9.
Capital investment offsets: AQ Group's ongoing capital expenditures in low-tax countries help maintain the absolute size of its asset-based exclusions. In 2025, the company invested SEK 39 million in production equipment in Estonia, which expanded its local tangible asset base. Continued capital investments in these regions are critical because they help offset the scheduled decline in carve-out percentages.

Segment Profiling and Asset Intensity

10.
Asset-heavy Component segment: The Component segment holds the majority of AQ Group's tangible assets and qualifies for the largest carve-outs. This segment manufactures transformers, wiring harnesses, and sheet metal, which require significant machinery and factory space. Because these manufacturing processes are capital-intensive, the Component segment provides a robust shield against the global minimum tax.
11.
Asset-light System segment: Conversely, the System segment operates with a much lighter asset footprint and has limited exclusion capacity. This segment focuses on electrical cabinets and complete system assembly, which rely primarily on labor rather than heavy machinery. Consequently, the System segment has minimal tangible asset carve-outs and is more vulnerable to top-up taxes in low-tax jurisdictions.
12.
Segment payroll differences: Payroll-based exclusions are also unevenly distributed between the segments due to different labor requirements. The Component segment employs a vast manufacturing workforce across Bulgaria and Poland to support its sheet metal and wiring operations. This large headcount creates a significant payroll exclusion buffer that helps protect the segment's operating income.
13.
Segment capital expenditure differences: The Component segment's capital expenditures consistently exceed those of the System segment. In 2025, the company made substantial investments in winding machines and painting facilities for its Component factories. These ongoing investments ensure that the Component segment maintains its asset exclusions over time.

Long-Term Exclusion Trajectories

14.
Exclusion cliff effects: The sharp reduction in carve-out rates after 2028 poses a long-term risk to AQ Group's earnings. Between 2028 and 2033, the payroll exclusion rate drops from 9.0% to 5.0%, representing a steep decline. Unless local profits decrease, this decline will eventually trigger much higher cash tax payments starting in 2029 and beyond.
15.
Staffing level adjustments: Shifting headcount to high-exclusion jurisdictions can be used as a strategic tool to manage top-up tax liabilities. However, AQ Group's historical employee data shows that headcounts in Bulgaria and Lithuania have been slowly declining since 2023. This downward staffing trend will further accelerate the loss of payroll-based tax exclusions over the next decade.
16.
Other side of the story: While the substance-based exclusions are scheduled to decline, AQ Group's decentralized model allows it to reallocate production to high-tax or high-exclusion jurisdictions to minimize tax impacts. Management has a long history of moving production lines between its 17 operating countries to optimize costs and client delivery. Therefore, operational flexibility may successfully prevent major tax increases by ensuring that profits are always aligned with available carve-outs.
