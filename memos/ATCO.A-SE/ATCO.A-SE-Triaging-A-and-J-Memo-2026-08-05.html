Business Description -- A plain-English description of what the company does, how it makes money, and its primary market.
1.
What it does. Atlas Copco AB designs and manufactures compressors, vacuum solutions, industrial power tools, assembly systems and portable power equipment sold to industrial customers across roughly 180 countries (Jefferies Research; ARS)
2.
Segment structure. Four business areas: Compressor Technique (46% of 2025 orders), Vacuum Technique (semiconductor and scientific vacuum), Industrial Technique (assembly tools, automotive-weighted) and Power Technique (portable power and rental)
3.
Organisational scale. The group runs 24 divisions and more than 650 individual company P&Ls (Business Foundations report)
4.
Financial scale. FY25 revenue SEK 168,343m, down 4.8% year on year on industrial destocking, with 56,413 employees and 100 production units worldwide (DNB Carnegie/PROSPFI tearsheet data; ARS)
5.
Margin character. FY25 adjusted EBIT margin 21.7% on a Deutsche Bank Research basis
6.
Margin basis note. The Business Foundations report separately cites a FY25 adjusted EBITDA margin of 26.4%, a different and higher basis, both confirming margins above the sector average (see F.2)
7.
Capital efficiency. A ten-year average ROIC of 25.4% more than doubles the 11.5% European capital-goods sector median
8.
Leverage note. Net debt/EBITDA is low across every cited vintage, though the exact figure varies 0.2x-0.5x by source (see F.2)
9.
Revenue mix. Aftermarket parts, technical support and connected maintenance generate 38% of group revenue at 32-35% gross margins, versus roughly 10% margins on new equipment (Business Foundations report)
10.
Geography. Asia/Oceania generated 36% of 2025 revenue, Europe 28%, North America 26% (Business Foundations report); non-current assets sit more concentrated in Europe, 59.1% of the total, than revenue alone would imply
11.
Listing structure. Dual-class shares trade on Nasdaq Stockholm (ATCOA SS / ATCOB SS); the B-share trades at a persistent 10-13% discount per the tearsheet (45-tearsheet/raw-AS.md cites 10% to 13%; the Risks report cites a tighter, persistent 12.00% to 13.00% range) to the voting A-share for identical cash dividends (DNB Carnegie)
