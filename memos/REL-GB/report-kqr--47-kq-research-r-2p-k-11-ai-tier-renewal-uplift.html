Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

The Key Question Defined

1.
Key question: "At RELX (LexisNexis), Wolters Kluwer (UpToDate Expert AI, CCH Axcess agentic modules, Libra) and Karnov, what price uplift do the artificial intelligence tiers add at renewal across the whole installed base, not only on top-tier packages or new sales? What share of each customer base has already renewed onto these tiers, and how much of the uplift is offset by customers buying fewer seats? By how much do brokers and the companies expect these tiers to lift organic growth in 2026 and 2027, company by company?"

Valuation and Earnings Impact

2.
Critical valuation: The answers to pricing power and seat dynamics will directly determine whether professional information providers can sustain their valuation multiples because their historical premium has relied on low-earnings beta and high cash conversion. Bernstein Research's note of 29 September 2026 indicates that Wolters Kluwer trades near the bottom of its media coverage multiples due to ongoing competitive concerns surrounding UpToDate. This means that clarifying renewal uplifts is essential to alleviating the AI penalty and restoring historical P/E multiples of 25x or higher.
3.
Accelerating earnings: Sustaining high-single-digit organic EPS growth requires these companies to successfully upsell advanced AI modules to offset print declines and standard software price pressure. Morgan Stanley's note of 17 April 2024 argues that RELX and Wolters Kluwer combine 6% underlying revenue growth with margin expansion to deliver double-digit EPS CAGRs. This means that if AI tiers fail to gain traction at renewal, organic growth will decelerate and outer-year EPS forecasts must be trimmed.

Shifting Structural Models

4.
Licensing shift: A closely related structural question is the transition of billing models from traditional headcount-based seat licenses to enterprise or outcome-based contracts because automation naturally reduces user counts. Bernstein Research's note of 1 June 2026 argues that while legal user-population declines are likely, vendors can successfully shift billing to outcomes or volume processed given the massive scale of corporate productivity gains. This structural change protects the revenue pool unless customers resist nonseat pricing.
5.
Defending margins: Incremental margin gains from AI depend on whether high product development spend is offset by internal operational efficiencies. Deutsche Bank Research's note of 4 September 2026 points out that Wolters Kluwer is raising its product development spend to 12% to 13% of revenues in 2026 compared to 11% in 2025 to accelerate its AI transformation. This means that operating margins will face pressure unless internal AI coding productivity gains offset the increased investment.

Contrary and Counter Views

6.
Contrary evidence: Morgan Stanley's note of 5 August 2026 suggests that the legal AI opportunity may be more gradual because law firms face high change-management burdens and litigator demand remains limited, making them unlikely to adopt expensive new platforms in the near term. This means that near-term organic growth benefits could be much lower than the double-digit rates bulls expect.
7.
Other side of the story: Investors may be overestimating the threat of seat contraction because there are massive untapped penetration opportunities in the mid-market and client bases. DNB Carnegie's note of 14 January 2026 indicates that while seat compression is a valid concern, Karnov still has significant room to expand licenses because sharing among users remains common. This suggests that stable seat counts can persist alongside pricing uplifts, sustaining organic growth.

Pricing Models and Revenue Protections

1.
Structured pricing models: Professional information providers are adopting multi-layered contract structures to protect their recurring revenue streams from localized user attrition. Wolters Kluwer relies on a hybrid user-tiered approach that groups user bands such as fifty to one hundred and fifty users to cushion moderate headcount swings. This model gives the company ample time to adjust its commercial terms in the event of larger operational shifts. This pricing architecture ensures that near-term EPS forecasts remain insulated from sudden firm-wide seat contractions.
2.
Shifting licensing frameworks: Legal and tax software developers are exploring alternative pricing models to detach their commercial growth from pure user headcounts. Bernstein Research indicates that Wolters Kluwer utilizes outcome-based pricing linked to the number of tax returns processed to bypass seat-based limitations. This pricing mechanism aligns software costs directly with corporate output volume rather than individual office work desks. Consequently, group-level organic revenue is positioned to grow even if automation tools decrease the necessary staff headcount.
3.
Vulnerable seat models: Investors are closely examining how much corporate revenue is tied directly to legacy seat-based pricing structures as AI automation tools proliferate. Bernstein Research raised specific queries regarding the share of RELX revenues generated through a per-seat pricing model. The broker also questioned whether specific professional segments would experience user attrition due to more efficient AI productivity. Clarifying this exposure is critical because high seat-dependence would make outer-year EPS estimates highly vulnerable to seat-count declines.

Client Seat Pruning Strategies

4.
Year-end budget discipline: Corporate clients are actively auditing their software portfolios to eliminate underutilized user licenses and manage operational expenditures. An artificial intelligence executive at a professional services firm stated that year-end IT software license pruning acts as a notable offset to conversational AI pilot growth. This discipline means that corporate software budgets are highly scrutinized even as firms pilot new agentic features. This cost control suggests that information providers must continuously demonstrate high ROI to protect their recurring seat volumes during renewals.
5.
Unfavourable consumption pricing: Transitioning to consumption-based metrics can inadvertently trigger client pushback and lead to defensive seat reductions. A technology services consultant observed that Salesforce's consumption-based flex credits are causing significant friction for corporate clients. This pricing complexity is leading some clients to reduce license counts and seek alternative predictable pricing models. This client reaction suggests that professional publishers like Wolters Kluwer must maintain predictable subscription models to avoid driving users to drop seats.
6.
Demanding baseline proof: Chief financial officers are requiring robust empirical evidence of productivity gains before approving high-cost AI software renewals. A technology services director noted that CFOs demand a baseline proof that software improvements outweigh licensing and integration costs. If AI saves time but fails to reduce absolute expenses or expand capacity, establishing financial ROI becomes exceptionally difficult. Unless software vendors can justify this value, enterprise renewals are likely to face severe delays or seat reductions.
