Plausible problems to monitor

Outliers worth noting

Jungheinrich's reported ROCE collapsed to 8.3% unadjusted in FY25, down sharply from 17.3% in the prior year, yet adjusted ROCE reached 15.03% in FY25 when excluding M&A effects, representing an extreme divergence between headline and underlying capital returns that places the reported figure in the bottom 5% of European capital goods peers [Cross-ref: Section 4]. This anomaly reflects massive one-time impairments rather than structural operating deterioration, yet the headline figure remains an unexplained oddity relative to sector norms where such divergences rarely exceed 300-400 basis points.

Jungheinrich recorded EUR 220 million in cumulative negative one-offs in FY25, comprising EUR 109 million from the Russian subsidiary disposal, EUR 93 million in transformation expenses, and EUR 18 million from a discontinued warehouse automation project write-down, representing an exceptional charge intensity of 4.0% of revenue that significantly exceeds the European industrial peer median of 0.5-1.0% [Cross-ref: Section 5].

This magnitude of non-recurring items represents a statistical outlier in the materials handling sector, where even heavily restructuring peers like KION Group typically report annual exceptional charges below 2% of revenue. Free cash flow deteriorated to EUR 0 million in Q1 2026, down from EUR 16 million in Q1 2025, as capital expenditures and working capital expansion completely consumed operating cash flow, revealing a stark divergence from headline EBIT progress and positioning Jungheinrich's cash conversion in the bottom 10% of European industrial peer rankings for the quarter [Cross-ref: Section 6].

This unexplained oddity contrasts sharply with the group's historical cash generation patterns and the average >80% cash conversion target articulated in Strategy 2030+. Heike Wulff amicably resigned as CFO in May 2026, only four months after taking full responsibility, forcing CEO Brzoska into an interim role after Wulff had originally been appointed as Designated CFO in May 2024, representing an exceptionally short executive tenure of approximately 11 months that ranks in the bottom 5% of European DAX/MDAX CFO tenures [Cross-ref: Section 1].

This rapid departure, coupled with simultaneous divisional leadership exits, creates an unexplained governance oddity that significantly exceeds sector base rates for Board-level attrition during strategic transformation periods.
