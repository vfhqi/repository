Hellenic Telecom (HTO-GR): what the Q3 2026 results, and the response to them, do to the case

These results, with four working days of response behind them, leave the case where it was and tilt it slightly in its favour: the quarter landed on the case's own numbers, the street left its 2027 figures where the case already has them, the retail-price threat the results-morning reading raised did not survive the later evidence, and the shares have recovered to above the price the case was written at; what is new is a pair of questions about the cash leg, fibre activation and sports content, that the case does not carry.

What changed

Hellenic Telecom reported Q3 2026 on 30 October 2026. Revenue was 882.7 EURm, +1.0% year-on-year against 874.0 EURm, a 1.4% beat of the Bloomberg consensus of 870.6 EURm, in line with Morgan Stanley's 883.5 EURm and 1.5% below NBG Securities' 896 EURm; excluding the international transit phase-out, about 160 EURm of which rolls off during 2026, underlying growth was 5%. Adjusted EBITDA after leases was 371.4 EURm, +3.1% against 360.1 EURm, 0.7% above the Bloomberg consensus of 368.7 EURm and above NBG's 370.2 EURm; the margin rose 90 basis points to 42.1% on cost discipline and personnel savings, with Greek electricity supply indices up 13.2% year-on-year in August 2026. The case's model had 371 EURm (+3.0%) for the quarter, and the case said H2 needed about +3.1%: both were met. Adjusted profit attributable to parent owners rose 7.4% to 182.5 EURm; the reported line fell 39.7% on the prior year's 129.8 EURm tax effect. Reported free cash flow after leases was 137.4 EURm, +27.7% from 107.6 EURm, on lower income-tax payments and working capital; adjusted capex was 176.4 EURm, +17.8% from 149.7 EURm, on the timing of TV content payments. Net debt was 683.0 EURm at end-September 2026, 0.5x adjusted EBITDA after leases; a 350 EURm bond was issued in September 2026 to partially refinance a maturing 500 EURm bond.

Guidance was reiterated, not tightened and not raised: FY2026 adjusted EBITDA after leases growth of about 3%, capex of about 600 EURm against 612 EURm in 2025, reported free cash flow of about 750 EURm against 543 EURm underlying in 2025, underlying free cash flow of 570 to 580 EURm, and a 2026 shareholder payout of 532 EURm under a 70% to 100% policy. Nine months of reported free cash flow on the review's quarterly table (60, 150 and 137.4 EURm) leave about 400 EURm for Q4 against 168 EURm in Q4 2025; Morgan Stanley reads the 750 EURm as implying exceptionally strong fourth-quarter cash flows, with the 120 EURm Romanian cash tax break inside it. Nothing was said that commits the company to the inflation-linked tariff increase; NBG poses it as an open question and dates it to February 2027.

On the operating lines: mobile service revenue +2.0%, 49,000 postpaid net additions to a base of 3.20 million (+7.5%), prepaid still about 55% of mobile customers, data use +18% to 24 GB, unlimited plans 37% of the consumer base. Fixed retail service revenue +2.9%, which Morgan Stanley reads as an acceleration from -0.6% in Q2 2026; pay-TV revenue +8.9% on 802,443 subscribers (+5.9%, +9,231 in the quarter); FTTH 739,081 retail lines, +52,250 in the quarter, 31% of the broadband base from 21% a year earlier and 56% of eligible customers in covered areas; the broadband base 2.37 million, -0.4%, which management attributes to intense competition and which the transcript reads as fibre additions being migrations of existing copper customers rather than new users; fixed wireless 134,355 lines, +15,461, +116%. Homes passed are 3.0 million under a new definition that counts every home passed irrespective of an active connection, against 2.3 million on the old active-line basis; utilisation is 44% on the old basis and 32% on the new; the year-end target is more than 3.1 million (it was about 2.4 million in the Q2 updates on the old basis) and 4.6 million by 2030, 70% of Greek lines. System Solutions was 105.9 EURm, +9.0%, with the Recovery and Resilience Facility cycle concluded and management pivoting to private-sector projects. Wholesale revenue was -24.1%, not split between transit and national wholesale. PPC's FiberGrid reached 2.3 million homes passed with 1.5 million ready for service in October 2026; the last count of PPC's retail customers in the material is at most 20,000 at end-March 2026. The Vodafone-PPC venture is at heads of terms for more than 1.6 million homes. Greek inflation was 5.6% in October 2026 from 5.1% in September.

The sell-side response. Morgan Stanley kept Overweight and its 23.80 EUR target (raised from 21.50 EUR in September 2026), raised FY2026 EPS to 1.53 EUR from 1.51 EUR (+1.3%), trimmed FY2027 to 1.67 EUR from 1.68 EUR (-0.6%), carries 1.83 EUR for FY2028, and calls the 3.1% growth a near-term cyclical high point ahead of tougher Q4 comparisons. NBG reiterated Outperform, reads the margin gain as structural (copper migrating to fibre and fixed wireless), flags the UEFA rights tender closing in November 2026 with OTE's Magenta TV holding exclusive rights, and reads the company as skipping standalone bids in favour of partnerships. The review records the brokers as divided on whether 3.1% is structural or a peak. Deutsche Bank published nothing on the company after the results in the material; its last note on OTE is April 2026. The dashboard at 5 November: consensus EPS 1.70 EUR, revisions 0% over one month, +1% over three, -1% over six; consensus target 20.88 EUR, flat over one and three months; buy share 54%, down 8 points over three months; revision-momentum score -2; sell-side pin D. The price: 18.57 EUR at the close before the release, 18.85 EUR on 2 November, 19.14 EUR on 4 November, +3.1% since the results and above the case's 19.03 EUR of 27 October; -1.3% over three months, +25.3% over twelve; 5.9% above the 200-day average of 18.07 EUR, above the 150-day at 18.79 EUR, below the 50-day at 19.50 EUR; technical pin B; P/E 10.7x on 24-month-forward consensus EPS of 1.786 EUR, at the 34th percentile of ten years. A beat the shares rewarded modestly, after a day's hesitation.

The challenge

The results-morning piece put up three readings. One survives, changed; two are knocked down by the evidence now in hand and we drop them. The later review opens two readings the case does not hold.

First, kept: the core is not flat, and cost is doing more than the case's 8 EURm a year. The case's spine is that FY2026's +3% rests on the December 2025 price step and the public ICT boom, and that without them core EBITDA falls 0.6% to 0.9% a year. This review strengthens the company side of the reading and weakens the sell-side side. On the company side, the ICT support the case dated to 2027 fell away in Q3 (System Solutions +9.0% after the case's +64% in H1), energy went the wrong way, and adjusted EBITDA after leases still grew 3.1% with the margin at 42.1% against NBG's full-year margin target of 41.3%; NBG reads that as a structural mix shift. On the sell-side side, the one house that re-ran its model after the quarter took FY2027 down by a cent, not up, and reads the quarter as a cyclical high with tougher Q4 comparisons; no house carries extra cost-out into 2027. In its strongest form the reading says net cost-out is running at 20 to 25 EURm a year rather than the case's 8, which on the case's own FY2027 bridge (price +27, ICT -12, wholesale -12, energy and content -8, volume +5, other +6, net cost +8, totalling +14 EURm or +1.0%) lifts FY2027 to the case's "+2% to +3%" branch, the one the case gives 30%, without any help from the step; the February cut the case's sequence waits for then does not come. For it to be right: Q4 adjusted EBITDA after leases has to grow about 3% on the 351 EURm Q4 2025 base with ICT flat to down; the voluntary-leave charges excluded from adjusted EBITDA after leases, which the case counts at 28.3 EURm in H1 alone, have to be no larger than usual, and this review does not carry the Q3 figure; and the February guide has to read "+2% to +3%". The case's own answer, that the company lands guidance with 10 to 15 EURm of cost timing, fits two quarters as well as it fits one. Plausible, not probable, and the quarter that would decide it is the next one.

Second, dropped: PPC moved from lines to price. The results-morning reading rested on a -0.6% retail fixed service line attributed to PPC's promotions. The later review places that -0.6% in Q2 2026 and reads Q3 at +2.9%, the strongest fixed retail quarter in the material, with pay-TV revenue +8.9% and 52,250 fibre additions achieved, in Morgan Stanley's words, despite PPC. A broadband base at -0.4% is what the case called flat. The reading is knocked down on this quarter. What survives of PPC belongs to the case already: a network of 2.3 million homes passed and 1.5 million ready for service, allied to a tenant, is the wholesale attack the case dates to 2027; NBG's observation that PPC is forcing incumbents to offer volume discounts is a wholesale point, not a retail one.

Third, dropped: the estimate reset is happening now, quietly. Four working days on, one house has moved FY2027 by one cent; the consensus EPS is 1.70 EUR, where the case has it; revisions are 0%, +1% and -1%; the target is 20.88 EUR against the case's 20.92 EUR; and the shares are 3.1% above the pre-results close, back above the case's own price, with the technical pin at B. No reset is forming before February. The case's sequence, a December positive followed by a February negative, stands as the case wrote it, with one change of date noted below.

New second: the fibre asset is less monetised than the case assumes, and the capex leg of the cash return is exposed. The review's own hidden-weakness reading is that the new homes-passed definition shows 3.0 million homes but only 2.3 million carry an active line, that utilisation on the comparable European basis is 32% rather than 44%, and that bullish broker models carry higher monetisation risk than they show. The case's cash leg rests on capex falling from about 600 EURm to about 560 EURm by FY2030, worth 8% to 9% of free cash flow, and on free cash flow per share growing about 8% a year from 2027; it also says returns on new fibre capital at about 500 EUR a line stay low until utilisation passes 50% to 60%. In its strongest form the reading says the footprint race with PPC is now being run on reported numbers; that about 700,000 passed homes still need activation capital; that 1.6 million more homes to 2030 sit on top of that; that the pending 10% to 13% regulated cut in FTTH wholesale tariffs lands on a network less filled than the case thinks; and that sports content now runs through capex (the +17.8% in Q3 was content timing), so the capex decline the case banks on arrives later and smaller, and the cash leg compounds at less than 8%. For it to be right: the FY2027 capex guide has to come in above 600 EURm before spectrum; the cost of activating a passed home has to be material, which the company does not disclose; and fibre net additions have to slip toward the 40,000 a quarter that Morgan Stanley names as its own overbuild signal despite the larger footprint. Against it: capex guidance for 2026 was held at about 600 EURm, below 2025; active-line utilisation of 44% is above the 42% the case worked on; 2.3 million active-line homes against the old-basis 2.4 million year-end target is on track; and the definition change aligns with European practice rather than hiding anything new. Plausible, slow, and it presses on the one leg of the return the case calls secure.

New third: content cost is a sixth driver, and the bundle moat's TV pillar is re-priced this month. The case names five judgements that move the value and treats content as a line inside "energy and content -8 EURm" in FY2027; its retail moat is bundling (70% of the broadband base bundled; PPC has no mobile and no TV). Pay-TV is the line doing the work in fixed retail: revenue +8.9%, 802,443 subscribers past the 800,000 mark, helped by the removal of the pay-TV tax and anti-piracy law. The UEFA rights tender closes in November 2026, OTE's Magenta TV holds the exclusive rights today, NBG says intensifying competition could raise content costs, and the review reads the company as skipping a standalone bid and pursuing partnerships. In its strongest form the reading says the outcome is either dearer, with content inflation landing in capex and pressing the cash leg again, or thinner, with shared or lost exclusivity weakening the bundle in the same half-year that the venture gives PPC's network a retail tenant. For it to be right: UEFA rights have to be a large share of content cost, which is undisclosed; the partnership route has to mean shared rather than retained exclusivity; or a rival has to win outright. Against it: reciprocal sports-content sharing is already how the Greek market works, which the case itself records, so a partnership outcome is the market's normal state, and with legal pay-TV penetration below 35% the growth is coming from legalisation rather than from exclusivity. Plausible, dated, and the only reading in this piece that is settled within weeks.

The obvious bull reading still fails. A beat, a 90 basis point margin gain and 27.7% free cash flow growth have produced a two-cent raise to FY2026, a one-cent trim to FY2027, an unchanged target and unchanged guidance. The case already holds that even its bull case produces a held consensus rather than upgrades, and four working days of response have confirmed it.

One change of date that is not a reading. The case dates the price step to December 2026, visible in Q1 2027 revenue, and relies on it as the one un-guided positive that precedes the February guide. NBG now frames the inflation-linked increase as a February 2027 question. If the step is applied in February rather than December, the positive and the negative the case sequences arrive at the same event, the step cannot be observed before the guide, and about a month and a half of FY2027 benefit, around 3 EURm on the case's 27 EURm, falls out. The size argument, if anything, strengthens with October inflation at 5.6%, and so does the political exposure the case names.

What would settle it

The results-morning piece left four questions to this review. Post-results estimates and targets: answered, and the answer is that one house moved by one cent, the consensus EPS is 1.70 EUR and the target 20.88 EUR. Deutsche Bank's stance: not answered; nothing from that house on the company appears after the results. The consensus target: effectively unmoved. The Q3 voluntary-leave charge excluded from adjusted EBITDA after leases: not in this review either; the company's interim tables carry it and the material we have does not.

No later review follows this one. What settles each reading still open:

  • The cost-out reading is settled by Q4 2026 adjusted EBITDA after leases against the 351 EURm base and by the FY2027 guide, both at the FY2026 results in February 2027, together with the full-year voluntary-leave charge. If the guide reads "+2% to +3%" with ICT flat to down, the case's sequence changes; if "+1% to +2%", the case stands.
  • The fibre and capex reading is settled by the FY2027 capex guide (February 2027), by Q4 fibre net additions against the review's 40,000-a-quarter marker, and by the gap between homes passed and active-line coverage, which the company now reports on both bases each quarter. The cost of activating a passed home is a disclosure the company has not committed to, so the size of the tail is likely to stay unquantified.
  • The content reading is settled first: the UEFA tender closes in November 2026, and the outcome, whether OTE retains, shares or loses the rights and on what partnership terms, is likely public within weeks; the cost lands in Q4 capex and the FY2027 capex guide.
  • The step is the open question that matters most for 2027, and the one nothing before the company's own announcement answers: whether it is applied, in December or February, at what rate, and whether on fixed as well as mobile. The company has not committed to it. It becomes visible in Q1 2027 mobile service revenue, reported in May 2027, unless announced earlier.
  • The Q4 cash ask of about 400 EURm, which sets the base for the 2027 distribution under the 70% to 100% policy, is settled in February 2027.
  • The venture is settled by the Hellenic Competition Commission's clearance and any remedy, targeted for H1 2027; PPC's own retail line count is PPC's disclosure and not on OTE's calendar.
