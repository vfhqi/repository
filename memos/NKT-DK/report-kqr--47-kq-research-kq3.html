Section 2 – Hedging Policy and SEK-Denominated Cost Structure 18 findings

Section summary
  1. NKT's centralized treasury policy strictly prohibits currency speculation while managing a highly concentrated Swedish crown cost base at Karlskrona
  2. Structural SEK operational expenditures expose group margins to currency translation and transaction mismatches prior to project-level hedging execution

Centralized treasury and hedging mandate

1.
Centralized control: NKT manages all financial risk under a centralized treasury framework approved by the Board of Directors
2.
Strict prohibition: Internal guidelines explicitly forbid speculative currency trading or derivative transactions outside of defined commercial exposures
3.
Monitored tolerance: Treasury policy hedges all reported risks but accepts monitored open positions within strict internal risk thresholds
4.
Standardized instruments: Group Treasury utilizes forward exchange contracts, currency swaps, and currency options to manage foreign exchange risk
5.
Rigid credit: Derivative counterparties must maintain a long-term credit rating of at least A- from Standard and Poor's, Moody's, or Fitch

Karlskrona operational expenditures and SEK cost base

6.
Highly concentrated: NKT focuses its high-voltage subsea manufacturing at Karlskrona, which secures local labor and logistical advantages
7.
Escalating local: Swedish operations generate major SEK outflows across salaries, energy procurement, local subcontractors, and facility overhead
8.
Expanding personnel: Group staff costs increased to EUR 482 million in FY2025 from EUR 393 million in FY2024 due to capacity ramp-up hiring
9.
Transitory dilution: Pre-hiring and ramp-up payroll diluted group EBITDA margins by approximately 100 to 150 basis points in late FY2025
10.
Unprotected exposure: Since metals are hedged back-to-back, unhedged Swedish manufacturing costs represent NKT's largest ongoing transactional exposure because they escape standard project derivative locks

Transaction hedging and project currency thresholds

11.
Strict matching: Currency transaction risks on high-voltage projects are hedged back-to-back at the precise time of contract award
12.
Adaptive adjustment: Group Treasury continuously modifies outstanding derivative positions as project delivery milestones and installation timelines shift
13.
Structural mismatch: Karlskrona's local operations utilize SEK functional currency while executing contracts priced primarily in EUR
14.
Consolidation distortion: Intercompany currency hedges protect the Swedish entity but are completely eliminated upon consolidation at the EUR group level

Non-consensus reads on SEK exposure

15.
Heightened vulnerability: Jefferies Research warns that concentrating NKT's entire high-voltage subsea manufacturing on a single Swedish site exaggerates currency concentration risk
16.
Favourable dynamics: DNB Carnegie argues that pricing power allows NKT to improve margins faster than underlying Swedish inflation, offsetting nominal SEK-denominated labor increases
17.
Volatile bidding: Historical patterns suggest that a volatile SEK could distort bidding competitiveness, as local competitors face different cost currency configurations
18.
Other side of the story: Bears argue that since local SEK labor and energy costs are unhedged prior to award, sudden currency spikes or Swedish wage inflation before project sign-off can permanently erode estimated contract margins
