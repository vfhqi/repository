Section 2 – Ownership Stake Changes and Minority Buyouts

Section summary
  1. Deutsche Telekom leverages creeping stake mechanisms and opportunistic buyouts of minority positions to systematically concentrate control over its listed European and American subsidiaries.

Slovak Telekom Consolidation Case

1.
Successful buyouts: Deutsche Telekom executed a complete takeover of its Slovak subsidiary, representing one of 5 major subsidiaries analyzed across 12 relevant sources and 3 historical precedents. This strategic acquisition allowed the parent to eliminate dividend leakage to minority shareholders entirely. The transaction was successfully completed on June 18, 2015.
2.
Attractive valuations: The financial terms of the Slovak transaction demonstrated the disciplined capital allocation strategy of the parent. Deutsche Telekom management stated in an August 2015 earnings call that the purchase price represented a multiple of just under four times EBITDA. This low multiple contrasted with more expensive transactions in Western Europe where regulatory hurdles are higher. The transaction was funded through cash reserves and helped simplify the subsidiary corporate governance structure effectively.
3.
Pragmatic consolidation: The parent company approaches minority buyouts primarily based on strict economic attractiveness rather than a blanket consolidation mandate. Deutsche Telekom management stated during a February 2015 presentation that they bought out Czech minorities because it presented a clear business case. The executive team reiterated that there is no need for structural action across the Pan-European network unless the economics are compelling. This approach ensures that cash is preserved for high-priority spectrum investments.
4.
Pragmatic governance: Structural choices at Slovak Telekom illustrate how full ownership enables corporate restructuring. Deutsche Telekom noted in its August 2015 interim report that acquiring the Slovak minorities allowed them to simplify the governance structure. The transaction was designed to reduce dividend payments to non-controlling interests significantly over the long term. This strategy directly prevents capital leakage back to domestic governments.

These strategic moves allowed the parent to gradually build its holding to forty percent of OTE.

Morgan Stanley reported in October 2025 that the parent stake in OTE increased to 50% in 2022 due to nonparticipation in OTE's buyback program. This non-participation allowed the parent to attain absolute majority control of OTE without capital outlay. The parent company now maintains a stable majority stake of approximately 55% in the Greek operator.
14.
Substantial repurchases: OTE has dedicated significant financial resources to executing these equity buyback programs. Deutsche Telekom reported in its November 2026 interim report that OTE share buy-backs accounted for EUR 126 million in transactions with owners during the first nine months of 2026. This followed an expenditure of EUR 151 million on share buy-backs during the 2025 financial year. Section 3 – Dividend and Buyback Policy Alignment

Section summary

  2. Confidence in this alignment is high, backed by T-Mobile US and OTE filings detailing direct capital extraction and passive parent ownership accretion.

Financial analysts argue that subsidiary payout ratios are highly sensitive to the parent's mid-term leverage targets. This operational alignment helps ensure that leverage reduction targets remain intact across the entire European segment.

Dutch Asset Monetization

Diluted cash flows: Standalone financial models indicate that the divestment of T-Mobile Netherlands had immediate dilutive effects on group cash generation. This dilution was accepted by management to prioritize long-term equity growth in the United States.

These corporate actions show how the parent prioritizes structural optimization before executing country exits.

Section 8 – If the Answer Differs from the Common View

The prevailing view. The consensus reading of the Key Question is that Deutsche Telekom (DT) is a disciplined, mostly non-extractive controlling shareholder. On this view, minority returns at OTE are protected by low standalone leverage and strong cash conversion. Several data points support it.

The parent's adjusted leverage of 2.68x under IFRS16 sits inside its 2.25x to 2.75x comfort zone. That suggests no immediate balance sheet pressure to divest subsidiary holdings. Morgan Stanley forecasts OTE's EPS rising from €1.53 in 2026 to €1.67 in 2027 and €1.83 in 2028. OTE's framework targets a 70% to 100% payout of free cash flow, with at least 50% paid as cash dividend.

The alternative outcome is that DT behaves as a cash-seeking controller whose decisions on payouts, intra-group charges or its own stake override minority interests. The evidence does not show that outcome today. It does show how it could arise.

Earnings and distributions if the answer differs. The most plausible divergence is a parent-driven change in OTE's capital-return range. Morgan Stanley itself notes that OTE's conservative balance sheet might tempt the parent to adjust capital return ranges to satisfy its own debt commitments. The pressure points are visible.

Group net debt rose by EUR 1.7 billion to EUR 134.2 billion in the first nine months of 2026, driven by high spectrum costs and share buy-backs. The parent's buyback reached only EUR 1.8 billion by September 2026 against an aspirational EUR 5 billion, which Morgan Stanley reads as tight financial flexibility ahead of spectrum auctions. The CFO has also said the board wants flexibility to expand buybacks beyond EUR 2 billion or invest directly in T-Mobile US.

Dividends from subsidiaries fund these competing uses. A more extractive outcome would not necessarily cut OTE's earnings. It would more likely shift the mix. A payout drifting toward the top of the range, or a higher dividend share relative to buybacks, would change how EPS accretion and cash returns reach minorities.

OTE's buybacks systematically reduce the public float while raising the parent's relative voting power, so buybacks tend to favour the controller more than cash dividends do. A policy shift toward dividends would therefore be a more neutral outcome for minorities than the headline payout suggests.
