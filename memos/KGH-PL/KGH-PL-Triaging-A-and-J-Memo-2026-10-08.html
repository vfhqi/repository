[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = D

Thesis. KGHM is a copper price-leverage case that has already happened: the 2025 to 2026 metal surge doubled its earnings and its share price, and at PLN 334.6 the shares pay for the peak while the currency, the costs, silver and the owner's capital plan all point the earnings the other way. It is a late-stage 'Demand Inflection' whose inflection has been paid for, and our 'Modal return' is negative at eighteen months and at three years.

  • The external force is real, sector-wide and structural, and it has already done its work.
    • Copper reached a record USD 14,527 per tonne on 2026-01-29 and was USD 14,360 per tonne in mid-August 2026, on a forecast 2026 deficit of 350,000 tonnes and inventories of 15 days of consumption
    • Grids, electric vehicles and data centres give the demand more than two years of visibility
    • The force reached KGHM's accounts in full: Q2 2026 revenue rose 49 percent year on year to PLN 12,839 million, and consensus FY2026 EPS of PLN 39.27 compares with PLN 18.36 in FY2025 (brokers' basis, 2026-10-02)
    • It is no longer new.
      • FY2026 consensus EPS rose 131.8 percent in the twelve months to 2026-10-02
      • A force the sell-side has modelled is not a force with further to travel
  • KGHM captures the force through price alone, which is why the leverage is symmetric.
    • Payable copper is guided at over 730,000 tonnes a year on average for 2026 to 2030, the level of 2025's 710 kt plus recycling
    • The domestic C1 cost of USD 2.94 per pound in Q2 2026 sits in the upper half of the curve
    • A 10 percent copper move is 31 percent of EBITDA and 66 percent of EPS.
      • It works both ways, and from a record it is more likely to work downwards
  • The internal change since January 2026 is large and points the wrong way for a minority shareholder.
    • The owner dismissed the operator-chief executive on 2026-01-30 after about ten months and installed a state-sector restructuring executive with no mining record
    • Strategy 2055+ (approved 2026-07-03) commits PLN 32 billion of capital through 2030, a polyhalite fertiliser project of up to PLN 10 billion and a copper acquisition search of USD 2 to 5 billion
    • The sell-side read the strategy as the end of capex containment and a dilution of the copper story: a downgrade, a target multiple cut by one standard deviation to 5.9x, and Underweights rising from 10 percent in late 2025 to 36 percent
  • 'Financial Outputs' have peaked and are turning down on consensus and on our path.
    • Consensus has EPS falling 7.7 percent in FY2027 and 0.9 percent in FY2028
    • Our modal path is about PLN 40 in FY2026, PLN 34 in FY2027, PLN 32 in FY2028 and PLN 31 in FY2029, about -8 percent a year compounded
    • Silver has halved from USD 115 to USD 58 per troy ounce
      • The zloty strengthened 6 percent to an average USD/PLN of 3.62 in H1 2026
      • Standalone labour costs rose 21 percent in H1 2026
      • The extraction tax rises with metal prices and resets higher in 2029
    • The quality of the peak earnings is low: PLN 2,200 million of non-cash inventory revaluation in Q1 2026, a PLN 502 million loan-impairment reversal in Q2, and PLN 3,776 million of working capital absorbed in the half, so H1 operating cash flow was PLN 2,556 million against adjusted EBITDA of PLN 9,198 million
  • The change has travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain, including the share price.
    • It is in the operating metrics, the financial statements and the sell-side's estimates, and the shares rose 95.8 percent in twelve months to PLN 334.6 (2026-10-07), peaking at PLN 394.70
    • Where the chain holds: from the copper price to EBITDA, through published sensitivities.
      • Where it breaks: between EBITDA and cash (free cash flow negative in each of FY2021 to FY2025) and between the owner's objectives and the shareholder's
  • What it costs: 9.6x consensus 24-month-forward EPS of PLN 34.86, above the stock's own centre.
    • That is the 68th percentile of five years and the 73rd of two years, above the five-year median of 8.65x and the ten-year median of 8.10x
    • The price is cheap only against copper peers (Antofagasta 22.0x, Lundin 19.5x), a discount that is a decade old and reflects state control, the cost curve and zero insider equity
  • The return does not clear the bar at either horizon.
    • On our modal path (copper flat, silver near USD 58, USD/PLN near 3.6, domestic costs inflating 6 to 8 percent a year, the multiple reverting to its ten-year median of 8.1x) the value including cash returns is PLN 252.9 at 2028-04-07, -17.0 percent a year, and PLN 275.1 at 2029-10-07, -6.3 percent a year
    • A plausibly worse drop-through from EBITDA to EPS (net income at 44 percent of EBITDA rather than about 48 percent) takes the eighteen-month figure to -21.7 percent a year
      • The rating is F either way
    • The bull branch, a copper rise of 30 percent or more within eighteen months and a multiple at the ten-year 75th percentile of 10.2x, has odds of about 15 percent and does not lift the probability-weighted return out of the F band
  • The pattern it fits is 'Demand Inflection', loosely, a D.
    • The mandatory demand condition passes at B and fixed-cost leverage is an A
    • Dominant position, one-to-many scalability and oligopoly fail.
    • The inflection the pattern wants ahead of the shares is behind them

  • The case therefore is a bet that copper rises another third from a record while a state owner spends the proceeds wisely; we decline the bet.

    • The force was real, the leverage was real, and both are paid for
    • What remains is the symmetric half of the leverage and an owner whose next capital decision is not ours to forecast

  • Rating = D, because the change is material and spent: 'Change Forces' is C (one strong external force, the internal forces pointing the wrong way), 'Financial Outputs' is D (a peak, then a decline), and the 'Modal return' is F

    • The Building Block measures materiality, and the material change has produced its outputs and been paid for in the price.
    • Not F, because the force is real and a further copper rise would re-open the case
    • Not C, because nothing still in motion is favourable to the earnings from here.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • In its strongest form: KGHM is the cheapest copper producer in the world on EV/EBITDA, at 4.1x spot 2026 and 4.6x 2027 against Western peers at 10 to 11x
      • Its balance sheet is at 0.4x net debt to EBITDA
      • Its one world-class asset, Sierra Gorda, is first-quartile at a C1 cost of USD 0.72 per pound and sanctioned to grow 30 percent
      • Copper inventories are the lowest in history and Deutsche Bank sees USD 22,050 per tonne by Q2 2027
      • The Polish state has just cut the extraction tax
      • The sell-side has already taken a 13 percent downgrade to 2027 EBITDA, the Underweight share is 36 percent, and consensus revenue of PLN 47,759 million for FY2027 assumes no further price rise
      • The technical picture is a proven Stage 2 at the 86th percentile of relative strength
      • And every governance objection we make has been true for a decade during which the shares have also doubled.
    • If copper does what the bulls say, EPS could exceed PLN 50 (UBS has PLN 58.54 for FY2027) and the shares could reach PLN 450 to 467 (UBS's target; Morgan Stanley's bull case) on no re-rating at all.
    • We concede the balance sheet, the tightness of the copper market, the quality of Sierra Gorda, and that the EV/EBITDA discount is genuine and large.
    • We hold our view because the framework's question is about the modal path and not the bull path: on the modal path the earnings fall and the multiple sits above its own centre
      • The EV/EBITDA discount is a cross-sectional fact that has not closed in ten years and whose closing needs the owner to change, not the copper price
      • The sell-side's own re-rating arithmetic (Morgan Stanley's 5.9x target EV/EBITDA) produces a target of PLN 350, 5 percent above the price
      • And the bull path, at our 15 percent odds, does not lift the probability-weighted return (PLN 291.7 at eighteen months, -8.7 percent a year) above an F.
    • The competing hypothesis does not need a sum-of-the-parts argument
      • It needs copper, and a case that is compelling only if copper rises another 50 percent is not a modal case.
Change Forces: are the input change forces sufficiently strong? (E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because the external force on copper is strong, sector-wide and favourable but mature rather than new, and the internal forces under way since January 2026 are strong in the wrong direction for a minority shareholder: capital intensity up, governance weaker, the pure-play copper story diluted.
  • Weighting: the external forces are B and the internal forces D.
    • The Element is not an average: the copper force gives the case its leverage and we weight it, but a force the sell-side has already modelled (FY2026 consensus EPS up 131.8 percent in the twelve months to 2026-10-02) is not a force with further to travel through the chain.
  • Direction of what is still moving: the two external forces that are intensifying, zloty strength and domestic cost inflation, are headwinds
    • The internal forces that are moving are capital commitments, not operating improvements.
  • Not B, because a B would need the forces to be new or intensifying in the case's favour, and the ones that are intensifying work against it.
External forces: are there sufficiently strong external forces acting on the company, new or intensifying, and in which direction? (RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because the demand force meets the 'Demand Inflection' test in full (sector-level; structural on grids, electric vehicles and data centres; evidenced by record prices, a forecast 2026 deficit of 350,000 tonnes and inventories of 15 days of consumption) and it is a tailwind
    • It is marked below A because it is no longer new, because silver has already reversed, and because two other external forces, currency and domestic labour, are headwinds that intensified in H1 2026.
  • Copper: a record USD 14,527 per tonne on 2026-01-29 and USD 14,360 per tonne in mid-August 2026
    • Deutsche Bank forecasts USD 22,050 per tonne by Q2 2027.
    • A strong tailwind.
  • Silver: USD 115 per troy ounce in early 2026 to USD 58 per troy ounce by August 2026, with the correlation to copper fallen to zero and a forecast 19 percent fall in solar silver demand in 2026 from thrifting.
    • A fading tailwind turning to a headwind: silver is 12 percent of sales, and a 10 percent silver move is 8 percent of EBITDA.
  • Currency: the average USD/PLN rate was 3.98 in 2024, 3.76 in 2025 and 3.62 in H1 2026
    • About 75 percent of revenue is dollar-linked and over 80 percent of costs are in zloty.
    • A headwind, intensifying.
  • Not A, because the force's effect is already in the numbers
    • An A is for a force the market has not yet priced.

Political and regulatory: is a change moving demand, pricing or cost? (KQ 4, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because the Minerals Extraction Tax reform, signed 2025-12-15 and in force from 2026-01-01, is a dated, enacted cost tailwind: the coefficient falls from 0.85 to 0.74 in 2026 and 0.68 in 2027 to 2028, worth savings of PLN 500 million in 2026 and PLN 750 million a year in 2027 and 2028 on Morgan Stanley's estimate (management said about PLN 600 million for 2026), before a reset to 0.85 from 2029 with a 40 percent capex deduction capped at 30 percent of the monthly tax due.
  • The offset: the tax is formula-driven on metal prices, so standalone MET reached PLN 2,158 million in Q2 2026 and the absolute tax rose even as the coefficient fell.
    • The relief is real but the tax base has risen faster.
  • Other jurisdictions: Chile's royalty law caps the effective rate at 46.5 percent and Sierra Gorda is shielded by a stability agreement for now
    • The EU ETS free-allocation phase-out has slowed (91.5 percent in 2028, 59 percent in 2030).
  • Not A, because the tax relief reverts in 2029, inside our three-year horizon.

Summary: Written Summary of the Investment Case (not rated) (E 3, BB 4)
The case in 8 to 10 sentences (the thesis as tested), kept in full at both ends of the record (Part 1, E 3, BB 4)
  • KGHM is a cyclical price-leverage case that has already happened, dressed as a recovery and now turning into a capital-spending story.
  • A sector-wide copper force, structural in demand and tight in supply, met the most operationally geared cost base in the European mining universe and took earnings from PLN 18.36 a share in FY2025 to a consensus PLN 39.27 in FY2026, with Q2 2026 revenue up 49 percent and the share price up 95.8 percent in twelve months to PLN 334.6 (2026-10-07).
  • The change has travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain: it is in the operating metrics, the financial statements, the sell-side's estimates and the price.
  • From here the chain runs the other way: consensus has EPS falling 7.7 percent in FY2027 and 0.9 percent in FY2028, and our modal path is lower still (about PLN 34 and PLN 32), because silver has halved from its spike, the zloty has strengthened 6 percent, domestic labour costs rose 21 percent in H1 2026, the extraction tax rises with prices and resets higher in 2029, and nothing in the company's volume or cost position grows into the copper price.
  • Internally, the owner replaced the operator-chief executive in January 2026 with a state administrator, who in July launched a PLN 32 billion capital plan, a 12-to-14-year fertiliser mine and a USD 2 to 5 billion acquisition search.
  • The shares trade on 9.6x 24-month-forward earnings, the 68th to 73rd percentile of their own history and above their ten-year median of 8.1x.
  • On our modal path the value including cash returns is PLN 252.9 at eighteen months (-17.0 percent a year) and PLN 275.1 at three years (-6.3 percent a year), and the bull branch at about 15 percent odds does not lift the probability-weighted return out of the F band.
  • The case fits 'Demand Inflection' loosely and late, and no other pattern.
  • It is not compelling: the force was real, the leverage was real, and both are paid for
    • What remains is the symmetric half of the leverage and an owner whose next decision is not ours to forecast.
Setup verdict: The sorting-box verdict: primary 'Target Setup', any secondary, and the mechanism tying Thesis and Foundations to the pattern (Part 2, E 3, BB 4)
  • Primary: 'Demand Inflection', with a fit of D.
  • No secondary: 'Major Corporate Change' is the only other pattern with any input, and its inputs point away from an EPS upgrade.
  • The mechanism tying the thesis and the 'Foundations' to the pattern is fixed-cost operating leverage to a sector-wide, structural rise in the copper price, in a price-taker with an upper-half cost curve, a deep, fixed, zloty cost base and a 66 percent EPS sensitivity per 10 percent of copper.
  • The 'Foundations' give that mechanism geology that lasts past 2055 and an owner that makes it a vehicle for national policy.
  • The fit is not crisp because the pattern wants the inflection ahead of the shares and here it is behind them (FY2026 EPS up 114 percent on consensus; the share price up 95.8 percent in twelve months), because the company captures it only through price (flat volume, no dominant position, no one-to-many scalability), and because the industry is not an oligopoly.
