The model's FY2025 opening balance is FactSet's receivables plus inventories less payables (€16,064m, 41.1% of revenue), against which the second set's company-basis figures (-20.9% to -10.1%, with contract liabilities netted) produced a spurious +€25.2bn working-capital inflow in FY2026 (the flagged free cash flow of €28,193m against €6,870m), so the line is restated on the model's own basis so that the year-on-year changes equal the second set's intended cash flows of +€3.6bn (FY2026), +€1.0bn, -€0.8bn, -€1.5bn and -€1.2bn (FY2030).

Working capital is the balance at the end of each fiscal year as a percentage of the last twelve months' revenue.

Note for the Financial Analyst. These percentages are a device: they are not forecasts of FactSet's own line (which will keep rising as inventories grow) but the balances that make the model's working-capital cash flow equal the second set's view. The equivalent fix, if preferred, is to keep the second set's figures (-20.9, -20.5, -16.8, -12.7, -10.1) and replace the model's FY2025 opening balance with the company's reported operating net working capital of -€6,071m (-15.5% of FY2025 revenue); apply one fix, not both. The FY2027 to FY2030 flows in the model are already right; only FY2026 and the net cash line in every year are affected. The model's net cash should then be about €8.1bn at FY2026, €8.9bn at FY2027 and about €4.5bn at FY2028 after the India payment, before the model's own dividend-timing convention.

The reasoning behind the inputs

Three cautions for the Financial Analyst before using Parts 1 to 3. 1. Working capital is on the company's net working capital basis, with contract liabilities netted, not on FactSet's "receivables plus inventories less payables" line. FactSet's line (41.1% of revenue at FY2025, 46.4% at Q3 FY2026) leaves out €22.3bn of contract liabilities and would show this company as absorbing cash when it is generating it. My FY2025 opening balance on the company basis is about -14.1% of revenue (about -€5.5bn; an estimate, uncertain by about ±€1.5bn, because the company's definition and the Bernstein "-78 days" figure do not reconcile exactly). If the model takes its FY2025 opening from the FactSet line, replace it with -14.1%, or FY2026 cash flow will be misstated by about €20bn. What matters is the change: my working-capital cash flows are +€3.6bn (FY2026), +€1.0bn, -€0.8bn, -€1.5bn and -€1.2bn (FY2030). I fold the use of Siemens Gamesa's warranty and onerous-contract provisions (about €0.4bn to €0.5bn a year) into these flows. 2.   • Margins. Backlog repricing (new gas orders about 20% above backlog pricing; grid backlog margin rising about 300bps a year), service growth on a growing fleet (over 70% long-term agreement attach rate, 17-year contracts), wind repair, the end of the brand royalty, and overheads growing about 7% a year against revenue at 12% to 13% (selling, general and administrative costs from 8.9% to about 8.0% of revenue by FY2028).   • Depreciation. Rises with the €6bn FY2026 to FY2028 capacity programme, from about 3.5% to about 3.2% of revenue.
  • Net financial items. Interest income on €10bn to €11bn of gross cash at about 3%, less about €190m of bond, loan and lease interest and about €20m of pension interest; guarantee fees have ended. Lower from FY2028 after the India payment.

The bridge from the company's own reported figures to the basis consensus uses

  • Operating profit. The company's KPI, profit before special items, is EBITA before special items and before amortisation of intangibles acquired in business combinations. FactSet's consensus "EBIT" (FY2026 €5,303m) is on this basis. FactSet's "EBIT (adjusted)" history is lower (nine months FY2026 €3,577m against €3,946m), the difference being special items and acquired-intangible amortisation.
  • EBITDA. Brokers' basis equals profit before special items plus depreciation and amortisation excluding acquired intangibles: FY2025 €2,355m + €1,359m = €3,714m (9.5%). FactSet's operating EBITDA (€3,513m, 9.0%) is after special items.
  • Net income and EPS. Adjusted EPS = (profit before special items + net financial items) x (1 - tax rate) - minorities, over weighted average shares. Reported EPS also deducts special items (about €0.35bn, €0.5bn, €0.4bn, €0.2bn, €0.15bn pre-tax in FY2026 to FY2030) and acquired-intangible amortisation (about €0.25bn a year); my reported EPS estimates are €4.26, €5.91, €7.76, €9.50 and €10.91. The FY2025 brokers'-basis EPS of €1.63 equals reported basic EPS, while FactSet's adjusted diluted EPS for FY2025 is €1.93. The forward consensus median mixes adjusted and reported definitions (FY2026 per-broker range €4.11 to €5.26; the lower figures, for example Metzler €4.11 and Barclays €4.26, are close to my reported estimate). So my adjusted EPS sits about 3% to 5% above the consensus median in FY2026 to FY2027 partly for definitional reasons.
  • Free cash flow. The company guides "free cash flow pre-tax": operating cash flow before income taxes, less capital spending; lease payments are not deducted. FactSet's FCF (and its consensus, €7,615m for FY2026) is operating cash flow after tax less capital spending, also before lease payments. My FCF deducts tax and lease payments, so it is about €0.45bn a year below a like-for-like FactSet figure. On a like-for-like basis my FCF is about €0.3bn below consensus in FY2026, about €0.9bn below in FY2027 and about €1.1bn below in FY2028.
  • Net cash. FactSet's debt includes about €2.1bn of lease liabilities; the company's adjusted net cash (about €7.7bn at Q3 FY2026) and FactSet's (€8.1bn) differ for this and other reasons.
  • Currency. Reported revenue includes translation (-560bps in Q2 FY2026, -50bps in Q3); consensus revenue is reported, not comparable. My inputs assume no translation effect after FY2026.

How to read these figures

  • Amounts are in millions of the reporting currency; per-share figures are in units of that currency. Negative figures are shown in brackets.
  • Historical figures use FactSet's standardised lines, which can differ from the company's own presentation.
  • Earnings per share appears on four bases, which must not be mixed: reported basic; FactSet's adjusted diluted, before unusual items; the Viewforth definition, which is the model's standing convention; and the brokers' adjusted basis, on which consensus is based.
  • Consensus is FactSet's median of the broker estimates it holds. Where FactSet's line-by-line consensus differs from the broker-by-broker detail, the line-by-line table takes precedence.
  • Viewforth's forecasts are the Excel model's base case. There are no bull or bear cases.
