EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Maersk's pricing power varies dramatically across operating conditions, with the company demonstrating strong immediate surcharge implementation during route disruptions but facing severe rate erosion during supply-wave years when fleet capacity loosens and shippers revert to transactional behavior. The Ocean segment exhibits exceptional earnings sensitivity to loaded freight rate movements, with a USD 100 per FFE rate change impacting EBIT by USD 1.3 billion, making pricing dynamics the primary driver of Maersk's cyclical earnings trajectory over the next 18 months to 3 years.

During the 2024-2026 Red Sea diversions, Maersk successfully implemented emergency transit disruption surcharges of USD 200 per TEU and USD 400 per FEU under contractual force majeure provisions, demonstrating strong immediate pricing power when route safety concerns dominate shipper decision-making. The company recovered incremental Middle East costs on approximately 44% of its contracted volumes through formulaic surcharges, while the remaining 56% spot-exposed volumes depended on market pricing discipline.

However, the gradual return of ocean services to the Suez Canal in late 2026 rapidly released effective vessel capacity back into the market, with Suez Canal transits doubling to 50% of 2023 levels and placing immediate downward pressure on Asia-Europe spot rates. The consensus view among sell-side analysts anticipates severe pricing pressure from the looming supply wave, yet this bearish outlook may underestimate structural capacity constraints.

The container shipping sector faces an oversupplied market with a record 14.9 million TEU orderbook representing roughly 44% of the active global fleet, suggesting that a prolonged downturn and severe cash burn for liners is inevitable. Broker forecasts expect global container capacity to grow 7.7% in 2027 and 12.3% in 2028 against muted demand growth tracking real GDP, which will severely compress average loaded rates unless lines implement strict capacity discipline.

However, record port congestion in East Asia has tied up 4.3 million TEU of capacity, with Maersk management arguing that structural terminal underinvestment will cause frequent bottlenecks that support rates against global fleet expansion. Historical precedent reveals Maersk's vulnerability during supply-wave years characterized by low scrapping activity. During the 2015-2016 supply wave, Maersk's average loaded rates declined 18.7% to USD 1,795 per FFE despite positive volume growth of 9.4%, leading to an underlying net loss of USD 384 million and demonstrating that the company lacks sufficient pricing power to resist broad vessel oversupply. Similarly, in the 2023 post-pandemic normalization, Maersk's average loaded rates fell by 50% to USD 2,313 per FFE while Hapag- Lloyd's average rate declined 47.7% to USD 1,496 per TEU, proving that the sector lacks pricing power during rapid capacity normalizations.

Maersk's commercial contract portfolio demonstrates a structural tension between rate protection and market flexibility. The company targets an equal split of volumes between long-term and short-term rate products, aiming for roughly 50% contract and 50% spot exposure, though this balanced target faces tactical adjustments during periods of extreme rate volatility and network changes. Shippers have increasingly pushed to replace traditional multi-year and annual fixed agreements with shorter, more flexible commercial arrangements, with expert interviews confirming that the traditional annual container negotiation cycle has fragmented into monthly or quarterly renegotiation windows.

Most customers exhibit highly transactional behavior and will easily move volume when spot rates fall below negotiated contract terms, routinely demanding price concessions and questioning why they should support fixed contract rates when spot rates are cheaper. The proposed Gemini Cooperation reliability premium remains fundamentally unproven in loose capacity markets. Carriers historically have not been rewarded with rate premiums for high schedule reliability, with expert calls confirming that standard customers simply demand the cheapest spot rate, indicating that Maersk's Gemini reliability premium will likely fail to materialize in an oversupplied market.

The statistical correlation coefficient between reliability and spot rates stands at negative 0.81 because reliability naturally improves when overcapacity leaves vessels underutilized and ports uncongested, suggesting that Maersk cannot command a premium for reliability unless the broader shipping market remains structurally tight. Nevertheless, specialized B2B shippers such as chemical manufacturers exhibit willingness to pay premium rates of up to 10% to secure reliable space for high-value logistics because they cannot afford production shutdowns caused by delayed raw materials. rate realization dynamics vary significantly across carrier business models and regional deployment patterns.

ZIM Integrated Shipping Services operates a highly volatile commercial model due to heavy spot exposure compared to its peer group, struggling to maintain customer commitments when spot rates fall and facing rapidly declining rate realization and cash burn during standard container market downturns. In contrast, Hapag-Lloyd stabilizes its rate realization through a high contract share, typically locking in 50% to 65% of its volumes through long-term contracts, which allows the company to target high-value cargo and support premium pricing even in soft rate environments.

Private competitor MSC's operating fleet has reached 6 million TEU representing 20% of global capacity, with this rapid expansion forcing peers like Maersk to alter their conservative fleet investment strategies and implying that long-term rate realization will face severe pressure as MSC prices aggressively to fill space.

Maersk's integrated logistics strategy creates material commercial friction that threatens core Ocean volumes. The company's strategic pivot to bypass intermediaries and sell end-to-end services directly to cargo owners has alienated traditional freight forwarders, with expert insights revealing that Maersk is actively trying to bypass forwarders to secure direct customer accounts, creating a major conflict of interest that poses substantial volume churn risks because forwarders historically controlled up to 50% of ocean volumes.

Freight forwarders are actively retaliating by rerouting their ocean container volumes to more cooperative carriers, with DB Schenker having intentionally executed global shipments minus the support of Maersk to exit the carrier's commercial ecosystem, while forwarders prefer collaborating with ocean carriers like Hapag-Lloyd, MSC, and CMA CGM who continue to treat forwarders as valuable commercial allies. This strategic tension is particularly acute because asset-heavy carriers like Maersk suffer massive profit collapses during rate downturns, whereas forwarders like DSV and Kuehne+Nagel protect their conversion ratios through flexible cost bases, with Kuehne+Nagel and DHL quickly restoring operating margins during the 2009 shipping recession while Maersk Line fell into a USD 1.87 billion operating loss.

APM Terminals demonstrates substantially greater pricing resilience than the Ocean segment through structural concession protections and inflation-linked tariff frameworks. The Terminals segment demonstrated strong pricing resilience in the second quarter of 2026 with revenue increasing 11% year-on-year to USD 1.45 billion, as revenue per move on a like-for-like basis improved 7.7% due to increased tariff rates and higher storage revenue which successfully offset a 7.4% labor inflation cost increase.

Global terminal concessions have an average remaining term of 22 years and typically prescribe maximum tariffs but include automatic indexation mechanisms to shield operators from local cost inflation, ensuring that APM Terminals can defend its operating margins because concession terms prevent arbitrary price reductions by cash-strapped liner alliances. APM Terminals operates 53 container terminals across 28 countries holding a 5.6% global equity-adjusted throughput share and is the only operator with a top ten position in every global region, with this extensive physical footprint protecting the terminal segment's profitability and sustaining high-margin cash flows even during broad ocean downturns.

Landside logistics pricing exhibits material volatility and structural margin pressure compared to asset-light forwarder peers. While the Logistics & Services segment revenue grew 15% to USD 4.22 billion in the second quarter of 2026, the Solutions subsegment lagged with an EBIT margin of only 1.7% due to warehousing whitespace, with broker analysis arguing that Maersk's integrated logistics strategy struggles to achieve profitable growth because integrating asset-heavy ocean networks with logistics services creates operational friction.

Asset-light freight forwarders exhibit superior margin protection compared to Maersk's asset-heavy integrated logistics model, with no statistical correlation between sea freight rates and unit gross profits at DSV or Kuehne+Nagel because forwarders adjust subcontracted capacity costs dynamically, as evidenced by Kuehne+Nagel's Sea Logistics EBIT rising 55% to CHF 172 million in the third quarter of 2026 through cost discipline despite volatile markets. cost pass-through mechanisms provide critical EPS protection across both ocean and landside operations.

Ocean carriers successfully pass through volatile environmental compliance costs to shippers by utilizing formulaic, non-negotiable surcharges, with Maersk recovering regulatory costs including the EU

Emissions Trading System through structured customer surcharges, ensuring that regulatory compliance expenses do not dilute ocean operating margins and protecting EPS from carbon tax inflation because formulaic charges are strictly enforced even in loose markets. Landside transportation relies on highly frequent surcharge adjustments, with Maersk announcing a 25% Intermodal Fuel Fee across multiple Australian states reviewed bi-weekly to recover costs linked to Middle East transit issues, allowing the Landside division to react immediately to fuel price volatility and safeguard landside operating margins from energy shocks.

The overall confidence in Maersk's pricing power assessment is medium due to strong near-term rate visibility contrasted against high long-term contract settlement uncertainty and unclear vessel scrapping thresholds. Critical intelligence gaps remain regarding the precise service level agreements within the Gemini Cooperation and whether clear financial penalties exist if schedule reliability falls below the targeted 90% threshold, as Gemini focuses mainliner transshipments on a few central hub terminals which introduces significant failure risks if a single port faces congestion.

The precise rate levels at which the upcoming 2027 Asia-Europe and Transpacific annual contracts will settle remain a major commercial blind spot, with industry sources expecting double-digit contract rate declines of up to 30% compared to 2025 levels due to massive looming fleet capacity, information that is critical for determining whether undisclosed 2027 contract settlement rates will drop below cash breakeven next year.

If container freight rates remain structurally elevated due to persistent port bottlenecks contrary to consensus expectations, Maersk's earnings trajectory would be fundamentally transformed, as bearish consensus expects a severe rate downturn due to the record orderbook, but persistent port bottlenecks could continue to absorb vessel capacity and support freight rates, meaning Maersk's ocean segment would avoid the expected cash burn and deliver substantial upside to consensus EPS.

Conversely, accelerating Suez Canal transits and returning vessel capacity could inevitably collapse Asia-Europe rates, with Suez transits rebounding to 54% of late 2023 levels indicating that the unwinding of Cape rerouting benefits is already well underway, suggesting that any nearterm rate strength is temporary and cannot prevent a severe 2027 downturn. The forward-looking analysis reveals that Maersk faces a bifurcated earnings outlook heavily dependent on whether effective capacity constraints persist or dissolve over the next two years.

The company's ability to sustain Ocean segment profitability hinges critically on three unresolved factors: the speed of Suez normalization, the timing and magnitude of vessel scrapping in response to falling rates, and the persistence of terminal bottlenecks that artificially restrict nominal fleet capacity. While the Terminals segment provides a structural earnings hedge through concession-protected tariffs and the company successfully passes through environmental costs via formulaic surcharges, the integrated logistics strategy introduces material execution risk through forwarder alienation that threatens core Ocean volumes.

The ultimate resolution of Maersk's pricing power question will emerge through the 2027 contract negotiation season, when shippers confront the tension between securing reliable capacity and demanding aggressive rate reductions in an oversupplied market.

Section 2 – Pricing Power under Canal and Route Disruptions

Section summary
  1. Route disruptions provide container lines with temporary pricing power through surcharges but normalisation quickly erodes these gains.
  2. Maersk successfully implemented emergency surcharges during the 2024-2026 diversions but faces rapid rate erosion as services return to Suez.
  3. Geopolitical disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz inflated landside costs but had limited direct impact on global ocean freight rates.

Rerouting and Surcharge Implementation

1.
Rapid cost pass-through: Maersk demonstrated strong immediate pricing power by implementing transit disruption surcharges when the Red Sea crisis escalated. Morgan Stanley's report of 31 January 2024 notes that Maersk invoked Clause 20(a) to introduce transit disruption surcharges of USD 200 per TEU and USD 400 per FEU. These emergency levies allowed Maersk to offset the steep operational cost increases associated with the longer voyage around the Cape of Good Hope. This initial success suggests that sudden route changes permit carriers to command strong immediate surcharges because shippers prioritised cargo safety over rates.
2.
Shared pricing behavior: Major container carriers followed Maersk's surcharge lead to establish industry-wide emergency pricing during the initial Red Sea disruption. Morgan Stanley's report of 31 January 2024 notes that Hapag-Lloyd introduced an Operational Recovery Surcharge of USD 500 per TEU and CMA CGM implemented a Red Sea Charge of USD 1,575 per 20-foot container. Since these competitors faced similar routing challenges, the coordinated implementation of these surcharges prevented destructive rate competition. Consequently, this industry-wide alignment helped Maersk sustain robust ocean segment cash flows during the peak of the diversions.
3.
Protected contract volumes: Maersk managed to secure cost recovery on its contracted volumes because these agreements contained formulaic surcharge clauses. Morgan Stanley's report of 7 May 2026 notes that Maersk recovered incremental Middle East costs on about 44% of its contracted volumes through formulaic surcharges. The remaining 56% of Maersk's volumes were spot-exposed, which meant that cost recovery was highly dependent on market pricing discipline. This contract structure indicates that Maersk's EPS has strong structural protection against cost spikes only when contract terms are strictly enforced.

The Suez Canal Service Return

4.
Rapidly expanding supply: The gradual return of ocean services to the Suez Canal in late 2026 is rapidly releasing effective vessel capacity back into the market. Bernstein Research's report of 23 October 2026 highlights that Suez Canal transits have doubled to 50% of 2023 levels, freeing up substantial industry capacity. This capacity release has placed immediate downward pressure on Asia-Europe spot rates, with major ocean freight indices rolling over. As a result, this supply influx is likely to severely compress Maersk's average loaded rates and drag down Ocean segment margins.
5.
Broad industry normalization: All major container shipping lines are simultaneously restoring services through Suez, which accelerates the unwinding of Cape rerouting benefits. Morgan Stanley's report of 14 September 2026 notes that Maersk and Hapag-Lloyd rerouted four additional Gemini services via the Red Sea. Morgan Stanley's report of 5 October 2026 confirms that weekly containership transits rose 61.9% week-on-week to 752,000 TEU. This coordinated return means that Maersk and its partner Hapag-Lloyd will struggle to defend freight rates as the Gemini network normalizes.
6.
Contrary evidence: Persistent terminal and landside congestion in major Asian hubs continues to absorb effective vessel capacity despite the Suez return. Bernstein Research's report of 9 October 2026 highlights that record port congestion in East Asia has tied up 4.3 million TEUs. Bernstein Research's report of 25 September 2026 notes that port bottlenecks continue to constrain supply chains and support elevated freight rates. These operational bottlenecks mean that the capacity release from Suez is partially offset by terminal delays, sustaining rates.
