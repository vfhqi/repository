Terms used throughout. EBITA: adjusted earnings before interest, tax and amortisation of acquired intangibles, the company's main profit measure. EBITDA: EBITA before depreciation. A first point for Richard before the questions. Sdiptech is held as a Core Investment at 5.63% of the fund, so the questions are weighted to whether to add, hold or cut, and to what would break the case. The memo (31-Oct-2026) calls it "good niche infrastructure businesses whose group-level record was spoiled by what it paid and how it financed itself, and which a new management has cleaned up quickly and well": EPS flat at SEK 11 to 13 for FY2022 to FY2025 while revenue grew 48%, and now compounding at 15% to 17% (SEK 15.27 to 27.96 over FY2026 to FY2030) because dilution, a seven-fold rise in interest and 9x entry prices have gone. About a third of FY2027's EPS increase is already contracted. Its verdict on sizing: "a case to size for the multiple's volatility and to add to on the fourth-quarter proof point rather than before it", that proof point being fourth-quarter cash conversion and year-end leverage on 10-Feb-2027. Two things since: the third-quarter earnings summary (29-Oct) found the two drivers the case needed (organic growth, deals) well ahead and the margin, cash and Water & Bioeconomy alarm going the wrong way; and the Industry's relative strength fired Sell Criterion 1 for Sdiptech with four other holdings on 10-Nov-2026 (an Industry-wide reading, not a stock-specific one). The Rater's four ratings: Thesis's Change Forces' Strength 75, Foundations' Robustness 55, Setups' Fit/Crispness 74, Case Riskiness 48.

A second point: what this call can and cannot give. A chief financial officer can go further than investor relations on capital allocation, the cash and leverage path, the finance line, earn-outs and the steer within the building blocks, but will not give numbers outside them: the company gives no annual guidance, only building blocks (organic EBITA growth of 5% to 10% a year, SEK 100m of acquired run-rate EBITA a year, cash conversion of 70% to 90%, leverage below 3.0x, ROCE above 15%, more than 15% EBITA growth a year and SEK 2,000m of EBITA by 2030). Frame questions as "how should we model this" or "is our reading of what you published right". He was Chief Executive until mid-2025, so he can speak to the reset from both seats. The Executive Assistant's suggested openers: thank him for his patience over the February call (the gap was Richard's), then the third-quarter margin against 13% organic growth, then the London roadshow. What the call should settle, and why

The case in one line: Sdiptech's EPS now compounds mechanically because the drags have gone, and two numbers decide whether it keeps doing so, the conversion of growth into profit and cash, and the price and pace of acquisitions inside a 3.0x ceiling; the share price, meanwhile, answers to the Riksbank and the cohort. Six judgements decide whether to add, hold or cut.

  1. Judgement 1, the fourth-quarter cash release and year-end leverage. Third-quarter conversion was 62% and leverage 2.90x to 2.95x against the 3.0x ceiling; we have 2.6x to 2.7x at year end after the seasonal release (134% in the fourth quarter of 2025). Above 100% and 2.6x to 2.7x on 10-Feb-2027 unlocks the engine at SEK 100m a year; below 90% and 2.8x pauses deals for two quarters, and the memo's proof point for adding fails.
  2. Judgement 2, the drop-through in H1 2027. Organic sales +7%, +11%, +13% in 2026 against organic EBITA +4%, +5%, +6%. We read most of the gap as about SEK 50m a quarter of near-zero-margin chassis pass-through at Hilltip and the Mecno overrun, both ending in the fourth quarter of 2026. If H1 2027 shows organic EBITA growing faster than organic sales, RedEye's "structural cost pressure" reading is disproved; if Supply Chain & Transportation stays at 18% to 19%, organic sales of 4% and a 20.2% margin take FY2027 EPS to about SEK 17.1, 7% below the model.
  3. Judgement 3, the acquisition engine's price and pace. Base SEK 90m of EBITA in 2027 and SEK 100m a year after, at about 6.5x upfront and 8x total; each SEK 10m closed adds about 0.7% to EPS and consensus models none. A year of SEK 50m costs 2.3% of FY2027 EPS and 3.3% of FY2028, and stops the upgrade stream; deals at 8x upfront instead of 6.5x add about 25% less EPS; a deal at 9x total is the memo's alarm.
  4. Judgement 4, the earn-out write-backs. About SEK 90m of gains in nine months of 2026 against SEK 48m in 2025, the 2026 figure unattributed. If they trace to units bought in 2024 and 2025 missing their plans, the quality of reported profit and the deal record both weaken; if timing, as in 2025, no change.
  5. Judgement 5, sterling and the finance line together. 49% of sales in the United Kingdom, hedges within twelve months; 40% to 45% of credit swapped. A 10% weaker pound removes SEK 83m of EBITA and SEK 0.9 to 1.7 of EPS; 100bp on STIBOR adds SEK 11m to 33m of interest (SEK 0.2 to 0.7); together, 5% and 100bp take FY2027 EPS to about SEK 17.3 (6% lower).
  6. Judgement 6, the margin's floors: Water & Bioeconomy and Safety & Security. Water & Bioeconomy at 20.3% has triggered our alarm (below 21.8%); Safety & Security at 36.2% is called unsustainable by the Chief Executive. Water & Bioeconomy still below 22% after the fourth-quarter and first-quarter reports meets the case's invalidation level; Safety & Security falling below the model's 31% with a data-centre pause costs SEK 30m to 50m of EBITA (SEK 0.6 to 1.0 of EPS).

Range A: scope for guidance and sell-side upgrades or cuts over the next four quarters (the FY2026 report on 10-Feb-2027, the first-quarter report in late April 2027, the second-quarter report in mid-July 2027 and the third-quarter report in late October 2027)

A1 ★ The fourth-quarter cash release and leverage at year end. New from the reports. "Third-quarter conversion was 62%, with SEK 230 million spent on deals and SEK 172 million on earn-outs. How much of the working-capital build unwinds in the fourth quarter, where should leverage land at the year end, and is the 3.0x ceiling measured on the 2.95x that includes earn-outs or the 2.39x that does not?"
- Why: fourth-quarter 2025 conversion was 134%; the aim is 80% rolling (81% now), but FY2023 ended at 67%. DNB Carnegie's fourth quarter needs SEK 1,365m of sales at a 21.4% EBITA margin (SEK 292m) and it has leverage at 2.0x by end-2027; we have 2.6x to 2.7x at end-2026. Which ratio the ceiling uses decides how much room the engine has: on the financial measure there is about 0.6x of headroom, on the total measure almost none. One number on one date gates deal pace, upgrades and the multiple.
- Tell: "a normal seasonal release; the ceiling is on total leverage, below 2.8x at year end" = Judgement 1 on track and the proof point likely met; "the ceiling excludes earn-outs" = more room than we assumed, and a faster 2027; "growth keeps absorbing working capital" = deals slow in H1 2027, and adding should wait.

A2 ★ The drop-through in 2027. New from the reports. "Is our reading right that the gap between 13% organic sales and 6% organic EBITA is mostly Hilltip's truck-chassis pass-through and Mecno's unindexed raw materials, both ending with the fourth quarter, so the first half of 2027 shows organic EBITA growing faster than organic sales?"

RedEye has cut its long-run gross margin to 59%, reading part of it as lasting. Each 50bp of EBITA margin is about SEK 0.58 of EPS.
- Tell: "yes, both end in the fourth quarter, indexation in place" = the debate ends in two reports and Judgement 2 passes; "Hilltip's larger road segment continues" or "cost pressure broader than Mecno" = RedEye's 59% is right, and FY2027 EPS is nearer SEK 17.

A3 Supply Chain & Transportation's margin path. New from the reports. "What margin should we expect from Supply Chain & Transportation through 2027, how much of its fixed-price work now carries indexation, and when do you expect the pricing project to show?"
- Why: the memo's test is the margin back above 20% by the second quarter of 2027; DNB Carnegie expects pressure into the fourth quarter. The pricing project started in four companies in the first quarter "with no effect yet". This is the largest segment (38% of 2025 EBITA) and the one the Chief Executive ran from 2018.
- Tell: "back to 20% to 21% by mid-2027" = our base; "18% to 19% is the level with this mix" = a lower margin path.

A4 ★ Organic growth in 2027. New from the reports. "How should we think about organic growth next year, given comparisons of 7%, 11% and 13%, the refrigeration orders moved from 2025 into 2026, the chassis sales leaving the base, and the backlogs at GAH, Certus and Hilltip?"

- Tell: "mid-to-high single digits; the backlogs carry the first half" = our base, and the slowdown is normalisation; "low single digits once the timing effects go" = RedEye's fade comes sooner.

A5 ★ The acquisition pace. New from the reports. "You said four to six deals this year would be a good outcome and that 2027 is the full ramp-up. With about SEK 75 million of run-rate EBITA bought by October against SEK 100 million, will you reach it by year end, and what pace should we assume for 2027 inside the ceiling?"

- Tell: "close to 100 this year, more next year" = the upgrade stream runs at 1.5% to 2% a quarter; "we will wait for the cash release" = Judgement 3's downside for H1 2027.

A6 ★ What the deals cost. New from the reports. "You have spoken of around six times EBITA, up to seven for faster growers, and a 20% return hurdle. What did the five deals cost on average including earn-outs, and would you disclose total consideration per deal as Halma does?"

Our "deal above 9x" alarm cannot be read.
- Tell: "about 6x upfront, 7x to 8x total" = the discipline the case relies on; "higher for the UK platforms" = returns close more slowly.

A7 ★ The earn-out write-backs. New from the reports.
