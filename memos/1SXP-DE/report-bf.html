Section 10 – Disruption Forces + Structural Evolution + LT Growth + Paradigm Fit 23 findings

Section summary
  1. long-term disruption from oral formats is heavily bounded by biological and cost constraints, while structural evolution leans toward integrated drug-device combinations
  2. growth adjacencies in ready-touse formats and digital health offer durable high-margin expansion paths across evolving macro regimes

Barriers to entry and exit dynamics

1.
Extensive capital requirements: establishing pharmaceutical packaging facilities requires immense upfront capital expenditure to construct cleanrooms and meet strict regulatory standards, creating a steep entry barrier
2.
Extended validation timelines: the timeline to qualify new sterile fill-finish lines spans years rather than months due to mandatory technical transfers, demonstration runs, and stability batches prior to regulatory approval
3.
High customer stickiness: once a contract manufacturer is selected, switching partners involves significant costs and time, protecting incumbent operators from sudden market-share erosion
4.
Fixed asset rigidity: high barriers to exit exist because manufacturing lines are highly specialized, capital- intensive investments designed to run for at least 20 years to recoup initial investments

Disruption forces and oral GLP-1 threats

5.
Low oral bioavailability: oral GLP-1 formulations are structurally limited by the hepatic first-pass effect and the harsh gastrointestinal environment, which degrades active peptides
6.
Prohibitive API requirements: to match the efficacy of a standard subcutaneous injection, oral tablets require up to five times more active pharmaceutical ingredient, dramatically increasing production costs
7.
Patient tolerability limits: delivering the high payload of active ingredients necessary for oral efficacy frequently triggers severe adverse gastrointestinal effects in patients
8.
Persistent injectable dominance: due to these biological and economic constraints, industry experts project that injectables will maintain a dominant 80% to 90% volume share of the long-term GLP-1 market
9.
Gradual dosing transitions: a more immediate volume headwind than oral formulations is the potential clinical shift from once-weekly to once-monthly subcutaneous injections, which would directly reduce syringe unit demand
10.
Polymer material tradeoffs: while cyclic olefin polymers (COP) offer superior break resistance and cryogenic performance, they are more permeable to oxygen than glass, making them a niche material reserved for specific oxygen-insensitive or ultra-low-temperature drugs. & Co Redburn High polymer scrap rates: polymer syringes are highly susceptible to cosmetic scratching during automated handling, leading to a 1% technical reject rate that becomes financially unacceptable when filling high-value therapeutics

Structural value chain evolution

11.
Accelerating format migration: the injectables market is undergoing a structural transition as drug developers migrate drug master files from standard vials toward ready-to-use (RTU) prefilled syringes and cartridges
12.
Standard-setting RTU alliances: to accelerate the historical 20-year transition from bulk to RTU formats, major competitors Schott, Gerresheimer, and Stevanato formed a strategic alliance to standardize sterile vial and cartridge configurations
13.
Downstream integration focus: primary packaging operators are increasingly moving up the value chain by developing proprietary drug delivery systems, such as Stevanato's Alina and Deora pen injector platforms
14.
Consolidated CDMO partnerships: biopharma innovators are consolidating their outsourcing volume with larger, stock-market-listed CDMOs that possess proven quality compliance records and integrated capabilities
15.
Strategic portfolio pruning: major packaging competitors are divesting non-core, lower-margin plastic assets, such as Gerresheimer's carve-out of its Centor and primary packaging plastics units, to aggressively focus on high-value injectable solutions

Adjacency expansion and long-term growth

16.
Capturing advanced modalities: growth is increasingly driven by specialized clinical pipelines in antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs), mRNA therapies, and cell and gene therapies, which require advanced barrier-coated containers

Standardized RTU platforms: Schott is expanding its commercial footprint in high-margin adjacencies by ramping up domestic US production of adaptiQ pre-sterilized RTU vials and EVERIC chemically durable vials

Paradigm fit with future macro regimes

20.
Insulation from policy shocks: pharmaceutical primary packaging operates with a high degree of insulation from macroeconomic cycles and healthcare policy shifts, as containment is a non-negotiable regulatory requirement for drug delivery
