Section summary
  1. The balancing co-product loop is operating as immunoglobulin growth generates a surplus of albumin that faces a deeply depressed Chinese market.

Co-Product Imbalances and Yield Dynamics

1.
Complex product yields: Fractionators face a persistent balancing act because plasma contains a fixed ratio of proteins that do not match market demand. Jefferies Research reported in October 2026 that each liter of plasma yields approximately 4 g of immunoglobulin and 24 g of albumin. Because immunoglobulin demand drives collection volumes, manufacturers must continuously find a commercial home for the resulting albumin coproduct. This structural yield mismatch exposes the industry to margin compression when secondary markets weaken.
2.
Escalating plasma requirements: The rapid adoption of subcutaneous immunoglobulin formulations significantly amplifies this co-product yield imbalance. Jefferies Research highlighted in November 2026 that subcutaneous administration requires approximately 20% to 25% more plasma per dose than equivalent intravenous therapies. This shift turns a moderate 6% to 8% long-term immunoglobulin volume growth into a demanding 8% to 10% plasma collection growth. Consequently, the industry is forced to process more plasma, generating even greater volumes of excess albumin.
