Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Vodafone Group's consolidation of Safaricom significantly expands accounting scale but delivers neutral immediate parent free cash flow due to heavy minority leakage, reduced Vodacom dividend payouts, and transaction debt service.

Vodafone Group Plc ("Vodafone Group" or "the Group") completed a highly transformative African transaction on June 30, 2026, which altered the consolidation structure of its emerging market assets. Specifically, Vodafone Group's 65.10% owned subsidiary, Vodacom Group Ltd ("Vodacom"), acquired an additional 20% interest in Safaricom Plc ("Safaricom")–consisting of 15% from the Government of Kenya and 5% directly from Vodafone Group–taking Vodacom's total stake in Safaricom to 55% and establishing outright corporate control. As a result of this restructuring, Safaricom transitioned from associate equity accounting to full consolidation within the financial statements of both Vodacom and Vodafone Group starting July 1, 2026.

The Key Question under investigation is: "Vodafone Group Plc (VOD-GB), a European and African telecommunications operator reporting Germany (cable, fibre wholesale access and mobile, including national roaming for 1&1), the UK (VodafoneThree, wholly owned since July 2026), Other Europe (Portugal, Ireland, Greece, Romania and others) and Türkiye, and Africa (Vodacom, including Vodafone Egypt and, since July 2026, a consolidated Safaricom and M-Pesa), together with Vodafone Business and a Vodafone Investments unit holding non-controlled stakes such as 50 percent of Oak Holdings (Vantage Towers). How much of Safaricom's and Vodacom's profit and cash reaches Vodafone Group's shareholders after the July 2026 consolidation: what are Vodafone's effective economic interests in Safaricom, Vodafone Egypt and Vodacom's other subsidiaries, what non-controlling interests and dividends to minority shareholders do the company and brokers expect for FY27 and FY28, how is Vodacom's R5.1bn purchase of the Kenyan government's Safaricom dividend rights accounted for in earnings and in cash, what does Vodacom's acquisition debt cost, and what would the Kenyan Court of Appeal's ruling on the government's stake sale change?" For an investor in Vodafone Group, the answer to this Key Question is crucial as it directly impacts reported Earnings Per Share (EPS), the mechanics of upstreaming cash to the parent level, and the overall valuation of the company. In this comprehensive report, we analyze the structural elements of Vodafone's African portfolio based on 10 different documents from four distinct source types: company filings and transcripts, broker research, regulatory updates, and financial news. The consolidation of Safaricom brings significant top-line and EBITDA scale to Vodafone Group, but it also introduces a massive layer of Non-Controlling Interests (NCI) that limits the actual profit reaching Vodafone's parent shareholders.

Legal Dispute and High Court Ruling

Unlawful divestiture judgment: The Kenyan High Court delivered a landmark judgment on 15 September 2026 that threw Vodacom's acquisition of Safaricom into chaos. The three-judge bench ruled that the unilateral 15% stake sale was unconstitutional and void because the state failed to conduct meaningful public participation. This ruling means Vodacom faces the risk of being forced to return the shares to the state. Consequently, any permanent reversal would negatively impact Vodafone Group's consolidated earnings per share by forcing a de-consolidation of Safaricom's €1.40 billion EBITDAaL pool.
2.
Procedural procurement violations: The High Court also faulted the transaction for bypassing competitive bidding and violating public procurement rules. According to the judgment, the state failed to justify bypassing competitive selection and illegally procured transaction advisory services. These legal infractions undermine the validity of the contract, raising corporate governance concerns for international investors. Therefore, Vodafone Group's earnings visibility will remain depressed unless these procurement challenges are resolved on appeal.

National security concerns: The Kenyan judges raised significant national security arguments by ruling that ceding control of Safaricom to a foreign entity was unconstitutional. They argued that ceding control of a critical digital asset like M-Pesa to foreign ownership threatened the country's sovereignty. This nationalistic legal reasoning is a severe headwind for multinational telecom operators expanding in emerging markets. This means that Vodacom's long-term control over Safaricom remains legally fragile despite initial shareholder approval.
