Section 1 – Brief company description and history

Business model and strategic position ASML is the dominant supplier of semiconductor lithography equipment, providing chipmakers with hardware, software and services used to print integrated-circuit patterns onto wafers. Its portfolio spans basic i-line and krypton fluoride systems, argon fluoride dry and immersion systems, extreme ultraviolet systems, metrology and inspection, computational lithography, process-control software and installed-base services.

ASML’s strongest asset is its monopoly in extreme ultraviolet, or EUV, lithography, while its broader value proposition is “holistic lithography”, which combines scanners with computational lithography, metrology, inspection and software to improve overlay, yield and good wafers per day. That breadth makes ASML less dependent on new scanner shipments alone, because customers can also buy field upgrades, productivity packages, service and refurbished systems over a tool’s long operating life.

The installed-base business is strategically important because upgrades can add wafer capacity inside an existing cleanroom faster than building a new fab, and management identified this as one of the principal ways to address customer capacity shortages alongside higher system output. This became especially significant in 2026, when Installed Base Management revenue was growing faster than new-system revenue and carried a margin benefit because of its high-value software and productivity content.

Three-year operating development ASML’s reported revenue increased from €27.6 billion in 2023 to €32.7 billion in 2025, while net income rose from €7.8 billion to €9.6 billion over the same period. The three-year history shows a business that moved from a semiconductor downturn and 2024 transition into a sharp artificial-intelligence-led recovery, rather than a steady linear expansion. - Earnings Calls The 2023 base included €9.1 billion of EUV system sales, €12.3 billion of DUV system sales and €536 million of metrology and inspection sales, with EUV revenue up 30% and DUV revenue up 60% year on year.

ASML also shipped the first modules of its High-NA EUV system in 2023, establishing the technology bridge toward future logic and memory nodes.

The 2025 results were materially stronger than the 2024 transition implied, with revenue reaching €32.7 billion and net income €9.6 billion, while initial 2026 guidance of €34 billion to €39 billion was subsequently overtaken by demand.
