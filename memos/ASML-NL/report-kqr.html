Section 4 – Export Controls, China Re-allocation, and Service Revenue Exposure

Section summary
  1. The proposed MATCH Act represents a significant regulatory risk that could restrict all DUV immersion shipments to China.
  2. Barred immersion DUV tools can be easily re-allocated to global customers due to severe lithography capacity shortages.
  3. Growing recurring service revenues provide a highly resilient floor as Chinese tool shipments decline.
  4. Peer companies show diverse levels of China exposure and face similar localization pressures on mature-node tools.

Impact of the MATCH Act

1.
Tightening export controls: The proposed MATCH Act in the year-end US defense bill represents a severe regulatory threat to lithography shipments. Jefferies Research highlighted on 28 July 2026 that this bipartisan legislation could introduce a nationwide ban on all DUV immersion tools to China. This legislation would designate facilities like SMIC and CXMT as covered entities, which means ASML faces a 15% revenue headwind if the bill passes. This regulatory shift indicates that earnings are highly vulnerable to geopolitical alignment unless Dutch authorities successfully resist US pressure.
2.
Bipartisan legislative momentum: The MATCH Act is highly likely to be integrated into the National Defense Authorization Act as a must-pass vehicle. Morgan Stanley noted on 28 September 2026 that congressional committees view the year-end bill as the primary path for export policy. If enacted, the legislation would give allied governments a 150-day window to adopt matching rules. This means that FY2027 estimates face notable risks unless the executive branch decides to delay implementation.
3.
Contrary evidence: The likelihood of the MATCH Act passing as a standalone bill in its most restrictive form remains limited due to diplomatic friction. Hana Financial Investment reported on 16 October 2026 that recent improvements in US-China relations could restrict the adoption of severe hardware bans. Furthermore, any blanket ban on older immersion systems like the NXT:1980i would disrupt Western automotive supply chains. This alternative perspective suggests that near-term earnings remain insulated from sudden legislative shocks.

Immersion Tool Re-allocation Dynamics

4.
High re-allocation feasibility: Immersion tools blocked from shipment to Chinese fabs can be rapidly redirected to alternative customers due to severe global supply deficits. Morgan Stanley reported on 15 October 2026 that customer capacity demands remain deeply underserved across both advanced logic and DRAM. This tight supply environment means that re-allocating barred immersion tools to non-China fabs is highly feasible. Consequently, overall system sales are unlikely to contract meaningfully even under stricter controls.
5.
Intensifying global lithography demand: Global memory and advanced logic manufacturers are accelerating their capital investment plans, creating a highly receptive market for redirected tools. CEO Christophe Fouquet noted on 14 October 2026 that customer capacity requests are outpacing planned capacity increases of 30% through 2028. This supply deficit means that redirected tools find immediate homes in Taiwan, South Korea, and the US. Therefore, ASML can easily offset Chinese revenue losses by shipping to leadingedge logic customers.
6.
Historical re-allocation precedent: The industry correction of 2023 demonstrated ASML's ability to navigate regional demand shifts by leveraging its deep global backlog. Moody's Ratings reported on 11 April 2025 that ASML utilized its massive backlogs to shift tool shipments to China when other regions experienced temporary utilization slowdowns. This flexible shipment pattern suggests that ASML can successfully pivot capacity between regions during trade disputes. Consequently, localized demand shocks do not permanently impair total system revenues.
