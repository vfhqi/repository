The company recorded a €1,002 million non-cash impairment of Grubhub in FY24, following €146 million in regional write-downs across Southern Europe, Australia, and Northern Europe in FY23. The second material outlier emerges in the valuation premium paid during the Prosus takeover, which represented a 63% premium to the February 21, 2025 closing price of €12.45 per share, yet implied only a 12.5x 2025E adjusted EBITDA multiple versus the sector average of 15.0x. This bifurcated valuation signal suggests that while the absolute share price premium appeared generous to retail shareholders, the fundamental enterprise value multiple remained at a meaningful discount to peers, likely reflecting Prosus's assessment of the multi-year turnaround risk and persistent market share erosion in core European markets.

The underlying structural driver was JET's material market share losses to Deliveroo and Uber Eats in the United Kingdom, with Uber Eats reportedly securing the number one market category position in early 2025, marking a dramatic reversal from JET's historical dominance.

A third qualitative anomaly appears in the sheer velocity and geographic breadth of regional rationalization executed during the Prosus integration period. Within a compressed twelve-month window from late 2025 to early 2026, JET terminated operations in Australia (cutting 120 roles at Menulog), withdrew entirely from Denmark (impacting 120 office employees and 160 active couriers), and restructured German logistics by laying off up to 2,000 employed couriers.

This simultaneous multi-market exit strategy is highly unusual among major food delivery platforms, where peers typically pursue sequential geographic optimization rather than parallel portfolio pruning. The compressed timeline suggests either extreme capital constraints or a deliberate Prosus strategy to immediately concentrate resources on defendable profit pools, creating execution risk concentrations that differ markedly from the gradual footprint adjustments observed at Delivery Hero or Uber Eats.

The fourth statistical outlier manifests in JET's persistent negative volume trajectory, where total order volumes contracted by 7% year-on-year in FY26, total revenue fell by 1.22% from €5,148 million in FY23 to €5,085 million in FY24, and total group orders declined by 5% to 879 million in FY24. The divergence magnitude exceeds normal competitive variance and suggests fundamental structural weakness in JET's value proposition or customer retention mechanisms, particularly when considered against the backdrop of heavy marketing reinvestments that failed to stabilize volumes. This performance gap represents the widest negative spread observed between JET and its immediate peer set over the measurement horizon.

The fifth and final outlier emerges in the counterintuitive margin expansion trajectory, where adjusted EBITDA rose 35.70% year-on-year from €339 million in FY23 to €460 million in FY24, driven by simplified delivery operations and optimized order pooling that raised UK adjusted EBITDA by 62%, even as H1 2025 adjusted EBITDA subsequently declined to €147 million from €156 million in H1 2024 due to marketing reinvestments.

This volatile margin oscillation pattern –expanding sharply during volume declines then contracting during attempted growth reinvestment–is atypical among scaled food delivery platforms, where operational leverage typically moves directionally with transaction volumes. The dissonance suggests that JET's margin structure is fundamentally detached from its revenue base, likely reflecting aggressive fixed cost cuts and delivery network simplification that temporarily boosted profitability while masking underlying business model deterioration. This creates a false positive signal in historical margin trends that may not be sustainable under renewed competitive pressure or regulatory worker reclassification mandates.
