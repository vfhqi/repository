Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. The evidence answers most of the Key Question but not all of it. Overall confidence in the evidence on BT's pension agreement is medium. The accounting treatment, the FY27–FY34 cash schedule and the covenant terms are well supported by company documents. The Fitch, Moody's and S&P treatment of the zerocoupon bonds cannot be confirmed, because the agencies have not released updated reports detailing their quantitative treatment of the bonds.
1.
Balance sheet classification: BT will report the £2.68bn of zero-coupon bonds as loans and borrowings. They were issued by BT Finance plc under its Euro Medium Term Note Programme, which places them inside reported net debt.
2.
IAS 19 treatment: BT judged that it does not control the BT-BTPS co-investment vehicle under IFRS 10, so the vehicle's assets can be classified as plan assets. Because the bonds are transferable debt securities, they reduce the net pension deficit. This contrasts with the non-transferable EE asset-backed funding arrangement of £1.1bn, which is excluded from plan assets. Sainsbury and Whitbread consolidated their property partnerships, so those investments did not qualify as plan assets.
3.
Accretion in finance expense: BT must recognise annual non-cash interest accretion on the bonds in finance expenses using the effective interest method, until the final maturities in September 2033. The exact annual accretion is not yet quantified in public disclosures. Rating agency treatment (unresolved): The report expects the agencies to treat the bonds as debt-like within adjusted leverage, citing net debt including leases of £21bn. It remains unclear whether S&P will treat the bonds as debt or as pension liabilities. Morgan Stanley reported no immediate impact on the credit rating. Cash schedule, FY27–FY34: The table shows the new schedule at £210m in FY27 against £780m under the 2023 schedule, then £770m a year in FY28–FY31 against £780m. FY32 and FY33 are £180m under both schedules, and FY34 is £600m against £180m. The bonds mature at £590m a year from April 2027 to April 2029, £490m in April 2030 and £420m in September 2033. FY27 £210m question: The £210m comprises a £30m deficit payment brought forward and the £180m asset- backed funding payment. It reflects only the post-valuation months, not a full-year run-rate. The scheduled contributions of £800m paid under the 2023 plan before signing contributed to net debt of £21bn at September 2026.
4.
Distribution covenant terms: The distribution ceiling rises to £1.5bn for FY27 from £1bn under the 2023 schedule. It then escalates by ten percent annually in FY28 and FY29 before reaching £2bn by FY30. BT must consult the trustee on buybacks other than those relating to employee share awards, or on any special dividend. It must also consult if annual normalised free cash flow falls below £1bn.
5.
Disposal terms: The agreement removes the non-core disposal trigger, which previously required one-third of net proceeds above £750m to go into the scheme. Network infrastructure disposals above £2bn in net cash proceeds over three years still require additional contributions, set at the lower of the low-dependency deficit or one-third of proceeds. What broker figures already assume: The agreement is read as cutting the funding deficit from £3.7bn to £2.7bn. Morgan Stanley's raised target reflects a lower-than-expected deficit and enhanced cash flow flexibility. Morgan Stanley assumes no immediate rating impact. It also assumes greater dividend headroom without triggering pension payments. Consensus therefore assumes a favourable balance sheet and covenant outcome, even though the agencies' treatment is unconfirmed.
6.
Differing views and figures: BT must provide an additional £282m in net present value to ensure full funding. Deutsche Bank reads this as an increase in total cash required to reach full funding. That contrasts with the optimistic deficit-reduction reading above. The report's own figures also differ: the text describes flat annual payments of £600m under the 2023 schedule, while the table shows £780m for FY27–FY31. Morgan Stanley puts leverage at 3.5x including leases and pensions, and a reclassification of the bonds as pure financial liabilities could push adjusted leverage higher and risk downgrades.
7.
Peer context: Deutsche Telekom's pension cash payments of €385m in 2024 were under one percent of its €44.24bn adjusted EBITDA. Orange's statutory pension contributions were only €28m in 2025. BT's relative cash funding burden therefore remains much heavier.
8.
Open gaps and forward risks: The Verizon joint venture remains subject to regulatory clearances before its expected completion in the second half of 2027. The TalkTalk and PlatformX acquisition awaits final CMA and Secretary of State clearance. First-half free cash flow of only £236m leaves the full-year £2bn target dependent on the second half. This matters because high leverage and the trustee consultation triggers could constrain buybacks if cash flows weaken. Closing these gaps would require the annual report, the agencies' credit updates and the regulatory decisions.

Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. BT Group's November 2026 pension agreement represents a transformative shift in capital allocation and balance sheet management.
  2. The resolution of the pension deficit and accounting treatment will directly impact BT's equity valuation and leverage metrics over the next three years.

Defining the Pension Inquiry

1.
Central inquiry: "For BT Group plc's November 2026 pension agreement, how will the zero-coupon bonds issued to the BT Pension Scheme co-investment vehicle be accounted for (inside or outside BT's reported net debt, their IAS 19 treatment as plan assets or not, and any accretion in finance expense), how do Fitch, Moody's and S&P treat them in adjusted leverage, what is BT's total deficit cash in each year FY27 to FY34 under the new schedule against the 2023 schedule (including whether the FY27 figure shown as £210m covers the full year or only the months after the June 2026 valuation date), and how do the distribution covenant's thresholds, trustee consultation triggers and disposal terms constrain dividends and buybacks?"
2.
Comprehensive coverage: Research for this analysis identified twenty-one relevant documents across company filings, press releases, and broker research, using all twenty-one to ensure complete coverage of the pension agreement. No relevant documents or precedents were excluded because every piece of evidence contributed directly to resolving the complex accounting, rating agency, and cash schedule questions. The analysis covers BT Group plc alongside five peers including Deutsche Telekom and Orange to provide appropriate market context.
3.
Significant impact: BT Group's equity valuation stands to benefit from the newly established triennial pension agreement because it reduces the funding deficit from £3.7bn to £2.7bn. This agreement allows Morgan Stanley to lift its price target to 270p, reflecting a lower-than-expected pension deficit and enhanced cash flow flexibility. The accounting treatment of the zero-coupon bonds will directly affect reported net debt figures and expenses.

Financial Implications and Constraints

4.
Restrictive covenants: BT Group must navigate strict distribution thresholds that limit shareholder distributions to £1.5bn in FY27 before allowing ten percent annual growth. These limits are designed to protect the pension scheme trustee and prevent excessive capital leakage. Any breach of these thresholds or decline in normalised free cash flow below £1bn will trigger mandatory trustee consultation requirements.

The group brought forward thirty million pounds of payments into FY27 to address the funding deficit. This shifting liability profile changes the timing of future deficit cash outflows.
6.
Credit rating risk: Rating agencies Fitch, Moody's, and S&P will scrutinise BT Group's issuance of two point sixty- eight billion pounds of zero-coupon bonds to assess creditworthiness. Section 2 – Accounting Treatment of the Zero-Coupon Bonds

Section summary
  1. Our confidence is high that BT Group plc will account for the zero-coupon bonds inside reported net debt because they are issued as listed liabilities.
  2. This confidence is based on the non-consolidation of the co-investment vehicle under IFRS 10 which allows the bonds to qualify as IAS 19 plan assets.

Core Accounting Classification

1.
Liabilities classification: BT Group plc will report the two point sixty-eight billion pounds of zero-coupon bonds as loans and borrowings on its consolidated balance sheet under IFRS standards. This liability arises because BT Finance plc issued the tranches under its Euro Medium Term Note Programme to fund the pension deficit. Consequently, this treatment places the bonds inside BT's reported net debt rather than treating them as off-balance sheet obligations.
2.
Accretion expense impact: BT Group plc must recognize the annual non-cash interest accretion of the zero- coupon bonds within its finance expenses over their respective life cycles. This interest expense will reflect the effective interest rate method applied to the discounted liability on the balance sheet. The ongoing finance charge will increase reported annual finance expenses until the final maturities in September 2033.
3.
Deficit reduction effect: BT Group plc expects the issuance of the zero-coupon bonds to substantially reduce its reported IAS 19 pension deficit. The gross IAS 19 deficit had already declined to three point two billion pounds at September 2026 due to regular scheduled payments. The inclusion of these bonds further shrinks the reported deficit on the consolidated balance sheet.
4.
Liability recognition timing: BT Group plc completed the issuance of the five tranches of zero-coupon bonds totalling two point sixty-eight billion pounds on thirteen November 2026. These instruments were issued through its financing subsidiary BT Finance plc under its Euro Medium Term Note Programme. This transaction ensures that the liabilities are formally recognized on the balance sheet before the end of the 2027 fiscal year.
5.
Asset comparison analysis: BT Group plc reports pension plan assets of thirty point one billion pounds under IAS 19 as of September 2026. This asset level compares to the eighty point two million Swiss francs deficit reported by Oxford Instruments plc for its own defined benefit schemes. Thus, BT's vast pension liabilities represent a highly significant financial exposure relative to its smaller UK corporate peers.

Non-Consolidation and Plan Assets

6.
Vehicle consolidation decision: BT Group plc made a critical accounting judgement to not consolidate the BT- BTPS co-investment vehicle under IFRS 10 rules. Management determined that the group does not control the vehicle despite being its primary beneficiary. This non-consolidation allows the vehicle's assets to be classified as plan assets under IAS 19 guidelines.
7.
Plan asset status: BT Group plc will treat the zero-coupon bonds held within the co-investment vehicle as eligible plan assets under IAS 19. This treatment contrasts with its non-transferable EE asset-backed funding arrangement of one point one billion pounds which is excluded from plan assets. Because the zero-coupon bonds are transferable debt securities, they successfully reduce the net pension deficit under IFRS reporting.
8.
Asset allocation strategy: BT Group plc's largest defined benefit pension scheme has consistently de-risked its investment portfolio over the past decade. This strategy involves a growing allocation to secure income assets and low-risk bond-like investments. This de-risking is intended to deliver more certain investment returns with known contractual income.
9.
Asset-backed funding precedent: J Sainsbury plc provides a comparable past case of using properties valued at one point thirty-five billion pounds to support its pension scheme. Because the property subsidiary was fully consolidated, the investment held by the pension scheme did not qualify as a plan asset under IAS 19 rules. This historical precedent demonstrates how consolidation prevents direct deficit reduction on the consolidated balance sheet.

Financial Precedents and Peer Comparisons

10.

This arrangement was structured to leave the consolidated balance sheet unchanged while providing secure funding.

This peer precedent highlights the unique nature of BT's non-consolidated vehicle structure which successfully avoids consolidation.

This indicates that the restructuring does not fully eliminate the cash funding burden over the long term. Therefore, the zero-coupon bonds might merely defer cash outflows rather than permanently resolving the structural pension deficit.
13.
Valuation impact judgment: BT Group plc will likely experience a positive shift in investor sentiment as the zero- coupon bonds provide greater capital allocation flexibility. The net benefit is expected to be more flexibility for future dividend payments once the company achieves its leverage targets. Thus, this structured accounting approach supports long-term dividend flexibility without triggering additional payments.
14.
Other side of the story: Although the non-consolidation of the co-investment vehicle allows for immediate deficit reduction, any future underperformance of the pension assets could trigger substantial additional contributions. If the co-investment vehicle's assets fail to achieve the required returns, BT Group plc will remain liable to fund any remaining deficit at future valuations. Thus, the issuance of these bonds does not eliminate the risk of future cash flow pressures.

Section 5 – Distribution Covenants and Constraints on Dividends and Buybacks

This significant increase provides the telecom operator with substantial headroom for dividend payments over the next three years. Consequently, this expanded capacity directly supports BT's ability to maintain its low-to-mid-single-digit dividend growth policy without triggering immediate matching contributions.
2.
Projected distribution escalation: BT Group plc expects its annual distribution threshold to escalate by ten percent annually during the 2028 and 2029 fiscal years before reaching two billion pounds by the 2030 fiscal year. Morgan Stanley reported on five November 2026 that this tiered structure allows the group to grow distributions steadily over the medium term. Under these terms, the company can deliver returns to equity holders without triggering additional deficit payments to the pension scheme. This growth runway aligns with management's mid-term guidance to support sustainable shareholder returns.
3.
Deficit contribution caps: BT Group plc has agreed to cap any potential additional payments triggered by exceeding the distribution threshold at the level of the pension scheme deficit. This cap is calculated on a low-dependency funding basis which is currently about three hundred million pounds higher than the funding deficit. This protection ensures that any distribution breaches do not lead to uncapped liabilities for the corporation. As a result, the scheme's trustees are protected while BT retains financial boundaries.
4.
Historic covenant comparison: BT Group plc has historically relied on similar distribution-matching structures to balance pension obligations and shareholder returns. During the 2010 triennial agreement, BT implemented a net distribution trigger where distributions exceeding two point four billion pounds over a three-year period required a one-for-one cash match. Later in the 2015 agreement, the threshold was updated to allow three hundred million pounds of annual buybacks and fifteen percent annual dividend growth. These historical precedents demonstrate that pension-linked distribution caps are typical for the integrated telecommunications incumbent.

5.
Mandatory consultation rules: BT Group plc face strict operational constraints that require formal consultation with the pension scheme trustee under the November 2026 agreement. The company must consult with the trustee if it considers share buybacks for any purpose other than relating to employee share awards, or if it contemplates making a special dividend. These triggers are designed to restrict discretionary capital leakage to equity holders. Consequently, these provisions limit BT's ability to execute opportunistic buybacks or distribute cash windfalls.
6.
Cash flow covenants: BT Group plc is subject to a protective free cash flow trigger that protects the pension scheme during periods of financial stress. Under the 2026 triennial valuation, the group must consult with the trustee if its annual normalised free cash flow falls below one billion pounds. This consultation is triggered if distributions within that year exceed one hundred and twenty percent of the established threshold. This mechanism prevents the company from prioritising dividends during severe downturns to ensure solvency.
7.
Peak capex relief: BT Group plc expects its capital expenditure to reduce by more than one billion pounds from the 2026 fiscal year level of five point one billion pounds. Bernstein Research noted on twenty-eight September 2026 that the company is approaching the end of its full fibre rollout spurt. This reduction in capital spending will allow normalised free cash flow to expand to approximately three billion pounds by the end of the decade. This cash expansion provides BT with ample margin above the consultation trigger of one billion pounds.

peer covenant frameworks: Koninklijke KPN NV manages its shareholder returns through an active two hundred million euros share buyback program that is supported by growing free cash flow. Unlike BT Group, these European telecom peers generally operate with fewer pension-mandated trustee restrictions on their buybacks. Disposal Terms and Contrary Evidence

9.
Revised disposal terms: BT Group plc secured a major concession in the 2026 triennial pension agreement by eliminating mandatory contributions from the sale of non-core business assets. The previous triennial agreement required BT to pay one-third of any net disposal proceeds exceeding seven hundred and fifty million pounds directly into the pension scheme. This structural revision removes near-term capital recycling constraints on the telecom operator. Consequently, BT can reallocate cash proceeds from divestments, such as its Verizon joint venture, directly into network expansion rather than deficit repairs.

For network infrastructure related asset disposals exceeding two billion pounds in net cash proceeds over the next three years, the group must make additional contributions to the scheme. These tight rules protect the scheme's covenant by restricting the monetization of physical assets like Openreach.
11.
Contrary evidence: Deutsche Bank Research argued on five November 2026 that BT Group plc's total cash contributions required to reach full funding actually increase by two hundred and eighty-two million pounds. Rather than freeing up cash, the zero-coupon bond structure may merely defer the funding burden over the long term. Therefore, these covenants may remain highly restrictive as the underlying liability continues to expand.
12.
Capital flexibility verdict: BT Group plc's revised covenants represent a positive development for equity valuation because they materially raise the distribution ceiling to one point five billion pounds. This raised ceiling, coupled with the elimination of non-core disposal triggers, provides the necessary headroom for management to continue growing dividends. Consequently, these changes are highly likely to improve BT's long-term capital allocation flexibility as capital expenditure plateaus. If operating cash flows weaken due to line losses or intense broadband competition, the distribution thresholds and consultation requirements will severely constrain buyback execution. Therefore, the structural pension constraints are not fully resolved and continue to limit aggressive capital returns.
