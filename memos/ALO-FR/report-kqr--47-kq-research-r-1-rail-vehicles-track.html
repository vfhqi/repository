Section 2 – Dimension 1: Public procurement and order backlogs 20 findings

Section summary
  1. Large-scale national public rail procurement programs across core European markets are driving record backlogs, although execution timelines remain vulnerable to non-consensus operational delays.
  2. High-margin inflation-indexed bookings are steadily replacing legacy low-margin contracts, providing strong visibility for progressive operating margin expansion over the next 24 to 36 months.

European public procurement programs

1.
Expanding national networks: The German government is deploying €150 billion through Deutsche Bahn to support long-term rail modernization and network upgrades
2.
Robust Polish investments: Poland's national rail operator PKP Polskie Linie Kolejowe increased its planned tender volume to €2.7 billion, with extensive high-speed rail tenders announced under the spatially integrated Centralny Port Komunikacyjny program
3.
Fast-tracked Southern corridors: The Italian Recovery and Resilience Plan is accelerating high-speed rail links, including the €4.9 billion Verona-Padua route, to improve national infrastructure integration with trans-European networks
4.
Decarbonizing British transport: The United Kingdom's Network Rail committed to a £44 billion budget for Control Period 7, supporting projects like TransPennine Express's €1.2 billion order of mainline batteryelectric trains
5.
Liberalizing French regions: The gradual opening of French regional public service contracts is driving massive public orders for state-of-the-art regional passenger fleets

Manufacturer order backlogs

6.
Record Alstom backlog: Alstom's massive order backlog reached €104 billion as of March 2026, driven by a strong book-to-bill ratio of 1.4x over the fiscal year
7.
Surging Stadler orderbook: Stadler expanded its order backlog to CHF 33.3 billion in H1 2026, underpinned by accelerating passenger train demand across core central European nations
8.
Unprecedented Vossloh book: Vossloh's rail infrastructure order backlog crossed the €1.14 billion milestone in H1 2026, heavily boosted by the strategic acquisition of sleeper manufacturer Sateba
9.
Steady CAF momentum: CAF maintains a solid order backlog of €14.2 billion, demonstrating consistent mid-sized commercial wins and high customer retention across southern European commuter networks
10.
Strong regional player: Newag is capturing high-capacity Polish rolling stock contracts as domestic passenger operators rapidly modernize regional transit fleets

Backlog execution and margin transition

11.
Improving contract pricing: Rail manufacturers are slowly executing legacy unprofitable contracts while booking newer orders with much healthier operating margins above 20%
12.
Prudent margin protection: Alstom has embedded protective escalation clauses in 72% of its backlog to neutralize persistent input cost inflation
13.
Phasing out headwinds: Legacy loss-making contracts from the Bombardier acquisition are progressively rolling off, which should mechanically support operating margins over the next 2 years
14.
Robust indexation clauses: Stadler has secured price escalation clauses in approximately 80% of its major long-term rolling stock contracts to insulate future earnings from inflation
15.
Temporary margin pressure: Vossloh's near-term profitability remains temporarily burdened by material and logistics cost inflation that cannot be fully passed through in the current fiscal year

Non-consensus timeline risks

16.
Widespread project delays: Independent broker sweeps of Alstom's top 100 projects revealed 23 major programs experiencing demonstrable schedule slippage or delay
17.
Uncontrolled assembly issues: Production at Alstom's German plants was temporarily halted to adapt to engineering delays, highlighting the extreme operational complexity of regional platforms

Homologation bottleneck risks: Complex national regulatory requirements and safety authorization delays continue to postpone train handovers and cash collection

Section 3 – Dimension 2: Digital signalling, ERTMS, and technology shifts 17 findings

Section summary
  1. The digital signalling market has consolidated into a highly profitable triopoly where high-margin software-led solutions outperform traditional heavy rolling stock.
  2. While ambitious trans-European

mandates drive multi-billion Euro upgrades, progress is slowed by standardization friction and national technical baselines.

Competitive dynamics in European rail control

1.
Dominant signaling triopoly: The European rail control market has experienced intense consolidation, leaving Alstom, Siemens Mobility, and Hitachi Rail as the three global giants that control the vast majority of projects
2.
Strong Siemens positioning: Siemens Mobility maintains its lead in European mainline signalling and enjoys superior margins due to its highly profitable Vectron platform combined with a dominant presence in DACH rail networks
3.
Challenger Hitachi share: Hitachi Rail has expanded its wayside control footprint to reach thirty to forty percent share of the European Train Control System market following its strategic acquisition of Thales Signalling
4.
Targeted Alstom focus: Alstom ranks third globally in signalling but commands a leading fifty to sixty percent market share specifically in European onboard automatic train protection units
5.
Specialized local players: Smaller regional suppliers like AZD Praha and Mer Mec occupy narrow product niches and remain five to ten times smaller than the leading triopoly players

Margin profiles and software-led economics

6.
High software margins: Digital signalling represents Alstom's highest-margin division, delivering solid mid- teens operating margins compared to the lower margin heavy rolling stock segment
7.
Sticky service revenues: Signaling contracts create highly lucrative lifecycle-management franchises because operators outsource software maintenance and cybersecurity to the original manufacturers
8.
Favorable business mix: Manufacturers are aggressively shifting their backlog mix towards high-margin signalling and services to mechanically expand future cash conversion
9.
Escalating service demand: Industry specialists at McKinsey point out that original equipment manufacturers currently capture only twenty percent of rail maintenance, leaving substantial room for digital expansion
10.
Value-added digital platforms: Hitachi is embedding its HMAX digital asset management platform across all new fleets starting in 2027 to generate recurring high-margin SaaS revenue

Regional rollouts and regulatory friction

11.
Austrian mega-program: Austria's ÖBB-Infrastruktur awarded a landmark thirty-year interlocking modernization framework worth €2.6 billion split equally between Siemens Mobility and Hitachi Rail
12.
Cloud interlocking transition: Siemens is deploying its cloud-ready Signaling X technology in Austria, decoupling safety-critical logic from proprietary hardware to significantly increase existing track capacity
13.
Trans-European mandates: The revised Trans-European Transport Network regulations establish binding digital signaling installation deadlines of 2030 for core networks and 2050 for comprehensive networks
14.
German investment scale: Germany's long-term digital rail modernization package includes an estimated thirty billion Euro budget dedicated exclusively to ERTMS and digital interlocking technology
15.
Delayed national strategies: Deutsche Bahn was forced to withdraw its ETCS migration strategy in 2025 because operators found the retrofitting timelines and funding assumptions unfeasible
16.
Costly baseline mismatches: National infrastructure variations introduce severe retrofitting friction, as seen in Greece where upgrading regional rolling stock to the latest Baseline 4 specifications threatens to delay passenger deployments until 2030
17.
side of the story: While the long-term shift to digital signalling is structurally assured, near-term margins remain vulnerable to costly software homologation delays and if national operators slow down funding, manufacturers will face severe under-absorption of engineering costs
