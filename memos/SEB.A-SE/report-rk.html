Across the entire scope of this pre-mortem analysis on Skandinaviska Enskilda Banken, three statistical outliers and unexplained patterns emerge that warrant particular attention. These anomalies represent significant deviations from peer group norms, historical patterns, or expected relationships, and collectively underscore the structural vulnerabilities that could precipitate earnings disappointments over the forecast horizon.

The most significant outlier centers on SEB's massive and unresolved German tax exposure, which represents a potential liability of up to SEK 20 billion stemming from ongoing investigations into historical "cum-cum" dividend transactions. This figure is extraordinary both in absolute terms and relative to SEB's capital base, representing nearly 9% of the bank's SEK 220 billion equity base. What renders this exposure particularly anomalous is its binary nature and the complete absence of comparable liabilities among Nordic peers.

The outlier status is further reinforced by the specific mechanism through which this liability could crystallize, namely final German fiscal court rulings confirming historical cum-cum tax liabilities against subsidiary DSK, which would force a direct charge of up to SEK 20 billion.
