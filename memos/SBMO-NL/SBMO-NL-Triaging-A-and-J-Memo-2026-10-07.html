[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = C

Thesis. SBM Offshore is a business-model transition case in a first-rank offshore contractor, caught after its first leg has paid: the shift from owning floating production units to building them for clients who pay during construction and buy early has cut net debt by a third, doubled consensus earnings and funded a 57 percent step-up in distributions, and at EUR 34.94 the shares already pay for that. What the case now needs is a second leg, new awards at margin, which is a client decision rather than an internal force, and until it comes the modal return is 14.6 percent a year over eighteen months and 11.0 percent a year over three years, short of the 20 percent bar.

  • The change is internal, broad and real.
    • An insider succession in April 2024 was followed by a model shift to Sale & Operate and Build-Operate-Transfer contracts, thirteen speculative Fast4Ward hull orders, a pruned portfolio, two guidance raises in 2026 and a USD 2.1bn minimum distribution framework to 2031
    • Five of the twelve internal forces are strong and none of the screen's red flags is present.
    • ExxonMobil bought FPSO One Guyana for USD 2,321m in February 2026
      • Petrobras awarded SEAP I and II, USD 8bn, in May 2026
      • 45 percent of FSO Chalchi was sold to NYK.
    • FY2026 Directional revenue guidance went from about USD 6.5bn to above USD 6.9bn in May to about USD 7.6bn in August
      • EBITDA from about USD 1.8bn to about USD 1.9bn.
  • The change has travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain, which is the central fact of this record.
    • It shows in the company's resources, its choices and its actions, in its key operating metrics (pro-forma backlog USD 35.6bn, up 14 percent; uptime 98.9 percent; Directional net debt USD 3,680m, down 35 percent in six months), in its statements (H1 2026 Directional revenue USD 4,904m, up 112 percent; EBITDA USD 1,310m, up 92 percent; net profit USD 826m, up 201 percent) and in the sell-side's estimates (FactSet mean FY2026 earnings per share up 98.9 percent in twelve months to USD 5.16).
    • Nothing of it remains to propagate, so the thesis cannot rest on propagation
    • It must rest on new awards.
  • The external forces net to neutral.
    • Deepwater capital spending rises from about USD 150bn in 2026 toward USD 211bn by 2029 and the clients' breakevens are USD 20 to 35 a barrel, which is a sector-level tailwind with visibility beyond two years
    • Against it, shipyard orderbooks stand at 4.3 years of capacity and Asian yards are bidding full scopes directly, which are headwinds on the margin line the case rests on.
  • The outputs the market now values are a one-off peak.
    • FY2026 earnings per share of about USD 6.0 to 6.1 carry a USD 608m gain on One Guyana
    • Consensus falls to USD 4.47 in FY2027 and recovers to USD 4.90 in FY2028, and on our path to USD 4.6 and USD 5.1.
    • The engine beneath grows (about USD 2.8 ex-gain in FY2026 to USD 4.6 in FY2027), but the headline the framework rates falls for eighteen months, and the three-year rate from FY2025 is about 9.8 percent a year, below the 12 to 20 percent band.
  • The growth that is there is the wrong kind.
    • Over three years revenue and margin, the components investors extrapolate, net to about zero (revenue up 6.8 percent a year, the blended EBITDA margin down from 33.7 percent to about 26.6 percent by design as Turnkey rises in the mix)
    • The earnings growth comes from lower interest as USD 1,740m of project debt is repaid and from a share count falling about 4 percent a year on a USD 270m buyback.
  • The price already assumes the first leg.
    • At EUR 34.94 (USD 39.81 on the block) the shares stand at 8.40x 24-month-forward earnings, the 66th percentile of the stock's five-year range against a median of 7.68x, after a 69 percent rise in twelve months
    • The six-month return of −2.6 percent since the SEAP award says the price has stopped moving on delivered news.
    • The One Guyana sale, the SEAP awards, both guidance raises, the distribution framework, the deleveraging and most of Longtail are in the price
    • A second award a year and a multiple above 9x are not.
  • The return does not clear the bar on the modal path.
    • With the multiple held at 8.5x and a dividend yield of about 3 percent a year, the 24-month-forward earnings rising from USD 4.74 to about USD 5.60 and USD 5.95 give 14.6 percent a year over eighteen months and 11.0 percent a year over three years
    • The eighteen-month figure borrows from the window rolling past the FY2027 trough, so the three-year horizon binds.
    • The bar is cleared only if the multiple re-rates toward the ten-year median of 9.70x (24.9 percent a year over eighteen months), which we judge possible at about 35 percent, not probable, or below a price of about EUR 28.
  • The pattern is 'Major Corporate Change', crisply as a description and late as an entry.
    • Every element of the pattern is present and evidenced
    • The pattern is at full output, which is its last stage, and the framework's pattern pays best at input plus early output.
    • 'Compounder' is the secondary fit, because the sell-side models from booked backlog and each award is an upgrade, but that mechanism is a forecasting choice rather than a mechanical error.

  • The case is therefore material to the company and not yet material to the return.

    • The chain holds through the inputs and the delivered outputs and weakens at the return, where it depends on awards not yet won and a re-rating we do not make modal.

  • Rating = C, because the change is material to the company (its balance sheet, its cash cycle and its estimates have all moved) but not yet material enough to the return: 'Change Forces' are a B, 'Financial Outputs' a C and the 'Modal return' a D, and the Building Block's question, whether the change drives a 20 percent a year total shareholder return in the modal case, is answered no at this price.

    • The parts combine as a chain that holds through the first two links and weakens at the third: strong inputs, delivered outputs, a return that depends on a re-rating we make possible rather than probable.
    • Not B, because no horizon clears the bar without the re-rating
    • Not D, because the inputs are real and the eighteen-month return is a C on its own.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The strongest counter is that SBM is a false friend: the near-term windfalls (the USD 608m One Guyana gain, Sale & Operate milestones pulled forward) mask structural decay.
    • Early buyouts strip the leased annuity (three Guyana units already bought out)
      • The Lease & Operate EBITDA margin is already 46 percent from 50 percent and ING models lease gross margins at 45 to 46 percent by 2030-31
      • The mix shifts to lump-sum construction at 15 to 20 percent margins won on price (SEAP at USD 3.7bn against MODEC's USD 5.3bn), executed in Chinese yards at nine times their historical output, with five builds at once and a six-project benchmark just abandoned
      • The company's own history holds a 35 percent overrun (Frade, 2008) and a USD 470m termination (Yme, 2013) from the last time it bid aggressively into an upcycle.
    • The IFRS accounts show USD 253m more profit than the cash-aligned Directional ones, the equity is lifted USD 1,885m by finance-lease treatment, and the bear multiple is the European peers' 3x to 5x EV/EBITDA, which gives Morgan Stanley's bear case of EUR 15.30, 57 percent downside.
    • We concede the one-off peak, the annuity erosion, the mix-driven margin decline, the earnings-basis opacity, the yard concentration and the price-led SEAP win
      • Those are why the 'Investment Thesis' is a C and the 'Modal return' a D.
    • We do not concede decay: the backlog is USD 35.6bn, contracted to 2050 with USD 8.0bn of net cash in it
      • The Lease & Operate book is more than 95 percent fixed-price
      • The clients' breakevens of USD 20 to 35 a barrel mean they keep producing and paying through a downturn
      • Uptime of 98.9 percent is the reason those clients re-hire
      • The client that buys the vessels early keeps SBM as operator and keeps ordering (Longtail)
      • And the deleveraging to 1.6x is cash in the bank, not an accounting view.
    • The counter-case sets the floor on the multiple and the ceiling on the rating
      • It does not describe a business in decay, and it does not make us bearish.
