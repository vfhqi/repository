1.
Unfavorable pricing: Greek regulator EETT proposes to cut fiber wholesale access rates by 10% to 13% while raising copper-based wholesale tariffs by 4% to 5% to speed up migration. According to Deutsche Bank Research on April 24, 2026, this regulatory framework aims to incentivize consumers to migrate quickly from copper to fiber connectivity. Consequently, OTE expects these pricing changes will reshape its fixed-line revenue mix by late 2026 as copper revenues decline.
2.
Delayed clearance: The Hellenic Telecommunications Organization is currently waiting for the European Commission to approve EETT's proposed tariff adjustments before implementation. According to Deutsche Bank Research on April 24, 2026, these proposals match market expectations but remain pending formal finalization. Management expects that the implementation of these tariffs will begin driving FTTH uptake by early 2027 once approvals are completed.
3.
Steady transition: Hellenic Telecom expects its domestic wholesale revenues to remain stable as competitor fiber rollouts expand. According to Deutsche Bank Research on April 24, 2026, OTE has signed strategic wholesale agreements with key market players like Nova and Vodafone Greece to mitigate copper declines. These mutual infrastructure agreements will support greater wholesale fiber utilization going forward as competitors migrate.
4.
Progressive regulations: Hellenic Telecom's regulatory landscape benefits from a progressive stance taken by domestic authorities. Deutsche Bank Research reported on March 6, 2026, that the Greek regulator remains firm but supportive of OTE's digital infrastructure investments. This positive environment ensures that wholesale tariff cuts will not jeopardize long-term returns on OTE's fiber rollouts over the next three years.

This comparable Spanish case suggests that regulated wholesale pricing can support infrastructure investments while protecting incumbent market shares.

These peer benchmarks indicate that regulators design wholesale structures to align markets with European standards and accelerate digital transition.

Comprehensive research scope: This analysis evaluates the upcoming spectrum auction in Greece by utilizing six distinct documents spanning broker research and company filings. 2.
Elevated historical outlays: Hellenic Telecom originally secured its key 900 MHz and 1800 MHz spectrum allocations back in 2011 for a combined cost of EUR 119m. Specifically, the operator paid EUR 78m for the 900 MHz band and EUR 41m for the 1800 MHz band. These historical licenses are scheduled to expire in September and November 2027 respectively.
3.
Favorable regulatory terms: Greek regulator EETT published a key spectrum consultation proposing to extend the new licenses to 20-year or indefinite terms. 4.
Substantial renewal discount: Deutsche Bank Research estimates that OTE will pay approximately EUR 78m in total to renew its mobile spectrum licenses. This expected capital cost represents a significant 35% discount compared to the original historical license outlay of EUR 119m. OTE intends to fully fund this cash commitment using a EUR 125m domestic tax exemption generated by its Telekom Romania Mobile disposal.
5.
Contrary evidence: Hellenic Telecom could face higher spectrum acquisition costs if EETT executes an open multi-round auction instead of a direct renewal. Although Deutsche Bank Research expects a simple renewal, EETT has proposed a two-stage process if spectrum demand exceeds available supply. Any competitive bidding from rivals would eliminate the anticipated EUR 41m savings and threaten OTE's projected cash flow targets.
6.
Negligible entry threat: Public Power Corporation is highly unlikely to compete against the three incumbent operators for the 900 MHz and 1800 MHz spectrum blocks. The utility company's chief executive officer explicitly stated that PPC has no interest in entering the retail mobile market in Greece. Consequently, OTE faces almost zero risk of a fourth mobile competitor driving up spectrum prices during the 2027 renewal process.
