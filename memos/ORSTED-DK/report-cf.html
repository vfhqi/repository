  8. Persistent interest rate headwinds and regulatory policy shifts could undermine project returns and delay Orsted's long-term pipeline execution.

Demand

1.
Expanding volumes: Orsted is positioned to benefit from a substantial expansion of European offshore wind capacity as regional green targets rise. Nine North Sea nations have committed to build out 300 GW of offshore wind by 2050 compared to 38 GW installed in 2025. Ambitious regional scaling highlights the strong, long-term structural demand across core markets. Higher generation volumes will directly support future earnings as new projects enter operation.
2.
Strong demand: High European power prices have increased customer demand for long-term power purchase agreements. Corporate utility demand remains particularly strong in the United States due to fast-growing data center electricity needs. Orsted continues to capture attractive corporate offtake agreements for its onshore wind and solar assets like the Blackwater Solar project. Long-term contracted revenues mitigate wholesale price volatility and stabilize future earnings.
3.
Abundant opportunities: Over 37 GW of offshore wind auctions are scheduled over the next twelve months in Orsted's core markets. Upcoming rounds in Germany, Poland, and the UK offer substantial capacity to developers. Upcoming tenders present material opportunities to secure attractive index-linked revenue support agreements. Winning value-accretive capacity in these rounds remains crucial to reboot longterm growth.
4.
Declining protection: Orsted faces a significant reduction in revenues as legacy wind assets progressively exit their subsidy schemes. The developer is projected to lose DKK 11 bn of subsidies between 2030 and 2040. Expired contracts expose a larger share of generation to volatile spot power prices, including German assets that exited subsidies in 2026. Depressed merchant prices would directly hurt net income unless compensated by new projects.

Competition Intense competition

European offshore wind auctions remain highly competitive despite recent sector-wide challenges. Recent tenders continue to attract aggressive participation from major developers including Vattenfall, RWE, Iberdrola, and TotalEnergies. Competitive bidding pressure continues to compress implied project internal rates of return to around four percent, as seen in Denmark's latest tender. Low returns limit the financial viability of new developments and threaten earnings.

5.
Rational bidding: Orsted maintains a highly disciplined bidding strategy to protect its targeted project returns. Orsted lost both the North Sea Mid and Hesselø tenders in Denmark to Vattenfall due to disciplined bidding on returns. Walking away from low-yielding tenders protects the balance sheet from value destruction. However, failing to win capacity leaves a substantial gap in the medium-term pipeline.
6.
Aggressive expansion: Peer developers continue to build out massive competitive renewable portfolios. Iberdrola reported nearly 3,200 MW of operational offshore wind capacity and has a further 2,700 MW under construction. Similarly, RWE secured contracts for five British offshore projects totaling up to 6.9 GW. Strong competitor execution highlights the challenging competitive landscape Orsted faces to maintain its market-leading position.
7.
Uneven profitability: Regional returns vary significantly across Orsted's global offshore wind portfolio. The UK remains the strongest value creator with a ROIC-WACC spread of ~7.6% (calculated) from a 13.6% project internal rate of return. By contrast, the US is the only region currently destroying value on Bernstein's estimates with a negative spread. Subdued returns across the global portfolio highlight the challenge of creating value from the pipeline.

Disruption Technological challenges

Rapid wind turbine technology upsizing has created significant operational and quality risks. Wind turbine manufacturers are placing greater emphasis on reliability after rapid upsizing led to quality issues. Quality failures and compressed development cycles remain key challenges for the industry. Unplanned maintenance and project delays directly increase operating costs and drag on developer margins.

8.
Improved discipline: Turbine manufacturers are reassessing designs and restoring staged testing to address warranty risks. Vestas introduced the V182-7.2 MW turbine featuring improved business case certainty and pricing power. Stricter contract terms and cost indexation help protect turbine suppliers from inflation. Higher pricing discipline from major suppliers ultimately increases capital expenditure requirements for developers.
9.
Shifted models: European offshore wind development is shifting from traditional subsidy-free tenders toward robust revenue-support frameworks. Proposed German reforms acknowledge that purely merchant projects are no longer sufficiently attractive to developers. Regulators are introducing fallback contracts for difference mechanisms to protect multi-billion euro investments. Revenue support schemes reduce merchant power price risks and support stable project economics.
10.
Decline in nearshore sites: Offshore wind projects are moving farther from shore and into deeper waters. This geographic shift increases the need for specialized installation and support vessels during construction. Deep-water and floating wind technologies represent the next major phase of industry growth. However, high installation complexity and immature supply chains elevate execution risks and project capex.

Regulation

Core European markets like Denmark, Germany, and the Netherlands are adopting two-sided contracts for difference structures. These inflation-indexed contract designs insulate developers from merchant power price risks. Stable regulatory frameworks directly support the timely execution of Orsted's growth plans.

The revised framework establishes a flexible annual auction corridor starting in 2027 to align with grid capacity. This policy shift acknowledges the economic challenges of purely merchant offshore wind developments. While the reform is constructive, the legislative process will take time before driving Orsted's earnings.
13.
Policy uncertainty: The UK remains a critical market but faces near-term policy shifts. The UK government intends to pause before Allocation Round 9 to assess changes to the CfD scheme for consumers. This review could push the next major auction into 2028, delaying final investment decisions. Delaying these crucial rounds increases the uncertainty surrounding the timeline for Orsted's Hornsea 4 project.
