[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = B

Thesis. Adyen is a top-decile payments compounder whose shares fell 40 percent in twelve months and now sit at the lowest 24-month-forward multiple in the stock's history, while the business kept growing net revenue at 21 percent constant currency and converting 86 percent of EBITDA to cash. At EUR 870.0 the market is paying for the bear case, low-teens earnings growth and a capped margin, so the shares return about 12 percent a year over three years with no re-rating at all, and about 24 percent a year over eighteen months if three more prints confirm that organic growth has held at or above 20 percent and the market pays 21x rather than 15x for it. The case turns on that re-rating, which is the leg least in the company's control.

  • The change is the company's own cycle turning, and it is modest.
    • The Cash App and eBay volume roll-offs that depressed reported growth through 2025 have lapped, and the last two quarters show the underlying engine.
    • Q2 2026 net revenue grew 22 percent constant currency, from 20 percent in Q1
      • Digital, the largest pillar, re-accelerated from 9 percent constant currency in Q4 2025 to 13 percent in Q1 and 16 percent in Q2 2026
    • Q2 2026 processed volume was EUR 421.7 billion, +26 percent, 3 percent above consensus
      • The net take rate was flat quarter on quarter at 16.2 basis points after falling from 17.1 basis points over the prior three quarters
    • No external force is strong enough to carry a case on its own: demand is structural digitisation at a mid-single-digit to low-double-digit pace, competition is tightening at the enterprise end, regulation is a slow, mild headwind on cross-border margins, and FX turns from a 2 percent headwind in H1 2026 to a 1 percent tailwind in H2
  • The internal change is a step into adjacent software, not a transformation.
    • The strategy changed in kind on 1-Jul-26, when the company made the first acquisitions in its history: EUR 750 million for Talon.One (loyalty) and USD 335 million for Orb (usage-based billing), run under an incubator model.
    • Beside it, the widest product cadence in the company's history: Adyen Agentic (June 2026), Intelligent Money Movement, Uplift and Dynamic Identification, embedded finance with Issuing volume up eightfold and Capital loans doubled in 2025 and doubled again in H1 2026
    • There is no chief-executive change (the co-chief executives were reappointed to 2030), the FY2026 guidance rise from 20 to 22 percent to 21 to 23 percent constant currency was wholly the inorganic point, no cash is being returned, and the finance director left as a surprise on 31-Aug-26 with an interim in place.
      • The framework's screen rule (three strong forces, or chief-executive change plus a guidance increase) is not met
  • 'Financial Outputs' are inside the framework's 12 to 20 percent band on every horizon, and the market is not yet learning it.
    • On our own path (no Viewforth model exists; our estimate stands in) earnings per share grow from EUR 33.72 in FY2025 to about EUR 38.1 in FY2026, EUR 44.7 in FY2027 and EUR 53.0 in FY2028, 16.3 percent a year, with net revenue compounding at 19.6 percent and the EBITDA margin flat at about 51.7 percent in FY2026 before rising to 54.5 percent by FY2028.
    • Consensus sits above us at EUR 38.80, EUR 47.20 and EUR 58.12, which is 15.1 percent to FY2026, 18.3 percent a year to FY2027 and 19.9 percent a year to FY2028 on the reported base
      • The gap is our lower revenue path (a point a year of deceleration as EMEA matures), a margin of 54.5 rather than 55.6 percent, and financial income held flat rather than growing as policy rates fall
    • Sell-side upgrades are unlikely in the next six to twelve months: consensus already sits at the guidance midpoint on revenue and above it on margin, the company's record is one of resets rather than raises, and revisions are flat (FY2026 consensus EPS +0.2 percent over three months after -6.3 percent over twelve)
  • The re-acceleration has travelled four of the six layers of the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain.
    • It shows in the company's choices (the H2 2023 pricing reset), its actions, its key operating metrics (volume, Digital growth, the take rate) and its financial statements (H1 2026 net revenue 1 percent above consensus).
    • It has not reached the sell-side's estimates, which are flat, or the share price, whose relative strength sits at the 11th percentile of the universe.
    • The acquisitions are at the actions layer with no output yet.
  • What it costs: nothing today on the stock's own history, and no discount against its peers.
    • At EUR 870.0 the shares are 15.7x the dashboard's 24-month-forward consensus EPS of EUR 55.57 and 17.1x our own 24-month-forward EPS of EUR 50.9.
    • That is the 0th percentile of the stock's five-year range (low 15.04x, median 33.16x, 10th percentile 19.36x) and below the ten-year "crash" band of 24.90x
    • Against the comparison set it is the highest usable multiple (Wise 14.5x, Nexi 6.0x, Worldline 3.3x), and on an enterprise value that counts only the roughly EUR 5.0 billion of unrestricted cash the shares are about 15x consensus FY2026 EBITDA against a payments sector at about 9x forward EV to EBITDA.
      • The cheapness is an own-history cheapness, not a relative one
  • What we think it returns: about 24 percent a year over eighteen months and about 19 percent a year over three years, on a prudently blended multiple.
    • We value the shares at 18.6x the earnings two years beyond each horizon, a blend of an uncleared branch at 15x with 40 percent odds and a cleared branch at 21x with 60 percent odds
    • The full convention, the one-year-forward footing beside it, and the drop-through sensitivity are set out under 'Modal return' below.
    • Earnings growth carries about two thirds of the return
      • The cash leg is nil, because there is no dividend and no buyback and management calls near-term capital returns highly unlikely
    • With the multiple held at 17.1x on our earnings the return is 17.1 percent a year over eighteen months and 16.1 percent over three years, a C
      • At 15x, the uncleared branch, it is 7.3 percent and 11.2 percent a year, a D
      • At 21x, the cleared branch alone, 34.3 percent and 24.3 percent a year, an A over eighteen months.
      • The multiple is the swing factor and it moves the rating from D to B to A across the branches
  • The pattern it fits is 'OSHQC CfC Clearing', recognisably and not crisply.
    • The quality and the oversold conditions are met beyond argument
    • The clearing conditions are met partly, because the concern is a cluster of four (commoditisation and Shopify in Europe, a guidance-reset record, an investment and M&A phase with a finance-director vacancy, an AI-sector sell-off read across) rather than one named concern, and one half-year is the whole of the clearing evidence against a framework that assumes a concern is more persistent than it looks.
  • Where the chain holds and where it breaks.
    • It holds from the business's mechanics (cohort wallet-share expansion from under 20 percent to over 40 percent over twelve years; a fixed personnel cost base; churn under 1 percent) through the outputs to an earnings path inside the band.
    • It breaks at the market: the price is where it is because the bear case (Europe saturating, the take rate compressing below 15.5 basis points, the margin capped near 50 percent) is what is priced, and the return at the bar needs the market to stop pricing it.
    • It also breaks, on the framework's own count, at breadth: 46 percent of the scoped attributes are at C and eight sit at D, so the case is broadly mediocre in the framework's arithmetic even though its centre is not.

  • What the case therefore is: a compounder-at-a-trough case with a modest change attached, compelling on the arithmetic and not yet on the pattern.

    • The three-year return without any re-rating is 12 percent a year from a floor multiple on a business with no debt, which bounds the downside in a way most C cases do not
    • The return at the bar needs one more half-year of organic growth at or above 20 percent and a market willing to pay for it.

  • Rating = B, because the expected change is material on the framework's test: the earnings engine sits inside the 12 to 20 percent band on every horizon, the 'Modal return' clears 20 percent a year over eighteen months and reaches 19.4 percent over three years on a multiple below the stock's own ten-year crash band, and the price asks nothing for it.

    • The parts combine as 'Change Forces' C, 'Financial Outputs' B and 'Modal return' B
    • The weak Element does not bind, because this is not a change case but a compounder at a trough multiple whose outputs are already in the band.
    • It is not A, because a thesis whose swing factor is a multiple the market has refused for eighteen months is not top-decile material and the change forces are modest.
    • C was not live, because the arithmetic clears the bar over eighteen months at a blended multiple of 18.6x, which is below every band the stock's own history offers.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The bear case in its strongest form is that Adyen is a commoditising incumbent, not an oversold compounder.
    • Card acquiring is commoditising from underneath: Mastercard and Visa aim for 100 percent network tokenisation by 2030, which flattens the authorisation-rate edge that earned Adyen its premium
      • Orchestration layers cut switching costs
      • Stripe matches the licences over time.
    • Europe is mature: Adyen already holds about 32 percent of European e-commerce excluding Amazon and about 50 percent of the enterprise segment, EMEA (55 percent of net revenue) slowed from 21 percent to 15 percent in a year, and Shopify's Shop Pay on Stripe rails is taking European enterprise checkout with over 30 brand migrations on Rothschild & Co Redburn's channel checks.
    • On that reading core Digital net revenue compounds at 8 percent to 2028, the take rate compresses below 15.5 basis points, the company's first M&A is a defensive reaction to a saturating organic market (Morgan Stanley's phrase), and the finance-director exit after two years of resets is a tell.
    • EPS then grows in the low teens, the multiple compresses to 16x on a lower EPS, and the fair value trends towards Jefferies' bear value of EUR 539
      • An optically cheap valuation rarely presents a floor when terminal-value concerns persist.

    • The evidence for it is real: Digital grew 9 percent constant currency in Q4 2025

      • The take rate fell from 17.1 to 16.2 basis points in three quarters
      • The company missed four of nine quarters since 2024
      • The Q2 2026 acceleration coincides with the easiest comparison.

    • What we concede: European maturity is real and our path carries it (EMEA at 13 to 15 percent, group growth slowing a point a year)

      • The take rate will not rise
      • The multiple's floor is not a bound, and a 20 to 30 percent mark-to-market fall on the multiple alone is possible
      • And the three masking mechanisms the risks research names (mix-driven take-rate beats hiding volume loss, interest income hiding core deceleration, constant-currency growth hiding unit-economics deterioration) are real enough to monitor.

    • Why we hold our view: the bear's near-term predictions were tested in H1 2026 and failed in the clearing direction.

    • Digital re-accelerated from 9 to 16 percent, the take rate was flat sequentially, and volume (+24 percent) outgrew net revenue (+21 percent constant currency), which is the opposite of mix masking volume loss.
    • Shopify has begun moving European volume from Stripe towards Adyen because it now reads Stripe as a competitor, and the company reported no European enterprise churn to Checkout.com in 2026.
    • Even the bear's own FY2027 EPS of EUR 43.40 is +13 percent a year from FY2025 and puts the shares at 20x that figure, which is the ten-year crash band, not below it
      • The bear case describes a C return from here, not a loss, unless the multiple falls below anything the stock has traded at.
    • The bear case is the reason the multiple is 15x.
    • On the last two quarters' evidence it is not the modal path.
    • Where it would win: organic growth below 18 percent for two quarters, EMEA below 12 percent for two quarters, or the take rate below 15.5 basis points sustained.

Political and regulatory: is a change moving demand, pricing or cost? (KQ 4, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because regulation is shifting from interchange (which Adyen passes through) to network fees and cross-border corridors (where some of Adyen's margin sits), a slow, mild headwind rather than a moving force.
  • Live changes: the European Commission is surveying scheme fees
    • The UK Payment Systems Regulator is proposing caps on UK-EEA cross-border interchange
    • New Zealand caps from May 2026
    • The Reserve Bank of Australia is cutting domestic interchange caps and ending surcharging from 1-Oct-26
    • A US Regulation II proposal cuts the debit cap by about 30 percent
    • The Credit Card Competition Act carries a 20 to 30 percent probability on Jefferies' reading.
    • The Illinois interchange prohibition was blocked by a federal court on 1-Jun-26.
  • Pass-through and the live risk: Interchange++ pricing passes scheme costs through, so interchange cuts do not touch Adyen's take.
    • The live risk is localisation: a former Visa executive warns that as merchants set up local entities in India, Malaysia and Brazil, high-margin cross-border FX markups of 2 to 4 percent are localised away.
  • Direction: mild headwind.
    • Not D, because the company's licences turn fragmentation into an advantage (India OPA and cross-border aggregator licences; DORA alignment)
    • Not B, because no regulatory change is moving demand or pricing in Adyen's favour.
