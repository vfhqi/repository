EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Montana Aerospace faces no active published short-seller reports or public activist letters, and direct short interest data remains highly restricted or unavailable in public tracking systems. Despite this absence of organized short campaigns, the comprehensive bear case analysis reveals substantial structural vulnerabilities centered on execution risk, working capital inflation, and governance transitions that collectively warrant a cautious hold-and-watch investment stance rather than outright rejection or high-conviction progression. The most critical bear cases cluster around four interconnected themes. First, the stock dropped 20% year-todate in early 2024 and underperformed European aerospace and defense peers who gained 24%, signaling market skepticism about near-term execution. Second, trade working capital rose significantly to EUR 343 million in H1 2026, driven by lower factoring utilization to manage potential customer default risks, creating persistent cash conversion headwinds. Third, former employees highlight significant leadership churn as a severe risk to execution consistency, compounded by a very slim corporate head office that lacks strong central coordination capabilities across decentralized international manufacturing sites. Fourth, former CEO Kai Arndt resigned unexpectedly in April 2026, while Michael Tojner's Montana Tech controls over 44% of voting rights, presenting substantial related-party influence risk and governance concentration concerns.

The winning pre-mortem hypothesis identified through the Analysis of Competing Hypotheses matrix is the FALSE FRIEND profile, which exhibits the fewest hard inconsistencies across fifteen critical operational evidence inputs. This deceptive scenario posits that massive backlogs mask underlying cash conversion constraints during production ramps, while falling operational cash despite rising organic net sales growth supports this narrative.

The FALSE FRIEND profile outperforms both standard bullish and bearish cases because the YES profile carries seven hard operational inconsistencies regarding working capital and governance, while the NO profile shows seven hard structural inconsistencies because sales, backlog, and margins continue expanding. This verdict reflects the paradox that Montana Aerospace demonstrates strong topline momentum and margin expansion while simultaneously experiencing deteriorating cash generation and governance stability.

The highest-plausibility failure modes span revenue, margin, execution, and false-friend vectors. On the revenue side, unexpected OEM price negotiations could trigger a forced 5% contract pricing discount on mature aerostructures platforms, with early warning signals visible through monitoring contract RFQ pricing trends. Margin dilution risks concentrate in persistent local labor wage inflation threatening budgeted operating profit margins in Romania and Vietnam, with rising average factory payroll costs signaling direct best-cost country margin erosion.

Execution bottlenecks center on localized component quality failures prompting immediate contract termination actions from major commercial aerospace customers, with recurrent component defect notices serving as critical operational warning signals. The integration challenge remains acute as difficult titanium composite assembly integration drives unexpected post-merger margin dilution at acquired ASCO sites, with monthly ASCO scrap rate data highlighting persistent manufacturing execution friction.

The key false friends that could mislead investors include elevated order backlogs and aggressive inventory accumulation. Growing order backlog figures falsely imply immediate cash generation while capital intensity remains structurally elevated, with persistent trade working capital inflation exposing the backlog's cash flow limitations. Similarly, aggressive buffer inventory accumulation falsely signals accelerating customer demand ahead of industry-wide destocking cycles, with rising finished goods inventory levels indicating upcoming customer delivery halts.

These misleading operational indicators create the dangerous illusion of commercial momentum while masking fundamental cash conversion weaknesses that define the FALSE FRIEND hypothesis profile.

The net currency verdict reveals Montana Aerospace operates with a structural long US dollar position that acts as an operational tailwind during dollar strength but poses severe non-cash balance sheet headwind risks during depreciation cycles. The company maintains a long dollar position of thirty percent, equating to over EUR 300 million in annual net sales, while management utilizes conservative currency planning rate assumptions of 1.18 to 1.19 for financial forecasts.

However, USD depreciation historically triggered substantial non-cash net foreign exchange losses of EUR 28 million during fiscal year 2025, driven by balance sheet revaluation of intercompany loans of EUR 300 million during US dollar depreciation cycles. The currency exposure becomes particularly problematic when Montana Aerospace represents an outlier as 95% of commercial sales are USDdenominated, compared to peer Senior plc with only 62% of sales originating in North America.

This extreme concentration magnifies both upside and downside volatility, though approximately 70% of total commercial sales are naturally hedged by local purchases, while the remaining 30% presents an unhedged long USD position without corresponding local manufacturing expenses.

The largest cross-part risks emerge from the interaction between decentralized operational execution, severe customer concentration, and inadequate hedging discipline. Deliberate OEM supplier diversification erodes historical sole-source positions on complex movable wing flap assemblies, threatening the core vertical integration thesis. This vulnerability compounds with machinery underutilization at Vietnam Da Nang creating high localized depreciation charges that suppress total Aerostructures margin performance, particularly if capacity utilization drops below 60% as monitored through local plant electricity consumption.

The governance transition risk crystallizes through the sudden resignation of CEO Kai Arndt in April 2026 signaling friction in leadership, compounded by Michael Tojner assuming the Executive Chairman role limiting independent oversight, which eliminates traditional board check balances during a critical production ramp phase. These risks converge most dangerously in the scenario where underlying operating cash flow is insufficient to fund organic growth capex when Energy segment proceeds are excluded, while major customers demand significant mature platform discounts that contradict projected vertical integration margin expansion.

The investment progression recommendation reflects these compounding vulnerabilities. We estimate the aggregated failure probability is medium due to the persistent cash flow gap, and recommend a hold-and-watch stance rather than progressing the idea to a high-conviction buy. Priority data gaps include segment-level reporting on whether the Da Nang plant is currently diluting margins, and better visibility on inflation escalators adjusting pricing under contract terms. Critical re-evaluation triggers encompass the appointment of a qualified independent permanent CEO triggering a bullish re-evaluation, while cash conversion rate falling below 40% triggering an automatic pass. The ultimate verdict acknowledges that Montana Aerospace exhibits genuine operational strengths in vertical integration and market positioning, but these advantages are currently overshadowed by persistent cash conversion weakness, governance instability, and extreme currency concentration that collectively justify a skeptical monitoring posture until management demonstrates sustained improvement across these fundamental execution dimensions.
