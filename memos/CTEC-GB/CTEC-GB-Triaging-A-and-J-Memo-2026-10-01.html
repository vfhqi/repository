[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = B

Thesis. Convatec is a solid recurring-consumables business whose second act, 6 to 8 percent organic growth and a 24 to 26 percent margin by 2027, is priced as if it had already failed. At 217.0p the shares trade at 11.6x the earnings two years out, the lowest multiple of five years, so the cash and the earnings alone pay about 14 percent a year and the odds-weighted return is about 21 percent if one demanding half-year is delivered. The case turns on the FY2026 results of 23 Feb 2027.

  • The change forces are internal, mature and still working; the external backdrop is a mild headwind.
    • The internal forces pass the framework's screen with room to spare: mid-term guidance raised to 6 to 8 percent organic growth from 2027 with a 24 to 26 percent margin, standards lifted through automation and shared services, sixteen product launches in three waves, and $500m of buybacks across 2025 and 2026 after none in 2021 to 2023.
    • But this is the second act of a program that has run since 2019, so most of what it has produced is already in the numbers, and the two newest internal changes are a continuity chief executive and a vacant finance seat.
    • Outside the company, demand is structural and slow at 4 to 5 percent a year, the US payer has already cut one line's price by 85 percent and returns in 2028, and the raw-material hedge rolls off in 2027
    • the one external force turning in the company's favour is the InnovaMatrix hit leaving the base.
  • The outputs are improving on every horizon, and the earnings path sits inside the framework's band.
    • Revenue growth turns up from a 1.8 percent organic trough in H1 2026 to 6 to 8 percent guided for H2 2026 and for 2027
    • the adjusted operating margin has risen 460 basis points since 2021 to 22.3 percent and is guided to at least 23.0 percent in FY2026 and 24 to 26 percent by 2027
    • leverage returns to 2.0x while $200m is bought back
    • cash conversion dips in 2026 on a planned capital-expenditure peak and recovers from 2027.
    • Our modal earnings per share grow about 13 percent in FY2026, 14 percent in FY2027 and 12.9 percent a year to FY2028, revenue contributing 5.7 points a year, margin 3.3, interest and buybacks 3.4
    • the parts reconcile.
  • The change has travelled the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain to the operating metrics and the first half of the financial statements; the sell-side layer has barely moved.
    • Esteem Body runs at $60m annualised and 15 percent of its segment, ConvaFoam holds about 6 percent of the global foam market, Infusion Care grew 7.4 percent in H1 2026 and is contracted to double-digit growth from 2027
    • the H1 2026 margin rose 50 basis points at constant currency.
    • Consensus earnings have moved only 3 to 5 percent in twelve months, and the upgrades after the H1 results were buyback and tax arithmetic, not operating upgrades.
  • The price assumes a miss.
    • At 217.0p the shares are 11.6x our 24-month-forward earnings (11.17x on the FactSet series of 2026-09-25), the lowest of 262 weekly points over five years against a median of 16.70x, the 2nd percentile of ten years, and about a quarter below the peer median of 15.4x.
    • The shares fell 23.5 percent relative to medtech over the twelve months to Aug 2026 and did not move on a beat, a full write-off and a new buyback.
    • What is priced is a margin miss or the odds of one, 5 percent growth, and the sector's discount
    • the buyback, the amortisation roll-off, Natura Body and the Parkinson's supply contracts are not.
  • The return meets the bar at eighteen months on the odds, and the earnings leg carries it.
    • Summing the three legs once: a cash return of 4.5 percent of today's price over eighteen months, 24-month-forward earnings growth of 16.6 percent, and a re-rating from 11.56x to a blended 12.7x give about 21 percent a year, the framework's B anchor
    • over three years the same legs give about 17 to 18 percent, the top of C.
    • The re-rating is the swing: the cleared branch at 14.5x gives 33 percent a year, no re-rating gives 14 percent, both prints missed at 10.0x gives about 8 percent.
    • The plausibly worse drop-through, 2027 inflation at 6 percent unhedged with tax at 25 percent, takes the FY2027 margin to 23.2 percent instead of 24.0 and the eighteen-month return to about 11 percent a year at 12.0x, a D.
  • The chain holds at the inputs and at the outputs already printed; it breaks, if it breaks, at one link.
    • The H2 2026 margin step to about 24.6 percent from 21.2 percent in H1 is the largest sequential step the company has set, and it is to be delivered without a permanent chief financial officer.
    • The mechanics are ordinary, flat operating costs of about $477m on seasonal H2 revenue of about $1,324m at a 60 percent gross margin, and the InnovaMatrix drag halves in H2 on the company's own base
    • we give the floor 65 percent odds, not 90.
  • The pattern is 'OSHQC CfC Clearing', fit B.
    • A 'Foundations' B business whose payer took one line to zero has been priced as if the whole had been hit
    • the external cause is cleared in the accounts and the coverage threat withdrawn
    • the residual concern is internal, dated and measurable, and the earnings growth pays the holder to wait for the two prints that resolve it.

  • The case is therefore a good business bought cheap ahead of a demanding half-year, not a great business bought at any price.

    • It is compelling on price and on the odds, bounded on the downside by earnings that still compound at 13 percent if the multiple does nothing, and not compelling on novelty, on quality or on the market's appetite for its sector.

  • Rating = B, because modal earnings growth sits inside the 12 to 20 percent band on every horizon, all four key outputs are improving, there is a dated staircase of steps, and the odds-weighted return meets the 20 percent bar at eighteen months

    • the Building Block is rated on the whole, and the weak 'Change Forces' Element at C does not bind, because the outputs that change has already produced are in the numbers and the 'Modal return' Element carries the Block.
    • Not A, because the return clears the bar only with the re-rating leg, the input change is a continuation rather than something new, and sell-side upgrades of size are unlikely.
    • Not C, because the earnings and cash legs alone return about 14 percent a year and the valuation cost of the case is nil.

  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.

  • The case against: Convatec is a 5 percent-growth, number-three player whose second-act targets require a back-end-loaded margin bridge that the sell-side itself calls the most demanding in the company's history, to be delivered by a chief executive from retail finance without a permanent chief financial officer
    • the 11x multiple is the sector's new level, with Coloplast at 14.8x on a 27.5 percent margin and Smith & Nephew at 10.5x, so there is no re-rating to earn and the return is the earnings growth, 10 to 14 percent a year, a C.
  • Our response: we concede the sector's de-rating and the bridge's difficulty, which is why our multiple is a blend that sits below the stock's own ten-year 'crash' band of 12.9x rather than at its median, and why we put the odds on the cleared branch under even.
  • We hold the B for three reasons.
  • The arithmetic of the bridge is ordinary: H1 2026 operating costs of about $477m held flat on H2 revenue of about $1,324m at a 60 percent adjusted gross margin gives about $324m of operating profit, or 24.5 percent, and H2 2025 was already 23.2 percent.
  • The InnovaMatrix drag of 140 basis points in H1 halves in H2 on the company's own base and vanishes in 2027.
  • And at 11.6x with a buyback running, the downside on a miss is measured, about 10x and 14 percent, while the earnings still compound.
  • Where the case against would win: if the sector's re-basing is permanent and the bridge is missed, the case is a C and belongs parked
    • the February print decides which.
