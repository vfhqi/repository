[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = C

Thesis. MTU Aero Engines is a cause-for-concern-clearing case in a high-quality aftermarket annuity business, not an earnings-growth case. A partner's defect, the Pratt & Whitney GTF powder-metal recall of July 2023, turned an annuity into a cash problem while earnings kept compounding, and that problem is now clearing on hard operating data. At €368.1 the shares pay a C return for the part of the clearing we can prove, because what the clearing buys is cash conversion and a dividend, not earnings growth inside the band.

  • The change is external, transient by its own arithmetic, and it is clearing.
    • Gross airline compensation for the recall was $1.0bn in 2024 and $360m in 2025
    • it is guided at $250m for 2026, ran at $110m in H1 2026, and then ends.
    • Grounded GTF aircraft are down 25 percent in 2026 to date, turnaround is under 100 days at the core sites, and management says inspections and settlements complete by year-end 2026.
    • Cash conversion went from 24 percent of adjusted net income in FY2024 to 39 percent in FY2025 to 59 percent in H1 2026, and on 30 July 2026 the FY2026 floor was raised from 45 to 50 percent, with a 2030 target of 75 to 99 percent.
    • The demand behind the annuity is not in dispute even by the bears: commercial MRO grew 29 percent organically in US dollars in H1 2026 against a full-year guide of low-to-mid teens, flight hours are at 106 percent of 2019, and the order backlog is €30.4bn, more than three years of workload.
  • The change inside the company is a new team executing the old plan, and it is real.
    • All four Executive Board members changed within twelve months
    • the chief executive since 1 September 2025 ran Lufthansa Technik for seven years and has already refined the operating KPIs.
    • The 2030 targets (revenue €13bn to €14bn, adjusted EBIT margin 14.5 to 15.5 percent, conversion 75 to 99 percent) were set under his predecessor and none has been raised.
  • The change has travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain except its last link, and that is the point.
    • It shows in the people, the choices (KPIs, the raised cash floor), the actions (PW1100G output +40 percent year-on-year), the key operating metric (conversion doubled), the financial statements (H1 2026 free cash flow €294m) and the sell-side's cash estimates (+10 to 13 percent after the results).
    • The sell-side's earnings estimates moved only 1 to 3 percent, because the earnings were never the problem.
  • The outputs the change drives are cash and dividend, not the outputs that set the multiple.
    • Revenue growth is decelerating from 16 to 18 percent a year toward 8 to 11 percent
    • the adjusted EBIT margin is flat to falling (15.5 percent in FY2025, 14.8 percent in H1 2026) because MRO at an 8 percent margin grows faster than OEM at 31 percent
    • our modal earnings-per-share path is about +6 percent in FY2026 and 10 to 11 percent a year after that, a 9.2 percent compound rate to FY2028, below the 12 to 20 percent band at every horizon.
  • What the price assumes is a partial clearing.
    • At €368.1 the shares trade at 16.7x 24-month-forward consensus earnings of €22.08, the 29th percentile of the stock's own ten years and the 16th percentile of five, a full turn below the ten-year median of 17.7x, and a 30 percent EV/EBIT discount to Safran.
    • The discount is company-driven (the propulsion peers trade near highs), so proof on cash removes its cause
    • part of it is structural (a partner, not a prime; conversion half of Safran's; a mix shifting toward MRO), which is why our modal multiple is 17.0x, not the median.
  • The return therefore clears a C, not the bar.
    • On our modal path the total shareholder return is about 14.3 percent a year over eighteen months (value €445.4 at 17.0x on €26.2 of 24-month-forward earnings, plus a €4.50 dividend) and 11.5 percent a year over three years: a C at eighteen months on the framework's anchors, a D at three years.
    • The 20 percent bar is reached only if the multiple returns to the 75th percentile of its own history (19.5x, about 25 percent a year) or the earnings follow the most bullish broker rather than our path.
  • The pattern is 'OSHQC CfC Clearing', crisp on its mechanism and loose on one condition.
    • The concern is named, external and dated
    • the business passes the quality gate on its moat
    • the clearing is evidenced.
    • The shares are cheap against their own history but no longer oversold in price after a 24.8 percent six-month advance, so the market has begun to pay.
  • Where the chain breaks is the drop-through.
    • If the OEM spares mix normalises from a 32.0 percent segment margin and the MRO margin stays at 7.9 percent while receivables keep absorbing cash, FY2028 earnings per share are about €21.2 rather than our €23.2 and the return falls to an F even at our multiple.
    • That is why the entry level matters: the modal path clears 20 percent a year over eighteen months only at or below about €342.

  • The case is therefore clean, trackable and congruent, with a C return at this price.

    • We take the side that the concern clears, and we decline to pay €368 for it.

  • Rating = C, because the change is real and far along the chain but it is a cash change more than an earnings change: the forces are B, the outputs are C, the modal return is C, and the return binds, because no earnings output is inside the 12 to 20 percent band and the 20 percent bar is cleared only at the 75th-percentile multiple.

    • Not B for that reason
    • not D because the forces are strong and the cash inflection is evidenced, not promised.

Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.

  • The case against, at its strongest: the "false friend".
    • MTU's record margins are a temporary artefact of the crisis they appear to have survived.
    • OEM margins above 30 percent for three quarters rest on a spare-engine delivery ratio of about 23 percent against a normal 15 percent, forced by grounded fleets, and on V2500 spares from a fleet whose retirements accelerate at $152 fuel
      • management itself projects declining spare ratios.
    • The growth is in an 8 percent margin MRO business that pre-finances its own shop visits, so receivables (€4,600m at June 2026, +€385m in H1 2026) absorb the cash the compensation no longer takes, and conversion stays near 50 to 60 percent through 2028 rather than rising to 75 percent.
    • Redburn (Sell, €355) and UBS (Sell, €320) hold this view
      • Bernstein's own FY2026 free-cash-flow estimate of €429m sits below the guidance floor
      • the risks research's competing-hypotheses matrix picks this profile as its winner, with one hard inconsistency against ten for the bull profile.
    • On that reading earnings stall near €19 to €20, the multiple stays at 15x, and the shares are worth about €300 to €330.
  • What we concede.
    • The OEM margin is at a mix peak and will fall as Airbus deliveries rise in H2 2026.
    • The receivables lag the operational clearing by a year or more.
    • Our own earnings growth is below the band.
    • Part of the discount is structural and we carry it as a 0.7x mix haircut.
    • The plausibly worse drop-through takes the return to F.
    • We also concede that no division covers the enterprise value: at the propulsion peers' about 20x EV/EBIT, FY2025 OEM segment EBIT of €872m is worth €17.4bn, 87 percent of the €19,968m enterprise value
      • at the lowest named multiple in the comparison set, 13x, it is €11.3bn, 57 percent
      • even both divisions at 13x (€1,351m x 13 = €17.6bn) fall 12 percent short.
      • There is no bounded-downside argument here
      • the downside is the multiple.
  • Why we still hold our view, as a neutral and not a bull.
    • The cash clearing is arithmetic, not narrative: compensation of $250m in 2026 goes to nil, and that alone is about 20 points of conversion.
    • The demand side of the aftermarket is undisputed
    • retirements run at 0.4 percent a quarter with ten-year airframe backlogs, so the V2500 cliff is a 2029 question, not a 2027 one.
    • The company has beaten its initial guidance every year since 2022 and raised the cash floor only after earning it.
    • And the price is not asking us to believe the bull case: at 16.7x it prices consensus growth and a multiple below the median.
  • Where the case against would win.
    • If receivables grow by more than €300m in another half-year, or the February 2027 guide for FY2027 cash conversion comes in below 60 percent.
    • What the bear case does prove is that the return at this price has no margin: a C on our path, an F on theirs.
Change Forces: are the input change forces sufficiently strong? (E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because two external forces (the aftermarket demand supercycle and European defence spending) and two internal ones (a complete Executive Board renewal in twelve months and a raised cash-conversion guide) are strong and pointing the same way, and the one disruption that mattered, the GTF powder-metal recall, is being disproved as a design failure and is clearing on hard operating data.
  • External and internal together: the external forces supply the demand and the clearing
    • the internal forces supply the discipline that turns the clearing into cash.
    • Neither alone would make the case.
  • Not A because the strongest external force lands in the 8 percent margin MRO division, and the internal change is continuity of a plan set by the previous chief executive rather than a new direction.
External forces: are there sufficiently strong external forces acting on the company, new or intensifying, and in which direction? (RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because demand for engine maintenance is running well ahead of capacity (commercial MRO +29 percent organic in US dollars in H1 2026 against a full-year guide of low-to-mid teens) and defence budgets are rising, both with visibility beyond two years, while the headwinds (castings and forgings, currency, Middle East traffic) are contained by hedges and backlog.
  • Direction by force: demand a tailwind
    • disruption fading, a tailwind for this case
    • political and regulatory a net tailwind
    • the company's own cycle mid-way up on volume and turning up on cash
    • competition a mild headwind on original-equipment share
    • supply a moderate, stable headwind
    • macro a mild, hedged headwind.
  • Not A because the aftermarket volume that is exploding is the lower-margin GTF workscope, and the original-equipment share trend on the A320neo is moving against the GTF.

    • +29 percent organic in US dollars in H1 2026 against a full-year organic guide of low-to-mid teens.
  • Flight hours: global commercial flight hours 106 percent of 2019 for MTU's value-weighted fleet in July 2026

  • Not A because the demand that is strongest (GTF overhauls, 46 percent of MRO revenue in H1 2026) carries an 8 percent segment margin, and original-equipment demand is capped by Airbus's build rate (commercial OEM revenue -11.5 percent in Q2 2026 to €571m).
Disruption: is a disruption force strengthening or being disproved? (KQ 3, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because the disruption that has governed the shares since July 2023, the GTF powder-metal recall, is being disproved as a design failure and is clearing on data, while the long-dated disruptions (open rotor, hydrogen) sit beyond 2035.
  • Nature of the defect: the contamination was a raw-material manufacturing defect, not a structural design failure
    • nine quality modifications since 2021
    • all new production and MRO replacements now use certified full-life disks.
  • Clearing data: grounded GTF aircraft -25 percent year-to-date
    • PW1100G MRO output +40 percent year-on-year in H1 2026
    • turnaround under 100 days at core sites.
  • Next architecture: the CFM RISE open rotor (targeting 20 percent fuel burn) and the Rolls-Royce UltraFan (seeking a Tier-1 partner) are post-2035 contests
    • Pratt & Whitney rejected open rotor and airframers are wary of a single-source open-rotor supplier.
  • Direction: the operative disruption is fading, which is a tailwind for this case.
  • Not A because the GTF Advantage enters service only in H2 2026 and a new field defect would reopen the whole question.

Competition: is competitive intensity easing or tightening? (KQ 2, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because competitive intensity is tightening at the original-equipment margin (CFM taking share on the A320neo) and in the aftermarket's long run (OEM insourcing), while in the near term tight capacity keeps independent MRO in demand.
  • OE share: CFM's LEAP captured 86 percent of announced firm narrowbody engine orders at Farnborough 2026
    • LEAP-1A took a trailing 90-day A320neo delivery share of 64 percent against the GTF's 36 percent
    • CFM secured 80 percent of unallocated A321neo orders since 2025.
    • Against that, around 44 percent of A320neo aircraft delivered in H1 2026 were GTF-powered, above the platform's historical 40 percent.
  • Aftermarket access: OEMs steer high-margin maintenance in-house through bundled long-term agreements
    • GE and Safran control the LEAP MRO network through "Premier MRO" licences.
  • MTU's position: a 40 to 46 percent network shop-visit share of the GTF MRO ecosystem against an 18 percent programme share, and severe capacity constraints force airlines onto independents.
  • Direction: a mild headwind on original-equipment share, neutral on the aftermarket for the next two to three years.
  • Not D because the aftermarket capacity shortage is the binding fact for the horizon of the case.
Supply side: input costs, availability, concentration? (KQ 5, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C

  • Cost structure: materials were 83.4 percent of H1 2026 cost of goods sold (€3,226m of €3,870m), personnel 14.8 percent.

  • Direction: a headwind, moderate and stable.
  • Not D because material availability for the GTF (powder-metal disks) has eased over the past twelve months and the constraint is now sector-wide rather than company-specific.

    • Once an engine is on wing the customer is locked in for its life and catalog prices rise every year.
    • It is the reason the recall deferred revenue rather than destroying it, and it is what a median multiple would be paying for.
    • It grows with the installed base and the network share.
  • ‘Case/SP Risk’: MRO capital intensity
    • MRO pre-finances its own shop visits and earns a thin margin on passed-through material.
    • It is the reason the mix shift caps the group margin and why receivables absorb cash.
    • It is rising while the new sites ramp.
Leadership and management
  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: The new board's cash discipline
    • The new Executive Board has refined the operating KPIs, raised the cash floor only after earning it, and signalled the payout restoration.
    • It is what turns the clearing into cash and dividends.
    • It is three quarters old and improving.
  • ‘Case/SP Risk’: An unproven team
    • All four board members are new within twelve months and the chief financial officer comes from outside aerospace.
    • It matters because the cash stewardship and the capital allocation are what the case needs most.
    • It is possible, moderate in size, and fades with each delivered half.
External
  • ‘Case/SP Key Driver’: The GTF recall clearing
    • A partner's manufacturing defect grounded aircraft and drained cash through compensation, and both are now falling on published data.
    • It decides whether the case is right and whether the shares re-rate, because it is the concern the pattern says is clearing.
    • It is probable, large, and improving, with the GTF Advantage entering service in H2 2026.
  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: European defence budgets
    • European governments are lifting defence spending and ordering Eurofighters into the 2030s.
    • It adds a growing, higher-margin revenue stream beside the civil aftermarket.
    • It is strengthening.
  • ‘Case/SP Key Risk’: A new GTF Advantage field defect
    • A new durability or quality failure on the Advantage after it enters service in H2 2026 would reopen the whole question.
    • It threatens both the share price and the case, because it would restart the compensation and the groundings the case assumes are ending.
    • It is unlikely, very large if it happens, and untested until the fleet flies.
  • ‘Case/SP Risk’: The dollar beyond the 2026 hedge
    • Most of the revenue is in US dollars and the hedge coverage falls away after 2026.
    • It is a share-price risk to the reported earnings path, not a case risk.
    • It is possible and moderate, with the exposure growing each year out.
Internal
  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: MRO capacity at low-cost sites reaching scale
    • The new shops in China, Poland and the United States are in their first years and are designed for several times their current output.
    • It is the mechanism by which the MRO margin recovers and the network share grows.
    • It is plausible and slow.
  • ‘Case/SP Risk’: Ramp costs persisting
    • The MRO margin has fallen under the weight of new-site ramp costs and heavy GTF workscopes.
    • It is a share-price risk, because the sell-side reads it as dilution, and a case risk if it does not stabilise.
    • It is plausible, moderate, and currently moving the wrong way.
Key operating and financial metrics
  • ‘Case/SP Key Driver’: Cash conversion
    • The share of adjusted net income that becomes free cash flow has more than doubled in eighteen months and management targets a further doubling by 2030.
    • It decides whether the case is right and whether the shares re-rate, because it is the output the concern damaged and the one the multiple will be paid on.
    • It is probable, large, and improving, with the February 2027 guide the test.
  • ‘Case/SP Key Driver’: The OEM spares mix holding
    • Heavier V2500 workscopes and a high spare-engine ratio keep the OEM segment margin above its long-run corridor.
    • It decides the earnings path, because OEM carries most of the group's profit.
    • It is plausible to hold through 2027 and flat, with a negative skew.
  • ‘Case/SP Key Risk’: Spares normalisation
    • This is the inverse of the driver above: the spare-engine ratio returns to normal as Airbus deliveries rise and V2500 retirements accelerate.
    • It threatens both the share price and the case, because it is the bear's whole earnings argument.
    • It is possible, large, and beginning as deliveries rise in H2 2026.
Key financial statement items
  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: Free cash flow
    • Free cash flow is guided to a new high for FY2026 and consensus carries it higher each year.
    • It is what funds the dividend restoration and the net cash position the consensus path implies.
    • It is probable and improving.
  • ‘Case/SP Risk’: Receivables
    • Pre-financed trade receivables are still rising and absorb the cash the compensation no longer takes.
    • It is a case risk, because the conversion target cannot be met while they grow.
    • It is plausible, moderate, and rising, with stabilisation expected in 2027.
Sell-side estimates
  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: Free-cash-flow upgrades
    • The sell-side raised its cash estimates sharply after the results and the most bullish house sits far above consensus for 2029.

    • It is improving.

    • Earnings estimates barely moved after the results and consensus EBIT already sits at the top of guidance.
    • It is a share-price risk, because without earnings upgrades the re-rating has to do the work alone.
    • It is probable and stable.
Cash return, earnings growth, re-rating
  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: The dividend restoration
    • Management intends to reinstate the payout policy once conversion stabilises and consensus assumes it does.
    • It is the smallest and most predictable leg of the return.
    • It is probable, with the February 2027 results and the May 2027 AGM the dates.
  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: Earnings growth
    • Earnings per share grow at a high-single-digit to low-double-digit rate, revenue-led with a margin drag.
    • It carries most of the modal return and it is the consequence of the drivers above rather than a driver of its own.
    • It is probable and below the framework's band.

    • The multiple stays at or below today's level because the market decides the discount is structural.
    • It is a share-price risk, not a case risk, and it is the swing factor of the whole return.
    • It is possible, large, and it is what the price is assuming today.
