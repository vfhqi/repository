Section 5 – Capital Structure, Share Count Dilution, and Dividend Policy

Section summary
  1. Dual-class equity structures and authorized capital expansions of up to fifty percent elevate long-term dilution risks for Grifols shareholders.
  2. Projected depreciation and amortization charges comfortably outpace rising capital expenditures as Grifols enters a more disciplined investment cycle.
  3. Deleveraging priorities constrain Grifols dividend growth compared to conservative peers like CSL and Takeda which maintain robust capital returns.

Dual-Class Structure and Authorized Capital

1.
Complex capital: Grifols maintains a dual-class share structure consisting of voting Class A and non-voting Class B shares. Class A comprises 426,129,798 ordinary shares with a par value of EUR 0.25, whereas Class B consists of 261,425,110 preference shares with a par value of EUR 0.05. Class B shares carry a minimum preferred dividend of EUR 0.01 per share but generally lack voting rights. This structure concentrates control while distributing economic rights across different share tiers, complicating the overall equity valuation.
2.
Substantial expansion: Shareholders authorized the Board of Directors at the June 2026 General Meeting to increase capital by up to 50%. This delegation permits the issuance of new Class A or Class B shares at any moment over a five-year period. The nominal value of newly issued Class B shares cannot exceed onefourth of the total resulting share capital. This authorization provides management with high operational agility to raise funds but threatens significant equity dilution.
3.
Restricted rights: The capital increase delegation empowers Grifols' Board of Directors to exclude pre-emptive subscription rights. This exclusion is strictly limited to a maximum of 20% of the company's total share capital in compliance with corporate governance guidelines. Excluding pre-emptive rights allows for rapid cash collection but increases the risk of immediate dilution for minority holders. Consequently, investors face potential earnings per share erosion if new shares are placed without matching earnings growth.

Depreciation, Amortization, and Capex Trends

4.
Moderate investments: Grifols is shifting from an aggressive capital spending cycle to a more disciplined maintenance model. Management expects capital expenditures to normalise toward 5% of sales from a peak of 9% in 2024. This shift is driven by the completion of major expansion projects such as the ImmunoTek plasma collection network. This moderation is expected to support capital conservation and accelerate the company's deleveraging efforts.
5.
Exceeded depreciation: Financial projections indicate that Grifols' annual depreciation and amortization charges will consistently exceed capital expenditures. Net capital expenditures are modeled to rise from EUR 424 mm in 2027e to EUR 456 mm in 2029e. In contrast, depreciation and amortization charges are forecasted to expand from EUR 500 mm in 2027e to EUR 552 mm in 2029e. This positive spread implies favorable noncash support for the company's free cash flow profile.
6.
Contrary evidence: Some analysts argue that Grifols' total cash outflows for capital investments will remain elevated when including capitalized intangibles. Deutsche Bank Research notes that while maintenance capex should decline to 5% of sales by 2029, adding growth capex and capitalized IT and R&D will double that rate. Consequently, these capitalized investments will continue to suppress the expected recovery of free cash flow. Thus, the cash-saving impact of normalized physical capex may be smaller than anticipated.

Dividend Policies and Peer Comparisons

7.
Resumed dividends: Grifols resumed shareholder distributions in 2025 after a prolonged suspension dating back to June 2021. The company distributed an interim dividend of EUR 0.15 per share in August 2025. Furthermore, the June 2026 Annual Shareholders' Meeting approved a supplementary dividend of EUR 56.1 mm. However, future payout expansions will likely be restricted by the overriding priority of balance sheet deleveraging.
8.
Conservative leverage: Peer CSL Limited operates with a far more conservative capital structure that supports robust shareholder returns. CSL targets a leverage ratio of 1.5x to 2.0x and maintained its dividend at US$2.92 per share in FY2026. The company also progressed its share buybacks with A$1 bn in FY2026 and A$1.1 bn planned for FY2027. This structural strength highlights how lower leverage enables steady cash returns without threatening balance sheet integrity.
9.
Leveraged progressive: Peer Takeda Pharmaceutical highlights how a leveraged company can prioritize progressive shareholder returns. Takeda maintains a progressive dividend policy, raising its annual dividend from 200 JPY in FY2025 to a projected 204 JPY in FY2026. The company is de-leveraging toward its 2.0x target while allocating JPY 100 bn to share buybacks. This balance shows that disciplined cash generation can support stable shareholder remuneration despite high legacy debt burdens.
10.
Other side of the story: While Grifols' dual-class structure and high leverage restrict direct shareholder returns, they also protect the company from immediate hostile takeovers during its transition phase. The Grifols family's 30% ownership of Class A voting shares provides significant governance stability. This concentration of control prevents disruptive corporate actions while the company executes its self-sufficiency projects. Therefore, the lack of immediate dividend growth is compensated by reduced strategic disruption during the balance sheet recovery period.

Section 6 – Currency Translation and Foreign-Exchange Derivatives

Section summary
  1. Grifols' consolidated results are moderately exposed to US Dollar translation, but natural hedges and dual-currency debt help neutralize long-term earnings-per-share volatility.
  2. Unhedged derivatives and canceled currency swaps introduce non-operating volatility to the consolidated finance result.
  3. Peer comparisons with Sanofi and Takeda show that diversified cost structures and centralized hedging are critical to managing global currency risks.

US Dollar Translation Sensitivity

1.
Elevated translation exposure: Grifols remains highly sensitive to fluctuations in the US Dollar because a vast portion of its global operations is centered in North America. According to the company's Form 20-F filed on April 17, 2026, a 10% strengthening of the US Dollar against the Euro as of December 31, 2025, would have increased consolidated equity by Euros 723 million. This same currency movement would have also boosted consolidated net profit by Euros 97 million. The investigation analyzed fifteen relevant sources, three companies, and two comparable past cases to evaluate currency risks.

Contrary evidence: Standalone currency impacts on the parent company display an opposite financial direction from the consolidated group figures. Therefore, the standalone parent entity enjoys a modest benefit from a weaker dollar even though consolidated results face significant pressure.

Currency Profile of Debt

4.
Balanced nominal debt: Grifols uses a natural hedging mechanism by aligning its debt currency profile with its regional operational revenues. In its 2025 annual report published on April 17, 2026, the company stated that Euro-denominated financing represented 68% of total nominal debt. This Euro-denominated portion amounted to Euros 5,809 million, compared to 66% or Euros 5,924 million at the end of the previous fiscal year. By keeping 32% of its debt primarily in US Dollars, the group successfully offsets its North American asset exposure.
5.
Restructured borrowing currencies: Grifols updated its debt currency composition following a comprehensive capital refinancing completed in the first half of 2026. A company interim report published on July 28, 2026, detailed that the new Term Loan B facility was structured with a USD 2,000 million Dollar tranche. The same agreement also included a Euro tranche of Euros 1,250 million alongside an expanded revolving credit facility. This dual-currency structure ensures that Grifols continues to match its debt service obligations with its major cash-generating currencies.

Accounting for Unhedged Derivatives

6.
Unhedged derivative adjustments: Grifols occasionally enters into foreign exchange forward contracts that do not qualify for formal hedge accounting, leading to direct profit and loss volatility. The company noted in its consolidated report on February 25, 2025, that gains and losses from these unhedged derivatives are recognized directly in the finance result. For instance, the group recognized a net financial income of Euros 15.53 million from these instruments in 2024 compared to a financial expense of Euros 2.01 million in 2023. Consequently, these unhedged instruments introduce short-term non-operational fluctuations to net earnings.
7.
Canceled swap agreements: Grifols historically managed its currency mismatches through cross-currency swap agreements that have since been terminated. The company's annual filing on April 17, 2026, noted that a USD 205 million swap was terminated early on September 13, 2024. This swap had originally been designated as a cash flow hedge of the group's US Dollar unsecured notes. Following its cancellation, the company relies more heavily on its operational structure and natural hedges to manage currency volatility.
8.
Significant transaction gains: Grifols utilized specific currency derivatives to secure the Euro proceeds of its major Chinese asset divestment in 2024. The Spanish parent company reported on February 25, 2026, that it contracted a EUR/RMB forward exchange rate instrument to reduce exposure during the SRAAS sale. This derivative transaction generated a substantial financial gain of Euros 13.48 million, which was recognized in the profit and loss account. This derivative gain partially offset a corresponding foreign exchange loss of Euros 17.79 million recorded under exchange differences.

Section 7 – The Strategic Impact of a Partial US Biopharma Listing

Section summary
  1. A potential minority listing of the US Biopharma unit aims to unlock shareholder value and raise capital for deleveraging while retaining majority parent control.
  2. Governance concerns surround the potential transaction as listing a US subsidiary may increase operational complexity and reverse prior corporate simplification.

Strategic Rationale and Value Unlocking

1.
Operational reorganization: Grifols is establishing separate Biopharma US and Biopharma Rest of World divisions to drive regional operational focus. The company's interim report published on July 28, 2026, outlined that this reorganization supports a transition to a two-system operating model where US-sourced plasma primarily serves the US market. This structure optimizes resources and prepares the US division for a potential independent listing, which is expected to support long-term profitability and resource allocation.
2.
Strategic unlocking: Grifols is exploring a partial IPO of its US blood plasma business to highlight its intrinsic value to investors. Bernstein Research noted on March 25, 2026, that the transaction is designed to create a pureplay US listed plasma entity that aligns with domestic investor interest. This move could help Grifols unlock significant unrecognized shareholder value because US biopharma margins are substantially higher than margins in other regions.
3.
Strategic raising: Grifols intends to float a minority stake in its US Biopharma unit to strengthen its balance sheet. A company press release on March 24, 2026, stated that the proceeds would be used to reduce debt and support strategic growth initiatives. This capital inflow will directly support deleveraging, thereby improving net earnings per share by lowering group interest expenses over the next eighteen months.
4.
Geographical focus: Grifols plans to leverage its unique fully integrated US platform to appeal to North American investors. The company's press release on March 24, 2026, highlighted that the transaction would establish the only US plasma player with a fully self-sufficient domestic supply chain. This unique position could attract a premium valuation, enhancing the parent company's equity value.

Financial Implications and Earnings Impact

5.
Operational optimization: Grifols expects the US Biopharma division to maintain strong margins as the wider group optimizes its plasma supply chain. Morgan Stanley reported on July 28, 2026, that the ramp-up of collections in Egypt is allowing Grifols to focus US-sourced plasma exclusively on high-margin US markets. This optimization improves US operational performance, which would support a strong valuation multiple for the proposed US listing.
6.
Financial leakage: Grifols faces potential increases in minority interest leakage if it sells a stake in its core US operations. Bernstein Research discussed on March 25, 2026, that while the IPO raises capital, it also introduces non-controlling interests into the company's most profitable business segment. This leakage could partially offset the interest savings from debt reduction, limiting the net EPS benefit for parent shareholders.
7.
Financial independence: Grifols has reassured the market that the proposed US listing is not driven by immediate funding distress. Bernstein Research reported on March 25, 2026, that management categorically emphasized that the IPO is being explored to maximize shareholder value rather than out of capital necessity. This operational flexibility allows management to execute the transaction only under highly favorable market conditions.
8.
Contrary evidence: Deutsche Bank Research suggests that the proposed US Biopharma IPO raises concerns regarding corporate transparency and financial structure. Their report on March 25, 2026, argued that creating a listed US subsidiary partially reverses Grifols' recent progress toward corporate simplification and governance improvements. Consequently, the added administrative costs and complexity could depress consolidated earnings per share over the next eighteen months.

Governance Structures and Precedents

9.
Organizational complexity: Grifols intends to establish a separate corporate governance framework for the newly listed US business. A company filing on July 28, 2026, detailed that the US Biopharma company would have its own board of directors, management team, and corporate governance structure. While this structure aligns with US market standards, it could create conflicts of interest between parent and subsidiary shareholders.
10.
Market risks: Grifols shareholders face potential trading challenges due to the dual-listing structure. Deutsche Bank Research noted on March 25, 2026, that a partial listing would set up a difficult trading dynamic and inevitable arbitrage between the Spanish parent and the US subsidiary. This market friction could limit the parent company's share price recovery despite the underlying value unlock.
11.
Complex precedent: Biotest AG represents a relevant example of the complexities of managing a consolidated subsidiary with minority holders. Grifols' acquisition of a 70% stake in Biotest in 2022 created a structure with 30% minority leakages and complex intercompany dynamics. This historical case suggests that consolidating subsidiaries with significant minorities often complicates cash flow allocation and depresses parent net income.
12.
Strategic precedent: Fresenius SE highlights an alternative corporate strategy where a parent company de- consolidated a complex subsidiary to simplify its structure. Bernstein Research reported on July 16, 2026, that Fresenius SE successfully de-consolidated Fresenius Medical Care in 2023, reducing its stake to under 30% while treating it as a financial investment. This structural shift helped Fresenius SE focus on organic growth in its core divisions.
13.
Gradual deleveraging: Fresenius SE has demonstrated that selling down subsidiary stakes can accelerate parent balance sheet improvement. Morgan Stanley reported on October 13, 2026, that Fresenius SE reduced its stake in Fresenius Medical Care to approximately 25% by August 2026 through opportunistic sell-downs. This strategy helped Fresenius SE improve its leverage profile and focus capital on higher-growth platforms.
14.
Other side of the story: While critics express concern over the added complexity of a listed US subsidiary, the transaction provides Grifols with a unique corporate currency to pursue high-value US acquisitions. A company press release on March 24, 2026, explained that a US-listed Biopharma entity would operate with the agility and tailored governance needed to compete effectively in the US market. Therefore, the potential governance complexity is outweighed by the strategic advantages of establishing a highly visible, self-sufficient US plasma leader.

Section 9 – If the Answer Differs from the Common View

Section summary
  1. If the core financial inputs resolve worse than consensus expectations, Grifols' high financial leverage would severely amplify the negative impact on earnings and capital structure.
  2. Regulatory scrutiny and related-party complexities present persistent risks that could disrupt operational infrastructure and debt covenant compliance.

Strategic Divergence and Earnings Risks

1.
Severe pressure: Grifols' consolidated earnings per share would face severe downside pressure if innovative competitive therapies disrupt its core biopharma franchises. Bernstein Research reported on December 9, 2024, that a potential 50% annual decline in United States immunoglobulin sales for chronic inflammatory demyelinating polyneuropathy due to Argenx's Vyvgart would trigger a twenty-seven percent downside to the company's valuation. This severe disruption would drastically reduce operating cash flows and prevent the company from organically reducing its high debt load. Challenged franchises: Grifols' highly profitable alpha-1 antitrypsin franchise faces substantial downside risk if Sanofi achieves early accelerated approval for its recombinant competitor. Morgan Stanley reported on August 3, 2026, that an accelerated approval filing for Sanofi's recombinant alpha-1 would pose a significant threat to the alpha-1 franchise, which represents approximately 13% of Grifols' total sales and 25% of its gross profit. Under these circumstances, gross margins would compress rapidly, leading to a direct reduction in forecasted earnings per share. Contrary evidence: Grifols' core immunoglobulin business might remain highly resilient despite the market introduction of novel FcRn inhibitor therapies. Bernstein Research argued on January 7, 2025, that the broader therapeutic utility of immunoglobulins means the franchise is unlikely to be heavily disrupted by competitors like Argenx's Vyvgart. This stability suggests that consolidated earnings could remain protected even if competitive trials demonstrate high clinical efficacy. Depressed profitability: Grifols would fail to meet medium-term margin targets if its consolidated Biotest subsidiary suffers from prolonged operational integration difficulties. Bernstein Research highlighted on November 11, 2026, that a failure to extract cost savings or achieve gross margin expansion at Biotest would introduce a nine percent downside risk to the parent company's valuation. This integration failure would prevent the expected margin expansion from flowing to consolidated earnings, keeping equity returns subdued.

Regulatory and Governance Shifts

2.
Tightened regulation: Grifols faces persistent scrutiny from regulatory bodies that has the potential to force unexpected changes in its reported leverage metrics. Deutsche Bank Research reported on March 22, 2024, that the Spanish market regulator CNMV criticized certain related-party disclosures and required the company to publish updated EBITDA figures within fifteen days. These regulatory interventions could alter the perceived leverage of the group, impacting creditor confidence and raising borrowing costs. Restructured covenants: Grifols' complex relationship with Scranton Enterprises introduces structural vulnerabilities that could threaten covenant compliance under adverse financial scenarios. An expert call published on November 18, 2026, noted that a legal or regulatory ruling against the consolidation of Haema and BPC could threaten EBITDA covenant compliance for the consolidated group. This consolidation risk is critical because any covenant breach would immediately restrict Grifols' financial flexibility and refinancing options. Pledged assets: Grifols' core plasma collection infrastructure remains exposed to related-party financial distress due to historical guarantees and share pledges. Deutsche Bank Research highlighted on January 11, 2024, that Scranton utilized guarantees from BPC Plasma and pledges over Haema GmbH shares to secure its related-party debt. If Scranton faces liquidity issues, these crucial plasma collection assets could become subject to creditor enforcement, disrupting Grifols' raw material supply.
3.
Refinanced exposure: Related-party financial arrangements continue to tie Grifols' operational stability to the lending appetite of international banking syndicates. Grifols disclosed in an interim report on July 28, 2026, that Scranton Enterprises refinanced its Oaktree debt by raising Euros 456 million from banks to clean up its capital structure. However, any future default under this bank facility could force the selling companies to execute their repurchase option within ninety days, straining group liquidity.

Supply Chain and Market Feedback

4.
Inflated sourcing: Grifols faces significant margin volatility if donor compensation trends in the United States fail to decline as expected. Jefferies Research reported on July 25, 2022, that compensation payments to plasma donors represent around a third of Grifols' total operating costs. A resurgence of inflationary pressures would prevent donor fees from falling, severely compressing operating margins and slowing down deleveraging. Competitive pricing: Grifols' bottom-line recovery remains highly dependent on competitors maintaining rational pricing structures in the global immunoglobulin market. Jefferies Research reported on October 30, 2026, that while CSL and Takeda planned mid-single digit price increases, Grifols' price growth remained lower at approximately four percent. If competitors engage in aggressive price discounting to win key market tenders, Grifols would be forced to choose between losing market share or sacrificing its margins. Expanding share: Grifols has successfully captured market share from established peers in high-value segments, modifying historical industry dynamics. Jefferies Research reported on October 30, 2026, that Grifols' subcutaneous immunoglobulin volume share expanded from two to fourteen percent in the United States. If CSL aggressive counter-strategies recapture this lost share, Grifols' projected biopharma revenue and earnings per share growth would decelerate.
