[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = C

Thesis. Porr is a self-help margin case in a medium-quality Austrian contractor, riding a funded multi-year public infrastructure cycle in Austria, Poland and, from 2027, Germany. At EUR 31.25 the shares pay about 15% a year over eighteen months and 12% a year over three years on our modal path, below the 20% bar, because the margin climb is slow and steady rather than fast, revenue is flat, and the market has just withdrawn the multiple that would make the case pay.

  • The internal change is one strong force, not many.
    • A cost and selectivity programme has lifted the EBIT margin from 2.3% in FY2023 to 2.6% in FY2024 and 3.1% in FY2025, with FY2026 guided at 3.2% to 3.3% and a 2030 target of 3.5% to 4.0%.
    • Overheads: 8.2% of revenue in 2020 to 7.4% in 2025, toward a 6.7% target for 2030.
    • Selectivity: LEAN on every project above EUR 20m
      • A gate system
      • Board approval above EUR 100m
      • "cherry-picking" bidding stated by management.
    • Breadth: standards and cost discipline is the only force we rate B
      • Leadership below the chief executive, strategy, perimeter, new products and the balance sheet are each modest.
      • There is no chief-executive change and no guidance increase, so the framework's screen rule for a strong internal change is not met.
  • The external force is strong at sector level and has not reached Porr's own revenue.
    • Public civil demand is funded and dated for two years or more in all three main markets, and it is visible in the leader's order book before it is visible in Porr's top line.
    • Austria: ÖBB EUR 19.5bn to 2032
      • ASFINAG EUR 12.5bn to 2031, more than EUR 2.0bn in 2026 alone.
    • Poland: PKP PLK tenders of about EUR 2.7bn in 2026
      • An open pipeline of EUR 5.5bn.
    • Germany: a EUR 500bn fund
      • EUR 1.5bn of tenders from October 2026
      • Real volume from 2027.
    • Porr's own line: revenue +1.7% in FY2025 and -1.1% in H1 2026
      • Q2 2026 order intake -32.2% to EUR 1,701m
      • Strabag's backlog +27% to EUR 35,985m (28 Aug 2026) over the same period.
  • The outputs have reached the financial statements and stopped before the sell-side's estimates.
    • The change has travelled to layer 5 of the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain and not to layer 6.
    • H1 2026: EBIT +15.6% to EUR 56.3m on revenue -1.1%
      • EPS +34% to EUR 0.71
      • Order backlog EUR 9,839m, eighteen months of capacity.
    • Estimates: consensus EPS 0%, +1%, +1% over one, three and six months (2026-10-08)
      • The dashboard pins sell-side momentum at D.
    • Why they stopped: the Q2 EBIT beat of 8% was absorbed because revenue missed consensus by 3.5% and the half-year cash outflow was EUR 257.9m.
  • The earnings path compounds at about 9.5% a year, below the framework's 12% to 20% band.
    • Two-thirds of it is margin, the component investors extrapolate
    • None of it is the share count.
    • Our path: EPS EUR 3.00 in FY2025 (EUR 2.98 on the brokers' basis) to EUR 3.22, EUR 3.60 and EUR 3.90 in FY2026 to FY2028
      • Consensus EUR 3.24, EUR 3.65 and EUR 3.78.
      • We sit on consensus to FY2027 and 3% above it in FY2028, the difference being the below-EBIT line, not the operating case.
    • The quiet risk: the path needs the ratio of attributable earnings to EBIT to rise from 0.595 to 0.62 as about EUR 150m of hybrid capital is redeemed
      • If the working capital does not unwind, the hybrid is not redeemed and that leg is lost.
  • What it costs on the valuation: 8.3x 24-month-forward earnings, mid-range of the stock's own history and cheap against peers.
    • The shares have fallen a third from the 52-week high of EUR 46.55 with estimates unchanged.
    • Own history: the 72nd percentile of the stock's five-year range (median 5.63x) and the 59th percentile of ten years (median 7.30x).
    • Peers: the fourth cheapest of 29 (peer median about 11.7x)
      • The continental general contractors sit at 9.2x to 10.6x, Strabag at 9.4x.
    • What is priced: the consensus earnings path, a governance and margin discount to Strabag, and doubt about the 2026 cash.
      • What is not priced: a re-rating to the continental cluster, which the market gave this stock at EUR 46 in the spring and has withdrawn.
  • The return on the modal path is 15.0% a year over eighteen months and 11.9% a year over three years, a C and a D against the framework's anchors.
    • It is carried by earnings growth and swung by the multiple.
    • The blend: 8.7x at both horizons, 65% odds of 9.5x if the year-end cash arrives and 35% odds of 7.3x if it does not.
    • The swing: at 9.5x the eighteen-month return is 21.8% a year, a B
      • At 7.3x it is 2.7% a year, an F.
    • The entry price that clears the bar: below about EUR 29.3 for the eighteen-month horizon and EUR 25.3 for three years
      • The 52-week low is EUR 24.61.
  • The pattern is 'Major Corporate Change' at C.
    • A contractor without pricing power or scale raises its margin by refusing bad work and shedding overhead while its order book is full, and the book is full because public clients are funded.
    • The fit is recognisable, not crisp: one strong input force, output in the profit line only, and estimates unmoved after three years of delivery.
  • Where the chain holds and where it breaks.
    • It holds from the programme to the margin to EBIT, three years running.
    • It breaks twice: between EBIT and earnings per share, where the hybrid coupon, minorities and an unreadable tax line take about 40% of EBIT
    • And between the statements and the price, where the market has moved from assuming the year-end cash to doubting it.

  • The case is therefore real, slow and not compelling at this price.

    • It becomes compelling below about EUR 29, or if the Q3 update of 19 November 2026 and the FY2026 cash print earn the shares the continental peers' multiple of about 9.5x.

  • Rating = C, because the change is material enough to be real and visible (margin up three years running; a funded public cycle) but not material enough for the bar: EPS growth of about 9.5% a year and a 'Modal return' of 15% and 12% a year at the two horizons, with flat revenue and an unproven 2026 cash print.

    • The parts combine as a chain whose first link, external demand, is B, whose middle links, internal forces and outputs, are C, and whose last link, the return, is C to D
    • The chain holds but carries a modest load.
    • Not B, because the return does not reach 20% on the modal path at EUR 31.25 and revenue is not growing.
    • Not D, because the margin force is evidenced in three years of statements and the yield and the year-end balance sheet make the floor under the earnings path reasonably firm.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The hypothesis, at its strongest: the stock is cheap because it deserves to be.
      • This is the "false friend" the risks research of 8 August 2026 named, sharpened by the H1 2026 print.
      • Underneath the backlog: Porr is a thin-margin fixed-price contractor whose receivables over 180 days rose to EUR 338.6m in 2025 from EUR 252.4m, about 41% of trade receivables, because clients dispute its claims.
      • Percentage of completion: contract assets of EUR 7.24bn move EUR 36.2m for each 0.5 point of EBT margin, so a small margin miss unwinds a large number.
      • Overheads: 7.4% of revenue after ten years of programmes, against a target set at 6.7%.
      • Governance: the controlling family and the chief executive take value through UBM and IGO (related-party flows about 2.4% of revenue against a 0.5% peer median; a EUR 56.4m hybrid subscription with the chief executive chairing both sides) and through pay (+33% in a year of a margin below peers)
        • The owners sold a 4% block near the high.
      • The H1 print as its best evidence: operating cash flow EUR -257.9m against EUR -100.7m
        • Net debt EUR 460.6m
        • Q2 intake -32%
        • Germany's output -16.7%
        • While Strabag grew output 12% and raised its FY2026 margin guidance to 5.5% to 6.0%.
      • The conclusion it draws: the multiple's home is the ten-year median of 7.3x or lower, and the margin story is a slow walk to a level the leader passed years ago.
    • Our response, the concessions first.
      • The earnings-quality questions are real.
      • The cash is unproven until 19 November 2026 and March 2027.
      • The governance discount is deserved.
      • The market's refusal to re-rate the stock while estimates held is the crowd telling us something.
      • These are why we do not progress the case.
    • What we do not concede is the direction of the operating line.
      • Three years of margin expansion delivered on cost, a backlog of eighteen months, energy hedged to 2028 and 2030, indexed contracts, no estimate cuts and a dividend covered about three times are not the marks of a business about to disappoint on earnings.
    • So our view is neutral rather than bearish.
      • The competing hypothesis is why we do not progress
      • The operating evidence is why we do not invalidate the idea.
      • We make no claim that any division or asset covers the enterprise value, so there is no cover to show.
    • Where the hypothesis would win: no working-capital release at the 19 November 2026 update, or year-end net debt above EUR 50m
      • Then the cash was not seasonal, the hybrid is not redeemed, and the below-EBIT leg of the earnings path is lost with the multiple.
Change Forces: are the input change forces sufficiently strong? (E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because the external demand force is sector-wide and strong but has not reached Porr's revenue (FY2025 +1.7%; H1 2026 -1.1%), and the internal forces reduce to one strong one, standards and cost discipline, with the rest modest
    • The framework's screen rule, three strong forces or a chief-executive change plus a guidance rise, is not met.
  • How the two combine: the external force fills the order book and the internal force decides what margin is earned on it
    • Neither force on its own is enough for a 20%-a-year outcome, and together they deliver about 20 to 30 basis points of margin a year on flat-to-3% revenue growth.
  • Propagation: the forces have travelled from management's choices (selectivity, LEAN) through its actions (exits, bolt-ons, hedges) into the key operating metrics (overhead ratio, materials ratio) and the statements (margin)
    • The sell-side's estimates have not moved.
  • Not B, because a B needs forces sufficient for a 20%-a-year outcome, and these are not.
  • Not D, because the margin force is evidenced in three consecutive years of statements and in H1 2026, and the public demand programmes are funded and dated.
External forces: are there sufficiently strong external forces acting on the company, new or intensifying, and in which direction? (RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because public civil infrastructure demand in Porr's seven home markets is sector-level, funded for two years or more and visibly rising in peers' order books (Strabag backlog +27% to EUR 35,985m, 28 Aug 2026), while the forces acting against (the residential downturn, labour scarcity, Austrian stagnation) are smaller than the forces for.
  • Direction by force: demand, tailwind
    • Competition, neutral
    • Disruption, neutral
    • Political and regulatory, tailwind
    • Supply side, tailwind on materials and energy, headwind on labour
    • Porr's own cycle, flat
    • Macroeconomic, mixed.
  • Where the force is visible: in the leader's backlog and in tender pipelines, not yet in Porr's own revenue, which is why the force is a B and not an A.
  • Not A, because the force is not yet visible in Porr's own top line, and the German leg is dated 2027 and beyond by both the company and the sell-side (27 Aug and 28 May 2026).
  • For the case this means the demand force is an input to the margin programme rather than a volume story in its own right
    • It feeds 'Major Corporate Change', not 'Demand Inflection'.

Political and regulatory: is a change moving demand, pricing or cost? (KQ 4, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because German fiscal reform (the debt-brake relaxation, the Infrastructure Future Act, Bau-Turbo permitting), the Polish exclusion of third-country bidders and the EU Recovery and Resilience Facility's 2026 "use it or lose it" deadline all move demand toward Porr's activities.
  • Against: Berlin's municipal authorities are short of at least 500 staff (4 Sep 2026), which slows the conversion of money into tenders

    • Polish building-code tightening

  • For the case this means the political force is a tailwind on demand with an administrative headwind on timing
    • The timing risk sits with the German leg.

[Robustness of] Foundations upon which the Investment Thesis is built upon (BB 2) Rating = C

The Foundations make the modest thesis plausible and a larger one implausible. They are a floor rather than a spring: funded public customers, hedged costs and a delivering operator hold the margin case up, while a fragmented market in which Porr is not the leader, a 3.1% margin with no pricing power and a syndicate-controlled governance keep it from being anything more.

    • Porr's customers are public infrastructure authorities on multi-year funded frameworks, and residential is below 10% of output.
    • Who pays: ÖBB and ASFINAG in Austria
      • GDDKiA and PKP PLK in Poland
      • DB InfraGO and the Bundeswehr in Germany
      • Public contracts are about 70% of major contractors' books.
    • The fragility: the public customer's timing is political and administrative, which is why German volume is dated 2027 and why municipal staffing shortages matter.

    • Energy is hedged to 2028 and 2030, procurement is over 80% local, cement is covered by indexation on contracts over twelve months, and Porr owns quarries and gravel pits.
    • Labour and 7,650 subcontractors are the uncovered input, and subcontractor fatalities rose to 8 in 2025 from 1 in 2024.

Summary: Written Summary of the Investment Case (not rated) (E 3, BB 4)
The case in 8 to 10 sentences (the thesis as tested), kept in full at both ends of the record (Part 1, E 3, BB 4)
  • Porr is a self-help margin case in a medium-quality Austrian contractor, riding a funded multi-year public infrastructure cycle in Austria, Poland and, from 2027, Germany.
  • The change is an internal programme (overheads from 8.2% of revenue in 2020 to 7.4% in 2025, toward 6.7% in 2030; LEAN on every project above EUR 20m; a gate system and board approval above EUR 100m; a mix shift to civil works and specialised niches) that has lifted the EBIT margin from 2.3% in FY2023 to 3.1% in FY2025, with FY2026 guided at 3.2% to 3.3% and a 2030 target of 3.5% to 4.0%.
  • The outputs so far are in the statements: EBIT up 15.6% in H1 2026 on revenue down 1.1%, EPS up 34% to EUR 0.71, a backlog of EUR 9,839m worth eighteen months of capacity.
  • They are not in the estimates, which are flat, nor in the price, which has fallen a third from EUR 46.55 since the spring on a half-year cash outflow of EUR 257.9m, a Q2 order-intake fall of 32% and the syndicate's sale of a 4% block.
  • Along the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain the change has reached layer 5, the financial statements, and stopped before layer 6, the sell-side's estimates.
  • It produces EPS growth of about 9.5% a year to FY2028 and, at a blended 24-month-forward multiple of 8.7x, a 'Modal return' of about 15% a year over eighteen months and 12% a year over three years from EUR 31.25, below the 20% bar at both horizons.
  • The quiet risk is the below-EBIT line, which decides whether 15% is 15% or 10%.
  • The case is real, slow and not compelling at this price
    • It becomes compelling only below about EUR 29, or if the year-end cash print and a recovered order intake earn the shares the continental peers' multiple of about 9.5x.
