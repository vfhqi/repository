  2. The company's construction portfolio is highly contracted under inflationindexed CfDs and long-term corporate PPAs.

European project execution and contracts

1.
Accelerating German generation: The Borkum Riffgrund 3 project in Germany represents a milestone as one of the first zero-subsidy bids in the offshore wind sector. Orsted's press release from 1 September 2026 reports that the 913 megawatt asset has successfully entered full commercial operation. Instead of state-backed price guarantees, the project's revenues are fully secured through corporate power purchase agreements such as a long-term supply contract with Covestro. This merchant-based contract structure protects Orsted from power price volatility.
2.
Securing British cash flows: The 2852 megawatt Hornsea 3 project in the United Kingdom is progressing on schedule for commissioning between the fourth quarter of 2027 and the first quarter of 2028. Orsted's interim report from 13 August 2026 notes that the asset has secured an inflation-indexed CfD. This contract type provides a highly predictable revenue stream because the strike price is linked directly to the British consumer price index. The inflation protection mitigates macroeconomic risks during its operational life.
3.
Asymmetric partnership structures: Orsted completed a 50% farm-down of Hornsea 3 to Apollo in December 2025 to manage capital constraints. Deutsche Bank's report from 4 June 2026 highlights that the deal involves asymmetric cash flows which resulted in an upfront non-cash EBITDA loss in the 2025 accounts. However, the developer maintains that the transaction will be EBITDA neutral over the asset's total operational lifetime. This structural compromise shows that Orsted prioritizes near-term capital preservation over maximum earnings retention.

North American and Asian assets

4.
Progressing American construction: The 704 megawatt Revolution Wind project in the United States has achieved significant milestones with turbine installation reaching over 95% completion. According to a regulatory order from 13 October 2026, Rhode Island Energy is recovering costs under a long-term power purchase agreement set to commence commercial operations. This structure removes merchant exposure and stabilizes cash flows. The project is anticipated to achieve full commercial operation by the end of 2026.
5.
Mitigating US policy risks: The 924 megawatt Sunrise Wind project is 50% complete and is progressing toward commercial operations in the second half of 2027. Orsted's presentation from 13 August 2026 states that the asset has a nominal OREC contract with the State of New York. Unlike Hornsea 3, this contract is not indexed to inflation, exposing Orsted to cost overruns from US trade tariffs. To mitigate this, Orsted assumes conservative tariff rates of up to 50% in its impairment testing.
6.
Securing Taiwanese demand: The Greater Changhua 2b and 4 projects in Taiwan are 85% complete and are on track for commissioning by the back end of 2026. DNB Carnegie's report from 7 August 2026 indicates that Orsted has fully secured the offtake with semiconductor manufacturer TSMC via a corporate power purchase agreement. This corporate contract structure isolates Orsted from local merchant power price fluctuations. The successful completion of this project is expected to trigger a 50% divestment deal later in the year.

Operational earnings contributions

7.
Sustaining mid-term growth: Orsted's management is targeting group EBITDA excluding new partnerships of more than DKK 32 billion in 2027. Morgan Stanley's preview from 7 August 2026 notes that the under-construction portfolio will add DKK 11 billion to DKK 12 billion of annual EBITDA from 2028. This incremental contribution represents an approximate 35% expansion over Orsted's 2026 baseline guidance. Consequently, these projects are the primary engine of the company's operating earnings growth through 2028.
8.
Comparing asset-level returns: Individual project earnings show that the US offshore wind assets will become major contributors to Orsted's post-2027 financial performance. Morgan Stanley's model from 7 October 2025 estimates that Sunrise Wind will generate DKK 3.3 billion in annual EBITDA, while Revolution Wind will contribute DKK 1 billion to group EBITDA. These figures compare favorably with peer utility EDP Renewables, which has faced higher merchant volatility in its onshore portfolio. Successful delivery of these US assets is essential to secure Orsted's target return on capital.
9.
Contrary evidence: The forecast of rising operational earnings is threatened by persistent supply chain bottlenecks and inflationary pressures. DNB Carnegie's note from 17 August 2026 highlights that rising financing costs and supply constraints have compressed project-level internal returns. Additionally, unexpected interest rate hikes in the US triggered a DKK 1.2 billion impairment charge in Q2 2026, proving that earnings forecasts remain highly sensitive to macroeconomic shifts. Therefore, actual earnings contributions could fall well below management's guided ranges.

Structural comparisons and risk management

10.
Managing macro exposures: Orsted utilizes a sophisticated hedging program to align its diverse offtake revenue profiles with its debt obligations. Orsted's disclosure from 19 August 2026 reveals that approximately 50% of its revenues are fully indexed to inflation. This compares with approximately 35% nominal fixed revenue and 15% merchant exposure. The company matches its inflation-linked cash flows against its operating costs to protect its operating margins from severe cost escalation.
11.
Evaluating peer terms: Orsted's contract terms are more conservative than those secured by some of its major European peers. Deutsche Bank's research from 3 September 2026 notes that other developers face more severe stop-work and tariff exposures on peer US assets, such as Equinor's Empire Wind. This contrast indicates that Orsted's decision to maintain 100% ownership of Sunrise Wind was accompanied by strict capital discipline. This risk-averse strategy helps Orsted preserve its investment-grade credit ratings.
12.
Other side of the story: While Orsted has secured substantial contracted prices, some analysts believe that macro factors are far more influential than specific project milestones. Morgan Stanley's update on 9 October 2026 argues that the major stock debate is driven by global bond yields rather than individual project completions. Consequently, even if all five under-construction assets commission on schedule and meet their EBITDA targets, persistent high interest rates could still depress the company's equity valuation. Therefore, operational successes might not translate directly into share price appreciation.

Section 3 – Polenergia Offshore Wind Pipeline and Polish Baltic Integration

Section summary
  1. Polenergia's Polish Baltic pipeline represents a key long-term operational earnings driver with construction progressing on schedule.
  2. The inflation-indexed contracts-for-difference protect future Baltic project cash flows from electricity price volatility.
  3. Meeting the ambitious strategic targets for 2030 remains contingent on overcoming supply chain inflation and project delays.

Project specifications and timelines

1.
Accelerating offshore progress: Polenergia has reached an advanced stage of development on its Baltic offshore wind farms. Together with its joint venture partner Equinor, the company is constructing the Bałtyk II and Bałtyk III projects with a combined capacity of 1.44 GW. Offshore installation of all 100 wind turbine foundations was completed successfully in the Baltic Sea by September 2026. This crucial milestone indicates that the projects are progressing strictly on schedule.
2.
Secure project funding: Financing for the Baltic developments has been fully resolved through external arrangements. The joint venture partners secured non-recourse project finance totaling approximately EUR 5.8 billion from an international syndicate. Polenergia's share of this debt stands at PLN 4.7 billion based on exchange rates as of June 2026. This massive capital raising ensures that the company can fund its core development program without relying solely on internal cash generation.
3.
Sequential commissioning schedules: The Baltic wind farms will commence power generation in a phased manner over the next two years. Under current schedules, the company expects both Bałtyk II and III to deliver first power in 2027. Commercial operations are set to follow in the first half of 2028 for Bałtyk II and the second half of 2028 for Bałtyk III. Consequently, these assets are on track to generate substantial operating revenues in the 2028 financial year.

Contract pricing and inflation indexation

4.
Indexed price protection: The revenue profiles of the Baltic assets are shielded by Poland's contract-for-difference regime. The baseline strike price was initially established at 320 PLN/MWh in 2021. Due to cumulative inflation indexation, the expected price in the 2028 contract has escalated to approximately 486 PLN/MWh. This automatic price escalation ensures that Polenergia can offset long-term operational inflation and secure predictable cash flows.

Polenergia's expected offshore CfD strike price escalation from 2021 to 2028

5.
Mitigating merchant exposure: Polenergia's long-term commercial strategy prioritizes price stability over spot market exposure. The company has successfully hedged 83% of its planned 2027 power output at a weighted average net price of 408 PLN/MWh. For 2028, the company has already secured hedges for 63% of its total generation. This cautious hedging program minimizes merchant volatility, meaning that earnings risks are driven by construction execution rather than power prices.
6.
European contract comparisons: Polenergia's Polish offshore contracts compare favorably with those of international peers. For instance, other developer projects have faced fixed nominal offtake contracts that lack inflation indexation. In contrast, Polenergia's strike price increases from a 320 PLN/MWh baseline up to 486 PLN/MWh by 2028. This structural indexation means Polenergia faces much lower margin compression than developers with unindexed nominal contracts.
7.
Capital risk management: Polenergia has taken extensive steps to insulate its construction budget from interest rate fluctuations. The project companies entered into Deal Contingent Hedge transactions which were later novated into target interest rate hedges. These derivative agreements successfully hedge approximately 90% of the joint venture's planned exposure to EURIBOR volatility. This comprehensive risk management framework ensures that interest expenses remain highly predictable throughout the capital-intensive construction phase.

Operational earnings and 2030 target

8.
Solid current foundations: Polenergia's existing operations provide a profitable base ahead of the Baltic integration. The company generated sales revenue of PLN 1.75 billion and EBITDA of PLN 214 million during the first half of 2026. Millennium Dom Maklerski reported on 1 October 2026 that Polenergia's EBITDA rose 21.1% year-on-year in the second quarter of 2026. These solid historical results demonstrate that the developer is maintaining a strong financial baseline before its offshore capacity comes online.
9.
Speculative strategy targets: The group's Strategic Plan 2030 targets a tripling of its 2025 operating earnings. Polenergia's CEO stated that the 1.44 GW Bałtyk II and III projects will more than double the group's generation potential. However, achieving the 2030 target depends on the subsequent 1.56 GW Bałtyk I project, which has not yet received a final investment decision. Consequently, the long-term earnings targets remain speculative until Bałtyk I reaches final approval.
10.
Escalating development costs: The capital intensity of the Baltic projects has increased due to technical adjustments and unexpected events. In April 2026, Polenergia approved EUR 28.9 million in compensation to Hanab Projects for unforeseen ground conditions. Furthermore, changes in landfall design added an estimated EUR 43.5 million to the export cable contracts. These cost overruns are compressing project returns, meaning that Polenergia's future EPS may face near-term downside pressure from rising CAPEX.

Regulatory integration and peer comparison

11.
Baltic grid integration: Onshore grid connection works are advancing rapidly to support the offshore capacity. Construction is ongoing at the onshore substation near Ustka and the 400 kV transmission line to the Słupsk- Wierzbięcino substation. This grid infrastructure is essential to transmit the expected 5.0 to 5.5 TWh of annual Baltic wind generation. Securing these transmission routes is critical to ensure that commissioned capacity converts to earnings.
12.
Contrary evidence: The timeline for Polenergia's earnings growth is threatened by severe bottleneck risks in the global marine supply chain. Polenergia's report on 29 September 2026 warns that high demand for installation vessels has drastically extended service delivery schedules. Additionally, intense competition for skilled labor and specialized offshore ships continues to drive up installation prices. Therefore, supply constraints could delay full commercial operations past the guided 2028 window.
13.
Advanced stage validation: Despite global supply pressures, physical progress on the Baltic projects remains ahead of many regional peers. While some European offshore developers have paused projects due to rising costs, Polenergia has successfully installed all foundations and substations. The group's H1 2026 report indicates that permitting readiness for all key offshore works has been fully secured. This execution track record suggests that Polenergia is de-risking its construction pipeline compared to broader sector laggards.
14.
Other side of the story: While asset-level evidence shows that Bałtyk II and III are progressing on schedule, macroeconomic factors could still undermine Polenergia's growth trajectory. Polenergia's H1 2026 report highlights that persistent high interest rates significantly increase financing costs for unhedged debt. Furthermore, extreme exchange rate volatility between the euro and the zloty introduces substantial FX transaction risk. Consequently, even if all physical offshore assets are completed on time, macroeconomic headwinds could still erode projected net earnings growth.
