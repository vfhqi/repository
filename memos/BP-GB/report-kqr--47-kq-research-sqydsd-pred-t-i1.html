Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. BP's financial trajectory depends on executing its simplified corporate strategy and resolving capital distribution uncertainties.

Defining the Key Investor Questions

1.
Pivotal question: The investor focus is "how likely is company guidance to be raised, met or reduced at the earnings events over the next 18 months, and how likely are sell-side consensus forecasts, and the net breadth of revisions, to move higher or lower over the same period?"
2.
Strategic realignment: BP has restructured its reporting segments into Upstream and Downstream from January 2027 to simplify its operational footprint and focus on core profitability. This structural shift under CEO Meg O'Neill aims to drive capital discipline and improve returns across both divisions, directly affecting future earnings per share. The simplified model is expected to eliminate past structural complexities that weighed on valuation relative to peers.
3.
Accelerated deleveraging: BP has prioritized the settlement of its outstanding Deepwater Horizon liabilities using excess cash flow, which deferres its share buyback restart framework. This focus on reducing total financial obligations to a target range of $33-35 bn by end-2026 is critical for lowering the company's long-term cash break-even point. While the pause in buybacks may limit near-term earnings per share accretion, it significantly de-risks the balance sheet for long-term investors.
4.
Contrary evidence: Bernstein Research argued in November 2025 that focusing on earnings performance versus consensus somewhat misses the point for integrated energy majors. The broker stated that cash flows matter most to distribution because the oil and gas sector functions as an income rather than a growth sector. Thus, short-term earnings per share fluctuations may be less critical to valuation than structural cash resilience.

Industry Headwinds and Valuation Catalysts

5.
Normalising margins: Refining margin normalization across the European sector represents a major headwind that could depress downstream earnings over the next 18 months. Morgan Stanley models European refining margins converging back to mid-cycle levels of about $12 per barrel by 2028. This shift makes operational excellence and discipline key to maintaining the dividend growth trajectory.
6.
Operational updates: Rothschild & Co Redburn raised its 2026-28 earnings per share forecasts for BP by an average of 4% following a strong third-quarter performance. This upgrade was primarily driven by robust underlying results in the gas and low carbon energy and customer divisions. However, the broker notes that cost savings have yet to fully reach the bottom line, making the upcoming strategy day critical.

Section 2 – Current Guidance and the Corporate Track Record

Near-Term Financial and Operational Guidance

1.
Improved fiscal efficiency: BP lowered its full-year 2026 underlying tax rate guidance to between 31% and 34%. This adjustment reflects an underlying effective tax rate of 31% achieved during the first nine months of the year. Management expects this rate to remain highly sensitive to the geographical profit mix.
2.
Disciplined capital allocation: BP maintains its 2026 capital expenditure guidance at $13.5-14.0 bn. Capital spending in the third quarter of 2026 was reported at $3.0 bn, keeping the company on track to meet its full-year parameters. This disciplined budget compares favorably to previous years where capital limits were frequently tested.
3.
Stable depreciation outlook: BP expects its full-year 2026 depreciation, depletion, and amortization to be $17.0- 17.5 bn. This outlook remains unchanged from prior quarters despite ongoing asset reorganizations. Achieving this guided range will support the company's medium-term return on capital targets.
4.
Divestment proceeds upgraded: BP upgraded its 2026 divestment and other proceeds guidance to approximately $9 bn. This is about $1 bn higher than the prior guidance of around $8 bn. The increase was driven by the timely sale of a 5% stake in Aker BP.

Deleveraging was accelerated by strong third-quarter cash generation and a $0.53 bn working capital release.

Elevated debt metrics: BP historically carried a weaker balance sheet compared to its integrated European peers. Optimized liability management: BP expects total financial obligations to fall to between $33 and $35 bn by the end of 2026. This projected range represents a significant reduction from the $44.3 bn reported in the third quarter of 2026. Deleveraging is supported by imminent large-scale divestment proceeds including $6 bn from Castrol.
