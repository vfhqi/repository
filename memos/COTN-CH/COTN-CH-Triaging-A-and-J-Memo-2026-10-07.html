[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = D

Thesis. COMET is a cyclical demand-inflection case with a self-help margin overlay: compelling as a business story and not compelling as an investment at CHF 375.2 (2026-10-06). The market is already paying a mid-range multiple for what consensus expects the peak-cycle year of 2028 to earn, so on the framework's convention the modal return from here is negative; the same change, with the same mechanism, would clear the 20 percent bar at about CHF 240 to CHF 285.

  • The change is external, cyclical and of the strongest kind the framework recognises.
    • Wafer-fab-equipment spending is inflecting on AI, 3D memory and gate-all-around logic, and the evidence is orders and customer capital spending rather than forecasts.
    • Lam Research, Comet's largest customer, raised its 2026 WFE growth forecast to 38 percent from 27 percent
      • Applied Materials guided 2026 systems shipment growth above 30 percent
      • The front-end makers report visibility into 2027.
    • Comet's own book-to-bill was 1.48 for H1 2026 on orders of CHF 354.8m, and Q2 2026 orders of CHF 210m were up 45 percent on Q1.
    • Half of Comet's exposure is memory, the most cyclical end of the sector, and management itself dates the peak to 2028.
      • The framework treats a demand inflection as cyclical until proven structural, and so do we.
  • The internal change is real and multi-dimensional, and it decides how much of the cycle reaches earnings per share, not whether the cycle arrives.
    • A Penang plant completed on schedule in Q2 2026 more than doubles matching-network capacity at labour costs a fifth of Switzerland's
    • An efficiency programme worth CHF 20m to CHF 30m a year is dated 2028
    • The chair, the finance director and two board seats have changed within eighteen months
    • The X-ray perimeter is being cut back.
    • The duplicate-cost period through H2 2026 and 2027 is the execution risk the research keeps returning to, and the chief executive is unchanged in his fifth year.
  • 'Financial Outputs' have turned up at every layer the chain reaches.
    • H1 2026 sales rose 12.7 percent in constant currency (5.6 percent reported, to CHF 239.8m)
    • The EBITDA margin rose to 13.1 percent from 10.1 percent despite CHF 4.5m of Penang one-offs
    • EBITDA rose CHF 8.4m on CHF 12.6m of extra sales, a 67 percent drop-through
    • Consensus EPS for FY2027 is up 41 percent in six months to CHF 14.91.
    • FY2026 is guided at CHF 540m to CHF 570m of sales and a 14 to 17 percent EBITDA margin against CHF 457m and 10.1 percent in FY2025, and the sell-side calls the guide soft.
    • Cash is the laggard: H1 2026 free cash flow was CHF -19.3m on capital expenditure of CHF 29.4m and consignment inventory, and consensus free cash flow is still negative for FY2026.
  • The change has travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain: resources (a new chair and CFO), choices (all-in semiconductor; a declared harvesting phase), actions (Penang; restructuring), key operating metrics (orders), financial statements (H1 2026) and the sell-side's estimates.
    • Nothing in the chain is waiting to show
    • What is waiting is the Penang saving and the memory greenfield leg, both dated 2027 to 2028.
  • What we think it earns: EPS of CHF 1.57 (FY2025, reported) rising to about CHF 6.5 in FY2026, CHF 13.4 in FY2027 and CHF 17.8 in FY2028, 10 to 12 percent below consensus (CHF 7.64, CHF 14.91, CHF 20.10), because consensus FY2028 revenue of CHF 876m already sits above the top of management's own 2028 range and the margin needs the Penang saving to flow in full.
    • Then a 2028 to 2029 peak and a moderate 2030 dip (revenue about minus 10 percent; EPS CHF 15.0), because the last cycle took revenue down 32 percent in a year and management calls 2028 the peak.
  • What it costs is the problem.
    • At CHF 375.2 the shares trade at 19.6 times consensus 24-month-forward EPS of CHF 19.16, which by its date weighting is almost wholly consensus FY2028: the 35th percentile of the stock's ten-year range, the 52nd of five years, and the ten-year median (22.1 times) on our lower path.
    • The market is paying a median multiple for the peak-cycle year.
    • The stock's own history runs the other way to its earnings: the 24-month-forward multiple fell to 11.1 times at the last peak and rose to 31.8 times at the trough.
    • The price is up 82.1 percent in twelve months, so the contracted catalysts are in it
      • The three-month return of minus 3.7 percent says the market has paused since the July print.
  • So the chain holds to the last link and breaks there.
    • Because the framework values each horizon on the earnings two years beyond it, the eighteen-month horizon (April 2028) is valued on April 2030 earnings, which on our path sit below 2028's
    • At a conservative 19.6 times that is CHF 313.6 including the dividend, about minus 11 percent a year, and the three-year horizon is CHF 327.6, about minus 4 percent a year, against the 20 percent bar.
    • The case clears the bar only on the joint event of a cycle that proves structural into 2030 and a multiple that holds the ten-year median or better: possible, about 20 to 25 percent, not probable.
    • A plausibly worse drop-through from EBITDA to EPS (a 25 percent tax rate, higher depreciation, higher interest) moves the entry level from about CHF 240 to about CHF 220 and does not move the rating.
  • The pattern it fits is 'Demand Inflection', crisply in mechanism and at the framework's minimum count, with 'Major Corporate Change' as a loose secondary.
    • The pattern says growth above 40 percent is the point and is to be priced as cyclical
    • The price does not.

  • The case therefore is a material change with an immaterial return at this price.

    • We would bet on the same change at about CHF 240 on the modal path or CHF 285 on the blend of our two branches, or at any price if the evidence moves the 2028 peak out of the window.

  • Rating = D, because the Building Block measures materiality along the causal chain and the chain breaks at its last link: the input change forces are strong ('Change Forces' B), the financial outputs are improving sharply ('Financial Outputs' B), and the modal return is negative ('Modal return' F)

    • The parts bind, since a return below the bar makes the change immaterial to the capital we recommend on at this price, but they do not bind all the way to F, because the change and the outputs are real and a price about 35 percent lower would make the same chain clear the bar.
    • The rating is a judgement, not an average: it sits one notch above the weakest Element and two below the others, because the return is what the Building Block exists to test.
    • Not C, because a C would say the return is merely short of the bar, about 13 to 17 percent, and on the convention it is negative
    • Not F, because an F would say the change itself is immaterial, and it is not.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The strongest case against this rating is the sell-side's, in its strongest form.
    • The case against, stated at its strongest: consensus is still too low and the multiple is too low with it.
      • Deutsche Bank sits 29 to 31 percent above consensus on 2027 and 2028 EBITDA and carries FY2028 EPS of CHF 24.62 against the median CHF 20.10, because consensus under-counts the Penang saving, the efficiency programme and Synertia's conversion of 100-plus engagements.
      • The FY2026 guide is set to be beaten: the midpoint implies an H2 2026 run-rate of CHF 315m and the H1 book-to-bill of 1.48 supports more
        • The planning currency of USD/CHF 0.70 against spot near 0.80 is a built-in upgrade.
      • The AI-driven cycle is structural (WFE from USD 100bn to USD 170bn by 2030; over 70 new fabs; gate-all-around and 3D DRAM intensity), so there is no 2030 down-leg to look through.
      • At 19.6 times Comet is the cheapest equipment name in its sector, at a 40 to 45 percent discount to VAT against a historical 20 percent
        • As the upgrades arrive it re-rates towards the sector median of 25 times.
        • At the sector median 25.3 times on CHF 19.16 the shares are worth CHF 485 within twelve to eighteen months, which is where the consensus target sits
        • At 25 times on a raised FY2028 EPS of CHF 24 they are worth CHF 600, a 35 percent-plus annual return.
    • What we concede: that consensus FY2027 EPS is more likely to rise than fall over the next six to twelve months
      • That FY2028 EPS of CHF 24 is plausible if memory greenfield spending arrives and Penang's labour saving flows through in 2028 (UBS CHF 24.01 and Deutsche Bank CHF 24.62 against the median CHF 20.10)
      • That the discount to VAT is partly unearned
      • And that on the structural branch at the ten-year median multiple the return is a C and at the "defendable" 75th-percentile band an A.
    • Why we hold our view: the bull outcome is the joint event of two conditions, each short of probable.
      • That the cycle does not peak inside the three-year window: possible, roughly 40 percent, because management itself dates the peak to 2028, half the exposure is memory, the last cycle took revenue down 32 percent in a year, and the framework treats a demand inflection as cyclical until proven structural.
      • That the multiple on earnings beyond the peak holds at or above the ten-year median: possible given the first, roughly 50 percent, because the five-year low of 11.1 times was set at the last peak and the peers against which the re-rating is argued sit at the 70th to 90th percentiles of their own histories.
      • Their product is about 20 to 25 percent, "possible", and a 20 percent chance of plus 25 percent a year does not make a modal return.
        • The blend of our two branches at those odds returns about zero over eighteen months: the shares are fairly priced for the odds and dear on the modal case.
      • The company's own record argues against paying for the top of the brokers' range: four of five guidance windows missed or reset, and a peak-to-peak PCT sales gain of only 7 percent over 2022 to 2028 on the bull broker's own numbers.
    • Where the case against would win: customer visibility extending into 2029 and management dropping the word "peak" from its 2028 language.
      • That would move the structural branch from possible to plausible and the case from park to progress, which is why the rating is D rather than F.
Change Forces: are the input change forces sufficiently strong? (E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because the external demand force is of the strongest kind the framework recognises (sector-level, evidenced by industry data, customer capital spending and Comet's own orders, with visibility to 2027 and a management-dated peak in 2028) and the internal forces meet the screen's "three strong" rule, while the headwinds (currency, generator competition, tariffs) trim the direction rather than reverse it.
  • The external force: it is the case
    • The internal forces (Penang, efficiency, new products, a refreshed board) decide how much of it reaches earnings per share.
  • Propagation: the forces have reached the financial statements (H1 2026) and the sell-side's estimates
    • What has not yet propagated is the Penang saving itself, which is built but not producing.
  • Not A, because the demand force is cyclical and treated as such until proven structural, and the largest internal force (Penang) is mid-execution with duplicate costs still running in H2 2026.

    • Advanced Energy's Q2 2026 sales rose 30 percent

Competition: is competitive intensity easing or tightening? (KQ 2, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because competitive intensity is tightening at the margin where the thesis wants growth (generators) and in the legacy lines (China), while the core (capacitors and matches) is unchanged.
  • Generators: Advanced Energy launched eVerest generators and NavX matches specifically to replicate Synertia's fully digital capabilities, and Lam Research appears to favour them.
  • Capacitors and matches: Meidensha (40 percent of global capacitors) is proposing direct supply to US OEMs
    • Kunshan GuoLi (about 10 percent) copies designs for domestic Chinese tools
    • MKS benchmarked Advanced Energy's pricing to push matching-network prices down.
  • Offsets: XP Power exited RF in late 2025
    • Comet won a trade-secret case against XP Power
    • Advanced Energy still buys vacuum capacitors from Comet.
  • Direction: headwind, mild.
    • Not D, because the tightening is in a segment Comet does not yet earn from.
Disruption: is a disruption force strengthening or being disproved? (KQ 3, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because the solid-state and electronically-variable-capacitor threat to mechanical matches is neither strengthening visibly nor disproved, and qualification cycles of years give a buffer.
  • The precedent: ASM International's acquisition of Reno Sub-Systems (2022) embeds sub-millisecond tuning in deposition tools, bypassing vacuum-capacitor matches
    • Experts call Comet's anti-solid-state narrative "designed to protect legacy capacitor market share".
  • The limit: the research itself rates full displacement of low plausibility if power limits remain restricted well below thirty kilowatts
    • Comet counters that solid-state matches impair repeatability at high power.
  • Inspection: in packaging inspection, Rigaku (30 percent share), Bruker and PVA Tepla's acoustic method compete with the CA20.
  • Direction: latent headwind.
    • Not B, because an in-sourcing precedent exists (ASM International with Reno; Naura with Beiguang) and the market can price it at any time.
Political and regulatory: is a change moving demand, pricing or cost? (KQ 4, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because a 31 percent US tariff on Swiss imports and tightening export rules are live costs, partly bypassed by shipping to Malaysian and Singaporean assembly hubs, while regional subsidy programmes add demand.
  • Tariff: the 31 percent tariff hits the 20 percent of sales that are US-direct, most sharply in X-ray modules where Varex blocks pass-through.
  • Export rules: the proposed MATCH Act could restrict Swiss sub-supplier sales to Chinese entities
    • Chinese domestic customers are 10 to 15 percent of sales.
  • Offsets: Comet's Swiss heritage lets it sell to Naura and Amec where US rivals cannot
    • EU, Japanese, Korean and Indian subsidies extend tool demand.
  • Direction: mild headwind.
    • Not D, because the semiconductor shipments route around the tariff.
