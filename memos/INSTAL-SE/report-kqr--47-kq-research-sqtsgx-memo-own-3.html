Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The Spring 2027 Swedish collective wage round is expected to result in moderate wage growth of 3.3 percent.
  2. Strong domestic recovery and disciplined union negotiations should prevent a destructive wage-price spiral for Swedish technical installers.

Defining the primary research question

1.
Critical inquiry: "For Instalco AB (INSTAL-SE) and the Swedish technical installation industry, what is known about the spring 2027 collective wage round for the electrical installation agreement (Installatörsföretagen with Svenska Elektrikerförbundet) and the plumbing and ventilation agreement (Installatörsföretagen with Byggnads), including the negotiation timetable, the parties' opening positions and demands as published, the outcome of the 2023 and 2025 rounds for the same agreements, the Riksbank's and the Swedish Mediation Office's expectations for 2027 wage growth, and the SEB and Nordea forecasts cited in 2026 for negotiated minimum and actual wage increases; how Swedish installation contracts under AB 04, ABT 06 and partnering forms typically treat wage and material cost indexation (the Entreprenadindex and other index clauses, their coverage and lag) compared with the Danish AB 18 indexation above twelve months that InstallatørGruppen describes; what Instalco, Bravida, Assemblin Caverion and Nimlas have disclosed about the share of their revenue or backlog on fixed-price contracts and on indexed terms; and how the 2021 to 2023 inflation period affected the margins of Swedish installers on fixed-price work signed before the cost increases, as reported by the companies and by DNB Carnegie, Deutsche Bank and ABG Sundal Collier at the time?"

Financial impact and earnings sensitivity

2.
High labor sensitivity: Technical installation is highly personnel-intensive, making wage rounds the single most critical driver of operational expenses. A former divisional head and historical reports show that personnel expenses typically constitute about 60 percent of net sales for technical service companies. If the spring 2027 collective wage round results in higher than expected wage growth, installers will face immediate margin pressure unless they can successfully pass these costs onto customers. Therefore, the outcomes of negotiations between Installatörsföretagen and unions will directly determine whether margins can expand toward historical targets over the next 18 months.
3.
Unindexed pricing risk: Installers often enter fixed-price contracts that do not allow for price adjustments, creating substantial profitability risks during periods of high cost inflation. While indexation clauses like the Entreprenadindex help mitigate material cost volatility, any mismatch or lag in indexation can lead to severe margin compression. Historical evidence from the 2021 to 2023 inflation period highlights how unindexed fixed-price backlogs depressed installer operating margins for multiple quarters. Consequently, understanding the exact contract terms and indexation mechanics across Swedish and Danish operations is vital to forecasting earnings per share stability in volatile markets.
4.
Varying contract structures: Nordic peers exhibit different structural exposures to fixed-price work, which determines their relative vulnerability to wage shocks. For instance, Instalco reported that 43 percent of its 2025 revenue came from fixed-price projects. In contrast, companies with higher service shares, such as Bravida with nearly 50 percent service revenues, typically experience greater margin resilience during wage inflation. Analyzing these contract disclosures allows investors to identify which installers possess the most defensive contract profiles and pricing power during the upcoming collective agreement negotiations.

Connected industry macroeconomic questions

5.
Connected demand drivers: Several closely related questions regarding Nordic construction activity will also shape the installation industry's pricing power and volume recovery. Investors must monitor whether residential building permits have bottomed out, as housing construction has faced its deepest downturn since the 1990s. Additionally, the rate at which public infrastructure, data centers, and defense investments translate into active tenders remains a key uncertainty. The timing and strength of this volume turnaround will determine whether installers can maintain strict project selection discipline or face intensified price competition.
6.
Other side of the story: While rising labor costs and fixed-price contracts present clear earnings risks, a recovering Swedish construction market and improved pricing power could offset these headwinds. Leading indicators in late 2026, such as rising transaction volumes and falling interest rates, suggest that the industry is approaching a broad-based recovery. If demand for critical infrastructure and energy-efficiency renovations accelerates, installers may find it much easier to implement price increases that fully absorb wage inflation. Thus, cyclical volume growth could ultimately neutralize the negative impact of wage hikes on group EBITA margins.
