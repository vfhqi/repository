Double Framework Mechanics

1.
Inception-based fee structure: The first performance fee model utilizes an absolute high-water-mark framework based on the maximum net asset value reached by the fund. This specific mechanism allows performance fees to be withdrawn only when the fund achieves a completely new peak. Because the reference period spans from inception, the bank secures stable, high-quality fee generation during sustained market upswings. This design ensures that investors only pay for genuine capital appreciation over the life of their investment.
2.
High-on-high fee structure: The second model utilizes a high-on-high mechanism that restricts performance fees to when the current net asset value exceeds the level on the previous withdrawal day. This framework operates on a five-year rolling reference period and crystallizes annually with daily accrual. This multi-year reset structure means that earnings volatility remains highly sensitive to medium-term market corrections. Consequently, prolonged market downturns can lock these assets out of performance fee eligibility for several fiscal periods.

Peer Performance Fee Models

1.
Abolished performance fees: FinecoBank represents the most radical departure from traditional Italian performance fee structures by completely eliminating them. Since 2020, the bank's asset management arm, Fineco AM, has committed to a strict "No Performance Fees" strategy as a core marketing tool to ensure price transparency and competitive pricing. Consequently, FinecoBank cedes short-term capital market fee upside but secures highly predictable, low-volatility recurring revenues.
2.
Absolute and relative benchmarks: Anima Holding utilizes three distinct criteria for charging performance fees depending on the specific product mandate. These include absolute high-water marks, relative high-water marks where returns must exceed a designated benchmark, and standard "fee against benchmark" models. Performance fees are calculated and accrued daily at a rate of 20% but crystallize annually, representing a structured, multi-tier approach to capturing market outperformance.
3.
Capped incentive structures: Banca Mediolanum applies performance fees of 15% to 20% depending on the fund range, including its flagship Challenge and Best Brands products. Crucially, Mediolanum enforces a strict maximum performance fee cap of 1% of the fund's net asset value. This regulatory cap limits extreme revenue surges during bubble periods but cushions the franchise against extreme subsequent downcycles.

Sector Crystallization and High-Water Marks

4.
Standardized annual crystallization: Across the Italian wealth management sector, the implementation of European Securities and Markets Authority guidelines has standardized performance fee crystallization to a calendar year-end frequency. Banca Mediolanum and Azimut completed their structural alignment to these guidelines in 2019, implementing absolute high-water marks alongside this annual frequency. This transition reduces the unfair application of short-term fees on retail clients and aligns manager incentives with annual performance cycles. Accrual versus payout: Anima Holding's prospectuses confirm that performance fees are calculated and accrued daily at each valuation point. However, the actual crystallization and payment to the manager occur strictly on an annual basis at the end of the calculation period. This setup prevents intermediate market swings from resulting in premature cash withdrawals before the annual performance is locked in.
5.
Regulatory compliance evolution: The transition to annual crystallization was historically accompanied by a parallel increase in recurring management fees. To offset the loss of frequent crystallization, Italian players raised their base asset management fees by 10 to 15 basis points on average. This pricing pivot has permanently improved the baseline margin profile of the sector while muting historical peak-year fee spikes.
