Storskogen: repaired and buying again, under-forecast by consensus, but a low-quality business whose 18-month return leans on the multiple

Prepared for Richard on 24 October 2026, the day after the Q3 2026 report. Share price SEK 11.0; market capitalisation SEK 19.1bn; enterprise value SEK 27.6bn. Money is in Swedish kronor (SEK) unless stated. The end notes define the two earnings-per-share bases and the forecasts used.

#0Summary of key insights, implications and conclusions

Storskogen offers a central 18-month return of about +27% (+37% at the guideline multiple) because consensus omits the acquisitions that drive its growth. More than half of that return depends on the share price multiple rising, so the position should be held as a dated bet that the February 2027 report will test.

What it is. Storskogen is a Swedish serial acquirer that owns 114 small, founder-built businesses. In 2025 it had sales of SEK 33.1bn and adjusted EBITA of SEK 3.1bn, a 9.4% margin. It has three business areas:

  • Industry (43% of sales), which is in a short-cycle upturn;
  • Trade (29%), whose consumer-facing units are weak and whose professional channel is firm;
  • Services (28%), of which the part tied to Swedish construction, a fifth to a quarter of the group, has not turned.

Storskogen is the lowest-quality business in the Nordic compounder league. Its margin is 9.4% against Lifco's 22%; its return on capital employed (ROCE) is about 10% against 14% to 21% for the leaders; it has no moat; and it is a price-taker in construction. It also has the sector's worst recent history: 119 acquisitions in 2021 to 2022 at up to 10x, leverage that peaked at 4.35x, a SEK 920m impairment and SEK 1.5bn of sales sold at a loss.

Storskogen is interesting now because the financial repair is finished and the acquisition engine has restarted. Net debt has fallen by more than SEK 4bn in three years. Interest cost is down by a quarter (net financial items of SEK 473m for nine months against SEK 633m a year earlier). Debt maturities now run from 2027 to 2030, twenty loss-making units have been sold, and the Q3 settlement of SEK 738m of legacy minority buyouts cut the minority put liability from SEK 1.7bn to SEK 1.1bn.

Since mid-2025 the company has bought seventeen businesses, with SEK 950m of annual sales, at 6.5x to 7.0x EBITA, and management says the pace "steps up quarter by quarter". The Q3 2026 report beat the only detailed broker by about 5% on EBITA (SEK 806m, a 9.7% margin); organic EBITA grew for a second quarter; and Industry's margin rose to 10.0%.

The sector research names Storskogen the "purest deal-restart case" in a cohort that the market is expected to reward over the next 12 to 24 months. The reason is mechanical: consensus models only the businesses a company already owns, so every completed acquisition is an upgrade.

The forecasts of record grow adjusted EPS from SEK 1.13 in 2026 to SEK 1.44 in 2028, slightly above DNB Carnegie's SEK 1.11 and 1.36. Adjusted EBITA rises from SEK 3.18bn (a 9.3% margin) to SEK 3.87bn (9.9%), and free cash flow after leases rises from SEK 1.6bn to SEK 1.85bn. Leverage drifts down to about 2.1x even with SEK 1.9bn to 2.5bn a year of acquisitions from 2028. Roughly 55% to 60% of the EBITA growth to 2028 is bought, about 20% comes from the recovery of two under-used pockets (construction Services and Product Solutions), and the 2026 EPS step comes mostly from the banked interest saving.

Reported EPS is only about two-thirds of adjusted EPS. After deducting the minority charge of about SEK 130m a year, which the model does not carry, reported EPS is about SEK 0.70, 0.77 and 0.90 for 2026 to 2028.

I differ from the market on three points and agree with it on three.

#1Tailwinds in the operating environment, and the absence of headwinds

The operating environment is about average. A real but short-dated industrial upturn helps 43% of the group, while Swedish construction and rising Swedish interest rates weigh on the rest.

Three external forces matter to Storskogen, and they point in different directions. The table below sets out each force by business area, with its evidence and the model's assumption.

Industry, the largest business area, is in a genuine short-cycle upturn. The Swedish manufacturing purchasing managers' index (PMI) is 58.1 and the Eurozone's is 52.9, its highest since May 2022. European customers finished destocking in H1 2026, Industry's order books have been "solid and stable for several quarters", and Industry's incremental margin has printed at 19% to 21% twice.

The construction-exposed quarter of the group has not turned. Swedish housing starts are less than half their 2021 level, Eurozone construction is in its fifty-third month of contraction, and Infrastructure Services faces "intense industry-wide price pressure" with negative organic sales. In Trade, Nordic consumer retailers are destocking health and beauty products.

The interest-rate regime sits over all three business areas. The Riksbank is expected to raise its policy rate in November 2026 (to about 2.0%, on a path toward 2.4% by late 2027), the market prices further tightening by the European Central Bank, and European swap rates rose 70bp in August and September. In the words of the one house covering the cohort, the sector's multiples are "hostage" to the gap between their earnings yield and the swap rate.

The market for buying small companies, which sets a serial acquirer's economics, is structurally kind. Succession-driven sellers form an inexhaustible pool, prices outside auctions are stable at 6x to 8x, and the large competitors are rational and rarely bid against each other. Competition is tightening mildly at the small end, where Tången has paid 4.8x and Auroora, Röko and search funds are active.

The krona works in both directions. The company stopped hedging currency in 2026. A stronger krona therefore cuts reported growth by 1% to 2% a year, and it lifts Trade's import margins by 50 to 100bp in 2026 to 2027, with a lag of one to four quarters.

Inference. The industrial tailwind is real but narrow and short-dated. It reaches only 43% of the group, Industry's backlogs cover only months, the sector's own advice is to "watch for a restock plateau by Q4 2026", and the European upcycle is six quarters into a cycle that historically lasts fourteen.

The headwinds are concentrated in Swedish construction and Swedish interest rates, and the two are correlated: a rate rise that lowers the share price multiple also hurts Services, the weakest business area, and raises the finance cost. No external force is structural in either direction.

So what. The environment makes flat-to-modestly-rising 2026 margins and 3% to 4% organic growth deliverable. It is not strong enough to carry Storskogen to its own 10% margin and 15% growth targets without internal action. The segment assumptions in the table above (Industry organic growth 5% fading to 3% through 2027; Services flat to low single digits, with no construction recovery before H2 2027; Trade flat, with a currency-driven margin gain that reverses when the krona does) are the right ones to model.

Strongest counter-case. The macro evidence can be read more bullishly. PMIs are at four-year highs, German industrial production rose 2.3% in August, Bravida and Instalco are printing 8% to 9% organic growth in Swedish installation on data centres and public works, and the Swedish Transport Administration (Trafikverket) has a SEK 1,171bn plan and a 48% rise in road maintenance budgets. If Storskogen's Services units sat in those sub-markets, Services would already be recovering. Services' nine-month organic EBITA of −16% says instead that its units sit in private construction, alongside Volati's S:t Eriks (−9%), not alongside Instalco.

Market view and mine. The sell-side and I agree on the Industry upturn and on the construction drag. I am more cautious than DNB Carnegie, which calls the outlook "relatively resilient in coming months", on interest rates, which I treat as the dominant force because a rate rise hits the multiple, Services and the finance line at once. I am more positive on the acquisition market: the supply of sellers is not the constraint on the engine, and higher-for-longer rates keep heavily indebted newcomers out of the bidding.

#2Internally-driven corporate change forces, and the absence of problems

Storskogen has finished everything it controls on the balance sheet. The acquisition restart and the Services turnaround, which depend on the organisation and the cycle, are in motion but unproven.

The hard changes are financial, and they are complete. Interest-bearing net debt has fallen from 4.35x adjusted EBITDA at the 2022 peak to 2.3x to 2.5x. The 2027 bond has been refinanced into 2029, and a new SEK 1bn 2030 bond pays 265bp over STIBOR (the Stockholm interbank offered rate), against 687.5bp at the 2023 peak. Twenty divestments in 2023 to 2024 removed SEK 1.5bn of low-margin sales and SEK 110m of losses, and Motavo and Perfect Hair followed in 2025 to 2026. A 2026 share savings programme vests in full only at 15% annual EBITA growth and also tests cash conversion.

The soft changes are in motion and unproven.

  • The acquisition restart: seventeen deals, SEK 950m of sales, at 6.5x to 7.0x EBITA. The average deal brings SEK 56m of sales, against Lifco's SEK 96m.
  • The pivot in what it buys: toward business-to-business customers, recurring revenue and niches outside Sweden (LEP in Switzerland, Darlington in the UK, Safi in Singapore, Duratec in Germany, Hawedo in Benelux).
  • The Q3 settlement of SEK 738m of minority put options: management calls it "the last piece of the balance-sheet puzzle" and "immediately accretive to EPS", because the minority charge shrinks with it. That charge was SEK 136m in 2025, 11% of net profit and fifteen to twenty times the ratio at peers.

Five problems remain.

  • Services has had three heads in under a year. Co-founder Ahlgren stepped back, Kronstrand lasted three months, and Head of M&A Ekström is the interim head, so the person running the acquisition restart is also running the turnaround of the weakest business area.
  • There is no internal audit function across 114 units in about 30 countries, just as the unit count begins to rise again. The 2020 fraud cost SEK 30m, and failures in this sector surface late, through impairments.
  • Currency hedging has been discontinued, so Trade's margin and the finance line now follow the krona; Q3's finance line carried a translation loss.
  • The chief executive's own shareholding is small, with no purchases in the open market.
  • The targets set at the 2021 listing have never been met.

Inference. The company has done everything it controls and nothing yet that the cycle controls. Its internal change is broad on the financial side and narrow on the operating side. The acquisition engine is restarting at a tenth of the scale needed to move a SEK 33bn group, and the organisation that must triple its deal volume is the same one that could not retain a head of Services.

So what. Treat as banked the lower finance line, the leverage path, the price discipline on deals and the shrinking minority leakage. Treat the deal cadence as a statement of capacity until the quarterly deal count rises to three to five. Model the Services recovery later and smaller than management implies until a permanent head is in place and Services has printed two consecutive quarters above a 10% margin.

Strongest counter-case. The repair record is the strongest evidence in the file, and the company delivered it faster than the sell-side expected, in a tough environment. A management that beat its own deleveraging schedule, settled its put options early and restarted deals ahead of broker expectations may be under-estimated on the acquisition engine too. The fall in the put liability from SEK 1.7bn to SEK 1.1bn in one quarter also removes the balancing loop that most worried earlier research.

Market view and mine. The market's narrative fully reflects the repair and the restart. It under-weights the leadership vacuum in Services, which gets one sentence in one broker note although it is the main risk to the margin target, and it ignores the missing internal audit function and the unused SEK 1,337m buyback authorisation. On balance, the internal positives the market has not articulated (shrinking minority leakage, and cash redirected from buyouts to deals from 2027) outweigh the negatives it has not articulated.

What would make me more positive

  • A quarterly deal count of three or more, with SEK 150m or more of acquired sales.
  • A share buyback.

What would make me more negative

  • Another departure from the Services leadership.
  • A quarter with no deals.
  • A control failure at unit level.

#3Industry structure, business model, financial profile and management

Storskogen is a mediocre business that has been made safe again. It sits at the bottom of the Nordic compounder league on every quality measure, and nothing in the forecasts closes that gap.

The industry is kind, but it rewards the buyers Storskogen is not. The market for buying founder-owned small and medium-sized companies is unconcentrated (the top five Nordic compounders hold under 5% of it), deep (about 25,000 quality targets in Europe, plus a wave of German successions) and rationally priced. Value in that market accrues to the buyers with the lowest cost of capital and the best reputation with sellers.

Storskogen's competitive position in that market is weak. It pays more for debt and equity than the leaders, its reputation with sellers was dented by the 2024 divestments, and its compounding spread (the gap between the multiple of its own shares and the multiple it pays for businesses) is two to three turns, against twelve to fifteen for the leaders. The table below compares each measure.

The business model has no group-level moat. Storskogen is a collection of local niche positions whose units are "independent price-takers" with no backward integration. Group procurement saves only SEK 20m on SEK 33bn of sales, and the head office of 79 people is several times the size of Lifco's or Indutrade's. Pricing power is cost pass-through with a lag in Industry (demonstrated again in Q3), absent in construction Services, and driven by currency in Trade.

The financial profile is weak on returns but strong on cash. The adjusted EBITA margin has been flat at 9.0% to 9.4% for three years. Goodwill is SEK 18bn (43% of assets), amortisation of acquired intangibles runs at SEK 700m a year, and the minority charge takes 11% of net profit. At unit level, however, the business is capital-light: capital expenditure is 2% of sales, the return on working capital is 62%, cash conversion is 70% to 80%, and free cash flow of SEK 1.6bn to 1.9bn a year is a 9% yield on the market capitalisation.

Management is proven in repair and untested in growth. Chief executive Hansson (since 2024) and chief financial officer Glader (since 2019) have a strong record on what they control and a weak one against targets. The founders hold 7.4% of the capital and 44.5% of the votes, chair the investment committee, and were the architects of the 2021 to 2022 buying spree. The board added an international investor's perspective (Adam Parker) in May 2026.

Inference. The team running this mediocre business is proven in defence and untested in offence. The quality gap to the peers is the fundamental reason for the valuation discount, and nothing in the model closes it: self-help and mix lift the margin toward 10% to 10.5% by 2028 to 2029 and ROCE toward 11% to 12%, which is where consensus already sits. The investment case therefore rests on execution and the cycle, not on the quality of the business.

So what. Do not pay for convergence with Lifco, Addtech or Indutrade at any point in the horizon. The right comparators are Sdiptech (11.2x EV/EBITA, 2.9x leverage, a 21% core margin) and Indus Holding (8.5x P/E, 9.3% ROCE). The right prize is a re-rating from about 9x to 10x to 12x EV/EBITA if the acquisition engine proves itself, and no more.

Strongest counter-case. Storskogen is a professionalised owner with scale in sourcing deals and financing them, compared with new entrants; its addressable market is unlimited; and its surviving units are probably "not inferior" in their niches. Industry's ability to raise prices with raw materials while growing 6% and expanding its margin is evidence of adequate market positions. Management's capital allocation since 2023 (a 7x price cap, digestible deal sizes, cash-settled incentives and no cash bonus for two years) is exactly the behaviour the proven compounders exhibit.

Market view and mine. I agree with the market's verdict on quality. I differ from the commentators who argue for a "permanent discount": the sector pays for the direction of travel as well as the level of quality (Sdiptech re-rated 42% in a year on a clean-up and organic growth), so a stock at the bottom of the league can still earn a re-rating of two to three turns by moving up one place.

#4Conservatism of guidance; proactivity with the sell-side; track record of delivery and upgrades

Storskogen hits the targets it controls and misses the ones it promises. The sell-side has cut its estimates for a year, but the cuts have stopped and the next revision is more likely up than down.

Storskogen gives no annual guidance, by policy ("I don't want to guide", in the chief executive's words). Its mid-term targets were set at the 2021 listing, restated at the November 2024 capital markets day and never raised, and the company has missed its growth and margin targets in every year they applied. In 2025, EBITA fell 3% and the margin was 9.4%. The other targets are organic growth above GDP plus 1 to 2 points and a 15% dividend payout.

The 15% EBITA growth target is now out of reach. It implies 2027 adjusted EBITA well above SEK 4bn, against the model's SEK 3.47bn and even its bull case of SEK 3.65bn. The chief executive now says organic profit growth is "a prerequisite", which is as close to a concession as this management comes. The qualitative steers given since Q2 ("cautiously optimistic", "step up quarter by quarter", "at least 10% is reasonable", and leverage "toward the lower end" by year-end) have so far been honoured.

Despite the beats, the sell-side has cut for a year. Over twelve months, FY2026 consensus EPS fell 18.5% and FY2027 fell 13%, on margins and items below EBITA rather than on volume. The mean target price fell 11% in three months: DNB Carnegie cut from SEK 18 to SEK 15 in four steps, the last two days before a beat. Over the same period the share of buy ratings rose 20 points to 80%, and the Master Dashboard rates sell-side momentum D. The cuts have now stopped: one-month revisions are flat to +0.6%.

Inference. Storskogen's guidance framework is the opposite of conservative: ambitious long-run targets, no near-term anchor, and misses explained by external factors rather than followed by a reset. The company has no record of raising anything, because it has nothing it can raise.

The sell-side has already discounted the targets (consensus EBITDA grows 0.7% in 2026 and 5.4% in 2027) and models delivery rather than capacity, which is right. The one guidance event likely in the window is a re-base of the targets, with a probability of about 65% by the Q4 2027 report and possibly as early as February 2027. The market will read a re-base as a cut, although it changes no estimate.

So what. Anchor on consensus EBITA and the model, not on any company number, and treat the targets as the upper bound of the range of outcomes. Because the company gives no near-term anchor, each quarterly report moves outer-year estimates more than it would at a peer that guides, in both directions. The seasonally weak quarters (Q1 and Q3) are where cuts happen, and the strong quarters (Q2 and Q4) are where small beats happen.

Strongest counter-case. A management that refuses to guide and consistently beats a stale broker preview in the strong quarters is, in effect, keeping expectations low. The 15% hurdle in its own pay plan says it believes the acquisition engine can reach that growth over a cycle, and Lifco runs the same no-guidance model on a 20x multiple.

Market view and mine. The sell-side and I agree that the 15% target is unreachable: every DNB Carnegie note since September calls it "highly challenging". I differ on the Q4 2026 number. DNB's pre-report forecast for 2026 implies fourth-quarter EBITA of about SEK 750m, 8% below last year's SEK 816m. That looks too low against a flat nine-month run-rate, three newly consolidated deals, a krona tailwind in Trade and an easy 9.4% margin comparison. The model's SEK 875m looks more likely, so the next move in the one visible broker's numbers should be up.

#5Pricing power, operating leverage, financial leverage, cost scope and feedback loops

Revenue growth gears into profit through several weak-to-moderate channels, not one strong one. Storskogen has no real pricing power, no cost programme and no flywheel; only Services and the multiple are geared enough to surprise.

Pricing is cost recovery with a lag, not real pricing power. Industry recovers input inflation within a quarter (its Q3 margin rose to 10.0% from 9.5% while material costs were still rising), Trade recovers it within two to four quarters and is enjoying a krona windfall now, and construction Services does not recover it at all until volume returns. Price contributes about 2 points of the 3% to 3.5% organic growth and roughly nothing to the margin.

Operating leverage is low for the group and high in two pockets. At group level, materials are 55% of sales, personnel 23% and central costs SEK 170m. The two pockets are construction Services and Product Solutions, about SEK 10bn of sales with under-used capacity, where 25% to 35% of each extra krona of sales drops through to profit; in Q1 2026, Services EBITA fell 30% to 36% on flat sales. The rest of the group drops through 12% to 20%, and Industry's incremental margin has printed at 19% to 21%. One point of organic volume moves group EBITA by SEK 70m to 100m within the year.

Financial leverage gave a one-off boost in 2026, and the boost is mostly banked. Net financial items of about SEK 630m sit against EBITA of SEK 3.2bn to 3.9bn, so a 10% move in EBITA moves adjusted pre-tax profit by about 12%. The step-down from SEK 828m in 2025 is worth about SEK 0.09 to 0.11 of EPS, and three-quarters of it is already banked. After 2026 the finance line is flat to rising (SEK 620m to 690m), because deals add debt and the Riksbank's path adds 25 to 65bp to SEK 6bn to 7bn of floating-rate debt.

The scope to cut costs is small and defensive. The decentralised model rules out a group cost programme. At most SEK 50m to 80m is conceivable at the centre, and SEK 100m to 150m from standardisation, procurement and pruning in Services over 2027 to 2028.

The acquisition loop is self-funding, but its balancing loops cap reported EPS. The reinforcing loop works as follows: cash buys EBITA at 7x, EBITDA rises, debt headroom widens, and consensus catches up deal by deal. At SEK 1.6bn to 2.3bn of deals a year this loop funds itself and does not reach the 2.5x leverage ceiling within four years. The balancing loops are rising amortisation (SEK 56m a year per SEK 1bn deployed), goodwill at 78% of the purchase price, put liabilities that grow with subsidiary performance, and the lack of internal audit as the unit count rises. #9Riskiness and simplicity of the forecasts and expectations

The case is simple and testable, but risky. Its two operating drivers are unproven at the scale assumed, both answer to Swedish interest rates and construction, and every tail risk is on the downside.

The case is simple to state and concentrated in its drivers. Its summary is four words: repaired, restarting, cheap, under-forecast. Two operating variables, the deal cadence and the Services margin, account for three-quarters of the growth the forecasts add, and one banked financial item, the interest saving, accounts for most of the 2026 growth.

That concentration is both a strength and a weakness. It makes the case testable every quarter, through the deal count, the sales and EBITA of each year's acquisitions, Services organic EBITA and leverage. But both operating drivers are untested at the scale assumed, and both answer to the same two external variables, Swedish interest rates and Swedish construction, so a bad outcome in one is likely to come with a bad outcome in the other.

#13Summary conclusion

Storskogen is the clearest "repaired and buying again" case in the Nordic compounder cohort, and the market is pricing it as if the repair were the whole story.

The positives are mostly done or in motion. The balance sheet is fixed, the interest step-down is banked, and the legacy minority claims were cut by a third in a quarter. The acquisition engine is buying at 6.5x to 7.0x, with seventeen deals behind it. Industry is in a short-cycle upturn with a demonstrated 19% to 21% incremental margin, organic EBITA has grown for two quarters, and the estimate cuts stopped two days before a beat.

The market's expectations are set too low for the next six months. Consensus is two brokers on two different EPS bases; the detailed broker models no new deals and leverage falling to 1.7x; and the narrative has just swung to "no headroom" two months before leverage falls. That gap is favourable for the next six months and moderately favourable for months six to eighteen. The sector's own precedents (Sdiptech, Indus) say that a restart which holds leverage below 2.5x and prints delivery earns two to three turns of re-rating.

The negatives are structural. The business is at the bottom of its league on margin, returns and moat. A quarter of it is tied to Swedish construction, which has not turned, and is run by an interim head who is also the head of M&A. The growth in the forecasts is mostly bought, by an organisation that must double or triple its deal volume. A third of adjusted EPS is amortisation and another 7% is minorities. The targets will be missed and re-based. And three-quarters of the model's 18-month return is a multiple that interest rates and the cohort set.

The fundamental outcomes are not unusually skewed to the positive; the probability of sell-side upgrades is. Upgrades are skewed up for a structural reason, because consensus omits the acquisition engine, while the range of fundamental outcomes stays wide and skewed to the downside. The right way to hold Storskogen is therefore as a dated, testable position. The Q4 report in February 2027 (organic EBITA, EBITA of SEK 840m to 910m, Services organic EBITA, leverage at or below 2.3x, three or more deals) and the monthly deal releases will either confirm the turn and justify the guideline multiple, or fail to, in which case the trough multiple applies.
