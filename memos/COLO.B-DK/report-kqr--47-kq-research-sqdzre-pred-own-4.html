Section 2 – ROIC Calculation Methodology and Capital Base

ROIC Numerator and Tax Assumptions

1.
Diluted returns: Coloplast reports its Return on Invested Capital (ROIC) after tax and before special items, which reached 16% in the FY 2025/26 period. This measure utilizes a normalized operating profit after tax (NOPAT) to reflect underlying performance. Company targets aim to rebuild this return to around 20% by the FY 2029/30 period. This strategic goal is designed to rebuild long-term investor confidence.
2.
Normalized performance: Coloplast's management uses ROIC before special items as a primary metric to align executive incentives with long-term shareholder value. This key performance metric holds a substantial 20% weight in the annual cash bonus calculations. By focusing on this return, the company aims to ensure disciplined capital allocation. This internal focus is designed to improve long-term earnings growth.
3.
Decreasing tax: Coloplast assumes a normalized effective tax rate of approximately 22% during its current strategic period. This normalized tax rate has benefited from the intellectual property transfers of both Kerecis and Atos Medical. However, the actual effective tax rate reached 20.1% in the FY 2025/26 period. This tax structure allows the company to protect its underlying earnings power.
4.
Rising pressure: Coloplast's tax expense was historically affected by the transfer of Kerecis intellectual property from Iceland to Denmark. This transfer resulted in an extraordinary tax payment that raised the effective tax rate to 41% in the prior year. However, normalized calculations exclude these special items to show standard operating efficiency. This methodology helps investors assess the long-term sustainability of the tax rate.
5.
Contrary evidence: One-off gains on exchange rate adjustments of DKK 188 million inflated the net profit in FY 2025/26. These balance sheet adjustments reduced net financial items and artificially supported the overall return on capital. Investors must note that these non-operating items do not represent structural margin improvement. Thus, the underlying operational return might be weaker than the reported figure.

Denominator and Capital Base Components

6.
Expanded denominator: Coloplast defines its invested capital denominator as assets less cash and marketable securities, plus accumulated goodwill amortized before October 2002, less non-interest-bearing debt including provisions. This denominator has expanded significantly to DKK 36,254 million by September 2026. This capital base expansion has been driven by the Atos Medical and Kerecis transactions. Consequently, the enlarged asset footprint has diluted the historical capital efficiency.
7.
Substantial assets: Coloplast's capital base includes total assets of DKK 46,269 million as of 30 September 2026. Intangible assets represent the largest component of this base, valued at DKK 26,804 million. Meanwhile, property, plant, and equipment assets stood at DKK 7,359 million at the end of the period. These heavy investments in fixed assets are meant to support international manufacturing expansion.

1.

2.
Underwhelming divestment: Coloplast's planned portfolio optimization would face severe headwinds if the Interventional Urology divestment fails to realize the expected DKK 4.50 bn to DKK 6 bn in proceeds. This would force management to maintain a flat dividend per share, limiting near-term capital reallocation flexibility.
3.
Depressed valuation: Coloplast's valuation premium over its historical average and key competitors could suffer permanent erosion if execution continues to disappoint. Bernstein Research observes that the company's revised 2030 ambitions already reflect increased external pressure, including pricing reforms and competitive bidding. If organic growth drops below the rebased 5% to 7% range, the stock's multiple will likely contract further to reflect lower earnings quality. This operational underperformance would erode the historical premium over Convatec.

Competitive and Supply Dynamics

4.
Aggressive pricing: Coloplast operates in an increasingly commoditized wound dressings market where competitors are using aggressive pricing to secure hospital contracts. Experts indicate that Smith & Nephew dropped its dressings prices by 40% overnight in certain European markets to capture massive market share. Additionally, medical distributors are introducing own-brand wound care alternatives at a 5% to 10% discount, further undermining Coloplast's premium positioning. This intense commercial rivalry limits the company's pricing power and threatens long-term gross margins.
5.
Increasing vulnerability: Coloplast's centralized manufacturing footprint represents a key risk to its long-term cost structures. DNB Carnegie flags that the group remains highly exposed to raw material price inflation, with plastics and paper representing approximately 50% of its cost of goods sold. Escalating geopolitical tensions in the Middle East could drive double-digit increases in freight rates and energy costs. These supply disruptions would compress the company's gross margin unless offset by substantial manufacturing automation.
6.
Improving performance: Convatec is executing a highly successful operational turnaround that narrows the quality gap with Coloplast. Deutsche Bank Research expects Convatec's adjusted operating margin to reach the mid-20% range by 2027, driven by product mix and productivity. This strong execution supports a narrowing of Convatec's historical valuation discount. If Convatec continues to gain market share, Coloplast's earnings growth will face increasingly direct competitive pressure.

Disrupted Expectations and Warnings

7.
Regulatory intervention: US Medicare regulators hold the ultimate power to disrupt Coloplast's financial expectations through reimbursement adjustments. The Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services implemented a flat outpatient reimbursement of $127/cm² in January 2026, which triggered a DKK 3 bn impairment on Kerecis assets. Any further regulatory price cuts in the inpatient channel would severely damage biologics profitability. Investors must monitor CMS policy updates as a primary risk to medium-term earnings.

Intensifying competition: Low-cost challengers in the Chinese ostomy market are actively eroding Coloplast's dominant regional market share. Experts note that local players are introducing cheap alternative products that appeal directly to price-sensitive hospital procurement departments. This competitive pressure has forced Coloplast to implement a new sales channel strategy and write down regional inventories. Consequently, failure to defend this high-margin segment would permanently lower China growth expectations.

Early indicators: Investors must closely track specific early warning indicators to detect shifts away from the consensus trajectory. A lack of rapid reacceleration in inpatient volumes would indicate that outpatient declines are spilling over. Additionally, any delay in the US launch of the Intibia system would dilute projected Interventional Urology growth.

Contrary evidence: Bipartisan legislative support in the US Congress could completely insulate Coloplast from upcoming competitive bidding pressures. DNB Carnegie highlights that the Access to Medical Supplies Act proposed on 9 October 2026 would exclude ostomy and urological supplies from competitive bidding for five years. If passed, this legislation would remove the single most significant regulatory overhang from 2028. This positive policy shift would protect long-term Chronic Care margins from anticipated pricing cuts.
11.
Other side of the story: While operational hiccups and inflation have diluted near-term capital returns, Coloplast's core Chronic Care franchises retain an exceptionally resilient annuity business model. Consequently, these fundamental strengths could quickly restore the historical valuation premium.
