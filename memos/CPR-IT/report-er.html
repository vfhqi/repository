Section 1 – Brief company description and history

Section summary
  1. Campari demonstrated superior growth fundamentals compared to global spirits peers over the last three years.
  2. Absolute and relative share price performance remained robust in 2026, outpacing consumer staples indices.
  3. Broker sentiment moved in a positive direction prior to the earnings release, supported by targeted upgrades.
1.
Historic description: Davide Campari-Milano N.V., founded in 1860, is a leading multinational player in the global branded spirits industry that manages a portfolio of over 50 premium and super-premium brands. The Italian group operates 21 production facilities worldwide and leverages its own direct distribution network in 27 countries to market its brands in over 190 nations. Through this direct model, Campari ensures tight control and operational agility over its core global priorities. By focusing on direct route-to-market channels, the group is able to achieve industry outperformance and share gains across key on-premise and off-premise accounts.
2.
Clear leadership: Campari operates in the global consumer staples sector and maintains a highly dominant position as the undisputed leader in the high-growth aperitif category. The brand portfolio is organized around key priority houses including the House of Aperitifs, House of Agave, and House of Whiskey and Rum. These prioritized divisions allow the group to concentrate capital on high-margin, fast-growing brands like Aperol, Campari, and Espolòn tequila. Such structured portfolio prioritization has helped the company sustain robust volume and value growth despite broader industry challenges.
3.
Superior growth: Campari has delivered highly resilient top-line organic growth over the last three years, significantly outperforming its major European spirits peers. While large-cap beverage companies experienced flat to declining sales, Campari achieved a robust organic sales growth CAGR of 5.0% from 2022 to 2025. In contrast, Diageo registered an organic CAGR of negative 0.8% and Pernod Ricard posted negative 1.0% over the same three-year period. This relative performance demonstrates that Campari's aperitif-led portfolio insulated the group from destocking pressures that heavily impacted its rivals.
4.
Subdued performance: Diageo's fundamental growth has decelerated over the last three years as weak demand in North America and China pressured key spirits categories. The group was forced to abandon its medium-term organic net sales growth target of 5% to 7% due to prolonged US market volatility. Rothschild & Co Redburn assumed operating margins would remain broadly stable at around 28% to 29% for fiscal 2026. Analysts noted that Diageo's heavy reliance on scotch and brown spirits underperformed Campari's highmomentum aperitif mix over the multi-year cycle.
5.
Soft execution: Pernod Ricard struggled with subdued top-line momentum and structural margin pressures over the three years leading up to 2026. The group's performance was severely dragged down by destocking in the United States and weak consumer spending in China. Cost-saving initiatives under a €1 billion operational efficiency program were designed to help defend operating margins. This aggressive cost-cutting regime, combined with subdued underlying growth, reflects a fundamentally weaker volume trajectory than Campari during the same timeframe.
6.
Sharp decline: Rémy Cointreau suffered the most acute fundamental downturn among European spirits peers over the last three fiscal years. The cognac specialist saw its organic sales collapse at a negative 12.8% CAGR from 2022 to 2025 due to a severe US cognac destocking cycle. Volume growth for the three months ending January 2026 fell by negative 3.3% as cognac depletions remained deeply depressed. This drastic reduction in volume growth highlights how Rémy's hyper-concentration in premium cognac exposed it to intense cyclical volatility compared to Campari's stable aperitif demand.
7.
Relative strength: Campari shares demonstrated strong defensive qualities and solid outperformance against European peers over three-month and twelve-month horizons. Rothschild & Co Redburn reported that Campari achieved a relative share price outperformance of 8.5% over the three months leading up to October 2026. Over the longer twelve-month period, Campari recorded a minor relative decline of negative 6.2%, which was far superior to Pernod Ricard's plunge of negative 39.5%. This performance differential confirms that equity markets valued Campari's superior top-line resilience over its highly leveraged competitors.
8.
Strong absolute gains: Campari registered impressive absolute share price gains throughout the first ten months of calendar 2026, comfortably outperforming the broader market. Bernstein reported that Campari shares achieved an absolute gain of 21% year-to-date up to October 2026, while the MSCI Europe Consumer Staples Index rose by just 8%. This advance significantly outpaced key peer Diageo, which posted a 12% absolute gain, and Pernod Ricard, which declined by negative 8%. The robust absolute performance reflects rising investor confidence in Campari's sustained commercial momentum heading into the peak summer trading season.
9.
Positive sentiment: Sell-side sentiment toward Campari improved significantly in the six months leading up to the October 2026 earnings release. Morgan Stanley upgraded its investment rating on Campari from Equalweight to Overweight on 14 September 2026. This upgrade was driven by the group's ongoing volume market share gains and a highly favorable outlook for the core aperitif portfolio. This structural rating shift signaled that major brokers anticipated a highly resilient financial delivery relative to struggling global spirits peers.
10.
Stable price targets: Broker price targets for Campari remained remarkably stable over the six months preceding the third-quarter earnings update. Morgan Stanley adjusted its price target for Campari from €6.10 on 9 June 2026, to €6.20 on 30 June 2026, and further up to €6.50 on 30 July 2026. This upward trajectory in the target valuation reflected a solid commercial execution during the peak H1 season and a lower-than-expected impact from international trade disputes. Consequently, the stable pre-earnings valuation baseline set a constructive bar for the stock ahead of the October release.
11.
High expectations: Consensus expectations for Campari's third-quarter results remained highly optimistic, reflecting its history of consistent execution. Deutsche Bank reported on 15 October 2026 that it expected organic revenue growth of 3.6% for the third quarter, compared to the company-compiled consensus of 3.4%. This forecast was supported by anticipated organic sales growth of 4.0% in the United States and 2.6% in European markets. The demanding organic expectations proved that the market anticipated no material deceleration in Campari's primary growth engines.
12.
Other side of the story: The historical outperformance of Campari could be severely tested if persistent consumer wallet contraction in Germany and category softness in the United States trigger a prolonged volume slowdown. If organic growth dips below the confirmed 3% full-year target, broker forecasts may face substantial downward revisions. This risk is further compounded by unseasonable summer weather in the northern hemisphere, which historically threatens the highmargin aperitif category and undermines structural EBIT margin accretion expectations.
