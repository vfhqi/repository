EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Gerresheimer enters this review period as a highly leveraged restructuring case following severe accounting restatements and a radical portfolio simplification, contrasting sharply with the operational recovery trajectories of primary packaging peers such as Schott Pharma. The company's preliminary Q1 2026 results (reported August 27, 2026) and audited FY 2025 results (reported June 29, 2026) mark a critical inflection point, as management executes an aggressive cash-preservation strategy while simultaneously divesting high-margin assets to stabilize its distressed balance sheet.

The audited FY 2025 results revealed reported revenues of €2,321 million, representing a 16.6% reported increase due to the first-time consolidation of Bormioli Pharma, though on a constant-currency, pro forma basis, organic revenue growth stalled at just 0.3% year-over-year. More critically, the group's adjusted EBITDA for FY 2025 came in at €384 million, down 1.0% from the restated €388 million delivered in FY 2024, resulting in an adjusted EBITDA margin of 16.5% for FY 2025, representing a severe margin compression of 300 basis points compared to the restated 19.5% margin of the prior year. This operational deterioration was exacerbated by conscious production halts designed to optimize working capital rather than maximize asset utilization.

The preliminary Q1 2026 results demonstrated the painful trade-offs inherent in this capital preservation approach. For the preliminary Q1 2026 period, Gerresheimer delivered group revenues of €524 million, representing 4.4% organic sales growth, which materially exceeded the Vara consensus expectation of just 0.3% organic growth. However, adjusted EBITDA for the first quarter of 2026 fell sharply by 19% to €66 million, compared to the restated €81 million in Q1 2025, missing published consensus expectations of €83 million by a significant margin.

This severe profitability miss was driven by deliberately reduced asset utilization in the Moulded Glass segment, where the segment suffered an EBITDA decline to €6 million, representing a margin of only 4.5% due to inventory reduction measures. The BaFin accounting investigation remains a persistent governance overhang. Gerresheimer's historical growth trajectory was severely disrupted by a federal BaFin investigation into its accounting practices, with the supervisory authority auditing the 2024 accounts over premature revenue recognition practices under bill-andhold agreements.

In response, the group completed intensive internal investigations and officially ceased bill-andhold practices, with these corrective actions resulting in restating historical revenues downwards by €45 million. More specifically, Gerresheimer's finalized audits led to a retroactive downward restatement of FY 2024 results, dropping revenues by €45 million to €1,991 million, with adjusted EBITDA for FY 2024 corrected downward by €31.4 million to €388.0 million, stripping 110 basis points from the company's previously reported FY 2024 adjusted EBITDA margin.

Although management has completed corrective actions, the regulatory probe remained incomplete as of June 2026, creating ongoing compliance uncertainty that restricts nearterm investor confidence.

The most transformative development for the investment case is the €1.5 billion divestiture of the Centor pharmacy vial business and the global Primary Packaging Plastics division to Apax Partners. In late July 2026, Gerresheimer entered a definitive agreement to sell its Centor business and global Primary Packaging Plastics division to Apax Funds for an enterprise value of €1.5 billion, designed to refocus the company on high-value primary packaging and drug delivery, with proceeds also substantially reducing its net debt from a leverage of approximately 5x.

This transaction represents a radical portfolio contraction that will eliminate approximately 25% of group revenue but critically achieve the stated leverage target of below 3x net debt to EBITDA. The gross proceeds of €1.5 billion from the Apax transactions will be deployed entirely to reduce the group's highly elevated debt, with this cash inflow expected to reduce the group's current debt burden by approximately 70%, successfully reducing net debt to EBITDA leverage to a sustainable level of below 3.0x. - Earnings Calls However, this deleveraging comes at a substantial margin dilution cost.

Deutsche Bank's Friedrichs warned that the divestiture of Centor and PPP will heavily dilute the remaining company's profitability, with the sale reducing leverage to a pro forma 3.1x, while the remaining group's adjusted EBITDA margin is estimated to drop to 14.5%, significantly lower than the 17% to 18% adjusted EBITDA margin originally guided, occurring because Gerresheimer is selling its highest margin plastic assets to private equity. This structural trade-off means that while the balance sheet will stabilize, the remaining core business will operate at materially lower profitability than historical norms. Critical deviations from consensus centered on the severe Q1 2026 EBITDA miss and the structural margin dilution from the asset sales. Deutsche Bank's Friedrichs noted that the adjusted EBITDA margin compressed by 310 basis points to 12.6% in preliminary Q1 2026, with this severe margin erosion caused by a conscious trade-off to prioritize liquidity through temporary production halts and lower asset utilization.

The sell-side had underestimated the magnitude of management's willingness to sacrifice near-term profitability for cash flow generation, as evidenced by Q1 2026 free cash flow before M&A improving by €109 million to negative €32 million, compared to negative €141 million in Q1 2025, representing the company's best first-quarter cash flow generation since 2019, attributed to a €57 million reduction in Net CapEx and a €41 million positive swing in inventory management.

The cash-preservation strategy dominated operational decision-making throughout Q1 2026. In response to its strained financial situation, Gerresheimer entered an aggressive cash preservation mode in Q1 2026, including planned production halts to optimize inventories and selective investment cuts. While these temporary measures successfully delivered the best first-quarter free cash flow in five years, they severely impacted Q1 operating margins, with capital allocation remaining strictly focused on prioritizing liquidity over top-line growth.

Management defended these measures as necessary to prevent covenant breaches, though the approach has created a back-end loaded recovery profile for the full fiscal year 2026. Leadership instability remains a governance concern, as former interim CEO Uwe Röhrhoff voluntarily stepped down from his executive post on August 24, 2026, just weeks prior to the scheduled October 2026 conclusion of his mandate, originally appointed on November 1, 2025, to steer the company through its acute operational and financial crisis following the mutual departure of former chief executive Dietmar Siemssen, with his early exit leaving the firm without a permanent leader.

The company now operates under a transitional co-CEO structure, with Chief Financial Officer Wolf Lehmann and Management Board member Achim Schalk immediately assuming joint leadership of the company, with Lehmann focusing heavily on capital structure, debt management, and the ongoing corporate transformation offensive, while Schalk took direct operational charge of Containment and Delivery Systems and Primary Injectable Solutions.

The Gerresheimer Transformation Offensive (gto) represents management's operational response to the margin compression. The program targets an annualized adjusted EBITDA run-rate improvement of €50 million to €70 million across 2026 and 2027, with these savings executed across five workstreams supported by the Boston Consulting Group, and management expecting the full run-rate impact of these operational excellence and efficiency initiatives by 2028.

However, achieving these targets requires successful execution across procurement optimization, manufacturing consolidation, and overhead reduction while simultaneously managing the Centor and PPP carve-out processes.

The sell-side response to these results has been cautious, viewing the transactions as necessary financial triage rather than an operational re-rating catalyst. Bernstein's Le Louet viewed the €1.5 billion Apax asset sales as a major credit success that rescued the company from bankruptcy risk, allowing equity investors to shift their focus back toward orderly quarterly execution and the upcoming medium-term guidance. Conversely, Deutsche

Bank's Friedrichs maintained a highly cautious view, classifying the group as an ongoing major restructuring case, highlighting that the dramatic collapse in Q1 profitability demonstrates that the company's underlying operations remain in a deep transition. Looking forward, the investment case hinges on three critical execution milestones: first, successfully closing the Centor transaction in November 2026 and the PPP sale in H1 2027 to achieve the sub-3x leverage target; second, stabilizing Moulded Glass profitability sufficiently to enable a clean carve-out sale; and third, ramping high-value medical device manufacturing at Peachtree to offset the structural margin dilution from divesting Centor and PPP.

The company's extreme dependence on a heavily back-end loaded H2 2026 recovery creates significant execution risk, as Jefferies' Richardson noted that meeting the reiterated FY26 guidance requires an extremely back-end loaded performance in the second half of the year, depending heavily on accelerated customer medical device project ramps to offset standard glass weakness. In summary, Gerresheimer has successfully averted insolvency risk through aggressive portfolio surgery and creditor negotiations, but the remaining core business faces a multi-year margin recovery journey.

The preliminary Q1 2026 results demonstrate that management is willing to sacrifice near-term profitability to preserve liquidity, a trade-off that has delivered strong cash flow at the expense of operational momentum. The unresolved BaFin investigation, leadership vacuum, and structural margin dilution from divesting high-margin plastic assets ensure that this remains a distressed restructuring case rather than a conventional pharma packaging growth story, requiring at least three years to rebuild margins toward peer-level profitability of 20%.
