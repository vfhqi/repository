Outliers worth noting

The examination of bear evidence against Scout24 SE reveals three statistical outliers that deviate significantly from sector norms, warranting investor attention despite the overall weakness of the bear case. These outliers represent either extreme positioning relative to peers or unexplained anomalies that conflict with reported operational performance. The first outlier centers on Gold tier membership adoption, which remains confined to approximately 10% of professional customers compared to over 30% penetration at UK peer Rightmove.

This positioning places Scout24 in the bottom 5% of classified portal peers regarding premium package migration success, representing a structural divergence from the typical monetization trajectory observed across mature digital marketplaces. The magnitude of this gap is particularly striking given Scout24's 70% market share and dominant traffic position in Germany, suggesting either profound structural differences in German realtor economics or underexploited pricing architecture.

This adoption ceiling directly contradicts the bull thesis assumption of seamless tier migration driving unlimited ARPU expansion, creating a load-bearing vulnerability where headline customer growth masks underlying revenue quality deterioration. The 109% price spread between Bronze and Gold packages indicates substantial untapped monetization potential, yet actual conversion remains stubbornly suppressed, raising questions about whether German market fragmentation imposes an insurmountable willingness-to-pay ceiling among single-office agencies. The primary driver stems from share-based payment fluctuations and M&A earn-out revaluations, which introduce significant quarter-to-quarter noise that obscures underlying cash generation quality.

The €25.4 million LTIP cash settlement in H1 2026 represents a 9.6% drag on operating cash flow, highlighting how equity compensation structures can create material cash conversion friction despite strong headline EBITDA margins.
