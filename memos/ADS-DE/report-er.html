Broker reaction to opex overruns – shock (Bernstein): Bernstein's Sherman noted that the stock corrected by 12% immediately following the Q2 print, as a rare profit miss after nine consecutive beats caught the market off guard and focused attention on higher-than-expected marketing costs and additional DTC-driven distribution headwinds. Messaging criticism – management execution (Bernstein): Bernstein's Sherman pointed out that management could have executed better messaging to the Street regarding the near-term SG&A deleverage, which would have mitigated the sudden mismatch and minimized the stock's negative shock value. EBIT target shortfall – disappointment (Morgan Stanley): Morgan Stanley's Aubin highlighted that the Q2 EBIT of €574 million missed the company-collated consensus of €623 million by 8% and undershot his own estimate of €670 million by 14.4%, driven by a nearly €170 million quarter-on-quarter step-up in marketing opex.

Value perception – unwarranted de-rating (Deutsche Bank): Deutsche Bank's Cochrane re-iterated his Buy rating, arguing that the company continues to deliver robust operational performance with improving brand equity and that the post-results sell-off makes the valuation of approximately 13x calendar 2027 PE look exceptionally cheap. EBIT delivery comparison – mismatch against expectations (Deutsche Bank): Deutsche Bank's Cochrane observed that in absolute terms Q2 was a solid quarter, but against a rising tide of World Cup expectations, the EBIT print of €574 million fell 8% below consensus, disappointing short-term investors.

Guidance and Estimate Revisions

EBIT estimate changes – minor downgrades (Deutsche Bank): Deutsche Bank's Cochrane reduced his fullyear calendar 2026 EPS forecast by 3.7% to €9.46 to reflect the Q2 EBIT miss, but kept outer-year forecasts unchanged based on structural support from a 10% EBIT margin target in 2027. Guidance conservatism debate – upside potential (Bernstein): Bernstein's Sherman chose to maintain her full-year 2026 operating profit estimate of €2.5 billion, which sits well above the company's official €2.3 billion guidance midpoint, arguing that the company's guidance is overly conservative and fails to reflect stronger H2 DTC sales and wholesale re-orders.
