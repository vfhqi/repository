B.1 Business Description 10 findings
Business Description -- A plain-English description of what the company does, how it makes money, and its primary market.
1.
Core business. Elia Group operates statutory monopoly high-voltage electricity transmission grids in Belgium (wholly owned) and northeastern Germany (80% of 50Hertz), plus small non-regulated ventures
2.
Revenue mix. Group revenue reached EUR 4,273m in FY25, up 4.1% year on year, split roughly EUR 2,721m Germany and EUR 1,604m Belgium
3.
Regulatory economics. Approximately 98% of group EBITDA derives from cost-plus and revenue-cap regulated tariffs, eliminating volume risk through automatic multi-year tariff reconciliation
4.
Margin profile. Group EBITDA margin rose from 37.3% in FY24 to 41.9% in FY25; the source also cites regulated EBIT margins near 42%, a conflict flagged in F.2 rather than silently resolved
5.
Balance sheet and funding. Net financial debt reached EUR 14.1bn (up 7% year on year); a EUR 2.2bn equity raise and EUR 900m hybrid bond issuance de-risked near-term funding
6.
Capital intensity. The EUR 31.6bn 2024-2028 capital expenditure programme is roughly four times the prior five-year run-rate, driving a record EUR 5.2bn deployed in FY25 alone
7.
Cash generation. Free cash flow was negative EUR 3.8bn in FY25 (Morgan Stanley; ARS separately cites negative EUR 2.88bn to EUR 4.07bn depending on source), with positive inflection not expected until 2034
8.
Geography and perimeter. Operations sit in two core regulated jurisdictions (Belgium, Germany) plus minor non-regulated adjacencies: Nemo Link (50% stake, UK interconnector), WindGrid and Elia Grid International
9.
Governance. Bernard Gustin became Group Chief Executive Officer in January 2025, resolving a 16-month interim leadership period; Marco Nix became permanent Group Chief Financial Officer in April 2025
10.
Investment style implication. This reads as a structural, regulator-mandated growth compounder, not a classic low-growth defensive utility, with return timing risk concentrated in the 2026-2034 cash-burn window
