[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = B

Thesis. flatexDEGIRO is a 'Major Corporate Change' case that has reached full output and is priced as though the output were about to reverse. A new chief executive closed a two-year regulatory chapter on his first day and then reset the cost base of a business whose economics are fixed-cost and scale-driven, so that revenue growth of 22.2 percent in H1 2026 became net income growth of 41.1 percent. At EUR 28.0 (01-Oct-26) the shares stand at 10.1x consensus earnings 24 months forward, a 41 percent discount to Avanza and Nordnet, and what the price charges for is a three-week-old governance reshuffle and a slowdown in new-customer additions, not the earnings that are already reported.

  • The change is internal, multi-dimensional, and already in the accounts.
    • The screen's rule for this pattern is met twice over: a chief-executive change (Oliver Behrens from 01-Oct-24, the day BaFin's special commissioner left) plus repeated guidance increases, and at least five internal forces strong.
    • Cost base: personnel expenses fell 21 percent to EUR 24.1m in Q2 2026 and operating expenses fell 1.0 percent to EUR 59.6m while revenue rose 26 percent
      • Headcount was 1,210 at end-2025 against 1,271 a year earlier.
    • Guidance: the 2027 targets of EUR 650m revenue and EUR 200m net income were pulled into 2026 on 26-Jun-26, and FY2026 net income guidance was raised on 22-Jul-26 to EUR 200m to EUR 230m against EUR 160.4m in FY2025.
    • Capital returns: a dividend of EUR 0.30 per share for FY2025 against EUR 0.04, a 20 percent payout policy adopted in February 2026, and a EUR 50m buyback completed in May 2025.
    • Products: crypto, securities lending, fractional savings plans, deposits-as-a-service, and a pension account for the German reform that goes live on 01-Jan-27.
  • The external forces net to a modest tailwind against one large headwind.
    • The German pension reform, the European ban on payment for order flow and the ECB's June 2026 rate rise are real and dated, but small in near-term earnings
    • Competition for new customers from neobrokers is large and intensifying.
    • New customers: gross additions fell 12.3 percent in H1 2026 to 212,300 while marketing expenses rose 55.8 percent to EUR 29.6m
      • Acquisition cost was EUR 171 per account in Q1 2026 against EUR 86 a year earlier.
    • Installed base: customer accounts rose 11.1 percent to 3.66m at June 2026, assets under custody rose 29.7 percent to EUR 108.3bn, and customers invested 100 percent of EUR 5.16bn of net inflows
      • The base is compounding while the funnel narrows.
  • 'Financial Outputs' have travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain.
    • The change shows in the people, the choices, the actions, the key operating metrics, the financial statements and the sell-side's estimates.
    • Operating metrics: settled transactions rose 22.3 percent in Q2 2026 to 21.9m
      • Trading activity per customer was 24.2 annualised trades, +9 percent.
    • Financial statements: an operating profit margin of 58.8 percent of gross profit in Q2 2026 against 47.3 percent a year earlier
      • An EBITDA margin of 55.1 percent in H1 2026 against 48.5 percent in FY2025 and 37.9 percent in FY2023.
    • Sell-side estimates: FY2026 consensus EPS rose 34.6 percent over twelve months to a mean of EUR 2.07
      • Every named broker raised FY2027 after the Q2 print
      • The last month's revisions are flat.
  • What we think it earns: brokers'-basis EPS of EUR 1.63 in FY2025 rising to EUR 2.15 in FY2026, EUR 2.40 in FY2027 and EUR 2.80 in FY2028, a compound 19.8 percent a year, at the top of the framework's 12 to 20 percent band.
    • Shape of the path: a 32 percent year (FY2026) as the cost reset meets an elevated trading year
      • A 12 percent year (FY2027) as activity normalises, marketing is spent on the pension launch and no platform savings arrive yet
      • A 17 percent year (FY2028) as the platform merger's savings and the pension accounts' assets arrive.
    • Against consensus: we sit 2 percent above the median for FY2026 (EUR 2.11), level for FY2027 (EUR 2.40) and 2 percent below for FY2028 (EUR 2.87), because we do not bank the 59.2 percent EBITDA margin consensus implies for FY2028 while a growth officer is being hired to spend, and because we treat FY2026's trading activity as elevated.
  • What it costs: EUR 28.0, or 10.1x consensus earnings 24 months forward of EUR 2.78, and 10.4x our own 24-month-forward figure of EUR 2.70.
    • Own history: the 25th percentile of the ten-year range, below the 'prudent' median of 12.60x
      • The 5th percentile of the last two years, whose median is 14.22x.
    • Peers: the lowest multiple in the set by a distance (Quilter 11.6x, AJ Bell 15.2x, Fineco 15.3x, Avanza 17.1x, Nordnet 19.3x), and the two closest comparators carry the highest.
    • Price against news: the shares fell 28.7 percent in three months to the 52-week low while FY2026 consensus EPS rose 6.2 percent
      • The contracted catalysts (the raised guidance, the payout, the pension account) are not in the price.
  • The return clears the bar at eighteen months and not at three years.
    • Our 'Modal return' is 23.1 percent a year over eighteen months and 17.3 percent a year over three years, against the framework's 20 percent bar.
    • The legs: the earnings growth carries the return
      • The re-rating from 10.4x to 11.5x is the swing factor and rests on a governance resolution we put at about 40 percent
      • With the multiple held where it is the figures are 14.9 and 13.5 percent a year, both a C.
    • The sensitivity: a plausibly worse drop-through from EBITDA to EPS takes FY2028 EPS from EUR 2.80 to about EUR 2.50 and the return to 14.7 and 13.3 percent a year, still a C
      • With the uncleared multiple as well, 9.5 and 10.7 percent a year, a D.
  • The pattern is 'Major Corporate Change' at B, with 'OSHQC CfC Clearing' at C beside it.
    • The first describes the case: internal change on strong foundations showing in the accounts before it shows in the price.
    • The second describes the price: oversold on a named internal concern that has not begun to clear.
  • Where the chain holds and where it breaks.
    • It holds from the people to the sell-side's estimates, every layer with a reported figure behind it.
    • It breaks at the share price, and the one link we cannot read is the September 2026 reshuffle: two Supervisory Board resignations on 10-Sep-26 and a co-chief executive structure from 25-Sep-26, which the sell-side reads as a dispute about capital allocation and M&A, and for which the files give no stated reason.

  • The case is therefore compelling on the earnings leg and conditional on the re-rating leg: a good case with one unresolved question, and the question has a date, the February 2027 mid-term plan and the board appointments before the June 2027 AGM.

  • Rating = B, because the expected change is material on every leg the framework measures (net income guided +25 to +43 percent for FY2026; consensus EPS +29.3 percent for FY2026 and +13.5 percent for FY2027 like for like; a 'Modal return' of 23.1 percent a year over eighteen months) and it has already propagated to the sell-side's estimates, the sixth layer of the chain.

  • Not A, because the three-year 'Modal return' of 17.3 percent a year sits below the 20 percent bar and the external forces are mixed, with the strongest of them, competition for new customers, a headwind.
  • Not C, because a C would need the change to be either weak or unproven, and this one has a half-year of reported figures behind it.

Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.

  • The counter-case at its strongest: the FY2025 and H1 2026 profits are a cyclical peak dressed as a structural change.
    • Trading activity per customer ran at 25.1 annualised trades in H1 2026 against 23.0 in FY2025 and 22.0 in FY2024, and interest income is lifted by EUR 6.5bn of deposits on which the company pays nothing
    • Both are cyclical.
    • Acquisition: the peak masks a structural slowdown in customer acquisition: gross additions -12.3 percent in H1 2026, acquisition cost EUR 171 per account in Q1 2026 against EUR 86 a year earlier, against neobrokers spending ten to twenty times more on marketing.
    • Reverse leverage: when activity normalises the operating leverage runs in reverse, consensus for FY2027 (EUR 2.40) is cut, and the multiple, already at a discount, falls toward the 2022 trough of 8.7x.
  • Our response, first concession: we concede the cyclical component.
    • We model FY2027 EPS growth of 12 percent, not the 29 percent of FY2026, and we put no re-rating beyond the ten-year median at odds above even.
  • Our response, second: the slowdown is in gross additions, not in the installed base.
    • Customer accounts rose 11.1 percent to 3.66m at June 2026
    • Net cash inflows were EUR 5.16bn in H1 2026 against EUR 5.56bn a year earlier
    • Customers invested 100 percent of inflows.
    • The base that earns the commissions and holds the deposits is growing, so a normalisation of activity per customer is a slower year, not a reversal.
  • Our response, third: the cost base is a structural change, not a cyclical one.
    • Headcount was 1,210 against 1,271 a year earlier
    • Personnel expenses were EUR 24.1m in Q2 2026, -21 percent
    • The AI call centre is complete and the platform merger is dated for end-2027.
    • Operating leverage in reverse needs costs that were cut only in variable pay, and most of these were not.
  • Our response, fourth: the price already discounts a normalisation that has not begun.
    • At 10.1x the shares price the FY2027 consensus as either undelivered or not worth a median multiple, while August 2026 trades were +17 percent year on year.
  • What would make us change sides: two consecutive quarters of acquisition cost above EUR 200 per account, or customer growth below 8 percent a year.
