Fevertree's Line-by-Line Cost Structure

Dominant variable costs: Fevertree's operating model is defined by a highly variable cost structure because it utilizes third-party co-packers. Broker reports from Jefferies on 27 September 2024 show that co-packing charges represent 32% of cost of goods sold, while glass bottles account for 24%. This outsourced production means that raw material and packaging costs dictate gross profit margins. As a result, adjusted earnings per share are highly sensitive to input cost inflation rather than fixed asset depreciation.
2.

According to Fevertree's 2024 annual report, underlying operating expenses remained flat at 23.8% of group revenue. Within this cost base, brand marketing was 9.4% of brand revenue, while combined staff and central costs were flat at 14.8% of sales. This stability indicates that administrative and selling expenses offer limited near-term cost-cutting potential during periods of sluggish organic growth.

This high concentration of glass bottles means that fluctuations in energy and spot glass prices will quickly squeeze operating profits. Consequently, the company relies heavily on hedging strategies to stabilize its cost base and protect earnings per share.

This operational shift shields Fevertree's operating margin from macroeconomic shipping disruptions and supply chain shocks.

Deal Mechanics and Profit Sharing

1.
Structural partnership terms: Fevertree signed an exclusive US licensing agreement with Molson Coors Beverage Company effective 1 February 2025 to accelerate its brand expansion. Under this long-term agreement, Molson Coors acquired Fever-Tree USA, Inc. and gained the exclusive rights to produce, market, and distribute the portfolio. To preserve historical comparison, Fevertree reports adjusted US revenue representing sales to end customers alongside statutory revenue consisting of royalty fees and inventory sales. Profit sharing calculations: Fevertree extracts its US financial returns through a royalty fee that represents its agreed share of partnership profits. This structure creates a direct profit-sharing model at the EBITDA level where partner P&L dynamics dictate the final cash drop-through.
