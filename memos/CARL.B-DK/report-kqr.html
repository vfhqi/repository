EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Key Question asks what specific inputs will drive Carlsberg's earnings per share over the next 18 months to four years, focusing on the EUR 1.8bn hybrid issued in May 2026 with coupons of 4.37% and 4.875%, the treatment of those coupons in adjusted EPS (with Jefferies estimating about DKK 475m a year, Morgan Stanley about 2% FY2027 EPS dilution, and Bernstein about 1%), net finance costs, the Sapporo joint venture, tax rate guidance, associates under IFRS 18, the restated 2025 operating profit base, share buybacks, the Indian listing, and dividend policy.

The answer can be substantially answered now based on the available evidence, although critical pricing and timing gaps remain for the Indian listing and final Sapporo minority calculations. The core evidence demonstrates that below-the-line items increasingly dictate Carlsberg's post-acquisition valuation trajectory, with hybrid capital, joint venture proceeds, and accelerated Britvic synergies reshaping the earnings bridge more significantly than operating profit growth alone.

The EUR 1.8bn hybrid bond issued in May 2026 significantly reduces reported net debt because it is accounted for as equity under IFRS, though rating agencies Moody's and Fitch provide equity credit for only half of the issuance, meaning adjusted leverage models must add back approximately DKK 750m to net debt. Bernstein's analysis indicates that the hybrid bonds carry a higher coupon than Carlsberg's legacy debt, estimated to be approximately 1% dilutive to annual earnings per share, with more than half of the impact hitting in fiscal 2026 and the full effect felt in fiscal 2027.

Morgan Stanley's report notes that the hybrid bonds will dilute fiscal 2027 earnings per share by approximately 2%, with this higher estimate reflecting that hybrids are treated as equity under IFRS, meaning the coupons do not flow through financing costs in the income statement but instead are recognized in equity when payments fall due. The broker consensus clearly assumes elevated coupon dilution ranging from 1% to 2% annually, with the variance driven by different accrual versus cash accounting assumptions.

Net finance cost guidance was reduced from DKK 2.2bn to DKK 1.8bn for fiscal 2026, primarily driven by the redemption of outstanding bonds under its EMTN program using the hybrid proceeds, with reclassifications of bank fees and trade loans under IFRS 18 shifting some costs to operating profit while resulting in a neutral net earnings impact. The hybrid bond proceeds and the USD 643m Sapporo joint venture cash inflow are expected to accelerate deleveraging, with these capital inflows projected to reduce Carlsberg's net debt to EBITDA ratio to approximately 2.46x by the end of fiscal 2026.

DNB Carnegie's model projects that Carlsberg's net debt will fall to DKK 46,936m by the end of fiscal 2026, translating to a net debt to EBITDA leverage ratio of 2.3x for fiscal 2026, falling further to 2.0x in fiscal 2027 and 1.9x in fiscal 2028. Therefore, Carlsberg's tax burden remains relatively competitive within the Danish beverage peer group.

Historic tax drivers: Carlsberg's historical tax rates have been influenced by several recurring adjustments, such as non-deductible expenses and withholding taxes. The nominal weighted tax rate was 20.5% in fiscal year 2025. However, non-deductible expenses of DKK 662m and withholding taxes of DKK 182m pushed the reported rate higher. This suggests that minimizing non-deductible costs is crucial to achieving the guided 23% rate.

In the first half of 2026, Heineken faced significant tax and minority interest leakage that diluted its net profits.
