Section 1 – Brief company description and history

Section summary
  1. Unilever entered the H1 2026 period undergoing a significant portfolio transformation to focus on high- growth beauty and personal care categories while separating its lower-growth food divisions.
  2. Absolute three-year performance remained stable with strong volume recovery, but Unilever continued to trade at a margin deficit compared to its large-cap household and personal care peer group.
  3. Broker price targets leading into July 2026 reflected a split consensus, with bearish concerns over tough consumer demand offset by positive long-term restructuring expectations.
  4. Sell-side expectations for Unilever's H1 2026 earnings centered on volume-led sales growth and emerging market resilience, particularly in India.
  5. Analysts remained divided over Unilever's margin trajectory as persistent commodity cost inflation threatened to offset near-term operational cost-saving benefits.
  6. Market participants closely scrutinized the proposed structural separation of the Foods division with McCormick as a critical step to accelerate portfolio premiumization.
  7. Peer comparisons highlighted Unilever's persistent margin and valuation deficit, amplifying the strategic urgency of its ongoing corporate restructuring.
1.
Structured portfolio: Unilever operates as a global consumer giant with four focused business groups following the strategic separation of its Ice cream division. The restructured entity concentrates its operations on Beauty and Wellbeing, Personal Care, Home Care, and Foods. This portfolio simplification helps the group focus on its power brands which generate the vast majority of its sales.
2.
Tempered delivery: Unilever recorded a total turnover of €50.5 billion in fiscal year 2025, representing a decline of 3.8% due to severe foreign exchange headwinds. The company achieved underlying sales growth of 3.5%, which was supported by 1.5% underlying volume growth and 2.0% pricing actions. This performance demonstrated sequential improvement led primarily by volume recovery throughout the year.
3.
Resilient momentum: Unilever's Home and Personal Care segments delivered a robust performance over the three years leading up to 2026. The segment generated a top-line compound annual growth rate of over 5% accompanied by a volume and mix growth rate of over 2.5%. This expansion was supported by a significant gross margin increase of over 290 basis points during the same timeframe.
4.
Uncompetitive margins: Unilever pro forma Home and Personal Care underlying operating margin stood at 19.1% for the year 2025. This profitability level was well below its listed peers who recorded an average turnover-weighted margin of approximately 22% to 23%. Management intends to close this gap by leveraging pricing actions and structural value chain cost interventions.
5.
Resilient compounding: L'Oreal continued its steady expansion in 2025 with reported sales reaching €44.05 billion and underlying sales growth of 4.0% The cosmetics leader delivered an operating margin of 20.2% and adjusted earnings per share of €12.71 This delivery confirms L'Oreal's strong competitive positioning in beauty despite wider macroeconomic slowdowns across China.
6.
Subdued environment: The global household and personal care peer group experienced weaker pricing leverage of just 1.0% in 2025 compared to previous years. Procter & Gamble managed to maintain its guidance for fiscal year 2026 organic sales growth of 0% to 4% despite these headwinds This flat environment forced companies to focus on reinvestments and productivity initiatives to drive market share.
7.
Depressed valuation: Unilever share price traded near multi-year lows of 4,257.50p in May 2026 ahead of the half-year results. The equity performance was constrained within a 52-week range of 4,068p to 5,542p as market participants remained highly cautious. This trading level reflected investor concern over challenging consumer demand dynamics and high input cost inflation.
8.
Other side of the story: Jefferies maintained an Underperform rating on Unilever with a price target of 3,700p in May 2026. The broker expressed concerns that the company faces a much tougher strategic recovery path than the market consensus currently models. Analysts at the firm argued that valuation is not as cheap as bulls suggest when adjusting for regional demand risks.
9.
Moderate optimism: Morgan Stanley set a price target of 5,499p for Unilever in late 2025 while maintaining a positive fundamental view. The analyst Sarah Simon noted that Unilever should offer progressively increasing sales growth through 2026. This positive trajectory was expected to trigger further expansion of the multiple despite a generally difficult consumer staples backdrop.

Descending targets: Bernstein modified its long-term price target for Unilever over the course of 2025 and 2026. The broker recorded target revisions from 5,900p in mid-2025 down to 5,800p by July 2026. These moves reflected a balanced stance on the execution of structural transformations under the current leadership team.
