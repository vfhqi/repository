Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. European food retailers have significantly outperformed due to robust volume recoveries and manageable cost increases, while tobacco shares remained flat due to regulatory and tax headwinds.

Defining the Key Question

1.
Core investigation: "Sub-sector question about European food retailers (Tesco, Sainsbury, Marks and Spencer, Ahold Delhaize, Carrefour, Jeronimo Martins, Dino Polska, Colruyt, Kesko, Axfood) and tobacco companies (British American Tobacco, Imperial Brands, Scandinavian Tobacco); Tesco (TSCO-GB) is only the anchor. The median European food retail share outperformed the Stoxx Europe 600 by about 15 percentage points over the six months to mid-November 2026, while tobacco shares were roughly level with the index over six months. For each group: why? Cover food inflation and volumes, market shares against discounters, price competition, labour and tax costs (including United Kingdom Budget and business rates changes), margins, cash returns, and for tobacco the growth of smokeless products, regulation and excise. How much is earnings and how much is the multiple? What does consensus assume for FY2027, what would end the outperformance of food retail, and what do brokers say is in the price? Cite broker and company sources with dates."
2.
Significant earnings: Tesco faces substantial sensitivity in its bottom-line performance relative to its sales momentum. According to a Bernstein Research report dated October 26, 2026, each additional 1% of likefor-like sales growth is estimated to have a roughly 1% positive impact on Tesco's earnings per share. This direct relationship means that consumer demand and volume recovery will be key determinants of Tesco's EPS growth over the next three years.
3.
Defensive capacity: Retailers with superior operational efficiency are better insulated from wage and tax headwinds. Bernstein Research reported on October 28, 2026, that higher EBIT and net income margins make retailers better able to absorb cost increases such as the minimum wage. For example, Tesco's projected FY2027 EBIT margin of 4.5% is significantly higher than Sainsbury's at 3.5%, giving Tesco a stronger defensive profile against cost inflation.
4.
Strategic shifts: Carrefour faces critical structural and geographical challenges that could depress its future profit delivery. Morgan Stanley reported on October 21, 2026, that Carrefour cut its capital expenditure envelope by €100 mm for 2026. This reduction is expected to delay the retailer's store remodels and could ultimately slow its market share recovery, leading to a downgrade in group EPS projections by 5% for 2026.

Section 2 – Food Retail: Inflation, Volumes, and Competition

Section summary
  1. Overall confidence in the operational drivers of European food retail outperformance is high based on standout volume growth at Marks & Spencer and resilient margins across peers.
  2. Competitive pricing strategies and loyalty programs have successfully defended market shares against hard discounters, though promotional intensity is rising.

Operational Drivers and Volume Growth

1.
Strong operational results: The researcher analyzed 10 peer companies, 1 comparable past case, and 24 distinct documents across company filings and broker research. The collective evidence shows that European grocers are delivering robust performance despite a highly competitive grocery retail landscape. Bernstein Research noted that volume recovery represents a key driver of defensive earnings stability. This operational strength underpins recent share outperformance.
2.
Outstanding volume expansion: Marks & Spencer reported exceptional H1 Food sales growth of 13.9% and like- for-like sales up 11.7%. This standout performance was driven by an 11.9% expansion in UK volumes. Bernstein Research noted on November 5, 2026, that M&S added 1.5 million customers over two years, reaching a Food market share of 4.3%. This volume momentum directly supports elevated group earnings stability.
3.
Resilient European trading: Ahold Delhaize delivered net sales of €9.8 billion in Europe for Q3 2026, representing constant-currency growth of 3.1%. European comparable sales excluding gasoline grew by 2.1% due to positive volume trends. Jefferies Research reported on November 4, 2026, that European margins remained stable at 4.0% despite extra price investments in Europe. These stable metrics indicate that cost discipline is successfully offsetting disinflationary headwinds.
4.
Robust Nordic performance: Kesko Oyj grew its Q3 2026 grocery trade division net sales by 2.7% to €1,690 million. This growth outpaced the Finnish grocery market which grew by only 0.6% during the quarter. DNB Carnegie reported on October 30, 2026, that Kesko gained 40 basis points of market share. This continuous market share gain supports strong grocery margins clearly above 6% for FY2027.

Market Share Battles and Discounters

5.
Aggressive pricing gaps: Discounters Aldi and Lidl have expanded their price discounts against the Big 4 supermarkets to 16% and 15% respectively. Morgan Stanley reported on October 13, 2026, that Lidl stepped up its price investments to £475 million in price cuts. This aggressive price positioning puts substantial pressure on the margins of traditional supermarkets. Traditional grocers must reinvest their efficiency savings to narrow these price gaps.
6.
Persistent basket deflation: Jeronimo Martins faced a demanding Polish market where food disinflation turned to deflation in Q3 2026. Biedronka reported a 9M LFL sales decline of 0.4% as a 5% volume growth was offset by significant basket deflation. Bernstein Research reported on October 28, 2026, that the competitive environment remains intense. Reflationary trends expected in 2027 should ultimately relieve these pressure points.
7.
Declining operating leverage: Dino Polska experienced a slowdown in Q3 2026 LFL sales growth to 0.1% due to negative grocery price growth. Morgan Stanley reported on November 5, 2026, that Dino's operating expenses rose 21% year-on-year, outgrowing sales by almost two times. This mismatch created an EBIT margin decline of 70 basis points due to severe operating de-leverage. Dino's earnings per share growth will remain constrained until price deflation stabilizes.
8.
Slower discount growth: Axfood AB reported Sweden retail sales growth of 2.9% for Q3 2026, which lagged the overall market growth of 4.0%. The company's discount banner, Willys, grew net sales by 2.1% against high comparison figures. Axfood successfully turned its acquired City Gross hypermarket banner from lossmaking to profitable in Q3. This structural improvement supports Axfood's capability to defend margins against persistent food price deflation.
9.
Disadvantageous Sunday trading: Colruyt Group experienced a decline in its Belgian food retail market share to 28.3% during the first five months of the financial year. Bernstein Research reported on October 1, 2026, that Colruyt's competitors are opening on Sundays while Colruyt Lowest Prices stores remain closed. Automatic wage indexation in Belgium is also driving personnel costs higher. These combined factors continue to squeeze Colruyt's retail margins.

Price Competition and Loyalty Strategies

10.
Scaled loyalty advantage: Tesco has maintained its position as the cheapest of the Big 4 supermarkets by leveraging its extensive Clubcard pricing. Morgan Stanley reported on May 18, 2026, that Tesco offers 10,000 SKUs on Clubcard with an 85% sales penetration rate. Tesco's loyalty discount remains best-in-class at more than 10%. This loyalty card data provides rich first-party insights to monetize retail media and optimize supplier-funded promotional discounts.
11.
Growing loyalty engagement: Sainsbury's has focused its value proposition on Nectar Prices across approximately 11,000 products. Bernstein Research noted on October 26, 2026, that Sainsbury's achieved 9% growth in Nectar users and 17% growth in personalized pricing. However, these loyalty price cuts have slightly reduced overall operating margins. This lack of operating leverage means the strategy is not yet translating into robust profit growth.
12.
Improving price competitiveness: Carrefour has implemented a more competitive commercial strategy, improving its price image by 16 points. Carrefour's private labels are positioned on average 30% cheaper than national brands. Bernstein Research reported on November 19, 2026, that Carrefour still suffers from structural risks in France. These risks include a lack of price competitiveness on national brands and overreliance on hypermarkets.
13.
Contrary evidence: Sainsbury's reported a significant deceleration in Q2 like-for-like sales growth ex-fuel to 1.2% against a consensus expectation of 1.9%. Bernstein Research reported on October 22, 2026, that grocery sales growth missed consensus due to cautious consumer spending. This softer sales momentum shows that the outperformance of food retailers is smaller than it looks. Any sustained volume pressure will immediately restrict future retail margin progression.
14.
Historical discount encroachment: Traditional UK supermarkets suffered severe margin compression in 2017 as they failed to match prices. Jefferies Research reported on March 7, 2017, that the German discounters grew by 15.2% in February, having averaged 10.8% in the prior year. Traditional grocers allowed a huge price gap to emerge on basic items. This historical precedent suggests that any delay in matching discounters' prices will immediately trigger severe volume loss.
15.
Other side of the story: While traditional food retailers have successfully protected market share through price matching, this strategy permanently depresses margins. Bernstein Research warned on May 14, 2026, that committing to beat supermarkets on FMCG pricing will lead to permanently depressed margins. A sudden intensification of price wars would lead to a permanent reset of grocery profitability. This structural margin pressure represents the primary threat to future earnings per share growth.

Section 3 – Food Retail: Cost Headwinds, UK Budget, and Margins

Section summary
  1. The UK Autumn Budget has introduced substantial cost headwinds for domestic food retailers, primarily through increased employer National Insurance contributions and elevated wage rates.
  2. European peers are managing these localized burdens through accelerated cost-saving programs, supply chain optimizations, and targeted capital expenditure into store automation.

Impact of UK Autumn Budget

1.
Severe tax impacts: Tesco faces a substantial increase in its annual operating costs due to the recent tax modifications announced in the UK Autumn Budget. According to a report by Bernstein Research on October 26, 2026, the reduction in the employer National Insurance threshold from £9,100 to £5,000 and the rate hike to 15% will create significant headwinds. Tesco previously estimated that these changes would result in an extra £250 mm a year in tax expenses. This massive payroll headwind represents a direct risk to future retail operating profit margins unless offset by efficiency gains.
2.
Elevated payroll burdens: Sainsbury's is also grappling with a heavily expanded tax burden that threatens its bottom-line performance. Deutsche Bank Research reported on December 3, 2024, that the grocer quantified the impact of the National Insurance contribution increases at approximately £140 mm. This calculation is based on Sainsbury's extensive workforce of about 148k employees in the UK, translating to an incremental cost of roughly £950 per colleague. Such localized fiscal pressures will restrict the group's ability to expand retail margins over the next two years.

Business Rates and Margin Pressure

5.
Inconsistent property taxation: Business rates on commercial properties continue to represent a highly volatile cost element for United Kingdom food retailers. Bernstein Research noted on October 26, 2026, that retail properties are currently subjected to a complex system of multipliers based on rateable values. Scenario analysis implies that if the highest rate multiplier is increased for all properties, it could have a 3% to 5% negative impact on Tesco's earnings per share. Conversely, exempting retail properties from the highest multiplier could yield a significant 8% uplift to Tesco's EPS.
6.
Persistent margin constraints: Traditional grocers are struggling to rebuild their core operating margins to historical levels due to these ongoing cost escalations. Morgan Stanley reported on February 2, 2024, that both Tesco and Sainsbury's had yet to recover their pre-pandemic EBIT margins. The average EBIT margins for these two leading UK grocers were on track to end the fiscal year about 30 basis points below prepandemic averages. This slow recovery highlights the difficulty of fully offsetting persistent structural headwinds from elevated energy and wage costs.
7.
Depressed peer performance: Colruyt Group is facing intense operating margin pressure in the Belgian grocery sector due to high automatic indexation. Bernstein Research reported on May 14, 2026, that Belgium remains one of the most challenging grocery markets because personnel costs are among the highest in Europe. Colruyt's EBIT margin declined from over 5.0% down to 4.0% by its late 2025 reporting period. This margin erosion is exacerbated by Belgian collective bargaining agreements that limit staffing flexibility and prevent cost relief.
8.
Severe margin contraction: Colruyt updated its financial guidance to reflect an expected decline in profitability due to these localized headwinds. Bernstein Research reported on October 1, 2026, that the Belgian discounter now forecasts a limited decrease to EBIT for the financial year. This downgrade was driven by a combination of rising personnel and energy costs alongside low food inflation. Competitors opening on Sundays have also caused Colruyt's Belgian market share to slide to 28.3% during the year.

This shifting balance of power is expected to help grocers offset rising input costs over the next three years.

Morgan Stanley reported on October 8, 2026, that Tesco management announced an increase in its capex budget to £1.7 bn for the year. This represents an increase of £100 mm over prior guidance and a £200 mm expansion year-on-year. The incremental capital will be directed into high-return opportunities such as electronic shelf label rollouts and advanced artificial intelligence stacks.

Reduced capital envelopes: Carrefour has taken a more conservative approach to its capital allocation in response to recent domestic operational disruptions. This reduction accompanies a downward revision to full-year free cash flow guidance following social unrest in France. Finnish retail dynamics: Kesko is actively expanding its technical trade presence through strategic capital deployment while maintaining solid grocery margins. DNB Carnegie reported on October 19, 2026, that Kesko's investment boom is starting to feed through to financial performance. This structural expansion supports an exceptionally profitable Finnish grocery business with stable margins. This resilient grocery operation continues to produce strong free cash flows to enable high dividend returns.

Section 4 – Tobacco: Smokeless Product Transition, Regulation, and Returns

Section summary
  1. Tobacco shares underperformed food retail due to severe regulatory pressures and excise tax shocks in Germany.
  2. Next-generation products show strong growth, but legal margins are threatened by rising excise duties and illicit disposable vapes.
  3. Cash returns remain highly attractive with multi-billion pound buybacks, but valuation multiples trade at a steep discount to food retailers.

Smokeless Category Transition

1.
Accelerated transition growth: Imperial Brands delivered double-digit net revenue expansion in next-generation products for the financial year ended September 30, 2026. The company grew modern oral categories through the bolt-on acquisitions of Helwit in Sweden and Black Buffalo in the United States. Pricing gains in tobacco offset a 3.4% volume decline, helping to secure a 3.7% constant-currency operating profit increase. This growth underpins the firm's transition away from traditional combustibles.
2.
Strong brand leadership: British American Tobacco has established a commanding lead in the European modern oral market with its Velo brand. Velo captured approximately 75% of the industry retail offtake growth in Europe during 2026. However, an expert call with a former Vice President on October 12, 2026, notes that BAT struggles to scale next-generation products globally because of slower innovation compared to more focused competitors. This category fragmentation threatens long-term profit margins.
3.
Positive niche performance: Scandinavian Tobacco Group expanded its smokeless presence through nicotine pouch sales in Sweden, Denmark, and the United Kingdom. DNB Carnegie reported on November 12, 2026, that nicotine pouches offer strong growth potential but remain highly competitive and barely profitable. Structural volume declines in cigars and pipe tobacco are accelerated by weak consumer confidence and downtrading. Consequently, the group relies on acquisitions to expand its alternative categories.
4.
Stable margin progression: Scandinavian Tobacco Group achieved an improved gross margin of 53.8% in its machine-rolled cigars segment during the first nine months of 2026. Deutsche Bank Research reported on November 12, 2026, that upgraded profitability guidance was supported by elevated tariff refunds. Section 5 – Valuation and Drivers: Earnings, Multiples, and FY2027

Section summary
  1. European food retail outperformance was primarily driven by structural improvements in capital efficiency and robust return on invested capital rather than extensive multiple expansion.
  2. Tobacco valuations remain capped at deep discounts due to regulatory headwinds and excise tax changes despite delivering exceptional dividend and buyback yields.

Delayed valuation adjustments: European grocers expanded their return on invested capital by approximately 200 basis points to 11.2% between 2019 and 2025. This operational improvement was driven almost entirely by capital turns rather than margin expansion. However, average sector valuation multiples remained slow to follow, exhibiting only a 6% P/E re-rating over the same period. This discrepancy suggests that earnings quality rather than multiple expansion has anchored the sector's performance.

Analysts believe the stock has room to re-rate further toward 17.5x as investors gain confidence in its returns.

Moderate discount valuations: Ahold Delhaize continues to trade at an attractive discount relative to its wider European food retail peers.
