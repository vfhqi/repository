Structural Mechanics of Phase Two Put-Call Option

1.
Delayed execution: Kerry Group plc structured its dairy divestment to complete in two distinct phases. The Co-Op has an immediately exercisable Call Option to purchase the remaining 30% stake in Kinisla for €150 million cash up to July 2035. However, Kerry can only execute its Put Option between August 2030 and July 2035. This structured timeline prevents Kerry from forcing a final exit prior to late 2030.
2.
Attractive yield: Kerry Group plc receives a fixed annual dividend of €7.5 million during the period of joint Kinisla ownership. This dividend represents a highly attractive yield of 5% on the €150 million retained valuation. Because this dividend is guaranteed, Kerry is financially incentivized to retain the stake until the put option window opens. This high-yield income stream protects Kerry's near-term cash generation while Kinisla stabilizes its standalone operations.
3.
Favorable accounting: Kerry Group plc accounts for the remaining 30% Kinisla stake as a financial asset measured at fair value through profit and loss. The carrying value was recorded at €148.5 million in mid- 2026, reflecting a minor credit risk adjustment. This accounting treatment is justified because the immediately exercisable call option gives the Co-Op substantive control over Kinisla's board. Consequently, Kerry does not consolidate Kinisla's financial results or report share of equity associate losses.
