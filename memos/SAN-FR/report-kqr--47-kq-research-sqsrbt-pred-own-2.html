Precedents of Debt-Funded Acquisitions

1.
Extensive historical analysis: Research identified four relevant historical precedents across three major pharmaceutical acquirers to evaluate debt-funded transactions between ten billion and twenty-five billion euros while facing patent cliffs. 2.
Accelerated leverage expansion: AbbVie completed its acquisition of Pharmacyclics in May 2015 for approximately twenty-one billion dollars to secure the oncology blockbuster Imbruvica. To finance the cash portion of twelve billion dollars, AbbVie issued seventeen billion dollars in unsecured senior notes, significantly increasing its debt load. This financing structure demonstrated how mega-cap biopharma entities rely heavily on debt markets during portfolio transitions.
3.
Depressed credit ratings: S&P Global Ratings and Moody's lowered AbbVie's credit ratings to A- and Baa2, respectively, following its aggressive debt issuances in 2015 and 2016. These downgrades reflected the increased leverage risks of funding late-stage acquisitions ahead of Humira's patent expirations. Rating agencies monitored AbbVie's cash flows closely as its net leverage ratio hovered around elevated levels.
4.
Strong EPS trajectory: AbbVie grew its adjusted diluted earnings per share from five dollars and sixty cents in 2017 to seven dollars and forty-six cents in 2018. This substantial growth was supported by the rapid commercial expansion of Imbruvica and the launches of next-generation immunology assets. The company's business earnings successfully outpaced the initial dilution of its debt service obligations within two years of closing.
