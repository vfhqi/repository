Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. The Key Question can be partly answered now. The evidence points to strong but uneven pricing power at Sonova: list prices and premium mix hold up well, but net price realization is diluted by rebates, buyer power and reimbursement caps. The review covered 612 relevant documents, 9 companies and 3 historical precedents. Evidence on net (post-rebate) prices in the independent channel, on Section 232 tariff outcomes and on Amplifon's purchasing shift is still too thin for a firm conclusion.
1.

Its high-margin wholesale segment recorded a core gross margin of 74.7% in H1 2026 and is highly sensitive to price and product mix. If Sonova fails to defend wholesale pricing, its raised core EBIT growth guidance of 10% to 13% will be unachievable unless premium mix expansion continues.
2.

Sonova's own clinics cushioned this. Management said retail growth was more skewed toward price increases than volume.

Section 4 – Tariffs, Import Duties, and Foreign Exchange Pass-Through

Section summary
  1. Hearing aid manufacturers maintain robust pricing structures because they successfully pass through inflationary input costs and mitigate trade risks through supply chain agility.
  2. Potential Section 232 medical equipment investigations introduce structural tariff risks, but companies plan to deploy local manufacturing networks to protect corporate earnings.

Historical Tariff Impacts and Price Adjustments

1.
Positive price adjustments: Hearing aid manufacturers successfully implemented selective wholesale price increases to offset rising raw material costs. Sonova and other major suppliers raised wholesale prices by about 5% over the past year. Independent audiologists in July 2026 confirmed that these targeted increases offset persistent global supply chain inflation without triggering immediate volume contractions. This price execution protects wholesale margins during periods of soft consumer volume growth.

Aggressive pricing: Sonova through its Phonak brand raised wholesale list prices more aggressively than its medical technology peers. Carnegie reported in May 2025 that GN announced price hikes of around 10% on regional portfolios to offset import costs.
