EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Diploma PLC has executed a highly orderly executive transition, with permanent CFO Wilson Ng promoted from within in December 2025 following the abrupt departure of former CFO Chris Davies, ensuring financial continuity and governance stability. The group demonstrates exceptional cultural strength with colleague engagement reaching 78% in FY25, significantly outperforming the external benchmark of 68%, though rapid decentralized expansion places immense strain on thin local management layers. There is no active activist presence within the company, and the decentralized operating model of 17 agile, entrepreneurial businesses remains resilient with no pressure to centralize operations.

Strategic acquisition campaigns have meaningfully reaccelerated, with management completing 15 acquisitions in the last year deploying approximately £310 million at an average EBIT multiple of 8x, and deploying nearly £1.5 billion on acquisitions over seven years. The acquisition of interconnect specialist CDM significantly increases US defense exposure to approximately 7% of group pro forma revenue, though the group paid a premium multiple of approximately 11x EBIT compared to its historical 8x acquisition average.

Integrating CDM, Spring, and FC Lane increases group-level defense revenue exposure to approximately 6% to 7%, with management successfully expanding from air to land defense leveraging Eastern European facilities and CDM's capabilities. Data centers provide promising structural growth tailwinds representing a low-single-digit percentage of overall group revenues, with cabling specialist Windy City Wire delivering exceptional mid-teens growth driven by strong, non-binary data center demand.

Historical performance shows exceptional organic revenue acceleration, with group organic growth accelerating from 8% in FY23 to 11% in FY25, beating long-term targets, while adjusted operating margins rose from 19.7% in FY23 to 22.5% in FY25, driven by margin-accretive Peerless performance and deployment of over £1 billion on specialist acquisitions since 2020.

Earnings quality is underpinned by excellent cash conversion reaching 105% in FY25, showcasing a capital-light model, while fully loaded ROATCE grew 180 bps to 20.9% in FY25, reflecting exceptional capital recycling and disciplined bolt-on multiples. Management raised organic growth targets three times in FY26, highlighting highly conservative forecasting frameworks.

Recent earnings delivery has been outstanding, with H1 2026 results delivering significant outperformance across all core lines, with group revenue reaching £851.1 million representing 16.8% growth, adjusted operating margin expanding by 300 bps to 24.5%, and adjusted EPS reaching 109.2p beating consensus estimates of 107.3p by 1.8%. Management maintains a highly conservative guidance framework characterized by sequential upgrades, having raised organic growth targets three times in FY26 from 6% to 14% and lifted full-year operating margin guidance to 26.5% in July 2026 from 22.5%, highlighting management's tendency to sandbag initial expectations under volatile macro backdrops.

Controls organic revenue grew by 26% in H1 2026 fueled by aerospace and defense end markets, while Life Sciences organic revenue grew only 4% in H1 2026 constrained by product lifecycle refreshes. The top five to eight ranked key EPS drivers for forward earnings compounding include M&A deployment with an estimated 12% impact supported by low net leverage of 0.8x allowing up to £600 million in M&A deployment, estimated to be 10% to 14% EPS accretive; Peerless normalization representing a negative 240 bps impact as Peerless margins are projected to moderate over three years as spot pricing begins to normalize, though management is actively converting spot customers to contract to secure long-term volume baseline; Windy City Wire scaling with 15% estimated impact as data centers have grown to represent 15% of Windy City Wire revenue, proprietary labor-saving solutions support an exceptional 99% customer retention rate, and non-binary hyperscaler capital commitments support high mid-teens organic growth; Seals aftermarket recovery with 6% estimated impact as Seals organic revenue growth is projected to improve to mid-single digits, with heavy public spending supporting North American Seals growth at high-single-digit rates; defense interconnect integration with 7% estimated impact as integrating CDM increases group-level defense revenue exposure to 6% or 7%, provides a critical platform to expand from air to land defense, and increased defense budgets are a highly stable, multi-decade structural support; and opex reinvestment program creating a negative 30 bps impact as operating investments of 0.75% of sales will dilute group margins by 30 bps, with reinvestment focusing on building an internal GM pipeline to solve succession bottlenecks. & Co Redburn 16 Sell-side consensus dynamics show overwhelmingly bullish positioning, with Morgan Stanley modeling a base case 8,700p target price assuming 14% organic growth in FY26, implying a 2027 P/E multiple of 33x, while Jefferies maintains a Hold rating with 7,000p target price applying a 28x multiple, noting high comps limit near- term upside. Fundamental peer positioning demonstrates that Diploma leads its peer group across all four fundamental momentum dimensions, with the company ranking in the top quartile of compounding peers and outperforming the broader industrial distributor universe driven by superior organic top-line velocity and structural end-market niches that decouple group revenue performance from standard industrial cycle downturns.

However, the elevated operating margin peak relies heavily on temporary Peerless outperformance, and if spot fastener pricing normalizes rapidly or aerospace supply chain normalizes unexpectedly, margins could quickly contract back toward 22% to 24%, severely decelerating group EPS growth and potentially triggering a sharp multiple contraction.

A sixth plausible problem to monitor stems from cyclical industrial exposure, with approximately 80% of group revenues remaining linked to general industrial distribution cycles across major geographies. This cyclical exposure differential becomes particularly relevant when benchmarked against peers like Halma, whose medical and safety portfolios demonstrate materially lower beta to manufacturing cycles, or specialist distributors with higher recurring revenue business models.

Finally, translational foreign exchange exposure represents a seventh plausible problem to monitor, as the group earns 80% of revenues outside the UK, creating significant reported earnings volatility from currency movements. This exposure is particularly relevant given recent sterling volatility and represents a structural disadvantage relative to more geographically concentrated peers or those with natural currency hedges through matched assets and liabilities. These seven plausible problem to monitor, while individually modest in magnitude, collectively suggest that Diploma's exceptional recent performance may face structural headwinds to sustainability as Peerless margins normalize, Life Sciences recovers slowly, and reinvestment costs compress reported profitability.

Outliers worth noting

Diploma PLC exhibits several genuine statistical outliers relative to its specialty distribution peer group, reflecting both structural portfolio advantages and temporary operational dynamics that position the company at the extremes of sector performance distributions. The company's adjusted operating margin expansion from 19.7% in FY23 to 22.5% in FY25 represents a 300 basis point improvement over a two-year period, placing Diploma in the top decile of industrial distributors for margin expansion velocity.

This trajectory accelerated further with record FY26 operating margin expectations reaching 26.5% by July 2026, a level that significantly exceeds the peer median and creates a 400 to 700 basis point premium over comparable specialty distributors like Bunzl and RS Group. The exceptional margin profile stems from the structural mix shift toward high-margin aerospace fasteners at Peerless, which operates at materially elevated profitability during supply-constrained environments, as detailed in Section 4 and Section 6.

The company achieved 105% free cash flow conversion in FY25 alongside a 20.9% return on adjusted trading capital, metrics that position Diploma in the top 5% of capital-light industrial distribution models. This cash generation intensity, referenced in Section 4 and Section 5, reflects an unusually efficient working capital management framework combined with minimal maintenance capital intensity, creating a structural financial profile that outperforms typical specialty distribution economics by 15 to 20 percentage points on cash conversion metrics.

Diploma's organic revenue growth accelerated from 8% in FY23 to 11% in FY25, with H1 2026 reaching 15% yearon-year, placing the company in the top quartile of specialty distributors for organic growth momentum over a three-year observation window. This organic acceleration significantly exceeds the 5% to 7% sector median established by peers like Bunzl and RS Group, as analyzed in Section 7. The outlier growth profile reflects concentrated exposure to structural niches including aerospace, defense, and data centers, which decouple Diploma's top-line trajectory from standard industrial production cycles that constrain broader peer group performance.

Management deployed nearly £1.5 billion on acquisitions over seven years, completing 15 deals in the last year alone at an average 8x EBIT multiple, representing an inorganic deployment velocity that places Diploma in the top 10% of compounding specialty distributors. This acquisition cadence, detailed in Section 2, significantly exceeds the historical baseline and peer median activity levels, reflecting both substantial balance sheet capacity and privileged access to fragmented private seller markets.

The scale and consistency of dealmaking create a differentiated growth platform relative to peers who face greater capital constraints or operate in more consolidated acquisition markets.
