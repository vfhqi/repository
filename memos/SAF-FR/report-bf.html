Section 1 – Business Model + Organisation 32 findings

Section summary
  1. Safran operates a highly profitable civil propulsion and aerospace equipment model where zero- margin original equipment engine deliveries establish decades of high-margin contracted aftermarket cash flows.

Operational Activities and Divisional Structure

1.
Segmental Composition: Safran structures its global industrial operations across three primary business divisions: Aerospace Propulsion, Equipment & Defense, and Aircraft Interiors
2.
Aerospace Propulsion: This division is Safran's primary engine of profitability, designing, developing, and servicing civil turbofans, military aircraft engines, helicopter turbines, and rocket/missile propulsion systems
3.
Equipment & Defense: This division covers the full lifecycle of critical subsystems, including landing gears, carbon brakes, nacelles, avionics, evacuation slides, and defense electronics
4.
Aircraft Interiors: This division encompasses buyer-furnished and supplier-furnished cabin components, including passenger seats, overhead bins, galleys, lavatories, and complex cabin water and waste systems
5.
Divisional Profit Skew: While the Propulsion division generated 52% of adjusted segment revenues in first- half 2026, it accounted for 69.6% (calculated) of total segment recurring operating income, highlighting its superior profitability
6.
SVS Segment Realignment: Effective January 1, 2026, Safran reallocated Safran Ventilation Systems (SVS)– which generates under €200 million in annual revenues at a double-digit operating margin–from Aircraft Interiors to Equipment & Defense to drive synergies with its power electrical activities

Revenue by Geography: Safran's adjusted revenue of €17,571 million in first-half 2026 was geographically distributed as 30% in the Americas, 23% in Europe (excluding France), 18% in France, 19% in Asia-Pacific, and 10% in Africa & Middle East

Key Country Exposures: The United States represents the group's single largest customer geography, accounting for 24% of total group sales in first-half 2026, while France represents 18%

CFM International Joint Venture Mechanics

16.
CFM Partnership Structure: CFM International is a 50-50 non-consolidated joint venture between Safran Aircraft Engines and GE Aerospace, originally formed in 1974 and recently extended through the year 2050
17.
Revenue Sharing Mechanism: Fundamentally, the CFM JV is structured as a pure revenue-sharing agreement where Safran and GE Aerospace each receive exactly 50% of all OE and aftermarket spare parts revenues
18.
Independent Cost Bases: Each partner is individually responsible for managing its own development and manufacturing costs, meaning any internal cost-saving initiative translates directly to 100% margin retention
19.
Industrial Division of Work: Operationally, GE Aerospace produces the engine core (high-pressure compressor, combustor, and high-pressure turbine), while Safran manufactures the fan, low-pressure compressor, and low-pressure turbine
