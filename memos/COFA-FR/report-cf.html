EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Coface's Power the Core strategic plan has delivered exceptional underwriting margins averaging 72.2% since launch, comfortably outperforming the 78% through-the-cycle target and validating the group's structural franchise superiority in credit insurance. Net income progression demonstrates robust cyclical performance, climbing from €240.5 million in FY23 to a peak of €261.1 million in FY24 before normalizing to €222.0 million in FY25. Leadership stability is anchored by CEO Xavier Durand's four-year mandate renewal in 2024, ensuring strategic continuity through the current plan cycle. The shareholder base remains highly stable with Arch Capital maintaining a strategic 30% ownership stake, viewed as critical to developing uncorrelated underwriting income streams. However, external headwinds are intensifying as global corporate insolvencies surge 6% in 2026 with North American failures up 22%, compounded by slowing global growth revised down to 2.3% amid widespread geopolitical shocks.

MATERIALITY OF CHANGE

Organic revenue growth has decelerated sharply to just 0.8% in H1-2026 as average pricing remained negative at -1.3%, reflecting intense competitive pressure across the credit insurance market. Underwriting margin superiority remains intact with Coface recording a 71.3% combined ratio, representing an 8-point outperformance versus closest primary credit peers. Management abandoned the business information 50 bps RoATE target in H1-2026 to prioritize long-term value creation, compensating shareholders through an upward adjustment to the 2027 dividend.

Capitalization remains highly defensive at 194%, substantially exceeding the target range of 155% to 175% and providing significant strategic optionality. Systemic macro shocks have materialized with the virtual closure of the Strait of Hormuz reducing global growth by 0.5 points and US Section 301 tariffs of 10% to 12.5% covering 99% of goods imports, creating unprecedented trade corridor disruptions.

TOP RANKED KEY DRIVERS OF EPS

Reserve release run-rates (estimated 12% EPS impact, medium plausibility, medium confidence): Coface's highly conservative reserving framework has generated 47 to 52 percentage points of annual recoveries since FY23, structurally insulating reported combined ratios from decade-high corporate insolvencies. Premium pricing cycle dynamics (estimated -8% EPS impact, high plausibility, high confidence): Persistent negative pricing of -1.3% in H1-2026 reflects soft-cycle competitive pressures that constrain top-line growth despite record client retention levels.

Business information services acceleration (estimated 5% EPS impact, medium plausibility, high confidence): Standalone service revenues surged 12.0% organically in H1-2026, accelerating non-insurance fee diversification despite the cancellation of the standalone RoATE target. Reinvestment yield trajectory (estimated 4% EPS impact, high plausibility, high confidence): Investment income peaked at 4.3% reinvestment rates in 9M-24, with management signaling gradual normalization creating a structural net financial income headwind.

IFRS 17 discounting effects (estimated 3% EPS impact, high plausibility, medium confidence): Technical provisions discounting provides a 3-4 percentage point combined ratio benefit under current interest rate environments, though this creates offsetting discount unwind drag quarterly. Global insolvency cycle normalization (estimated -6% EPS impact, high plausibility, high confidence): Corporate bankruptcies are rising 6% globally with North American failures surging 22%, putting upward pressure on net loss ratios as favorable back-book cycles mature. Systemic trade and tariff shocks (estimated -4% EPS impact, medium plausibility, medium confidence): Compounding effects of Strait of Hormuz closures reducing global growth by 0.5 points and universal US tariff regimes of 10-12.5% could materially reduce premium growth and elevate claims severity.

RECENT FINANCIAL AND OPERATIONAL DELIVERY H1-2026 results confirmed resilient technical profitability with a net combined ratio of 71.3% driven by a 2.7 point improvement in net loss ratio to 37.4%, offset by strategic technology investments that elevated the net cost ratio to 33.9%. Net income declined 13.2% to €107.8 million in H1-2026 with annualized RoATE normalizing to 10.9%, reflecting premium deceleration and investment yield compression despite superior underwriting execution. Geographic performance diverged sharply with Northern Europe growing 2.6% driven by German factoring strength, Western Europe flat at 0.2% growth, and North America contracting 1.3% organically, while fee-based services surged 8.8% to €88.6 million. Negative pricing of -1.3% persisted despite near-record client retention of 93.6%, validating defensive franchise quality amid heightened competitive intensity. Strategic acquisitions progressed with Cedar Rose and Novertur integrations completed alongside the launch of Coface Lloyd's Syndicate 2546, enabling AA-rated product offerings and 2-point solvency ratio improvements through enhanced capital efficiency.

FORWARD CREDIBILITY AND MANAGEMENT ARCHETYPE

Management has consistently exceeded long-term targets across consecutive strategic cycles, delivering 72.2% average undiscounted combined ratios, 12.3% average RoATE, and 82% average dividend payout ratios since plan initiation. Coface operates as a classic conservative sandbagger archetype, systematically setting high initial loss picks at 81.2% in H1-26 to engineer predictable quarterly earnings beats through structured back-book reserve releases.

The abandonment of the business information 50 bps RoATE target demonstrates pragmatic strategic flexibility, with management pledging to compensate shareholders through a 4-point upward dividend adjustment in 2027. Regulatory capital management remains highly prudent with ACPR-approved partial internal models supporting a 194% Solvency II ratio that would remain comfortably at 170% even under 1-in-50 year GFC-style macro stress scenarios.

Deutsche Bank notes that IFRS 17 discounting increases quarterly earnings volatility without changing core economics, while management historically sandbags expectations via conservative initial claims picks to create systematic positive surprise patterns.

SELL-SIDE POSITIONING

Sell-side consensus has shifted to cautious Hold recommendations following Deutsche Bank's downgrade in December 2024, with price targets trimmed from €16 to €14.20 reflecting elevated trade uncertainty and tariff risks. Consensus earnings revisions turned negative with estimates trimmed 2% to 3% post-FY2025 results on rising claims normalization and investment income compression. Structural takeover optionality remains embedded in valuations as Deutsche Bank argues Arch Capital could ultimately acquire 100% of Coface over time, though Jefferies notes trade credit insurance expansion remains more attractive than increasing the Coface stake before sector consolidation accelerates.

Bears consistently focus on deflationary top-line premium drags while underestimating actual technical combined ratio profitability, with Coface delivering sequential quarterly net income beats of €9 million in Q1-24 operating profit. Valuation models apply sum-of-the-parts methodologies incorporating a 12% cost of equity and 78% over-the-cycle combined ratio assumption, with Deutsche Bank highlighting that Coface carries excess capital representing 16% of market capitalization supporting generous shareholder distributions.

FUNDAMENTAL PEER POSITIONING Coface commands superior underwriting margins with a 71.3% net combined ratio in H1-2026, significantly outperforming competitor Atradius at 75.9% and maintaining an 8-point structural advantage versus closest primary credit peers. Negative pricing cycles constrain all primary players with Coface experiencing -1.3% pricing declines while maintaining excellent 93.6% client retention, as peer Atradius grew earned premiums only 0.8% in FY2025. Non-insurance services differentiation accelerates with Coface's business information revenue surging 12% organically, sharply outpacing specialty peer Beazley's 12% cyber premium contraction and Hiscox Retail's 8% written premium growth.

Capital resilience ranks favorably with Coface maintaining a 194% Solvency II ratio versus competitor Atradius at 236.3%, while Deutsche Bank calculates excess capital of 16% of market cap underpinning sector-leading payout ratios. Consensus revision momentum diverged sharply across specialty insurers with Hiscox and Conduit seeing upward EPS revisions of 10% and 8% respectively on catastrophe reserve releases, while Coface faced 2% to 3% downward estimate trims reflecting tariff and claims normalization headwinds.

ACTIVIST PRESENCE

No active activist presence has been identified in the shareholder base, with the ownership structure anchored by Arch Capital's stable 30% strategic stake.

MATERIAL RISKS

Compounding macro shock scenario (cross-referenced Section 3): A simultaneous prolonged Strait of Hormuz closure and universal tariff increases would trigger elevated claims severity through supply chain disruptions, rapidly exhausting 47 percentage point reserve release cushions and unraveling recent combined ratio outperformance below the 78% target. Reserve release exhaustion and claims normalization (cross-referenced Section 5): Underwriting outperformance is highly dependent on massive back-book releases; a rapid claims severity surge from decadehigh insolvencies would exhaust historical reserves and push combined ratios above the 78% target, severely contracting net income margins.

Artificial peer outperformance compression (cross-referenced Section 7): Apparent underwriting superiority can be artificially amplified by rapid reserve releases; a sudden macroeconomic shock will reverse 8-point combined ratio gaps as back-books exhaust and claim severities normalize across the sector, eliminating competitive differentiation.

Plausible problems to monitor

Decelerating organic revenue growth shifted from 6.0% expansion in FY23 to just 0.1% reported growth in FY25, underperforming the sector's ability to capture volume-driven premium expansion during the same period (Section 5). Sluggish consolidated organic growth of just 0.8% in H1-2026 highlights weak client activity levels and deliberate exposure filtering, materially lagging behind specialty insurance peers that delivered mid-single-digit organic growth rates during comparable periods (Section 6).

North American revenue contraction of -1.3% organically and -6.2% at reported FX in H1-2026 significantly underperforms the regional peer base, exposing geographic concentration risk and limited pricing power in a strategically important market (Section 6). Rising net cost ratio escalated to 33.9% in H1-2026, up 2.7 percentage points year-over-year, driven by deliberate technology infrastructure investments that materially exceed the peer-median expense ratio trajectory and compress operating leverage (Section 4, Section 6).

Normalizing return on average tangible equity declined to 10.9% annualized in H1-2026 from a multi-year average of 12.3%, falling below the stated 11% through-the-cycle target and trailing specialty insurance peer-median RoE levels of approximately 12% to 14% (Section 4, Section 6). Downward sell-side earnings revisions of 2% to 3% post-FY2025 results and a trimmed price target from €14.50 to €14.20 reflect moderating underwriting expectations and investment income normalization, contrasting with positive revision momentum across specialty insurance peers that saw estimate upgrades of 8% to 10% (Section 6, Section 8).

Strategic target abandonment of the business information 50 bps RoATE contribution goal signals that standalone services growth has failed to offset escalating technology investment costs, requiring compensatory dividend adjustments and undermining the diversification thesis relative to peers with successful fee-based revenue streams (Section 2, Section 6). Claims normalization pressure pushed the net loss ratio from 35.2% in FY24 to 40.3% in FY25, driven by decadehigh global corporate bankruptcies that exceed the peer-median loss ratio deterioration rate and threaten the sustainability of sub-78% combined ratios (Section 5).

Persistent tariff and macro headwinds including Section 301 tariffs covering 99% of US goods imports and the virtual closure of the Strait of Hormuz create compounding top-line growth constraints that disproportionately impact Coface's international trade-dependent client base relative to domestically focused specialty insurance peers (Section 3).
