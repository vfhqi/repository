Section 2 – The Price, Volume, and Subscription Dynamics

Section summary
  1. CVS Group and its corporate peers face weakening routine veterinary visit volumes as historically high fee inflation meets subdued UK consumer confidence.

The Price and Volume Feedback Loop

1.
Depressed routine visits: The compounding effect of double-digit veterinary fee inflation has contributed to a notable softening in companion animal footfall across the United Kingdom. Jefferies reports in September 2026 that CVS registered flat like-for-like growth in its core practices during FY26 due to this weaker consumer spending environment. This implies that further price increases risk triggering a vicious balancing loop of lower volume and fixed-cost deleverage.
2.
Escalating service fees: Large corporate veterinary groups have historically raised professional service fees to offset inflation and defend margins during the regulatory review. Dr. Cees Bennett noted in February 2026 that corporate practices are highly likely to protect their turnover by shifting revenue toward consultation fees and diagnostic intensity. This fee-shifting behavior ensures near-term margin protection but threatens to further alienate cost-sensitive clients over time.
3.
Inelastic emergency demand: Non-discretionary emergency and reactive clinical care remains highly resilient despite broader macroeconomic pressures on UK pet owners. Morgan Stanley's April 2026 survey indicates that while discretionary and routine check-ups are being deferred, high-income cohorts prioritize critical veterinary treatments regardless of cost. This divergence creates a two-speed demand structure where basic preventive volume declines while complex clinical care holds steady, supporting resilient diagnostic spending.
4.
Fixed cost delegation: Veterinary practices operate with high fixed cost structures driven by professional clinical salaries and advanced diagnostic equipment investments. Morgan Stanley argues in October 2026 that balancing payroll inflation with clinical affordability remains exceptionally difficult for standalone clinics. Consequently, any persistent decline in routine clinical footfall leads to operating margin compression unless operators successfully leverage service pricing upward.

Subscription Plans and Volume Lock

5.

Deutsche Bank notes in January 2025 that preventative health plans help lock in recurring revenue and maintain customer engagement. To further strengthen this loop, CVS launched the Healthy Pet Club Advanced tier in July 2026 to offer unlimited clinical consultations.

Membership churn headwind: Subdued consumer spending has started to pressure discretionary pet care subscriptions, leading to minor membership contractions. Deutsche Bank highlighted in September 2026 that CVS's Healthy Pet Club membership declined by 2% to 508,000 from 519,000 in the prior year. This contraction suggests that subscription plans are not entirely immune to persistent cost-of-living pressures, meaning they might struggle to secure volume growth.

Divergent peer loyalty: Pets at Home exhibits superior subscription loyalty through its integrated VIP club and Care Plans. Jefferies notes in July 2026 that Pets at Home's subscription revenue share increased to 15.3% of sales during the first quarter. The Transparency Loop and Market Share

9.
CMA transparency intervention: Legally binding transparency orders published by the Competition and Markets Authority on 22 September 2026 have dismantled historical pricing barriers. The regulator has mandated that practices must publish standardized price lists online and provide written estimates for treatments exceeding £500. This regulatory change effectively compresses the information gap that corporate chains previously used to sustain historically elevated margins.
10.
Corporate price premium: Corporate-owned veterinary practices maintain a significant pricing premium over independent competitors, leaving them vulnerable to price-based switching. The Competition and Markets Authority reported that corporate groups charge approximately 17% to 18% more than independent practices. This clear price premium means corporate clinics must justify their high value proposition under public price listings.
11.
Three speed market: The veterinary services sector has entered a three-speed phase where independent practices and joint ventures outperform corporate chains. Dr. Cees Bennett highlighted in February 2026 that independent practices are poised to win market share on price. Conversely, fully corporate veterinary groups face limited pricing headroom and negative public brand perception.
12.
Contrary evidence: Although price transparency is designed to stimulate intense price competition, some independent veterinary practices are actually raising their rates. Dr. Julian Samuelson argued in October 2025 that many independent vets will increase prices to match corporates rather than discounting. This suggests the price gap will narrow through upward convergence, which means that corporates will face less severe competitive pressure.
13.
Prescription fee caps: The implementation of a capped written prescription fee directly restricts a high-margin corporate revenue stream. Deutsche Bank reports in March 2026 that the Competition and Markets Authority finalized a written prescription fee cap of £21 in March 2026. Since written prescriptions historically carried high prices, this cap forces corporate chains to raise professional consultation fees.
