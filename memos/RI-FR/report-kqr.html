Section 2 – Pernod Ricard's Stated Dividend Policy and Scrip Options

Section summary
  1. Pernod Ricard's scrip dividend option on the final fiscal year 2026 distribution preserves critical cash while maintaining a flat absolute payment.
  2. Strong commitments from reference shareholders mitigate cash outflows, but the resulting share dilution threatens long-term earnings per share growth.

Stated Policies and Historical Payouts

1.
Stable distributions: Pernod Ricard has proposed a flat dividend of €4.70 per share for the fiscal year 2026, keeping it stable relative to the prior fiscal year. This absolute dividend protection comes despite a significant 26% decline in net profit to €1.20 bn. By holding the distribution flat, management is trying to protect retail shareholder sentiment despite a highly challenging global spirits environment.
2.
High payout: Pernod Ricard's stated policy targets a payout of approximately 50% of net profit from recurring operations. However, because net profit from recurring operations fell to €1.48 bn in fiscal year 2026, the absolute payout ratio has significantly exceeded this long-term target. This elevated payout ratio compares unfavorably with peers like Riken Keiki Co Ltd which reported a much lower consolidated dividend payout ratio of 25.3%.

Without asset disposals or a sharp turnaround in major markets, this flat dividend policy will keep leverage ratios elevated well above historical averages.

That comparable past case demonstrates that offering stock options helps highly leveraged firms maintain dividend continuity while addressing balance sheet stress.

Scrip Mechanics and Key Dates

Controlled dilution: Pernod Ricard capped the maximum number of new shares to be issued under the scrip program at 9,730,289 shares. This potential share issuance represents approximately 3.8% of the company's total voting shares as of November 19, 2026. While this mechanism provides vital cash protection, it introduces a corresponding risk of earnings per share dilution that could depress future valuation metrics.
7.
Strategic comparisons: Pernod Ricard's maximum 3.8% dilution limit is structured differently from peer return programs. For comparison, Japanese firm Takuma Co Ltd utilizes share repurchases of approximately JPY 18 bn over three years to offset dilution and improve capital efficiency. This highlights that Pernod Ricard is prioritizing near-term cash preservation over equity dilution management.
8.
Anchored support: Reference shareholders Société Paul Ricard and Groupe Bruxelles Lambert, who collectively own approximately 21% of the capital, have committed to elect the scrip. This significant commitment reduces the immediate cash outflow for the final €2.35 dividend, keeping the capital structured in favor of deleveraging. Having these key anchor investors opt for shares provides a highly stable floor for the scrip's overall success.

Section 3 – Leverage, Free Cash Flow, and Rating Constraints

Section summary
  1. Pernod Ricard's high leverage constraint forces management to prioritize cash retention and deleveraging over cash dividends to preserve its investment-grade credit profile.
  2. Rating agencies demand strict debt-to-operating-profit thresholds, while peer comparisons highlight that competitors are deleveraging faster through hybrid issuances and structural rebasing.

Leverage Targets and Deleveraging Levers

1.
Elevated leverage: Pernod Ricard reported a high net debt-to-EBITDA ratio of 3.8x during its Q2 FY2026 earnings call. This leverage increase was driven by softer recurring operating profits, which has forced management to focus heavily on cash preservation. To address this balance sheet stress, the group's clear intention is to bring the leverage ratio below 3.0x by the fiscal year 2029.
2.
Targeted initiatives: Pernod Ricard outlined four main levers during its Q2 FY2026 earnings call to achieve its debt reduction target. These strategic initiatives include normalising capex investments from peak levels, improving working capital, pursuing active portfolio management, and restoring organic operating profit growth. Management expects these self-help programs to yield a cash conversion ratio of circa 80% or above.
3.
Restrictive forecasts: Morgan Stanley reported on October 19, 2026, that Pernod Ricard's leverage remains a significant headwind. The broker's financial modeling suggests that the company will struggle to meet its 3.0x net debt-to-EBITDA target by FY2029. Consequently, Morgan Stanley argues that this goal is only achievable if the cash dividend is reduced or if further scrip options are used.
4.
Strategic options: Rothschild & Co Redburn projected on October 9, 2026, that Pernod Ricard's leverage will remain elevated at 3.6x in FY2027. To accelerate deleveraging, the broker suggests a partial IPO of the group's Indian business to sell a 25% stake. This capital transaction would reduce the net debt-to-EBITDA ratio to a more comfortable 2.9x.

Rating Requirements and Downgrade Triggers

5.
Strict rating: Rating agencies impose strict credit parameters that govern Pernod Ricard's capital allocation decisions. For example, Moody's indicated that maintaining its Baa2 stable rating requires Pernod Ricard to keep its adjusted debt-to-EBITDA ratio sustainably below 4.0x. A failure to keep leverage under this limit or letting the retained cash flow to net debt ratio fall below 12% would trigger an immediate credit downgrade.
6.
Disciplined investment: Pernod Ricard has attempted to optimize cash flow by tightening capital expenditures to preserve its credit standing. Jefferies reported on August 27, 2026, that the company successfully grew its free cash flow by 6% to €1.2 bn in fiscal year 2026. This improved cash generation was supported by capping strategic investments at €700 mm, representing a clear shift toward capital discipline.
7.
Contrary evidence: Bernstein Research reported on September 29, 2026, that the group's cash conversion target remains strong at 90%. Combined with tight investment caps, this high conversion rate allows the company to protect its nominal dividend while keeping its leverage ratio on a gradual downward leverage trajectory without absolute cuts.

Peer Constraints and Capital Structure Solutions

Diageo targets an adjusted net debt-to-EBITDA leverage ratio of 2.5x to 3.0x, which is consistent with its A- credit rating from S&P. However, Diageo's leverage stood at 3.1x as of June 30, 2026, which is currently above its official target band.

Jefferies reported on November 5, 2026, that Diageo's recent dividend rebasing will significantly accelerate its debt reduction path. Rémy Cointreau issued a €250 mm hybrid bond on September 30, 2026, carrying a fixed coupon of 6.75%. Because this issuance is classified as equity under IFRS and receives 50% credit from Moody's, it significantly enhances intermediate financial flexibility.
