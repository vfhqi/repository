Section summary
  1. Vodafone faces significant financial volatility over the next three years as its critical commercial wholesale relationship with challenger 1&1 transitions.
  2. The resolution of German spectrum allocation and potential consolidation talks will heavily influence Vodafone's long-term earnings per share and risk profile.

Section 2 – Commercial Terms and Revenue Contribution of the 1&1 Roaming

Section summary
  1. Vodafone Germany's national roaming agreement with 1&1 features a capacity-based pricing mechanism that provides long-term operational stability but introduces significant revenue volatility as subscriber migration lapped.
  2. The historical precedent of Telefónica Deutschland's wholesale loss of 1&1 illustrates the massive cash flow shifts associated with anchor wholesale contracts in the consolidated German mobile market.

Agreement

Commercial structure of the wholesale agreement

1.
Long-term commercial partnership: Vodafone Germany secured an exclusive national roaming agreement with 1&1 on August 2, 2023, to host the challenger's active mobile subscriber base. This contract has a commercial duration of up to 18 years and provides nationwide 5G coverage alongside legacy 2G and 4G mobile technologies. The commercial partnership commenced 5G services in the second half of calendar year 2024. This extended duration secures a stable, long-term wholesale revenue stream that protects Vodafone Germany's capital intensity over the decade.
2.
Unique pricing mechanics: Vodafone Germany's wholesale pricing with 1&1 utilizes a capacity-based model instead of a traditional per-subscriber tariff. Under these terms, 1&1 reimburses Vodafone for a share of total network costs proportional to its customers' share of total data volume. This arrangement provides economic security for both operators in the event of surging data consumption. However, the capacity model leaves Vodafone's wholesale income highly sensitive to the underlying data traffic ratio between the networks.
3.
Wholesale volume shifts: United Internet's mobile brand 1&1 completed the migration of its entire active base of 12.48 million mobile contracts to the Vodafone Germany network in November 2025. Prior to this transition, 1&1 utilized Telefónica Deutschland's network via a Mobile Bitstream Access MVNO agreement. The shift of these 12.48 million lines represents the largest mobile subscriber migration in German telecommunications history. This complete transfer of customer volume underpins Vodafone Germany's mobile service revenue expansion.

Financial contributions and quarterly revenue trends

4.
Wholesale revenue acceleration: Vodafone Germany reported that its mobile service revenue grew by 3.0% in the fourth quarter of fiscal year 2026, driven by the final phases of 1&1 onboarding. This performance was supported by wholesale revenue as more than 12 million 1&1 customers transitioned onto Vodafone's nationwide 5G infrastructure. The wholesale contract reached its full run-rate contribution during this fourth quarter. This positive tailwind illustrates how the contract initially supported German service revenues before the lapping period.
5.
Lapping revenue headwind: Vodafone Germany saw its mobile service revenue decline by -1.9% in the second quarter of fiscal year 2027. Deutsche Bank reported that this quarterly slowdown was primarily caused by a severe -2.2 percentage point drag from lapping the previous year's 1&1 wholesale contribution. Additionally, the phasing of service provider payments last year created an extra -0.6 percentage point drag in the quarter. This sharp deceleration demonstrates that the wholesale revenue contribution has peaked and is now acting as a negative comparator.
6.
Profitability trend deceleration: Morgan Stanley predicts that Vodafone Germany's adjusted EBITDAaL will continue to contract by -2% annually over the next two fiscal years as the wholesale boost laps. The broker noted that although H2 FY26 EBITDAaL trends showed modest improvement to -2.1% compared to -4.3% in H1 FY26, the segment faces a sustained tail-off. This down-trend means that the high-margin wholesale profit cannot permanently offset underlying retail pressures. Investors must expect sustained pressure on German EBITDAaL through 2028.

Historical wholesale precedents in Germany

7.
Precedent wholesale loss: Telefónica Deutschland suffered a severe financial blow when its long-term MBA MVNO contract with 1&1 Drillisch was terminated in August 2023. Historically, this single wholesale contract hosted approximately 12 million subscribers and generated over EUR 700 million in annual revenues. Morgan Stanley estimated that this partnership had a high OIBDA margin of approximately 75%, contributing EUR 300 million to EUR 400 million in clean free cash flow. This historic precedent demonstrates the extreme concentration risks of anchor contracts in the German wholesale sector.
8.
Severe financial damage: Telefónica Deutschland's total annual revenues fell by 3.8% to EUR 8.2 billion following the loss of the 1&1 wholesale agreement. According to union reports, the company's adjusted EBITDA dropped by 8.8% to EUR 2.5 billion, making the prior fixed-cost structure unsustainable. To mitigate this structural cash flow loss, the operator initiated a major restructuring that cut its workforce by 1,000 employees, representing a 15% headcount reduction. This case highlights how wholesale migration can severely destabilize a peer's profitability and force drastic cost cuts.
9.
Contrary evidence: The financial impact of the wholesale transition was partially negative for 1&1 itself because the migration to Vodafone resulted in much higher-than-planned national roaming expenses. In its 2025 annual report, 1&1 disclosed that because Vodafone's network grew slower than expected, 1&1's data usage share was higher, leading to elevated procurement costs of EUR 575 million. This cost overrun demonstrates that the agreement has proven less profitable for the buyer than initially modeled.
10.
Long-term transition timelines: Morgan Stanley analysts observed that migrating a massive subscriber base between physical networks is a highly complex process that takes several years to complete. The broker compared the 1&1 transition to the historical multi-year customer migration from Sprint to T-Mobile US, which experienced several operational delays. Similarly, 1&1's migration off the Telefónica grid took several years before Vodafone reached its full contract run-rate. This historical context indicates that wholesale cash flows decline gradually rather than falling off an immediate cliff.

Comparative operational metrics and market positioning

11.
Competitive service growth: Deutsche Telekom reported a second-quarter mobile service revenue growth rate of 2.9% YoY in its home market. This performance was approximately 5.0 percentage points higher than Vodafone Germany's mobile service revenue decline of -1.9% during the same period. This performance gap highlights that Vodafone is underperforming the German market leader despite its wholesale contract. The comparison reveals that Vodafone's wholesale gains failed to offset retail market weakness relative to key peers.
12.
Wholesale accounting discrepancies: 1&1 reported that its national roaming agreement with Vodafone requires all capacity costs to be recognized entirely within operating expenses. Under the previous Telefónica Deutschland contract, 1&1 was permitted to partially capitalize and depreciate these wholesale expenses on a straight-line basis. This accounting change reduced 1&1's reported EBITDA by approximately EUR 16.5 million in the third quarter of 2026. These different reporting methods show that wholesale commercial terms carry unique opex implications for the partners.

Network investment and long-term targets

13.
Network capacity investment: Vodafone Germany committed to investing heavily in its network infrastructure to support the massive volume of 1&1 national roaming traffic. This commitment includes a focused capital expenditure of EUR 100 million specifically allocated to ensure seamless customer migration and integration. Despite this specific migration budget, Vodafone Group's management stated that they expect to maintain a broadly stable overall capital intensity in Germany. This network investment allows Vodafone to monetize spare network capacity without increasing long-term capital intensity.
14.
Strategic market disruption: Morgan Stanley analysts discussed the strategic implications of Vodafone's aggressive pursuit of the 1&1 wholesale contract. The broker noted that this commercial move de-stabilized the entire German mobile sector because it left competitor networks partially empty and drove a massive spike in retail tariff competition. Competitors like Deutsche Telekom criticized the transaction as a highly disruptive market event. This disruption reveals that wholesale contract acquisition can de-stabilize broader retail market pricing.

Section 3 – Expansion of 1&1's Open RAN Network and Traffic Migration Dynamics

Section summary
  1. 1&1 is expanding its virtualized Open RAN network across Germany, but the pace of traffic migration off Vodafone remains highly constrained by spectrum limitations.
  2. International precedents from Rakuten and Dish highlight the severe system integration and capital hurdles that delay physical network independence for greenfield operators.

Operational rollout and coverage metrics

1.
Accelerating household coverage: 1&1 increased its German household network coverage to approximately 36% by the third quarter of 2026, according to the company's interim report published on November 12, 2026. This coverage expansion represents a steady increase from the 34% coverage reported in its second-quarter interim report on August 6, 2026. The operator originally exceeded its regulatory rollout mandate by reaching 27% household coverage at the end of December 2025. This rapid initial coverage expansion demonstrates that 1&1 is expanding its physical network footprint across major urban centers.
2.
Steady antenna expansion: 1&1 expanded its physical network infrastructure to approximately 2.5k active antenna sites during the second half of 2026, as reported by Deutsche Bank on August 7, 2026. The operator expects to expand this infrastructure further to reach 3k active antenna sites by the end of calendar year 2026. This projection represents a major acceleration from the 1.2k sites in operation during the first half of 2025. These ongoing antenna deployments allow 1&1 to gradually reduce national roaming dependency on Vodafone's commercial network.
3.
Complex customer migration: 1&1 successfully migrated its entire active customer base of more than 12 million mobile users to its private cloud network by early November 2025, according to its annual report published on March 18, 2026. Although these customers are now fully on the 1&1 core network, they still automatically access Vodafone's physical antennas in areas where 1&1 has not yet deployed its own infrastructure. This dual structure means that actual on-net physical traffic remains low despite the administrative completion of the migration. This operational lag will limit the near-term reduction of Vodafone's wholesale revenues.
4.
Limited on-net traffic: 1&1 is expected to face substantial delays in moving actual mobile data traffic off Vodafone's network due to a lack of low-frequency spectrum, as noted by Deutsche Bank on August 7, 2026. The broker points out that while household coverage is rising, the actual percentage of on-net traffic remains low because the network is not dense enough. Without access to low-band spectrum, 1&1 cannot achieve the indoor penetration required to host high data volumes. This technological bottleneck means that Vodafone will retain wholesale revenues for a longer duration than originally anticipated.

Technical hurdles and international benchmarks

5.
System integration challenges: 1&1 encountered severe technical and program management complexities during its early deployment phase, leading to a reorganization of its vendor supply chain, as disclosed by an industry expert on October 7, 2025. The operator originally utilized Rakuten Symphony as its primary system integrator but ultimately replaced them with Tech Mahindra due to persistent execution delays. This transition highlights the immense difficulty of orchestrating disaggregated multi-vendor networks in a large geographic market. This vendor disruption will prolong the physical network build and delay traffic offloading.
6.
Japanese network precedent: Rakuten Mobile faced severe capital cost overruns and sluggish subscriber acquisition during its pioneering Open RAN deployment in Japan, as reported by Deutsche Bank on September 23, 2022. Rakuten incurred massive operating losses because it was forced to pay high roaming fees to incumbent operator KDDI while constructing its physical base stations. Although Rakuten eventually reached 96% population coverage, the high capital cost highlights the financial risks of greenfield rollouts. This case study suggests that greenfield operators face prolonged losses before achieving scale.

If these spectrum barriers persist, 1&1 may be forced to scale back its physical rollout and return to an asset-light wholesale model. This strategic shift would protect Vodafone's wholesale income from rapid degradation.

A consolidation transaction or a complete return to a wholesale-only model would unlock significant equity value for shareholders. Section 4 – Telefónica Deutschland and 1&1 Consolidation Talks

Section summary
  1. Speculation of a strategic transaction between Telefónica Deutschland and 1&1 highlights a major consolidation opportunity to repair the challenging German mobile telecom market.
  2. Any corporate combination faces high domestic regulatory scrutiny alongside minority shareholder resistance and parent balance sheet constraints.

Rumored transaction value and structure

1.
Rumored acquisition talks: Spanish telecom operator Telefónica is reportedly negotiating a deal to purchase its German rival 1&1 in an asset transaction valued between EUR 4.5 bn and EUR 5.0 bn. Reports indicate that initial discussions between Telefónica and 1&1's parent company, United Internet, commenced in November 2025 before progressing to formal negotiations in early 2026. This strategic transaction represents a major push to consolidate the challenging German mobile market.
2.
Substantial premium valuation: The rumored transaction range of EUR 4.5 bn to EUR 5.0 bn implies an equity value of approximately EUR 27 per share for 1&1. Deutsche Bank analysts calculate that this mid-point valuation allocates roughly 25% of the estimated EUR 7 bn in total synergies to 1&1 shareholders. If 1&1 were to capture a full 50% of the synergies, its implied transaction value would rise to EUR 36 per share.
3.
Flexible financial architecture: Telefónica faces significant balance sheet constraints because it must protect its strict leverage target of 2.5 times debt to EBITDA. Deutsche Bank suggests that Telefónica could structure the deal by using Telefónica Deutschland as a vehicle for an equity split with United Internet. To support this transaction, the parent company in Madrid might execute a capital rights issue to protect its credit rating.
4.
Favorable shareholder structure: United Internet currently controls approximately 78% of the outstanding equity in 1&1. A potential transaction would allow Ralph Dommermuth's United Internet to exchange its subsidiary holding for a direct equity stake in a combined German entity. This structured merger would significantly reduce United Internet's standalone network capital expenditure burden.

Strategic rationale and synergy potential

5.
Significant cost savings: A merger between Telefónica Deutschland and 1&1 has the potential to generate massive operating cash flow improvements. Morgan Stanley estimates that combining their respective operations could deliver annual cost savings between EUR 700 mm and EUR 1.4 bn. These savings are derived from a cost reduction ratio of 6% to 12.5% applied against their joint annual cost base of EUR 11.5 bn.
6.
Massive net present value: The net present value of the combined entity's synergies is estimated at least EUR 7 bn before considering any broader market repair benefits. Deutsche Bank notes that these synergies would be extracted through network consolidation, capital expenditure reductions, and the elimination of duplicate tower rentals. Additionally, the transaction would eliminate the expensive wholesale national roaming fees that 1&1 must pay to host operators.
7.
Stable wholesale revenue: Telefónica Deutschland has a clear strategic interest in acquiring 1&1 because it must find a way to neutralize the severe financial impact of losing 1&1's wholesale contract to Vodafone. Analysts estimate that losing the anchor wholesale agreement represented an annual EBITDA decline of approximately EUR 500 mm for Telefónica Deutschland. Integrating 1&1 into its own network footprint would stabilize future cash flows.
8.
Challenged infrastructure investment: The newly appointed CEO of Telefónica Deutschland, Santiago Argelich, has questioned the long-term sustainability of having a fourth physical mobile network operator in Germany. Deutsche Bank reports that Argelich advocates a three-player market structure as more rational because a fourth player demands redundant infrastructure investment in a stagnating market. A strategic combination represents a logical path to consolidate these redundant network costs.

Regulatory frameworks and historical precedents

9.
Historical consolidation precedents: The proposed German mobile transaction draws comparison to historical in- market consolidations such as the landmark merger of Telefónica Deutschland and E-Plus in 2014. That transaction established Telefónica as a leading integrated network operator by combining two smaller challengers to compete with Deutsche Telekom and Vodafone. Precedent European mergers suggest that substantial network and commercial synergies are achievable if regulatory clearance is obtained.
10.
Strict regulatory oversight: Any transaction combining Telefónica Deutschland and 1&1 will face extensive scrutiny from the European Commission and domestic German regulators. The Federal Network Agency, or Bundesnetzagentur, and the Federal Cartel Office, known as the Bundeskartellamt, maintain strict oversight to promote effective wholesale market competition. Deutsche Bank analysts suggest that while European regulatory attitudes toward horizontal consolidation are slowly easing, behavioral or structural remedies may still be required to approve a deal.
11.
Contentious spectrum allocation: The regulatory landscape remains highly contentious following the Bundesnetzagentur's decision on November 19, 2026, to prolong low-band mobile spectrum to the three established operators. This decision excludes 1&1 from accessing critical low-frequency bands, prompting the challenger to appeal to the European Commission. This ongoing dispute demonstrates the intense regulatory intervention characterizing the German mobile telecommunications sector.
12.
Compensatory remedy requirements: In response to denying 1&1 access to low-band spectrum, the Bundesnetzagentur has mandated that the three incumbents pay 1&1 a nominal fee of EUR 5 mm each. Deutsche Bank notes that this ruling reinforces the status quo national roaming remedy as the primary mechanism for supporting market entry. This complex regulatory framework highlights why outright consolidation faces a highly uncertain antitrust path.

Standalone opex restructuring versus consolidation

13.
Aggressive opex reductions: Telefónica Deutschland is pursuing an organic standalone recovery strategy focused on rightsizing its operational cost structure to offset wholesale revenue losses. The company plans to implement significant headcount cuts, including a proposed 15% reduction affecting 1,000 employees. Additionally, the company entered into an agreement with TP to outsource customer service call centers in Bremen and Rostock.
14.
Sustained margin pressure: Standalone opex cuts may prove insufficient because Telefónica Deutschland's operating free cash flow margin remains weak post the 1&1 wholesale contract loss. Deutsche Bank notes that the subsidiary's operating free cash flow margin was trending at approximately 10% in late 2026. In contrast, Telefónica's operations in Spain delivered a significantly stronger operating free cash flow margin of 19% during the prior year.
15.
Strategic wholesale reformulation: To offset the 1&1 departure on a standalone basis, Telefónica Deutschland is working to expand alternative wholesale revenue streams. The company formed a significant alliance with digital platforms, including a partnership to host Revolut Mobile on its network. Deutsche Bank reports that while these digital alliances open fresh growth avenues, they cannot fully substitute the scale of a primary carrier contract.
16.
Superior consolidation economics: A consolidation transaction with 1&1 offers superior financial accretion compared to any standalone efficiency program. Deutsche Bank calculations show that a combined entity would deliver more than 20% free cash flow per share accretion for Telefónica. This substantial financial uplift explains why corporate leadership remains highly incentivized to pursue a transaction despite their public focus on organic growth.

Structural transaction hurdles and counterviews

17.
Contrary evidence: Regulatory hurdles are highly likely to block any outright consolidation of 1&1 because the challenger is a remedy-enabled competitor. Bernstein Research analysts argue that authorizing a 4-to-3 merger involving the primary beneficiary of prior merger remedies would represent an unacceptable about-turn for European antitrust authorities. Consequently, any proposed transaction faces extremely high competition policy barriers that would prevent full corporate integration.
18.
Disruptive minority rebellion: Internal corporate challenges represent another major barrier to a strategic transaction between the operators. Several investment funds holding minority stakes in Telefónica Deutschland have rebelled against the Spanish parent company, launching legal proceedings over cash pooling agreements. This litigation creates significant legal and administrative friction that complicates the financial architecture of any potential acquisition.
19.
Alternative network consolidation: Given the immense antitrust barriers to a full equity merger, operators may be forced to pursue less valuable network sharing models. Bernstein Research suggests that a physical network consolidation or RAN sharing agreement is a more plausible outcome in Germany. While network sharing reduces capital intensity, it fails to deliver the pricing repair and retail consolidation benefits of a full merger.
20.
Other side of the story: The structural barriers to consolidation could be overcome if 1&1's network rollout challenges become entirely untenable. Deutsche Bank reports that the regulator's strict low-band spectrum decisions make 1&1's path to physical network independence highly difficult. If spectrum bottlenecks and high deployment costs prevent 1&1 from expanding its network, parent company United Internet will face intense pressure to abandon its standalone build in favor of a lucrative combination.
