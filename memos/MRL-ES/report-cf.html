EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

MERLIN Properties SOCIMI SA stands at an inflection point in its corporate evolution, executing a bold transformation from a diversified Iberian real estate portfolio into a digital infrastructure powerhouse targeting 730 MW of data center capacity by 2032. This strategic pivot represents the most material change in the company's history, fundamentally altering the earnings trajectory from stable rental income toward hyper-growth digital infrastructure revenues expected to comprise 65% of total rents within six years.

The transformation is supported by exceptional balance sheet strength with EPRA LTV at 24.40%, placing MERLIN in the lowest peer leverage quartile and enabling the €7.84 billion data center development pipeline without threatening financial stability. Net Signal on Change Materiality: The change magnitude is profound and multi-dimensional. MERLIN has executed a €768 million capital increase at zero discount in March 2026, refreshed board governance with a new Chairman and proprietary director representation, hired a dedicated head of development to resolve construction bottlenecks, and accelerated €122.9 million in signed office disposals earmarked for residential reconversion.

Executive rhetoric has shifted from establishing digital credibility to addressing physical construction and licensing bottlenecks directly, with CEO Ismael Clemente describing the current phase as requiring "hard boots and helmet gear" execution. The company's traditional business continues performing solidly with like-for-like rental growth of 3.5%, but the Phase III data center expansion of 412 MW represents the true strategic bet requiring flawless execution over the next four to six years. Top Drivers Ranked by Impact: The forward earnings case rests on six operational drivers evaluated across distinct impact and plausibility thresholds.

First and dominant, data center capacity scaling from 64 MW to 730 MW IT capacity represents the highest-impact driver with management targeting gross rental income of €656 million from Phase III stabilization alone. However, this driver carries high dependency on securing power grid connections, which face severe Eurozone-wide bottlenecks. Second, logistics lease spreads remain resilient at 6.2% release spreads in early 2026, supported by structural undersupply in Barcelona where vacancy sits at just 3.7%, though like-for-like growth has moderated to 0.6%.

Third, office occupancy recovery targeting 94% stabilization faces mixed signals, with Madrid CBD reaching 99.6% physical occupancy while Barcelona struggles at 88.5% with an impending Meta exit. Fourth, Spanish inflation indexation at 3.2% provides a powerful revenue buffer compared to French competitors facing 0.5% tertiary indexation, directly contributing to the 3.5% like-forlike rental growth. Fifth, retail division optimization is extracting 6.1% like-for-like rent growth and driving tenant occupancy cost ratios to a record low 10.8%, positioning the segment for substantial property valuation writeups.

Sixth and negative, refinancing drag and equity dilution present intermediate headwinds, with FFO margins contracting to 59.8% from 63.5% and the March capital increase diluting FFO per share by 5.6% despite absolute FFO growth of 3.9%. Net Signal on Recent Delivery and Forward Credibility: MERLIN operates as a classic sandbagger archetype, consistently setting conservative FFO targets that are subsequently upgraded and beaten.

The company delivered FY25 FFO of €0.58 per share, beating initial guidance of €0.54 and upgraded guidance of €0.56, representing the third consecutive year of outperformance. The Phase I data center execution achieved a 100% say-do ratio, delivering 64 MW on time and budget with stabilized yields of 15.8% exceeding the 14.5% base case. However, Phase II Madrid properties face a one-year delay to 2029 stabilization due to external licensing bottlenecks, providing a tangible example of permitting risk materializing.

Management guided to flat FY26 FFO of €0.58 per share due to higher financing costs and uncapitalized development interest, though Morgan Stanley upgraded estimates to €0.55 expecting stronger legacy indexation. The guidance credibility remains high on operational metrics but vulnerable to external infrastructure dependencies, with Barcelona office occupancy representing a key tracking metric requiring monitoring below the 85% warning threshold.

This extreme deleveraging was achieved through the €768 million capital increase in March 2026, which represents the largest equity raise in Spanish SOCIMI history relative to market capitalization.

The leverage outlier creates asymmetric optionality for future capital deployment while simultaneously constraining near-term per-share earnings accretion due to substantial dilution effects.

The second critical outlier is MERLIN's unprecedented portfolio transformation velocity toward digital infrastructure assets. Management targets data centers growing from 6% of rents to 65% by 2032, representing a 59 percentage point sectoral rotation over a six-year window. This transformation pace sits at the top 1% of European REIT strategic pivots by speed and capital intensity. The third material outlier is MERLIN's accounting policy on development interest capitalization, where management maintains a strict policy against capitalizing development interest expenses during construction.
