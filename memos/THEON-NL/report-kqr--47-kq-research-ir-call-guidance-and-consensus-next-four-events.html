Section 2 – Corporate Guidance, Frameworks, and Management Commentary

Section summary
  1. Theon has solidified its short-term and medium-term financial frameworks by reconfirming upgraded FY2026 targets and raising organic revenue growth projections.
  2. Balance sheet leverage is projected to peak temporarily following the HGH acquisition, but management plans a rapid deleveraging trajectory.
  3. Sell-side analysts project robust revenue expansion for Theon through FY2028 supported by the integration of higher-margin platform and thermal acquisitions.
  4. Temporal leverage expansion from the debt-funded HGH acquisition remains a key concern for brokers despite projected rapid deleveraging.
  5. Discrepancies between consensus models and management's raised medium-term targets suggest that the market has not fully priced in the company's mix shift.

Upgraded Revenue and Profitability Targets

1.
Strong revenue momentum: Theon International reconfirmed its upgraded FY2026 revenue guidance of approximately EUR 600 mm at its Q3 2026 trading update in November 2026. This target represents a massive increase compared to the EUR 443.40 mm reported in FY2025 because of secular tailwinds in defense spending. Management expressed high confidence in achieving this top-line goal due to seasonal acceleration in Q4 deliveries. Therefore, the company's near-term sales trajectory remains underpinned by exceptionally robust defense demand across Europe.
2.
High margin baseline: Theon International established a baseline adjusted EBIT margin target of more than 26% for FY2026 during its H1 2026 results presentation. This upgraded margin target reflects successful costcontrol measures in the core night-vision business and a shifting sales mix. Operating margins have remained resilient at 26.20% for the first nine months of FY2026 despite integration costs from recent bolt-on acquisitions. Consequently, these operational efficiencies are expected to sustain industry-leading profitability levels over the near term.
3.
Significant growth acceleration: Theon International raised its medium-term organic revenue growth target in November 2026 to more than 20% per annum from its prior target of more than 15% per annum. This upgrade is supported by robust commercial momentum and enhanced revenue visibility from an expanded addressable market. Analysts at Deutsche Bank noted that their model already forecasts a 21% organic revenue compound annual growth rate through FY2029. This indicates that the company's new targets align with optimistic broker assumptions regarding market expansion.
4.
Contrary evidence: Theon International faces structural profitability pressure as European governments increasingly procure night-vision equipment through competitive OCCAR-type agencies. Deutsche Bank analysts noted in November 2025 that these centralized contracts are highly price-competitive and could compress margins on man-portable devices. This institutional shift threatens the sustainability of historical profit margins if lower-margin platform sales dominate the product mix.

Capital Expenditures and Financial Strategy

5.
Prudent capital discipline: Theon International revised its FY2026 capital expenditure guidance downward to approximately EUR 25 mm from the previous estimate of EUR 30 mm. This reduction reflects the near-term phasing of investments with the deferred balances expected to carry over into the FY2027 Capex profile. Key strategic expansion projects remain fully on track including the Athens ISR facility expected to open in Q2 2027. Therefore, the short-term Capex cut represents temporary investment timing differences rather than any reduction in operational scale.
6.
Stable capital intensity: Theon International expects capital expenditures to stabilize at approximately 4% of annual revenue over the medium term. This capital framework supports the ongoing expansion of production capacities at Harder Digital and other facilities. Management reconfirmed this 4% ratio during their November 2026 investor meeting to assure markets of their disciplined capital allocation. Thus, the group intends to maintain a highly efficient asset-light model even as production capacities scale up globally.
7.
Efficient capacity expansion: Theon International has committed EUR 15 mm in additional capital to Harder Digital to support capacity expansion. This strategic investment has increased Theon's equity holding in Harder Digital from 60% to 65% as of Q3 2026. Management highlighted that Harder Digital's tubes are already being integrated into recent night-vision orders. Consequently, this supply chain integration is expected to materially enhance group profit margins by reducing third-party tube dependencies.

Leverage Trajectory and Exosens Strategy

8.
Temporary leverage expansion: Theon International executed a new EUR 325 mm syndicated term loan facility in September 2026 to finance strategic acquisitions. This facility will fully fund the EUR 300 mm acquisition of HGH which is scheduled to close in late Q4 2026. Management expects the pro forma leverage ratio to temporarily peak at approximately 3x EBITDA post-closing. This temporary leverage spike is expected to elevate short-term financing costs and dilute near-term earnings per share.
9.
Rapid deleveraging plan: Theon International plans to rapidly reduce its leverage ratio back below 2.5x EBITDA during FY2027. Management expects leverage to fall further below 2x EBITDA in FY2028 through strong cash conversion and operating leverage. The group has also highlighted the option to monetize its liquid financial assets to accelerate this process. Consequently, the group's deleveraging path relies heavily on the successful integration of its newly acquired businesses.
10.
Strategic strategy shift: Theon International unexpectedly reversed its strategy regarding its minority equity stake in supplier Exosens during its H1 2026 results call. Management confirmed that they are no longer pursuing a board seat or a stake increase due to complex jurisdictional anti-trust clearance processes. Sriram Krishnan of Deutsche Bank commented that this sudden shift in capital allocation priorities is less comforting to long-term investors. Therefore, the company's long-term relationship with its primary supplier remains an area of strategic uncertainty.
11.
Alternate leverage mitigation: Theon International possesses significant financial flexibility to lower its leverage through its liquid financial assets. Management stated that monetizing these assets, which include its Exosens stake, could reduce pro forma leverage to below 1x EBITDA in FY2027. However, the company emphasized that asset monetization is a secondary option rather than an immediate priority. Thus, the group maintains substantial balance sheet headroom to navigate potential integration shocks.
