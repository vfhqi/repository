Volex (VLX-GB): what the half-year results of 12 November 2026 do to the existing case

These results strengthen the case a little on FY2027 delivery, leave its defining risk (one account at renewal) untouched, and fail to settle the cash question the case had set for this date; the bigger change is the share price, which now sits at a level the case itself associates with a renewal loss.

1. What changed

  • Profit came in above the case's range. Underlying operating profit for the half year to 30 September 2026 was $80.6m, a margin of 11.1% (9.8% a year earlier). The case expected 75m to 77m USD and 10.6% to 10.8%. The half-year margin is already the case's FY2028 margin.
  • Revenue was as flagged. $723.6m, up 23.9%; organic growth 23.6% at constant currency against guidance of at least 21%. Data centre and electric vehicles each grew 47% organically; Jefferies puts organic growth excluding data centre at 20%.
  • The gain was all below gross profit. Underlying gross margin fell to 23.0% from 23.3%. Underlying basic EPS was 27.7 cents, up 40.6%, against operating profit up 40.9%.
  • The second half is guided flat and full-year expectations were maintained, not raised. About $10m of data-centre revenue was pulled forward into the first half; a full half of Kepler SignalTek offsets its reversal. Management puts dual-running costs at 60 basis points of margin through the fiscal year and lifts capital expenditure from 3.3% of revenue ($23.7m) to about 5% in the second half, aimed at "customer-backed" capacity including data centres.
  • Cash is not resolved. Underlying free cash flow was $22.3m ($4.8m a year earlier) and cash conversion 56.5% (33.4%), but working capital requirements expanded and net debt was $241.3m ($184.1m a year earlier; the review does not state the basis). The case expected 200m to 215m USD including leases and wanted working capital near 27% of trailing revenue. The review carries no working-capital ratio, no inventory figure and no absolute data-centre revenue.
  • Sell-side. Jefferies: EBITA 2% ahead of its estimate and 4% ahead of consensus; estimates up 1% to 2% for FY2027 to FY2029; FY2027 revenue $1,435.6m (the case's own figure); FY2028 EPS 57.92 cents, still below the case's 0.64 USD; Buy and 750p unchanged; downside scenario 445p, lower than the 465p to 490p the case recorded. Other brokers made minor changes.
  • Price. 523p on 13 November 2026, against the case's 590p. The review's own price figures do not reconcile (a 6.9% fall to 528p against a prior close of 540p), so we use the dashboard's.

2. The challenge

First, one obvious alternative fails. "Data centre up 47%" reads like a re-acceleration that breaks the case's plateau. It is not. The case put the line at about 85m USD a year earlier and about 125m USD in this half, which is a 47% rise and a sequential fall from about 150m USD. The review's rounded figures imply somewhere between roughly $103m and $128m, on our arithmetic, including the $10m pull-forward. The plateau stands; if anything the underlying half was at or below what the case assumed.

Reading A: the operating gearing is real, and the case has the mechanism the wrong way round. The case's distinctive claim is that EPS outgrows revenue below the line, and that operating leverage is modest (incremental margins of 10% to 14%). This half showed the opposite. Revenue rose $139.7m and operating profit $23.4m, an incremental margin of about 17%, with gross margin down, so the whole gain came from overheads growing more slowly than revenue. EPS grew no faster than operating profit, so the below-the-line legs added nothing. Management attributes 1.5 points to cost optimisation and operating leverage, and its Centres of Excellence now carry 49% of revenue against 24% five years ago. If this is right, the case's margin path is too slow, the 12% target is reachable without acquisitions, and the brokers' "operating leverage" story is the better description. For it to be right, the second-half margin would have to hold near 11% on flat revenue despite the 60 basis points, and the gain would have to survive the pull-forward reversing. Against it: the case had already seen a 17.2% incremental margin in one half on data-centre mix, and a management that had this gearing in hand would probably have raised the year.

Reading B: the unchanged full year is information, not habit. The case treats flat guidance as conservatism from a management that guides under. But a 4% beat with no raise is, arithmetically, a lower implied second half. Jefferies' FY2027 EPS of 52.96 cents against 27.7 cents delivered implies about 25.3 cents in the second half, a fall of about 9% on flat revenue. The case already calls the second-half margin its one fragile number; what it does not hold is that the company is choosing to add cost in that half. Capital expenditure steps up by roughly half, pointed at the one line the case holds flat and regards as a single customer's decision, shortly before the case's renewal window. That cuts two ways and the case holds neither. Either management has sight of an allocation the case gives only about a 20% chance, and the spending is the evidence. Or capacity is going in ahead of a renewal, in which case the case's renewal-loss arithmetic (16m to 19m USD of operating profit) is too kind, because it counts lost contribution and not stranded fixed cost and depreciation. Which it is depends on what "customer-backed" means contractually, and that is not disclosed.

Reading C: the breadth is narrower than "20% excluding data centre" suggests. Electric vehicles reached $134m in the half, about 18.5% of revenue, from about $90m. Management says most of the growth was charging products and a larger share of its largest EV customer's requirements; Jefferies notes majority exposure to Tesla. That is roughly $44m of the $140m revenue increase from one account's programmes, alongside a similar amount from data centre. The case files EV charging in the defended, price-making half and rests its 5.5% to 6% organic growth on a diversified base. The alternative is that the group now has two wallet-share surges at two customers, the second looking much like the first did a year ago, and annualising well above the case's about 227m USD for the segment in FY2027. For this to be right, the EV programmes would need the short tenure and re-award risk of the data-centre work. The licence and certification position argue against that, which is why we rate this plausible, not probable.

The ratings file carries no Headline or Building Block entries; we name the four Master Ratings the memo set and the topic scores beneath them.

  • Thesis's Change Forces' Strength : slightly less pressure. FY2027 is more secure. Reading A, if confirmed, would push it up; one half is not confirmation.
  • Foundations' Robustness : the same. Delivery supports it; Reading C and an unproven cash line press the other way.
  • Setups' Fit/Crispness (C in the digest and the dashboard; the ratings file shows B): the same. No acquisition news; the "mechanical EPS" element was not visible in this half.
  • Case Riskiness : slightly more pressure, from capacity being added ahead of the renewal and a second concentrated line.
  • Pricing power and operating leverage topic : less pressure, on Reading A.
  • Guidance and delivery topic : the same; the beat fits the record.
  • Scope for upgrades topic : the same; upgrades of 1% to 2% are what the case forecast.
  • Current momentum topic : clearly more pressure. Relative strength has gone from the 85th percentile to the 33rd, the price is 6.5% below its 200-day average, and the pinned technical letter is D against B. Sell-side momentum is still A and the buy share is 100%, against the case's view that it could only fall.
  • Lifecycle topic : more pressure toward "stalling"; the date the case set for the company to decide the phase passed without the cash evidence.
  • Total shareholder return topic : less pressure. The case's values are unchanged, so from 523p its September 2027 value of 7.31 GBP is about +40% against about +24% at 590p, on our arithmetic.
  • Price against the case's thresholds: 523p is just below the case's 525p (9x its base earnings, or 10x its renewal-loss earnings) and short of the 500p at which the case said the set-up turns unusually positive. That condition also required a fall on sector noise without company news; this fall came on results.

4. What would settle it

  • The second review, four working days after the first. It should carry the remaining brokers' estimates and targets, which shows whether consensus for the second half moved down as Reading B implies. It is unlikely to carry the working-capital ratio unless it draws on the balance sheet, and it cannot carry the data-centre revenue, customer shares or the contractual backing of the capacity, because the company does not publish them at the half year.
  • Working capital and net debt on the case's basis. These are in the published half-year accounts; the review did not carry them. This is the one question that could be closed now.
  • The second-half margin. Above about 11% supports Reading A; the review's own trigger for concern is below 10.5%. Expected at the April 2027 pre-close and the June 2027 results. A January 2027 update is possible on last year's pattern but not committed.
  • Customer shares. The largest customers' shares of revenue appear annually, so Reading C waits for June 2027.
  • The capacity question. What share of the second-half spending is under binding commitment is a disclosure the company has not committed to make. Until November 2027 nothing scheduled answers it.
