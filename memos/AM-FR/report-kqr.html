Section 2 – Financial economics of the Indian 114-aircraft deal

Section summary
  1. The €31 billion multi-role fighter contract provides highly visible, long-term revenue backlog despite margin dilution from Indian localization requirements.

1.

2.
Inflated unit pricing: Deutsche Bank's 16 February 2026 report indicates the contract would value each Rafale at €265 million, which is inflated by spare parts and weapons.
3.
Lower real flyaway: Bernstein's 26 February 2026 note argues the real flyaway price sits below $150 million per aircraft after stripping out weapons and training packages.

Prior benchmark comparison: Bernstein's 26 February 2026 note highlights that this compares to the 2016 contract for 36 Rafales that was priced at €7.9 billion.
5.
Support package inflation: Rothschild & Co Redburn's 20 January 2026 note states that the unit values include heavy customization packages, similar to the €1.7 billion in modifications spent in 2016.

6.

7.

8.
Development cost recoupment: Rothschild & Co Redburn's 19 November 2024 report explains that export profits are structurally boosted by a 1987 state agreement allowing partners to recoup 25% of development costs.

10.
