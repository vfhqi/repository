Reconstructing the Accounting Treatment

1.
Held-for-sale criteria: The Coca-Cola Company entered into a definitive agreement in October 2025 to sell a 75% interest in its African bottling operations to Coca-Cola HBC AG, with the transaction expected to close by the end of 2026. The operations met all the required criteria for held-for-sale classification during the year ended December 31, 2025. Under United States Generally Accepted Accounting Principles (US GAAP), the assets and liabilities of the disposal group were subsequently recorded at the lower of their carrying amount or fair value less costs to sell. This accounting classification also dictated that the company record a $1,274 mm impairment charge in 2025 due to expected reclassifications of cumulative foreign-currency translation adjustments. 2 38 4 10K Depreciation cessation: The Coca-Cola Company's filings confirm that depreciation is not recorded on assets once they are classified as part of a held-for-sale disposal group. Consequently, the depreciation and amortization of the African bottling operations' assets ceased upon meeting the held-for-sale criteria at the end of 2025. For the prior full year ended December 31, 2024, the company recorded total consolidated depreciation and amortization of $1,075 mm, of which depreciation alone accounted for $997 mm. Within the Bottling Investments segment specifically, depreciation and amortization charges amounted to $319 mm in 2024 and $313 mm in 2025. 39 40 5 3 10K Contrary evidence: The filings do not explicitly specify the exact single calendar day on which the African bottling assets were formally designated as held for sale, reporting only that the criteria were met during the year ended December 31, 2025.

This structural difference suggests that Egypt's success may not easily replicate in South Africa, where severe consumer pressure and intense competition constrain pricing power.

Competitive Threats and FX Volatility

15.
Intense competition: Varun Beverages is aggressively expanding its manufacturing capabilities and route-to- market presence across Africa's largest soft drinks markets. The PepsiCo bottling partner acquired South African manufacturer Twizza and established a subsidiary in Kenya in CY2025 to capture regional growth. Pepsi products are priced at a significant discount to Coca-Cola SKUs in major South African retail outlets. This aggressive discount strategy has driven market share losses for Coca-Cola, forcing the company to invest heavily in promotional trade spend and diluting its regional earnings contribution.
16.
Extreme volatility: Jefferies Research on 29 June 2026 points out that taking on these new African markets introduces 14 new currency exposures and higher macroeconomic volatility. South Africa alone represents 60% of CCBA's total sales volumes, making the consolidated group highly vulnerable to South African Rand depreciation. Since currency hedging is unavailable across many of the company's emerging territories, sudden devaluations would severely impact translated operating income. Unhedged foreign exchange losses would directly depress group net profit margins and restrict annual earnings per share expansion.
17.
Other side of the story: Coca-Cola HBC AG could still achieve its financial targets if it successfully executes its revenue growth management playbooks and expands cold-drink equipment placement to drive volume. Jefferies Research on 29 June 2026 suggests that sub-Saharan markets outside of South Africa offer unprecedented long-term volume growth potential due to favorable demographics. However, this growth requires massive upfront capital expenditure on manufacturing lines and cooler infrastructure, which will restrict shortterm cash flow conversion. Therefore, even under a successful integration scenario, near-term returns are likely to remain diluted before contributing to earnings per share upgrades.
