OEM International (OEM.B-SE): what the Q3 2026 report and the response to it do to the case

28 October 2026

These results strengthen the operating case materially and, because the shares fell while the earnings rose, they bring the return the case rated F to within reach of the case's own entry level for the first time since the memo; the one thing that cuts the other way is cash, which a record quarter did not convert.

What changed

The quarter, reported 15 October 2026, against what the case assumed:

  • Net sales SEK 1,582m, up 23.6 percent, against DNB Carnegie's pre-results forecast of about 16 percent. Organic growth 14 percent against 7 percent expected; acquisitions added 8 percent; currency turned positive. The case's path carries 5 to 6 percent organic over the cycle and about 8 percent acquired.
  • EBITA SEK 261m, up 22.7 percent; adjusted EBITA up about 25 percent, 6 percent above the broker and 4 percent above Bloomberg consensus. The EBITA margin was 16.5 percent against 16.6 percent a year earlier and against the 16.9 percent the broker expected; adjusted, 16.7 percent against consensus 17.0 percent. The case holds the margin at about 16 percent, at 70 percent odds, and said the first-half drop-through was unlikely to repeat.
  • Diluted EPS SEK 1.44 against SEK 1.16, up 24.1 percent, against consensus SEK 1.29. Nine months: EBITA SEK 739m against SEK 594m, a 16.0 percent margin against 14.9 percent; diluted EPS SEK 4.04 against SEK 3.21.
  • Orders SEK 1,683m, up 40 percent; like-for-like including currency up 33 percent; organic orders up 31 percent; book-to-bill 1.06x against a three-year Q3 average of 0.94x; backlog SEK 1,386m, up 57 percent. The case's alarm is a book-to-bill below 1.0x outside the seasonal pattern.
  • Breadth: Sweden 11 percent organic on sales of SEK 841m and EBITA of SEK 160m; Finland, the Baltics and China 11 percent on SEK 286m and SEK 43m; Rest of Europe sales up 46 percent to SEK 455m with 25 percent organic and 19 percent acquired, EBITA up 70 percent to SEK 70m, orders up 92 percent and 18 percent above sales. The intake includes an Irish order of about SEK 117m, for delivery mainly in the second half, which the broker links to data-centre demand. Construction in Sweden and Finland is still weak and unquantified.
  • Cash: operating cash flow SEK 88m against SEK 179m. Working capital consumed SEK 155m, receivables up SEK 126m and inventories up SEK 55m, against an outflow of SEK 15m a year earlier. Calculated free cash flow about SEK 43m against SEK 168m in the quarter and about SEK 81m against SEK 482m over nine months. Fixed-asset investment SEK 45m against SEK 11m in the quarter, SEK 309m against SEK 24m over nine months; acquisitions SEK 20m in the quarter and SEK 275m over nine months; dividends paid SEK 243m over nine months. Cash SEK 359m against SEK 663m; interest-bearing liabilities SEK 180m against SEK 151m; liquidity including facilities SEK 941m against SEK 1,221m; net debt to EBITDA negative 0.8x on the broker's calculation. Management attributes the receivables to a limited number of larger units and gives no collection timetable. The company did not report a gross-margin percentage; the review's calculated proxy, on a basis that is not the case's 53 to 55 percent, rose to about 37.0 percent of sales from 35.9 percent.
  • Unpaid fixed and contingent consideration rose to SEK 44m from SEK 13m; the revaluation of contingent consideration cut Q3 EBITA by SEK 3.4m, with SEK 4.9m of acquisition costs in 2026.
  • No numerical guidance; the chief executive says the backlog and the balance sheet give favourable conditions for a strong finish to 2026. No successor is named; he must leave the managing-director role by 31 December 2026 at the latest and then serve six months in another role. The chair announced on 21 September 2026 that he leaves; the extraordinary general meeting called for 23 October 2026 proposes Patrik Andersson as chair and Simon Tillmo, described as dependent in relation to major shareholders, as a director, with five directors retained; investor representatives asked for more transparency on EQT's management participation programme for directors. The review's evidence runs to 22 October and does not carry the meeting's outcome. ABSSAC, a UK motion-control distributor with about GBP 2.5m of annual sales, was bought on 1 October with a marginal 2026 contribution.

The challenge

The readings put up on the day, tested first.

  • The top of the company is being rebuilt twice. It stands and has gained a little force. The Q3 release was silent on the successor with nine weeks to the contractual date; the board proposal is now specific, a new chair and a director dependent on the major shareholders; and the review carries no outcome from the meeting. Nothing in the quarter argues against it.
  • The order book is borrowed. Knocked down. Its own conditions were a book-to-bill below 1.0x and organic order growth well below the second quarter's rate; the quarter delivered 1.06x in the seasonally weakest period and organic orders up 31 percent, with like-for-like orders up 33 percent, so the surge was not acquisitions. We drop it. What survives is narrower and lands on the case's own path: if 14 percent is a spike flattered by the SEK 117m Irish order and Rest of Europe, the reversion is to the 5 to 6 percent the case already models, and with a backlog up 57 percent the alarm, organic growth below 5 percent for two quarters, is unlikely to trip before the middle of 2027.
  • The cadence is kept by count, not by size. It stands and has gained a little. Acquisition cash was SEK 20m in the quarter; ABSSAC is the only deal since June and is the smallest yet; the multiples paid are still undisclosed; and the balance sheet is being drawn by working capital and property, not by deals. It remains possible rather than plausible, because the count alarm, fewer than four deals in a year, is nowhere near.
  • The de-rating without a miss. The day-one reading was that a print merely meeting the bar would read as relief. The print beat on every line but margin and the price fell anyway. The de-rating leg is confirmed as a fact; the explanation offered for it lost force.

Three readings the case does not hold.

1. The quarter was a cash quarter, and growth itself is the drain. The case treats working capital as the place a demand reversal would show first and reads the fall in conversion as investment by choice. The quarter shows the opposite mechanism: demand accelerated and cash fell. Operating cash flow was 34 percent of EBITA; calculated free cash flow over nine months, SEK 81m, did not cover the SEK 243m of dividends paid, let alone the SEK 275m of acquisitions. The strongest form: a 92-day working-capital model growing at 11 to 14 percent organic consumes cash at roughly a quarter of every krona of added sales, so the faster the organic engine runs the less of the deal programme it funds; the leverage branch arrives through receivables rather than through acquisitions, the "net-cash" foundation the case leans on erodes with nothing bought, and the sell-side's compounder label, which needs about 65 percent free-cash-flow-to-EBITA conversion in the broker's own scenario, is being applied before the conversion exists. The review calls this the reading consensus has ignored. What would have to be true: the receivables not normalising in the fourth quarter, when the Irish order is delivered and billed; inventory staying up; working capital to sales not moving toward the broker's 24 percent for 2027. What runs against it: the comparable quarter's outflow was SEK 15m, so a swing of this size in one quarter is more plausibly timing at a few units than a new ratio; management's explanation is narrow but specific; the broker still models net cash through 2026; and the property spend is a choice that ends. We think it plausible, and the case does not carry it at all.

2. The park has been overtaken by the case's own arithmetic. The case said: own the business below about SEK 150, or about SEK 165 if the Q3 report and the successor announcement together make the multiple's hold probable rather than possible. The report did its half, hard. The earnings base moved up 5 to 7 percent on the broker's numbers. The price moved down 9 percent. On the case's own method, which values the shares at a blended 26.2x on the 24-month-forward figure, the price alone takes the modal return from about 3 percent a year to about 10 percent a year over eighteen months, the border of the framework's D and F; carrying the broker's upgrade into the case's path takes it to about 14 to 15 percent a year over eighteen months and about 12 to 13 percent over three years, a C and a D on the framework's anchors; and the modal entry level moves from about SEK 150 to about SEK 158. The strongest form: the stock now trades at about 26x the case's own path, within a turn of the blend the case called modal, so the de-rating the case waited for has been paid in five weeks while the earnings rose, and the return the case rated F has done most of its work toward the bar without the case noticing, because the case's trigger was written as a price and the price has not quite reached it. What would have to be true: the multiple settling near the blend rather than continuing to the case's lower branch of 24.3x, now about 10 percent below; organic growth at 5 percent or better, which the quarter made probable; a named successor by year-end; and the cash normalising. What runs against it: the price fell on a record beat, which says the market is de-rating for something the beat did not cure, and the framework's own caution is that multiples fall further than people accept beforehand; the lower branch alone still returns about 4 percent a year; the re-based valuation pin on 28 October held F on the price alone; and the entry level the case wrote with the successor named is still SEK 4 below the price. We think it plausible, and it is the reading Richard would most want tested, because the case's own second trigger is now 2.8 percent away rather than 12.

3. Part of the organic growth is the change's, not the cycle's. The case credits the owner change only with acquired growth and assigns the whole organic acceleration to the cycle. The quarter's 14 percent sits against a broker median of about 4 percent for Swedish compounders, Addtech at 1 to 2 percent organic, Beijer Ref at 8 percent, and an automation expert who puts end-customer spending growth in the low to mid single digits. Organic orders grew 31 percent. Management claims share gains in several markets. The case's own counter-case notes that the sponsor's playbook at Beijer Ref accelerated organic growth as well as deals. The strongest form: five or more of the fourteen points are share, won by a market-share model applied harder under the new owner; the durable part of the case is larger than the case counts; the organic leg of the path, 5 to 6 percent, is too low for 2026 and 2027, where the broker now has 11 and 6 percent; the setup's defining output, a major upgrade to the earnings trajectory, is arriving through the organic line the case excluded; and the price paid for "the cycle" was partly paid for the change. What would have to be true: the fourth quarter again well above the peer median with Sweden and Finland holding near 11 percent, not only Rest of Europe; the gap persisting when Indutrade and Lagercrantz report. What runs against it: there is no share data; the hot part of this cycle, capital goods, data centres, artificial intelligence and defence, is exactly where OEM's automation and electrification mix sits, so outgrowing the median may be mix rather than share; currency and the Irish order flattered the headline. Plausible, not probable, and the case has no line for it.

The reading we are not putting up is a margin one. The quarter sided with the case against the brokers: 16.5 percent with no operating leverage on 24 percent sales growth, against the 16.9 percent the broker wanted, and the broker's new path of 15.9 to 16.6 percent now sits on the case's 16 percent. That is a confirmation, not a challenge.
