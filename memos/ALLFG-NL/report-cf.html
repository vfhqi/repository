EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF) 4 findings

Allfunds is undergoing transformative strategic change following the recommended €5.3 billion takeover by Deutsche Börse announced January 21, 2026, with consideration of €8.80 per share comprised of 70% cash and 30% treasury shares, approved by shareholders on March 12, 2026 with over 99.9% votes in favor.

The company delivered strong 1H26 results with total net revenues reaching €337.6 million (+9.9% year-on-year), adjusted profit after tax of €141.5 million (+10.1%), and record assets under administration of €1.94 trillion (+21.3%), demonstrating robust operational momentum ahead of the pending acquisition.

The materiality of change is highly significant as the transaction fundamentally alters the equity investment case from standalone platform growth to Deutsche Börse strategic value realization, while internal leadership transitions and portfolio simplification structurally improve core margin quality and reduce execution complexity.

1.
Materiality of Change and Strategic Inflection: The CEO transition to Annabel Spring in June 2025 signals a rising probability of change of control preferred by minority investors, with the founder's departure removing opposition to takeover bids

The Deutsche Börse transaction remains subject to complex antitrust and regulatory approvals before expected closing in 1H27, combining Allfunds with Clearstream's Fund Centre to double segment pro-forma revenue share to 18% and target annual run-rate cost synergies of €60 million and capex savings of €30 million, with 50% of run-rate synergies expected by year-end 2028.

Portfolio simplification through strategic divestments of WebFG (generating €11 million beneficial results ) and ManCo units is projected to enhance group EBITDA margin by ~3%, structurally improving core business quality.

2.
Most Recent Earnings Delivery and Quality Assessment: 1H26 performance demonstrated solid top-line momentum with core commission revenue expanding 14.4% year-on-year to €208.1 million, while transactionbased revenue posted a moderate 3.1% increase to €63.5 million due to subdued retail trading activity, and net treasury income rose 3.9% to €41.6 million despite lower interest rate environment

Adjusted EBITDA margin reached 67.8% in 1H26, representing a 20 bps improvement on constant-perimeter basis, with the completed sale of Allfunds Digital (WebFG) generating €11.0 million beneficial result and portfolio exits structurally raising core group EBITDA margin by approximately 3%.

Platform service net flows of €51.0 billion were balanced between €26.1 billion from existing clients and €25.0 billion from new migrations, with asset base expansion highly supported by favorable financial market performance contributing €83.8 billion.

General and administrative expenses were impacted by €7.9 million increase in legal and professional costs associated with the pending acquisition, and subdued platform inflows from existing clients if sustained through late 2026 will limit organic asset leverage, leaving EPS expansion heavily dependent on volatile market performance.

3.
Sell-Side Positioning and Consensus Narrative: Morgan Stanley uses a DCF methodology with 10% cost of equity valuing Allfunds at €8 per share, Deutsche Bank applies probability-weighted valuation of €9 per share assigning 67% chance of deal success, while ING utilizes price-to-AUA peer regression deriving standalone fair value of €12.99 per share, with target prices largely converged to DB1 bid price limiting standalone multiple expansion until regulatory completion

Morgan Stanley lowered FY26-28 EPS by ~5% to reflect subdued platform inflows from existing clients, while Deutsche Bank reduced medium-term subscription growth rate to 8% reflecting slower ESG product sales.

Analyst narratives emphasize that exposure to broader DB1 investment case provides substantial upside, platform open architecture shifts remain highly structural and insulated from short-term cyclicality, the combined pro-forma ETF servicing footprint acts as defensive shield against competitive pricing pressure, and switching B2B platforms is operationally complex ensuring highly defensive network effect. Fundamental Peer Positioning and Competitive Dynamics: Allfunds holds a premium margin profile with adjusted EBITDA margin of 67.8% in 1H26 exceeding digital wealth platforms Avanza at 65% and AJ Bell at 38%, with longer-term base case modeling projecting structurally high EBITDA margin of approximately 67% through late 2028.

However, platform revenue yield faces pressure as ongoing passive asset migration drags transaction margins down to 0.2 to 0.3 basis points, though standalone adjusted EBIT margin of 60% in FY24 compares favorably to traditional European asset managers' average margins of under forty percent.

The banking license imposes capital constraints with Common Equity Tier 1 ratio reaching 34.4% in 1H26 versus minimum regulatory requirement of 17.5%, carrying structural capital costs not shared by capital-light platforms like AJ Bell.

Strong cash conversion with adjusted normalized free cash flow increasing 10.5% to €142.5 million and net financial debt declining to €67.2 million representing low net debt-to-EBITDA ratio of 0.15x supports strategic optionality.

The company demonstrates best-in-class profitability architecture but lags UK wealth platforms in organic net flows and consensus revision momentum, with organic asset growth rate of 8.2% sitting below sector leaders though exceeding traditional asset manager averages. annual run-rate cost synergies of €60 million and capex savings of €30 million, expecting 50% of run-rate synergies fully delivered by year-end 2028 through migrating high-volume transactions onto Clearstream processing infrastructure.

4.
Material Risks and Downside Scenarios: The most immediate material risk is that antitrust authorities could block or restrict the €5.3 billion merger, forcing Allfunds to revert to a standalone model with substantially lower valuation support

Structurally, platform revenue yield faces persistent pressure as ongoing passive asset migration drags transaction margins to 0.2 to 0.3 basis points versus 3.0 basis points for active products, with ongoing consolidation among large asset managers intensifying downward margin renegotiations.

Accelerating retail fee compression and higher capital requirements if rate cuts resume swiftly would depress cash conversion and trigger regulatory capital distribution traps under the banking license framework.

The compound downside scenario involves accelerated passive fund migration diluting average fee margins if ETP and alternative solutions fail to achieve meaningful scale, while unexpected central bank rate cuts if executed rapidly in late 2026 will depress treasury income breaching margin warning thresholds.

Subdued platform inflows from existing clients if sustained through late 2026 will limit organic asset leverage, leaving EPS expansion heavily dependent on volatile market performance rather than controllable operational execution.

Deal failure would collapse valuation if antitrust blocks or activist campaigns derail the acquisition, reverting Allfunds to its standalone floor with Morgan Stanley's bear case model valuing the standalone business at €6 per share under stagnant flows.

Plausible problems to monitor

Allfunds exhibits best-in-class adjusted EBITDA margins of 67.8% in 1H26, exceeding digital wealth platforms Avanza at 65% and AJ Bell at 38%, and surpassing traditional European asset managers' average margins of under forty percent. However, beneath this premium margin profile, several structural weaknesses emerge when benchmarked against peer medians and sector base rates that warrant close monitoring. The sell-side consensus has consolidated around a neutral holding pattern with 60% Equal-weight ratings, 30% Overweight, and 10% Underweight, reflecting a defensive pricing model anchored to the Deutsche Börse offer price rather than standalone fundamental upside.

This rating mix contrasts unfavorably with highermomentum peers like AJ Bell, which commands more constructive sell-side positioning driven by record gross inflows and double-digit revenue expansion. Analyst target prices have largely converged to the DB1 bid price, limiting standalone multiple expansion until regulatory completion, with Morgan Stanley using a DCF methodology valuing Allfunds at €8 per share and Deutsche Bank applying a probability-weighted valuation of €9 per share.

Platform revenue yield faces persistent pressure as the ongoing passive asset migration drags transaction margins down to 0.2 to 0.3 basis points, compared to 3.0 basis points for active products. This margin compression risk is materially more severe than for custody-based peers like Clearstream, whose fee book relies on settlement and custody billed per transaction, neutralizing changes in cash resting locations.

The active mutual fund concentration at 87% of total assets as of FY24, with passive products representing only 9% versus the industry average of 23%, exposes Allfunds to structural fee dilution risk as the secular shift toward passive investing accelerates across European wealth markets. The banking license obligations carry structural capital costs not shared by capital-light platforms like AJ Bell, with the banking group Common Equity Tier 1 ratio reaching 34.4% in 1H26, comfortably above the minimum regulatory CET1 requirement of 17.5% set for 2026.

This capital constraint compares unfavorably to wealth platforms Avanza and AJ Bell which operate capital-light, high-return business models without regulatory capital distribution traps. The elevated solvency cushion, while providing regulatory comfort, limits capital agility and shareholder distribution flexibility relative to non-banking peers.

Despite strong cash conversion with adjusted normalized free cash flow increasing by 10.5% in 1H26 to EUR 142.5 million, the balance sheet carries significant intangible assets of EUR 864 million, highlighting historical acquisition-driven development capital that creates ongoing amortization drag on reported earnings quality. This elevated intangible footprint contrasts with organic growth stories among digital wealth peers and raises questions about the sustainability of margin expansion absent continued acquisition synergies.

Within the most recent 1H26 earnings delivery, subdued existing client flows of EUR 26.1 billion contrasted with new client migrations of EUR 25.0 billion, signaling potential saturation or competitive pressure within the installed base. This organic flow weakness sits materially below sector leaders like AJ Bell, which increased total customer numbers by 39,000 in 3Q26 representing a 5% quarterly increase, and Amundi, which generated medium-and long-term net inflows of EUR 51.4 billion in 1H26 showcasing solid distribution momentum.

Transaction-based revenue posted only a moderate 3.1% year-on-year increase to EUR 63.5 million due to subdued retail investor trading activity, while general and administrative expenses were impacted by a EUR 7.9 million increase in legal and professional costs associated with the pending acquisition. These elevated transaction costs and weak transactional momentum create a dual headwind to near-term earnings quality that is more pronounced than for peers with diversified revenue streams not dependent on pending corporate actions.

The consensus revision momentum lags higher-growth peers, with Allfunds experiencing estimates down 6% over the last 12 months compared to AJ Bell's upward revisions of 15% over the same period. This negative revision trajectory, combined with Morgan Stanley lowering FY26-28 EPS by approximately 5% to reflect subdued platform inflows from existing clients and Deutsche Bank reducing its medium-term subscription growth forecast to 8% following softer organic flow trends, signals deteriorating forward earnings visibility relative to the peer group. The historical delivery track record reveals frequent restructurings and asset write-downs that dilute earnings quality, including the substantial EUR 135.2 million non-cash impairment of held-for-sale assets in the FY25 accounts and persistent below-the-line adjustments that regularly exclude restructuring, IT carve-out, and redundancy costs from headline adjusted EBITDA calculations.

This pattern of recurring exceptional items suggests adjusted margins may mask underlying recurring operational integration costs, creating a material gap between reported and cash profitability that is wider than for peers with cleaner income statements.

Outliers worth noting

Allfunds exhibits a cluster of distinctive operational and financial characteristics that position the company at the extremes of the B2B fund distribution peer universe. The most pronounced outlier is the company's best-in-class adjusted EBITDA margin of 67.8% in 1H26, which represents a structural premium of approximately 1,650 basis points above the sector median of 51.1% and materially exceeds digital wealth platforms such as Avanza at 65% and AJ Bell at 38%.

This margin profile reflects the inherent operating leverage of a platform business model where incremental transaction volume generates limited marginal cost, creating a powerful earnings conversion mechanism that stands apart from traditional asset managers and custody operators. The margin superiority is further amplified by the company's exceptionally low capital expenditure intensity, with underlying capex of EUR 21.4 million in 1H26 representing approximately 6.3% of adjusted EBITDA, significantly below the capitalintensive infrastructure requirements observed at competitors such as Deutsche Börse's Clearstream division which must continuously invest in settlement and custody technology infrastructure.

A second critical outlier emerges in Allfunds' regulatory capital positioning, where the banking group Common Equity Tier 1 ratio reached 34.4% in 1H26, operating at nearly double the minimum regulatory requirement of 17.5% and creating an extraordinarily large capital buffer that exceeds typical banking sector standards. This extreme capital surplus reflects both the company's strong cash generation capabilities and management's conservative balance sheet philosophy, but simultaneously highlights a structural inefficiency where excess regulatory capital remains trapped on the balance sheet rather than being deployed for shareholder returns or growth investments.

The capital constraint represents a unique drag relative to capital-light UK wealth platforms such as AJ Bell and Hargreaves Lansdown, which operate without banking licenses and can distribute substantially all free cash flow to shareholders without regulatory restriction.

The company's asset mix composition presents a third statistical outlier, with active mutual funds representing 87% of total platform assets as of FY24, placing Allfunds at the extreme conservative end of the industry spectrum where the sector average passive asset allocation stands at 23%. This pronounced active fund concentration creates both a defensive moat against immediate fee compression and a latent vulnerability to secular industry migration toward low-cost passive products, which generate drastically lower revenue yields of 0.2 to 0.3 basis points compared to 3.0 basis points for active products.

The active fund skew reflects Allfunds' historical roots serving traditional European private banks and wealth managers, but the deviation from industry norms creates medium-term margin compression risk if the passive migration accelerates beyond management's conservative modeling assumptions. A fourth outlier manifests in the company's net treasury income volatility, where treasury revenues declined by 23.7% in FY25 to EUR 77.6 million during the monetary easing cycle, demonstrating extreme sensitivity to central bank policy shifts that materially exceeds the treasury income exposure observed at peer platforms.

This heightened rate dependency stems from Allfunds' business model of capturing the full interest margin on client cash deposits without paying interest to underlying clients, creating a zero-cost funding structure that amplifies both upside during rate hiking cycles and downside during easing periods. The treasury income swing represents a structural earnings quality dilution relative to pure-play transaction platforms whose revenue streams exhibit greater stability across macroeconomic cycles.

The company's Alternatives distribution asset growth trajectory constitutes a fifth statistical outlier, with alternative assets expanding by 54.4% year-on-year to EUR 41.4 billion in 1H26, representing one of the fastest growth rates in the European wealth distribution sector and positioning Allfunds at the forefront of the private markets democratization trend. However, this explosive growth occurs from a negligible base representing approximately 2% of total platform assets, creating a stark contrast with established custody giants such as Euroclear FundsPlace which maintains over EUR 3.6 trillion in total fund assets under custody.

The alternatives platform expansion demonstrates genuine execution momentum in a high-margin product category, but the absolute scale remains too small to materially offset structural fee compression in the core mutual fund business over the near-term forecast horizon. A sixth operational oddity emerges in Allfunds' persistent restructuring and integration cost adjustments, where the company excluded EUR 135.2 million of non-cash impairments in FY25 related to the divestment of WebFG and Luxembourg ManCo units, following prior periods of elevated integration and redundancy expenses.

This pattern of recurring below-the-line adjustments raises fundamental questions about the sustainability of reported adjusted EBITDA margins, as frequent corporate reorganizations and portfolio rationalization activities begin to resemble recurring operational expenses rather than truly one-time exceptional items.
