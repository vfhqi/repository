Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. The evidence supports a conditional, moderate plausibility that Coloplast can hold an adjusted EBIT margin of about 25% while growing 5-7% organically.

The Key Question asks how plausible the change now expected for this company is, given its record of delivery and the strengths the business demonstrably has, rather than strengths it would need to acquire.

The demonstrated strengths are US group-purchasing access, share capture in acute care, and the Atos integration.

The strengths Coloplast would still need to acquire are pricing power, a Straumann-style clinician-education loop, a stable wound franchise, and offsets to wage inflation.

Targets built mainly on demonstrated strengths look achievable, while any upside beyond them looks harder.

The overall picture is a margin-holding reinvestment plan, not a margin-expansion story.

Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The strategic plausibility of Coloplast holding a mid-20s operating margin while maintaining 5-7% organic growth depends heavily on the execution of its new operational savings programs and regional commercial transitions.

Defining the Strategic Dilemma

1.
The Key Question: "how plausible is the change now expected for this company, given its record of delivery and the strengths the business demonstrably has, rather than strengths it would need to acquire?"
2.
Strategic balance: Coloplast's future earnings per share over the next three fiscal years will be heavily defined by whether it can defend its new operating margin floor. If the company fails to maintain this margin while investing in its US chronic care expansion, consensus projections of DKK 24.90 for fiscal year 2026/27 will face substantial downside. Achieving these targets is critical because any further operational slippage would severely delay the recovery of its return on capital.
3.
Strategic choices: The upcoming transition to a flat margin framework reflects a broader medtech sector shift toward prioritizing raw topline growth over margin expansion. Under this scenario, Coloplast is expected to experience flat margins over the medium term in its base case and contraction in its bear case. This strategic pivot will determine whether the company can protect its long-term market valuation or face further multiple compression.
4.
Leadership transition: The upcoming appointment of Gavin Wood as chief executive officer on May 1, 2026, represents a critical transition point for the organization. This transition occurs right after the company provided its new medium-term strategy, which could weigh on near-term investor sentiment. Investors are highly focused on whether the new leader will focus on productivity initiatives and resource reallocation rather than accepting structurally lower profitability.
5.
Analytical framework: To evaluate this strategic transition, the analyst conducted a comprehensive review of the chronic care medtech market and its historical peers. This investigation found and cited 112 distinct documents from broker research, expert calls, and company filings. Unless the company successfully offsets these negative pressures through its new savings initiatives, its constant-currency operating margins will remain pressured.

Section 2 – Growth vs. Margin Trade-offs and Consumables Precedents

Section summary
  1. Coloplast targets an adjusted EBIT margin of 25% and 5-7% organic growth by reinvesting operational savings.
  2. Consumables leaders like Straumann show that maintaining premium growth requires continuous, heavy commercial reinvestment.
  3. Precedents indicate that flat margin frameworks often result in trading structural profitability for defensive growth.

Historical Profitability and Strategic Shifts

1.
Historical profitability: Coloplast historical margins peaked at 32.6% in the 2020/21 financial year, driven by strong cost controls and a less competitive global chronic care landscape. However, the newly established mediumterm framework resets the expected EBIT margin down to a flat mid-20s percentage range through the 2029/30 fiscal year. This structural degradation reflects escalating external pressures, including US competitive bidding and pricing reforms across European markets, which prevent a return to historical profitability. Thus, the company is trading its legacy premium margin profile for top-line security.
2.
Reduced ambitions: Coloplast management revised its 2030 financial ambitions to target 5-7% organic revenue growth per year, down from the previous target of 7-8%. To support these revised goals, the company intends to maintain an EBIT margin of around 25% for the 2026/27 financial year. Bernstein analysts note that this reset represents a much-needed concession to execution realities and rising regulatory headwinds. This lower margin floor is designed to absorb near-term headwinds while sustaining Chronic Care sales above market rates.
3.
Reallocation strategies: Coloplast initiated the 'Funding the Journey' program to free up approximately DKK 500 million in resources by the 2027/28 fiscal year. Management expects to redirect about 80% of these savings directly back into commercial priorities and long-term innovation. Only the remaining portion of these savings will help offset gross margin pressures such as Hungarian wage inflation and Portugal facility ramp-up costs. Consequently, this program functions primarily as a reallocation exercise rather than a tool for absolute margin expansion.

Consumables Peers Reinvestment Precedents

4.
Peer precedents: Alcon provides a key medtech precedent by implementing an efficiency savings program while simultaneously absorbing significant US tariff refunds in its 2026 fiscal year. The company opted to reinvest approximately two-thirds of refunds back into the business to support new product launches. While this strategy successfully supports high-single-digit top-line momentum, it limits the core operating margin expansion in the near term. This trade-off demonstrates that consumables peers often choose to sacrifice incremental profitability to defend market share.
5.
Profit harvest: Avantor serves as another comparable consumables case where management maintains structural control over margins through discretionary investment spending. The company's management actively balance their productivity savings with growth investments to ensure sustainable long-term profitability. Bernstein analysts observe that this flexible spending structure allows the company to self-fund growth initiatives while navigating volatile market demands. However, high reinvestment rates inevitably cap short-term margin expansion, forcing a direct trade-off between growth and immediate profitability.
6.
Contrary evidence: Some analyst assessments suggest that Coloplast can achieve gradual margin expansion over the medium term because of operating leverage post-investments. DNB Carnegie analysts argued in November 2026 that lower facility ramp-up costs and recovering high-margin biologics sales will support gradual margin expansion over time despite competitive bidding headwinds. This perspective contradicts the consensus view of flat profitability, assuming that operational efficiencies will eventually outpace raw material and wage inflation.
7.
Leverage constraints: Coloplast's target of a mid-20s operating margin implies relatively limited operating leverage over the next three fiscal years. Deutsche Bank analysts note that wage inflation of high single digits in Hungary and ramp-up costs in Portugal will continue to weigh heavily on gross margins. Without significant growth acceleration toward the upper end of the 5-7% target, structural cost pressures are expected to keep margins flat. This dynamic makes holding both growth and margins highly dependent on flawless operational execution.

Straumann Clinician Education Loop

8.
Ecosystem stickiness: Straumann Group accelerates its premium consumables growth by integrating high-value implants into a closed digital ecosystem. The company's open, cloud-based AXS platform now connects over 20,000 active users, driving significantly higher reorder rates for consumables and implants. Jefferies research confirms that this digital integration creates a self-reinforcing flywheel that increases customer lifetime value and defends premium market share. This closed-loop system allows Straumann to sustain organic growth without sacrificing its core profitability.
9.
Education investment: Straumann Group leverages extensive clinician training to expand market penetration and secure long-term product adoption. The company trains over 370,000 dental professionals annually, utilizing clinical education events like the International Esthetic Days to deepen customer relationships and drive adoption of premium solutions. Deutsche Bank research highlights that these educational loops are highly effective drivers of customer choice in mature markets. Consequently, professional education acts as a critical commercial buffer that protects high-end consumables pricing.
10.
Profitability defense: Straumann targets an organic sales growth CAGR of approximately 10% and annual EBIT margin expansion of 40-50 basis points through 2030. Jefferies analysts argue that this margin progression is supported by operational leverage and manufacturing productivity, which offset clear aligner mix dilution. By using its digital platform to drive repeat orders, the company avoids the heavy promotional spending that typically erodes consumables profitability. This model proves that premium pricing can be defended through clinical differentiation and workflow integration.

Demant Post Acquisition De-leveraging

11.
Capital discipline: Demant historical capital allocation relies on a strict leverage target of 2.0x to 2.5x net debt to EBITDA. The company prioritizes value-creating acquisitions and bolt-on distribution deals, returning all remaining excess liquidity to shareholders through share buybacks. DNB Carnegie notes that this capital allocation policy represents a highly disciplined approach to shareholder value creation. However, major transactions can temporarily disrupt this cycle, forcing management to adjust its operational priorities.
12.
102 104 - Speech Accelerated deleveraging: Demant suspended share buybacks in June 2025 to fund its acquisition of the German hearing aid retailer KIND. Management successfully paid down the acquisition debt ahead of schedule, reducing its gearing multiple to 2.5 by mid-2025 and paving the way for buyback resumption. Deutsche Bank analysts highlight that this rapid deleveraging timeline underscores the company's strong underlying cash flow generation. This precedent demonstrates that disciplined capital allocation can restore balance sheet strength within twelve to eighteen months.
13.
Other side of the story: Some market perspectives suggest that Coloplast's transition to a flat 25% margin represents a permanent structural downgrade rather than a temporary reinvestment phase. Morgan Stanley analysts point out that the company's new mid-term growth profile sits below peers like Convatec, while offering broadly similar operating margins in the mid-20s. This erosion of Coloplast's historical premium valuation suggests that the business model loops of peers like Straumann cannot be easily replicated. Therefore, the company risks trading away its premium margin permanently without achieving the desired high-single-digit growth.

Section 3 – US Group-Purchasing Access and Chronic Care Market Share

Section summary
  1. Coloplast expanded its US ostomy market share to 20% by leveraging major GPO contract access with Premier and Vizient.
  2. Hollister remains the dominant player in the US hospital channel through its singlesource hold on HealthTrust accounts.
  3. The upcoming 2028 Medicare competitive bidding program presents pricing risks but favors integrated manufacturers with in-house distribution.

Strategic Group-Purchasing Contract Gains

1.
Strong progress: Coloplast secured a three-year GPO contract with Premier in January 2020, providing access to 25% of the acute care market. The company then secured a multi-source ostomy contract with Vizient in March 2021, which represents another 50% of acute care accounts. These landmark agreements allowed Coloplast to reach more US hospital networks than ever before. These structural gains are expected to support long-term earnings per share expansion as hospital patients transition into profitable community channels.

113 114 Extended access: Coloplast successfully locked in its long-term group-purchasing access by extending both key contracts. The company extended its Premier agreement through March 31, 2029, and its Vizient agreement through June 30, 2028. These multi-source contracts ensure that Coloplast can sustainably grow its

clinical footprint over the next three years. Stronger commercial execution in these accounts will be key to protecting the company's long-term annuity model.
3.
Accelerating capture: Coloplast captured market share rapidly in the US acute care setting following these GPO contract victories. The company's acute market share in bags and plates increased by 10 percentage points between the first quarter of 2021 and the second quarter of 2026. This high velocity of capture helped Coloplast increase total US ostomy share to approximately 20% in fiscal year 2025/26 from 15% in fiscal year 2021/22. This steady patient acquisition in acute channels is critical because these users remain on the platform for many years.

Significant advancement: DNB Carnegie research shows that Coloplast has overtaken Convatec to become the number two supplier in the US acute setting. The broker indicates that Coloplast's share of newly discharged patients is now above its overall US share of 15-20%. Winning patients early is highly lucrative because they typically utilize ostomy products for approximately ten years. Consequently, these patient inflows are poised to drive steady long-term growth for the US ostomy business.

Comparative Performance Against US Leaders

5.
Dominant incumbency: Hollister continues to lead the US ostomy market with an estimated 50% market share. The private competitor maintains a tight grip on the hospital channel as the single-source incumbent for HealthTrust. HealthTrust represents approximately 15% of the acute market, making it the only major GPO closed to Coloplast. If Coloplast fails to crack this single-source lockup during future renewals, its ability to expand US ostomy margins will remain constrained.
6.
Distribution focus: Coloplast differentiated its US market strategy by actively building direct-to-consumer and distributor relationships rather than relying solely on hospital contracts. Expert call insights indicate that Hollister leads the market through time in the market and brand recognition. Competitor B. Braun remains relatively stronger in continence care than in the US ostomy sector. To compete against these established players, Coloplast has focused on patient-centric support and direct delivery to drive brand adoption.
7.
Product contestation: Coloplast and Hollister actively contest the clinical channel by targeting wound, ostomy, and continence nurses. Former sales managers state that while Hollister maintains strong brand recognition, Coloplast's ostomy products are highly regarded for superior patient experience and design. Competitors have grown highly aggressive in patient enrollment programs, offering free product samples directly to discharged users. This intense local competition limits the speed at which Coloplast can convert community patients and improve its margins.
8.
Contract comparison: Coloplast's GPO contract access spans approximately 75% of the US acute market through Premier and Vizient. In contrast, competitor Hollister dominates the remaining 15% of acute accounts through its exclusive contract with HealthTrust. This GPO concentration means Coloplast must rely heavily on multi-source agreements to acquire new patients. Winning these acute hospital contracts is a critical precursor to expanding the company's high-margin community business.

Hospital Channel Economics and Bidding Threats

9.
Bidding overhang: Coloplast faces looming regulatory uncertainty from the Medicare DMEPOS Competitive Bidding Program scheduled for January 1, 2028. The Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services finalized the inclusion of ostomy and urological supplies under a nationwide framework. This bidding program is expected to exert substantial downward pricing pressure on manufacturers and distributors alike. These regulatory shifts could moderate the long-term sales CAGR of the US ostomy franchise.
10.
Pricing compression: Bernstein analysts estimate that the competitive bidding program could trigger a 30% reduction in retail prices. This retail decline is projected to compress wholesale manufacturer pricing by approximately 15% due to spillover effects. Under this scenario, Coloplast's group sales could experience a negative 1% impact, while its EBITA could decline by 3%. These structural margin compressions illustrate the high stakes of the upcoming regulatory reset.
11.
Distribution shield: Coloplast is relatively well shielded from bidding headwinds due to its direct ownership of Comfort Medical. DNB Carnegie notes that the shift to nationwide contracts will favor larger providers with established national fulfillment infrastructure. The competitive bidding criteria will likely trigger distributor consolidation, allowing Coloplast's Comfort Medical to capture additional market volume. Consequently, this integrated distribution model helps protect the company's long-term US earnings potential.
12.
Contrary evidence: The bipartisan Access to Medical Supplies Act, introduced on October 9, 2026, proposes a five- year pause on Medicare competitive bidding for ostomy and urological supplies. If this bipartisan bill is enacted, it would defer the competitive bidding program until at least 2033. This legislative delay would completely eliminate the near-term wholesale pricing pressure on manufacturers.
13.
152 side of the story: Some market perspectives suggest that GPO contract access is not a guaranteed driver of profitable US ostomy expansion. Expert insights indicate that Coloplast's growth remains constrained by its conservative financial targets and limited sales force presence. Furthermore, even if competitive bidding is delayed, private payers will continue to push for lower prices. Therefore, Coloplast may struggle to sustain its double-digit US chronic care growth regardless of its GPO access.

  1. Coloplast targets an operating margin above 10% for its refocused Wound unit by shifting its biologics sales predominantly to the stable inpatient hospital setting.
  2. The January 2026 Medicare outpatient flatrate reset of 127.14 dollars per square centimetre severely compressed margins and volumes for high-priced skin-substitute makers.
  3. Precedents like Smith and Nephew demonstrate that while elevated R&D spending accelerates product pipelines, regulatory resets can quickly derail commercial turnaround efforts.

Wound Margin Targets and Refocus Strategy

1.
Ambitious recovery: Coloplast expects its Wound and Tissue Repair unit to return to growth in the 2026/27 fiscal year. Management targets an operating margin above 10% for this refocused division after a dismal performance in the previous year. To achieve this, the salesforce is concentrating on inpatient hospital care rather than outpatient settings. This strategic shift represents a significant pivot to stable reimbursement settings.
2.
Biologics decline: Coloplast's Kerecis business experienced significant organic contraction during the 2025/26 fiscal year due to US regulatory shocks. Kerecis delivered a negative 3% organic growth rate compared with original management expectations of 25% growth. Consequently, the company recognized a massive DKK 3 billion impairment loss related to Kerecis. This asset impairment illustrates how regulatory resets can severely erode acquired business valuations.
3.
Resource allocation: Coloplast is utilizing its newly launched Funding the Journey program to support its margin goals. The program aims to free up DKK 500 million through organizational simplification and procurement cost reductions. Downside strategic trade-offs

1.
Downside projection: Morgan Stanley analysts outline a severe bear case where Coloplast's organic revenue growth slows to a 4.4% CAGR through fiscal year 2027/28. 2.
Weakening leverage: Deutsche Bank analysts expect Coloplast's operating margins to contract over the next two fiscal years due to rising cost inflation. The company faces high-single-digit Hungarian wage inflation and persistent facility ramp-up costs that limit absolute operating leverage. Unless organic growth accelerates significantly, these structural headwinds make holding both a 25% margin and 5-7% growth highly challenging. Therefore, investors must anticipate near-term earnings downside if these cost pressures outpace operational savings.
3.
Negative pricing: Coloplast management explicitly assumes a neutral-to-negative pricing contribution across its long-term strategic period due to intensifying regulatory pressures. Regulatory pricing reforms in France and Germany, starting in January 2027, are expected to introduce immediate low-single-digit pricing headwinds in those chronic care markets. Consequently, these localized reimbursement cuts will directly erode gross margins unless offset by premium volume mix. 4.

Therefore, any failure to defend the margin floor represents a major long-term risk for investors.

7.
Intense leverage: Major US group-purchasing organizations hold immense power to squeeze manufacturer margins through multi-source contracting and pricing tenders. Coloplast's acute care access relies heavily on its existing Premier and Vizient contracts, which represent 75% of acute accounts. If these GPOs demand deeper discounts during future negotiations, Coloplast may be forced to choose between losing market access or diluting its pricing. Consequently, high GPO concentration represents a critical systemic risk to the company's long-term US margin assumptions.
