Section 2 – Status of BC Partners' Sale of Nova

Section summary
  1. BC Partners is actively pursuing a strategic exit of United Group either as a whole or through constituent divestments like Greek operator Nova.
  2. United Group's divestment of its non-EU telecom and media assets has successfully raised one point five billion euros to date.

Corporate Exit Strategy

1.
Strategic exit: BC Partners has appointed JP Morgan and Morgan Stanley to advise on the sale of United Group to optimize capital returns. The private equity firm expects the transaction to achieve an enterprise value of approximately eight billion euros, representing a high single-digit EBITDA multiple. This potential divestment would restructure United Group's portfolio and directly influence long-term earnings per share trajectories across its European assets.
2.
Substantial contribution: Nova represents a crucial piece of United Group's portfolio as the second-largest contributor to its earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization. Deutsche Bank reports that Nova accounted for 27% of the parent company's consolidated EBITDAaL in fiscal year 2023. However, Nova has consistently failed to generate positive operating free cash flow under its current ownership structure.
3.
Structural refinancing: United Group successfully completed a one point five billion euro bond refinancing in January 2026 to stabilize its capital structure. This transaction refinanced existing high-yield notes due in 2029 and 2031 to lower overall interest expenses. This refinancing is expected to reduce debt-servicing drags on earnings as the company consolidates its EU footprint.
4.
Corporate restructuring: United Group initiated a major leadership transition in June 2025 with Stan Miller taking over as chief executive officer. This transition followed the departure of founder Dragan Solak and former CEO Victoriya Boklag from executive roles. This management shift aligns with the group's strategy to sharpen focus on high-growth EU markets like Greece.

Broader Asset Sales

5.
Streamlined operations: United Group finalized agreements to divest its non-EU telecom assets for a combined enterprise value of one point five billion euros. The transaction included the sale of Serbia Broadband to e& PPF Telecom Group for 825 million euros. This shift is designed to maximize growth in core EU telecom markets like Greece.
6.
Targeted media sale: United Group signed a definitive agreement to sell its regional media assets under the Adria News Network umbrella to Alpac Capital. Serbian journalism sources estimate the final transaction pricing for these news assets at approximately 30 million euros. This sale helps insulate core telecom cash flows from volatile Balkan media operations.
7.
Contrary evidence: Public Power Corporation informed the investor community in February 2024 that it has not entered into any discussions or agreements to acquire Nova. This official statement directly contradicts market speculation that local energy giants are actively bidding for the telecom operator to expand their fiber networks. This lack of a major local bidder could dampen final transaction valuations for BC Partners.
8.
Bulgarian consolidation: United Group confirmed the sale of Nova TV Bulgaria in July 2026 after receiving competitive bids from multiple global media networks. US investment bank LionTree advised the company on the transaction, which held a target valuation exceeding 400 million euros. This divestment helps monetize highly valued regional media assets to support core network infrastructure.
9.
Strategic independence: United Group issued a statement in September 2025 denying any plans to sell its Belgrade-based N1 and Nova TV channels to state-controlled Telekom Srbija. The company emphasized that its news businesses operate under a strict separation between commercial management and editorial decisionmaking. This commercial firewall protects the brand's reputation and prevents reputational damage from undermining subscriber loyalty.

Transaction Hurdles

10.
Operational takeover: Alpac Capital owner Pedro Vargas Santos David was officially registered as the director of United Group's former Serbian media outlets in August 2026. This corporate registry update occurred amid executive removals of previous local directors at Adria News. This transition ensures that the new owner assumes full operational control ahead of final regulatory approvals.
11.
Complex valuation: NBG Securities reports that BC Partners' alternative plan involves standalone divestments of group constituents like Nova if a total group sale fails. This dual-track approach allows the private equity sponsor to maximize value but risks prolonging the transaction timetable. This extended process could delay projected capital distributions and weigh on competitor OTE's long-term wholesale pricing dynamics.
12.
Other side of the story: Dragan Solak and former CEO Victoriya Boklag filed a lawsuit in London's Commercial Court in May 2026 to block the sale of Southeast European media assets. They argue that the planned divestments breach shareholder agreements and destroy the distinctive value of converged services. This litigation could freeze the broader transaction process, preventing BC Partners from achieving its strategic exit from Nova.

Section 3 – The Founder's Lawsuit and Governance Disputes

Section summary
  1. The legal battle between United Group's minority founders and BC Partners highlights deep governance disputes that threaten to disrupt the ongoing telecom divestments.
  2. Dutch and British courts have become key battlegrounds over shareholder consent rights and corporate control within United Group.

Leadership Transition and Internal Revolt

1.
Unexpected removal: The abrupt departure of United Group's core leaders triggered widespread organizational shock in mid-2025. British sponsor BC Partners removed founder Dragan Solak and former chief executive Victoriya Boklag from their active operational roles in June 2025. This sudden governance restructuring led to the appointment of industry veteran Stan Miller as the new chief executive officer. This corporate shakeup represents a pivotal shift that ended the founder's executive control over the company's daily operations.
2.
Executive resistance: The parent company faced a major management revolt following the executive changes. Eight country-level chief executives from United Group's operating subsidiaries co-signed a protest letter in August 2025. These managers collectively represent businesses contributing approximately 90% of the group's total cash flow. The severe internal friction highlights how the leadership transition damaged executive cohesion across key European markets.
3.
Damaging allegations: Local executives warned that the aggressive management overhaul threatened the conglomerate's commercial stability. The co-signed protest letter addressed to the board of directors criticized the majority owners for a major governance breakdown. These subsidiary leaders argued that the forced removals risked causing long-term damage to the group's regional operations. This corporate conflict demonstrates that sponsor decisions alienated local managers who run the core business.

Jurisdictional Battle in Amsterdam

4.
Delayed intervention: The minority shareholder faced early procedural setbacks before the final Dutch court ruling. Solak attempted to fast-track his legal actions to secure an urgent court intervention in July 2025. The Amsterdam Enterprise Chamber rejected this request and denied his petition for immediate oversight. This early defeat demonstrated that courts resisted quick injunctive relief against the majority owners.
5.
Judicial defeat: The ousted founder failed to regain corporate influence through the Dutch legal system. The Amsterdam Enterprise Chamber dismissed an unfair dismissal lawsuit brought by Dragan Solak in October 2025. The Dutch court rejected his urgent petition to initiate an independent investigation into alleged governance abuses by the new leadership. This decisive legal setback isolated the minority shareholder from the company's decision-making processes.
6.
Jurisdictional dismissal: The Dutch judges determined that local courts had no legal authority over the parent entity. The court ruled the legal challenge inadmissible because the parent company is registered in Luxembourg. This critical jurisdictional limitation prevented the founder from securing an urgent judicial intervention to freeze management decisions. Consequently, the corporate sponsor successfully defended its management structure from external court interference.
7.
Corporate stabilization: The majority owner viewed the Dutch court ruling as a vindication of its corporate governance. United Group declared that the litigation was a manufactured distraction designed to advance the founder's personal goals. Greek business daily ProtoThema reported that the judicial victory signaled a stabilization of the group after months of internal turbulence. This legal outcome effectively reinforced the legitimacy of Miller's leadership during the asset sales.

Contractual Consent Rights and London Lawsuit

8.
Formal litigation: The legal conflict escalated significantly when the founders filed a new lawsuit in the United Kingdom. Dragan Solak and Victoriya Boklag launched legal proceedings in London's Commercial Court in May 2026. Their corporate entities, Gerrard Enterprises and Cable Management Company, filed the claim against three BC Partners units. This strategic move represents a direct legal challenge to the divestment of United Group's media assets.
9.
Contractual claims: The minority shareholders based their lawsuit on specific protections in the original corporate agreements. Solak and Boklag asserted that the shareholders' agreement grants them contractual consent rights over material business changes. They argued that BC Partners initiated major asset sales without their mandatory knowledge or approval. This contractual claim forms the legal basis to block transactions completed by the sponsor.
10.
Strategic transformation: The legal claim argues that selling the media arm would destroy the group's core business model. Solak and Boklag stated that disposing of United Media would transform the business into a telecoms-only operator. They claimed this divestment would destroy the distinctive value of their integrated telecoms-and-media services across Southeast Europe. The litigation threatens to undermine the commercial synergies that support Nova's bundled offerings.

Transaction Impacts and Counterarguments

11.
Disputed assets: The ongoing litigation focuses on a wide range of highly valued regional media outlets. The disputed portfolio includes Adria News Network and several major television stations across the Balkans. Solak and Boklag sought an injunction to prevent the sale of these news operations to Alpac Capital. This broad legal action aims to restrict the sponsor's liquidity options during its portfolio exit.
12.
Contrary evidence: Contrary evidence shows that United Group proceeded with its planned media divestments despite the active London lawsuit. The company agreed to sell Adria News Network to Alpac Capital on May 29, 2026. United Group dismissed the legal claims, stating that the company faces no restrictions preventing it from conducting its business in the best interests of shareholders. This successful transaction indicates that litigation has not paralyzed asset sales or stopped BC Partners' exit strategy.
13.
Broadened sales: The parent group continues to market other major media and telecom operations to international buyers. United Group is currently advising with LionTree on the sale of Bulgaria's Nova Broadcasting Group for over 400 million euros. US-based media firm Newsmax emerged as the frontrunner to acquire these Bulgarian assets in August 2026. These active negotiations show that investor appetite remains robust despite the ongoing governance disputes.
14.
Other side of the story: The persistent legal challenges by Dragan Solak could still delay or complicate the final exit of Nova in Greece. While BC Partners has pushed through smaller media transactions, a full sale of Nova or Vivacom involves much larger capital commitments. If the London court recognizes the founder's contractual consent rights, it could freeze the broader divestment processes indefinitely. This structural risk may depress Nova's eventual transaction price and delay OTE's projected wholesale market stabilization.

Section 4 – Nova's Fibre Network Strategy and Wholesale with OTE

Section summary
  1. Nova's independent fiber rollout and its commercial partnership with OTE will drive fiber migration and support high-margin broadband growth.
  2. The regulator's stop-sell on copper cabinet services forces customers onto high-capacity networks and expands OTE's wholesale receipts.

Infrastructure Footprint and Rollout

1.
Infrastructure disparity: Research across five relevant sources and five companies identified one key past precedent regarding Greek telecom infrastructure dynamics. This analysis utilizes all five companies, including OTE, Vodafone, Nova, Public Power Corporation, and Terna Fibre, and all five retrieved sources. Consequently, Nova must balance its independent infrastructure rollout against capital constraints while chasing OTE's dominant network reach across major urban markets.
2.
Customer contraction: The Greek broadband market maintains a highly concentrated three-player retail structure dominated by OTE. At the end of September 2025, Nova had one point zero seven million broadband customers, representing a seven percent year-over-year decline. In comparison, competitor Vodafone Greece reported zero point nine one million subscribers while OTE defended its market leadership with two point three six million accounts. This client loss pressures Nova to accelerate high-speed fiber migration to stabilize its retail telecom revenues.
3.
Geographic separation: The three established telecom providers in Greece continue to deploy their proprietary fiber networks without parallel overbuild. Industry metrics show that the commercial networks of OTE, Vodafone, and Nova remain highly contiguous but structurally non-overlapping. This rational rollout model ensures that Nova can earn appropriate infrastructure investment returns without experiencing destructive regional duplicate spending.
4.
Strategic interest: International telecom conglomerates are conducting active due diligence on Nova as BC Partners seeks an exit. Greek market reports indicate that Saudi Telecom Company, Etisalat, Warburg Pincus, and Searchlight Capital are exploring acquisition opportunities. A successful buyout by well-capitalized owners could infuse fresh capital into Nova to expand its proprietary fiber deployment.

Regulatory Wholesale Dynamics

5.
Wholesale agreement: Nova and Vodafone signed OTE's volume-based fiber wholesale pricing framework to reduce their access expenses. The regulatory scheme offers alternative operators up to twenty-eight percent unit discounts in exchange for specific volume commitments. This commercial arrangement helps OTE secure predictable long-term wholesale revenues while providing Nova with affordable national high-speed connectivity.
6.
Reciprocal access: Nova's parent United Group rejected Public Power Corporation's wholesale fiber prices as commercial terms were deemed unacceptable. Instead, Nova chose to wholesale OTE's fiber network under competitive bilateral agreements that protect capital expenditure. This cooperative wholesale approach makes it highly unlikely that Nova will underwrite Public Power Corporation's infrastructure in the near future.
7.
Wholesale uplift: OTE secures approximately ten percent higher wholesale revenues for every broadband client migrating to fiber. The commercial migration from older copper lines to fiber-to-the-home connections offsets OTE's broader wholesale revenue declines. Consequently, Nova's accelerated conversion of its subscriber base directly bolsters OTE's premium wholesale earnings over the medium term.
8.
Subsidy catalyst: The Greek government launched two digital voucher initiatives totaling one hundred and eighty million euros to accelerate fiber adoption. These state subsidies lower upfront connection costs for residential and small business subscribers seeking high-speed broadband. The increased voucher redemption allows Nova to transition low-value copper customers to high-speed fiber services.

Migration Dynamics and Market Threats

9.
Regulatory migration: The telecom authority approved a regulatory stop-sell on copper cabinet services where fiber infrastructure exists. This regulatory mandate prevents operators from marketing older copper-based broadband packages in fully-built fiber neighborhoods. This policy accelerates the commercial transition and reduces customer churn for OTE as subscribers migrate to stable lines.
10.
Contrary evidence: Nova's persistent negative operating free cash flow restricts its independent network construction capabilities. While OTE generated seven hundred and eighty-four million euros in cash, Nova recorded negative thirty-three million euros in fiscal year 2024. This severe financial shortfall indicates Nova cannot sustain aggressive standalone rollouts without substantial parent funding.
11.
Historical consolidation: Vodafone and Nova previously attempted a joint acquisition of alternative operator Forthnet to consolidate the fixed broadband market. That historical market attempt was suspended because macroeconomic instability prevented major corporate transactions. This past case suggests that macroeconomic shocks or corporate ownership transitions can delay strategic infrastructure integration among Greek operators.
12.
Other side of the story: While OTE's volume-based wholesale discounts secure near-term operator commitments, they risk accelerating competitor independence. If Nova successfully expands its network or merges with Vodafone, it will rapidly reduce its dependence on OTE's infrastructure. This structural transition would ultimately erode OTE's lucrative national wholesale revenues and pressure its long-term profit margins.

Section 5 – The Vodafone Greece and PPC Fibre Venture

Section summary
  1. The potential fifty-fifty joint venture between Vodafone Greece and Public Power Corporation will create Greece's second-largest wholesale fiber-to-the-home platform.
  2. We hold medium confidence in this strategic alliance because of concrete regulatory term sheets but notable diligence hurdles.
  3. We hold medium overall confidence in what the evidence shows regarding Nova's sale, the London lawsuit, and Greece's evolving fiber wholesale alliances.

Strategic Alliance Formation

1.
Collaborative steps: Public Power Corporation and Vodafone Greece signed a non-binding term sheet in June 2026 to explore a fifty-fifty joint venture. This potential partnership plans to merge Vodafone's Fiber2All with Public Power Corporation's FiberGrid alongside a cash consideration of approximately one hundred and thirty million euros paid to the utility. This transaction is designed to build a unified fiber-to-the-home network that will stabilize the local competitive landscape. Consequently, the alliance supports long-term earnings per share growth by optimizing capital expenditure.
2.
Expected milestones: Public Power Corporation and Vodafone Greece have targeted the first half of 2027 for the final closing of their proposed joint venture. The successful execution of this merger remains strictly subject to detailed due diligence, the signing of definitive agreements, and necessary regulatory approvals from competition authorities. Investors must monitor these milestones because any regulatory delays would directly extend OTE's wholesale infrastructure market dominance across metropolitan hubs.
3.
Optimized spending: Public Power Corporation aims to de-risk its capital expenditure through this strategic alliance while securing Vodafone Greece's extensive telecom expertise. The utility's business plan benefits because the merger preserves balance sheet capacity to fund other growth opportunities in southeastern Europe. By sharing rollout costs, the venture expects to improve project returns and accelerate fiber deployment without duplicating expensive parallel networks.
4.
Instructive precedents: Morgan Stanley notes that when challenger Digi entered the Portuguese market, incumbent operator Nos reported immediate competitive pressure within six months. In contrast, Public Power Corporation has retailed fiber for over six months in Greece without altering OTE's customer metrics. This difference indicates that Greek incumbents remain highly insulated unless the new joint venture develops convergent retail mobile products.

Infrastructure Scale and Open Access

5.
Expanded footprint: Vodafone Greece and Public Power Corporation report that their combined fiber operations currently cover more than one point six million homes. Within this footprint, Public Power Corporation's FiberGrid has reached one point one million homes ready for service, while Vodafone's network covers five hundred and fifty thousand households. This massive footprint will instantly establish the second largest wholesale platform in Greece.
6.
Open access: Vodafone Greece and Public Power Corporation plan for the joint venture to operate exclusively as a neutral wholesale provider. The new entity will offer its high-speed fiber services to all domestic telecom operators on a fully non-discriminatory basis. This neutral architecture aims to attract retail service providers who want to bypass OTE's dominant network. Consequently, this platform represents the first credible threat to OTE's wholesale revenues.
7.
Contrary evidence: Deutsche Bank reports that Public Power Corporation's net leverage has climbed from one point five times to three point two times over the last two point five years, constraining its independent funding capacity. Additionally, the utility lacks telecom experience, mobile services, and television packages, which restricts its ability to retail competitive bundles. These financial and commercial constraints suggest that the joint venture may struggle to gain retail market share against established incumbents.
8.
Resilient performance: Deutsche Bank highlights that OTE continues to experience strong demand for its fiber wholesale products despite Public Power Corporation's aggressive retail discounts. OTE's national wholesale segment remains protected because main rivals Vodafone and Nova have already signed long-term volume agreements with the incumbent. These bilateral agreements secure stable, high margin national wholesale revenue for OTE.

Competitor and Bidder Responses

9.
Strategic rejection: Deutsche Bank reports that United Group rejected Public Power Corporation's standalone wholesale fiber prices because the commercial terms were deemed unacceptable for Nova. Nova instead committed to wholesaling OTE's fiber network under competitive bilateral agreements that protect its operating cash flow. This decision shows that alternative operators are unlikely to finance rival networks unless pricing becomes highly discounted.
10.
Defensive positioning: OTE has minimized customer churn by focusing its promotional efforts on re-contracting existing subscribers in areas targeted by Public Power Corporation. OTE Chief Executive Kostas Nebis stated during the fourth quarter of 2025 earnings call that the company has not felt any material pressure on its operating numbers. This robust defense prevents aggressive competitor discounts from eroding OTE's core fixed-line retail revenue.
11.
Strategic focus: Deutsche Bank reports that Public Power Corporation confirmed it will not participate in the upcoming auction to renew spectrum licenses in the nine hundred and eighteen hundred megahertz bands. The utility's chief executive officer stated that the company is entirely uninterested in retail mobile services in Greece. This operational limit ensures that the joint venture remains a wholesale-only player rather than directly challenging incumbent mobile operators.
12.
Evolving outlook: The creation of the Vodafone and Public Power Corporation alliance will restructure Greek wholesale markets but will not immediately damage OTE's bottom line. OTE's financial performance remains insulated because the incumbent's dominant retail bundlings and premium customer service prevent material subscriber losses. Consequently, we conclude that OTE's projected earnings per share growth remains highly secure over the next three years.
13.
Other side of the story: The joint venture between Vodafone Greece and Public Power Corporation could eventually undermine OTE's long-term pricing power if it successfully consolidates alternative operator traffic. If the new platform scales rapidly, alternative operators may choose to migrate their core broadband connections off OTE's network. This transition would inevitably accelerate OTE's wholesale revenue erosion and pressure its future profitability.
