Spanish Political Platforms on Defense

1.
Supportive transition: The Spanish Socialist Workers Party led by Pedro Sánchez has quietly escalated the national defense budget from 11,172 million euros in 2018 to 35,419 million euros in 2026. This historic increase has allowed Spain to reach the 2% GDP defense target demanded by NATO allies. However, Sánchez has firmly rejected the new NATO target of 5% of GDP, pledging to prioritize social welfare over further military expansion. This policy suggests near-term continuity in defense funding but limits potential upside for prime contractors. Ambitious expansion: The conservative People's Party led by Alberto Núñez Feijóo proposes a much more aggressive military budget. Feijóo has publicly advocated for defense spending levels exceeding 3% of Spain's GDP by 2030. This target would require approximately 63 billion euros in annual spending, representing a 50% increase over current market expectations. The People's Party plans to fund this expansion by reallocating 40 billion euros of unproductive public expenditure. Such an increase would significantly accelerate long-term earnings for domestic defense suppliers. Critical opposition: Left-wing coalition partners such as Sumar and Podemos are deeply opposed to any further escalation of military spending. Podemos spokesperson Ione Belarra has strongly criticized the government's covert rearmament, pointing out that military spending has risen without parliamentary votes. These deep political divisions create significant risks of budget gridlock if the left retains influence after the election.
