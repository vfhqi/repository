Approximately 85% of European buildings were constructed before 2000, and more than 75% exhibit poor energy performance, driving long-term capital expenditure requirements for building systems. Industrial specialist Schindler reported 16.2% local currency growth in European modernization orders during the first half of 2026, confirming that regulatory enforcement is translating into orders, while competitor KONE delivered over 15% growth in modernization orders in the second quarter of 2026. US peer Otis recorded 24% organic growth in modernization sales and a 26% backlog expansion in the second quarter of 2026, demonstrating how modernization backlogs are successfully converting into revenue.

The structural electromechanical lock transition drives significant revenue multiplication through higher average selling prices and accelerated replacement lifecycles, with rising software attach and SaaS deployment enhancing recurring revenue mixes and expanding gross margin profiles across leading access control portfolios. Electromechanical locks achieved an 8-9% organic sales CAGR over a 12-year cycle, outperforming legacy mechanical lock sets, and represent 3.1x the revenue of mechanical alternatives over 15 years because they have a 40-70% higher average selling price and a much shorter 7-8 year lifecycle.

This structural growth opportunity remains in its early stages, as Assa Abloy management estimates that global electromechanical penetration stands at only 10% in non-residential markets, indicating several decades of structural growth tailwinds.

The landmark combination of KONE and TK Elevator creates unprecedented market concentration while Assa Abloy and dormakaba aggressively consolidate the fragmented access control market. This transformative merger allows KONE to jump from the number three global position to leapfrog industry leader Otis in terms of service base density and revenue, with KONE management targeting seven hundred million euros in annual runrate cost synergies by the end of year three post-completion, which underpins a path to higher margins.

Meanwhile, Assa Abloy continues its aggressive programmatic acquisition playbook by completing twenty-three bolt-on acquisitions in 2025 alone, and dormakaba achieved a record sixteen percent EBITDA margin under the leadership of Till Reuter by successfully executing the Shape4Growth program.

However, material second and third-order risks threaten the sustainability of these growth narratives. Proptech developers are applying open artificial intelligence standards to simplify predictive telemetry, allowing clients to bypass expensive proprietary software subscriptions, while software-first start-ups introduce direct mobile credentials that challenge traditional hardware specifications and disintermediate entrenched lock manufacturer relationships. Standardized third-party component suppliers enable independent operators to service advanced systems, eroding proprietary OEM pricing and accelerating structural maintenance margin decay.
