Section 16 – Conclusion

Solid State plc's FY26 earnings release materially strengthened the medium-term investment thesis by demonstrating that structural defence tailwinds and operational leverage can drive sustainable margin expansion, yet the single most important change to the investment case is the validation that management can execute multi-year earnings growth without relying on transformational M&A. Chief Executive John Macmichael's reaffirmation of the 2030 target of 20p adjusted diluted earnings per share, combined with FY26 delivery of 11.0p adjusted EPS, proves the group is tracking on schedule toward its long-term earnings ambition.

The 60.0% expansion in adjusted operating profit to £9.6m on 23.2% revenue growth to £154.1m demonstrates that operating leverage inherent in value-added manufacturing drives exponential profit growth when volumes recover. This operational gearing, combined with gross margin expansion by 200 basis points to 33.5% driven by higher-value design-in content and proprietary own-brand components like Durakool contactors, establishes a credible foundation for the group to restore operating margins toward historical double-digit benchmarks of 9.2% over the next 18 to 36 months.

The strongest counter-hypothesis to the bull case is that Solid State's apparent margin recovery and revenue acceleration in FY26 reflect periodic contract lumpiness rather than sustainable structural improvement. The £23.3m defence communications contract that drove constant currency organic growth of 25.3% creates a demanding top-line comparator for FY27, meaning underlying organic growth outside this single order was far more modest.

Broad industrial and European commercial electronics markets remained depressed due to customer inventory destocking, and subdued general industrial purchasing delays top-line recovery in traditional component distribution channels. Furthermore, delays in publishing the UK Defence Investment Plan created near-term procurement uncertainty, and government administrative friction risks pushing order book conversion out into future fiscal periods.

If the non-repeating £23.3m NATO order is not replaced by a combination of Project CAIN follow-ons, US Power scaling, and industrial component recovery, the group could face material earnings pressure in FY27 and FY28, potentially stalling progress toward the 2030 target and exposing the equity to significant downside re-rating risk.

The initial $10.8m Project CAIN order for British Army battlefield communications delivered in Q1 FY27 creates multi-year follow-on opportunities for spiral hardware upgrades and through-life support, and securing secondary procurement phases represents the primary catalyst for sustained defence revenue growth.
