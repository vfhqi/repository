Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Coca-Cola HBC faces highly concentrated risks from its Russian business and its major African M&A transaction.
  2. The resolution of these drivers will dictate the group's forward credit rating, cash repatriation, and consolidated EPS growth.

The Core Question

1.
The Key Question: "how concentrated or diffuse are the drivers that separate the next 18 months to four years' financials from the last four to six years, and how wide and how skewed is the range of plausible outcomes over the next 18 months?"

Financial Mechanics of EPS

2.
Critical exposure: Coca-Cola HBC faces high concentration risks from its Russian operations which historically contributed a significant portion of profits. Jefferies reported on November 11, 2026, that a full loss of the Russian business would cut operating profit by approximately 21% and earnings per share by 26% in fiscal year 2027. This earnings sensitivity represents a major risk to the company's forward financial trajectory. Therefore, the concentration of these drivers directly determines the stability of the company's forward EPS.
3.
Structural shifts: The consolidation of Coca-Cola Beverages Africa on November 27, 2026, is expected to reshape Coca-Cola HBC's geographic and financial mix. Deutsche Bank noted on October 27, 2026, that this transaction should yield more than 5% EPS accretion in the first full year. This expansion helps diversify the group's profit streams away from highly volatile regions. Ultimately, the speed of African margin recovery will dictate whether this deal successfully offsets other regional headwinds.
4.
Valuation divergence: Coca-Cola HBC's valuation multiple remains heavily depressed due to persistent geopolitical overhangs from Russia. Bernstein Research highlighted on November 6, 2026, that the market has historically applied a substantial discount to the company's Russian earnings. Resolving the ownership uncertainty could unlock a significant re-rating of the stock toward European consumer staples peers. However, a sudden asset seizure would permanently cement a lower valuation base for the consolidated entity.

Risk Profile and Debt Ratings

5.
Leverage constraints: Debt-funded acquisitions threaten to stretch Coca-Cola HBC's balance sheet just as cash repatriation from key markets remains restricted. Jefferies estimated on November 5, 2026, that consolidated net debt to EBITDA will rise to 1.6x in 2027 with the Africa transaction. If the company completely loses access to its Russian cash, leverage could escalate to 2.7x EBITDA. This elevation would push the company well above its medium-term target range of 1.5x to 2.0x.
6.
Contrary evidence: Despite these mounting balance sheet pressures, Coca-Cola HBC has managed to preserve its investment-grade status. Standard & Poor's reaffirmed the group's solid BBB+ credit rating with a stable outlook in October 2025. This rating indicates that the underlying business remains structurally resilient to localized shocks. However, any severe rating downgrade would immediately escalate refinancing costs on its newly issued debt.
7.
Restricted funds: Coca-Cola HBC has accumulated a massive cash balance in Russia that cannot be easily utilized to service global debt or pay dividends. Company filings on August 5, 2026, revealed that cash held in Russia exceeded €1 billion. Sanctions and local capital controls prevent the repatriation of these profits to the parent company. Consequently, the group must rely on other regional operations to maintain its progressive dividend policy.
8.
Other side of the story: While some investors fear that localized disruptions will derail Coca-Cola HBC, the broader business model possesses multiple defensive levers. Company reports on November 4, 2026, showed robust organic volume growth of 5.4% driven by strong execution in established European markets. This underlying momentum suggests that core operations can absorb geopolitical volatility without experiencing severe consolidated EPS decay. Therefore, the risk of a catastrophic earnings collapse may be overemphasized by short-term market participants.

Section 2 – The Concentration of Earnings Drivers: Russia and Africa M&A

Section summary
  1. Coca-Cola HBC has highly concentrated earnings drivers centered on Russian profit retention and African acquisition execution.
  2. The consolidation of African operations offsets Russian geopolitical risks but introduces immediate margin dilution and integration hurdles.
  3. Confidence is high that Russian asset seizure and African margin recovery are the primary drivers of Coca-Cola HBC earnings variance, based on detailed broker scenario analyses.

Russian Earnings Concentration

1.
Significant concentration: Coca-Cola HBC's earnings remain heavily dependent on its stand-alone Russian operations despite ongoing geopolitical uncertainty. Bernstein Research reported on November 6, 2026, that Russia accounts for about 13% of group revenue and a low-to-mid-20s percentage of operating profit. This high concentration means any disruption in Russia would significantly restrict the company's ability to achieve its consolidated medium-term growth targets.
2.
Severe disruption: Coca-Cola HBC faces a massive potential earnings decline if its Russian assets are fully expropriated. Jefferies reported on November 11, 2026, that a complete loss of Russia cuts FY2027 operating profit by 21% and earnings per share by 26%. This projected impairment includes the loss of interest income on local cash balances that are currently consolidated. Consequently, the group's overall earnings growth remains highly vulnerable to unilateral regulatory actions within the Russian market.
3.
Standalone structure: Coca-Cola HBC's Russian operations are managed as a self-sufficient entity that does not rely on group funding. Jefferies highlighted on October 1, 2026, that the local business now sells regional brands like Dobry Cola after losing the main Coca-Cola franchise in 2022. This operational separation helps insulate the wider group from immediate day-to-day business disruptions if ownership changes. However, the consolidated financials would still suffer a severe accounting blow upon deconsolidation.
4.
Extensive research: Research analyzed 32 relevant documents across four source types to examine these concentrated drivers. These sources covered 8 companies including Coca-Cola HBC and Coca-Cola Europacific Partners along with 2 comparable past cases of beverage mergers. This comprehensive dataset allows for a thorough evaluation of post-acquisition margins and geopolitical concentration risks.

African M&A Transaction Mechanics

5.
Substantial consolidation: Coca-Cola HBC completed the transformational acquisition of 75% of Coca-Cola Beverages Africa on November 27, 2026. The company announced in its Johannesburg Stock Exchange prelisting document on November 16, 2026, that it paid approximately US$1.3 billion in cash to The Coca-Cola Company. It also acquired Gutsche Family Investments' stake for US$308 million in cash and 21.03 million consideration shares. This large equity issue represents 5.47% of the group's enlarged share capital.
6.
Future ownership: Coca-Cola HBC has structured a clear path to full ownership of the African bottling business over the next six years. The group's pre-listing document on November 16, 2026, detailed a five-year call option to purchase the remaining 25% equity interest from The Coca-Cola Company. This option is paired with a put option exercisable by the seller between three and a half and six years post-completion. These options provide a structured mechanism for the group to consolidate its long-term African footprint.
7.
Scheduled pricing: Coca-Cola HBC has agreed to specific financial parameters for acquiring the remaining African minority interest. The company's prospectus on March 25, 2026, noted that the option price is based on the original purchase price per share plus an applicable coupon. This coupon is set at 2.75% per annum for the first year post-completion, with capitalised annual interest thereafter. This mechanism limits the risk of unexpected pricing surges when consolidating the remaining minority stake.
8.
Promising expansion: Coca- Cola HBC expects the African business to drive significant top-line growth despite lower initial operating margins. Morgan Stanley reported on October 22, 2025, that there is clear scope for synergies and earnings normalisation because the African EBITDA margin was 3.5 percentage points below 2019 levels. This gap represents a major operational recovery opportunity as the group integrates its digital capabilities. Consequently, margin recovery remains a key driver for medium-term earnings accretion.
9.
Topline focus: Coca-Cola HBC management has emphasized volume growth over immediate cost synergies for the African transaction. The company noted during an analyst meeting on November 4, 2025, that the acquisition is premised on top-line opportunity rather than rapid cost-cutting. However, management plans to optimize supply chains and digital logistics to drive gradual efficiency gains over time. This balanced strategy suggests that the financial returns will be a slower burn than typical cost-out mergers.
10.
Diluted exposure: Coca-Cola HBC's massive African expansion structurally reduces its concentration risk to the Russian market. Morgan Stanley reported on October 22, 2025, that the consolidated group will see Russia diluted to 10% of volume from 14% previously. Meanwhile, South Africa will emerge as the largest single market, representing 16% of total volumes. This geographic rebalancing significantly improves the group's defensive profile against localized geopolitical shocks.

This financial leverage, combined with the cancellation of the share buyback program, creates an immediate headwind for earnings per share growth. Defensive profile: Coca-Cola HBC appears well-positioned to navigate these concentrated drivers due to its extensive execution playbook. This accretion is supported by strong underlying momentum in other emerging markets like Egypt and Nigeria. Other side of the story: While bulls focus on the strategic diversification of the African transaction, the massive concentration of profits in Russia remains an unresolved risk. Section summary
  1. Coca-Cola HBC faces a significant increase in refinancing costs that exceeds most European beverage peers.
  2. Large debt-funded acquisitions across the bottling sector historically trigger extended deleveraging cycles and near-term earnings drag.

Refinancing and Interest Rate Risks

1.
Escalating charges: Coca-Cola HBC faces a potential step-up in financing expenses following its major African consolidation. Jefferies reported in an update on October 1, 2026, that CCH issued new bonds to fund the CCBA transaction, creating a long-term interest headwind. While a previous Jefferies report from October 15, 2026, modeled FY2027 interest of €180 million against a €102 million consensus, CCH’s strong cash generation could mitigate this risk. This financing pressure will ultimately drag on forward earnings per share unless cash flows exceed expectations.
2.
Unique exposure: Coca-Cola HBC stands out among European beverage companies for its projected interest rate increases. Deutsche Bank reported on October 27, 2026, that CCH is the only European beverage peer expected to see a meaningful finance-charge increase in the FY2027-29 period. This unique trend is driven by CCH’s heavy concentration of upcoming debt maturities. Consequently, CCH faces a meaningful financecharge increase that will constrain forward EPS growth compared to its sector peers.
3.
High concentration: Coca-Cola HBC’s balance sheet is highly vulnerable to higher interest rates due to its maturity profile. Deutsche Bank estimates on October 27, 2026, showed that CCH requires gross refinancing of 50% of its debt by December 2028. This represents the highest refinancing requirement in the European beverage sector, well ahead of Carlsberg at 18% and Heineken at 29%. This high concentration of maturities exposes the company to high refinancing costs that will pressure future earnings.
4.
Contrary evidence: Coca-Cola HBC’s underlying interest costs may remain lower than feared due to improved operational cash generation. Jefferies reported on October 1, 2026, that CCH lowered its FY2026 net finance cost guidance to €40-50 million, down from the prior €45-65 million range. Section 5 – Precedents of Russian Asset Seizures and State Temporary Management

Section summary
  1. Regulatory risks in Russia represent a severe capital threat to western consumer firms operating under temporary state administration decrees.
  2. Historical exits by beverage peers demonstrate that temporary management transfers trigger complete asset write-downs and massive translation reserve recycling.
  3. Confidence is high that a potential seizure of Coca-Cola HBC's Multon operations would impair its forward group EBIT by over one-fifth, based on legal precedents and broker impact assessments.

Seizure Timing and Mechanics

1.
Abrupt transition: Carlsberg experienced a rapid loss of operational control after attempting a coordinated exit from its Russian operations. The company announced a conditional agreement to sell its Baltika Breweries subsidiary on June 23, 2023. However, the Russian government issued Presidential Decree 302 on July 16, 2023, transferring management of the assets to a federal agency within twenty-three days of the sale announcement. This unexpected move forced the brewer to fully write down the asset value to zero because it could no longer influence operational decisions.
2.
Parallel seizure: Danone faced an identical state intervention when executing its own structured exit plan. The dairy group originally announced its intention to transfer control of its Russian business in October 2022. Yet, the Russian government unilaterally intervened on July 16, 2023, by placing Danone Russia under the temporary external administration of the Rosimushchestvo agency. This action was immediately followed by unauthorized board changes on July 18, 2023, which forced the group to completely deconsolidate its Russian operations that month.
3.
Retaliatory triggers: State authorities utilize a very broad legal scope to seize foreign corporate assets under Decree 302. Deutsche Bank Research reported on July 17, 2023, that the Russian state took temporary charge of Danone Russia JSC after the company tried to transfer control. The decree targets assets from countries deemed unfriendly in response to international sanctions or perceived threats of foreign asset freezes. These state interventions show that orderly divestments remain highly uncertain once local authorities initiate asset management transfers.
