[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = C

Thesis. Halma is an oversold-compounder case, not a change case: a serial acquirer of life-safety, environmental and healthcare niches with 23 consecutive years of record adjusted profit, whose shares at 3,464 pence sit in the bottom decile of their own ten-year multiple range because a fifth of the group, a contract manufacturer with one hyperscaler customer, is losing its claim on that customer's growth. The core four-fifths is upgrading margins and compounding through a record year of acquisitions, and that is not yet in the price; but on our modal path the return from here is about 12 percent a year, so the case is compelling only on the branch where the Avo Photonics discount clears, which we put at possible odds.

  • The change that caused the fall is real, and it is external.
    • Avo Photonics, the contract manufacturer of optical circuit switches for one hyperscaler customer believed to be Google, grew to about a fifth of group revenue
    • Its growth is guided to halve from 52 percent in FY26 to 30 percent in FY27, and the customer is now routing its incremental volume to merchant suppliers who own their intellectual property.
    • Lumentum guides to its first triple-digit-million optical-circuit-switch revenue quarter, has a multi-year, multi-billion-dollar agreement fulfilled from the second half of 2026, and expects to become the customer's largest supplier in early 2027.
    • The sell-side expects Avo's share of the customer's volume to fall from FY28
      • One broker downgraded on exactly that in September 2026.
    • The customer's own spending is rising, not falling: Alphabet raised 2026 capital expenditure guidance to 195 to 205 billion USD and expects a significant further increase in 2027.
      • The question is Avo's share of a growing pie, not a shrinking one.
  • The change the company has made in the meantime is also real, and it is internal.
    • On 24 September 2026 Halma raised FY27 adjusted EBIT margin guidance to 23.5 to 24.0 percent from about 22.7 percent, after a record 515 GBPm of acquisitions in the year to date at about 12x EBIT and the first run of disposals in years.
    • Slightly more than half of the roughly 100 basis points is operational execution
      • The rest is margin-accretive deals and disposals.
      • On a 23 percent base the raise is worth about 4 percent of EBIT.
    • The core four-fifths grows 6 to 7 percent organically, which is its ten-year average
      • It is not a false friend hidden behind photonics.
  • The margin and acquisition change has travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain; the Avo change has not yet reached Halma's own numbers.
    • Active portfolio management (a choice) became deals and disposals (actions), lifted the margin and return on total invested capital to 16.2 percent (key metrics), will show in FY27 EPS of about 133 pence, up about 17 percent (financial statements), and has moved the sell-side's estimates up 3 to 8 percent over one to six months.
    • The Avo shift has travelled from the customer's choices into Avo's guidance, which is why the debate is about FY28 and nothing in FY27 settles it.
  • The price already assumes the concern and not the recovery.
    • At 3,464 pence the shares trade at about 22.5x 24-month-forward earnings, the 5th percentile of the stock's five-year range and below the ten-year 'crash' band of 23.50x
    • The sell-side's sum-of-the-parts values Avo at 10.3 to 13.0x EV/EBITA against about 22x for the core, that is, a business expected to stagnate.
    • The Nordic serial acquirers the business most resembles (Diploma, Lifco, Addtech, Lagercrantz) hold 26 to 28x at the 52nd to 84th percentiles of their own histories
      • The de-rating is Halma's alone, so the re-rating is ours to earn on company evidence.
    • Consensus still models margins falling back to 23.0 percent in FY28, below the new guidance
      • The margin step-up holding and the record acquisition run-rate are the two things not priced.
  • The chain holds to the outputs and breaks at the return.
    • Earnings growth of about 11.7 percent a year to FY29 on our path is just under the framework's 12 to 20 percent band
    • The yield is 0.7 percent
    • And the re-rating, the swing factor, depends on the Avo discount clearing, which we put at 30 percent odds within eighteen months and even odds within three years.
    • Blending the cleared branch at the ten-year median multiple of 26.7x with the uncleared branch at today's 22.5x gives about 12.5 percent a year over eighteen months and about 12.1 percent a year over three years, a D against the 20 percent bar.
    • On the cleared branch alone the eighteen-month return is about 21 percent a year, a B
      • On the uncleared branch about 8 percent a year, an F.
      • The whole case lives in that spread.
  • The pattern is 'OSHQC CfC Clearing', and it is half-crisp.
    • A high-quality compounder, oversold to the bottom decile of its own history, carries one named concern confined to a fifth of the group
    • But the concern is not clearly transient and there is no hard evidence it is clearing within six to twelve months.
    • What is clearing is the debate's attention, pulled back to the core by the margin upgrade.

  • The case therefore is a good business at a price that pays for the concern but not yet for waiting.

    • We would progress at about 3,150 pence, where the modal path clears 20 percent a year at eighteen months, or on hard evidence on Avo
    • Neither is in hand today.

  • Rating = C, because the input change forces (B) and the financial outputs (B) are real and near-term strong, but the 'Modal return' they drive from 3,464 pence is about 12 percent a year at both horizons (D), below the 20 percent bar

    • Materiality is measured by whether the change drives a sufficient return, and on the modal path it does not, so the Building Block sits below two of its three Elements by judgement.
    • Not B, because a B needs the cleared branch on the Avo concern at better than even odds within eighteen months, and we have it at about 30 percent.
    • Not D, because the near-term outputs are very positive, upgrades are likely, and the price already carries the concern.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • In its strongest form: Halma's two-year acceleration was a single customer's capital cycle passing through a contract manufacturer, and that customer has now decided to buy its optical switches from merchants who own their intellectual property and their scale.

    • Avo's revenue, about 20 percent of the group and about half of Environmental & Analysis, flattens in FY28 and declines from FY29 as Lumentum takes first the incremental and then the base volume

      • Consensus, which still models 30 to 35 percent photonics growth for FY27 and only a gentle fade, is 5 to 10 percent too high on FY28 and FY29 EPS
      • The margin upgrade is partly a temporary mix effect that reverses as Avo's volumes and the healthcare rebound normalise
      • The serial-acquirer arbitrage is narrowing as rates stay at 5 to 6 percent and multiples paid drift to 12 to 16x
      • And so the multiple, far from recovering to 26.7x, continues toward the sector's 16 to 20x as a premium that peaked at 106 percent against a 70 percent average keeps normalising.

    • The evidence for it is the strongest in this record.

      • Lumentum's agreement and guidance
      • Silex's disclosure that Google is 24 percent of its sales
      • The September 2026 downgrade and FY28 share-loss call
      • Expert warnings from engineers at Microsoft and AWS that hyperscalers internalise manufacturing recipes and that co-packaged optics caps a contract manufacturer's long-term upside
      • The research's own count that Avo grew at an average of 50 percent a year for three years driven entirely by one client
      • The 12.1x average deal multiple
      • And a return on total invested capital that drifted from 16.3 percent in 2015 to 14.4 percent in 2024 before the FY26 recovery.
    • On the return it would take the case to an F.
      • Avo's revenue of about 500 GBPm declining 20 percent in FY29 would take about 4 points off group revenue growth that year and, at group margins, about 5 percent off FY29 EPS to about 151 pence
      • At the uncleared 22.5x the three-year return would be about 7 percent a year, and at the five-year low of 20.2x about 4 percent.
    • What we concede.
      • The share-loss risk is real and not yet in consensus.
      • We cannot see inside the relationship, and nor can the sell-side.
      • Our modal path already assumes Avo flattens, so the competing hypothesis is a matter of degree against our own base case rather than a different world.
      • The margin step-up has a temporary component.
      • And the fair multiple with Avo retained and discounted is about 24.0x, not 26.7x, which is why we blend rather than apply the median.
    • Why we still hold our view, as far as it goes.
      • The core is not a false friend: it grew 6 percent organically in FY26 and is guided at 6 to 7 percent, its long-run rate, with margins rising.
      • The customer's capital spending is rising, so the question is share of growth, not survival.
      • Avo's binding constraint in FY27 is its own labour and cleanroom capacity, which is not what a business losing its customer looks like.
      • A decade of co-design and a one-to-one attach to the customer's own TPU servers argue for a retained, if smaller, role.
      • And the stock already trades as if Avo were worth 10 to 13x and the core 22x, so the competing hypothesis is largely in the price while the margin upgrade and the acquisition run-rate are not.
    • We make no claim that the core alone covers the enterprise value, and we show why.
      • FY27 estimated adjusted net income of about 501 GBPm, of which about four-fifths, 401 GBPm, is core, is worth about 2.9 billion GBP at the comparison set's lowest P/E of 7.2x, about 6.4 billion GBP at the set's centre near 16x, and about 10.7 billion GBP at the stock's own ten-year median of 26.7x, against a market capitalisation of about 13.2 billion GBP at 3,464 pence on 380.5m diluted shares.
      • The core does not cover the market value at any multiple below about 33x on that line, so the downside is not bounded by the core and we do not argue that it is.
    • Where the competing hypothesis would win outright, and where it would lose.
      • It wins on a FY28 decline at Avo or a transaction foreclosed by the customer
      • It loses on a second hyperscaler customer or a transaction done.
      • The first check is the H1 FY27 results on 19 November 2026, which will show the margin and the 30 percent photonics guide but not the FY28 share question.
Change Forces: are the input change forces sufficiently strong? (E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because the internal forces meet the screen's decision rule (guidance raised materially, a record step-up in acquisitions, a new habit of portfolio pruning, with execution standards rising) and have already propagated into layer 5 outputs, while the external forces are mixed: strong regulatory and hyperscaler-capital tailwinds set against a specific, strengthening competitive headwind on the business that supplied about half of FY26's organic growth.
  • Internal, then external: the internal forces are of one kind, the established model run harder under a chief executive in his fourth year
    • The external force that matters most, the hyperscaler customer's supplier diversification, is turning against the company on a fifth of revenue.
    • The two Required Attributes therefore pull in opposite directions, and the Element's rating is the balance, not the average.
  • Not A, because the external force acting on a fifth of revenue is turning against the company and there is no leadership-level change at the top.
  • The internal forces are what make the near-term outputs very positive
    • The external headwind at Avo is why the mid-term growth rate falls and why the multiple is where it is.
    • Both facts carry through every later Element.
External forces: are there sufficiently strong external forces acting on the company, new or intensifying, and in which direction? (RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because the forces are strong but pull in opposite directions: hyperscaler capital spending and safety, water and healthcare regulation are tailwinds, while the hyperscaler's shift to merchant optical-switch suppliers is a new, intensifying headwind on the business that supplied about half of FY26's organic growth.
  • Direction, force by force: demand, a tailwind at the group but contested at Avo
    • Competition, a headwind at Avo
    • Disruption, a headwind at Avo and an opportunity in water digitisation

    • Supply side, a mild tailwind
    • The company's own cycle, a tailwind that is fading
    • Macroeconomic, a mild headwind (Sterling, and interest rates on the acquisition model).
  • Where the balance falls: the tailwinds are the long-standing base rate of Halma's markets
    • The one new force is the headwind.
    • A Required Attribute that asks for new or intensifying forces, and finds the intensifying one adverse, cannot be above C.

  • Lumentum: a multi-year, multi-billion-dollar optical-circuit-switch agreement fulfilled from the second half of 2026

  • Coherent: more than ten active optical-circuit-switch customer engagements and a 2 billion USD addressable market
    • One broker sees the optical-circuit-switch market at 1.5 billion USD by 2029.
  • Core competition: fragmented small private niche players with benign behaviour
    • Spectris and AMETEK report no direct overlap or share friction with Halma.
    • The core is not where intensity is tightening.
  • Not C, because the research is explicit that the incremental volume is going elsewhere and consensus does not yet reflect it.
  • This is the one force that, if it runs its course, makes the competing hypothesis right
    • It sets the ceiling on every rating above it.

Disruption: is a disruption force strengthening or being disproved? (KQ 3, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because the disruption forces on Avo are strengthening (standardisation of optical-circuit-switch designs, co-packaged optics, hyperscaler internalisation of manufacturing recipes) while the disruption in water and healthcare is an opportunity Halma is buying into.
  • Expert evidence against Avo: a Microsoft principal design engineer on blueprint internalisation as a massive threat to contract manufacturers like Avo
    • An AWS manager on co-packaged optics limiting Avo's long-term upside
    • Fabrinet expanding into early-stage research and development to move designs to low-cost Asian factories.
  • The counter, and its date: in June 2026 experts said co-packaged optics was too expensive for now, so Avo's standard optical box would stay dominant for years.
    • The later evidence governs on direction
    • The earlier evidence governs on timing, which is probably years rather than quarters.
  • Water: AMP8 is accelerating data-led real-time monitoring, and Halma bought Pyxis for exactly that.
