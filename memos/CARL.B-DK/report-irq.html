Why these five. The memo's case is that Carlsberg compounds EPS at about 8% a year on 3% revenue and 5% EBIT growth, and that the return beyond that depends on a multiple the sector half decides. Five things decide whether that holds and when to add: whether price keeps ahead of cost (the widest swing in FY2027 EBIT and the main engine after 2027); whether China is a cycle or a share loss (the largest share-price swing); whether there is any self-help after 2027 (the fade nobody quantifies); when and how big the buyback is (the one dated positive no consensus model carries, and the hinge of the memo's call that the entry is better after the spring); and what reaches EPS below EBIT (where consensus is right in level but wrong in composition, and where Peter Kondrup's treasury role makes him the best person to ask).

Order of play for the hour. Driver 4 first (his treasury ground, and it settles the timing of adding), then the guidance questions of the general pattern (6.1, 6.2), Driver 2 (China), Driver 1 (price against cost), Driver 5 (below EBIT), Driver 3 (after 2027) and the rest of the general pattern. If time runs short, ask only the ★ questions in that order.

Driver 1. Price against cost in 2027, and whether Carlsberg has real pricing power

Why it matters: a 1% price move is worth about 6% of EBIT; each 0.5 point of price and mix is about DKK 300m to 450m of EBIT (2% to 3%). The 2027 cost round is about 160bp of gross margin with input inflation near 5.4%, and the policy is to cover more than 80% of it in money. Carlsberg leads in only 24% of its volume and is a challenger in 66%, and in the United Kingdom and Poland competitors have turned to price. From FY2028, once most of the synergies and Kazakhstan are in, price over cost is the main engine (the only one from 2029): it is DKK 7.0 of the DKK 15.6 EPS rise from FY2025 to FY2028 in our bridge. If revenue per hectolitre rises only 1.5% against inflation above 5%, FY2027 EPS is about DKK 68.5 (3.4% below our base); if price holds at 3%, about DKK 72.9.

1.1 ★ "What input-cost inflation per hectolitre do you face in 2027 after hedges, and how much of aluminium, glass, energy and malt is already locked?"

1.2 ★ "How much price do you need to cover that in money, and in which markets is it already agreed with the retailers?"

1.3 ★ "Where are you the price leader and where do you follow? In the United Kingdom and Poland, where competitors have spent duty savings on price or discounted, do you follow, or give up volume to protect margin?"

1.4 "If price runs ahead of cost, does the surplus go to margin, or back into marketing towards your 8.5% to 9% framework?"

1.5 "What should we watch in the first-quarter statement in April to know whether 2027 pricing has landed: revenue per hectolitre by region?"

How to read the answers: costs largely locked, price agreed early and covering inflation in money = our base and the "price over cost" engine intact; "inflation higher than last year's view" or "we will protect volume" = the downside branch, and FY2027 nearer DKK 68.5; a clear answer on where Carlsberg leads and follows tells you whether pricing power is real or nominal, which is the foundation of the multiple.

Driver 2. China: a cycle or a share loss

Why it matters: China is about 14% of revenue and roughly a fifth of EBIT (about DKK 2.7bn to 2.9bn), two-thirds of it earned in the second and third quarters. China volume fell 6% in the second quarter and about 10% in the third (Chongqing's own third quarter: (9.6)%), while Heineken grew double digits through China Resources Beer; on-premise's share of Chinese beer has fallen from 51.5% (2019) to 47.5%. Because 40% of Chongqing belongs to others, the EPS effect is about half the EBIT effect, yet the shares have moved 5% to 15% on Chinese prints worth 1% to 3% of EPS. A second weak summer costs about DKK 320m of EBIT (2.1%) and DKK 1.1 of EPS; a structural 20% fall in China profit costs about 4% of EBIT, 2.9% of EPS and delays the buyback.

2.1 ★ "How do you split the third-quarter fall between weather, destocking, weak nightlife spending and share lost, and is the share loss concentrated in particular provinces or price tiers?"

2.2 ★ "Heineken grew in China this year while you fell. What is it doing that is working, and what are you changing in premium and in the on-premise channel?"

2.3 "You said trade inventories were clean at the end of September. How have October and November sell-out compared with sell-in?"

2.4 "Does the 2027 plan assume China volume grows on the easy comparisons from the second quarter, and how much of Chongqing's lost margin comes back with it?"

2.5 "What would you need to see before calling China structural rather than cyclical, and would that change your capital allocation or your appetite to own 60% of Chongqing? Or save for management."

How to read the answers: "weather and destocking, inventories clean, sell-out improving" = cyclical, the comparisons arrive from the second quarter of 2027, and the share-price risk is mostly sentiment; "competition in premium in the cities" or "nightlife structurally weaker" = part of the fall is share, China's 2027 recovery is smaller, and it argues for waiting for Chongqing's January figures before adding.

Driver 3. The self-help runway after 2027

Why it matters: named levers (the last Britvic synergies, the Kazakhstan plant, restructuring paybacks) supply more than half of EBIT growth in FY2026 and FY2027 and only about a sixth in FY2028, and self-help turns negative in 2029 with the Nordic bottling swap's transition costs. No broker quantifies this fade. Management's informal ambition is "compounding high-single-digit operating profit growth"; our base is 5%, because after 2027 growth must come from price over cost without a named programme. Each point of EBIT growth sustained over FY2028 to FY2030 is worth roughly 3% of FY2030 EPS (the Investment Analyst's arithmetic, not the memo's); a new efficiency programme at the next strategy cycle would extend the runway and lift the outer-year numbers.

3.1 ★ "Once the Britvic synergies and Kazakhstan are in, what carries EBIT growth in 2028 and 2029? Is there a next efficiency programme, and when does the next strategy cycle set it?"

3.2 ★ "Management has talked of compounding high-single-digit operating profit growth. Which building blocks get you there after 2027, given revenue has run at about 3%?"

3.3 "How large is the 2029 Nordic swap's dip in volume and cost, and is it inside your mid-term framework?"

3.4 "Is any margin gain in Western Europe coming from soft-drink density on the PepsiCo routes, and would you show the soft-drink gross margin so the market can see it?"

3.5 "With incentives now weighted more to revenue, how do you make sure growth spending does not absorb the savings? Or save for management."

How to read the answers: a named programme or a dated strategy cycle with savings beyond 2027 = the "high-single-digit" path is credible and the outer years have upside; "price, mix and density" with no programme = our 5% EBIT path and the fade the memo describes; a large Nordic dip = FY2029 consensus (implying +8% EBIT growth) is too high.

Driver 4. Capital return: leverage, the buyback and India

Why it matters: the buyback is the one dated, under-modelled positive. Our base is DKK 2bn from August 2027 and DKK 4bn in 2028, retiring 2.5% to 3.5% of the shares a year and adding 2.5 to 3.5 points of EPS growth that no consensus model carries. A one-year delay costs about 3.7% of FY2028 EPS. Leverage reaches about 2.5x at end-2026 if Sapporo closes (about 2.7x if not), and management has said reaching 2.5x "will not automatically trigger" a buyback. An Indian listing (up to USD 700m) is the only thing that brings it forward. The memo's timing call rests here: add after the spring, unless India or the buyback lands early. A February announcement or Indian proceeds committed to buybacks would argue for adding before the spring; "not before 2028" would argue for waiting.

4.1 ★ "You have said reaching 2.5 times will not automatically trigger a buyback. What else does the Board need to see: time at that level, India's proceeds, China, the rating agency? Is the half-year the natural point, or could February be?"

4.2 ★ "Is 2.5 times a level you will hold, so that cash beyond the dividend and capital spending comes back to shareholders, or a waypoint to something lower?"

4.3 "Is Sapporo still on track to close this quarter, and where does leverage land at the year end with and without it?"

4.4 "Where does the Indian listing stand after the updated draft prospectus of 27 October, and would the proceeds go to buybacks or to debt?"

4.5 "From 2028, as capital spending steps down from about DKK 6.5 billion, how large could annual buybacks be, and how does supplier finance (about DKK 3.2 billion) affect that?"

4.6 "Would a larger acquisition take priority over buybacks if one came along? Or save for management."

How to read the answers: "the half-year, subject to conditions; 2.5x held; Sapporo in December" = our base, and the memo's case to add after the spring stands; "February is possible" or "India's proceeds to shareholders" = the sequence reverses and the case to add now strengthens; "2.5x is a waypoint" or "acquisitions first" = smaller or later buybacks and a weaker case.

Driver 5. What reaches EPS: finance costs, the hybrid and minorities

Why it matters: consensus FY2027 EPS (DKK 69.25) is close to ours (DKK 70.91) in level but reached by a different route, and nineteen brokers' FY2027 EPS runs from DKK 65.57 to 75.79 on revenue within 4%. Three unguided lines explain it: finance costs (our DKK 1,650m all-in against DKK 1,800m to 1,944m at four brokers, DKK 0.9 to 1.7 of EPS); the hybrid coupon (DKK 620m gross, DKK 3.6 a share, not deducted by several brokers); and minorities (DKK 1,023m in FY2025, 14.65% of profit, rising with Sapporo; our DKK 1,280m against DKK 1,004m to 1,293m). The cut in finance-cost guidance from DKK 2.2bn to 1.8bn is partly reclassification under IFRS 18 (about DKK 150m to 250m). The February results, the first clean IFRS 18 year, will force the correction. A FY2027 finance-cost guide at or below DKK 1.6bn adds DKK 1 to 1.5 to the sell-side's EPS; at DKK 1.8bn or above, ours loses about DKK 1.2.

5.1 ★ "How much of the cut in finance-cost guidance is items moving between lines under IFRS 18 rather than lower debt, and what is the all-in cost of debt plus the hybrid coupon for 2027?"

5.2 ★ "Where will the hybrid coupon sit in the FY2026 accounts, and is your adjusted EPS before or after it?"

5.3 "How should we model minorities in 2027 as Sapporo's 25% of Southeast Asia is added: is about DKK 100 million a year for Sapporo's share the right order?"

5.4 "Will you publish restated 2025 figures on the new basis before 9 February, so the market can correct its models before the results?"

How to read the answers: "mostly reclassification, coupon after EPS" = the brokers who have not deducted the coupon lose DKK 1 to 3.6 a share in February, the spring dip the memo expects; "genuine savings, about DKK 1.6bn" = our EPS is safe and the sell-side gains; minorities near DKK 1.3bn = a further composition cut.

6. The general pattern: how guidance is set, the sell-side, and what has changed since April

Why it matters: the question behind every other is how far guidance and the sell-side's numbers will be raised or lowered over the next year. The memo's answer is that guidance is conservative by a habit the sell-side has learned: a range about four points wide, narrowed upward at the half year in each of 2023 to 2026, delivered at or above the top (2024: 1% to 5% raised to 4% to 6%, delivered 6.0%; 2025: 1% to 5% narrowed to 3% to 5%, delivered 5.0%). It expects 5.5% to 6.0% for 2026, an opening range for 2027 on 9 February with a midpoint of 4% to 5%, below consensus EBIT growth of 5.9% (about 70%), an upward narrowing in August 2027 (60% to 65%), a cut during 2027 at 10% to 15% and a raise above the initial top at about 10%. The two consensus figures differ: FactSet's EBIT for 2026 equals the guidance midpoint; Bloomberg's, as Deutsche Bank reports it, is 5.8%. An opening range of 4% to 7% or narrower would break the pattern and is the positive case; a midpoint below 4% is the downside case.

6.1 ★ "Guidance for this year is 4% to 6% organic EBIT growth. Bloomberg consensus is about 5.8%, near the top, and Deutsche Bank has 6.2%. What are your thoughts on 6.2%?"

6.2 ★ "For the last three years you have opened with a range about four points wide and narrowed it upward at the half year. Consensus has EBIT growth of about 5.9% for 2027. Should we expect the same kind of opening in February, and how do you think about a midpoint below where analysts already are?"

6.3 ★ "In April the Chief Financial Officer told us he was confident in the guidance, that Carlsberg would raise price if it had to and not eat into margins, and that weather is always the biggest swing. Since then your China volume has fallen two quarters running. Is the company more or less confident than in April, and has anything other than China moved?"

6.4 "Nineteen brokers' 2027 EPS runs from DKK 65.57 to 75.79 on revenue within 4%. Which analysts model the lines below EBIT the way you would, and what are the lowest and the highest missing?"

6.5 "What worries management most for 2027 and 2028: China, pricing against competitors in the United Kingdom and Poland, or household spending? Which of them could cost the top half of a guidance range?"

6.6 "The 4% to 6% revenue ambition has been missed each year. What do the investors who do not own the shares say about that, and in April we were told 20% to 30% of pack and product variants would be cut in 2026 and into 2027: how far along is that, and what is it worth per hectolitre?"

6.7 The final, difficult question. "If 2027 guidance has to be cut during the year, what will have caused it?"

How to read the answers: on 6.1, "we would not steer you away from the top" or no pushback on 6.2% = 2026 lands at or above 6% and the habit holds; a pointed "the range is the range" = the beat is shrinking faster than the memo's 150 to 200 basis points. On 6.2, "the same approach as always" = the memo's February opening below consensus, a sentiment dip the sell-side usually holds its numbers through; any hint of a narrower or higher opening = the positive case, and a reason to add before the spring. On 6.3, the April words repeated with China named as the only change = tone unchanged; hedging on price or on demand = less confident than April, and the downside branch of Driver 1. On 6.4, the houses he names show whose EPS is right below EBIT (Driver 5). On 6.5 and 6.7, the worry he names first is where a cut would come from; the memo's own answer is a Chinese relapse with price missing costs.
