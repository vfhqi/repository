Section 1 – Brief company description and history

Section summary
  1. Market expectations for Bank Pekao prior to the third quarter of 2026 focused on the pace of net interest margin compression and the resilience of domestic loan growth.
  2. Analysts anticipated gradual margin dilution from interest rate cuts and the transition from WIBOR to the new POLSTR benchmark.
  3. High valuations relative to historical averages raised the bar for Polish banks, leading some brokers to prefer regional peers.
  4. Resilient credit quality and stable corporate demand were expected to keep credit provisions low and support high dividend payouts.
1.
Strong domestic presence: Bank Pekao operates as a major player in the Polish banking sector, functioning as a universal bank that serves both corporate and retail customers. Pekao maintains the second largest asset base in the Polish banking market, representing approximately 16% of total system loans in 2025. Scale advantages support long-term earnings per share growth because they allow the bank to defend its transactional fee income.
2.
Dominant system duopoly: Bank Pekao and its state-controlled peer PKO BP collectively controlled approximately 40% of total loans in Poland during 2025. The market leader PKO BP held a larger loan market share of 24% in 2025, compared to 16% for Bank Pekao. Consolidated market ownership limits intense price competition across the Polish banking sector, which helps sustain the net interest margin of Bank Pekao.
3.
Superior profitability levels: Bank Pekao delivered highly attractive equity returns, reporting a nominal return on average equity of 22.6% in 2025 compared to 22.2% in 2024. Peer PKO BP generated a lower return on average equity of 21.9% in 2025, which was flat against its 2024 performance of 21.9%. Strong profitability supports high dividend payout ratios of 50% to 75% for Bank Pekao, bolstering investor returns over the next 18 months.
4.
Resilient margin performance: Bank Pekao achieved a robust net interest margin of 4.2% in 2025, slightly down from 4.3% in 2024. Peer ING Bank Śląski reported standalone income growth of 5% in 2025, driven by system-wide high interest rates. Stable margins allow Bank Pekao to sustain high core revenues even as central banks implement policy rate cuts.
5.
High capital buffers: Bank Pekao maintained a robust Tier 1 capital ratio of 15.0% in 2025, which exceeded regulatory minimum requirements by approximately 4.5%. Peer PKO BP reported a Tier 1 capital ratio of 15.57% in 2025, which was 4.1% above its regulatory minimum. Generous capital surpluses ensure steady dividend payments for Bank Pekao over the next three years, unless regulators tighten capital requirements.
6.
Improving credit quality: Bank Pekao successfully lowered its non-performing loan ratio to 4.4% in 2025, representing a steady decline from 6.4% in 2023. Peer PKO BP achieved a lower nonperforming loan ratio of 3.34% in 2025, indicating stronger asset quality in retail portfolios. Decreasing credit risk reduces future provisioning costs for Bank Pekao, directly boosting its earnings per share outlook.
