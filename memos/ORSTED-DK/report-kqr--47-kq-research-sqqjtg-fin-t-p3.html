Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Orsted faces a strategic crossroads where its financial recovery must be balanced against the risk of structural pipeline contraction after the current build-out phase.
  2. The tension between near-term deleveraging and long-term bidding discipline will directly dictate Orsted's future earnings capacity and credit rating stability.

Core strategic question

1.
Critical investigation: "which reinforcing or balancing feedback loops are at work in this business model, such that dynamics at play today create additional scope for further positive or negative dynamics in the near and medium term (reinforcing feedback loops), or the opposite (balancing loops)?"
2.
Significant tension: Orsted must navigate the delicate balance between accelerating wind farm completions to de- risk its balance sheet and maintaining strict bidding discipline. While completing committed projects helps restore investor confidence, skipping low-return tenders could shrink its long-term pipeline beyond 2028. This strategic trade-off will ultimately decide whether the company can maintain its market-leading position or face structural contraction.
3.
Contrary evidence: Orsted's management expects the medium-term growth gap between 2028 and 2030 to be successfully bridged by its US onshore business, which can develop 1 GW annually from a 6 GW safe-harboured pipeline. This onshore pipeline is projected to deliver healthy internal rates of return that supplement offshore earnings. This suggests that pipeline shrinkage in offshore wind may not result in an immediate earnings cliff.

Strategic earnings implications

4.
Muted prospects: Orsted faces a sharp decline in earnings after 2028 as older, highly subsidised offshore wind assets begin to lose their revenue support. Without securing new value-accretive tenders in 2027, the developer is projected to lose DKK 11 billion in subsidies between 2030 and 40. This subsidy roll-off could reduce offshore EBITDA by approximately 30% by the end of the decade.
5.
Favourable developments: European regulatory frameworks are shifting back toward bilateral, inflation-indexed, two-sided Contracts for Difference to protect developer returns. Germany's draft amendment of its Offshore Wind Energy Act explicitly introduces two-sided CfD support mechanisms for upcoming tenders. These changes improve revenue predictability and mitigate merchant price exposure for Orsted.

6.
Structural protection: Orsted benefits from strong government backing, as the Danish State retains a controlling 50.1% majority stake in the company. This ownership structure provides a crucial rating uplift from major credit rating agencies, which significantly lowers the company's funding costs. However, this state relationship also subjects Orsted to political scrutiny regarding capital allocation and dividend distribution policies.

Credit and funding costs

7.
Negative pressure: Orsted's capital structure remains highly sensitive to rating agency metrics, with Moody's maintaining a negative outlook on the developer. The company targets an FFO-to-adjusted net debt ratio above 30%, which currently stands at 37% after recent divestments. Sustaining this rating is vital, as any downgrade would increase borrowing costs and weaken bidding competitiveness.

If the company fails to secure new projects, it will face intense pressure to return capital to shareholders rather than reinvesting at thin spreads. This scenario mitigates funding risks but structurally caps the company's long-term earnings per share growth.
