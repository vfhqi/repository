Section 4 – Kymera's KT-621 Trial and the Threat to Dupixent

Section summary
  1. Kymera Therapeutics' oral STAT6 degrader KT-621 achieved highly competitive efficacy in its Phase 2b BROADEN2 trial, validating its potential to match Dupixent's profile in a daily pill.
  2. The trial demonstrated a manageable safety profile with mild-to-moderate conjunctivitis as the only notable side effect and no unexpected systemic toxicities.
  3. Dermatologists and brokers reacted with high optimism, viewing KT-621 as a major post-biologic treatment option and the clear front-runner in oral STAT6 degradation.
  4. Despite daily oral convenience, displacing Dupixent remains a formidable challenge due to its established safety track record and extensive first-line physician comfort.

Clinical Efficacy and EASI Results

1.
Outstanding efficacy: Kymera Therapeutics announced positive topline results from the Phase 2b BROADEN2 trial evaluating its oral STAT6 degrader KT-621 in patients with moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis on November 23, 2026. The once-daily oral small molecule met all primary and secondary endpoints with statistical significance at Week 16 across all doses. These positive results confirm the potential of STAT6 degradation to achieve high skin clearance and validate the long-standing therapeutic hypothesis.
2.
133 8K Impressive clearance: The mid-dose cohort of the BROADEN2 trial demonstrated the strongest clinical response, achieving a 72% mean reduction in EASI score versus 27% on placebo. Jefferies Research reported that 62% of mid-dose patients achieved EASI-75 at Week 16, representing a 40% placebo-adjusted benefit. This high level of skin clearance places the oral pill directly in the efficacy ballpark of established injectable biologics.

Unexpected drop-off: The high-dose cohort in the BROADEN2 study experienced numerical underperformance, with EASI-75 rates of 43% compared to 22% on placebo. Nevertheless, the lack of a linear dose response represents a wobbly trend that could complicate future Phase 3 registrational design.

This significant symptom reduction was evident as early as Week 1 and continued to improve through the 16-week treatment period. Such rapid itch relief is highly valued by both dermatologists and patients suffering from intense pruritus.

Safety and Ocular Side Effects

5.
Manageable safety: The safety profile of KT-621 remained highly favorable with overall adverse event rates similar to placebo and no serious treatment-related toxicities. Morgan Stanley reported that severe and serious treatment-emergent adverse events were less frequent than on placebo. This clean profile is crucial because oral therapies must avoid the severe systemic safety warnings associated with JAK inhibitors.
6.
Ocular findings: Conjunctivitis emerged as the only notable on-target side effect with a 13% incidence rate in the mid-dose group versus none on placebo. Jefferies Research reported that all reported ocular cases were mild or moderate in severity. This manageable rate of conjunctivitis sits comfortably below the 20% limit acceptable to dermatologists.
7.
Contrary evidence: The higher pooled conjunctivitis rate of 15% across all KT-621 doses represents a slight increase compared to the typical 10% rate observed in Dupixent trials. This elevated ocular signal could raise cautious questions among risk-averse dermatologists during initial real-world clinical adoption. While all cases were non-serious, some physicians may still prefer more established treatments until longer safety data becomes available.
8.
Encouraging retention: A remarkable 98% of patients who completed the 16-week trial chose to enroll in the 52- week open-label extension study. Deutsche Bank Research reported that this high rollover rate suggests strong tolerability and patient willingness to remain on treatment. Long-term data from this extension will be essential to confirm that STAT6 degradation remains safe over chronic periods.

Competitive Shifts and Market Reactions

9.
Enthusiastic feedback: Financial analysts reacted very favorably to the BROADEN2 data with Morgan Stanley raising its price target on Kymera to $198. The broker increased its probability of success for KT-621 in atopic dermatitis to 75% due to the compelling efficacy. This positive market sentiment reflects growing confidence that oral degraders can successfully compete in the multi-billion dollar immunology space.
10.
Pipeline validation: The positive Phase 2b results also provided a favorable read-through to other oral STAT6 programs in the industry. Deutsche Bank upgraded its probability of success for Kymera's atopic dermatitis program to 80% and raised its target price to $155. This validation highlights that targeted protein degradation can deliver biological-like efficacy in a highly convenient pill format.
11.
Strategic withdrawal: The competitive landscape shifted dramatically after Sanofi discontinued its Phase 1 trial of the oral STAT6 degrader SAR448272 on November 3, 2026. Jefferies Research noted that this early termination was likely due to molecule-specific issues rather than target biology. This unexpected exit leaves Kymera as the clear leader with a significant developmental head start.
12.
Broad threat: The strong 40-point placebo-adjusted EASI-75 benefit of KT-621 also poses a competitive threat to other emerging therapies in development. HC Wainwright reported that this strong oral profile could challenge Evommune's subcutaneous IL-18 binding protein fusion EVO301. Consequently, developers of injectable assets must demonstrate superior durability or unique efficacy to secure a market niche.
