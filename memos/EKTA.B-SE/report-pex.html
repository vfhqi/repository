1. The judgement in brief

  • The bar on profit looks low; the bar on sales and orders does not. Consensus adjusted EBIT is SEK 494m (11.8% margin) against SEK 412m a year ago. On the first quarter's own pattern, profit lands nearer SEK 550m. Organic growth of 1.9%, orders of SEK 4,177m and Americas sales up 9% are where the last four reports fell short.
  • The likeliest outcome repeats the last four quarters: profit at or above consensus, sales or orders soft, guidance kept. About 4 in 10 on both views.
  • That outcome has not been rewarded after a run-up. The shares fell (13.2)% on 28 May 2026 and (3.0)% on 27 August 2026 (a 26% EBIT beat), each after a rise of 11% to 15% in the thirty sessions before. They have risen 11.2% in the thirty sessions to 24 November 2026, ahead of their sector (Elekta +18.6% over three months against a median of +10.3% for thirteen medical-technology capital-goods stocks).
  • The sell-side has turned more cautious this month. Four houses rate Elekta Sell with notes dated since 10 November 2026 (Goldman Sachs, UBS, Barclays and one FactSet does not name); the share of Buy ratings fell 8 points in a month to 35%; the mean target price (about SEK 59) is below the share price.
  • Where the evidence leans: the report's risk and reward is skewed modestly to the downside. The probability-weighted move is about (1)% on the balanced view and about (2)% on the sceptical view. A clearly better report would need orders and organic growth to beat, not profit alone.

5. How the issues link

  1. Cost and price drive profit this year; volume does not. In the first quarter adjusted EBIT rose SEK 163m on a year earlier (SEK 233m to SEK 396m) on sales down 3%; SEK 53m was a tariff refund, so about SEK 110m was the cost programme net of inflation and of the change in development-cost accounting. Repeat that SEK 110m, add the guided refund of about SEK 28m to last year's SEK 412m, and the second quarter lands near SEK 550m (derived; medium-low confidence). Consensus needs only SEK 82m of improvement.
  2. Mix is the brake on that arithmetic. The second quarter carries more hardware installations, which earn less than service (Jefferies expects gross margin to step down from the first quarter's 42.6%), and last year's second quarter was flattered by SEK 73m of development costs put on the balance sheet, which DNB Carnegie expects to be neutral this year.
  3. Orders drive the multiple. Orders fell 15% in the fourth quarter of 2025/26 and have lagged sales for several years (Jefferies); the year depends on converting a SEK 34bn order book. The market has paid for margin already (two-year-forward multiple from about 11.6 to about 13 times since late September with estimates little changed), so the next leg needs evidence that revenue is turning.
  4. Currency flatters reported sales. DNB Carnegie raised sales 2% and EPS 2% to 4% for currency on 10 November 2026; reported growth may exceed organic growth this time, which helps the headline and not the case.
  5. China and Accela are the two-sided swing. China sales are expected up 11% as tenders convert; Accela will not affect this quarter's numbers (meaningful orders from 2027 and 2028, Jefferies) but any comment on US orders pausing would weigh on the multiple.

9. The balanced view

The cost programme is real and the profit bar is low, so an outright miss is unlikely, and China and currency give the headline sales a better chance than in May. Against that, the shares have priced the margin recovery (up 18.6% in three months, ahead of their sector) and the market has twice sold "profit without growth" after a run-up. Implication for the report's risk and reward: close to balanced, tilted slightly down; the upside needs orders.

10. The sceptical, risk-centred view

Four houses moved or reaffirmed Sell in the last two weeks, the Buy share fell 8 points in a month, and the mean target is below the price. The sales range needs about 3.1% for the rest of the year after a (2)% first quarter, on an order book that has lagged for years and a US market where Varian has just launched a new platform. A beat built on a tariff refund and mix would be read as low quality. Implication: skewed to the downside, with a fatter bad tail (1 in 4 for a cut or narrowing of the sales range) than the base rate.

11. Where the two views differ, and the strongest case against the lean

  • They differ on the bad tail: 15% against 25% for "expectations lowered". Both put "in line on profit but soft in tone" at 40%.
  • The strongest case against the lean (that the skew is up): in November 2025 the same outcome type produced +23.5% because the shares had fallen beforehand and the cost programme surprised; here, orders of SEK 4.3bn or more with Americas and China both up double digits would turn "profit without growth" into "growth arriving" for the first time under this chief executive, and with four Sell ratings outstanding there is fuel for a short-covering move. What would have to be true: orders and organic growth both beat, and management sounds confident on the second half (a narrowing of the sales range to its upper half would do it).

12. Threats, risks and fragilities

  • Company: hardware mix pulling gross margin down more than expected; installation timing in the Americas and TIMEA; development-cost accounting no longer flattering profit; employee morale after the cuts (expert calls in the Earnings Review); FY2027/28 risks that the call may touch (tariff refunds ending, a lower hedged dollar rate).
  • Industry: Varian's Accela and Siemens Healthineers' bundled hospital deals (over EUR 400m signed in its quarter to September 2026); United Imaging taking share in China (24.5% of volume-based tenders) and China's widening centralised procurement; a possible US tariff (Section 232) on machines built in the United Kingdom.
  • Peers: Accuray's gross orders halved in its quarter to September 2026 (USD 19.9m against USD 39.6m), with negative net orders after age-outs: a reminder of how fragile order books are in this industry.

14. The 12-to-24-month case, in one paragraph

The report tests the margin half of the case, which is largely delivered, more than the revenue half, which decides it. The Early-Stage Assessment memo of 17 November 2026 judged Elekta "a sound earnings-and-dividend return at a fair price" (about +17% to +22% to January 2028 at a fair multiple of about 14 times), not an unusually skewed one, and named three signs that would change that: a first guidance raise (earliest early March 2027); two quarters of organic growth of 4% or more with the gross margin above 40% excluding refunds; and 2027/28 guidance at or above consensus in late May 2027. Tomorrow can advance the second sign by one quarter at most. A miss on orders would weaken the revenue half without touching the margin half; a clearly better report would make the second sign plausible for the first time.
