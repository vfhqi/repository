Where the sell-side's view is heading

The Investment Analyst's judgement

The Investment Analyst judges the upgrade cycle over: consensus already matches the Investment Analyst's estimates, November's lift is mechanical, and cut risk rises from mid-2027.

  • Twelve and six months: financial year 2027 earnings per share rose 16%, EBITDA 15% and sales 16% over twelve months; over six months the target price rose 36% and the Buy share 50 points, to 75%.
  • Three months and one month: upgrades stalled: earnings per share up 1% and 0%, target price and Buy share flat; the Investment Analyst read this as the upgrade round pausing pending third-quarter follow-through.
  • What turned it: after the September 2025 rebasing the company beat in four of five quarters; the third quarter of 2026 was the largest beat, with earnings per share 37% above the four-contributor consensus.
  • Next, November: the Investment Analyst expects a 5-9% revision jump when FactSet absorbs Redeye's post-results update, but judges it the third quarter being recorded, not new information.
  • To April 2027: the Investment Analyst expects two beats to nudge financial year 2027 up 2-4%, yet consensus to end within 5% either side of SEK 2.85, as it already sits at the Investment Analyst's level.
  • From mid-2027: the Investment Analyst puts the chance of a cut at about 40% at each of the next two reports, and expects financial year 2029 to fall 10-15% toward SEK 3.6-3.8.
  • What would prove it wrong: a second broker initiating, Data Center above SEK 600m in a quarter, or supplier validation lifting the cap would each lift estimates by mid-single digits.

RedEyeFredrik Nilsson (a report of 1 Jun 2026 also names Rasmus Jacobsson, Tomas Otterbeck and Björn Fahlén)No buy, hold or sell rating; Base Case fair value SEK 46 (23 Oct 2026)

RedEye sees a working pivot to Data Center and Harsh Environment, with Fiber Solutions recovering, while repeatedly warning that Data Center may prove cyclical.

Stance and how it got there

  • RedEye gives no buy, hold or sell rating: it uses a fair value approach, and its Base Case stood at SEK 46 after the 23 Oct 2026 review, up from SEK 44.
  • RedEye cut twice in spring 2025: SEK 57 to 50 on 17 Apr 2025 on currency and macroeconomic uncertainty, and SEK 50 to 42 on 2 May 2025 on geopolitics, interest rates and cautious builders.
  • RedEye raised through 2026: SEK 32 to 34 on 30 Apr, 34 to 37 on 7 May, and 38 to 44 on 16 Jul for Fiber Solutions margin recovery and sequential EBITA beat.
  • RedEye was measured in July 2026: with the SEK 44 Base Case it set a Fair Value of SEK 41 and declared the shares fairly priced after the rally to SEK 45.42 (report of 7 Aug 2026).
  • After the third quarter of 2026 RedEye retained a positive view but discounted the Data Center outperformance because management expected fourth-quarter normalisation (22 Oct 2026).

How it frames the case

  • RedEye's core pillar (report of 7 Aug 2026): the move from a pure fibre-to-the-home play into a diversified group, with Fiber Solutions cash flow funding acquisitions in Harsh Environment and Data Center.
  • RedEye on 23 Oct 2026: Data Center and Harsh Environment are about 44% of group sales and over half of group profits, so Fiber Solutions no longer alone drives earnings.
  • RedEye's quality ratings (report of 18 Sep 2026): People 4 and Business 3, with Financials capped at 2 because of inconsistent historical returns on invested capital.

Main arguments

  • RedEye sees Data Center as less cyclical: on 23 Oct 2026 it called the segment far less cyclical than traditional fiber business; on 6 Feb 2026 it noted data centres re-cable every two to three years.
  • RedEye rates the deals as accretive: Superior Fiber cost 5.4 to 6.0 times EBITDA after a tax reorganisation, and the subsea line could return over 20% at operating profit level (report of 7 Aug 2026).
  • RedEye credits pricing power: it maintains Hexatronic can pass rising resin and glass fibre costs on to customers over time, despite near-term margin risk (report of 7 Aug 2026).
  • RedEye credits management: its March 2026 assessment praised chief executive Rikard Fröberg for a clear long-term pivot, a lean decentralised organisation and disciplined capital allocation.
  • RedEye's forecasts rose after the third quarter: earnings per share SEK 2.39, 2.85 and 3.36 for 2026 to 2028 (23 Oct 2026), after sales and operating profit beat its model by 10% and 18%.

What worries it

  • RedEye worries about Data Center cyclicality: on 27 Oct 2025 and 23 Oct 2026 it warned the market shares some of the boom-and-bust characteristics of FTTH.
  • RedEye flags acquisition dependence: reaching the 2028 targets depends on acquisitions, and weak Fiber Solutions cash flow would stall the pivot (6 Feb 2026); its 2028 sales forecast sits below target.
  • RedEye fears price competition: on 15 Jul 2025 it warned that if overcapacity triggers a price war, margin gains will simply filter through to its large customers.
  • RedEye flags financial quality: cash conversion averaged below 80% over five years, and it called the closed Weterings plant, bought in 2021, capital destruction (report of 18 Apr 2026).
  • RedEye raised its discount rate: on 22 Oct 2026 its risk-free rate assumption rose from 2.5% to 3%, which could offset part of the forecast upgrade.

How its view changed

  • Twelve months ago RedEye was rebuilding from a low: its Base Case sat near the 2025 low of SEK 24 after the September 2025 note confirming a softened long-term fibre-to-the-home outlook.
  • Six months ago RedEye turned more positive: it raised the Base Case to SEK 34 and 37 in April and May 2026 on the United States rebound, Data Center growth and the Superior acquisition.
  • In the last three months RedEye held, then raised: SEK 44 held in the 1 Oct 2026 preview, which trimmed Harsh Environment, then SEK 46 on 23 Oct 2026 after a broad third-quarter beat.

Where the brokers disagree

Is Data Center growth structural, or another boom-and-bust cycle like fibre-to-the-home?

The more positive side

  • RedEye (23 Oct 2026) calls Data Center far less cyclical than the fibre business, citing short replacement cycles and subcontractor flexibility.
  • Nuvama (15 Oct 2026) says artificial intelligence clusters need up to 64 times more fibre; Jefferies (report of 25 Oct 2026) says suppliers have at least two years of order visibility.
  • Rothschild & Co Redburn (13 Oct 2026) projects data centre spending of $2.2 trillion by 2030; Morgan Stanley (13 Sep 2026) sees cloud customers reserving capacity.

The more negative side

  • RedEye itself warned on 27 Oct 2025 and 23 Oct 2026 that Data Center shares some of fibre-to-the-home's boom-and-bust characteristics.
  • Bernstein (29 Sep 2026) warns hyperscalers would cut optical spending if artificial intelligence scaling laws break down; Huatai Research (May 2026) draws a dot-com parallel and warns that a spending peak often leads to a bubble burst.
  • Morgan Stanley (19 and 23 Oct 2026) warns credit stress could cancel data centre projects; Deutsche Bank (12 Oct 2026) says the bear narrative is hard to disprove.

Does the fibre shortage help Hexatronic through prices, or hurt it as a non-integrated maker?

The more negative side
