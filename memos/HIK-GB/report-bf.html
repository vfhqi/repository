Plausible problems to monitor

Hikma's working capital intensity stands out as a structural outlier, with net working capital days reaching 264 days in H1 2026, substantially elevated above the global generic peer median of approximately 150-195 days. While regional MENA peer Jamjoom Pharma exhibits similarly elevated cash conversion cycles at 285 days, Hikma's working capital absorption materially exceeds major global competitors Teva (150 days), Viatris (140 days), and Sandoz (195 days). This elevated intensity reflects deliberate inventory stockpiling strategies in MENA markets to buffer against geopolitical supply chain disruptions, but the persistent cash lockup constrains free cash flow generation relative to peer operating profit conversion rates [Cross-ref: Section 2, Section 8]. Extreme buyer concentration represents a fundamental structural weakness, as four major US generic purchasing organizations control 90-93% of retail generic procurement. The top three US drug wholesalers –McKesson, Cencora, and Cardinal Health–collectively account for 35% of Hikma's total group revenue, creating severe monopsony pricing pressure that generic peers face but which particularly constrains Hikma's lowermargin Hikma Rx oral solids division.

Expert transcripts confirm that procurement consolidation is intensifying, with buyers maintaining strict cost-driven purchasing decisions and increasing leverage over generic injectable formulators [Cross-ref: Section 3, Section 5, Section 8].

Hikma's five-year revenue CAGR of 7% and 25.5% EBITDA margin position the company favorably against largescale global peers, but regional champion Jamjoom Pharma delivers a structurally superior 23.5% ROIC versus Hikma's 16.0%, highlighting that pure-play MENA branded generic competitors achieve higher capital efficiency through lighter asset requirements and focused regional positioning. This return gap suggests Hikma's diversified three-engine model, while resilient, carries incremental capital intensity that dilutes returns relative to specialized regional operators [Cross-ref: Section 2, Section 7].

Generic pricing erosion dynamics remain structurally unfavorable, with US generic price deflation stabilizing at 5- 7% annually for injectables compared to 60-70% post-entry price destruction for oral solids. While Hikma benefits from superior injectable pricing resilience, former executives warn that generic injectables markets are expanding from traditional two-player structures to 4-10 players per molecule, threatening to replicate the severe margin compression dynamics of commoditized oral solids.

If aggressive capacity build-out floods the sterile injectables space, injectable gross margins could collapse from historical 40%+ levels toward single-digit oral solid norms [Cross-ref: Section 3, Section 7, Section 10].

Outliers worth noting

Hikma Pharmaceuticals exhibits several statistical outlier characteristics when benchmarked against global generic pharmaceutical peers, revealing both structural advantages and operational vulnerabilities that place the company at the extremes of industry performance distributions. The company's return on average invested capital of 16.0% in 2025 significantly exceeds the European generic peer median of 11%, positioning Hikma in the top decile for capital efficiency within the global generics universe, while sustained core EBITDA margins above 25.5% provide superior reinvestment headroom over global generic peers.

This profitability outlier stems primarily from Hikma's concentration in profit-favoured value chain stages, where generic sterile injectables capture 27-28% core EBIT margins and the Branded MENA division delivers 25-32.5% operating margins, both substantially outperforming commoditised retail oral solids which average 17-20% across global peers. The technical barriers inherent in sterile fill-finish manufacturing, combined with Hikma's dominant regional position as the number one pharmaceutical manufacturer by sales across MENA markets, create durable competitive insulation that translates directly into above-peer financial returns.

However, Hikma simultaneously demonstrates a material operational outlier weakness in working capital management that positions the company in the bottom quintile of cash conversion efficiency. Working capital days expanded to 264 days in H1 2026, driven by elevated inventory buffer requirements in MENA supply chains, substantially exceeding the 140-195 day range observed across global generic peers Viatris, Teva, and Sandoz.

While this elevated working capital intensity reflects deliberate inventory stockpiling in MENA to guarantee supply continuity amidst regional shipping disruptions, the strategic rationale does not fully mitigate the structural cash flow penalty relative to competitors operating with leaner supply chain models. Regional peer Jamjoom Pharma recorded an elevated cash conversion cycle of 285 days in H1 2026 and 256 days in 2025, with working capital expansion driven by receivable days rising to 182 days due to regional institutional payment delays, suggesting that prolonged working capital lockup represents a systemic structural characteristic of MENA pharmaceutical operations rather than Hikma-specific execution weakness.

Nevertheless, this working capital outlier directly constrains free cash flow generation and forces Hikma to maintain higher net debt balances than would otherwise be necessary given its strong operating profitability.
