Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The resolution of glass container pricing and energy risk represents a critical determinant of Fevertree Drinks PLC's gross margins and future earnings per share trajectory because glass represents twenty-four percent of its total product cost of goods sold.
1.
Key Question: "For a UK brand owner that buys glass bottles from Encirc (Vidrala), Verallia or O-I under contracts with energy price-adjustment factors, how much would 2027 and 2028 glass bottle prices rise at European gas and power forward prices as of November 2026, how quickly did glass makers pass through energy costs to customers such as Fevertree Drinks PLC, AG Barr, Britvic and Carlsberg in 2022-23 (with dated figures), and what hedging and pricing actions did UK soft-drink and mixer companies announce for 2027-28 in their latest results?"

Significance of the Packaging Risk

2.
Significant database: The research identified eighteen relevant sources, five major companies, and three historical precedents regarding glass contract pricing. All identified sources and companies were used in this analysis to ensure completeness. No relevant sources were excluded because all retrieved documents provided unique pricing or volume perspectives.
3.
Extreme exposure: Fever-Tree Drinks PLC faces disproportionate cost vulnerability because glass packaging represents twenty-four percent of its total cost of goods sold. This structural dependency means that changes in glass pricing have a direct and significant impact on gross margins. Consequently, any escalation in container prices directly threatens the company's projected earnings per share recovery.
4.
Vulnerable peers: Other beverage companies like Diageo and Campari also maintain high glass cost intensities of twenty-three percent and thirty-one percent respectively. In contrast, brewers like Carlsberg have a lower glass cost intensity of ten percent due to the dilution of costs through returnable bottle networks. Understanding these differences helps investors evaluate relative margin resilience across the European beverage sector.

Temporal Dynamics and Mitigation

5.
Delayed risk: The financial impact of energy volatility is a delayed threat rather than an immediate concern due to multi-year hedging strategies. Industry analysts believe the core cost of goods sold risk resides in 2027 and 2028 as current favorable hedges expire. This lag occurs because major European beverage players typically secure supply agreements twelve months in advance to protect near-term margins.
6.
Procurement protection: Industry analysts expect Fever-Tree Drinks PLC to continue its gradual margin recovery supported by procurement initiatives and pricing actions. Management has fully hedged glass costs for fiscal year 2026 and extended energy hedges through 2027 and 2028. However, renewed inflation across glass or energy following the expiry of these hedges remains a key downside risk for earnings.
7.
Historical disruption: The energy crisis of 2022 and 2023 serves as a critical precedent for how rapidly glass price inflation can compress brand margins. During this period, glass price hikes and logistics surcharges reduced Fever-Tree's margins, causing EBITDA margins to fall to 8.4% in 2023. In response, the company has contracted its glass needs in advance at elevated rates to prevent availability issues.
8.
Strategic defense: Beverage companies are actively managing this medium-term threat by expanding their energy hedging horizons through 2027 and 2028. For example, Fever-Tree has extended its energy coverage and is transitioning toward localized production to lower shipping costs. These mitigation actions will decide whether companies can protect their earnings per share from volatile European utility forwards.
9.
Other side of the story: Some industry evidence suggests that structural overcapacity and falling demand in Europe may prevent glass manufacturers from passing through higher costs. If glass makers face weak volumes and are forced to absorb inflation, Fever-Tree and its peers could see margin expansion despite elevated energy forwards. However, this outcome remains highly dependent on competitive dynamics and the strength of consumer demand in key spirits markets.

Section 2 – Projected 2027-2028 Glass Bottle Prices under Energy Forwards

Section summary
  1. European natural gas and electricity forward prices as of late 2026 remain historically high and are projected to drive elevated glass packaging production costs through 2027 and 2028.
  2. Glass manufacturers rely on a mix of multi-year price formulas and annual renegotiations that typically introduce a lag of up to twelve months before higher energy inputs affect brand owners.
  3. Structural demand weakness in the European spirits sector may limit the ability of container glass makers to fully pass through energy cost increases, putting pressure on glass industry gross margins.

European Energy Forward Curves

1.
Elevated forwards: European natural gas forward prices as of late October 2026 reflect persistent market tightness heading into the medium term. According to Morgan Stanley commodity research on October 30, 2026, Dutch TTF gas contracts are pricing at approximately €80/MWh on average for calendar year 2027. This elevated forward curve means glass packaging manufacturers will face historically high baseload energy expenses that will pressure their operating margins.
2.
Lower projections: Gas forward curves indicate some relief for European manufacturers further out in the projection period. Under a base case model published by Morgan Stanley on October 30, 2026, natural gas contracts for 2028 are expected to decline to €49.3/MWh. This projected reduction suggests that the worst of the mediumterm packaging cost inflation could subside from 2028 onwards as supply conditions normalize.
3.

Their presentation highlights that these forward prices will drop above $15/MMBtu in 2028, retaining a massive price premium over the domestic Henry Hub benchmark. This persistent global spread continues to put European glass makers at a significant structural cost disadvantage.

High electricity: Electricity prices in major European industrial hubs are also projected to remain highly elevated through 2027. Finnish utility provider Fortum reported on October 28, 2026, that German electricity forwards for 2027 are trading at approximately €150/MWh. Because glass manufacturing requires continuous electricity to power electric booster systems, these high power forwards represent a severe headwind for container glass producers.

Section 3 – Historical Speed of Cost Pass-Through in the 2022-2023 Energy Crisis

Section summary
  1. Glass packaging manufacturers experienced severe margin compression in early 2022 before executing unprecedented mid-year contract renegotiations and energy surcharges.
  2. Major European beverage companies suffered significant gross margin declines because of lag times in passing through packaging cost inflation.

Glass Maker Surcharges and Margins

1.
Rapid escalation: Verallia faced massive energy cost headwinds in early 2022 as natural gas and electricity prices surged. The company repeatedly renegotiated European contracts outside normal annual cycles to pass through these extreme costs. These dynamic pricing measures allowed Verallia to maintain a positive pricecost spread and achieve a 25.8% EBITDA margin in 2022. This proactive response shows that active contract management can defend manufacturer profitability.
2.
Extensive adjustments: Verallia successfully implemented massive extraordinary surcharges in the second quarter of 2022 to counter input cost inflation. These commercial actions raised the company's average selling prices in Europe by 15% to 20% on top of previous annual hikes. By the fourth quarter of 2022, regional glass container prices had risen by 25% to 30% compared to the prior year. This rapid pass-through protected Verallia's operating margins from immediate energy-related collapse.
3.
Severe compression: Vidrala suffered intense margin pressure in 2022 when its full-year EBITDA margin plummeted from 28% in 2021 to 20% due to unadapted pricing. In response, the glass manufacturer abandoned traditional annual reviews and implemented a 20% price hike in April 2022. This emergency action was taken on top of a 10% price increase implemented at the start of the year. Ultimately, these cumulative pricing interventions restored Vidrala's quarterly EBITDA margin to a record 34% by the fourth quarter of 2022.
4.
Protective insulation: Owens-Illinois managed the 2022 energy crisis through highly structured procurement strategies. The glass maker secured fixed-price supply agreements that covered approximately 80% of its European natural gas consumption. This extensive hedging, combined with a second round of market price increases, enabled Owens-Illinois to maintain positive net pricing. Consequently, these operational actions insulated the company's regional margins from the extreme volatility of European spot markets.

Downstream Impact on Beverage Customers

5.
Profitability collapse: Fevertree Drinks PLC experienced a devastating contraction in profitability as glass container costs escalated dramatically. The brand's EBITDA margins plummeted from 29.6% in 2019 to just 8.4% in fiscal year 2023. Unprecedented energy surcharges and restricted glass bottle availability heavily compressed its overall cost structure in late 2022. Consequently, these delayed pricing dynamics reduced Fevertree's gross margins to 32.1% in 2023 from 34.5% in the prior year.
6.
Delayed absorption: AG Barr successfully leveraged short-term hedging programs to partially protect its operating margins during the first half of 2022. However, the soft drink producer was forced to secure subsequent physical cover at highly inflated market rates. In response to persistent cost inflation, AG Barr chose to absorb a portion of the packaging price hikes. This deliberate commercial decision to prioritize customer value over short-term profitability compressed its first-half operating margins in 2023.
7.
Margin defense: Britvic PLC faced severe packaging cost headwinds as convertors passed through elevated utility expenses during fiscal year 2023. The soft drink manufacturer successfully defended its adjusted EBIT margin by executing significant price increases across its brand portfolio. This active pricing strategy offset the double-digit cost inflation and limited the full-year margin decline to only 10 basis points. These commercial actions demonstrate that robust brand equity can mitigate rapid cost pass-throughs.
8.
Margin erosion: Carlsberg AS reported a substantial escalation in manufacturing costs during the peak of the European energy crisis. The brewer's organic cost of sales per hectolitre surged by 13% in 2022 due to elevated packaging and raw material costs. Despite implementing substantial product price increases, Carlsberg's reported gross margin declined by 190 basis points in 2022. This severe compression highlights that even global brewers faced substantial lag times when trying to recover packaging inflation.
9.
Persistent pressure: Carlsberg AS continued to face severe manufacturing and energy cost inflation throughout fiscal year 2023. The brewer's organic cost of sales per hectolitre increased by another 13% in 2023, while reported gross margins fell by 160 basis points. To combat this ongoing erosion, Carlsberg expanded its commercial investments and implemented market-by-market price increases. This sustained inflationary environment shows that multiple consecutive pricing rounds were necessary to stabilize global brewing margins.

10.

In contrast, companies operating in regions without contract protections suffered immediate pricing impacts. This historical precedent suggests that formal contractual structures remain the primary defense against volatile cost pass-throughs.

A major packaging procurement manager reported maintaining absolute price per unit stability for five years under a direct relationship. This shows that some brand owners successfully insulated their gross margins through long-term partnerships with glass suppliers.

Strategic recovery: Fevertree and its beverage peers took almost eighteen months to structurally recalibrate their supply chains after the 2022 crisis. The mixer brand's subsequent tender in late 2023 for a new primary glass contract finally secured hedged energy pricing. This major procurement initiative is projected to drive a significant gross margin recovery of approximately 400 basis points in 2024. This evidence confirms that long-term contract restructuring is necessary to fully recover from temporary energy spikes.
13.
Other side of the story: While packaging cost pass-throughs severely compressed margins during the inflationary spike, the subsequent deflationary phase of 2023 and 2024 allowed glass manufacturers to enjoy super-normal profitability. Decreasing energy prices benefited earnings far more quickly than sales contracts adjusted downward, resulting in a record 34% EBITDA margin for Vidrala in late 2022. Section 6 – If the Answer Differs from the Common View

Section summary
  1. Structural overcapacity in European glass manufacturing could trigger a rapid decline in container prices that would expand brand owner margins.
  2. Persistent energy supply disruptions and regulatory bans on Russian gas imports could extend high packaging costs and depress beverage sector earnings.

Rapid Pricing Collapse Scenarios

1.
Potential overcapacity: Glass packaging manufacturers face a challenging European demand environment where recent capacity additions could trigger intense price competition. Verallia expanded its European footprint by starting up two new HeatOx oxy-combustion furnaces and its first hybrid furnace in Zaragoza, Spain, during fiscal year 2025. Because these additions coincide with structural volume declines in wine and spirits, manufacturers may be forced to aggressively cut contract prices to maintain utilization. This pricing collapse would immediately reduce packaging costs and expand downstream gross margins for brand owners like Fever-Tree.
2.
Intensifying competition: The entry of new, low-cost manufacturers in Europe could disrupt the pricing discipline of established glass packaging oligopolies. Industry analysts are closely monitoring whether the market entry of Ciner Glass in the beer and beverage segments will spark a localized price war. If this new capacity forces competitors like Verallia and Vidrala to lower prices, beverage brand owners will enjoy rapid gross margin expansion ahead of consensus forecasts. Consequently, Fever-Tree and its peers would see a substantial positive revision to their projected earnings per share.
3.
Rapid substitution: The persistent cost inflation of glass containers has already accelerated a structural shift toward alternative packaging formats. Ardagh Metal Packaging reported that beverage cans are continuously taking market share from glass in the European beer category due to elevated glass production costs. If this consumer migration intensifies, glass makers will experience severe volume deleveraging and be compelled to lower prices to retain customers. This competitive adjustment would force glass suppliers to absorb more energy inflation, thereby protecting beverage brand margins.

Severe supply threats: The European gas market remains highly vulnerable to structural supply constraints that could keep packaging manufacturing costs elevated. The European Union has mandated a legally binding phase-out of all Russian pipeline gas and liquefied natural gas imports by late 2027. According to the International Energy Agency, these phased restrictions will reduce European gas availability by thirty billion cubic meters in 2027. If alternative liquefied natural gas flows fail to fully bridge this gap, glass producers will face permanently higher baseload utility expenses, suppressing sector margins.

Depleted storage risks: Low European natural gas inventories heading into the winter heating seasons present a major threat of extreme price volatility. International Energy Agency simulations show that European gas storage levels could plunge below ten percent of working capacity by March 2027 under colder weather conditions. Such low fill levels would severely constrain withdrawal rates and trigger an intensive summer replenishment campaign, extending market tightness through 2028. This persistent tightness would force glass makers to trigger contractual price-adjustment formulas, severely compressing downstream beverage earnings.

Persistent margin pressure: Industry analysts remain highly cautious about the ability of European glass packaging manufacturers to sustain stable operating margins. Bernstein Research reported that still-challenging beverage consumption trends and rising input cost inflation make it difficult to call the end of the downward earnings revision cycle. If rising spot energy costs from geopolitical conflicts outpace planned capacity rationalization, Verallia and its peers will experience severe operating leverage pressure. Consequently, these packaging producers will be forced to aggressively pass through costs, directly threatening the gross margins of premium brand owners.

Elevated cost curves: Financial models indicate that sustained energy market tightness will significantly alter the industrial cost curve across Europe. Jefferies Research estimates that with European natural gas prices trading near eighty-five euros per megawatt-hour, approximately twenty percent of regional manufacturing capacity operates at a cash loss. Under these highly strained conditions, glass manufacturers are unlikely to offer pricing concessions during annual negotiations. Instead, producers are expected to aggressively pursue energy surcharges, passing the full inflationary burden onto glass-intensive beverage customers.
