Section 3 – European National Reimbursement Reforms and Insurer Consolidation

Section summary
  1. Unprecedented European reimbursement reforms scheduled for January 2027 will introduce direct pricing pressure on medical manufacturers.
  2. Retroactive Italian paybacks and consolidated Dutch insurance structures weaken supplier bargaining power unless offset by premium product mix.
  3. Commercial group purchasing organizations secure vital acute care market access for manufacturer ostomy portfolios but demand significant pricing concessions.
  4. High clinical switching costs and nurse brand loyalty shield established medical device manufacturers from volume loss when contracts are renegotiated.
  5. The consolidation of acute care tenders into multi-source contracts intensifies competitive bidding dynamics among the three dominant global suppliers.
  6. Rising input costs and wage inflation in Hungary put downward pressure on Coloplast's margins, though premium product mix remains a vital defensive lever.
  7. Premium innovations like the Luja catheter and SenSura Mio Concave maintain significant price premiums to support organic growth.
  8. The Medicare outpatient skin substitute price reset severely disrupted the biologics channel, forcing portfolio shifts to lowerpriced fish-skin grafts.

Upcoming French and German Reimbursement Resets

1.
Negative pricing: Coloplast anticipates notable reimbursement headwinds across major European markets heading into the fiscal year. Germany and France plan to implement price reforms on chronic care and advanced wound care starting in January 2027. These coordinated national policy changes are expected to depress net selling prices across the continent. Consequently, these regulatory pressures will weigh directly on the company's operating margin targets.
2.
Direct haircut: German reimbursement authorities are introducing systematic price reductions that threaten the operating profitability of medtech manufacturers. A general 3% reimbursement haircut is scheduled to take effect in Germany from January 2027. This direct pricing pressure is compounded by the GKV Stabilization Act, which limits hospital reimbursement increases to basic wage rates. For companies like Coloplast and B. Braun, this reform restricts the ability to pass on cost increases to hospitals.
3.
Escalating pressure: French medical device spending will face a severe fiscal squeeze under the proposed 2027 Social Security budget. The government plans to introduce a new 3.6% tax on 70% of reimbursed medical device spending. This statutory levy effectively represents a 2.5% cut on reimbursed spending before any adjustments. Analysts expect this tax to impact Coloplast, Convatec, and Smith & Nephew disproportionately.

Paybacks and Dutch Insurer Consolidation

4.
Drastic paybacks: Italy continues to enforce its highly controversial retroactive medical device payback mechanism. This regulation forces manufacturers to refund a percentage of regional healthcare expenditures that exceed national caps. The government implemented a settlement allowing companies to resolve historical 2015-2018 liabilities at 25% of their original value. However, ongoing liability assessments for the 2019-2025 period keep manufacturer risk high.
5.
Legal challenge: Affected medical device manufacturers are actively contesting the Italian payback system through judicial channels. In September 2026, the Italian Supreme Administrative Court indicated that the payback law is inconsistent with European Union law. The court intends to refer the matter to the European Court of Justice for a final ruling. If the European court invalidates the payback scheme, manufacturers could see a substantial reduction in their accrued liabilities.
