Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Ownership stability and leverage management represent the primary financial transmission mechanisms driving Storskogen's future earnings per share growth over the next three years.

Defining the Key Question

1.
Defining the inquiry: "Storskogen Group AB (STOR.B-SE), a Swedish serial acquirer and owner of about 114 small and medium-sized businesses in three business areas: Industry (industrial technology, automation and product solutions), Trade (business-to-business distribution and brand owners in professional products, health and beauty and consumer products) and Services (infrastructure services exposed to construction, and business services); net sales SEK 33,097m and adjusted EBITA SEK 3,117m in 2025 per the annual report of April 2026; listed on Nasdaq Stockholm since 2021; Q3 2026 report published 23 October 2026. For Storskogen Group AB (STOR.B-SE), who owns the shares and how has that changed over the last twelve months, what Swedish and international investors, fund managers, podcasts, investor forums and social-media commentators have said about the company since the January 2026 short-seller report on Lifco and since the Q3 2026 report of 23 October 2026, which of the company's dynamics they discuss (the restart of acquisitions, the Services business area, leverage and minority buyouts, the mid-term targets of 15% adjusted EBITA growth and a margin above 10% set at the November 2024 capital markets day, the valuation discount to Lifco, Indutrade and Addtech) and which they do not, and how this compares with investor commentary on Sdiptech, Volati, Indus Holding and Lifco over the same period?"
2.
Critical EPS drivers: Storskogen's forward earnings growth is closely tied to its transition back to growth-oriented capital allocation after a two-year deleveraging phase. DNB Carnegie's report from 13 February 2025 emphasizes that redeploying free cash flow into acquisitions is vital because historical growth was predominantly M&A-driven. This transition is expected to lift adjusted earnings per share from SEK 1.06 in 2025 to SEK 1.36 by 2028 unless macroeconomic integration hurdles or poor pricing discipline disrupt execution.

Financial Transmission Mechanisms

3.
Shareholder base shifts: Institutional and retail ownership concentration directly affects the stability of Storskogen's share price and its valuation multiple. DNB Carnegie noted in its Swedish Small Cap Report from 8 September 2026 that high financial risk and retail selling pressure historically left the stock out of favor. Consequently, major institutional backing from AMF Pension at 9.9% and Swedbank Robur at 4.9% serves as a critical buffer, meaning that any further stabilization of the shareholder register could lead to a significant multiple re-rating.
4.
Leverage and financing: Managing interest-bearing net debt is the primary transmission mechanism to protect forward earnings from dilution. Storskogen's CFO Lena Glader highlighted on 24 September 2025 that issuing SEK 1,000 million in 2030 bonds at Stibor plus 265 basis points removed near-term maturities and reduced interest costs. These refinancing actions directly support basic earnings per share, which rose to SEK 0.48 for the period ending September 2026 compared to SEK 0.44 in the prior year.
5.
Acquisition execution dynamics: Accelerating the pace of high-margin platform and add-on acquisitions is required to meet mid-term growth targets. DNB Carnegie argues in its report dated 10 March 2026 that leverage returning to the 2-3x target range allows the company to pursue compounder-style M&A again. Because these acquisitions are typically bought at attractive valuations of 7x EBITA or below, they will be highly accretive to earnings per share over the next 18 months.
6.
Other side of the story: Macroeconomic cyclicality and interest rate fluctuations could overshadow internal operational improvements regardless of ownership stability. DNB Carnegie cautioned on 9 February 2026 that more than half of the group is exposed to cyclical industrial production niches and construction-related business. Consequently, persistent macroeconomic headwinds in Europe could suppress organic growth and prevent the group from reaching its 15% adjusted EBITA growth target even if acquisition execution remains disciplined.

Section 5 – Peer Comparative Analysis and Valuation Discounts

  1. Storskogen trades at a persistent valuation discount to premium peers due to lower capital returns and higher cyclical construction exposure.
  2. Operational restructuring and completed minority buyouts are expected to gradually narrow the discount relative to second-tier peers by 2027.

Valuation Multiple Discrepancies

1.
Valuation multiple discrepancies: Storskogen trades at a deep valuation discount compared to premium peer groups. DNB Carnegie's peer table from 20 October 2026 shows Storskogen trading at an adjusted EV/EBITA of 9.6x for 2026e compared to Lifco at 22.5x and Addtech at 22.3x. This steep discount reflects investor skepticism about historical asset quality. Narrowing this gap is essential to restore long-term equity valuation parity and improve forward earnings per share.

Second-tier peer multiples: Other newer compounders like Sdiptech and Volati also command discounts compared to the absolute market leaders.
