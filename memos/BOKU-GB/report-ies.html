Boku (BOKU-GB) — implications of the interim results of 23 September 2026

These results leave Viewforth's recommendation where it stood and weaken the mechanism beneath it: the second-cut trigger did not fire and the delayed launches are live, but the reported underlying take rate of 77 basis points takes away the memo's best evidence against its own false-friend counter-case, and the pinned technical-momentum letter has fallen a full grade to B.

What changed

Boku reported the six months to 30 June 2026 on 23 September 2026. Revenue was $66.55m, up 5.1% on $63.34m, in line with the roughly $66.5m signalled in the July trading update and well below the $75.0m consensus that stood before the warning. Underlying revenue, stripping $3.4m of non-recurring launch-phase pricing from the prior period, grew 11%, or 10% at constant currency. Adjusted EBITDA was $19.59m, down 9.9% on $21.76m, marginally ahead of the revised $19.3m and against an original consensus of $24.0m; the margin was 29.4%, down 4.9 percentage points. Adjusted operating expenses rose 13% to $47.0m, IT and hosting costs rose to $5.66m from $3.74m, and headcount fell 1% half-on-half to 586.

Total payment volume was $8.60bn, up 16% on both a reported and a constant-currency basis, despite a roughly $200m headwind from the dual-sourced market and the two suspended carrier-billing connections in Russia. The blended take rate fell to 77 basis points from 85; on an underlying basis it fell 4 basis points, from 81 to 77. Wallets, account-to-account and bundling reached 47% of revenue against 43% underlying a year earlier. Own cash was $84.6m against $102.9m at 31 December 2025 after $23.6m was spent buying back 9.5 million shares, with a further 8.0 million-share programme running to the end of 2026. Free cash flow was $11.3m, but core free cash flow, excluding working capital, was $6.0m against $12.0m in the second half of 2025.

Full-year guidance was reiterated at $135m to $142m of revenue and $38m to $42m of adjusted EBITDA. Management said all the delayed launches for the key merchant are live and transacting in the second half, that those volumes are expected to more than offset the share lost in the dual-sourced market, that trading since the period end is in line, and that the take rate is expected to stabilise in the second half as in-house currency conversion launches across several markets. The medium-term ambition of over 20% organic revenue growth and margins above 30% was reaffirmed; the three-year growth rate to December 2026 is now put at 18% to 20%. Multiple merchants are live through Stripe, with no count given. Fourteen new and existing merchants delivered 47 connections across 24 markets in the half, and direct-sales wins in streaming, mobility and video went live in July and August 2026.

The sell-side read it as a de-risking print. Deutsche Bank raised its target to 180p from 155p on a target EV/EBITDA multiple lifted from 10x to 12x, moved its FY26 adjusted EBITDA estimate up 6% to $40.7m, the midpoint of guidance, and left FY27 unchanged at $155.1m of revenue and $44.6m of adjusted EBITDA, which is 12% growth and a 28.7% margin. Jefferies kept its 195p target and Buy, models FY26 revenue of $139m and adjusted EBITDA of $42m, and frames 18x forward earnings against a 29x long-run average. No notes from Investec or Peel Hunt were found in the window.

Set against the case: the case's record of the first half, taken from the July update, was volume growth of 12% to $8.3bn and an underlying take rate that held at 80 basis points. The reported half is volume growth of 16% and an underlying take rate of 77 basis points. Both revisions matter to the case, in opposite directions.

The challenge

The false friend has started to show in the reported numbers, and the sell-side is calling it mix. The case's single cleanest discriminator is the gap between volume growth and revenue growth. On the July figures the case judged that test ambiguous in the bull's favour, because the miss looked like a volume problem and the underlying take rate had held at 80 basis points. The reported half reverses that: volume up 16%, headline revenue up 5.1%, underlying revenue up 11%, underlying take rate down 4 basis points to 77. The case's own condition was that the underlying take rate must hold near 80; the review's own tripwire is 75. In its strongest form this reading says the yield decline is structural rather than a first-half weighting. The fastest-growing parts of the mix are the lowest-yielding: account-to-account rails at 10 to 20 basis points and bundling, which grew 39%. The Stripe channel arrives on revenue share at a shared economic. And the offset management points to, in-house currency conversion, grew cross-border settlement volumes 9% in a half when payment volume grew 16%; an offset that grows slower than the thing it offsets does not offset it. On this half's rate of yield decline, roughly 5% a year in relative terms, even Jefferies' own 23% volume growth to 2030 delivers revenue growth in the high teens, not over 20%. For this reading to be right, the second-half take rate has to come in at or below 77 basis points despite the currency-conversion launches, and the volume-to-revenue gap has to persist into 2027. The honest counter is that 77 is above the case's 70 park line, that Deutsche Bank reads the compression as product mix rather than pricing, and that a first-half weighting toward low-rate products is plausible. But the case can no longer cite a flat take rate as disconfirming evidence, and that was the memo's stated reason for not letting the false-friend guardrail bite harder.

"Live" is not "ramped", and the reiterated guidance is a lower bar than the sell-side's language suggests. The review's single most important finding is that the delayed launches are live in the second half. That is a management statement about timing, and the offset it promises is an expectation, not a reported number. The case's own moat facts say connections take six to twelve months to ramp and up to four years to mature, so a launch that went live in the third quarter contributes a fraction of its run-rate by December. In its strongest form this reading says the print de-risked FY26 guidance, not the case. On arithmetic from the review's figures, the reiterated range implies second-half revenue of roughly $68m to $75m against roughly $65m in the second half of 2025, so the bottom of the range needs about 5% growth and the top about 15%; landing inside it proves nothing about a return to over 20%. The test of the eighteen-to-thirty-six-month thesis has not been brought forward by this print; it has been pushed to the full-year results and the first half of 2027. For this reading to bite, the merchant's new markets need to be lower-yield wallet and account-to-account corridors than the market it dual-sourced, so that "more than offset" in volume terms is not offset in revenue terms. The review does not say which markets they are. This is plausible rather than probable, and it is the reading the case was built to wait for rather than pre-empt; the risk is that the sell-side has stopped waiting.

The case may be under-weighting the operating leverage now visible, and that reading is also live. Headcount fell to 586 while volume grew 16%, the cost increase is described as annualisation of prior hiring, and the memo's own transmission mechanism is volume times take rate with roughly 80% drop-through. If the cost base is now flat in people terms, any revenue re-acceleration falls through, and the over-30% margin becomes arithmetic rather than aspiration. Add the density of dated internal change in the half, which is above what the case graded: Stripe live, three direct-sales verticals live, a new Chief Product Officer, Chief Commercial Officer and Chief Data and AI Officer, a legacy platform retired, five new currency partners. For this to be right, second-half revenue has to land near the top of the range with adjusted operating expenses close to the first-half $47.0m, which would put the second-half margin well above 30%. The counter is that first-half opex grew 13% against revenue growth of 5.1%, IT and hosting costs rose by more than half, and the memo already scores the investment-phase test at A, so most of this is in the case. We carry it because the case's neutral stance on the next two quarters was set at 114.0p and the price is now 129.0p; if this reading is right, the near-term neutrality is the part of the case most at risk of being wrong in the expensive direction.

We do not carry the sell-side's re-rating as a reading. Deutsche Bank's target rose 16% on multiple expansion while its FY27 numbers, 12% growth and a 28.7% margin, show neither of management's medium-term commitments being met. That is a response to the print, not evidence from it.

What would settle it

The second review, four working days on, will carry what lands between now and then: refreshed broker estimates and targets, possibly notes from Investec and Peel Hunt, and revision data that post-dates 16 July 2026. It can settle whether the sell-side earnings momentum pin moves and where consensus FY26 sits inside the range. It carries no new company data: no second-half volume, no take rate, no Stripe merchant count, no merchant-level economics. The take-rate question, which is the one that matters, cannot be settled by it.

The full-year results, which the review expects in late February or early March 2027, settle most of the rest: second-half volume growth against the case's 15% park line and 20% conviction line; the second-half take rate against 80, 75 and 70 basis points; the second-half margin against 30%; whether FY27 guidance names over 20%. The review names third- and fourth-quarter trading updates but gives no dates and does not say what they disclose.

Three questions have no committed answer. Merchant-level take rates for the top four accounts, the Stripe channel's merchant count and economic split, and a segment-level yield for currency conversion have not been disclosed, and the company has not committed to disclosing them. Management's claim that the take rate stabilises in the second half is visible in the full-year blended figure; the reason for it is unlikely to be. Those three are Early-Stage Assessment work items, as the case already says, and this print has made the second and third more urgent rather than less.
