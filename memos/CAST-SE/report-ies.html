Castellum (CAST-SE): what the Q3 2026 results do to our case

These results strengthen our park, moderately: the operating evidence the case said to watch got worse, the first reassessment trigger failed at its first test, and the one thing that got bigger, the buyback, is three times the size our rejection of the bull case was built on.

What changed

  • Delivery was in line. Income from property management was SEK 1,126m in Q3 2026 against consensus of SEK 1,130m and DNB Carnegie's SEK 1,119m, down 10.14% on SEK 1,253m in Q3 2025. The case counted four consecutive misses; this is not a fifth.
  • Letting failed the case's test. Net letting was minus SEK 5m in Q3 2026 (plus SEK 16m in Q3 2025). Nine-month net letting of SEK 105m is minus SEK 35m without the Ericsson lease (Jefferies) and minus SEK 63m excluding projects (DNB Carnegie). The case asks for three consecutive quarters above SEK 50m.
  • Occupancy fell again. Economic occupancy was 87.3% at Q3 2026 against 89.2% at end-2025; the company attributes 1.7 points of the fall to the standing portfolio and 0.2 points to sales. The case's trigger is above 91%. Renegotiated leases worth SEK 181m repriced at minus 2.5%.
  • The buyback grew. Two programmes of up to SEK 8.0bn started on 22 October 2026, on top of SEK 7.6bn (62 million shares) bought in January to September 2026. Shares outstanding were 430,985,171 on 30 October 2026. Jefferies puts the total at 25% of the 2025 share count over 2026 to 2027. About SEK 5bn of the Skåne proceeds goes to debt. DNB Carnegie had expected part of the proceeds to go to extra dividends or the hybrid, and reads the choice as a board willing to raise leverage.
  • Balance sheet. Company loan-to-value 37.4% against a 40% ceiling; broad EPRA loan-to-value 52% (Morgan Stanley). Interest coverage 3.1x over the last twelve months against 3.2x a year earlier. The evidence states the policy floor two ways, 3.1x and "above three times"; either way headroom is a tenth of a turn or none.
  • Rates moved the wrong way. The Swedish five-year swap rose 77 basis points in Q3 2026 to 3.24%, and the Riksbank raised its policy rate 25 basis points to 2.0% in November 2026. Castellum's average exit yield rose 9 basis points to 5.73%; nine-month unrealised value change was minus SEK 953m.
  • Sell-side. DNB Carnegie raised its target to SEK 135 from SEK 125 (Hold, 23 October 2026) and lifted adjusted earnings per share for the buyback, then cut 2027 and 2028 by 3% on 30 October 2026 for financing costs. Its 2027 estimate of SEK 7.15 sits below its 2026 estimate of SEK 7.86 even with the buyback in. Jefferies stays at Hold. Morgan Stanley stays Underweight; its target is given as SEK 140 on 22 October 2026, which sits oddly beside an Underweight at a SEK 135.65 share price and we would not lean on it.

The challenge

1. The case's rejection of its strongest counter rests on arithmetic that no longer describes the company. The case rejected "shrink to quality" because disposals cut recurring earnings about 10% while the buyback retired about 8% of the shares: close to a wash. The retirement is now about 25% of the 2025 count. Against Jefferies' estimate that the Skåne sale dilutes EPRA earnings by about 10% annualised before buybacks, that is not a wash; mechanically it is per-share accretion of about a fifth. The nine-month numbers agree in direction: income from property management per share was SEK 7.39, up 5% on the year, while the absolute figure fell. In its strongest form: an equity holder owns the per-share line, the aligned owner is delivering it faster than the brokers expected, and the false-friend flags are the cost of transition. For it to be right, operating and financing drag must stay below the rate at which shares are retired, and disposals must keep clearing near book. The evidence against is DNB Carnegie's own path, which has 2027 per-share earnings below 2026 after modelling the full programme. So the case's conclusion probably survives, but for a different reason (financing cost and vacancy, not disposal dilution), and the memo's stated reason should not be carried forward unchanged.

2. The buyback is plausibly self-limiting, and the share price leans on it. The case rates technical momentum B and notes the company supplies much of its own bid. It does not consider that the bid has a ceiling set by the company's own policy. Interest coverage is at or within a tenth of a turn of the floor, net financial items rose from minus SEK 524m in Q3 2025 to minus SEK 580m in Q3 2026 while net operating income fell, and swap rates rose 77 basis points in a single quarter. On our approximate arithmetic, a fall of about 6.5% in property values takes company loan-to-value from 37.4% to the 40% ceiling with no further buyback; book yields have so far moved 9 basis points. For this reading to be right, coverage has to slip below policy as higher rates feed through and the board has to respect the policy rather than rewrite it. If so, the SEK 8.0bn is an upper limit that is probably not reached, the accretion in reading one shrinks, and the best letter in the case is the most fragile. The case's own bull mechanics rested on rapid Riksbank easing; the Riksbank has just tightened.

3. The buyback may be settling the ownership question in a direction the case never listed. The case's fifth trigger has two outcomes: the Akelius stake sold down in an orderly way, or declared long-term. A third is possible: a holder that does not sell into the programmes rises without paying for control. On the case's figures (25.4% of 477.5 million shares) and the 30 October 2026 count, such a holder would already be near 28%, and near a third if the remaining SEK 8.0bn were spent around today's price of SEK 136.2. We hold this as possible, not probable: the evidence does not give the current stake (one older source cites 13.52%), nor whether the holder is selling into the programmes. If true, minority holders are funding a creeping change of control with the company's balance sheet, and "aligned owner" needs re-examining.

The pinned letter is now F (9 November 2026): earnings revisions minus 5 over one and three months, buy share 24%, down 16 points in three months. The case's diagnosis, rising targets on falling earnings, has sharpened. Group 1, case momentum , takes the same pressure.
  • MR1, technical momentum : same. Pinned at B; price SEK 136.2 on 9 November 2026, 10.4% above the 200-day average. Reading two is about its durability, not its level.
  • The entry-point side: mixed. The case said the shares stood roughly 15% above the SEK 115 broker median. Targets have come up to the price (dashboard consensus SEK 137.94), so that sentence no longer holds as written; DNB Carnegie still calls the multiples demanding at 16.3x cash earnings against a sector 14.3x. The case re-looks below roughly SEK 110; the price is about 24% above that and has not crossed it.
  • E8.RA8, false-friend fit , and E8.RA7 : same to more. Mega-lease masking and buyback masking are both confirmed by the nine-month numbers. The "wash" arithmetic inside the E8 counter needs redoing.
  • E8.RA3, corporate change : same, with reading three questioning what the change serves.
  • E11.RA1 and E11.RA2 (both D), E1.RA2, robust base : more pressure. Occupancy, renegotiation pricing and terminations (minus SEK 554m over nine months) all ran against the base.
  • Group 4, case riskiness : more pressure. Proceeds went to equity, not the hybrid; the second trigger is harder to meet.
  • E7.RA5, conservative investor relations : slightly less, on one in-line quarter. The Skåne sale at SEK 13.3bn against a carrying amount of SEK 13.93bn is still described by management as near book.
  • Triggers. One failed; three moved further away; four not crossed; five unresolved and possibly being resolved the other way; two under more pressure.

What would settle it

  • The second review, four working days on. It should carry whether other brokers follow DNB Carnegie in cutting 2027 and 2028 for financing costs, and whether targets hold at the share price. That bears on MR2 and on the per-share path in reading one. It carries no new company disclosure: nothing on letting, occupancy, coverage or ownership.
  • The Q4 2026 report (date not given; last year's equivalent was 18 February). It carries Q4 net letting, the first quarter that can count toward trigger one, so that trigger cannot be met before the Q2 2027 report. It carries interest coverage against policy and how much of the SEK 3bn that DNB Carnegie schedules for Q4 2026 was actually bought, which together test reading two. It carries year-end valuations after the swap move. It carries occupancy after Skåne left on 1 October 2026; sources put that portfolio's vacancy anywhere from 12.5% to 17%, so a small rise in group occupancy is probably mix, not recovery.
  • The hybrid. The case places the first call in early 2027. The company has not, in the evidence we have, committed to saying how it is handled.
  • The largest holder's participation in the buyback. No disclosure the company has committed to answers it. That makes reading three a different kind of open question: one we would have to ask.
