[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = D

Thesis. thyssenkrupp nucera is a bounded-downside option on green hydrogen orders, not a growth case: a profitable, cash-rich chlor-alkali core worth about the share price on the sell-side's arithmetic carries a loss-making electrolyser business that the market values at the present value of its next two to three years of losses and nothing beyond. At EUR 8.50 the modal return is about 10.5 percent a year over eighteen months, below the 20 percent bar, because the earnings the market prices two years out rise about 70 percent on our path but the multiple that today sits on depressed earnings compresses to meet them. The case becomes compelling at about EUR 7.50 or on the first gigawatt of firm orders, and neither is here.

  • The change the case needs is external, and it has not begun.
    • Customers must convert paid engineering studies into firm electrolyser orders, and they are not deciding.
    • Green hydrogen order intake was EUR 3 million in the quarter to June 2026 against EUR 13 million a year earlier
      • The nine-month figure of EUR 184 million rests on the single 300 MW Moeve order of March 2026
    • The backlog fell to EUR 638 million at June 2026 from EUR 732 million at March and covers about a third of forecast sales
    • Final investment decisions are slipping by several quarters
      • Below 7 percent of green hydrogen projects reach one, and only 12 percent of projects scheduled for 2030 have offtake agreements, 1 percent binding
    • The company pruned its active pipeline to 14 GW across 35 projects from a 57 GW total on 12 August 2026
  • The change inside the company is real, defensive and already in the numbers.
    • The board has been renewed, the strategy has pivoted to asset-light standardisation and services, the perimeter has been cut and the cost base attacked
    • None of it raises revenue.
    • A new CFO from March 2025 and a new CTO from July 2025
      • A strategy built on the 20 MW scalum module, a 120 MW plug-and-play system and a lifecycle service portfolio
    • The SOEC manufacturing exit of 11 August 2026 avoids a three-digit-million-euro commitment, costs a EUR 30 million mostly non-cash impairment and adds up to EUR 10 million a year of EBIT from FY2026/27
    • A cost programme of over EUR 75 million: EUR 25 million landing in FY2026/27, over EUR 50 million structural thereafter
    • Guidance has been cut three times (May 2024, March 2026, August 2026), which is honest and is the opposite of a change force
  • The change has travelled three of the six layers of the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain and stopped.
    • It shows in the company's resources, choices and actions
    • It has not reached a key operating metric, a financial statement or a sell-side estimate.
    • Revenue fell 47 percent in the nine months to June 2026 and 21 percent in the quarter
      • Green hydrogen sales fell 79 percent in the nine months
    • Sell-side estimates for FY2027 earnings per share fell 46 percent over twelve months and 19 percent in the last month
      • The one house that updated after the August results left its post-2026 forecasts unchanged
  • 'Financial Outputs' are falling on every line, and the recovery the sell-side models from FY2026/27 is a return from a hole, not a rise above the past.
    • Revenue growth, margin, cash conversion and net cash are all worse than the three-year history
    • Consensus revenue for FY2025/26 is EUR 480 million, down 43 percent.
    • The Chlor-Alkali segment is the exception: order intake EUR 78 million in the quarter to June 2026, up 56 percent
      • Sales EUR 109 million, up 34 percent
      • EBIT EUR 15 million, up 15 percent
  • What we think it earns: earnings per share of about EUR -0.60 in FY2025/26, EUR 0.02 in FY2026/27, EUR 0.18 in FY2027/28 and EUR 0.55 in FY2030/31, below the sell-side on every year.
    • Consensus has EUR 0.110 for FY2026/27 and EUR 0.250 for FY2027/28
      • We give the study-to-order conversions less weight and later timing, because the FY2026/27 consensus revenue of EUR 621 million needs roughly EUR 200 million of orders booked and executed within the year that the backlog does not hold
    • The path is a recovery rate, about 45 percent a year from FY2027/28 to FY2030/31, which the framework marks down as a rate likely to return to base rates
      • It is not growth inside the 12 to 20 percent band on any horizon
  • What it costs on the valuation: EUR 8.50 is 37.7x the 24-month-forward consensus earnings of EUR 0.2256, a premium of two and a half times to the comparison set's 13x to 15x, and about 21x ex-cash.
    • On the stock's own three-year listed history the multiple is at the 43rd percentile of a range of 23.09x to 118.38x, but that history was earned on FY2027 and FY2028 estimates since cut by 46 and 65 percent
      • The multiple stayed in range because the earnings fell with the price
    • On the enterprise basis the price is a trough: the enterprise value of EUR 455 million is 7.8x Chlor-Alkali's FY2024/25 EBIT of EUR 58 million, with green hydrogen at roughly the present value of two to three years of losses and no terminal value for its order book
  • The return against the bar: about 10.5 percent a year over eighteen months and 14.2 percent a year over three years, at a 24-month-forward multiple of 26x and then 23x
    • The bar is 20 percent.
    • Earnings growth carries all of the return and more (the 24-month-forward figure rises 1.68x in eighteen months and 2.44x in three years)
      • The re-rating takes a third back (26x over 37.7x)
      • The cash return is nil, by policy
    • The swing factor is the multiple, set by the sector's appetite for the hydrogen option rather than by the company: hold it at 37.7x and the eighteen-month return is 42 percent a year
      • Let it go to the peers' 14x and the value is EUR 5.32
    • The plausibly worse drop-through (a 40 percent tax rate, EUR 3 million more depreciation, half the financial income) takes earnings per share at the eighteen-month point from EUR 0.38 to EUR 0.29 and the return to a loss of 11 percent
      • The profit is thin, so the drop-through is thin
  • The pattern it fits: 'Major CfC Clearing', loosely, a major cause for concern (customers not deciding, compounded by the module remediation and the SOEC reversal) in a business whose core is solid and whose growth leg is not
    • The clearing has not begun on the outputs.
  • Where the chain holds and where it breaks.
    • It holds from the company's choices to its cost base, which is why break-even in FY2026/27 is possible and the floor is plausible.
    • It breaks at the link between a paid study and a firm order, which is outside the company's control, has not fired since Moeve in March 2026, and is the only link that moves revenue, margin and the sell-side.

  • What the case therefore is: a clearing-type case waiting for the clearing, with a floor that erodes by about EUR 0.40 a share for every EUR 50 million of losses funded from cash while we wait, priced at a level where the modal path does not pay for the wait.

    • We would rather bet on it at EUR 7.50, where the uncleared branch costs little, or on the first large order, where the competing hypothesis is disproved.

  • Rating = D, because the material change the case needs, green hydrogen orders converting into revenue and margin, has not begun on the outputs

    • The external forces are a net headwind
    • The internal forces are real but defensive (cost-out, a technology exit, a zero-dividend cash hoard)
    • And our 'Modal return' on the path we think most likely is about 10.5 percent a year over eighteen months and 14.2 percent over three years against the 20 percent bar.
    • Not C, because a C would need the outputs to be improving on at least one line against the last three years, and every output line (revenue growth, margin, cash, estimates) is worse than the three-year history
      • The recovery in the sell-side's numbers from FY2027 is a return from a hole, not a rise above the past, and the sell-side is still cutting.
    • Not F, because the skew of outcomes is to the upside even though the mode is below the bar: the cleared branch, a gigawatt of firm green hydrogen orders by the end of 2027 at roughly 30 percent odds, gives a value near EUR 15 at eighteen months, and the downside is bounded by the cash and the core on a condition we state.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The case against, at its strongest: the pipeline is a false friend, and the floor is a value trap that costs EUR 0.40 a share a year to hold.
      • The 1.9 GW of paid engineering studies and the 14 GW active pipeline are not demand but a study-to-order gap that widens as developers complete engineering and postpone equipment
        • Management has said conversions were "weeks to months" away since early 2026 and one order has come
      • The two mega-projects that made the revenue, Saudi Arabia (EUR 277 million) and Sweden (EUR 189 million of FY2024/25 sales), are finishing
        • The backlog covers a third of forecast sales
        • Green hydrogen orders were EUR 3 million last quarter
      • The sector's base rate for distress is set (McPhy liquidated; Nel's impairments), and Chinese pricing, 20 to 40 percent below Western equivalents, deflates the margin any conversion would earn
      • The cash that is the floor is spent at EUR 50 million to EUR 70 million a year while the parent, which controls the general partner and has pledged its stake under a June 2026 financing agreement, decides what the cash is for
      • On this hypothesis the shares drift to the cash-plus-core value at a trough multiple, EUR 7 to EUR 8, and stay there for years
        • The risk research's synthesis puts the downside at EUR 7.60 to EUR 7.80
    • What we concede.
      • This hypothesis had zero hard inconsistencies against twelve months of data in the risk research, and the bull profile seven.
      • The outputs agree with it, not with us
      • The conversion base rates are its evidence
      • The governance means minorities cannot force a buyback or a sale
      • And our own modal return is below the bar, which is the hypothesis in a milder form.
    • The cover, shown like for like.
      • The bounded-downside argument rests on Chlor-Alkali's profit covering the price with the cash, so we show it at a price-to-earnings multiple on post-tax earnings and an enterprise-value multiple on EBIT.
      • Chlor-Alkali EBIT of EUR 58 million in FY2024/25 (guided to EUR 45 million to EUR 65 million for FY2025/26) is about EUR 40.6 million after tax at 30 percent, EUR 0.32 a share
      • At the comparison set's lowest multiple, Cadeler's 5.7x, that is EUR 1.83 a share
        • Plus net cash of EUR 4.96 a share, the cover is EUR 6.79, 80 percent of the price: the claim fails at the lowest peer multiple
      • At Nordex's 13.0x the cover is EUR 9.14, 108 percent
        • At our preferred 14x (the midpoint of Nordex, SMA Solar and Vestas) EUR 9.46, 111 percent
        • On the enterprise basis, at the sell-side's 10x EBIT the core is EUR 580 million and with the cash EUR 1,207 million, EUR 9.56 a share, 112 percent
      • Every cover above falls by about EUR 0.53 a share if the cash reaches the consensus trough of about EUR 560 million at September 2027
        • The downside is bounded only if the core deserves at least 13x and the losses stop within two years, which is a bound with a condition
    • Why we still hold our view, and in what form.
      • We do not reject the hypothesis
      • We hold a milder version of it.
      • The paid studies are not announced projects, and one of them did convert within the base rate's timeframe (Moeve, 300 MW, about EUR 150 million, March 2026)
      • The German RED III transposition of April 2026 and the Hydrogen Acceleration Act of February 2026 are law, not drafts
      • The company has cut its cost base and its capital plans rather than built a gigafactory, so its burn is about EUR 50 million a year, not Plug Power's
      • The chlor-alkali duopoly is the kind of business that earns 15 percent margins through a cycle
      • That is why the case is parked rather than rejected, and why it would progress at EUR 7.50, where the hypothesis's outcome is in the price, or on the first large order, where the hypothesis is disproved

  • Sized effects: the SOEC exit and the EUR 75 million cost programme are sized changes with dated effects: EUR 10 million a year of EBIT and about EUR 20 million a year of cash from FY2026/27

  • Regulatory tailwind: Germany transposed RED III in April 2026 and passed the Hydrogen Acceleration Act in February 2026

    • Not D, because the sized, dated effects above are in the numbers and the European regulatory tailwind is law.
  • What this means for the case: the inputs are the part of the chain the company controls, and they are moving

Demand: is end-market demand changing, structurally or cyclically (a swing with two-plus years of visibility)? (KQ 1, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = D
  • Rating = D, because end-market demand for green hydrogen equipment is in a cyclical trough with no visible two-year turn: final investment decisions are slipping by several quarters (Deutsche Bank, February 2026) and the global pipeline shrank by 10 million tonnes (IEA Global Hydrogen Review 2026).
  • Conversion rates: below 7 percent of green hydrogen projects reach a final investment decision
    • 10 to 15 percent of announced European projects convert to installations
    • Only 12 percent of projects scheduled for 2030 have offtake agreements and 1 percent binding contracts
    • 75 percent of the global pipeline, 240 GW, is unlikely to operate by 2030 (IEA).
  • Company markers: the active pipeline was pruned to 14 GW across 35 projects from a 57 GW total on 12 August 2026
    • Green hydrogen order intake was EUR 3 million in the quarter to June 2026 against EUR 13 million a year earlier, and EUR 184 million in the nine months because of the single 300 MW Moeve order of March 2026.
  • Structural demand: large on paper (RED III's 42 percent industrial sub-quota by 2030 needs over 24 GW of European electrolysis; India's mission targets up to 75 GW of cumulative demand), but the framework treats an inflection as cyclical until proven structural, and here the inflection has not begun.
  • Not C, because the leaked RED IV draft of September 2026 proposing non-binding targets and the Dutch 4 percent transposition show the policy floor under demand is itself moving down.

Competition: is competitive intensity easing or tightening? (KQ 2, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = D
  • Rating = D, because competitive intensity is tightening on price while loosening on the number of solvent Western rivals, and the price effect is the larger for the next two years.
  • Chinese pricing: Chinese alkaline systems are 20 to 40 percent cheaper than Western equivalents and Chinese prices fell over 50 percent to around RMB 1,300 per kW
    • China holds 60 percent of global electrolyser production capacity
    • Western pricing is deflating 15 to 20 percent on overcapacity
    • At least 60 percent of global manufacturing capacity will not be needed through the end of the decade (Redburn Atlantic).
  • Western consolidation: McPhy's assets were sold for EUR 0.6 million in July 2025
    • Nel idled Herøya and cut headcount 19 percent to about 300
    • Plug Power is funding itself through asset sales.
    • This favours the bankable supplier, and nucera's EUR 627 million of net cash at June 2026 is the strongest balance sheet among listed Western peers.
  • Not C, because the margin pool in the midstream (stack assembly) is already thin (green hydrogen EBIT margin about -12 percent on EUR 459 million of FY2024/25 sales) and the deflation lands on it before consolidation helps.
Macroeconomic: is a macro force acting on this company more than on the universe? (KQ 7, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = D
  • Rating = D, because the macro forces that matter here, the cost of capital for project developers and the price of renewable electricity (about 70 percent of the levelised cost of hydrogen), act on this company far more than on the universe, and both are adverse.

  • Not F, because the other side is live: if renewable power purchase prices fall faster than expected, project margins could trigger a wave of orders (Morgan Stanley).
