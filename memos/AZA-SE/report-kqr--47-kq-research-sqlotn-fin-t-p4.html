Section summary
  1. Avanza's earnings development over the next 18 months to four years is highly geared to structural savings-capital growth and strategic international expansion to offset cyclical net interest income headwinds.

This comprehensive analysis examines the specific inputs that will drive Avanza Bank Holding's earnings per share over the next 18 months to four years, focusing on savings capital growth, net interest income sensitivities, commission dynamics, operating cost trajectories, and capital structure constraints. The evidence base encompasses company disclosures through September 2026, broker research from Deutsche Bank, DNB Carnegie, Morgan Stanley, and Jefferies, alongside systematic peer comparisons with Nordnet, FinecoBank, AJ Bell, and flatexDEGIRO.

The Key Question can be answered with moderate confidence for near-term drivers but faces meaningful uncertainty around Danish expansion execution, Riksbank policy volatility, and retail trading normalization patterns beyond 2027. Avanza's savings capital base reached SEK 1,277 billion by September 2026, having expanded from SEK 1,079 billion at year-end 2025, demonstrating the platform's ability to capture disproportionate market inflows despite domestic market maturity.

The company captured 11.4% of Swedish market net inflows in 2025 while holding only 8.3% overall market share, revealing sustained competitive advantages in retail asset gathering that underpin structural growth trajectories. However, the ambitious 2030 target of SEK 2,000 billion in savings capital requiring 15% average annual growth presents execution challenges, with Deutsche Bank noting the target demands 5% from market gains, 3-4% from market inflows, and 6-7% from market-share wins, suggesting that achieving this benchmark depends critically on accelerated customer wallet consolidation rather than raw customer acquisition alone.

Avanza's net interest income exhibits pronounced asymmetrical gearing to Riksbank policy shifts, with a 1 percentage point rate cut reducing annual income by SEK 420 million while a 1 percentage point hike increases it by SEK 1,100 million, driven by the zero lower bound on customer deposit rates. This structural asymmetry creates meaningful near-term earnings volatility because the Riksbank policy rate trajectory shifted from 1.75% in September 2025 toward an expected 2.4% by late 2027, implying that monetary headwinds will transition back into tailwinds sooner than initially modeled. Broker assumptions on deposit beta pass-through drive substantial forecast variance, with DNB Carnegie projecting net interest income climbing from SEK 1,835 million in 2026 to SEK 2,042 million in 2027 based on stabilized STIBOR at 2.25%, while Morgan Stanley expresses caution regarding deposit mix shifts toward high-yielding accounts compressing margins even as rates stabilize. The divergence between optimistic volume-driven Deutsche Bank projections and cautious Morgan Stanley margin compression scenarios represents the single largest driver of earnings per share dispersion across the broker consensus for 2026 through 2028.

Commission income faces dual pressures from compressing brokerage yields that fell to 10.3 basis points in Q3 2026 from 11.0 basis points in Q2 2026 due to greater Private Banking and Pro customer contributions and shrinking fund margins that declined from 25.0 basis points in 2025 to 22.2 basis points in Q3 2026 as index fund share rose to 55.5%. Major drivers of earnings per share dispersion across broker estimates center on three critical assumptions. First, divergent views on deposit beta pass-through and net interest margin progression create variance in projected net interest income ranging from cautious Morgan Stanley compression scenarios to optimistic Deutsche Bank volume-driven expansion forecasts. Policy Rate Sensitivity and Balance Sheet

1.
Significant rate gearing: Avanza's updated interest rate sensitivity analysis shows a substantial asymmetry between rate cuts and rate hikes. The company's October 2026 report indicates that a 1 percentage point cut decreases annual net interest income by SEK 420 million, whereas a 1 percentage point hike increases it by SEK 1,100 million. This wide asymmetry exists because interest rates on customer accounts cannot fall below 0%. This dynamic means that any Riksbank monetary easing will disproportionately compress immediate platform profitability unless deposit volumes expand significantly.
2.
Shifting deposit mix: The composition of Avanza's customer deposit base is experiencing structural adjustments as risk appetite recovers. Deposits reached SEK 111.9 billion by September 2026, with the interest-bearing share rising slightly to 61% of total deposits. This leaves 39% of customer cash as non-interest-bearing transactional liquidity, which represents a highly profitable funding source. This mix shift means that net interest income remains vulnerable if retail savers continue moving idle cash into investment wrappers.
3.
Flexible lending rates: Avanza's internally financed lending portfolio consists of mortgages and margin loans that reprice at varying speeds. In its October 2026 report, management noted that mortgage rates are directly linked to the Riksbank policy rate, while margin lending interest depends on demand and competition. This pricing structure allows the bank to quickly pass through rate cuts on lending to maintain competitiveness. Thus, flexible lending assets will quickly adjust to falling market benchmarks while protecting volume growth.
4.
Surplus liquidity allocation: Avanza's treasury portfolio size stood at SEK 81.5 billion in September 2026, primarily positioned in highly liquid assets. The company's Q3 2026 report indicates these funds are deployed in covered bonds, Riksbank certificates, and overnight central bank deposits. Because the treasury portfolio's interest rate duration is kept extremely short and linked to three-month STIBOR, assets reprice almost instantly with the policy rate. Therefore, any sudden policy shifts will rapidly impact the treasury investment yield.
