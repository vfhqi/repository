Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The structural shift toward electric arc furnace steelmaking will fundamentally reshape European metal recycling dynamics and define recycler earnings power between 2027 and 2029.
  2. Differing capital allocation strategies, leverage profiles, and project execution capabilities will drive significant valuation and earnings per share divergence among regional peers.

Defining the critical questions

1.
Crucial inquiry: "How much extra ferrous scrap will the electric arc furnaces starting from 2027 to 2029 at voestalpine, SSAB, ArcelorMittal and Salzgitter need each year, and what share will come from scrap rather than direct reduced iron? How have European scrap prices and recyclers' processing spreads moved since the 1 July 2026 steel measure? With the aluminium scrap export ban not expected before 2027, what do brokers expect it and the May 2027 copper scrap rule to do to European prices and recycler margins? How is the Scholz integration tracking at Derichebourg, and how does Befesa fit this story?."
2.
Decarbonizing momentum: European steelmakers are rapidly shifting from blast furnaces to electric arc furnaces to meet stringent carbon reduction targets. This structural change directly drives multi-year volume opportunities for recyclers like Befesa by raising regional demand for steel dust. The timing and magnitude of this transition between 2027 and 2029 will determine whether recycler earnings per share experience a massive structural uplift.
3.
Disciplined spending: Recyclers are focusing heavily on capital discipline and debt reduction as they navigate volatile commodity markets. Derichebourg has committed to keeping its capital expenditure below fifty percent of EBITDA to protect its liquidity. Similarly, Befesa is transitioning into a low capital expenditure phase of under eighty million euros annually to prioritize shareholder returns.

Investment purity and risk profiles

4.
Regulatory tailwinds: Upcoming European scrap metal policies are projected to structurally restrict material leakage and lower local feedstock costs for regional processors. The implementation of the May 2027 copper rules and potential aluminium export bans will retain strategic scrap feedstocks within Europe. This regulatory shift will directly impact the refining margins and valuation multiples of advanced multi-metal recyclers like Aurubis.
5.
EU aluminium scrap export destinations in 2026 highlighting major trade leakage partners Valuation divergence: Investors face unique risk-reward profiles across European scrap plays depending on their leverage and project execution. Aurubis trades at balanced valuation levels but faces ongoing project execution risks from its delayed Richmond recycling smelter ramp-up. This complexity shifts investor focus toward stable cash generation and execution over speculative commodity upside.
6.
Contrary evidence: Heavy industrial recyclers remain highly exposed to volatile primary metal price normalizations and treatment charge fluctuations that can quickly erode processing spreads. Experts note that the steel dust recycling industry currently suffers from regional overcapacity in the United States, which makes near-term volume expansions highly dilutive. This risk is compounded if steelmakers accelerate direct reduced iron usage over scrap, lowering feedstock zinc content.
7.
Other side of the story: While the long-term transition to electric arc furnaces provides a compelling structural growth runway, the immediate earnings impact for recyclers may be delayed. Technical complexities, commercial hurdles in feedstock sourcing, and capital expenditure overruns could dilute projected returns on capital during the 2027 to 2029 window. Consequently, the scrap metal thesis could manifest as a slow, volatile margin recovery rather than an immediate step-change in cash generation.

Section 2 – Blast Furnace to EAF Transition and Feedstock Mix

Section summary
  1. Transition timelines for major European steelmakers show high confidence because voestalpine, SSAB, Salzgitter, and ArcelorMittal have published detailed, binding capital projects.
  2. Feedstock strategies diverge significantly as producers balance local scrap availability with direct reduced iron imports to secure raw material supplies.

Voestalpine greentec steel milestones

1.
Ambitious transition: Voestalpine plans to commission its new electric arc furnaces in Linz and Donawitz from 2027 onwards to replace older blast furnaces. The company is investing over one point five billion euros to establish these two climate-friendly units with a combined annual capacity of two point five million tonnes. This strategic initiative is projected to reduce the group's carbon emissions by approximately thirty percent by 2029.
2.
Prudent procurement: Voestalpine is avoiding direct investment in domestic direct reduced iron production, choosing instead to secure external feedstock. The steelmaker intends to rely on upstream suppliers to provide the necessary direct reduced iron and hot briquetted iron. This capital-light strategy enables the company to migrate its business downstream while limiting near-term transition capex requirements.
3.
Secure sourcing: Voestalpine relies on long-term supply agreements to guarantee its raw material requirements throughout the carbon transition. The company maintains a twenty percent equity stake in a Texas hot briquetted iron facility to secure high-quality feedstock. This agreement ensures the annual supply of four hundred twenty thousand tonnes of hot briquetted iron to its Austrian mills.
4.
Volatile raw materials: Voestalpine recognizes that transitioning process routes from blast furnaces to electric arc furnaces is accompanied by a sharp increase in scrap demand. The company faces growing demand for scrap, special metals, and alloys, which could potentially lead to production delays or quality risks if bottlenecks occur. Price volatility associated with these raw materials makes planning difficult and reduces overall investment security.

Section 3 – Impact of the July 2026 Steel Measure on Spreads

Section summary
  1. The implementation of the revised European Union steel safeguard measures has successfully widened European steel processing spreads by restricting cheap imports.
  2. Regional recyclers are experiencing mixed margin effects as domestic scrap retention lowers local raw material costs but soft underlying demand caps volume growth.

Safeguards reshaping steel pricing

1.
Stronger regional pricing: The implementation of the revised European Union steel safeguard measures on July 1, 2026, has successfully curtailed low-cost steel imports. This regulatory protection drove Northern European hot-rolled coil steel prices up by seventy-one euros per tonne since late June to reach seven hundred thirty-five to seven hundred fifty euros per tonne in late October 2026. European steelmakers are experiencing expanding regional operating margins because the tariff quotas have reduced foreign competition.
2.
Contrary evidence: Weak downstream demand continues to plague European industrial sectors like construction and automotive, which limits the volume growth of domestic mills. This persistent industrial caution has prevented the firmer pricing environment from triggering a broad-based recovery in regional crude steel production. Consequently, recyclers face severely constrained volume intake despite nominal tariff protections.
3.
Orderly market entry: The European Commission's release of country-specific quota allocations has established a highly structured raw material trade environment. This country-specific framework is considered a significant game-changer for the European steel sector by preventing early-quarter import surges. By late October 2026, Turkey had completely exhausted its quarterly quotas for hot-rolled sheet, rebar, and wire rod, while India exhausted its hot-rolled sheet and plate quotas. This disciplined import allocation supports sustained regional pricing power.

Ferrous scrap and spread dynamics

4.
Widening processing spreads: The combination of trade safeguards and falling raw material costs has expanded the European electric arc furnace steel spreads. The benchmark German hot-rolled coil to scrap spread climbed to eight hundred thirty-three dollars per tonne in mid-October 2026. This robust spread environment improves regional processing margins for integrated metal recyclers who supply domestic mills.
5.
Diverging scrap prices: European and domestic United States ferrous scrap markets have decoupled significantly since the implementation of the new trade rules. European scrap prices receded by nine dollars per tonne to three hundred thirty to three hundred forty-five dollars per tonne in late October 2026. Meanwhile, United States domestic scrap prices rose by twenty-five dollars per tonne to three hundred seventy to three hundred eighty dollars per tonne. This pricing divergence lowers local raw material costs for European electric arc furnace operators.
6.
Increasing scrap utilization: European steel producers are accelerating the incorporation of recycled metals into their production processes to meet environmental targets. The use of scrap has structurally increased at the expense of primary production based on crude iron. This transition provides sticky volume demand for regional recyclers.

Peer comparisons and operational impacts

7.
Aperam recycling outperformance: Stainless steel recycler Aperam has demonstrated resilient financial performance through its integrated recycling segment. The Recycling and Renewables division posted sales of four hundred ninety-eight million euros in the second quarter of 2026, representing a fifteen point five percent increase quarter-over-quarter. Segment adjusted EBITDA rose to thirty-two million euros due to higher scrap prices and positive valuation effects. This highlights the robust cash-generative power of integrated recycling assets.
8.
Derichebourg revenue scale: Industrial recycler Derichebourg has maintained a massive market presence ahead of its upcoming corporate transformations. The company reported a rolling twelve-month recycling revenue of five thousand one hundred eighty-eight point one million euros up to June 2026. Ferrous metals contributed two thousand one hundred forty point nine million euros, while non-ferrous metals generated two thousand eight hundred forty-three point eight million euros. This scale positions the company to capture substantial market share as European scrap demand intensifies.
9.
Historically comparable margins: Historical scrap prices provide a baseline for assessing the current post- measure margin environment. In the first half of 2026, European domestic scrap of grade E3 averaged three hundred seventy-one dollars per tonne, which was fourteen dollars lower than the export price of North Europe origin scrap. During 2025, the average price for European domestic scrap of grade E3 was three hundred fiftythree dollars per tonne. This comparison reveals that domestic scrap remains cheaper than exportdestined material, shielding regional processors from global price spikes.

Diverging views on long-term availability

10.
Fragmented supply networks: Brokers hold differing views on whether regional scrap collection networks can support the long-term raw material demands of transitioning steelmakers. While tighter trade safeguards and CBAM keep material within Europe, local sorting and processing capacity remains a critical structural bottleneck. Restricting export outlets before expanding advanced local processing capabilities could depress lowgrade scrap prices and weaken collection incentives.
11.
Sustained price optimism: Several analysts expect that the combination of trade protections and environmental regulations will support high scrap prices over the next three years. Tighter import controls are expected to sustain high regional operating rates, which will incentivize steelmakers to pay premium prices for clean feedstock. This structural shift will permanently elevate recycler processing spreads over the multi-year transition period.

Other side of the story: While the July 2026 steel measures have successfully boosted regional steel spreads and protected local markets, they expose recyclers to severe regulatory and demand risks. If underlying European steel demand remains weak, domestic mills may restart idle blast furnaces rather than accelerate their transition to scrap-intensive electric arc furnaces.

Section 4 – Non-Ferrous Rules: Aluminium Export Ban and Copper Scrap Rule

Section summary
  1. The impending European Waste Shipment Regulation scheduled for May 2027 will restrict non-ferrous scrap exports to non-OECD nations and retain strategic secondary resources within Europe.
  2. Increased regional availability of aluminium and copper scrap is projected to lower local feedstock costs and support operating margin recoveries for advanced European processors.

Regulatory frameworks and export bans

1.
Tightening export controls: European scrap markets face historic regulatory shifts as waste shipment rules prohibit non-hazardous waste exports to non-OECD countries from May 21, 2027. This policy shift replaces the previously considered fifteen percent aluminium export levy after the European Commission concluded that trade measures would capture only half of exports. Consequently, advanced regional processors expect enhanced domestic scrap feedstock security as trade pathways to major Asian importers are legally restricted.
2.
Accelerating metal retention: European aluminium scrap exports reached approximately one point two seven million tonnes in 2025 and are annualizing at a record one point three three million tonnes in 2026. Non- OECD markets like India, Thailand, and China have historically absorbed seventy-seven percent of these strategic outflows. Retaining these volumes within Europe is expected to permanently widen domestic scrap spreads and support higher utilization rates for local metal refiners.
3.
Targeting recycling benchmarks: The European Union Critical Raw Materials Act establishes a clear strategic framework that supports secondary metal processing. Under this regulation, the region aims to expand its recycling capacity to meet twenty-five percent of strategic raw material consumption by 2030. This benchmark provides a strong regulatory growth mandate for established multi-metal processors like Aurubis and Befesa who process complex waste streams.
4.
Restricting copper leakage: European copper scrap outflows face similar regulatory constraints under the Waste Shipment Regulation starting in May 2027. The European Union previously implemented a customs surveillance system in July 2025 to track strategic scrap flows and build a legal basis for subsequent trade interventions. These cumulative measures will act as a major structural brake on outflows because major importers like China have not yet applied for authorized non-OECD status.

Local pricing and recycler margins

5.
Improving secondary margins: Befesa stands to capture meaningful profitability tailwinds in its secondary aluminium segment from the upcoming regulatory restrictions. Greater domestic metal availability is projected to lower raw material feedstock costs and support a recovery in secondary aluminium metal margins. While secondary aluminium historically represents ten percent of normalized group EBITDA, this recovery provides an incremental earnings tailwind as regional metal spreads normalize.
6.
Expanding recycling capacity: Norsk Hydro is heavily positioning its European operations to capitalize on tighter regional scrap availability. The company is scaling its post-consumer scrap usage as a core pillar of its growth strategy to target a material uplift in recycling EBITDA to five to six billion Norwegian kroner by 2030. Better local scrap access should support higher recycling plant utilization and help insulate the company from volatile primary metal import dynamics.
7.
Commissioning growth projects: Befesa has successfully completed the commissioning of its Bernburg secondary aluminium expansion in September 2026. This growth project is designed to enhance Befesa's recycling efficiency and lower operating costs as the plant enters its operational ramp-up phase. Capital expenditure for the expansion was front-end loaded within the seventy million euros group budget, allowing Befesa to transition into a high cash-generative low-capex cycle.
8.
Securing copper supply: Aurubis remains highly levered to domestic copper scrap availability as global raw material markets experience persistent supply chain fragmentation. The company has achieved robust operating EBT of three hundred seventy-four million euros in the first nine months of fiscal year 2025/2026 due to stronger recycling and product business contributions. Tighter European export rules are projected to protect Aurubis from spot concentrate volatility by ensuring steady secondary scrap feedstocks for its European smelters.
9.
Sustaining industrial scale: Derichebourg maintains significant market exposure to non-ferrous metal recycling with nine hundred forty-six thousand metric tons sold on a rolling twelve-month basis to June 2026. The nonferrous segment generated two thousand eight hundred forty-three point eight million euros in revenue, representing the largest portion of the group's recycling sales. Rising copper and aluminium prices have boosted unit processing margins because the group effectively manages working capital requirements and controls scrap processing costs.

Friction and implementation bottlenecks

10.
Contrary evidence: European recyclers face severe sorting bottlenecks that could limit the near-term benefits of the proposed non-ferrous export restrictions. European recyclers currently lack the sophisticated processing capacity required to absorb all the low-grade and mixed automotive scrap that is typically shipped to Asia. Restricting export outlets before expanding local separation capacity could depress low-grade domestic prices, which would weaken collection incentives and disrupt established recycling networks.
11.
Exploiting regulatory loopholes: Advanced industrial processors warn that the regulatory distinction between waste and product could dilute the effectiveness of upcoming export bans. Cleaner, high-grade aluminium scrap including profiles, wire, and wheels is more easily classified as end-of-waste under the new rules. This classification allows exporters to continue shipping premium scrap abroad, which potentially leaves European recyclers with only lower-grade feedstocks that require expensive domestic pre-treatment.
12.
Slowing regulatory implementation: Global recycling associations are actively lobbying the European Commission to delay or amend the proposed Waste Shipment Regulation restrictions. The Bureau of International Recycling called on October 19, 2026, for policymakers to reconsider the export bans and extend the public consultation period. Recyclers argue that premature restrictions will distort regional metal balances unless the European Union simultaneously provides capital subsidies to build advanced local processing infrastructure.
13.
Assessing regulatory outcomes: The non-ferrous scrap rules represent a highly likely but slow-burning earnings driver that will not fully materialize until domestic processing capacity expands after 2027. While Befesa and Aurubis will benefit from lower domestic scrap acquisition costs, the near-term positive earnings impact remains capped by technical sorting constraints and high energy prices. Consequently, the regulation is unlikely to trigger immediate EPS upgrades because operational friction will persist during the initial transition phase.
14.
Other side of the story: European metal exporters could experience significant margin dilution if broad restrictions on non-ferrous scrap exports create localized oversupply. If domestic secondary smelters cannot economically absorb this excess material, local scrap values will collapse and discourage independent waste collection networks. Furthermore, major non-EU producers might find loopholes by routing material through authorized OECD candidate nations, thereby undercutting European primary metal producers while maintaining high global market pricing.

This investment strengthens Aurubis's competitive position as European waste shipment regulations restrict scrap exports.

In contrast, Befesa offers a derisked post-capex cash cycle with clearer deleveraging milestones and immediate potential for enhanced shareholder returns.

Section 7 – If the Answer Differs from the Common View

Section summary
  1. Divergence from expected steel scrap policies will severely impact regional recycler margins.
  2. Technological and infrastructure bottlenecks could force steelmakers to abandon planned carbon transitions. Net read: Geopolitical lobbying and legal loopholes threaten to weaken non-ferrous scrap export restrictions.

Technological and feedstock substitution risks

1.
Accelerated transition: Accelerated direct reduced iron adoption could significantly displace premium scrap demand in Europe over the next decade. If European steelmakers resolve natural gas bottlenecks, they can scale direct reduced iron modules directly next to their electric arc furnaces. Consequently, regional recyclers would face diminished demand for premium ferrous scrap, leading to severe downward pressure on processing spreads.
2.
Severe shortages: Severe shortages of high-grade iron ore pellets represent a critical bottleneck that could derail the planned direct reduced iron transition in Europe. The direct reduction process requires DR-grade ore with an iron content of at least 67% to ensure economic viability. Currently, this premium feedstock represents only 4% of the global seaborne iron ore trade, meaning that supply remains incredibly scarce. If steelmakers cannot secure these scarce raw materials, they will be forced to return to scrap-intensive furnace operations to maintain production.
3.
Excessive power: Huge green electricity requirements could make hydrogen-based steelmaking commercially unviable across most of Europe. Converting high-quality iron ore into green sponge iron requires close to four terawatt-hours of electricity per million tonnes. Because most European steel mills operate in regions with severely constrained renewable power, a persistent electricity deficit remains a major threat. This infrastructure gap would force mills to abandon direct reduced iron plans and remain highly dependent on traditional recycled scrap units.

Regulatory friction and trade policy delays

5.
Prolonged regulatory: Prolonged regulatory delays in implementing the European Waste Shipment Regulation would keep global trade pathways wide open. Exporters warn that premature restrictions will disrupt international trade and distort regional metal balances. Any delay beyond 2027 would prevent the anticipated local scrap price deflation, keeping raw material costs elevated for domestic recyclers.
6.
Severe legal: Severe legal challenges from prominent European recycling associations threaten to dilute the updated waste export framework. The German metal recycling groups BVSE and CMA have obtained legal opinions showing that the European Commission's export limits contain critical legal errors. These groups argue that the proposed restrictions exceed the legal limits of the Waste Shipment Regulation. If these legal actions succeed, the European Union may be forced to water down export restrictions, allowing strategic metals to leak out of the region.
7.
Sophisticated loopholes: Sophisticated end-of-waste classification loopholes could allow high-grade scrap to bypass the new export bans entirely. Exporters can easily classify cleaner, premium-grade aluminium scrap like profiles, wire, and wheels as end-of-waste products. This legal designation allows these valuable materials to be treated as standard trade products, exempting them from waste shipment rules. Consequently, European recyclers would lose access to premium feedstock, leaving them with only lowergrade waste.
8.
Insufficient domestic: Insufficient domestic processing capacity could lead to stranded lower-grade scrap and collapse regional collection incentives. Europe currently lacks the advanced sorting and separation infrastructure required to absorb all the mixed automotive scrap that is typically exported to Asia. Restricting export outlets faster than local recycling capacity expands will cause a domestic oversupply of low-quality waste. This market imbalance will depress low-grade domestic prices and severely weaken the commercial incentive for independent waste networks to collect material.

Market disruption and key disruptor incentives

9.
Major automotive: Major automotive manufacturers have strong commercial incentives to resist paying premiums for green steel certifications. Historical transactions show that original equipment manufacturers remain highly reluctant to sign contracts containing significant green price premiums. If automotive companies continue to refuse green steel premiums, steelmakers will struggle to recoup their multi-billion euro electric arc furnace investment costs.
10.
Powerful international: Powerful international trade partners have the political leverage to disrupt European scrap retention strategies. Indian metal recyclers are actively lobbying European policymakers, warning that proposed export restrictions will disrupt trade relations and weaken circularity. These trade representatives are raising concerns regarding the impact of these rules on the upcoming India-EU Free Trade Agreement. Such geopolitical pressure could force European regulators to grant extensive export exemptions to major non-OECD partners.
11.
Integrated domestic: Integrated domestic steel giants possess the market power to manipulate local scrap prices and squeeze independent processors. Large electric arc furnace producers continue to acquire regional scrap companies to secure absolute control over the secondary raw material supply chain. This growing vertical integration allows major steelmakers to directly influence scrap purchasing volumes and pricing structures. Independent recyclers could see their processing spreads severely compressed if consolidated mills use their buying power to manipulate the local market.
12.
Evolving trade: Evolving trade diversion from major Asian economies could flood the European market and disrupt regional pricing. China has been actively sourcing global aluminium scrap, which has driven up Rotterdam premiums and disrupted European secondary capacity. However, if Chinese import policies change, massive volumes of diverted scrap could suddenly flow back into alternative OECD markets. This sudden trade diversion would undermine local scrap values and create intense margin volatility for European processors.
