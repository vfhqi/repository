[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = C

Thesis. Bank of Ireland is a capital-return case with a contracted earnings tailwind, in a consolidated home market, bought at a price that already pays for most of it. A structural hedge rolling from swaps written near 1.14 percent into swaps near 2.79 percent, a cost base held near 2.2 EUR billions and a fifth year of buybacks probably lift earnings per share from 1.63 EUR in FY2026 to about 2.12 EUR in FY2028, but at 18.11 EUR the shares stand at 8.7 times 24-month-forward earnings, the 86th percentile of their own five-year range, and our 'Modal return' is 9 to 10 percent a year against the framework's 20 percent bar. The bank probably earns what the market expects, and the market has already paid for it.

  • The change that drives the thesis is internal and mechanical, and it is already in the financial statements.
    • The structural hedge of about 70 to 77 EUR billions rolls off swaps written near 1.14 percent into swaps averaging about 2.79 percent, and management guides net interest income from c. 3.5 EUR billions in FY2026 to 3.75 EUR billions in FY2027 and above 3.95 EUR billions in FY2028.
    • This is contracted arithmetic on a book the company enlarged by 8 EUR billions in July 2026, not a hope about demand.
    • Net interest income troughed in the second half of 2025 after falling from 3,707 EUR millions in FY2023 to 3,371 EUR millions in FY2025
      • The H1 2026 figure of 1,705 EUR millions is flat on the half and the turn is guided for three years.
    • Beside the hedge sit two further internal forces that the framework's screen rates strong: guidance raised on four lines at the H1 2026 results on 31 July 2026, and a 530 EUR millions buyback with the interim dividend up 56 percent to 39c.
  • The external forces set the ceiling, not the path.
    • The consolidation that made the Irish market a three-player system is three years old, the housing deficit is structural but slow, and the forces that are new (a low-cost owner for Permanent TSB, Revolut at 3.4 million Irish customers, regulatory scrutiny of mortgage pricing) point against the deposit franchise rather than for it.
    • So 'Change Forces' (E 1, BB 1) is B on the strength of the internal forces, with the external forces at C.
  • 'Financial Outputs' (E 2, BB 1) are improving at the pace of the framework's target band, and the sell-side has already written most of it down.
    • H1 2026 total income rose 6.7 percent to 2,178 EUR millions, statutory return on tangible equity reached 14.4 percent, organic capital generation ran at 135 basis points in the half and the cost-to-income ratio fell to 50.5 percent on the company's basis.
    • Consensus earnings per share of 1.63 / 1.83 / 2.15 EUR for FY2026 / 2027 / 2028 grow 12 to 17 percent a year, inside the band
      • The FY2026 figure has risen 10.2 percent in twelve months.
    • The upgrade that mattered, net interest income to 3.5 EUR billions for FY2026, "aligns precisely" with consensus on Deutsche Bank's reading, so the upgrades left are the small ones: the cost of risk, the buyback, fees and the ECB's rate path.
  • The change has travelled all six layers of the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain, which is the thesis's strength and the price's problem.
    • It shows in the company's choices (the hedge addition of July 2026; the exit from US Leveraged Acquisition Finance decided in February 2026), its key operating metrics (statutory return on tangible equity 14.4 percent in H1 2026), its financial statements (profit before tax 960 EUR millions, up 33 percent and 15 percent above consensus) and the sell-side's estimates.
    • There is no layer left for the market to discover.
  • What we think it earns: earnings per share of 1.63 EUR in FY2026 rising to 1.83 EUR in FY2027, 2.12 EUR in FY2028 and 2.28 EUR in FY2029, about 11.8 percent a year to FY2029, slowing to 6 to 8 percent a year once the hedge roll is done.
    • Our path is our own estimate, since no Viewforth model exists, and it sits at consensus for FY2026 and FY2027.
    • Of that growth, income growth and the falling cost ratio are worth about 8.5 percent a year together and the falling share count about 3.3 percent
      • The lines below pre-provision profit subtract about 0.3 percent a year as the cost of risk normalises from a record 8 basis points.
  • What it costs is the point: 18.11 EUR is 8.7 times 24-month-forward consensus earnings of 2.0924 EUR, the 86th percentile of the stock's own five-year range (median 6.78 times) and the 72nd percentile of its ten-year range (median 7.00 times), against 10.0 times for AIB.
    • The shares are cheap against the closest peer and dear against their own history, and the discount to AIB widened from 3 percent to 15 percent over the past year for reasons the research names: a cost-to-income ratio of 52 percent against 44, a lower hedge yield, and a UK book in retreat.
  • The return therefore does not clear the bar on the modal path.
    • Summing the three components once each on a blended 24-month-forward multiple of 8.5 times, the 'Modal return' (E 3, BB 1) is 9.3 percent a year over eighteen months and 10.0 percent a year over three years: a cash return of about 3.3 to 4.3 percent a year, forward-earnings growth of 7 to 8 percent a year, and a re-rating of about minus 1 percent a year.
    • The 20 percent bar is cleared only on the branch in which the European bank multiple holds through the current credit-and-sovereign episode and Bank of Ireland closes its discount to AIB's 10.0 times, two conditions we put at plausible and possible, about one in four together
      • Or if the price falls to about 15.75 EUR.
    • A plausibly worse drop-through from pre-provision profit to earnings per share (a cost of risk of 30 basis points, non-core items of 200 EUR millions a year, buybacks of 400 EUR millions a year) takes the return to 2.2 percent a year over eighteen months and 6.3 percent over three years, an F at both horizons: the case has no margin below the pre-provision line.
  • The pattern it fits is 'Compounder', at a C.
    • A proven upgrader in a quality franchise whose remaining upgrades sit in lines the sell-side models cautiously, each worth 1 to 3 percent of earnings, while the line that carries the case is modelled exactly as guided.
    • The framework's own guidance settles it: a correctly modelled high-quality business is a C, not a D.
  • Where the chain holds and where it breaks.
    • It holds from the hedge to the sell-side's estimates: every link is guided in a number, delivered on the record of the last two years and visible in the accounts.
    • It breaks at the price: no component of the return is unrecognised by the market, and the one driver that could make the return sufficient, the multiple, is at the top of the stock's own range in a sector the Assistant Portfolio Manager moved from Overweight to Neutral this week.

  • The case therefore is a sound business case and an insufficient investment case at 18.11 EUR: material to earnings, immaterial to the return, and a park with two named re-entry triggers rather than a progression.

  • Rating = C, because the change is material to earnings and immaterial to the return at this price: strong, dated internal forces ('Change Forces' at B) drive outputs improving at the pace of the target band ('Financial Outputs' at B), but the chain's last link, a 'Modal return' of 9 to 10 percent a year (E 3 at D), does not clear the 20 percent bar, and this Building Block measures materiality to the investor, which includes what it costs.

  • How the parts combine: the return binds.
    • The first two Elements say the thesis is real and is happening
    • The third says the market has paid for it.
    • A C rather than a D because the earnings growth is probable and visible and the cash return is contractual in all but name
    • A C rather than a B because no component of the return is unrecognised by the price.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The case against, at its strongest: the H1 2026 beat was built on things that do not recur, and behind it the bank is a rate-cycle beneficiary at a plateau, bought at the top of its own range in the week its sector lost its Overweight.
      • This is the reading the risks research calls a 'false friend'.
      • The 15 percent profit-before-tax beat rested on impairment write-backs, a cost of risk of 8 basis points against a normal 20 to 25, 30 EUR millions of insurance benefit in the impairment line, and a 39c interim dividend 26 percent above consensus
        • None of these is a run rate.
      • Behind the beat the net interest margin is 2.68 percent, down from 3.03 percent in 2023, and the structural hedge yields 1.9 percent on 66 EUR billions against AIB's 2.4 percent on 75 EUR billions, a drag of 12 to 20 percent on reported net interest income.
      • The deposit franchise is about to be arbitraged by licensed neobanks with local IBANs and savings yields of 3.5 percent
        • The digital offering is "a poor second" to AIB after 1.4 EUR billions of spend
        • The UK book is retreating with new lending down 22 percent
        • And the UK motor finance tail is put by RBC at up to 950 EUR millions against 429 EUR millions provided.
      • All of it has been bought at the 86th percentile of the stock's own five-year range, with Deutsche Bank removing the stock from its top picks on 8 September 2026.
        • In this reading the forward estimates are right and the price is wrong, and the price corrects first through the multiple when the rate cycle or the credit cycle turns, as Morgan Stanley's bear value of 10.50 EUR (a return on tangible equity of 12 percent at 0.8 times tangible book) describes.
    • What we concede: that the H1 2026 cost of risk is not a run rate, and our path doubles it by FY2026 and triples it by FY2029
      • That the net interest income guidance is in consensus and no upgrade remains on that line
      • That the digital lag and the state pay cap are real and slow to fix
      • That the multiple is at the top of its own range and the sector's momentum has turned
      • And that the return at the modal multiple does not clear the bar, which is the point on which the competing hypothesis and our view agree.
    • Why we still hold our view of the business, and why it does not change the recommendation: the hedge drag the hypothesis cites is the same roll that lifts net interest income by 13 percent over two years, so it is the mechanism of the case rather than a weakness of it.
      • The deposit arbitrage it fears has been available for two years and the measured flow from current accounts to term halved to 0.5 EUR billions in H1 2026.
      • Mortgages, the profit pool, remain a product bought face to face in which the company writes 40 percent of new flows.
      • A 2.0 percent non-performing-exposure book with rising collateral values is not where a 42 percent drawdown starts, and a capital buffer of 410 basis points above requirement (13.7 percent fully loaded in the adverse stress) means the bear case is a de-rating, not a solvency event.
      • We therefore rate the operating risk low to medium, as the risks research itself does, and the price risk high.
        • Our recommendation rests on the second, which is where the competing hypothesis is right.
Change Forces: are the input change forces sufficiently strong? (E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because the force that matters, the mechanical repricing of a structural hedge of about 70 EUR billions plus a capital return running near 8 percent of the market value a year, is strong, dated and already in the financial statements
    • The external picture is mixed, with the big tailwind (the KBC and NatWest exits) three years old and the newest forces (BAWAG at Permanent TSB, Revolut) pointing the other way.
  • How the parts combine: the Element sits above its external child (C) because the internal forces (B) are the ones that move the numbers over the horizon
    • The external forces set the ceiling on what the franchise can earn, not the path it takes to FY2028.
  • Not A, because none of the forces is a break with the past: the chief executive, the strategy and the capital policy are continuations of the 2023-25 cycle, and the 2026 guidance upgrades are the rate environment being passed through a company that guides low.

The case in 8 to 10 sentences (the thesis as tested), kept in full at both ends of the record (Part 1, E 3, BB 4)
  • Bank of Ireland is a capital-return case with a contracted earnings tailwind, a 'Compounder' pattern at a C fit, in a business whose quality is good rather than great and whose market is as kind as European retail banking gets.
  • The change is mechanical: a structural hedge of about 70 to 77 EUR billions rolls off swaps written near 1.14 percent into swaps near 2.79 percent, and management guides net interest income from c. 3.5 EUR billions in FY2026 to 3.75 EUR billions in FY2027 and above 3.95 EUR billions in FY2028 while holding costs near 2.2 EUR billions and returning a 50 percent payout plus a buyback of about 3 percent of the shares a year.
  • The change has travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain: it is in the company's choices (the 8 EUR billions hedge addition of July 2026; the US exit of February 2026), in its key metrics (a statutory return on tangible equity of 14.4 percent in H1 2026 from 11.1 percent a year earlier), in its financial statements (profit before tax of 960 EUR millions, up 33 percent) and in the sell-side's estimates (FY2026 consensus earnings per share up 10 percent in twelve months to 1.63 EUR).
  • The business case is compelling: the earnings path to FY2028 is probable, the cash return is reliable and the 'Foundations' (a triopoly, the stickiest deposits in the euro area, a management that guides low) are robust.
  • The investment case at 18.11 EUR is not: the shares stand at 8.7 times 24-month-forward earnings, the 86th percentile of their own five-year range, and on our modal path the return is 9 to 10 percent a year against a 20 percent bar, carried by the dividend and by 7 to 8 percent a year of forward-earnings growth, with the re-rating slightly negative.
  • A plausibly worse drop-through from pre-provision profit to earnings per share turns that thin D into a clear F, so the case has no margin below the pre-provision line.
  • The 20 percent bar is cleared only if the sector's multiple holds and the discount to AIB closes, about one in four together, or if the price falls to about 15.75 EUR.
  • A reader could put it to a colleague in one sentence: the bank probably earns what the market expects, and the market has already paid for it.
