Section 4 – Tax Treatment and the Swedish Participation Exemption

Section summary
  1. The Swedish participation exemption will likely shield the Speria divestment from capital gains tax at the parent level.
  2. Low operational overlap and the standalone Speria brand will minimize separation and Transitional Service Agreement costs.

Swedish Participation Exemption Applicability

1.
Corporate tax exemption: Munters Group AB is structured as a Swedish public limited liability company with its registered corporate office in Stockholm. Under Swedish tax legislation, capital gains on the sale of unlisted shares in subsidiaries are generally tax-free under the Swedish participation exemption rules (Sw. näringsbetingade andelar). Because Munters' holdings in Speria consist of unlisted corporate shares, the transaction at the parent level is highly likely to avoid Swedish corporate income tax. This tax-exempt status protects the net cash proceeds available for strategic reinvestment into core industrial cooling segments.
2.
Subsidiary shareholding conditions: The participation exemption applies to unlisted shares held for business purposes by a Swedish parent company. Munters Group AB holds direct and indirect ownership stakes in various agricultural technology entities, including 100% of MTech Systems. Since these entities are held as long-term business-related assets, they meet the holding requirements for tax-exempt capital gains. This qualification means that Swedish exit taxes will not apply to latent gains within these subsidiary holdings.
3.
Swedish parent holdings: Swedish corporate structures facilitate tax-efficient internal reorganizations ahead of major divestments. Munters Group AB manages its subsidiaries through intermediate holding companies, such as Munters Holding AB. Standardizing these holdings under a unified entity ensures they remain qualified participations for tax-free treatment. This corporate positioning ensures that the eventual transaction does not trigger immediate parent-level capital gains taxes.
4.
Capital distribution rules: Swedish parent companies benefit from favorable rules when distributing tax-exempt capital gains to shareholders. The lack of exit tax on unlisted holdings allows Munters to maximize the net cash returned or reinvested. This tax flexibility directly supports the board's capital allocation targets and debt repayment strategies. Consequently, the Swedish regulatory framework provides a distinct financial advantage compared to corporate tax regimes in other European jurisdictions.
5.
Foreign asset tax risks: Divestments of global assets can still trigger localized tax liabilities in foreign jurisdictions where operations are physically located. Munters' digital agricultural business operates globally with entities in the United States, Brazil, and Europe. Local asset transfers or legal entity restructurings in these foreign countries may incur local capital gains taxes or transfer duties. These local tax frictions could slightly reduce the total net proceeds received by the group.

6.

Munters reported that the Equipment divestment resulted in external sales costs of MSEK 17. Furthermore, the discontinued operations statement for that period showed a localized tax expense of MSEK 148. This financial precedent proves that even with parent-level exemptions, multi-jurisdictional asset sales face notable localized tax and transaction leakages.
7.

The 2025 transaction was initially estimated to result in a capital loss of approximately SEK 0.5bn on a GAAP basis. 8.

9.
Contrary evidence: Localized asset transfers can trigger substantial taxes that override the Swedish parent-level tax exemption. During the 2025 FoodTech Equipment divestment, Munters incurred a tax expense of MSEK 148 despite realizing a capital loss of MSEK 40. This indicates that foreign assetlevel tax liabilities can be highly material, which means the upcoming Speria sale may face unexpected tax headwinds in jurisdictions like the United States and Brazil.
