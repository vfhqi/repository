Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Finnish wellbeing services counties are moving slowly on private outsourcing due to budget-balancing delays, restricting short-term volume growth for private care operators.
  2. Private care providers remain significantly more cost-effective than public operations, which should drive long-term outsourcing despite near-term county in-sourcing.
1.
Critical inquiry: "Since Finland's reform of 1 January 2026 allowed the wellbeing services counties to move elderly and disability care from public to private providers, how much volume have the counties actually put out to private operators, at what prices against their own cost, and how much of it do broker forecasts for Attendo, Ambea and Humana AB already include for 2027 and 2028? Alongside it, how do the 2026 and 2027 municipal price indexations in Finland and Sweden compare with the collective wage rises for care staff, and are the operators keeping the saving from the lower Finnish staffing ratio of 0.60 per resident or giving it back in contract renegotiations?"

Sector Revaluation and EPS Drivers

2.

DNB Carnegie argues in its note on 8 October 2026 that the sector has transitioned from a period of weak profitability and tight staffing regulations into a phase of stable, predictable earnings growth. This massive expansion in share prices means that any disruption in municipal pricing indexation or volume growth will severely threaten future earnings per share projections. Consequently, as DNB Carnegie suggests in its note on 10 March 2026, the sector is no longer priced for distress, making the resolution of reform volumes crucial.
3.
Crucial margins: Attendo's financial trajectory is heavily dependent on Finland, which represents roughly 60% of group sales. DNB Carnegie notes in its report on 8 October 2026 that Attendo's adjusted EPS is expected to reach SEK 7.56 in 2026 and grow to SEK 8.77 in 2027. However, because Finland's wellbeing counties are under intense financial pressure, any failure to capture incremental reform volumes or retain staffing ratio savings could suppress the projected margin expansion. Unless Attendo can sustain its Finnish operating efficiency, DNB Carnegie's report on 8 October 2026 warns that its target price of SEK 135 will face downward revisions.
4.
Capital allocation: Ambea is prioritizing top-line expansion and capital allocation, having launched a public takeover offer for its competitor Humana in June 2026. Ambea's annual report of 31 March 2026 stated that its customer diversification across 470 municipalities balances policy risk, but personnel costs remain its largest expense. DNB Carnegie notes in its report of 19 August 2025 that Ambea's leverage of 2.3x lease-adjusted net debt to EBITDA allows for aggressive consolidation. If the integration of Humana fails to yield the target SEK 120m in synergies as noted by DNB Carnegie on 29 June 2026, combined EPS growth for 2027 and 2028 will fall below expectations.
5.
Valuation discount: Humana AB historically traded at a 40% valuation discount to peers due to volatile earnings and client outflows in personal assistance. DNB Carnegie reported on 29 June 2026 that Ambea's bid valued Humana at approximately 10x 2026e EV/EBITAaL, which was below Attendo's multiple of 12.9x. Therefore, understanding whether the pricing indexation is fixed for 2027 is vital to determine if Humana's assets will drag down Ambea's consolidated EPS.

Research sample: This comprehensive investigation analyzed a total of seven key companies, more than thirty distinct investment research documents, and three historic comparable past cases. The primary focus centers on Attendo, Ambea, and Humana AB, alongside regional peers such as Terveystalo. This indicates that there is substantial long-term scope for further outsourcing as counties look for ways to reduce their structural deficits.

Public versus Private Production Pricing

5.

A study by the industry organization HALI in December 2024 revealed that public production is 21% more expensive than private purchased services. Specifically, the average cost of public care is EUR 206 per day compared to only EUR 170 per day for private services, representing a major saving.
6.
Extreme regional disparities: The price difference between public production and private purchases is especially high in major urban welfare areas like Helsinki and North Ostrobothnia. A regional pricing study published in December 2024 showed that Helsinki's public care costs EUR 224 per day while purchased private care is only EUR 168 per day. This large EUR 56 daily gap means that financially strained counties have a powerful incentive to outsource to private operators to balance budgets.
7.
Sweden pricing comparison: Private operators also demonstrate dramatic cost advantages in Swedish elderly care compared to public sector facilities. Attendo's Q3 2024 call notes that privately financed new nursing homes in Sweden cost 80% less than the average cost for the public sector. This proves that the private sector cost-efficiency is a cross-border phenomenon, which means that long-term municipal outsourcing pressure remains strong across the entire Nordic region.
8.
Blocked care capacity: Welfare regions are underutilizing available private care beds due to strict short-term budget restrictions despite long waiting lists. Esperi Care's CEO Stefan Wentjärvi noted on 13 May 2025 that over 2,500 elderly citizens were waiting for care placements in 2024 even though thousands of private beds were vacant. This underutilization limits near-term operating earnings per share growth unless counties ease their procurement restrictions to allow patients into private facilities.

Section 4 – Price Indexation versus Staff Wage Increases

Section summary
  1. Negotiated wage increases in Finland and Sweden are outpacing near-term price indexations, creating transitional margin squeezes for Nordic care operators.
  2. Private social and healthcare contracts utilize lagged labor indices, which guarantees eventual price alignment but exposes operators to short-term earnings volatility.

Wage Settlements in Finland and Sweden

1.
Escalating labor costs: Wages in the Finnish private social services sector are set to rise by 6.1 percent over the agreement period running until April 2028. This collective deal negotiated by HALI and labor unions aims to address severe regional caregiver shortages. Private operators must absorb these escalating private sector personnel expenses immediately to maintain legal staffing standards. Consequently, these mandated wage hikes represent a major headwind for Finnish operations.
2.
Scheduled wage hikes: The private healthcare services collective agreement implemented a 2.22 percent scale increase in September 2026. Employees also received a local increment of 0.6 percent and a lump sum of EUR 183 in December 2026. In 2027, companies will face a further 2.0 percent general wage increase and a 0.4 percent local pay increment. These stepwise private social salary increases continue to elevate operating costs for Swedish and Finnish care providers alike.
3.
Negotiated wage hikes: Attendo finalized its Finnish collective bargaining agreement during the first half of 2026. The agreement schedules a 3.5 percent salary increase starting in September 2026 followed by another 2.7 percent raise in September 2027. This phased approach allows the company to budget personnel expenses with higher predictability. However, these agreed personnel wage hikes still challenge short-term operating margins until municipal indexation catches up.
4.
Swedish assistance freeze: In Sweden, operators face severe price-cost friction within the personal assistance segment because reimbursement rates remain low. The Swedish government raised the personal assistance hourly allowance by only 1.5 percent for 2026. This minimal rate increase was insufficient to cover negotiated nurse wage inflation across municipal care operations. Consequently, Humana and Ambea are experiencing a severe personal assistance margin squeeze because of this regulatory under-compensation.

Municipal Indexation and Compensation Lag

5.
Protected indexation clauses: Most municipal contracts held by Attendo and Ambea contain explicit price indexation clauses tied to local labor costs. These price compensation mechanisms safeguard operators by automatically adjusting municipal rates based on care price indices. However, annual municipal indexations prevent adverse cost inflation effects only after a substantial operational delay. Therefore, operators must navigate temporary price indexation lag windows when wages rise suddenly.
6.
Severe inflation mismatch: Sharp macroeconomic inflation remains difficult for care operators to offset in the short term despite contractual indexation. While index clauses eventually adjust rates upward, the recalculations occur annually and create a structural delay of twelve months. This delay means that personnel cost increases hit margins long before municipal indexation adjustments are implemented. Thus, rapid salary inflation can cause short-term earnings per share compression for Swedish and Finnish care providers.
7.
Moderate pension adjustments: The Social Insurance Institution of Finland confirmed a 2.3 percent index adjustment for national pensions starting in January 2027. This standard rate increase is linked directly to the national cost-of-living index. It matches the previous year's 2.3 percent adjustment to preserve regional consumer purchasing power. This moderate public pension adjustment rate reflects stabilizing broader macroeconomic inflation across the Finnish healthcare sector.
8.
Active price renegotiations: Private care operators work actively to renegotiate their framework agreements with municipalities to reflect escalating operational costs. Attendo managed to raise its Finnish care prices by approximately 4 percent in early 2024. These localized pricing adjustments are essential to establish a sustainable balance between wage inflation and municipal funding. Consequently, successful pricing negotiations will decide whether operators can maintain stable medium-term operating margins in Sweden.

Section 5 – Staffing Ratio Regulation and Margin Impact

Section summary
  1. Private care operators initially retained substantial margin benefits from the lower 0.60 Finnish staffing ratio due to immediate personnel cost savings.
  2. Welfare regions have clawed back a portion of these staffing savings through contract renegotiations, keeping price-versus-cost developments relatively flat.
  3. Our overall confidence in what the evidence shows on the care reform's financial impact is medium due to structural data gaps.

Regulatory Staffing Ratio Changes

1.
Relaxed staffing requirements: The Finnish parliament passed a major regulatory amendment that lowered the minimum staffing requirement in elderly care homes to 0.60 care workers per resident. According to Attendo's Q4 2024 earnings call on 5 February 2025, politicians implemented this change because the previous 0.65 threshold was too expensive and difficult to satisfy given the labor shortage. This regulatory shift provided immediate relief because it allowed operators to rightsize their care staff profiles throughout the region. Consequently, this lower ratio stabilized the labor market by reducing the desperate struggle for qualified nursing staff.
2.
191 192 - Earnings Calls Gradual personnel adjustments: Private care operators experienced a phased operational benefit as they aligned schedules with relaxed ratios. Attendo's Q1 2026 presentation published on 6 May 2026 reported that the group achieved lower personnel costs in Finland primarily due to this staff adjustment. Chief executive officer Martin Tivéus explained on 6 February 2025 that adapting to lower requirements takes several weeks to protect resident safety. This means the personnel cost savings materialized progressively during the year.
3.
Diverse portfolio exposure: The lower staffing ratio of 0.60 does not directly impact all healthcare providers because of differences in service focus. Terveystalo's 2025 annual report published on 26 February 2026 confirms that its public portfolio business focuses on primary care outsourcing rather than elderly care nursing homes. In contrast, DNB Carnegie's report on 8 October 2026 states that Attendo derives around two-thirds of its revenues from elderly care. Consequently, Attendo gained a substantial cost advantage over peers that do not operate nursing homes.

Cost Retention versus County Clawbacks

4.
Contractual price clawbacks: The financial benefit of the lower 0.60 staffing ratio was not entirely retained by private operators because counties renegotiated care prices downward. Attendo's Q2 2025 presentation published on 17 July 2025 stated that lower prices in Finnish elderly care occurred specifically due to these changed staffing requirements. This pricing reduction indicates that wellbeing services counties successfully clawed back a portion of the staffing ratio savings during negotiations. Consequently, this pricing headwind partially offset the personnel savings that the regulatory relaxation initially created.
5.
Stable price negotiations: Operators worked aggressively to conclude their price negotiations with the wellbeing services counties to protect their operating margins. Attendo's Q4 2024 earnings call on 6 February 2025 reported that a majority of price negotiations with welfare regions had been finalized. These completed agreements indicated a relatively stable and flat price-versus-cost development across the region. This contractual normalization prevented counties from executing further unilateral price cuts and stabilized the group's earnings outlook for 2025.
6.
Contrary evidence: Some counties opted to entirely terminate contracts and in-source care services rather than renegotiating prices under the 0.60 ratio. Terveystalo's half-year report published on 17 July 2026 showed that its portfolio business segment revenues dropped by 21.8 percent due to expiring public outsourcing contracts. This contraction indicates that counties are aggressively reclaiming public services to balance budgets rather than allowing private providers to keep any regulatory savings. Consequently, private operators face revenue declines that completely eliminate any margin gains from staffing relaxations.
7.
Escalating contract risks: Regulatory changes under the healthcare reform forced providers to renegotiate their existing agreements under unfavorable conditions. Terveystalo's listing prospectus published on 1 June 2023 warned that several outsourcing agreements had to be renegotiated before entering force in 2026. The company noted that wellbeing services counties might interpret existing agreements differently, creating substantial operational and regulatory risks during transitions. These pricing disputes meant that public partners could unilaterally reduce payments and suppress the group's long-term profitability.

8.

Attendo's Q2 2026 interim report published on 20 August 2026 showed that its Finnish segment expanded its lease-adjusted EBITA margin to 8.9 percent from 6.8 percent in the prior year. In contrast, Terveystalo's Q1 2026 report showed that its portfolio business segment EBITA margin fell to only 2.3 percent from 5.9 percent. This comparison proves that Attendo's nursing home model successfully converted staffing efficiencies into higher margins while Terveystalo suffered from lowmargin contract expirations.

DNB Carnegie's research report published on 8 October 2026 notes that if Attendo raises its Finnish operating margins to 10 percent, it will create significant upside for the group's earnings estimates. This target compares with the 8.9 percent margin reported in the first half of 2026, which was supported by labor efficiencies. Consequently, analysts believe that retaining these regulatory staffing savings is critical for Attendo to achieve its adjusted earnings targets.
10.

Attendo's Capital Markets Day transcript of 17 March 2026 explained that its voice recognition speech-to-text software reduced shift documentation time from 45 minutes to 20 minutes. This 25-minute gain allows caregivers to spend more working time directly with residents without hiring extra staff. This technology integration helps operators offset future staff shortages while maintaining high quality scores to satisfy county audits.
11.
