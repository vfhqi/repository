EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Core leadership thesis and executive transition risks

Dirk Hahn was appointed Group CEO on September 1, 2023, following an internal succession from long-standing chief Alistair Cox, representing a favourable internal choice that emphasized strategic continuity. However, Hahn stepped down as Group CEO on February 27, 2026, following a medical leave, creating an unexpected vacancy after only two and a half years in office. Former technology chief Mark Dearnley was appointed Group CEO in May 2026, marking a tech-led operator pivot designed to accelerate digital transformation. The abrupt leadership turnover exposes elevated key-person risk and potential strategic execution gaps during a critical period of structural restructuring and market headwinds. Hahn's single-company career path spanning over 20 years at Hays delivered strong operational execution in Germany but raised questions about strategic adaptability when confronting prolonged sector-wide downturns beyond his regional experience base.

Financial delivery and operational execution under Dirk Hahn

Group turnover declined to £6,421.2m in FY26, while pre-exceptional earnings per share retreated to 1.21p in FY26, representing a steady decline from 4.0p in FY24. Despite declining revenues and depressed earnings, Hahn's operational restructuring delivered conversion margin recovery to 5.4% in FY26, which outperformed PageGroup's record low of 1.3% conversion in the period. Structural cost savings reached £50m in FY26, three years ahead of schedule, achieved through aggressive headcount reductions of 12% in FY26 and average consultant net fee productivity expansion by 7%.

Capital allocation shifted dramatically defensive with the FY25 dividend cut by 65% and disposal of six European operations to refocus resources. However, Performance Share Plan awards lapsed with zero vesting as TSR fell below the threshold peer-median performance, highlighting severe underperformance versus sector benchmarks.

Management quality and operational credibility assessment

Hahn maintained a highly transparent communication style, openly confronting challenging market headwinds, directly admitting that he was not satisfied with current profit performance. His operational decision-making was highly detailed, systematic, and executed on a market-by-market basis, anchored by the Golden Rule of headcount discipline. Sell-side analysts focused on the shift toward conversion profitability following the appointment of Dirk Hahn, though Deutsche Bank cut its target price to 75p from 90p, reflecting continued fee pressures in Europe.

Shares slid 10% on February 27, 2026, magnified by the sudden CEO resignation, exposing market concerns over leadership stability. Expert networks revealed healthcare services firms bypassing Hays for IT/AI roles, preferring specialized boutique firms for better expertise and lower fees, while health risk executives reported engagement with Hays dropped sharply by over 50% to 60% during recent corporate restructurings.

The operational reality diverges from management's market-share claims, with accelerating boutique displacement and client engagement erosion threatening the core business model.

CFO capability and senior leadership stability

James Hilton was promoted to CFO in October 2022 after a 14-year internal tenure, not the hypothetical James Benson referenced in initial briefing materials. Hilton delivered reduced global trade debtor days to 36 days in FY26 and disciplined cash collections generated a working capital inflow of £24.9 million alongside an outstanding 189% cash conversion rate. However, his strategic execution required exceptional restructuring charges of £89.6 million to fund aggressive operating model changes.

Pre-exceptional operating profit of £48.6 million in FY26 slightly exceeded the top-end consensus range of £37 million to £46 million, demonstrating cost-discipline credibility. Senior leadership stability faces headwinds from talent attrition when senior leader James Milligan left to join rival Robert Walters plc, while external candidate Tom Way was appointed as

UK&I CEO to reposition the division under new leadership. Regional bench strength remains robust with operational leadership in DACH under Alexander Heise managing Germany as the primary profit engine, providing critical continuity during group-level transitions.

Board governance structure and independence profile

The board comprises seven independent non-executive directors and only two executive directors, exceeding the minimum UK code requirements, with Michael Findlay appointed as Non-Executive Chair on May 1, 2025, succeeding Andrew Martin. Board refreshment remains balanced, with 77% of directors serving under six years as of June 2026, demonstrating orderly tenure management. However, non-executive directors hold negligible equity with the aggregate board owning under 0.4% of shares, raising alignment concerns. The board faces a critical recruitment sector skills gap, as only James Hilton possesses direct staffing experience, creating potential oversight vulnerabilities during cyclical downturns in European contracting markets. Anthony Kirby serves as Group CEO of Serco Group plc while sitting on three Hays committees, introducing potential overboarding risk. Governance structures include chartered accountant Zarin Patel leading the reconstructed Audit Committee and Helen Cunningham succeeding Susan Murray as Chair of the Remuneration Committee following the 2026 AGM, maintaining strong committee independence.

Executive compensation alignment and performance incentives

Dirk Hahn received total pay of £1,159k in FY26, with variable pay comprising 54% of his FY26 package, delivering moderate alignment through high variable ratios. The FY26 bonus required adjusted operating profit of £40m for threshold, which delivered 35.8% outturn against the maximum target, representing demanding hurdle rates during cyclical weakness. The 2026 PSP award sets cumulative EPS hurdles of 4.04p to 6.45p, requiring challenging c.25% annual growth rates, preventing payout gaming but risking demotivation during prolonged downturns.

Executive directors must build holdings of 200% of salary, with departing executives required to hold 200% of salary for Year 1 and 100% for Year 2, demonstrating robust post-employment mandates. However, current CEO Mark Dearnley holds only 68% of salary, representing compliance delays if tenure terminates early. Malus and clawback provisions remain enforceable for three years, covering material misstatements, fraud, and corporate failure, protecting shareholder interests.

Variable pay declined during cyclical downturns, mirroring declines in total shareholder returns, confirming pay-for-performance correlation rather than structural misalignment.

Primary governance red flags and capital structure vulnerabilities

Hays maintains a highly transparent capital structure with only one class of Ordinary shares in issue and no dualclass configurations or differential voting weight protections for insiders, ensuring full market exposure to corporate control. PwC has served as external auditor since 2016, representing a ten-year consecutive audit relationship, introducing auditor independence concerns despite orderly partner rotation with outgoing lead partner Jon Sturges succeeded by Alex Lazarus following the completion of the FY26 audit. Critically, PwC identified prohibited non-audit services provided by network firms to immaterial Group entities during FY26, though the Audit Committee concluded the prohibited services did not compromise the professional judgement or independence of PwC. The Audit and Risk Committee identified no material financial control failings or weaknesses during the fiscal year, while Group Internal Controls completed dry-runs of Financial Reporting Internal Controls to prepare for mandatory reporting in FY27. At the 2025 AGM, Resolution 16 on share allotment received just over 20% votes against due to policy-driven institutional shareholder opposition, signaling moderate governance friction.

Organizational culture and employee engagement deterioration

Hays reported a declining engagement score of 67% in FY26, down from 70% in FY25, which lags the stable 72% engagement reported by global peer Adecco and trails the 73% engagement score achieved by peer Robert Walters. Only 70% of employees recommended Hays as a great place to work in the 2025 Pulse survey, falling from 73% in 2024, reflecting rising internal friction from structural changes. Merely 46% of employees rarely think about looking for another job, down from 47% in May 2024, exposing retention vulnerabilities.

Operationally, Group consultant headcount shrank by 12% YoY to 5,194 fee earners in the year ending June 2026, with Germany consultant headcount decreased by 16% YoY and United Kingdom and Ireland consultants declined by 16% YoY. Hays closed 52 offices and consolidated 22 locations globally, with the UK office network halved from 59 locations to 29 active branches, demonstrating dramatic capacity reductions. Aggressive front-office headcount cuts may severely damage local morale and leave Hays under-resourced to capture customer demand during the next cyclical recovery.

Management quality verdict and primary operational risks

Management quality presents medium plausibility to execute the strategic thesis through the cycle. Dirk Hahn delivered strong capital-allocation discipline by rebasing the dividend 65% to protect cash flow and maintained high communication clarity, openly admitting strategic dissatisfaction during results. However, he exhibited slower decision velocity and weak adaptability due to intense single-company career history constraints.

The standalone bear case highlights severe permanent placement pressure with volumes remaining lower for longer, massive exceptional charges of GBP 89.6m, and disproportionate dependence on German contracting, which contributed 85% of FY26 EBITA. Expert networks expose large software clients bypassing Hays for specialized roles, preferring specialized boutique firms, while health risk executives reported engagement with Hays dropped 50% during recent corporate restructurings.

The divergence between management narrative and operational reality is most acute in client migration patterns, with clients using automated matching platforms to disintermediate high-cost traditional agencies and competitors and tech platforms threatening the core fee rate if online database disintermediation accelerates. Governance oversight remains supported by high nonexecutive director independence comprising seven independent directors, but non-executive directors lack direct recruitment sector expertise, creating a critical oversight gap during the most operationally complex period in company history.
