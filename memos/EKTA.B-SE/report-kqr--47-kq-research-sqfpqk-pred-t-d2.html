Section 3 – Historical Gross Margin and EBIT Margin Dispersion

Section summary
  1. Historical analysis shows Elekta faces significant downside margin dispersion driven by supply chain inflation and transaction currency headwinds.
  2. Peer comparisons with Siemens Healthineers and Accuray confirm that radiotherapy margins remain structurally vulnerable to global macroeconomic shocks.

Historical Volatility and Downside Risks

1.
Severe margin contraction: This analysis of 12 sources across 5 companies reveals that Elekta's gross margin fell to 37.1 percent in the first quarter of fiscal year 2021/22 compared to 45.9 percent in the prior year. This severe compression was mainly caused by unprecedented supply chain and logistics inflation which added approximately 500 basis points of headwind. The remainder of the decline was driven by currency transaction headwinds of 140 basis points and unfavorable solution-service mix of 80 basis points.
2.
Persistent margin erosion: Elekta experienced long-term downward pressure on its operational profitability over the ten-year period ending in fiscal year 2025/26. The adjusted gross margin hovered around 37 to 38 percent in recent years, failing to recover to pre-pandemic levels of 41 to 42 percent. This persistent stagnation highlights structural challenges in offsetting inflationary pressures through pricing actions. Consequently, the adjusted EBIT margin was dragged down from 13.9 percent in fiscal year 2020/21 to 9.6 percent in the first nine months of fiscal year 2025/26.
3.
Unstable profitability history: Elekta's adjusted EBIT margin has exhibited significant annual dispersion over the ten-year historical baseline. The margin bottomed at 10.3 percent in fiscal year 2021/22 during the height of the supply chain crisis before recovering to 11.8 percent in fiscal year 2023/24. However, this recovery was short-lived, with the margin declining to 11.6 percent in fiscal year 2024/25 after the company was forced to lower its financial guidance due to weaker volumes.
4.
Marked asymmetric dispersion: Elekta's historical margin adjustments demonstrate a structural skew toward the downside rather than the upside. The company's profitability has shown larger negative movements during macroeconomic disruptions, while expansions have been highly gradual. For instance, the operating EBIT margin plummeted from 11.3 percent to 4.9 percent in fiscal year 2024/25 because of heavy non-cash R&D impairments and cost escalation. This structural skew indicates that earnings projections remain highly sensitive to unforeseen operational or supply bottlenecks.
5.
Contrary evidence: Elekta's gross margin demonstrated a strong, temporary recovery during the fourth quarter of fiscal year 2024/25. The adjusted gross margin expanded to 40.3 percent, representing the best fourth-quarter profitability in five years. This sharp improvement was driven by price increases and positive product mix, contradicting the view that margin recovery is permanently constrained. However, this expansion was quickly reversed by renewed currency and tariff headwinds in the subsequent quarters, confirming that long-term volatility remains elevated.

Section 5 – Currency Sensitivities, Hedging Dynamics, and FX Transmission Lags

Section summary
  1. Systematic currency hedging programs isolate Elekta's near-term operating income from transaction volatility but introduce substantial transmission lags.
  2. Comparative analysis shows unhedged peers face immediate profitability exposure to foreign exchange fluctuations, resulting in asymmetric margin dispersion.

Currency Sensitivities and Pre-Hedged Exposure

1.
Significant financial sensitivity: Elekta maintains a highly global business structure that exposes its operating profits to substantial currency transaction volatility. Management reported that a general change of one percentage point in the Swedish krona exchange rate against other currencies impacts net profit by approximately thirteen million kronor under the current income structure. This transaction exposure primarily arises because the company has significant expenses denominated in British pounds and Chinese yuan while collecting a vast portion of its sales revenues in United States dollars.
2.
Adverse dollar impacts: The sensitivity of Elekta's operating result is heavily dominated by movements in the United States dollar against the Swedish krona. Specifically, a one percent weakening of the krona against the dollar provides a positive eighteen million kronor boost to the annual operating result. This relationship underpins the company-cited baseline where a ten percent depreciation of the dollar would trigger an estimated three hundred million kronor negative hit to pre-hedged operating income.
3.
Unfavorable sterling exposure: Elekta faces a structural margin squeeze whenever the British pound strengthens against the Swedish currency. A one percent weakening of the krona against the pound results in a negative twenty-seven million kronor impact on the group operating income. This exposure is particularly acute because the group manufactures major linear accelerator components in Crawley, thereby concentrating sterling-denominated cash production outflows against foreign sales.
4.
Minor currency inputs: Other currencies like the euro and Japanese yen have smaller but meaningful direct impacts on Elekta's operating profitability. A one percent weakening of the krona against the euro generates a minor positive benefit of two million kronor. However, the aggregate impact of these secondary currencies means that overall margins remain highly vulnerable to broad currency shifts before hedging mitigates the short-term volatility.

Rolling Hedge Mechanics and Currency Risks

5.
Managed mitigation programs: Elekta utilizes a systematic cash flow hedging program to smooth out the financial impact of sudden foreign exchange market movements. The company uses forward exchange contracts to cover anticipated sales denominated in foreign currencies for periods up to twenty-four months. Under this policy, recognized assets and liabilities are hedged within a target range of fifty to one hundred percent of their total outstanding transaction exposure.
6.
Substantial hedging drag: The delayed realization of forward contracts can create a significant headwind when exchange rates move against locked-in hedge positions. During the fourth quarter of fiscal year 2025/26, the combination of tariff costs and a strengthening krona resulted in a one hundred twenty basis point drag on gross margin. This negative impact demonstrates that rolling hedges into a strengthening krona delays the benefits of a favorable home currency environment.
7.
Unfavorable hedge outcomes: The cash flow hedge reserve acts as a key indicator of the forward financial impact of Elekta's currency contract positions. During the fiscal year 2025/26, the change in the hedge reserve was a negative fifty-four million kronor after accounting for taxes. This negative shift means that the accumulated fair value of derivatives has declined, reflecting the risks of locking in historical forward rates.
8.
Delayed profit transmission: Hedging dynamics inherently introduce a substantial lag in how currency fluctuations are transmitted to Elekta's net income. In the short term, the direct impact of exchange rate fluctuations is substantially reduced through the active forward contract layers. This smoothing mechanism ensures that although a strengthening krona eventually hurts reported export sales, the hedging buffer creates a multiquarter lag before the full impact hits net profit.
