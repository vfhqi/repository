High-precision focus: Medartis manufactures and distributes high-precision medical devices and implants for surgical bone fixation and joint reconstruction in upper and lower extremities and cranio-maxillofacial regions. Medartis was founded in 1997 by Thomas Straumann and Willi Miesch, developing from a Swiss start-up into a global innovation leader. Headquartered in Basel, Switzerland, Medartis maintains deep clinical relationships to design specialised joint and bone solutions for complex anatomical regions. High-precision product focus underpins long-term earnings per share growth through technological differentiation.

Section 16 – Conclusion

Ultimate thesis. The H1 2026 results shift Medartis's 18-month EPS outlook upward in direction and in quality of revenue, but they do not yet justify the same confidence for a three-year EPS path. H1 2026 group sales reached CHF 160.8 million versus CHF 123.0 million, a 30.7% reported increase. Organic core growth of 17.0% at constant exchange rates exceeded the FY2025 rate of 15.7% and beat the mid-teens consensus.

Management then raised FY2026 organic guidance to 17% to 19%. Core net profit swung to CHF 6.8 million from a CHF -0.4 million loss. That is a genuine inflection. However, the same release showed that earnings are converting into cash only weakly, so the path to durable EPS expansion depends on execution more than on demand.

Internal drivers. The strongest internal support is mix and ownership. Core gross margin rose to 81.5% from 80.7%, driven by internalizing the accretive KeriMedical business. The premium pricing of the TOUCH prosthesis absorbed 0.6 percentage points of currency drag and 0.6 points of tariff drag.

US core sales grew 27.4% organically. By October, more than 2,000 TOUCH surgeries had been performed. Strategy is also moving toward margin discipline. The CEO has said the group is no longer solely focused on growth at all costs.

Operating leverage and cash flow metrics are now built into variable compensation. Project Flash targets 70% local US manufacturing by end-2026 and about 80% in 2027. The Titan Nail is intended to replace the thirdparty NX nail without supply disruption. Together these form the margin-expansion mechanism behind a rising EPS path.

External drivers. The external picture is mixed but broadly supportive for extremities. Trauma care remains an urgent clinical necessity even when hip and knee replacements are deferred. Global hip and knee growth slowed to 1.7% at constant exchange rates in Q3 2026.

That contrast favors Medartis's niche. The offsets are persistent. Currency cost 0.6 points of gross margin in H1 2026, after a similar drag in FY2025. Management expects a 12.5% tariff rate on US shipments.

Group purchasing organizations negotiate at corporate level to secure the lowest pricing. Acumed's ASCent program offers fixed procedure pricing to ambulatory surgery centers. I read these as margin headwinds that cap upside, not as threats to the revenue trajectory. Consensus blind spots.

First, earnings quality. The profit improvement came partly from gross margin gains and partly from a CHF 6.7 million better finance result that benefited from foreign exchange gains. That is nearly the size of the whole core net profit, so the bottom-line inflection is less operational than the headline suggests. Second, cash.

Free cash flow was CHF -10.1 million versus CHF 0.5 million a year earlier. A CHF 25.0 million working capital increase weighed on cash generation. Cash reserves fell to CHF 25.8 million from CHF 120.5 million. The balance sheet also carries convertible bonds of CHF 98.1 million plus CHF 12 million of bank drawings.

Third, US leadership continuity. As of January 2027, the CFO has taken over as US head ad interim. There is also no permanent Latin America leader. Fourth, uneven growth beneath the headline.

Upper extremities grew 21.1% organically against 10.4% for lower extremities. APAC grew 5.5% and Latin America 9.7%. Finally, structured consensus data and individual broker target revisions were not available, so the "beat" is measured only against the mid-teens growth expectation.

Strongest case against this conclusion. The bearish view is that H1 flatters the story. TOUCH is a single-product catalyst, and its adoption is front-loaded, since the 2026 US unit projection was already raised from 1,200 to 1,800. Meanwhile the capital intensity behind it is rising.

Capex reached CHF 17.9 million versus CHF 8.2 million. Surgical sets are loaned to hospitals without purchase agreements. If utilization lags, the group is left with an overcapitalized cost structure and underutilized plants. Rivals are also strengthening where Medartis is most exposed.

Acumed has cleared its Acu-Loc NEXT wrist system. Zimmer Biomet has folded its lower extremities and trauma portfolios into Paragon 28's dedicated channel. On this view, three-year EPS is capped by cash burn, tariffs, currency and pricing pressure even if revenue stays strong. What would change my mind.

The core EBITDA margin would need to stay at or above H1's 18.0%, with core gross margin at or above 81.5%. Local US production would need to reach the 70% end-2026 target and approach 80% in 2027. The CHF 4.4 million tariff refund receivable would need to be collected, and free cash flow would need to turn positive on a half-year basis, with working capital reversing at least part of H1's CHF 25.0 million build. I would become more cautious if organic growth fell below mid-teens, if the gross margin dropped below 80.7% (the H1 2025 level), or if Project Flash slipped materially.

The same applies if free cash flow stayed negative through FY2026 while capex ran above the H1 level. A further drop in cash, or any need for dilutive funding, would also count against the thesis.
