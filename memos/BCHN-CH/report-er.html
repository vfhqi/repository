that forced management to delay its Mid-Range Plan targets Corporate description – global leader: Headquartered in Winterthur, Switzerland, Burckhardt is a global manufacturer focused exclusively on reciprocating compressor technologies and lifecycle services. Founded in 1844, the company has evolved to serve critical applications across energy transition, gas transport, hydrogen mobility, and petrochemical value chains. Its global presence spans over 80 countries, utilizing five manufacturing and assembly sites and more than 40 service centers worldwide
2.
Business model – integrated structure: The company operates an integrated business model split into two key segments: the Systems Division and the Services Division. The Systems Division handles sales of customized and modularized new compressors, which are typically large-scale capital projects. The customer-centric Services Division acts as a recurring, highly profitable stabilizer by providing spare parts, upgrades, and maintenance throughout the average 40-year compressor lifespan
3.
Segment mix – margin divergence: Jefferies analyst Fabian Piasta highlights that the product-centric Systems Division typically generates the majority of revenues (~65%) at lower margins, historically in the high-single to lowdouble-digit range. In contrast, the Services Division provides substantial earnings stability and higher profitability, contributing approximately 35% of group sales at recurring EBIT margins of around 24% to 25%. This cumulative interplay creates continuous cross-selling opportunities across the lifecycle

Section 16 – Conclusion

Section summary
  1. The single most important change to the Burckhardt investment case is the official postponement of the Mid-Range Plan targets, revealing that the severe 31.9% order intake collapse may represent a structural demand reset rather than a temporary cyclical delay. The fundamental investment thesis for Burckhardt Compression has undergone a material shift following the FY2025 full-year results reported on June 4, 2026. While the company delivered resilient operating margins of 13.3% and achieved a historic Systems Division profitability breakout at 10.7%, these achievements were overshadowed by the catastrophic 31.9% collapse in group order intake to CHF 784.3 million and management's decision to indefinitely defer the ambitious Mid-Range Plan timeline that targeted CHF 1.2 billion in sales and 12% to 15% EBIT margins by FY2027. The postponement marks a critical inflection point, forcing investors to reevaluate whether the company's specialized reciprocating compressor business model remains structurally

aligned with global industrial capital allocation trends or whether it faces a prolonged period of margin normalization as the protective order backlog depletes. The most significant change to the investment case centers on the credibility gap between management's narrative and underlying demand fundamentals. Throughout the earnings cycle, CEO Fabrice Billard and the executive team attributed the order shortfall entirely to temporary geopolitical project deferrals, citing US tariff uncertainties announced in April 2025 and Middle East conflicts that disrupted the traditionally strong final booking month.

However, the persistent structural overcapacities in China's petrochemical sector, the complete halt in new Low-Density Polyethylene and Ethylene-Vinyl-Acetate investments, and the disappointing 8.2% decline in the high-margin Services Division reveal deeper demand weaknesses that cannot be explained by short-term political disruptions alone. Jefferies analyst Fabian Piasta's projection that the Services Division will reach only CHF 364 million by FY2027 against the corporate target of CHF 480 million underscores the magnitude of the strategic shortfall.

This CHF 116 million revenue gap cannot be bridged through organic growth alone, suggesting that the original four-pillar strategic framework may be structurally incapable of delivering the promised returns without dilutive acquisitions or a fundamental reassessment of market positioning. The strongest counter-hypothesis to the bearish structural demand thesis rests on three interconnected tailwinds that could drive a faster-than-expected order recovery.

First, the marine LNG and alternative fuel segment demonstrated exceptional resilience, highlighted by Burckhardt securing its largest-ever marine order from Hanwha Ocean for 14 advanced boil-off gas compressors and winning the world's first purpose-built ammonia bunkering vessel contract. The company's proprietary Laby-GI zero-methane-slip technology provides an unassailable competitive moat as International Maritime Organization emissions regulations tighten progressively through 2030.

Second, the explosive growth in data center electricity consumption driven by artificial intelligence infrastructure is creating structural demand for natural gas balancing power, with US data center energy requirements projected to double by 2035. Third, the successful deployment of AI-augmented digital services such as Up! Detect and the upgraded PROGNOST NT Predictive Intelligence module is fundamentally transforming the aftermarket value proposition, enabling the company to lock in high-margin, multi-year service agreements that insulate earnings from cyclical Systems Division volatility.

If these three catalysts converge with an easing of US-China trade tensions that unlocks the 20-project hyper-compressor pipeline in solar applications, the order book could inflect positively much faster than the de-risked consensus models currently assume. What would definitively change the investment view is tangible evidence of sustained order momentum returning to the Systems Division over consecutive reporting periods.

Specifically, investors should monitor whether the book-to-bill ratio recovers above 1.0x by H1 FY2026 and whether management provides explicit forward guidance on the revised Mid-Range Plan timeline during the next Capital Markets Day. A critical litmus test will be the Services Division's ability to accelerate organic growth above 5% annually starting in FY2027, driven by the installed base from prior equipment waves exiting warranty periods.

Additionally, concrete contract wins in the European hydrogen backbone infrastructure, particularly follow-on orders from the H2CAST Etzel underground storage pilot, would validate management's long-term energy transition narrative and justify a re-rating of the stock's valuation multiples. Conversely, if the first half of FY2026 order intake continues to deteriorate and the Services Division fails to stabilize, it would confirm that the current downturn is structural rather than cyclical, forcing a permanent downward revision of long-term earnings power.
