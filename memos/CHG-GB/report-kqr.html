Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. The Key Question is partially resolvable from current evidence, as critical data gaps in private supplier financials and contract-specific pricing indexes prevent a precise assessment of the long-term subsystem pricing power trajectory, yet directional findings on capacity additions, contractual frameworks, and valuation anomalies provide substantial clarity on the near-term outlook through 2029.

High structural barriers support prolonged pricing power for subsystem specialists in energetics, solid rocket motors, and vehicle transmissions, driven by specialized chemical manufacturing facilities that require hazardous-process permits and site-level safety certifications taking several years to replicate and backward integration by suppliers like Alzchem maintaining near-monopoly positions for military-grade nitroguanidine.

The energetics pricing trajectory exhibits remarkable resilience, with military-grade TNT prices doubling from pre- 2022 levels to approximately $40 to $45 per kilogram and procurement quotes for 155mm artillery rounds skyrocketing from €1,500 to €2,000 in 2023-2024 to between €7,000 and €10,000 per round. Consensus anticipates rapid margin normalization after 2028, yet the high complexity of energetic supply chains indicates that scarcity rents will persist longer than the three-year window priced by the market, supported by Chemring Nobel remaining the sole qualified supplier of MCX energetic material for Nammo's 155mm munitions technology, creating a durable captive customer base for the next decade.

Aggressive manufacturing expansions by primary suppliers resolve acute rocket motor deficits but risk creating a capacity surplus after 2029. Solid rocket motor capacity is expanding dramatically, with Avio breaking ground on September 29, 2026, on its new 900,000-square-foot facility in Virginia aiming to produce thousands of tactical motors annually starting in early 2029, Northrop Grumman planning to triple its tactical solid rocket motor production capacity at its West Virginia laboratory by 2027, and Rheinmetall completing a new rocket motor plant in Unterlüß, Germany, aiming to achieve total production capacity of 3,500 tons of propellant annually by early 2027. These expansions are substantially de-risked through long-term frameworks, as Avio disclosed that more than two-thirds of its half-year orders were defense-related, with a backlog extending largely into 2029 and 2030 and Lockheed Martin secured a strategic cooperation agreement with Avio on October 13, 2026, to guarantee merchant supply capacity for GMLRS and Precision Strike Missiles. Nevertheless, sceptical industry experts warn that the rapid influx of new capital and start-up players into the solid rocket motor space threatens to trigger overcapacity once current stockpiles are replenished.

Renk's valuation discount relative to electronics and energetics peers stems from temporary sentiment rather than structural execution failures. Renk maintains a commanding seventy-five percent market share in transmissions for tracked military vehicles across NATO countries and benefits from every original equipment platform win converting into a thirty-year high-margin aftermarket annuity that yields several times the initial vehicle content value.

Bernstein's 22 July 2026 initiation highlights that Renk trades at a discounted 12.5 times 2028 adjusted EV/EBIT, sitting well below high-growth subsystem peers, while Jefferies' 5 August 2026 equity research shows Hensoldt trading at a premium 26.0 times 2028 earnings and DNB Carnegie's 6 October 2026 report shows Saab trading at a robust 20.1 times 2028 EV/EBIT. Morgan Stanley argues Renk's 12.5 times 2028 operating profit discount relative to electronics peers is driven by depressed sentiment regarding land defense spending rather than earnings execution, although competitive pressure is rising in North America where Renk lost the transmission award for the M1 E2 vehicle upgrade program to SAPA and competitor ZF is challenging Renk's domain by developing a parallel-hybrid drive with Rolls-Royce for the future European Main Ground Combat System.

Stricter European content mandates and accelerated regulatory frameworks will reshape trans-Atlantic supply chains, as European eligibility rules under the SAFE and EDIP programs now mandate that funded equipment must contain at least 65% European content under European design control and the newly approved "Omnibus V" legislative package simplifies procurement by increasing contract thresholds and extending framework agreements from seven to ten years.

These regulatory changes provide extended demand visibility that enables subsystem manufacturers to secure long-term capital investments with highly predictable cash flows, yet national fragmentation and slow multi-country certification may continue to delay contract execution, meaning European subsystem specialists may fail to achieve the manufacturing scale required to offset eventual demand normalization after 2030. topic-specific reports The second-order implications of this trajectory are substantial, as agile "Neo Primes" like Helsing and STARK are securing major framework agreements by integrating AI and sensor-fusion directly into low-cost platforms and legacy automotive giants are leveraging empty manufacturing lines to apply high-volume, low-cost assembly techniques to tactical unmanned systems, with Renault's partnership with Thales aiming to scale loitering munition throughput by a factor of 100 to reach 1,000 units monthly by 2027.

Simultaneously, major missile primes are actively qualifying secondary solid rocket motor sources to mitigate severe supply bottlenecks and capture procurement leverage, as demonstrated by Boeing and Saab's selection of Anduril to design and produce motors for the GLSDB system. These competitive dynamics threaten to erode legacy sole-source monopolies and accelerate pricing erosion for traditional component makers, particularly if coordinated European procurement or potential ceasefires trigger a rapid inventory normalization and leave producers with expensive, underutilized capacity.

Section 2 – Energetics and explosives pricing power

Section summary
  1. Extreme supply deficits and prolonged capital expenditure lead times secure multi-year pricing power for sole-source energetics suppliers, extending the scarcity rent runway well past the consensus two-tothree-year horizon.

Upstream bottlenecks and supplier moats

1.
Defensible moat: Specialized chemical manufacturing facilities require hazardous-process permits and site- level safety certifications that take several years to replicate and prevent new entrants from standing up capacity quickly.
2.
Consolidated control: Diehl deepened its grip on German explosives capacity through its June 2026 acquisition of EST Energetics, supplementing its unique Troisdorf operating authorizations.
3.
Backward integration: German chemical supplier Alzchem maintains a near-monopoly outside of Asia for military-grade nitroguanidine, which remains a critical bottleneck for Western munitions programs.
4.
De-risked expansion: Alzchem is currently doubling its German guanidine nitrate and nitroguanidine capacity with a €140 million program that is financially protected by non-refundable customer down payments and EU grants.
5.
Delayed capacity: Rheinmetall's delay in constructing its Eastern European energetics facilities from 2026 to 2027 further extends the period of severe European supply deficits.

Price escalation and contractual dynamics

6.
Doubling costs: Expert insights from a GE Aerospace program lead confirm that military-grade TNT prices have doubled from pre-2022 levels to reach approximately $40 to $45 per kilogram.
7.
Sticker shock: Procurement quotes for 155mm artillery rounds have skyrocketed from €1,500 to €2,000 in 2023 and 2024 to between €7,000 and €10,000 per round due to acute production deficits.
8.
Framework protection: Chemring Nobel secured long-term pricing visibility through a twelve-year framework agreement with Diehl Defence, which includes an initial five-year, €231 million purchase order commencing in late 2026.
9.
Specialty appreciation: In its second-quarter 2026 financial report, Alzchem recorded positive specialty pricing growth of 6.5% year-on-year, illustrating that pricing power remains highly resilient despite broader macroeconomic pressures.
10.
Persistent constraints: Czechoslovak Group is expanding its vertical integration by producing TNT in Lavrio, Greece, but output is not projected to commence until 2027.

Scarcity rent horizon and consensus edge cases

11.
Extended horizon: While consensus anticipates a rapid margin normalization after 2028, the high complexity of energetic supply chains indicates that scarcity rents will persist longer than the three-year window priced by the market.
12.
Subsystem lock-in: Chemring Nobel remains the sole qualified supplier of MCX energetic material for Nammo's 155mm munitions technology, creating a durable captive customer base for the next decade.
13.
Geological headwind: Capacity expansions face physical development hurdles, as evidenced by a £35 million cost overrun at Chemring's Norwegian facility due to unexpected infrastructure and geological challenges.
