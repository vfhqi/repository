APM Mini-Memo: Bavarian Nordic

The one-line view. A net-cash vaccine maker whose travel franchise is compounding at over 20% a year while margins rise from in-sourced manufacturing; the story depends on Travel Health outgrowing a 2027 lull in government stockpile orders, with the United States renewal the swing factor.

What the company does

Bavarian Nordic is a Danish specialty vaccine maker with two businesses of very different character. Public Preparedness sells JYNNEOS, the only approved non-replicating smallpox and mpox vaccine in the world, to governments that stockpile it, chiefly the United States, France, the European Commission and Canada. Travel Health sells rabies, tick-borne encephalitis, chikungunya, typhoid and cholera vaccines, mostly paid for out of pocket by travellers in the United States and Europe, through a portfolio bought from GSK in 2019 and Emergent in 2023.

Revenue in 2025 was 6,244 million Danish kroner, split almost evenly: Travel Health 2,963 million, up 30%, and Public Preparedness 3,105 million. The company makes everything itself, from bulk vaccine at Kvistgaard in Denmark to fill-finish and direct sales, and that integration is where the margin comes from.

A plausible investment thesis

The interesting part is Travel Health. Nine-month 2026 Travel Health revenue grew 24%, or 34% stripping out discontinued partner agreements, led by the rabies vaccines up 39% to 1,975 million kroner on a United States rabies market growing 36%. Management lifted the 2026 Travel Health target by 400 million kroner to 3,400 million and says incremental sales carry a margin of about 68% on earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation (EBITDA). The rabies position is dominant, with 79% share in the United States and 96% in Germany, and pricing power holds because the traveller pays, not an insurer.

The margin story is a manufacturing story. Bringing production of the GSK vaccines in-house lifted the second-quarter 2026 gross margin to 61%, and the research expects the transfers to add 15 to 20 percentage points of gross margin and cut production cost by up to 30% by 2027. That gain is largely already paid for, the GSK and Emergent milestones being complete.

How it reaches earnings per share. Full-year 2026 guidance is revenue of about 6,100 million kroner at a 32% EBITDA margin, raised in October after a third quarter that beat consensus on revenue, Travel Health, EBITDA and the chikungunya vaccine Vimkunya. DNB Carnegie's 2026 adjusted earnings per share estimate is 15.40 kroner. The catch is 2027: with 900 million kroner of stockpile orders secured against roughly 2,400 million for 2026, the same broker has 2027 at 11.10 kroner, a 28% fall. The upside case is that Travel Health keeps compounding and a renewed United States contract fills the gap, so 2027 earnings land above that low bar and the market stops valuing the company on its episodic engine.

Drivers to track: quarterly Travel Health growth, especially rabies; the United States stockpile renewal now under negotiation; new sovereign orders for 2027; Vimkunya's United States reimbursement; and buyback execution.

That fit is loose, because consensus has group earnings per share falling in 2027 while the sovereign engine rests; the setup becomes crisp only if Travel Health and a renewed stockpile contract together make 2027 a year of growth rather than a plateau.

Case Riskiness and simplicity

The case is not simple, because two engines pull in opposite directions and almost every forward number depends on which you weight. What needs to go right: Travel Health keeps growing at 20% or more into tough comparables; the United States renews its stockpile contract in 2027, the original 11.5 million dose order having been completed in May 2026; and Kvistgaard runs both product lines without conflict.

The main risk is sovereign order flow through one factory. The United States alone was 18% of 2025 revenue and France 11%; a failed renewal would strip out half of group revenue; and the research puts the joint success rate of its five drivers at only 35%, conditional on Kvistgaard avoiding bottlenecks. Two newer hazards sit alongside. Sanofi's next-generation rabies vaccine VaxRabeo won European approval on 19 November 2026 with a United States filing planned for late 2026, aimed straight at the franchise driving the beats; and the third-quarter beat rests on a 36% jump in United States rabies demand that could normalise. What to track: United States budget allocations and the renewal announcement, quarterly rabies sales and share, and any production slip at Kvistgaard. Leadership is also new: Tarja Stenvall became chief executive on 15 October 2026, the finance director leaves in April 2027, and a strategy update is due in the first half of 2027.

Business quality

Good franchise, poor predictability. The moats are genuine: the only approved non-replicating smallpox vaccine, regulatory and manufacturing barriers that take years to cross, dominant rabies shares, and an adjusted EBITDA margin of 40.7% in 2025. The balance sheet is strong, with nearly 3,000 million kroner of cash despite a 500 million buyback completed in July 2026 and a further 750 million launched in September.

Against that, Public Preparedness swung from 5,027 million kroner in 2023 to 3,105 million in 2025, which places the company in the bottom 5% for earnings predictability. Cash conversion in 2026 is weak: nine-month operating cash flow was 489 million kroner against 2,177 million a year earlier, on higher receivables and the final GSK payment. Our Sector Digest of 22 October lists Bavarian Nordic among beats built on stocking, accruals and one-offs.

Plausible returns and valuation

Cheap on this year, fair on next. At 252.4 kroner on 27 November the shares trade at 17.9 times earnings two years forward, the 51st percentile of their ten-year range, and 1.8% below the 52-week high. On DNB Carnegie's figures that is about 16 times 2026 earnings and 23 times 2027 (calculated). The weekly TSR/Valuation rating of 27 November is C on the valuation reading alone. The weekly routine's plausible return of 19 November is 54% a year, but that figure rests on the multiple moving from 17.9 to 30.2 times, which I would not lean on while consensus earnings fall next year.

Broker targets of 280 kroner (Jefferies, 13 November) and 290 (DNB Carnegie, 16 November) imply 11% to 15%, a D against Richard's bar. The shares trade at a 20% discount to European vaccine peers, and the Innosera consortium's withdrawn bid was 250 per share. A B return needs 2027 earnings above 11.10 kroner, which the renewal and Travel Health could deliver; Carnegie's bear target is 160.

Value chain and thematics: the pros and the cons

Pros. The weekly Thematic Fit/Momentum rating of 27 November is A, from the Pharma environment alone. The Sector Digest of 22 October puts the pharma sector at the 22nd percentile of its ten-year multiple range, shows Bavarian Nordic up 40% over three months and rising after a fall, and names its travel vaccines as a single-company story worth a look. Weekly Sell-side Momentum rose to A on 25 November and weekly Technical Momentum is B on 27 November. The Industry Digest's rule applies: where the patient pays, pricing power survives.

Cons. Half the revenue is set by sovereign budgets, with price caps in European joint procurement. Longer-dated, smallpox vaccines on the newer messenger RNA platform from Moderna and CSPC threaten the stockpile franchise, and the two-dose JYNNEOS regimen loses a quarter of patients between doses.
