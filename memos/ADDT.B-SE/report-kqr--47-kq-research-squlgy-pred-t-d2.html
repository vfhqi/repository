Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

1.
Key Question: " How concentrated or diffuse are the drivers that separate the next 18 months to four years' financials from the last four to six years, and how wide and how skewed is the range of plausible outcomes over the next 18 months?"

Understanding whether Addtech’s performance is concentrated in a few macro drivers or diffuse across its 150+ subsidiaries is critical for predicting its earnings per share (EPS) trajectory. Historically, Addtech achieved a revenue CAGR of 11.6% from 2019/20 to 2025/26, with reported EPS rising from SEK 3.21 to SEK 7.95, according to FactSet data cited in the research query. If future growth is concentrated in a single factor like the timing of an organic industrial recovery, the range of plausible outcomes for EPS is wider and more volatile.

Conversely, if growth is diffuse–driven by a combination of M&A, margin expansion, and mechanical currency effects–the outcome is more predictable. The answer directly impacts how the market values Addtech's decentralized model versus more centralized peers. Addtech’s recent record EBITA margin of 16.7% in Q2 2026/27, reported by the company on October 22, 2026, raises questions about whether this represents a structural peak or a sustainable new baseline.

If the margin improvement is structural, as management claims, the EPS floor is significantly higher than in previous cycles. However, if the result was heavily influenced by one-offs, such as the SEK 55 mm gain in the Safety business area or SEK 66 mm in earn-out revaluations, then the range of outcomes for the next 18 months is more skewed toward a potential reversion.

Closely related questions include the sensitivity of Swedish serial acquirers to a higher-for-longer interest rate environment and the impact of a volatile Swedish krona (SEK). Addtech reported SEK 7,148 mm in variable-rate external debt as of March 31, 2026, with a sensitivity of SEK 57 mm in after-tax profit for every 1% change in rates.

Furthermore, with the Riksbank tilting hawkish in late 2026, the mechanical impact of debt servicing costs could act as a concentrated headwind that offsets diffuse operational gains. Investors must also weigh the risk of "roll-up" complexity, as seen in the reversal of compounding for peers like Storskogen, against Addtech's proven history of decentralized scaling.

The research identified approximately 45 relevant sources, covering 10 named peers and providing detailed historical precedents for industrial recoveries. While specific historical dispersion data for the first six quarters of past recoveries was thin in the retrieved snippets, the recent performance of peers like Indutrade, Lifco, and Lagercrantz provides a contemporary "industrial recovery" proxy. For instance, Indutrade's Q4 2025 results were marred by a SEK 125 mm UK project write-down, illustrating how concentrated operational failures can derail decentralized performance.

By synthesizing these data points, this report establishes a framework for evaluating Addtech's 18-month range of outcomes. From Q3 2026/27, Addtech is also set to face easier comparisons, positioning it well for an acceleration in organic earnings growth in the coming quarters, we believe.

Section 2 – Concentration of Growth and Margin Drivers

Section summary
  1. Addtech's earnings growth has shifted from volume-driven to margin-driven, with medium confidence that operational mix will remain the primary driver over 18 months.

Shift from Volume to Structural Margin

Addtech's recent financial performance suggests a significant concentration of drivers in gross margin expansion rather than organic volume growth. The company reported a record EBITA margin of 16.0% for the full year 2025/26, up from 15.0% the prior year, despite organic sales growth of only 2%. DNB Carnegie noted on October 23, 2026, that Addtech's EBITA grew 17% in Q2 2026/27, significantly outpacing the 2% organic revenue growth reported for the same period. This implies that earnings are becoming more concentrated in "valueadd" drivers like pricing power and product mix rather than broad industrial volume.

Management asserts that the improvement in operating margins is structural and driven by active work to increase value-add in their propositions. During the Q4 2026 earnings call on May 20, 2026, Addtech's CFO stated that all business areas increased both gross and profit margins, excluding the impact of earn-out revaluations. The company’s rolling 12-month EBITA margin has shown a positive trend for five consecutive years, suggesting that internal operational efficiency is a more persistent driver than cyclical timing. This structural shift separates the next 18 months from the last six years, where growth was more reliant on double-digit organic expansion.

Quarterly organic growth for Nordic technical distributors is significantly impacted by working-day and Easter effects, though brokers increasingly adjust for these mechanical shifts. Indutrade noted that its Q2 2025 organic sales decline of 4% was partly explained by "fewer working days," and adjusting for this would have resulted in a -2% decline. Addtech also experienced margin pressure in its Automation business area during Q3 2024/25 due to "calendar effects" and a weaker product mix. These shifts create temporary "noise" that can move reported growth by several percentage points in any single quarter.

However, for an investor focused on quarterly momentum, a two-day difference in working days can represent a 3% to 4% swing in revenue for a distributor. Addtech frequently reports earn-out revaluation gains that move quarterly EBITA growth by more than two percentage points. While this provides an "optical" boost to reported SEK earnings, it does not change the underlying organic performance of the subsidiaries. However, for an investor focused on reported EPS, the unhedged nature of Addtech's translation exposure (as confirmed in its 2025/2026 report) makes FX a primary driver of outcome skew.
