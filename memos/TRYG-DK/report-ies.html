Tryg (TRYG-DK) — Q3 2026 results: implications for the existing case

These results leave Viewforth's judgements on Tryg where they were and move the case modestly against us on its return: the quarter delivered the margin the case already credited, failed the growth test the case set for a changed view, and the shares rose to DKK 150.2 (14 October 2026), further from the price at which the case says the return clears the bar.

What changed

The company reported its third quarter to 30 September 2026 on 9 October 2026.

  • Net profit DKK 1,625m against consensus DKK 1,410m, a 15.2 percent beat; reported earnings per share DKK 2.68 against DKK 2.34; operating earnings per share DKK 2.96 against DKK 2.67. Profit before tax DKK 2,123m against DKK 1,865m.
  • Combined ratio 76.8 percent against consensus 78.3 percent and 78.6 percent in Q3 2025. Underlying claims ratio 66.4 percent, 20 basis points better than consensus and 60 basis points better than Q3 2025's 67.0 percent; the sequential improvement has run 40, 50 and 60 basis points through 2026. Weather claims were 48 percent below expectation, large claims 14 percent favourable, and run-off DKK 257m, 17 percent above expectation.
  • Insurance revenue DKK 10,591m against consensus DKK 10,577m. Local-currency growth 2.3 percent against 3.4 percent in Q3 2025. Commercial -0.7 percent in local currency on corporate churn from the January 2026 renewals; management says growth is unlikely to return to Danish Commercial until the back end of the strategy period. Full-year 2026 revenue growth guidance stays at around 3 percent in local currencies. The 2027 targets are unchanged: combined ratio about 81 percent, insurance service result DKK 8.0 to 8.4bn, return on own funds 35 to 40 percent.
  • Private retention rose in all three countries: Denmark 87.9 percent (87.7 percent in Q2 2026), Norway 87.0 percent (86.8 percent), Sweden 89.2 percent (89.0 percent). Customer satisfaction 83, the 2027 target. Year-to-date average sales up 12 percent across the segments.
  • Norway reached its mid-eighties combined ratio target in 2026, a year early, with the best reported Norwegian combined ratio in ten years. Management says the profitability initiatives run since 2025 are tapering as claims moderate.
  • Motor distribution: Mercedes-Benz in Sweden (about 23,000 vehicles a year, from January 2027), XPENG in Norway (from August 2026), Tesla in Denmark; the Norwegian Tesla agreement was lost to Gjensidige in July 2026, Tesla being nearly 20 percent of first-half 2026 Norwegian car sales.
  • Solvency II 203 percent against consensus 199 percent (204 percent in Q3 2025). Quarterly dividend DKK 2.15. The decision on an extraordinary buyback is deferred to the year-end solvency review. Property holdings DKK 1.9bn after a DKK 250m sale in July.
  • Net investment result DKK 42m against consensus of -DKK 54m. Danish and Norwegian central banks raised policy rates in the quarter after oil rose 40 percent on the Middle East escalation; the Danish five-year swap rate rose 0.82 percentage points. The company's stated sensitivity: 100 basis points higher rates improve the combined ratio by about 100 basis points through reserve discounting.
  • Sell-side: DNB Carnegie Buy, target DKK 190, 2026 adjusted earnings per share +4 percent to DKK 9, 2027 and 2028 flat at DKK 11.1 and DKK 11.9 on its basis. Jefferies Hold, target DKK 155 from DKK 152, operating earnings per share +2 to 3 percent across the horizon, 2027 and 2028 revenue growth +1 point to 5 percent a year. Morgan Stanley Underweight, target DKK 145 unchanged, calls the beat largely transitory and notes consensus models only 10 basis points of underlying improvement for 2027. Pre-results consensus: target DKK 166; 8 Buy, 7 Hold, 3 Sell; about 13 times 2027 expected earnings.
  • Price DKK 150.2 on 14 October 2026 against the case's reference of DKK 143.5 on 7 October, up 4.7 percent; 0.4 percent below the 200-day average of DKK 150.80, on the 50-day of DKK 149.86; relative-strength percentile 32; price-to-earnings 13.1x at the 4th percentile of ten years. Consensus earnings per share DKK 9.58 and target DKK 167.54; revisions +2, +2 and +3 percent on earnings over one, three and six months, -1, -2 and -4 percent on the target; buy share 40 percent.

Set against the case: the case's two tests for a changed view were local-currency revenue growth above 4 percent and underlying improvement above 60 basis points. The first failed outright at 2.3 percent, below the quarter a year earlier and below the guided 3 percent; the second landed on the line at 60 basis points. Every confirming level the case set was met: Danish Private retention above 87 percent and rising elsewhere; solvency above 185 percent; the dividend growing; underlying improvement above 30 basis points; no reserve charge. Consensus sits above the company's 2027 range and the company held its targets rather than raising them, the beat-and-hold behaviour the case describes. The quarter is, almost line for line, the case's own sentence: the business is better than the case.

The challenge

The results admit three readings the case does not hold. Two are strong on this quarter's evidence; the third is the one the case treats as unnecessary and is the weakest on the numbers reported, but a broker has now put it in a model.

First, that the margin is the mis-modelled line, and the case has closed the margin leg too early. The case holds the combined ratio at the about 81 percent target, has the margin contribute about 1.2 percent a year to insurance-service-result growth to 2027 and close to zero after, and rejects 'Compounder' as the primary setup because no line in consensus is shown to be wrong. The strongest form of the alternative: the underlying claims ratio has improved 40, 50 and 60 basis points in three successive quarters, accelerating not fading, to 66.4 percent; Morgan Stanley's pre-results model carried about 68 percent for 2026 through 2028, and consensus models 10 basis points of improvement for 2027; the Norwegian book has reached its target a year early; and the structural tools are not yet fully deployed, the underwriting platform at 60 percent of the commercial book against a target of 80 percent and worth two to three points of technical combined ratio where applied, straight-through processing at about 220,000 claims in the quarter, and about 7 percent of travel-assistance cost coming out from 2027. If the 2027 combined ratio lands at 79 to 80 percent rather than the 80.6 percent consensus carries, the insurance service result beats by a margin the framework would call a wrong line, and the margin leg keeps contributing beyond 2027. What would have to be true: that the 60 basis points is structural and holds on normalised weather; that Norway does not give back margin as rate rises moderate, which management itself says they are doing; and that run-off at 17 percent above expectation and the discounting benefit of higher rates are not the hidden drivers. The quarter's evidence against it is real: the headline beat was mostly weather, large claims and run-off, the underlying was only 20 basis points ahead of consensus, and DNB Carnegie left 2027 and 2028 flat. But the bar this reading has to clear is low precisely because the sell-side has modelled so little, and that is the condition under which a quality compounder is mis-modelled rather than merely cheap.

Second, that the re-rating leg's trigger has reversed, and even odds on it are generous. The case puts the re-rating at even odds at three years, names a Nordic rate cut cycle as the event that turns the leg, and runs its macro sensitivity one way only, a 100 basis point fall in rates as an 8 percent longer-term earnings drag. This quarter Danish and Norwegian policy rates went up, not down, and the Danish five-year swap rose 0.82 percentage points. On the case's own logic that cuts both ways and the case has priced only one: higher rates lift the reported combined ratio through discounting, so part of the 76.8 percent is a rate effect the sell-side will not credit to underwriting; and elevated rates are the sell-side's stated reason for the sector de-rating, the loss of scarcity value on bond-like yield. If the rate reversal persists, the uncleared branch at 14.5x is the modal branch rather than a coin toss, and the case's own no-re-rating path gives 9.5 percent a year from DKK 143.5, which it grades F. What would have to be true: that a quarter's oil-driven rate rise is a cycle and not a spike; that the cohort stays at the bottom of its range; and that the sell-side strips the discounting benefit when it reads the margin. Against it: the shares rose after the print, the investment result beat consensus by DKK 96m because the match portfolio did what it is built to do, and 13.1x at the 4th percentile of ten years already discounts a great deal of this.

Third, that the volume engine is earlier and larger than the case allows. The case makes revenue re-acceleration a Driver and not a Key Driver, assumes about 3.5 percent revenue growth, calls the partnerships months old and Commercial flat by design. The strongest form: year-to-date average sales are up 12 percent; retention rose in all three countries while prices were still being pushed; Mercedes-Benz alone is about 23,000 vehicles a year from January 2027; the chief executive's tone has moved in three quarters from not being in a hurry to grow to citing leading indicators; and Jefferies has now written 5 percent a year revenue growth for 2027 and 2028 into its model, above the case's number. What would have to be true: that sales convert into earned premium at the retention rates reported; that original-equipment-manufacturer motor business is written at a margin that does not consume the margin leg, which is the same trade Jefferies describes as trading margin for volume; and that Commercial churn ends sooner than management says. The quarter's hard number runs the other way, 2.3 percent local-currency growth, decelerating, with Commercial negative and the Norwegian Tesla book gone to a competitor. We put this reading up because it is now a broker's base case and not only management's, but on reported evidence it is the weakest of the three, and the case's rule, to assume persistence of the growth concern until hard evidence clears it, still applies.

What we are not putting up: a bear reading on the retail franchise. The false-friend profile the case carries at the foundations predicts retail decay beneath reserve releases, and this quarter's retention rose in all three countries, the Private underlying improved, and run-off was lower than the case recorded for Q2 2026 (DKK 368m). The profile's overturning condition moved further toward overturned, not nearer to confirmed.

What would settle it

  • The second Earnings Review, four working days on. It will carry the broker revisions that land after the print: whether FY2027 consensus moves off the case's DKK 9.20 by more than the 2 percent the case sets as its sell-side alarm, in which direction; whether anyone takes the 2027 combined ratio below the 80.6 percent consensus carried, which is the first reading's early test; whether a Sell is added or removed; the target and buy-share lines. It will not carry a revenue number, a buyback decision, a weather-normalised margin, or any disclosure on partnership premiums; those come on the company's calendar, not the brokers'.
  • Q4 2026 results, January 2027. The next revenue print; because Q3 failed the 4 percent test, the case's two-consecutive-quarters condition cannot now be met before the Q1 2027 report in April 2027 at the earliest. The buyback announcement, DKK 1.0bn the case's baseline and above DKK 2.0bn in February 2027 its test for a changed view on capital; the chief financial officer has deferred the decision to the year-end solvency review, so it is coming but not committed in size. The full-year combined ratio on budgeted weather and large claims, which is where the first reading is tested against the case's 82 percent invalidate level and 30 basis point alarm.
  • First-quarter 2027, April 2027. The first quarter with Mercedes-Benz premiums earning, and the first fair read on the third reading.
  • Disclosures the company has not committed to. The structural share of the margin gain, the 30 to 40 basis points that would separate repricing from efficiency; a normalised Norwegian underlying claims ratio for 2027 and 2028; the premium contribution per partnership. The review asks for all three; the company has promised none. Without them the first and third readings stay open questions of the kind that quarterly results narrow but do not close.
  • The rate path. Whether the Q3 policy-rate rises in Denmark and Norway hold or reverse; the case's reading of Norges Bank at 4.25 percent with a tightening bias was already the wrong-way reading, and no date settles it.
  • The Danish Competition and Consumer Authority's indexation conclusion, expected by mid-2027. Unchanged by these results; still the single largest named risk and still the case's invalidate trigger if it bans automatic indexation.
