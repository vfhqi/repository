Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. The Key Question can be partly answered with medium confidence. While headline re-letting uplifts appear robust across Paris and London office markets, the evidence reveals a widening gap between face rents and net effective rents driven by historically elevated concessions, particularly in peripheral Paris submarkets. The prime rental growth window remains open for three to five years in central London and hyper-core Paris locations, but this trajectory depends critically on the absorption of a substantial supply peak in Paris during 2027 and the maintenance of severe supply constraints in the London West End through 2030. The central investor inquiry examines how far net effective rents have risen in 2025 and 2026 against reported headline uplifts, how much new top-grade office space will complete from 2027 to 2030, what share is pre-let, and for how many more years prime rents can keep rising before new completions cap them. Gecina reported average headline rental uplifts of 13% in the first half of 2026 on 48,000 square meters of signed leases, with central leasing momentum continuing through the third quarter at 11% average uplifts on renewals and relettings. Colonial captured exceptional release spreads of 26% in Paris during the first half of 2026, accelerating to 32% in the second quarter, with signed rents exceeding December 2025 market rents by 5% and supported by high-profile signings at Louvre Saint-Honoré near €1,100 per square meter. However, these impressive headline figures obscure a more challenging underlying reality in economic rental growth.

Greater Paris office rent concessions remained at an all-time high of 31% of headline rents in October 2026, meaning net effective rents in secondary locations continue to slide despite stable face rents. Average concessions rose to 32.2% across Greater Paris, with Central Paris maintaining relative resilience at 15.5%, while La Defense deteriorated sharply to 42.5%, approaching its historical peak. Gecina signed two long-term leases in La Defense covering 29,000 square meters with incentives near 30%, resulting in a painful negative 50% rental reversion on its Building B asset. This geographic bifurcation between prime central business districts and peripheral submarkets creates a fundamental structural divide that directly threatens the earnings quality of landlords with material exposure to fringe office locations. The supply outlook presents sharply divergent trajectories for Paris and London that will dictate the duration of the rental growth window. Paris office deliveries will drop from 377,000 square meters in 2026 and 272,000 square meters in 2027 to a mere 70,000 square meters annually from 2028 under strict bioclimatic regulations, which will severely restrict future supply and help central landlords maintain high occupancy levels. Completions will peak at 314,000 square meters in 2027, with 228,000 square meters remaining unlet at delivery, meaning near-term rental growth could soften as tenants gain bargaining leverage and landlords offer higher incentives to absorb this supply. This near-term oversupply peak in Paris contrasts sharply with the structurally constrained London pipeline.

Average London West End completions from 2027 to 2029 are projected to fall below the ten-year average, and this tightening supply combined with strong occupier demand is expected to drive prime rental growth higher. Pre-letting activity in London demonstrates robust corporate appetite for premium workspace well ahead of scheduled completions, effectively de-risking speculative development pipelines. IJM secured a 20-year lease with Simmons & Simmons for 62% of its 250,000 square foot 25 Finsbury Circus project slated for early 2028 delivery, while Gamuda is negotiating pre-lets for its 490,000 square foot 75 London Wall project ahead of its firstquarter 2028 completion.

JP Morgan Asset Management secured a massive 470,000 square foot pre-let with Jane Street at One Spitalfields, covering 64% of the building's total space ahead of its scheduled 2029 delivery, demonstrating deep corporate demand for premium capital workspace. These multi-year pre-commitments provide substantial visibility into sustained tenant demand and reduce the risk of speculative vacancy shocks that could prematurely cap rental growth.

The estimated duration of prime rental growth varies significantly by geography and asset quality. Savills forecasts prime rental growth of 4.3% across Central London over the next four years because occupier demand remains highly polarized, suggesting that Central London landlords can raise rents well into the next decade before supply caps them. Morgan Stanley noted that JLL estimates cumulative rental growth of 17% for the best offices in London over the next three years, supported by a London office supply pipeline that is materially below historical take-up levels across the capital.

In contrast, the Paris rental outlook faces a temporary growth pause during the 2027 supply absorption phase before regulatory supply restrictions reactivate rental growth from 2028 onwards. Paris office deliveries will drop markedly to just 62,000 square meters in 2028 under strict new bioclimatic PLU rules, which will severely restrict future supply and help central landlords maintain high occupancy levels, with Paris CBD rents expected to remain resilient.

Consensus broker forecasts currently assume that headline rental uplifts will translate into sustained earnings per share growth for prime office landlords, with limited recognition of the widening gap between face and net effective rents. Deutsche Bank reported in June 2026 that Colonial's development pipeline is the largest contributor to its mid-term earnings growth target, though higher interest expenses are projected to partially offset these positive operational drivers, with projected recurring EPS of €0.39 to €0.41 by 2028 potentially facing downward pressure if net effective rents flatten behind headline uplifts.

Morgan Stanley reported that British Land's rental growth in the first half of 2026 more than offset yield pressure, and the broker upgraded its earnings estimates for Derwent London following healthy leasing activity in the West End, though a surge in completions from 2028 could shorten the window for accretive lease renewals. Structural shifts in occupier behavior pose medium-term risks to consensus earnings trajectories across both markets. Hybrid working has structurally severed the historical link between headcount growth and office square footage demand, meaning corporate occupiers are shrinking their footprints while focusing exclusively on topgrade sustainable office spaces in core locations, and consequently secondary assets will face severe vacancy pressure while prime central assets capture all marginal demand. Remote job postings in France are rising again, and available office supply continues to rise faster than take-up in core Paris markets, indicating persistent structural weakness that could shorten the prime rental window and depress intermediate landlord earnings.

This demand elasticity introduces execution risk for landlords that fail to concentrate their portfolios in the highest-quality central business district submarkets. Early warning signals suggest that consensus expectations may underestimate downside risks to net effective rental growth and earnings compression. Paris central business district vacancy surged to 7.3% in October 2026, representing a substantial 230 basis point increase, with speculative space under construction potentially pushing central vacancy past the critical 8% threshold, which will shift tenant bargaining power and cap central rental growth.

Paris office valuations fell on negative revaluations as prospective tenants delayed lease decisions, with investment transaction prices in La Defense more than halving from their 2021 peak levels, and property devaluations will reduce net asset values per share across the sector. These deteriorating metrics in previously resilient submarkets represent inflection points that could accelerate the compression of the rental growth window if tenant leverage continues to strengthen through 2027.

The most critical evidence gap centers on the lack of transparency regarding private tenant concessions and undisclosed fit-out contributions that mask true economic rental pricing. Paris and London commercial property markets suffer from a lack of transparency regarding private tenant incentives and fit-out contributions, with nominal asking rents being kept artificially high to protect existing property valuations and support new trophy developments, making face rents unreliable.

Closing this information gap through structured expert interviews with regional letting agents and detailed analysis of lease accounting disclosures will be essential to quantify the true magnitude of net effective rent compression and refine the estimated duration of the prime rental growth window. Without this granular visibility, investors face material uncertainty regarding the sustainability of reported earnings trajectories and the timing of potential rental caps from upcoming supply completions.
