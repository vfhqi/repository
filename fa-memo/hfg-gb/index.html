The reasoning behind the inputs

Part 4 is the second set's reasoning as written. Where a later set changed an input, that input's figure in the tables above is the changed one and its reasons are in the changed-inputs file named above; Part 4 is not rewritten.

Conventions used in Parts 1 to 3 (read first). - EBITDA basis: the company's adjusted operating profit (continuing operations: excluding Fairfax Meadow, Foods Connected and Dalco) plus all depreciation, including right-of-use (IFRS 16) depreciation, excluding amortisation of acquired intangibles. EBITDA less the depreciation and amortisation line equals our adjusted operating profit: £95.5m, £101.0m, £108.0m, £116.5m, £123.9m. This EBITDA is about £24m a year higher than the brokers' EBITDA (see the bridge below). - Revenue base: FY2026 growth of 9.6 percent is measured on FactSet's FY2025 revenue of £4,214.6m (the figure in the model's history), giving FY2026 revenue of about £4,619m. FY2025 restated for Dalco (moved to discontinued operations in the H1 FY26 accounts) is about £4,108m on our derivation (FactSet's H1 FY25 £1,986m plus H2 FY25 £2,122m), on which FY2026 growth is about 12.5 percent. If the model's FY2025 base is the restated £4,108m, the FY2026 input should be 12.4 percent to reach the same £4,619m. Absolute revenue: £4,619m, £4,790m, £4,991m, £5,191m, £5,373m. - Net financial items: the company's adjusted net finance cost, including lease interest (about £7.6m a year), bank interest and interest on customer-provided supply-chain finance (about £8.5m a year), net of interest income. - Lease payments: the principal element only. Lease interest is already inside net financial items; entering total lease cash would double count it. - Dividend per share: in pounds (0.350 = 35.0p), as FactSet holds UK per-share figures. - Not in the inputs, but in our cross-check: minorities of £1.0m, £1.0m, £1.1m, £1.2m and £1.3m (the model's EPS will be about 1.1p higher than ours for this reason), and exceptional cash costs (restructuring, transformation, Foppen disruption) of £20m, £12m, £8m, £6m and £5m (the model's free cash flow will be higher than ours by these amounts). - NADEC (49 percent Saudi joint venture, equity-accounted): its share of profit (£0, £0, £0.7m, £1.3m, £1.5m) is included in EBITDA and operating profit for simplicity; it is non-cash unless distributed.

How to read these figures

  • Amounts are in millions of the reporting currency; per-share figures are in units of that currency. Negative figures are shown in brackets.
  • Historical figures use FactSet's standardised lines, which can differ from the company's own presentation.
  • Earnings per share appears on four bases, which must not be mixed: reported basic; FactSet's adjusted diluted, before unusual items; the Viewforth definition, which is the model's standing convention; and the brokers' adjusted basis, on which consensus is based.
  • Consensus is FactSet's median of the broker estimates it holds. Where FactSet's line-by-line consensus differs from the broker-by-broker detail, the line-by-line table takes precedence.
  • Viewforth's forecasts are the Excel model's base case. There are no bull or bear cases.
