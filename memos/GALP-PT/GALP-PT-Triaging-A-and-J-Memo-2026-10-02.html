[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = D

Thesis. Galp is a self-help and portfolio-reshaping case riding a commodity peak, not a demand inflection and not a recovery. The internal change is broad, mostly completed and visible along the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain, but the earnings it has produced belong mostly to a refining margin the company does not set, and at EUR 21.97 the shares already pay for the peak year and for Namibian oil that will not flow before 2032. On our modal path the total return is -9 percent over eighteen months and -1 percent over three years against the framework's bar of 20 percent a year.

  • The change inside the company is real, broad and completed, which is the strongest thing in the case.
    • Inside twenty months Galp replaced a chief executive who resigned in January 2025 with two permanent co-chief executives in April 2025, reset the strategy to "value over volume", sold Angola for USD 830 million and Mozambique Area 4 for USD 881 million of cash, farmed down 40 percent of its Mopane discoveries offshore Namibia to TotalEnergies for a 50 percent capex carry and a stake in Venus (completed 3 September 2026), brought Bacalhau to first oil in October 2025, capped its renewables, set out to combine its downstream with Moeve, raised FY2026 EBITDA guidance from more than EUR 2.6 billion to about EUR 4.0 billion and raised the 2026 dividend floor 10 percent to EUR 0.70.
    • The framework's screen for internal change is passed on both of its tests: at least three forces strong, and chief-executive change plus a guidance increase.
    • The two largest actions still to come, the Moeve signing and a renewables partner, are unsigned, and the leadership that delivered the rest is an untested pair on a mandate that ends in 2026.
  • The change has travelled all six layers of the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain, and that is why the market has already seen it.
    • It shows in the board's choices, in the actions, in the operating metrics (production from 111 thousand barrels of oil equivalent a day in 2025 to about 130 thousand in 2026), in the financial statements (H1 2026 RCA EBITDA of EUR 2,215 million against EUR 3,039 million for all of 2025) and in the sell-side's estimates (FY2026 consensus EPS up 80 percent in twelve months, from a low of EUR 1.17 to EUR 2.30 mean).
    • The shares have moved with the change: +53.3 percent over nine months and +32.0 percent over twelve months to 2026-10-01.
  • Most of the output change is the commodity's, not the company's.
    • Sines realised a refining margin of USD 16.80 a barrel in 2Q26 against a mid-cycle USD 6.50, and Brent ran above USD 90 a barrel.
    • The company's own second-half deck (Brent USD 70, refining USD 10) and every broker deck normalise both, and consensus already has EPS falling from EUR 2.23 in FY2026 to EUR 1.94, 1.65 and 1.53 in FY2027 to FY2029.
    • The company-specific change that reaches earnings inside eighteen months is Bacalhau's 40 thousand barrels a day net at plateau from late 2026.
      • Namibia's cash is 2032 or later.
      • The Moeve distribution is unsigned.
    • So 'Financial Outputs' are very positive for one year and then fall over eighteen months and three years, which fails the framework's conditional rule for the Element: a very positive near term needs only plausible logic for continuing, and that logic is absent.
  • The price already pays for the peak year and for the Namibia option.
    • At EUR 21.97 the shares trade at 12.35 times 24-month-forward earnings, their ten-year median (12.30 times) and the 71st percentile of their five-year range, about two turns above the Southern European integrateds (Repsol 8.0 times, Eni 9.6, OMV 9.8, MOL 8.9).
    • Redburn says the implied value of Mopane is already fully priced
      • Redburn and Morgan Stanley both attribute the premium to Namibia visibility.
  • The modal return does not clear the bar; it is negative.
    • On our path the 24-month-forward earnings the multiple applies to fall from EUR 1.776 now to about EUR 1.65 at the eighteen-month horizon and EUR 1.68 at three years, the multiple blends down to 11.65 times across the Namibia branches, and the cash return of about 4.7 percent a year (3.2 percent dividend plus 1.5 percent buyback) is the only positive component.
    • Value including cash returns EUR 19.93 at 2028-04-01 (-9.3 percent; -6.3 percent a year) and EUR 21.78 at 2029-10-01 (-0.9 percent; -0.3 percent a year).
    • The case clears the framework's bar only on the branch where the Mopane appraisal succeeds and the market re-rates the shares for cash it will not see before 2032
      • We put that branch at 30 percent, possible, not probable.
  • The pattern is 'Major Corporate Change', fitted at a C.
    • The input half of the pattern is crisp
    • The output half carries the refining crack's signature rather than the company's, and the EPS trajectory beyond FY2026 is down on consensus and on our path.

  • The case is therefore a good company whose change has been fully rewarded at this price.

    • It carries one binary branch (the three-well Mopane campaign from the fourth quarter of 2026) that could lift the return towards the bar and one (a disappointing appraisal) that takes about a third off the price.

  • Rating = D, because the Building Block measures materiality and the expected change is not material enough at this price: strong input forces ('Change Forces' at B) drive outputs that are very positive for one year and then fall ('Financial Outputs' at D), and the modal total return from EUR 21.97 is below zero at eighteen months and flat at three years ('Modal return' at F).

    • The parts combine by binding: a B on forces cannot carry an F on the return when the return is the quantity the Block exists to test.
    • Not a C, which was live on the strength of the forces, because the modal return is not merely short of the bar but negative.
    • Not an F because the change forces are real, the balance sheet and the cash return are among the best in the set, and the eighteen-month path has a credible upside branch (Mopane appraisal success with a re-rating; a Moeve special distribution) that would lift the return towards the bar.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The strongest case against our reading is Morgan Stanley's: Galp is a defensive, high-quality, capital-light holding that should trade at a premium to the European integrateds, because its gearing is falling to 19 percent by 2027, its free cash flow yield is about 9 percent for 2026-27, its dividend can grow about 7.5 percent a year to the late 2030s, it holds a 40 percent stake in a 10 billion barrels-in-place discovery for which the operator pays half the capex, and the Moeve combination and a renewables deconsolidation can crystallise EUR 1.2 billion and EUR 2.5 billion of value.
    • The evidence for it is real: the balance sheet at 0.40 times net debt to EBITDA, the EUR 0.70 dividend floor, the operator quality of TotalEnergies, Equinor and Petrobras, and the Bacalhau volumes.
    • We concede all of that, and we concede that at 12.35 times the shares are at their ten-year median and not at a mania level.
    • We hold our view because the case as a return from EUR 21.97 needs one of three things we do not have: a commodity above the consensus deck through 2028, which is not ours to call
      • A Namibia re-rating for cash that arrives in 2032 or 2033 and is already partly in a two-turn premium to the cohort
      • Or a signed Moeve deal with a confirmed special distribution.
    • Even on the 'defendable' ten-year band of 14.20 times, the multiple the competing hypothesis implies, the eighteen-month value on our earnings path is 14.20 × 1.65 + 0.71 = EUR 24.14, +9.9 percent, +6.5 percent a year, still a third of the bar
      • Only the 'mania' band of 17.20 times (value about EUR 29.1, +32 percent, +20 percent a year) clears it.
    • The competing hypothesis describes a good company at a full price, which is not an 'Investment Thesis' at the framework's bar.
