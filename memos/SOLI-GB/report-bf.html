Section 4 – Value Chain Map + Solid State's Position + Margin Distribution

Section summary
  1. Solid State operates in high-margin design-in and manufacturing stages, while leveraging its Components division as a high-touch procurement and diagnostic funnel.

Value Chain Mapping and Solid State's Position

1.
Upstream Semiconductor Fabrication: Silicon, advanced gases, and raw substrate materials are processed by global wafer foundries like TSMC to manufacture bare chips. This stage exhibits extreme capital intensity, often exceeding 30% of sales, and is dominated by multi-billion-dollar operators. Solid State has zero direct presence in this stage and relies entirely on external component manufacturers.
2.
Specialist Component Manufacturing: OEMs like Vishay or XP Power convert silicon wafers into discrete electronic components, MOSFETs, capacitors, or power control modules. This stage requires proprietary technical intellectual property (IP) and advanced manufacturing sites, generating gross margins of 30% to 42%. Solid State has highly selective, niche manufacturing capabilities here, specifically in specialized RF antennas via its Q-Par subsidiary.
3.
High-Touch Value-Added Distribution: Technical distributors like Solsta (Solid State's Components division) act as specialized intermediaries. They provide early-stage design-in services for complex industrial and aerospace clients, locking in components into long-term product lifecycles. This stage earns gross margins of 25% to 27% and serves as a vital diagnostic funnel for downstream systems.
