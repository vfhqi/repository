Mapping the Key Question

1.
Critical key question: The central query asks "how much pricing power does this company have, and how does it hold up across different operating conditions, specifically: - marine raw-material price spikes (the 2026 El Niño, with Peruvian fish oil and fishmeal at record prices); - raw-material deflation (2023 to 2025); - an energyprice shock (2022, and the 2026 oil spike); - consolidation and backward integration among salmon farmers; - salmon biomass shocks (biology, Norwegian and Chilean regulation); - Chinese export pricing and overcapacity (shrimp feed in Ecuador and Vietnam, European auto parts, Asian spunmelt nonwovens); - industrial destocking (EMS); - component allocation (memory chips); - moves in the Norwegian krone and the US dollar?"
2.
Elevated operational leverage: Understanding pricing transmission is critical because Schouw & Co's consolidated EBITDA guidance has risen to DKK 3.15 billion to DKK 3.35 billion for 2026. DNB Carnegie raised their 2026e adjusted EPS estimates by 14% to DKK 59.0 after the strong Q2 report. Operating margins directly dictate whether the company can meet its long-term targets, which means pricing failures would severely depress adjusted EPS over the next 18 months.
3.
SOTP valuation sensitivity: Investors apply a 10% conglomerate discount to Schouw's sum-of-the-parts equity value. This model incorporates standalone valuations for BioMar, GPV, and HydraSpecma, where margins dictate implied enterprise values. If segment-level pricing power deteriorates, the market is likely to widen the holding company discount beyond the historical average, which would suppress the stock's target price from its current DKK 920 level.

Operational Levers and Risks

4.
Aquafeed cost pass-through: BioMar utilizes cost-plus contracts to pass volatile raw material costs directly to salmon farmers. However, rapid raw material inflation can create temporary timing mismatches that squeeze margins. If BioMar cannot maintain its profit per tonne during the 2026 El Niño marine cost spike, segment EBIT would contract, depressing Schouw's consolidated earnings.
5.
Contrary evidence: Some brokers argue that raw material price fluctuations are passed on without impacting long-term earnings or cash flows. DNB Carnegie notes that BioMar's cost-plus contracts and extensive hedging provide high earnings visibility over 3 to 5 years. This means the holding company is insulated from localized agricultural and marine price shocks, unless contract renewals face intense customer resistance.
6.
Multi-industry margin pressures: Other portfolio segments like Borg Automotive face intense price competition from cheap Chinese imports. Meanwhile, Fibertex Personal Care is reducing capacity in Malaysia to counter Asian nonwovens overcapacity. Failure to recover energy and labor cost spikes in these divisions would drag down consolidated EBITDA, making the group's 2030 EBITDA target of DKK 4.5 billion difficult to achieve.
7.
Other side of the story: The diversified conglomerate model provides structural resilience because different industrial segments experience offsetting supply and demand cycles. Schouw's management argues that strength in hydraulics and nonwovens can offset temporary weakness in salmon feed or automotive parts. Thus, weak pricing power in any single division is unlikely to derail consolidated EPS growth unless a systemic global recession occurs.

Section 3 – Upstream, Downstream, and Species Shocks on BioMar

Section summary
  1. BioMar demonstrates robust operational resilience across historical biological and macroeconomic shocks.
  2. Customer consolidation in Norway created volume headwinds but absolute margins per tonne remained intact.
  3. The strategic shift to shrimp feed expands volume runway but dilutes group average profit per tonne.

Historical Shock Resilience

1.
Biological resilience: BioMar managed to protect its structural earnings during historical biological events such as the 2016 Chilean algae bloom and the 2019 Norwegian algae event because of its geographic diversification. DNB Carnegie's report of July 9, 2026, notes that serving multiple species across diverse regions isolates BioMar's overall performance from localized biomass disasters. This geographic footprint ensures that local biological shocks do not permanently impair consolidated earnings, which stabilizes long-term earnings per share even during extreme environmental volatility.
2.
Energy cost recovery: BioMar successfully navigated the severe energy price spikes of 2022 by adjusting its pricing mechanisms to recover utility inflation. Schouw & Co's interim report of August 16, 2022, shows that despite escalating energy and freight costs of DKK 95 million, selling prices were raised to increasingly reflect these inputs. This commercial agility allowed the group to preserve absolute gross profit per tonne, which means consolidated earnings per share remained insulated from utility inflation once the pass-through lags resolved.
3.
Margin defense: BioMar protected its absolute earnings per tonne during the raw-material deflationary period from 2023 to 2025 through commercial discipline. DNB Carnegie's report of July 9, 2026, reveals that BioMar's cost-plus pricing structures enabled a high degree of rawmaterial pass-through, protecting gross profit per tonne while revenues moved lower. This indicates that commoditized input deflation does not threaten absolute profitability, which means that underlying feed earnings remain highly stable across raw-material cycles.

Downstream Consolidation Dynamics

4.
Consolidated volume contraction: BioMar experienced an 11% year-on-year drop in Norwegian salmon feed volumes during the second quarter of 2026 due to downstream customer consolidation. BioMar's interim report of August 13, 2026, attributes this expected drop to a contract loss from a consolidated client, though pricing on retained volumes remained resilient. This volume decline did not damage absolute segment profitability because Atlantic salmon EBIT per tonne actually increased to DKK 834 from DKK 763 in the prior-year period.
5.
Vertical integration risks: BioMar faces ongoing competition from customer vertical integration as large salmon farmers evaluate captive feed options. Morgan Stanley's report of July 8, 2026, states that Mowi concluded its feed strategic review by establishing an agreement with competitor Skretting to share research and development under a service framework. This non-equity partnership suggests that full-scale backward integration remains difficult for most farmers, which protects BioMar's addressable commercial market from rapid captive feed replacement.
6.
Peer integration paths: Salmon farmers display divergent feed strategies, with Bakkafrost operating as the most vertically integrated peer. DNB Carnegie's report of October 6, 2026, highlights that Bakkafrost produces its own salmon feed via its Havsbrun subsidiary, whereas other top-ten players like SalMar rely on supplier partnerships. This structural division indicates that while self-sufficiency is a competitive differentiator for Bakkafrost, other major salmon producers will continue to outsource their nutritional requirements to independent suppliers like BioMar to limit capital expenditure.
7.
Supplier research alliances: Salmon producers are increasingly initiating research partnerships with feed suppliers to improve biological performance. SalMar's registration document of August 17, 2026, notes that SalMar and Cargill launched the Salmon Living Lab to solve aquaculture challenges. This collaborative initiative demonstrates that major farmers prefer strategic co-development over full backward integration, which strengthens long-term commercial ties and supports BioMar's partnership-focused commercial model during contract renewals.

Species Mix and Margin Trajectories

8.
Dilutive species mix: BioMar's volume shift toward shrimp feed is structurally dilutive to the group's average absolute EBIT per tonne. BioMar's interim report of August 13, 2026, reveals that shrimp feed carries a lower absolute EBIT per tonne than salmon feed due to different raw-material requirements. However, this product mix shift does not threaten overall return on capital because shrimp segment EBIT margins remain broadly inline with those achieved in the core Salmon division.
9.
Rapid shrimp expansion: BioMar achieved record-high volumes in its Shrimp division during the second quarter of 2026, cementing its market position. Schouw & Co's interim report of August 14, 2026, reports that shrimp feed volumes rose 18% year-on-year, driven by market share gains in Ecuador. This rapid volume expansion provides a vital growth engine for Schouw, which means shrimp feed will drive consolidated revenues even as mature salmon markets experience slower biological volume growth.
10.
Contrary evidence: Intensive competitive pricing in Vietnam and Ecuador threatens to compress BioMar's long-term shrimp feed profitability. Morgan Stanley's report of August 12, 2026, warns that lower average sales prices on key corporate accounts and aggressive regional competition are weighing on segment EBIT per tonne. This pricing pressure could prevent BioMar from fully capturing the financial benefits of its volume growth, which would depress consolidated holding company earnings unless raw-material costs decline.

Competitive Pressures in Ecuador

11.
Strategic Ecuadorian expansion: BioMar is doubling its production capacity in Ecuador to capitalize on the region's strong aquaculture fundamentals. DNB Carnegie's report of July 9, 2026, notes that BioMar's local capacity will rise to 410,000 tonnes by late 2026 with an initial 110,000-tonne expansion. This capital investment positions the group to capture incremental volume share in the world's most competitive shrimp market, which will accelerate absolute EBIT growth once the new lines are commissioned.
12.
Toll-milling margin dilution: BioMar utilized external manufacturing partners in Ecuador to support its rapid sales growth ahead of its own capacity commissioning. BioMar's interim report of August 13, 2026, notes that strong volume growth required external toll-milling, which increased near-term production costs and diluted segment profitability. This margin dilution is expected to be temporary, because commissioning the upcoming Duran factory expansion will eliminate these external manufacturing fees and restore optimal operating margins.
13.
Chinese competitive pricing: Chinese feed exporters and domestic producers present a significant competitive threat in the Ecuadorian shrimp feed market. DNB Carnegie's report of July 9, 2026, warns that aggressive pricing from Chinese shrimp feed producers could intensify local margin pressure for global players. This price competition forces BioMar to rely on advanced technical advisory services and acoustic feeding systems to defend its market share, ensuring long-term customer retention through performance rather than price-based competition.
14.
Other side of the story: Downstream farmer consolidation and aggressive feed competition could permanently erode BioMar's pricing power across both major species segments. Morgan Stanley's report of August 12, 2026, suggests that the sheer scale of raw-material inflation combined with declining farm-gate prices may force BioMar to absorb some input costs to protect its market share. If these commercial pressures persist, cost-plus frameworks will fail to protect absolute earnings, leading to margin compression for Schouw's consolidated feed business.
