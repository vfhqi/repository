Outliers worth noting

The bear evidence assembled across Part 1 reveals three extreme statistical outliers and structural anomalies that warrant heightened scrutiny. These findings represent material deviations from peer norms or internally inconsistent operational patterns that could signal latent vulnerabilities in Asmodee's standalone business model. The first and most glaring outlier concerns the uncontracted distribution arrangements underpinning 35% of total net sales, where Pokémon represents 20% of revenue and Magic represents 15% without any long-term contract in place.

This represents an extreme concentration of revenue dependency on informal commercial relationships that lack contractual protection. The Pokémon TCG distribution relationship operates without a formal written agreement, relying instead on a 20-year historical partnership that is paradoxically consistent with all three ACH profiles. The magnitude of this exposure is unprecedented among publicly traded specialty retailers, where peer firms typically secure multi-year distribution agreements for products representing even 5- 10% of revenue.

The peculiarity deepens when contrasted with the highly concentrated ownership structure where founder Lars Wingefors controls 16.9% of capital, suggesting management possesses sufficient negotiating leverage yet has chosen not to formalize these critical relationships. This oddity creates a binary failure mode where relationship termination would trigger immediate revenue collapse without contractual recourse or adequate lead time for alternative arrangements. [Cross-ref: Section 2, Section 6, Section 13] The second outlier manifests in the extraordinary volatility of currency translation reserves, which expanded from negative EUR 1 million in 2025 to negative EUR 27 million in 2026, representing a 2,600% deterioration in a single fiscal year.

This translation reserve movement far exceeds the volatility observed in peer toy manufacturers operating with comparable geographic footprints and significantly larger absolute revenue bases. Realized operating foreign exchange losses escalated to EUR 12 million in 2026 compared to EUR 8 million in 2025, compounding the reserve impact with tangible cash flow friction. The anomaly intensifies when juxtaposed against the company's historical policy of not hedging FX exposure despite generating revenues in various noneuro transactional currencies, with translation exposure remaining completely unhedged. This represents a structurally incoherent risk management posture for a EUR 1.7 billion revenue enterprise operating across 20+ currencies, particularly given that a 10% USD strengthening reduces equity by EUR 10,299 thousand in reserves while hurting profit or loss by EUR 8,940 thousand on net basis.

The lack of hedging discipline appears inconsistent with the company's stated commitment to balance sheet stability and creates unexplained exposure to macro volatility that management cannot operationally control. [Cross-ref: Section 17, Section 19, Section 20]

The third outlier concerns the severe divergence in publishing mix performance, where games published by partners grew 27% while internal studios grew only 1.8% in Q1 2027, a trend inconsistent with the YES profile but highly consistent with the FALSE FRIEND hypothesis. This 1,400 basis point growth spread between partnerpublished and owned-studio titles represents the widest performance gap in Asmodee's disclosed history and sits in stark contrast to management's strategic emphasis on high-margin first-party development.

Partner games now constitute 64% of revenues but deliver half the profitability of owned studios, creating a margin dilution paradox where top-line acceleration masks underlying studio decay. The anomaly becomes more pronounced when considering that disclosed publishing mix shifts confirm partner-published games grew 27% while internal studios flatlined organically, indicating systematic underperformance rather than temporary release timing effects.

This pattern directly contradicts the bull thesis predicated on evergreen portfolio resilience and first-party IP development, suggesting that explosive distributed TCG sales growth masks systemic maturity and declines in first-party board games rather than complementing them. The oddity is compounded by audited non-cash impairments of EUR 23.6 million on back-catalogue rights, which are inconsistent with the YES profile but consistent with NO and FALSE FRIEND scenarios, implying that management's internal valuations of owned IP assets are deteriorating even as partner-distributed products surge.
