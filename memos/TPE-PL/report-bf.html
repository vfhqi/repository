EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

BUSINESS MODEL CORE Tauron operates as Poland's largest electricity distributor, serving 6.05 million customers across a densely populated southern industrial heartland that represents 18.5% of national territory.

The company's business model centers on a highly predictable regulated distribution monopoly that generates stable cash flows through state-approved tariffs, while simultaneously managing a transitioning generation portfolio still dominated by 81% carbon-intensive coal capacity despite rapid renewable capacity expansion.

The distribution division benefits from natural monopoly economics with visible recurring revenues insulated from volume volatility through regulatory account mechanisms, whereas conventional generation operations face severe margin pressure from elevated carbon compliance costs and depressed clean dark spreads.

Strategic transformation is underway with unprecedented National Recovery and Resilience Plan funding exceeding PLN 16 billion in low-interest loans at exceptionally attractive 0.5% annual rates, supporting grid modernization initiatives and renewable development while the group executes organizational restructuring to separate clean energy units and establish dedicated hydrogen-focused divisions.

The regulated distribution network provides the foundational cash engine while structural state-backed financing de-risks the capital-intensive transition away from legacy fossil fuel generation assets.

FINANCIAL PERFORMANCE AND PEER POSITIONING Tauron's consolidated sales revenue reached PLN 33,277 million in 2025, positioning the company within a highly concentrated Polish utility sector where four state-controlled integrated groups dominate 52% of generation activities.

Group EBITDA expanded robustly to PLN 7.5 billion in 2025, representing 16% year-on-year growth driven primarily by strengthened distribution margins, although adjusted profitability reflects PLN 146 million in provisions for onerous household supply contracts and PLN 277 million in write-offs to the Price Difference Payout Fund.

The company maintains a conservative leverage profile with net debt to EBITDA standing at 1.4x at end-2025, providing substantial headroom below banking covenant limits of 3.5x to 4.0x, while subordinated hybrid bonds receive favorable exclusion from leverage calculations.

Capital expenditures increased to PLN 5,641 million in 2025, up 10% year-on-year, as the transition strategy demands heavy grid investment, though unlevered cash conversion faces restriction from substantial CO2 emission allowance liabilities settled through working capital.

Peer benchmarking reveals Tauron's EBITDA margin of 22.57% tracks closely with Enea's 22.92% but lags behind Energa's 25% margin, while PGE's superior scale advantages through lignite-heavy generation provide structural cost insulation unavailable to hard coal-reliant domestic competitors.

Net income rose to PLN 3,315 million in 2025 due to higher distribution margins, but historical earnings faced over PLN 1.5 billion in impairments on conventional generation assets, signaling persistent asset quality challenges in the legacy thermal fleet.

REGULATED DISTRIBUTION MOAT AND VALUE CAPTURE The electricity utility value chain exhibits a stark profitability divide where regulated distribution operates as the natural monopoly profit engine capturing the highest share of value accrual, while supply remains an inherently low-margin customer management layer and conventional generation suffers from volatile competitive market dynamics.

Tauron commands the largest distribution share in Poland, delivering power to 6.05 million industrial and residential customers, with the four major state-controlled groups collectively controlling 52% of domestic power generation and retail customer supply through vertically integrated operations.

Distribution remains the fundamental driver of utility group profitability under stable regulatory tariff schemes, where regulated revenues grow consistently as heavy network investments expand Regulated Asset Base values over time, with tariffs reflecting a lucrative baseline return on company invested capital calculated using WACC formulas that regulators set at a generous 10.48% WACC plus reinvestment premium during recent fiscal grid tariff reviews, while the introduced regulatory account mechanism automatically offsets volume deviations with a stable two-year delay.

Distribution captures estimated 40% gross margins and 15% EBIT margins versus generation's volatile 15% gross and 5% EBIT margins, with group strategy concentrating PLN 60 billion of capex exclusively in the distribution segment until 2035, where networks captured approximately 67.9% of total capital expenditure during recent reporting periods.

Polish utilities face severe carbon compliance liabilities as Polish power generation remains heavily reliant on fossil fuels representing 86.5% of total supply, though solar PV generation grew to 11.7% of total power in 2025 from 0.4% in 2019 displacing coal, while the average EU carbon allowance price rose to EUR 74.90 per Mg in fiscal year 2025, prompting Poland to lead a 10-country coalition demanding a slower linear reduction factor for EU ETS allowances by proposing flattening the linear factor to just over 2% from the current 4.4% level, with Polish opposition parties introducing a bill in late 2026 to unilaterally withdraw Poland from the EU ETS. CUSTOMER CONCENTRATION AND REGULATORY TARIFF CONSTRAINTS Tauron's retail supply division maintains a broad and diversified consumer footprint as Poland's second largest retail electricity supplier with a stable 19% market share, serving 5.83 million total final customers and supplying 25.17 TWh of retail electricity to end users during fiscal year 2025, with mass market households consuming 9.7 TWh while business markets absorbed 12.6 TWh out of 5.32 million household customer points of consumption, and the most prominent counterparty representing only 7.2% of total consolidated revenues ensuring minimal customer concentration.

State-controlled G tariff pricing decisions by the Energy Regulatory Office restrict retail supply profitability as household retail supply remains subject to strict tariff approvals where approved G tariff prices do not fully cover procurement and balancing costs estimated by supply subsidiaries, with the regulator approving a 2026 G tariff of PLN 495.16 per MWh for major utilities, triggering Tauron to recognize a PLN 146 million onerous provision in Q4 2025 because of G tariff shortfalls, while PGE booked a massive PLN 560 million retail provision, Enea established a PLN 173.3 million provision, and Energa recognized a PLN 304.1 million onerous contract reserve following the 2026 ERO tariff decision.

Rising prosumer selfgeneration creates sustained downward pressure on retail volumes displacing traditional demand, with household prosumers generating over 2 TWh of electricity through distributed installations in 2024.

High-value historical legal disputes introduce contingent liabilities as Tauron is embroiled in a PLN 182 million compensation claim initiated by industrial customer Huta Łaziska stemming from a failure to resume electricity supply following an October 12, 2001 regulatory order, where regional and appellate courts twice issued judgments dismissing Huta's claims in their entirety, but the Supreme Court set aside the dismissal judgment on August 20, 2025 prolonging legal uncertainty, with Tauron creating no balance sheet provisions because internal legal assessments rate the dispute's risk as remote.

SUPPLY CHAIN CONCENTRATION AND PROCUREMENT RISKS Tauron's fuel procurement relies on highly centralized domestic channels as wholesale gas is centrally managed by the parent securing over 90% of gas fuel volumes through the domestic exchange, with the Commodity Exchange Clearing House acting as the largest supplier accounting for 48.5% of purchases though there are no equity ties between Tauron and the Commodity exchange, while subsidiary Tauron Wytwarzanie represents 20.6% of the purchase share, and procurement from any single coal mine or transport partner remains below 10% of consolidated sales revenue.

Management maintains a highly conservative hedging policy ensuring carbon credits are fully covered for 2026 and 2027, actively deploying FX forward contracts to hedge EUR-denominated carbon purchases, though the cost of the obligation to remit allowances reached PLN 1,114 million in Q1 2026 with higher generation volumes driving a steep 28% year-on-year increase in carbon redemption expenses during Q1 2026.

Structural turbine procurement delays at the 600 MW Jaworzno gas project threaten capacity market commissioning timelines as the competitive procurement procedure for the turbine island was cancelled because no bids were received by the deadline, forcing management to initiate a non-competitive single-source procurement procedure for the unit, with the total contract period spanning a long 77 months and the project required to be completed by the fourth quarter of 2029 under capacity market rules. 29 COMPETITIVE DYNAMICS AND PROSUMER THREATS Tauron maintains a dominant regional distribution monopoly commanding a leading 37% share of Poland's distribution market while PGE holds a 29% market share, with Energa and Enea trailing at 16% and 14% respectively, though Tauron ranks second in retail supply with a 19% share while PGE secures a 25% market share, and structural transition factors caused Tauron's gross generation market share to shrink to approximately 6% share while four state-controlled groups collectively maintain market control holding a 52% generation share, with PGE leading generation with a massive 19.2 GW capacity while Enea operates 6.4 GW of capacity.

Vertical integration strategies are shifting toward capital-intensive offshore wind and gas peaking projects to replace coal, with Norway's Equinor and Polenergia securing a joint wind contract representing 1.56 GW of offshore capacity, PGE securing its 975 MW project with a 25-year contract after winning the first offshore auction, and RWE agreeing to sell its 350 MW offshore project to domestic competitor PGE signaling German utility transition exits, while independent clean developers like R.Power successfully secured lucrative battery contracts contracting 134 MW of capacity, and capital spending by the four main utilities increased significantly as investment surged to PLN 14 billion.

Prosumer rooftop solar capacity blocks grid connections and displaces lucrative retail household volumes as household prosumers generated over 2 TWh of electricity through distributed systems displacing traditional domestic energy sales, while national wind development was severely blocked because regulations enforced the stringent 10H rule restriction, and state-dominated market structures inhibit healthy competition and marginalize the critical prosumer sector, with Enea's ambitious transition plan requiring heavy capital spending totaling PLN 107.5 billion capex and PGE facing growing grid modernization pressure after receiving massive requests to connect 2 GW of data centers.

OPERATOR QUALITY AND STATE OWNERSHIP CONSTRAINTS Tauron's corporate strategy secures significant transition funding as the long-term strategy targets PLN 100 billion of cumulative capex by 2035, with Fitch assuming a stable distribution WACC of approximately 10% on average from 2024 through 2028, while board consultations successfully embedded ESG priorities into the 2025- 2035 strategy to guide investment decisions, analyses justified the reversal of PLN 297 million in asset impairments across heat and generation subsidiaries, and excellent operating performance allowed a PLN 350 million dividend recommendation after an eleven-year hiatus, with management projecting positive EBITDA growth in distribution segment while conventional generation results normalize.

Active liability management replaces expensive subordinated debt with highly attractive state-supported transition financing as management initiated a gradual departure from expensive subordinated hybrid bonds to optimize interest expenses, successfully drawing PLN 1.6 billion in funding from the National Recovery and Resilience Plan, with subordinated bonds completely excluded from bank leverage covenant calculations to protect financial flexibility while rating agencies assign 50% equity credit to subordinated bonds in their capital assessment models, though a subordinated bond program of PLN 450 million expired without being utilized by the company in 2025, and subordinated bonds of PLN 400 million subscribed by BGK were successfully redeemed in March 2026.

Flexible debt programs allow substantial ESG bond issues aligned with European taxonomy guidelines as the group established a bond program allowing PLN 3 billion in green bonds or sustainability-linked debt, extended through December 31, 2026, mandating that bond proceeds refinance expenses in line with taxonomy guidelines, with management deploying a dedicated IT tool for sustainability content preparation to guarantee data trace through formalized data approval pathways and independent auditor assessments, while Bernstein Research scores Tauron's environmental and governance framework at above-peer levels on relative metrics.

Polish utilities face severe long-term transition risk as state asset studies suggest Polish coal plants will become unprofitable as early as 2028, with plans to spin off coal units to NABE repealed as economically unjustified by the ministry, while PGE operates lignite assets providing domestic cost insulation against global hard coal price volatility though flagship Belchatow units will phase out by the mid-2030s based on reserves lifespan, Energa's Ostroleka B coal units face decommissioning by the end of 2026 or late 2028, and Enea projects the ultimate lifecycle of Bogdanka mine to end in 2051.

Network modernization accelerates through smart grid implementations and green hydrogen pilots to balance intermittent renewable generation as minimizing grid distribution losses relies on widespread smart grid and meter rollouts to secure network stability, with Energa completing its SERENE project implementing advanced local energy storage and smart technologies, Tauron planning construction of a 20 MW green electrolyzer project in Jaworzno, the NH3Engine program testing the feasibility of using green ammonia in reciprocating cogeneration systems, management analyzing converting coal assets to hydrogen ready peaking units or biomass, and grid modernization programs enabling connecting 11 GW of RES capacity for PGE. FOUNDATION VERDICT Adequate foundation with distribution moat strength offset by severe generation transition liabilities.

The regulated distribution monopoly serving 6.05 million customers provides a durable natural moat with predictable cash flows protected by regulatory account mechanisms and generous 10.48% WACC returns, secured by unprecedented PLN 16 billion in state-backed 0.5% financing that de-risks grid modernization capex.

However, the foundation remains constrained by 81% carbon-intensive generation exposure facing structural unprofitability post-2028 when capacity market support expires, requiring PLN 1.1 billion quarterly carbon compliance cash drains, coupled with state ownership dynamics that preclude rating uplifts and introduce political interference risks in capital allocation decisions, while prosumer displacement and regulatory tariff restrictions compress retail profitability through PLN 146 million onerous contract provisions.
