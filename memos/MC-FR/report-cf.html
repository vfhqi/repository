Appendix

Plausible problems to monitor

LVMH faces a structurally polarized demand environment wherein over 70 million aspirational consumers exited the luxury market since 2023 due to cost-of-living inflation, while the top 0.1% of global consumers now control 23% of luxury expenditure. This middle-class abandonment represents a foundational threat to the group's historically broad-based volume model, particularly as Chinese national spending remained flat sequentially in H1 2026 and domestic consumer confidence persists below historic peaks.

The combination of aspirational customer retrenchment and stagnant Chinese cluster demand places LVMH's core Fashion & Leather Goods division at elevated risk of prolonged volume pressure relative to hard luxury specialists who benefit from concentrated ultra-wealthy clienteles. LVMH's 3% organic sales growth in 2Q26 trails high-end peers, with Richemont posting 20% organic growth and Hermès maintaining 6.7% organic revenue expansion, while Kering managed only 2% organic revenue growth.

This middle-of-the-pack momentum underscores that LVMH is neither capturing the explosive hard luxury tailwinds enjoyed by jewelry-focused houses nor benefiting from the extreme operating deleverage reversals observed in turnaround situations. The group's performance sits uncomfortably between structural winners and distressed laggards, limiting its appeal to both growth-oriented and value-contrarian institutional investors.

Operating margin contracted 15 bps to 22.5% in H1 2026 despite rigorous cost discipline, with foreign exchange fluctuations creating a €700 million operating profit headwind. While Hermès delivered a sector-leading 41.1% operating margin and even struggling Kering maintained an 11.2% adjusted operating margin, LVMH's inability to expand margins during a period of self-help initiatives signals that transactional currency volatility and negative scale effects are overwhelming internal operational improvements.

This erosion occurs despite management's explicit commitment to protecting profitability through expense discipline, suggesting that the group's massive fixed-cost retail infrastructure creates structural margin vulnerability when organic growth decelerates below the critical 3% threshold referenced in Section 10. The Chinese cluster recovery case remains severely constrained by the lack of sequential improvement, with the second quarter of 2026 showing flat performance compared to the first quarter, while organic growth is narrowly driven by US and Korean nationals who represent only a minor portion of total customer mix, and half of the H1 2026 operating profit beat was driven by the lower-quality Wines & Spirits division benefiting from one-off foreign exchange tailwinds that will reverse.

This geographic concentration risk and earnings quality dilution highlights that LVMH's recent financial delivery lacks the broad-based momentum necessary to sustain multi-year margin expansion. The dependence on narrow demand drivers and temporary currency benefits undermines confidence in the durability of the current earnings trajectory, particularly as management guides to similar transactional currency headwinds persisting through H2 2026.

Non-family executives face retention friction if family dynamics intensify, while concentrating control within a single family risks internal rivalries and executive attrition, triggering an operations-destroying succession crisis. This governance concentration and key person risk is materially worse than the governance structures observed at publicly managed peers such as Richemont and Kering, where professional management operates with greater institutional independence.

Dissenting institutional investors are actively demanding a formalized succession plan, shareholders previously approved raising the executive age limit to 85, the family's assets are secured under a 30-year lock-up, investigative reporting of behind-the-scenes rivalries prompted Bernard Arnault to publicly defend family cohesion, and Arnault stated he will not discuss retirement or name his successor for another seven to eight years.

The combination of extreme ownership consolidation, prolonged succession ambiguity, and public family disputes creates a governance overhang that differentiates LVMH negatively from sector peers operating under conventional institutional frameworks. key Fashion & Leather Goods segment reported Q2 organic growth of 1%, missing the 1.7% consensus target and revealing wider aspirational demand erosion. This core division underperformance is particularly concerning given that Fashion & Leather Goods historically accounted for the majority of group operating profit and represents the primary vehicle through which LVMH captures aspirational consumer demand.

The miss relative to consensus expectations indicates that sell-side analysts overestimated the speed of the creative-led turnaround at Dior and the pricing architecture adjustments at Louis Vuitton, suggesting that product-driven recoveries require longer gestation periods than embedded in forward models. Persistent weakness in this flagship division places disproportionate pressure on the smaller Watches & Jewelry and Selective Retailing segments to drive consolidated earnings growth, creating an unbalanced operational profile relative to more diversified luxury conglomerates.

Outliers worth noting

A second operational outlier emerges in LVMH's earnings delivery quality mix during H1 2026. While the company generated robust operating free cash flow of €4.1 billion as detailed in Section 6, the composition of its interim EBIT beat diverges from peer patterns. Specifically, approximately half of the H1 2026 operating profit beat was driven by the Wines & Spirits division, considered a lower-quality driver due to one-off foreign exchange tailwinds projected to reverse in the second half.

This stands in sharp contrast to Hermès and Richemont, where recent beats have been driven by structurally higher-margin product categories like leather goods and hard luxury jewelry, suggesting unusual earnings volatility in LVMH's profit mix relative to best-in-class peers.
