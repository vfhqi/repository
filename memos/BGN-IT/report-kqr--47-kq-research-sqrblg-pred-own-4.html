Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The unsolicited exchange offer from Banca Monte dei Paschi di Siena introduces material risks to Banca Generali's core asset-gathering model and earnings stability.

The Key Question

1.
Unsolicited takeover offer: "For Banca Generali, what are the change-of-control provisions in the Alleanza insurbanking agreement and in the framework distribution agreements with Assicurazioni Generali group companies (which paid back EUR 137.1m of fees in 1H26, 18.5% of aggregate fees), how would they apply if Banca Monte dei Paschi di Siena's exchange offer succeeded, what have Assicurazioni Generali, Delfin and Caltagirone said publicly about the offer since 27 August 2026, and what do Italian crossshareholding rules imply for the voting of Generali's stake?"
2.
Strategic operational risks: Understanding these provisions is vital because they determine whether the bank can preserve its distribution channels. A key related question is whether a change of control would trigger the termination of brand licensing and joint operations. If these agreements are terminated, Banca Generali could lose its core distribution partners and experience a major drop in asset-gathering momentum. These contract clauses represent material threats to future earnings sustainability over the next 18 months to 3 years.

Asset Gathering Business Model

3.
Fee-driven revenue model: Banca Generali's profitability depends heavily on recurring management and advisory fees generated from client assets. In the first half of 2026, fee income paid back by Assicurazioni Generali group companies reached EUR 137.1 million, demonstrating BGN's reliance on parent distribution channels. This fee income represented a substantial 18.5% of BGN's aggregate fee income of EUR 137.5 million. Any disruption to these parent agreements would immediately impair recurring fee generation and EPS growth over the medium term.
4.
Key growth driver: The newly established insurbanking agreement with Alleanza Assicurazioni represents the cornerstone of BGN's medium-term commercial expansion. In the first half of 2026, Alleanza alone contributed EUR 126.9 million in distribution fees, which was up 6.4% year-on-year. BGN targets gathering EUR 7.0 billion to EUR 8.5 billion in total financial assets and EUR 40 million to EUR 50 million in additional revenues from this project by 2030. A successful transaction could derail this synergistic expansion plan and severely limit future asset inflows.

Voting Implications

Consolidated Law on Finance Restrictions

1.
Regulatory evidence scope: In analyzing the Italian cross- shareholding mechanics, our research found five relevant sources, three companies, and two comparable past cases. We used all five sources, which include Deutsche Bank Research, Jefferies Research, and Reuters, alongside companies like Assicurazioni Generali, Monte dei Paschi, and Mediobanca. No relevant precedents or peers were left out of this legal and regulatory analysis because completeness is essential to understand governance risks. This comprehensive data set suggests that legal mechanics represent the ultimate decider of transaction feasibility, which directly impacts BGN-IT's long-term EPS stability.
2.
Tightening regulatory limits: Under Italian Consolidated Law on Finance Article 121, cross-shareholding voting rights are capped at 2% for the shareholder who acquired its stake at a later date. Deutsche Bank Research's note of 21 August 2026 explains that if the Banca Generali offer is completed, Assicurazioni Generali would become a shareholder of MPS with a 10% stake. This situation would require the disposal of MPS's stake in Generali to below 2% to avoid freezing Generali's voting rights in the combined entity. Therefore, these strict statutory limits create a material governance hurdle that could depress Banca Generali's future EPS by dragging out the integration phase.
3.
Severe voting caps: The imposition of the 2% voting cap under the TUF rules would severely limit Generali's influence over MPS's strategic decisions. According to calculations by Deutsche Bank Research on 21 August 2026, Generali would end up with a 10% stake in MPS if only the Banca Generali deal is finalized. If both the Banco BPM and Banca Generali transactions are completed, Generali's stake would be diluted to approximately 6%. Because Generali would not be entitled to vote its full stake, MPS would eventually need to find an external buyer for this residual holding, which could depress short-term market prices.
4.
Shareholder structure dilution: The potential shareholder structure of the combined group highlights the regulatory friction of the cross-shareholding rules. In a report dated 21 August 2026, Deutsche Bank Research estimated that under a combined scenario, Delfin would hold 8%, Caltagirone would hold 6%, and Generali would hold 6% of MPS. Without a voting freeze, these three major Italian financial players would wield immense power. However, the cross-shareholding rules mean Generali's voting power would be legally capped, preventing coordinated governance and creating execution risks that could ultimately impact BGN-IT's operational performance.

Public Exchange Offer Exemptions Dispute

5.
Disputed legal exemptions: The applicability of the TUF cross-shareholding limits remains a subject of intense corporate debate between the involved parties. In an interview on 12 September 2026, Monte dei Paschi di Siena's CEO Luigi Lovaglio argued that neither company would have to reduce its stake. Lovaglio stated that MPS obtained a specific legal opinion asserting that no divestment obligation exists because Generali would receive shares through an exchange offer rather than market purchases. Consequently, MPS believes the cross-shareholding rules do not apply to this transaction, which would preserve full voting rights for both parties.
6.
Contrary evidence: Deutsche Bank Research's note of 21 August 2026 maintains that the cross-shareholding rules represent a firm hurdle, meaning Generali's voting rights would still be frozen unless MPS divests its holdings. The broker calculates that Generali's potential 10% stake would require the complete disposal of MPS's holding in Generali to below the 2% threshold. This analysis directly contradicts the positive legal opinion cited by MPS management. This disagreement suggests that regulatory authorities like Consob may ultimately have to intervene to settle the dispute, which creates prolonged legal uncertainty for Banca Generali's shareholders.
7.
Unresolved regulatory clearance: The dispute over whether an exchange offer qualifies for a statutory exemption from Article 121 of the TUF remains unresolved. Legal experts note that while some precedents suggest voluntary public offers can bypass certain market purchase restrictions, Italian regulators historically interpret crossshareholding limits strictly to maintain market stability. If Consob rejects MPS's legal interpretation, the transaction's financial feasibility will deteriorate. This regulatory deadlock presents major execution risks for the deal, which means BGN-IT's advisor recruitment and asset-gathering momentum will remain depressed.
