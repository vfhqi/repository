Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Puuilo and Clas Ohlson trade near ten-year multiple peaks, making operational beats and successful regional expansion critical to sustaining current share valuations.

Valuation Peaks and Expansion Risks

1.
Unresolved inquiry: "Pointed follow-up question for a stock picker's sector note, Sector H.3. Retail - general/physical, with Puuilo (PUUILO-FI) as one worked example only. Our first draft cannot yet judge this: Puuilo is due to open its first Swedish store, in Orebro, before the end of 2026. What sales per store, gross margin, operating margin and payback do brokers and industry experts expect for Puuilo stores in Sweden, against Rusta, Biltema, Jula and Clas Ohlson, and how many Swedish stores could the format support? In Finland, how fast is Puuilo taking market share from Tokmanni and other variety discounters as it moves towards more than 90 stores? For Clas Ohlson, how much further can own brands lift its gross margin in 2027 now that it no longer hedges its dollar purchases? "
2.
Elevated valuations: Nordic variety discount retailers trade at historically high valuation multiples that leave minimal margin for operational errors. Puuilo trades at a forward P/E of 21x, which represents a clear premium of approximately 35% over its peers. This means any near-term earnings disappointment would likely trigger a severe correction in the share price unless expansion targets are met. Consequently, the success of the Swedish market entry is critical to justifying these multiples over the next three years.
3.
Sustained momentum: Clas Ohlson shares have appreciated more than fourfold since 2023 because of continuous earnings estimate upgrades. The company targets an organic sales growth of 5% per year and an operating margin of around 12% over the next three years. However, DNB Carnegie expects tailwinds from lower input costs to turn into minor headwinds starting in late 2026. This shift means the company must find new growth drivers, such as deeper private label penetration, to sustain its valuation.

Operational Drivers and Margins

4.
Domestic saturation: Puuilo is rapidly expanding its Finnish store footprint toward its long-term target of more than 90 stores. Inderes reports that Puuilo's market share in the domestic discount sector grew from 6% in 2019 to 12% in 2025. However, as the network expands, suitability of new locations in smaller cities remains a key concern. This means that domestic sales growth might slow down, placing more pressure on Sweden to deliver profits.
5.
Unhedged currency: Clas Ohlson's strategic shift to eliminate its forward currency hedging policy exposes its cost of goods sold directly to the volatile US dollar. The company historically hedged 50% of its currency exposure but decided to manage short-term transaction risks through pricing and sourcing. DNB Carnegie notes that a weaker Swedish krona could pressure margins if cost inflation cannot be passed to consumers. Therefore, unhedged purchases introduce significant earnings volatility into the 2027 fiscal year.
6.
Contrary evidence: Puuilo's initial setup costs in Sweden are expected to remain at a very moderate level of approximately EUR 1 million for the 2026 financial year. Inderes reports that the financial risk of this expansion is well protected by a highly capital-light, asset-light pilot model. This means that even if the Swedish pilot fails, the negative impact on Puuilo's consolidated balance sheet will be highly limited.
7.
Other side of the story: Puuilo and Clas Ohlson have demonstrated strong operational resilience that could protect their high multiples even if expansion is slower than planned. Inderes highlights that Puuilo's young Finnish network still has a substantial runway of maturing stores that inherently drive high returns. Meanwhile, Clas Ohlson's strong brand and pricing power can offset currency volatility through active pricing adjustments and private label growth.

Section 2 – Puuilo's Swedish Store Economics and Expansion Format Potential

Section summary
  1. We have medium confidence that Puuilo's Swedish expansion can reach profitability within two years of opening individual pilot stores, supported by DNB Carnegie's unit economic models showing a 14.5% year five EBITDA margin.
  2. Major competitors in Sweden maintain larger footprints that will limit Puuilo's initial capacity to roughly ten pilot stores, although successful execution could eventually open a much longer growth runway across Northern Europe.

Pilot Launch and Store Economics

1.
Scheduled pilot: Puuilo plans to open its first Swedish pilot store in Orebro before the end of 2026, followed by a second location in Sundsvall in early 2027. The company expects setup costs for the Swedish market entry to remain around EUR 1 million for the 2026 financial year. Management expects this organic pilot approach to preserve their traditional capital-light model. Replicating this model in Sweden is designed to test the international appeal of the format without risking excessive capital.
2.
Modeled profitability: DNB Carnegie estimates that Swedish stores will operate at a loss in their first year due to high start-up costs. However, the broker expects these stores to reach profitability by year two, generating sales of EUR 3.9 million with an 8% EBITDA margin. By year five, store-level sales are modeled to ramp up to EUR 6.0 million with a 14.5% FAS EBITDA margin. This gradual margin expansion suggests that Sweden can deliver attractive, scalable returns once brand awareness is established.

This logistical strategy is crucial for protecting cash flow, but any transport disruptions between Finland and Sweden would immediately impact store availability. The company expects to open stores in central retail locations using existing premises.
10.
Profitable labels: Puuilo's balanced assortment includes private label brands that already carry Swedish-sounding names like Norrkoping and Kramfors. DNB Carnegie suggests that these pre-existing names will accelerate Swedish customer acceptance from the first day of operations. This private label strength is expected to support gross margins because consumers perceive these brands as premium. The balanced mix of private label and recognized brands strengthens Puuilo's value proposition.
11.
Balanced pricing: Puuilo's retail prices in Finland show no meaningful differences when compared with Jula and Biltema's local levels. DNB Carnegie analysis indicates that product-level margins should be broadly comparable between Sweden and Finland, assuming stable purchasing power. However, Biltema and Jula's lower gross margins suggest that more intense price competition in Sweden may force Puuilo to reinvest more of its sourcing benefits. Reinvesting these savings would prevent the Swedish operations from reaching profitability within the modeled timeframe.

Contrary evidence: Puuilo's brand remains completely unknown to Swedish consumers, which may prevent stores from achieving modeled sales ramp-ups. Inderes highlights that management is prepared to pull the plug and retreat from Sweden if the concept fails to achieve traction. This structured approach limits the total financial downside because the capital commitment remains low. This means that even a complete failure in Sweden would preserve Puuilo's highly profitable domestic cash flow.

Section 3 – Puuilo's Finnish Market Share Trajectory Against Tokmanni

Section summary
  1. Puuilo is rapidly taking market share from Tokmanni and other variety discounters in Finland, doubling its market share to 12% by 2025 through its superior operating efficiency and rapid store openings.
  2. The Finnish discount retail market is consolidating around larger, highly profitable concepts, where Puuilo's young store network provides a long runway of organic growth as older units achieve higher sales density.

Market Share Gains and Consolidation

1.
Rapid capture: Puuilo has doubled its share of the Finnish discount retail market from 6% in 2019 to 12% in 2025, according to Inderes. This rapid capture was achieved through aggressive store openings and high customer traffic growth, establishing the company as the second-largest player alongside Motonet. This market share momentum is expected to support strong consolidated earnings per share growth over the next three years.
2.
Steady contraction: Tokmanni remains the largest variety discounter in Finland, but its market share has contracted from 37.2% in 2019 to 34.8% in 2025, according to Inderes. This steady decline indicates that smaller, agile operators are successfully capturing Tokmanni's market share in key product categories. Consequently, Tokmanni faces mounting pressure to defend its core domestic market while integrating its Swedish acquisitions.

5.

Company disclosures from September 2026 show that the retailer had 59 stores open, leaving a substantial runway of over 30 new locations. This steady rollout pace of seven to ten stores annually is modeled to drive consistent double-digit revenue growth.

Organic maturity: Puuilo benefits from a highly youthful store network, with approximately half of its locations being under five years old. Inderes highlights that these maturing stores represent a significant source of organic growth, as older units inherently generate higher sales density. This maturation curve ensures that same-store sales growth can remain robust even as the overall market reaches physical saturation.
7.
Stepwise progression: Puuilo's store sales per square meter increase dramatically with age, moving from EUR 1.4k for one to two-year-old stores to EUR 4.3k for those over ten years old. Inderes reports that mature stores older than ten years typically generate over EUR 10 million in store-level sales. This density progression guarantees sustained margin expansion as a larger portion of the network enters maturity.
8.
Strong acceleration: Puuilo reported a same-store sales growth rate of 6.8% in the first half of the 2026 financial year, up from 3.7% in the prior year. Company financial statements indicate that this acceleration was primarily driven by positive customer traffic developments, which grew by 5.6% in like-for-like stores. This traffic-driven momentum indicates that Puuilo's low-price concept is gaining traction among price-sensitive Finnish consumers.
9.
Competitive integration: Rusta acquired the struggling Finnish discount chain Hong Kong in 2018, which was undergoing corporate restructuring, and rebranded its locations to establish its Finnish presence. Inderes notes that Rusta intends to expand this network to between 80 and 100 stores over the long term, showing how foreign operators scale variety formats in Finland. This historical case suggests that Puuilo will face persistent, well-funded competition as rivals expand their local footprints.
