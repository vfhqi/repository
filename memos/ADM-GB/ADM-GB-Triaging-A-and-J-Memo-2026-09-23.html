[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = C

Thesis. Admiral is a cyclical margin-recovery case in a high-quality, slow-growing business, and at 3,706p it is a sound case but not a compelling one. The written price rises of H1 2026 should rebuild the UK Motor earned margin through H2 2026 and 2027, as they did in 2023 to 2024; but the shares have already recovered 42 percent from their October 2025 low, so the return we can count on is about 16.7 percent a year over eighteen months and about 13.9 percent a year over three years, below the 20 percent bar unless the market pays more than sector parity for a recovery it can already see coming.

  • The change is external and cyclical, and Admiral is answering it rather than causing it.
    • In 2025 the UK motor market cut prices about 10 percent into claims inflation of 5 to 7 percent
    • Admiral followed by half
    • the soft premium earned through to an H1 2026 group pre-tax profit down 18 percent to £429.2 million (6 August 2026), which the market had braced for as the trough.
    • In H1 2026 Admiral raised written rates by high-single-digit percentages while the market premium index was flat, so it is now pricing about 1 to 3 points ahead of claims inflation in real terms, at the cost of a UK Motor policy count down 1 percent to 5.77 million.
    • The market has since started to follow: the premium index was up 8.5 percent year on year in July 2026, because an industry expected to run a 106 to 111 percent combined ratio in 2026 cannot hold its prices down.
    • The visibility on this cycle is about twelve to eighteen months, not the two-plus years the framework asks of a demand force, which is why the external forces rate C and not B.
  • The internal change is evolutionary, and that is the right reading of it.
    • No chief executive change, no guidance rise, a strategy the company itself calls an acceleration rather than a new direction
    • the real internal changes are a capital framework that swaps special dividends for buybacks (the first £45 million announced with the H1 2026 results), a perimeter clean-up (Elephant sold on 31 December 2025) and a measurable data and AI programme aimed at £200 million of efficiency and loss-ratio value by 2028.
    • None of these is a change force of the size the framework's screen wants
      • together they add about 1 percent a year to earnings per share through the share count and keep the cost advantage from narrowing further.
  • 'Financial Outputs' have troughed and begun to turn, but have not yet accelerated.
    • The current-year loss ratio of 77.6 percent in H1 2026 beat consensus in the trough half
    • the core UK Motor combined ratio of 78.8 percent beat by 520 basis points
    • FY2027 consensus earnings per share rose 7.2 percent in three months to 2.70 GBP mean (2026-09-18).
    • Against that, FactSet revenue grew 0.4 percent in H1 2026 after 10.4 percent in FY2025, the motor expense ratio rose to 18.3 percent from 16.7 percent, and the reported margin leans on prior-year reserve releases of 17.3 percent of premium that must fall toward 12 percent in H2 2026.
  • The change has travelled about half of the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain.
    • It shows in the company's choices (pricing ahead of the market; the December 2025 capital framework), in its actions (the rate rises; the buyback) and in its key operating metrics (written margins past their worst; policies down only 1 percent)
    • it has reached the sell-side's estimates for FY2027 and FY2028 but not yet the financial statements, where H1 2026 profit was still down 18 percent.
  • What we think it earns.
    • Our modal path takes earnings per share from 2.55 GBP (FY2025, reported basic) to about 2.35 GBP in FY2026, 2.72 GBP in FY2027 and 3.03 GBP in FY2028: about 5.9 percent a year over three years from the FY2025 base, or about 13.6 percent a year from the FY2026 trough.
    • The growth from the trough is inside the framework's 12 to 20 percent band
      • the growth from the FY2025 base is not, because FY2025 was a good year that carried about £30 million of Ogden benefit and a high release rate.
    • Beyond FY2028 we see about 6 percent a year: 1 to 2 percent policy growth, pricing at claims inflation, about 1 percent from buybacks, and the smaller businesses adding the rest.
      • That is the company's steady state, not a growth case.
  • What it costs.
    • At 37.64 GBP the shares stand at 13.15x consensus earnings 24 months forward (2026-09-18): the 26th percentile of the stock's five-year range (10.48x to 23.04x, median 14.29x), the 60th percentile of the last two years, and about the 17th percentile of ten years on the dashboard's measure (2026-09-22).
    • The shares have already travelled from a 52-week low of 2,622.8p to 3,706p and the multiple from about 10.5x toward its median, so the oversold part of the return has been taken by others.
    • Against the closest peers the shares sit at a small discount (Tryg and Gjensidige 15.1x, Sampo 14.4x, Protector 14.3x)
      • against a sector median of about 10.6x, set by composites and reinsurers with a different capital model, they carry a premium the market has already compressed.
  • The return does not clear the bar, and the component that could lift it is the least certain one.
    • At a blended multiple of 14.1x on the earnings two years beyond each horizon, with dividends, the modal return is about 16.7 percent a year over eighteen months and about 13.9 percent a year over three years: a C at both horizons.
    • The cash return (about 4.3 percent of today's price over eighteen months) is predictable
      • the earnings growth (24-month-forward earnings rising 13.6 percent) is probable
      • the re-rating (13.15x to 14.1x, plus 7.2 percent) is only plausible, at about 40 percent odds, because the market prices Admiral at sector parity while it discounts autonomous vehicles, and confirmation of the cycle need not lift that terminal fear.
    • Hold the multiple at 13.15x and the eighteen-month return is about 11.6 percent a year, a D
      • pay the ten-year median of 15.7x and it is about 25 percent a year, an A.
      • The multiple is the swing factor and it is not ours to earn.
  • Where the chain breaks.
    • A soft market that persists past H2 2026 (Direct Line buying volume on price-comparison sites; Hastings discounting; AXA calling the UK the one retail market softening; the premium index down month on month in August 2026) leaves Admiral choosing between margin and volume, and the reported margin's dependence on reserve releases means the trough may be deeper than the H1 2026 print shows.

  • The case therefore is a probable earned-margin recovery in a business that has done this before, bought half-way back in price, with a return that reaches the bar only if the market pays at least its five-year median multiple for it.

    • Sound, trackable, and not yet compelling.

  • Rating = C, because the change is material enough to move earnings per share about 14 percent a year from the FY2026 trough and to give a double-digit return even at an unchanged multiple, but the three-year growth rate from the FY2025 base is about 6 percent a year and the 'Modal return' is about 16.7 percent and 13.9 percent a year on the two horizons, below the 20 percent bar

    • not B because no component of the return reaches the bar without the plausible re-rating, and not D because the 4 to 5 percent cash yield and the probable earned-margin recovery hold the return in double digits even at 13.15x.

Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.

  • The case against, at its strongest: Admiral is a 'false friend', a volume story that masked margin compression, and the 2026 rate rises are a retreat, not a recovery.
    • The 2024 volume growth (policies up 15 percent by cutting rates the day after the Ogden change) was written at inadequate prices
    • the 2025 underwriting year's initial booked loss ratio rose to 78 percent net of reinsurance
    • UK Motor turnover fell 7 percent in 2025 despite record policies
    • the risks research holds that the 2021 and 2022 underwriting years sit at 106 and 111 percent combined ratios and cannot be commuted, so profit commissions stay suppressed
    • the reported H1 2026 combined ratio of 78.5 percent was flattered by 17.3 percent of releases against a core of about 84 percent
    • the expense advantage has narrowed from about 10 points to 5 to 7
    • and the sell-side that moved to sceptical holds in 2025 (four Sells among fourteen brokers in June 2026) saw all this.
    • A soft market through 2027 leaves Admiral choosing between margin and volume, and the outcome is a de-rating to Morgan Stanley's 10.5x bear multiple and 2,700p, or Jefferies' downside of 2,300p.
    • The risks research's own analysis of competing hypotheses finds this profile has zero hard inconsistencies with fifteen operational evidence items, against five for the bull profile.
  • What we concede.
    • The reported margin does lean on releases, and the H1 2026 beat was a reserving beat rather than an earned-margin beat.
    • The volume engine has decelerated to minus 1 percent and the structural growth of the pool is about 2 percent a year.
    • The cost gap has narrowed on two expert accounts.
    • The multiple has already recovered a good part of the way.
    • And the two research reports disagree about the 2021 and 2022 underwriting years in a way the later research does not settle, so the single most critical metric the bear case names, the net reserve development of those years, is one we cannot read from our inputs.
  • Why we hold our view, in its C form.
    • The false friend's own diagnostic, writing more policies without earning more premium, reversed in H1 2026, when the company wrote fewer policies at higher premiums and its current-year loss ratio beat consensus.
    • The company has run this exact play before, in 2022 to 2024, and the earned margin arrived.
    • The 17.3 percent release rate was guided down for H2 2026, so the drag to about 12 percent is in our path and in consensus.
    • The premium finance fear that drove part of the de-rating has cleared with no regulatory action.
    • And at 13.15x with a 4 to 5 percent cash yield the case does not need the multiple to rise to return double digits, which is why the downside from here is the 20 percent mark-to-market fall to a trough multiple rather than a thesis break.
  • We do not claim asset cover, and the test shows why.
    • UK Motor's FY2025 pre-tax profit of £1.02 billion, taxed at 22.2 percent, is about £794 million post-tax
    • at the comparison set's lowest multiple (Wuestenrot & Wuerttembergische at 6.1x, 2026-09-22) that is about £4.8 billion, 42 percent of the £11.5 billion market capitalisation
    • at the sector median of about 10.6x, about £8.4 billion, 73 percent
    • only at Admiral's own 13.8x does UK Motor alone reach about £11.0 billion, 95 percent.
    • The bounded downside is a matter of yield and multiple, not of asset cover.
  • Where the case against wins.
    • On the rating, not on the direction: it is why this is a C and a park rather than a B and a progression.
    • It would win outright if the Q3 2026 trading update (late October 2026) showed the policy count falling below 5.7 million while the premium index fell for a second consecutive month, or if the FY2026 results (late February 2027) showed a current-year loss ratio above 79 percent or releases below 10 percent of premium.

    • neither side of the ledger reaches the framework's 'sufficiently strong'.

Revenue and demand cycle: where is the company's own cycle, and is it turning? (KQ 6, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because the company's own cycle is turning: H1 2026 was the earned-margin trough (group pre-tax profit down 18 percent to £429.2 million), written margins were described by Morgan Stanley and Jefferies as fully past their worst, and the sell-side's FY2027 earnings per share has risen 7.2 percent over three months (2026-09-18).

Summary: Written Summary of the Investment Case (not rated) (E 3, BB 4)
The case in 8 to 10 sentences (the thesis as tested), kept in full at both ends of the record (Part 1, E 3, BB 4)
  • Admiral is a cyclical margin-recovery case in a high-quality, slow-growing business, and it is a sound case but not a compelling one.
  • Admiral is the lowest-cost, best-priced motor insurer in the most fragmented and cyclical personal-lines market in Europe
    • in 2025 the market cut prices about 10 percent into claims inflation of 5 to 7 percent, Admiral followed by half, and the soft premium earned through to an H1 2026 profit down 18 percent to £429.2 million (6 August 2026) that the market had braced for as the trough.
  • In H1 2026 Admiral raised written rates by high-single-digit percentages while the market index was flat, so the earned margin now rebuilds through H2 2026 and 2027 as it did in 2023 to 2024
    • the core combined ratio of 78.8 percent already beat consensus by 520 basis points in the trough half.
  • The change has travelled from choices (pricing ahead of the market; a capital framework that buys back shares) through actions into the key operating metrics (written margins past their worst; policies down only 1 percent) and has begun to reach the sell-side (FY2027 consensus earnings per share up 7.2 percent in three months to 2.70 GBP mean) but not yet the financial statements.
  • Our modal path takes earnings per share from 2.55 GBP (FY2025, reported basic) to about 2.35 GBP in FY2026, 2.72 GBP in FY2027 and 3.03 GBP in FY2028, about 5.9 percent a year over three years, or about 13.6 percent a year from the trough.
  • The price has already travelled 42 percent from its 26.50 GBP low to 37.64 GBP (2026-09-22) and the 24-month-forward multiple from about 10.5x to 13.15x, so the oversold part of the return has been taken
    • what remains is a 4 to 5 percent cash yield, the earned-margin recovery and a plausible but not probable drift of the multiple toward 14x.
  • That sums to about 16.7 percent a year over eighteen months and about 13.9 percent a year over three years, below the 20 percent bar on both horizons.
  • The case clears the bar on both horizons only below about 3,215p, and on the eighteen-month test alone below about 3,610p, against 3,706p today.
  • What we are betting on, in one line: that Admiral's written price rises of H1 2026 earn through to a UK Motor margin rebuild in 2027 while releases hold at 12 percent of premium or better, and that the market pays at least its five-year median multiple for it
    • the first two are probable, the third plausible, and at 3,706p the plausible one is the difference between a C and a B.
