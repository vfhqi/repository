[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = D

Thesis. 4iG is a transformation case that has not reached the shareholder: a founder-controlled Hungarian telecom-led conglomerate is re-plumbing itself through a holding structure, an infrastructure carve-out, a share swap with e& PPF and partner-funded defence, space and energy pillars, and the change has travelled through the company's resources, choices and actions into revenue and stopped there. At HUF 1,607 the shares are a geared option on transactions and political decisions outside the company's control, and on the framework's earnings convention they return about 1 percent a year over eighteen months and about 6 percent a year over three years, with no cash leg.

  • The input change is as broad as any in the universe, and most of it has already happened.
    • Fifteen or more transactions have closed or been announced in nine months, the governance restructure into a four-pillar holding was completed on 15 January 2026, the CSG Defence sale of 49 percent of the defence sub-holding closed on 2 September 2026, the e& PPF swap is under a term sheet, and a HUF 1.3 trillion defence framework through 2035 was signed in March 2026.
    • The forces do not point one way.
      • Three of the screen's red flags for a false positive fire: empire-building (an energy holding, wind farms in two countries, a nuclear letter of intent and a Nasdaq plan within three weeks), dangerous leverage (3.7x net debt to EBITDA on the company's basis at 30 June 2026, BB−, a 5.0x covenant, unhedged euro debt) and a 'PowerPoint' value claim (HUF 400 billion of enterprise value "unlocked" by the reorganisation; up to EUR 1 billion of synergies from a non-binding term sheet).
    • The largest single external force since 18 August 2026 is a headwind: the review of every state contract with the company, naming the defence framework, which took the shares down 11 percent in a day.
  • The outputs have followed the revenue line and nothing below it.
    • Revenue grew 16.7 percent in H1 2026 and the IT segment 20 percent, but EBITDA grew 3.1 percent with the margin down 400 basis points to 30.9 percent, operating profit fell 16.8 percent, and the reported half-year profit of HUF 21.9 billion rests on HUF 36.1 billion of unrealised foreign-exchange gains over a normalised loss of HUF 3.9 billion.
    • After minorities of about HUF 17 billion for the half, the shareholder's profit was HUF 5.0 billion with the currency gain and a loss without it.
    • The chain from EBITDA to attributable earnings per share runs through HUF 191 billion of depreciation, HUF 67 billion of net financial cost, tax, and the state's 37.9 percent of the telecom sub-holding.
      • That chain is the weakest link in the case, and it is why the framework's earnings metric gives almost nothing to value.
  • The sixth layer, the sell-side's estimates, does not exist for this stock.
    • FactSet holds no consensus at 2026-10-05
    • One broker is visible with a 'Without' rating and no figures
    • The dashboard's revision fields are all absent.
    • Nothing can be upgraded, and the only direction the research gives after the half is down.
  • What the price assumes is already most of the bad news and none of the swap.
    • At HUF 1,607 the market capitalisation is about HUF 479 billion against an enterprise value of about HUF 1.56 trillion, 5.6 times last-twelve-month EBITDA
    • The shares are 67.6 percent below the 52-week high after a December 2025 political allegation, the August 2026 review and the energy pivot of 27 August 2026.
    • On the earnings basis the price is 17.1 times our FY2028 adjusted attributable EPS of HUF 94, above every peer in the comparison set except Digi and Swisscom, so the price already pays for those earnings to arrive and for a multiple above the domestic incumbent's 9.4 times.
    • On the enterprise basis the price discounts the review, the pivot and the governance, and does not pay for the swap.
  • The return on the framework's convention does not clear the bar on any leg.
    • Magyar Telekom's 9.4 times on our adjusted attributable EPS two years beyond each horizon gives HUF 1,636 at eighteen months (1.2 percent a year) and HUF 1,936 at three years (6.4 percent a year)
    • The cash leg is zero, the earnings leg is a sign change from loss to small profit, and the re-rating leg is a de-rating from the 17.1 times the price implies to 9.4 times.
    • The bull case is an asset-value re-rating of the enterprise multiple from 5.6 to 7 times, worth about half the share price (HUF 2,381 at eighteen months, about 30 percent a year).
      • It needs the swap to close, the market to re-rate the group rather than the asset, and the review to spare the defence framework, a chain we put at about one in four.
    • The equity is 31 percent of the enterprise value, so every enterprise outcome is multiplied by about three on the shares, in either direction.
      • The left tail (a 10 percent forint depreciation erasing 57 percent of book equity; a Scope downgrade below B+ giving bondholders redemption rights on HUF 370.75 billion; a cancelled framework) is at least as large as the right.

  • The pattern it fits is 'Major Corporate Change', and the fit is not crisp.

    • The pattern's inputs are present in force
    • Its defining output, an upgraded earnings-per-share trajectory, is absent both because attributable earnings are negative and because no sell-side trajectory exists to upgrade.
    • The case is therefore a transformation whose financial statements do not yet show a transformation, priced at a level that already discounts much but not all of that.

  • Rating = D, because the change is material in scale (a HUF 745 billion-revenue company re-plumbing its whole structure) but not yet material in the outputs the framework measures: 'Change Forces' (E 1) are B and already acted upon, 'Financial Outputs' (E 2) are D with only revenue moving, and the 'Modal return' (E 3) is F at about 1 to 6 percent a year with no cash leg

    • The parts combine so that the weakest binds, because an investment thesis whose earnings line is negative and whose return rests on a possible asset re-rating is not a 20-percent-a-year thesis however many transactions close.
    • Not F, because the forces are real and the asset-value branch, though only possible, is large
    • Not C, because the framework's question is whether the change is sufficiently strong, driving sufficiently improving outputs, driving a sufficient return, and the answer at the second and third links is no.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The strongest case for 4iG is an asset-value case.
    • The market is capitalising a conglomerate at 5.6 times EBITDA when its largest asset, a Hungarian telecom operator with a 30 percent active mobile share, a 42,000 km wholesale backbone with a competitor already paying to use it and a telecom EBITDA margin of 40 to 48 percent, would change hands at 7.7 to 9.1 times on the Vodafone Hungary precedent
      • The e& PPF swap will put a mark on that infrastructure within six months
      • A UAE sovereign fund will pay for 11 percent of it
      • The defence pillar carries EUR 3.5 billion of committed backlog that nobody is modelling
      • The HUF 36 billion of H1 currency gains are a real reduction in a real euro liability as the forint strengthens
      • Scope has held BB− through the political noise
      • And the shares have already fallen 68 percent from the high, so a one-and-a-half-turn re-rating of the enterprise multiple adds about half to the share price.
    • What we concede: the enterprise multiple is low against the one precedent in the research
      • The swap would be a real mark
      • The gearing cuts both ways and the market has already cut it one way
      • The staircase of dated events is long
      • The forint has been a tailwind and may stay one.
    • Why we still hold our view: the framework values the shareholder's earnings, and after depreciation, a 6.75 percent coupon on HUF 370.75 billion of parent bonds, HUF 166 billion of leases, tax and the state's 37.9 percent, those earnings are negative today and small in two years.
    • The telecom division's attributable post-tax earnings, net of the parent's own interest, cover about 36 percent of the market capitalisation at Magyar Telekom's 9.4 times and 28 percent at the comparison set's lowest 7.3 times (shown under 'Re-rating', RA 2, E 3), so the "cover" exists only at transaction multiples that assume a buyer.
    • The swap gives away 38 percent of the best asset's earnings to get an equity-accounted 49 percent of a competitor's, and so dilutes the chain further.
    • The asset-value branch needs three conditions whose product is about one in four, and the left tail is a covenant and a currency, which no multiple bounds.
    • The case is an option on other people's decisions, priced as an option
      • The framework does not buy options.

[Robustness of] Foundations upon which the Investment Thesis is built upon (BB 2) Rating = D
