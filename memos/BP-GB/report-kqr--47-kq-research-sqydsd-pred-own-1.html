Section 2 – Components of the Conversion Gap

Section summary
  1. BP's cash conversion gap in FY2025 is driven by heavy tax charges and massive provision utilizations.
  2. Structural cash outflows such as joint venture earnings retention and Gulf of Mexico liabilities reduce conversion efficiency.

Taxation and Cash Payments

1.
High tax charges: BP reported an accounting tax charge on profit of $6,451 mm in the FY2025 financial period. This GAAP tax charge represents an effective tax rate of 83% on profit before taxation. However, the actual underlying replacement cost effective tax rate was much lower at 42% because of adjusting items. This massive tax expense represents a significant non-cash accrual that reduces earnings without immediately draining cash.
2.
Substantial cash tax payments: BP recorded cash income taxes paid of $6,589 mm in FY2025. This cash payment is slightly higher than the accrued tax charge of $6,451 mm. The company also reported a broader measure of $8.3 bn in corporate income and production taxes paid to global governments. These heavy cash tax payments directly reduce operating cash flow before working capital because they represent immediate cash outflows.
3.
Elevated tax rates: BP anticipates its underlying effective tax rate to remain around 40% in FY2026. This tax expectation is highly sensitive to the geographic mix of the group's global profits. Compared to peers, Morgan Stanley notes that BP's cash return framework is highly sensitive to commodity price shifts and elevated tax regimes. High tax rates in jurisdictions like the UK North Sea continue to weigh heavily on BP's underlying cash conversion efficiency.
4.
Contrary evidence: BP's cash income taxes paid of $6,589 mm in FY2025 actually declined from prior years. The company paid $8,761 mm in cash taxes during FY2024 and $10,173 mm in FY2023. This cash tax reduction was primarily driven by lower overall profitability and favorable geographical earnings mixes. Consequently, the cash tax payment was a smaller drag on cash flow than consensus had initially feared.

Joint Venture Cash Distributions

5.
Retained JV earnings: BP reported earnings from equity-accounted joint ventures less dividends received of $1,493 mm in FY2025. This positive reconciliation entry means that accounting earnings from JVs significantly exceeded the actual cash distributions. The total dividends received by BP from joint ventures and associates was $2,111 mm. This retention of cash inside joint ventures directly explains a major portion of the conversion gap between EBITDA and operating cash.
6.
Moderate joint venture cash: BP received cash dividends of $437 mm from Azule Energy Holdings Limited in FY2025. This cash inflow is down from $463 mm received in FY2024 and $708 mm received in FY2023. Under IFRS accounting rules, BP registers its 50% share of Azule's net profit on the income statement. However, the actual cash conversion is limited because retained earnings support local capital expenditures rather than being fully distributed to BP.
7.
Lower distribution precedents: National Company KazMunayGaz serves as a comparable case where adjusted EBITDA is calculated by replacing joint venture profit shares with actual cash dividends received. In FY2025, KazMunayGaz's adjusted EBITDA rose 19.7% due to higher dividends received from joint ventures and associates. Like BP, peers often report a large gap between JV book earnings and distributed dividends. This structural difference means that EBITDA figures are often inflated by undistributed joint venture income.

Provisions and Decommissioning Liabilities

8.
Massive legal settlements: BP paid $1.2 bn toward the Gulf of America Deepwater Horizon settlement liability in FY2025. This substantial legal cash payment was accounted for directly within working capital movements. The remaining payables and provisions related to the oil spill stood at $7,256 mm at the end of FY2025. These recurring annual cash outflows materially depress BP's net cash provided by operating activities compared to its reported adjusted EBITDA.

This provision utilization included environmental spending of $297 mm and litigation payments of $188 mm. However, the largest utilization was for emissions provisions which totaled $1,834 mm. This emissions utilization represents non-cash settlement via existing inventory allowances and does not represent actual cash spent.

Jefferies Research reported on October 30, 2026, that the new leadership has focused on improving predictability and streamlining asset ownership. Portfolio high-grading will structurally lower cash breakevens, helping to mitigate the conversion gap over the 2027-2030 period.

Influential Actors and Regulators

5.
Severe fiscal drag: Severe fiscal drag from the UK Energy Profits Levy has pushed BP to reconsider its long-term presence in the North Sea. Jefferies Research highlighted on October 28, 2026, that the headline tax rate on upstream oil and gas activities has reached 78%. This punitive tax regime has forced the company to launch a formal sale process for its North Sea assets. Redirecting capital away from high-tax basins will preserve cash, protecting the company's dividend coverage from fiscal leakage in future periods.
6.
Aggressive asset consolidation: Aggressive asset consolidation by independent operators could facilitate BP's rapid exit from mature, high-cost basins. DNB Carnegie reported on September 16, 2026, that players like Ithaca Energy are actively pursuing mergers to achieve tax optimization and operational synergies. These consolidators can combine substantial tax losses with acquired assets to offset a greater share of taxable profits. Consequently, interest from strategic buyers will help BP monetize its legacy North Sea portfolio at attractive valuations.

Operational and Market Outcomes

9.
Rising investor pressure: Rising investor pressure could force BP to abandon its complex transition strategies and focus entirely on core fossil fuel operations. Expert insights from Mayfair Energy Ventures on December 9, 2025, indicated that European integrated majors suffer from valuation discounts compared to US peers. Investors increasingly favor companies that prioritize near-term cash returns and capital discipline over long-term lowcarbon investments. If BP succumbs to this pressure, a complete pivot back to oil and gas would structurally raise operating cash flow at the expense of its green targets.
10.
Vulnerable cash generation: Vulnerable cash generation during commodity price downturns represents a major risk to BP's ambitious cost-reduction goals. Bernstein Research noted on January 30, 2025, that BP's heavy reliance on EBITDA targets is less relevant than actual after-tax cash flows. A sharp decline in Brent crude prices below planning assumptions would immediately squeeze operating margins across both Upstream and Downstream segments. Consequently, lower absolute cash inflows would prevent the company from achieving its structural cost reduction targets of $6.50-7.50 bn by 2027.
11.
Stronger trading contributions: Stronger trading contributions can provide a significant buffer to BP's operating cash flows during periods of high market volatility. Rothschild & Co Redburn reported on October 30, 2026, that exceptional performance in gas marketing and trading has consistently supported quarterly earnings. Integrating these trading assets into the new Upstream and Downstream segments starting in 2027 should enhance operational optimization. This integration is expected to stabilize converted cash flows, helping to close the conversion gap faster than consensus models currently project.
