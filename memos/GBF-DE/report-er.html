Section 1 – Brief company description and history 15 findings

Section summary
  1. Bilfinger has successfully transformed from a legacy construction conglomerate into a high-margin, asset-light industrial services leader, positioning it for predictable earnings expansion despite near-term macro volatility.

Corporate Origin and Business Model Transformation

1.
Corporate history – legacy transformation complete: Founded in the 1880s as construction pioneer Grün & Bilfinger, the group evolved through Bilfinger Berger into an asset-light industrial services provider across process verticals. The company shed legacy civil construction risks over a multi-year restructuring to establish a pure-play engineering, maintenance, and modification service model
2.
Business model – recurring revenue defensiveness: The Group delivers end-to-end services spanning consulting, engineering, prefabrication, maintenance, and asset performance enhancement for process plants. More than 90% of customers return for repeat business, underpinning a highly defensive, recurring cash flow profile
3.
Contract risk profile – reimbursable structure predominance: Management transitioned disclosures to remuneration types, revealing that ~80% of revenue stems from low-risk reimbursable frameworks. Time and material accounts for 44%, unit rates 19%, mixed models 17%, and fixed lump-sum contracts only 20%, materially de-risking the contract portfolio

Regional Segment Structure and End-Market Exposure

4.
Segment architecture – geography-based realignment: Effective January 1, 2026, Bilfinger collapsed its legacy service-line matrix into three geography-based reportable segments: Western Europe, Central Europe, and International. Deutsche Bank's Michael Kuhn noted that this realignment significantly enhances operational transparency and capital market readability
5.
Segment mix – Western Europe cash engine: Accounting for ~35% of Group revenue, Western Europe delivers €1,800m-€2,000m guided 2026 revenue with an industry-leading 7.0-7.4% EBITA margin.
