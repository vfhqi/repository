Section 2 – Sweden's Risk Tax Framework and Peer Exposure

Section summary
  1. Sweden's credit institution risk tax imposes a material regulatory cost on large banks exceeding the indexed liability threshold.
  2. Major Swedish banking groups remain fully exposed to this levy, while niche digital platforms currently operate safely below the threshold.
  3. Analytical confidence is high because current liability levels and tax filings from Swedbank and SEB provide concrete baselines.

Mechanics of the Risk Tax

1.
Progressive regulatory taxation: Sweden's risk tax on credit institutions imposes a direct levy on large financial groups with liabilities linked to domestic operations. According to SEB's interim report of 22 October 2026, the 2026 tax applies to Swedish credit institutions with liabilities exceeding an indexed group threshold of SEK 197bn. The tax rate has been increased to 0.07% of the total adjusted liabilities after deducting a basic taxfree allowance equal to that threshold. This framework ensures that smaller savings institutions remain exempt from the levy, which protects their net interest margin.
2.
Escalating tax rates: The tax rate has steadily risen since its inception in 2022 to target large bank profitability across Sweden. Morgan Stanley's research note dated 15 February 2024 shows that the risk tax was introduced in 2022 at a rate of 0.05% before rising to 0.06% in 2023. Swedbank's report on 22 October 2026 confirms the rate has now escalated to 0.07% for 2026 and subsequent years. This upward trajectory directly penalizes expanding wholesale-funded institutions, so banks must carefully manage their domestic liability structures.
3.
Annual threshold indexation: The Swedish government indexes the liability threshold annually to account for inflation and economic expansion. SEB's financial disclosure on 22 October 2026 states that the threshold increased from the initial SEK 150bn in 2022 to SEK 197bn for the 2026 financial year. Analysts at Morgan Stanley expect this indexing mechanism to gradually push the threshold higher, unless legislating modifications occur following the recent election. Consequently, this indexation provides a growing buffer for midsized banks trying to avoid the tax burden.
4.
Historical regulatory shift: The introduction of the bank tax represented a fundamental shift in Sweden's macroprudential policy. Morgan Stanley's report of 15 February 2024 notes that the tax was originally designed to build capital buffers against potential banking sector shocks. However, the tax has evolved into a reliable stream of public revenue, which makes reversing the legislation highly unlikely under any political coalition. This long-term persistence means that large banks must absorb these costs as part of their business-as-usual expenses.

This massive liability base forced Swedbank to pay a Swedish bank tax of SEK 863m for the Jan-Sep 2026 period alone.
