Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. Nexans's transition to a pure electrification player rests on highly concentrated growth drivers spanning subsea transmission margin conversion, the Republic Wire acquisition, and new Grid capacity expansion, creating a wide and positively skewed range of plausible earnings outcomes over the next eighteen months that ultimately depends on resolving near-term Halden loading uncertainties and successfully integrating North American lowvoltage operations. The fundamental inquiry examines how concentrated or diffuse are the drivers that separate the next eighteen months to four years' financials from the last four to six years, and how wide and how skewed is the range of plausible outcomes over the next eighteen months. Nexans aims to grow its adjusted EBITDA from €728 million in 2025 to €1,150 million by fiscal year 2028, yet this ambitious target exposes the company to significant execution risks across three primary growth pillars. FactSet data on 23 October 2026 highlights a wide range in Nexans's FY2027 EPS estimates from €7.72 to €11.78, contrasted with a narrow EBITDA range of €898m to €1,012m. This unusual disconnect stems from competing broker assumptions regarding post-acquisition depreciation charges, tax optimization benefits, and interest expense normalization following the €500 million bond issuance at 4.25% completed in September 2026. Nexans's reported depreciation and amortization surged to €153.4 million in H1 2026 compared to €101.1 million in H1 2025, while the effective tax rate reached 32.7% in H1 2026 compared to 30.9% in fiscal year 2025, creating substantial below-EBITDA drags that compress net income despite stable operating performance.

The unsigned Mediterranean replacement contract for the Halden mass-impregnated line represents the most immediate execution risk facing Nexans through year-end 2026. Nexans's Q3 sales update on 22 October 2026 revealed that the Mediterranean replacement contract remains officially unsigned, and Bernstein's report noted that this delay has revived investor anxiety regarding the Halden mass-impregnated line's loading. Bernstein's report on 19 June 2026 estimated that the halt of the Great Sea Interconnector represents a lost annual sales runrate of approximately €100 million. Each half-year of under-loading carries an estimated €7.5 million EBITDA penalty and approximately €0.15 erosion to earnings per share, threatening Deutsche Bank's projected FY27E EPS of €8.42.

Broker consensus implicitly assumes that Nexans will successfully replicate Prysmian's integration playbook following its Republic Wire acquisition completed in June 2026. Jefferies's report on 27 April 2026 points out that Nexans expects to capture €23 million in run-rate synergies from the Republic Wire acquisition by the third year, split evenly between commercial cross-selling and cost efficiencies. However, Jefferies Research's note on 11 May 2026 indicates that Southwire and Prysmian together control approximately 65% of the US low-voltage copper building-wire market, while Republic Wire holds only a 4% market share, highlighting substantial scale disadvantages in distributor negotiations and raw material procurement leverage.

The company's capital structure carries materially higher leverage than key peers during its capacity expansion phase. Nexans's net debt expanded significantly to €1,038 million at June 2026 compared to just €266 million at the end of December 2025, primarily driven by the cash-out required to finalize the Republic Wire acquisition. In stark contrast, NKT maintained a highly favorable net interest-bearing debt of negative €591 million at the end of June 2026, signifying a substantial net cash position, complemented by total available reserves of €1,250 million to fund ongoing capacity expansions.

This structural difference in financial flexibility limits Nexans's ability to pursue opportunistic acquisitions or weather prolonged project delays without pressuring its BB+ credit rating with stable outlook from Standard & Poor's.

Free cash flow normalization presents a secondary risk as the flattening Transmission backlog reduces the net inflow of advance customer down-payments. The company's 9M 2026 financial presentation shows the adjusted Transmission backlog stood at €7.8 billion in September 2026 compared to €7.7 billion at the end of December 2025, representing nominal growth of only 1.3%, signaling a transition into an execution phase where project cash consumption exceeds incoming prepayments.
