Section 3 – Balance Sheet Financing and Windfall Cash Release

Section summary
  1. INDUS Holding AG financed its tungsten-carbide windfall internally, causing working capital to surge and free cash flow guidance to drop to break-even.
  2. Structural tax inefficiencies from stranded trade-tax losses and rising interest expenses create ongoing headwinds for group earnings per share.
  3. Comparing corporate treasuries, INDUS's decentralized funding model contrasts with Gesco's isolated liquidity and the centralized cash pooling of Nordic serial acquirers.

Balance Sheet Windfall Financing

1.
Pre-financing inventory requirements: INDUS Holding AG financed its tungsten-carbide inventory buildup directly through its own balance sheet resources. In its interim presentation on 12 August 2026, management reported that working capital expanded significantly to EUR 627.0 million in the first half of 2026. This massive cash tie-up was driven primarily by seasonal effects and rocketing raw material procurement costs at its BETEK subsidiary. Consequently, the heavy pre-financing requirement restricted overall group liquidity and weighed heavily on operating cash flows.
2.
Rising debt burden: The massive working capital expansion forced INDUS to increase its short-term and long-term borrowing activities. In its quarterly update on 12 May 2026, management reported that net financial liabilities had escalated to EUR 653.3 million in the first quarter of 2026. This increase represented a significant sequential jump of 20.1% compared to the EUR 544.0 million reported at year-end 2025. Funding this material buffer with debt elevates gearing ratios and dilutes near-term earnings per share.
3.
Free cash flow compression: Financing the tungsten raw material buffer has severely compressed the holding company's cash generation capabilities. NuWays AG's research note on 8 October 2026 points out that free cash flow for full-year 2026 is projected to drop to just EUR 31.9 million. This estimate aligns with management's revised 2026 guidance, which was slashed to at least break-even from its previous forecast. This extreme cash compression restricts capital allocation and effectively halts the company's acquisition engine.
4.
Working capital unwind benefits: The upcoming normalisation of tungsten prices is expected to trigger a significant cash release that will accompany falling earnings. NuWays AG's report on 8 October 2026 notes that cash flows from operating activities will surge to EUR 205.7 million in fiscal year 2027. This cash release will occur as inventories decrease by EUR 32.2 million. While this unwind improves liquid assets, it marks the end of the windfall profits, which will reduce reported EPS to EUR 3.63.

Structural Tax and Interest Headwinds

5.
Stranded tax loss carryforwards: INDUS faces a structurally elevated tax rate because German tax law prevents offsetting trade tax losses among its autonomous subsidiaries. In its 2025 annual report, the company explained that trade tax is due at the level of individual limited partnerships, meaning that offsetting tax gains and losses between them is legally prohibited. Consequently, stranded trade tax losses remain trapped within underperforming entities. This structural friction permanently raises the corporate tax rate and depresses group net income.
6.
Reconciliation tax drag: Inefficiencies in tax offsetting create a persistent divergence between INDUS's expected tax rate and its actual cash tax expenses. The company's 2025 reconciliation statement showed that the inability to offset income for autonomous subsidiaries cost EUR 5.975 million in tax savings. This friction was even higher in 2024, when un-offset losses dragged earnings by EUR 7.115 million. These stranded losses directly inflate the effective tax rate, meaning that reported EPS remains structurally suppressed.
7.
Interest barrier limitations: Germany's restrictive interest barrier rules create additional tax headwinds that prevent the full deduction of financing costs. The company's 2025 financial notes revealed that the interest barrier had a EUR 2.451 million impact on actual tax expenses. Since INDUS cannot fully deduct its interest payments, its tax liability increases regardless of operating performance. Unless these regulations are loosened, the interest barrier will continue to restrict net earnings growth.
8.
Escalating net interest expenses: Rising interest rates and higher debt funding requirements have triggered a sharp step-up in net interest expenses. In its H1 2026 report, management disclosed that net interest expense escalated to EUR 13.058 million compared to EUR 10.890 million in H1 2025. This step-up resulted from increased borrowing to fund high-priced inventories and rising refinancing rates on promissory notes. This persistent interest drag increases financial overhead and weakens interest coverage ratios.
