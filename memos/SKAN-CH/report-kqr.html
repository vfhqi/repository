Section 2 – The Installed-Base and Consumables Annuity Loop

Section summary
  1. The regulatory compliance requirements of SKAN's 1,100 isolators fuel a highly profitable, non-cyclical services and consumables feedback loop.
  2. Sartorius and Getinge demonstrate how embedding highmargin proprietary consumables into validated workflows creates sticky annuities with immense pricing power.
  3. SKAN's strategic pivot toward a services-heavy model aims to insulate future earnings per share from volatile capital equipment cycles.

SKAN Installed Base Economics

1.
Expanding base: SKAN Group AG continues to expand its global footprint of active isolators to drive future service opportunities. The company currently maintains an active installed base of more than 1100 isolator systems worldwide. This expanding base serves as the direct physical foundation for securing highly profitable recurring revenues over a long horizon. Each new installation guarantees a reliable stream of aftermarket service demand.
2.
Mandatory requirements: SKAN Group AG benefits from strict pharmaceutical rules that require regular maintenance and annual checks. These systems require constant maintenance, spare parts, and regulatorymandated periodic requalifications to maintain Grade A conditions. This compliance-driven workflow ensures that customers cannot bypass essential services without risking their operating licenses. Consequently, the company enjoys a highly resilient and non-cyclical revenue stream.
3.
Surging demand: SKAN Group AG achieved outstanding growth in its Services and Consumables segment during the first half of 2026. The order backlog for this segment nearly doubled to reach CHF 79.8 million by June 30 2026. This substantial backlog provides the company with excellent planning visibility for future quarters as execution continues. Rising volumes of consumables and spare parts orders also helped support the segment margin.
4.
Contrary evidence: SKAN Group AG occasionally experiences temporary slowdowns in aftermarket activity due to customer-driven execution schedules. In the first half of 2025, Services and Consumables sales were flat because of delayed maintenance contracts and postponed retrofit project executions by clients. This volatility shows that even recurring service loops are not completely insulated from project timing variations.
5.
Extending lifecycles: SKAN Group AG successfully captures additional margin by upgrading older systems through its dedicated retrofit business. The typical service life of a process isolator spans up to 15 years before replacement. These retrofits bring older isolators up to current regulatory standards without requiring expensive process requalifications by the customer. This renewal loop delays capital expenditures for pharmaceutical clients while securing high-margin work.

Getinge Sterile Transfer Model

6.

Sales of the company's hardware DPTE alpha ports directly enable the future purchase of consumable beta bags by pharmaceutical customers. This physical lock-in creates a robust, long-term feedback loop. The system reduces human contamination risks during automated aseptic drug manufacturing.
7.
Improving mix: Getinge AB continues to shift its business model toward highly profitable, recurring product lines. The share of total sales represented by recurring revenue and services has reached 67% compared with only 60% in 2023. This growing mix of high-margin consumables shields the company from capital equipment fluctuations in the wider market. Investors expect this structural transition to drive sustainable operating leverage over the next several years.
8.
Accelerating growth: Getinge AB relies on its active life science and surgical workflows segments to boost high- margin consumable volumes. The group derived 64% of its fiscal year 2024 revenue from recurring consumables and services. Analysts project this high-margin revenue mix will reach 69 percent by 2030 through focused innovation. This ongoing expansion is vital because margins bottomed out and are now recovering.
9.
Mixed recovery: Getinge AB experiences mixed dynamics in its life science segment as project-based equipment sales lag behind strong consumable growth. High-margin sterile transfer products have performed exceptionally well, whereas capital-intensive washer-disinfectors suffer from subdued investment activity across the biopharma sector. This division of performance demonstrates that consumable demand is far more resilient than capital hardware cycles.

This recurring demand is tied directly to drug manufacturing volumes rather than capital budgets. As customer inventory destocking concludes, the stable bioprocess consumables business is returning to double-digit growth rates.

Recent improvements in bioprocess equipment demand indicate that customers are starting to invest in new systems. This recovery in capital equipment placements underpins future highmargin recurring demand as new manufacturing sites open. Analysts expect that bioprocessing utilization rates will continue to accelerate over the next several years.

These performance-critical systems represent over 60% of the division's revenue, boasting exceptionally high customer retention rates due to extreme switching friction. Section 4 – Working Capital, Cash Advances, and Capacity Balancing Loops

Section summary
  1. SKAN Group's negative working capital model relies on substantial customer advances to self-fund capacity expansions and preserve liquid reserves.
  2. The 2023 order normalization demonstrates how dependent SKAN's operating cash flow remains on steady project down payments.
  3. Capacity expansions built ahead of demand risk severe margin under-absorption during project deferrals, mirroring historical lifecycle volatility seen at Tecan Group.

Working Capital and Cash Advances

1.
Negative working capital: SKAN Group AG benefits from a structurally negative working capital model driven by its non-linear project business. Under this setup, pharmaceutical clients typically pay a 30% upfront deposit upon contract confirmation to fund early manufacturing. This cash flow cycle allows SKAN to finance large customized equipment projects without relying heavily on external debt.
2.
Growing customer advances: SKAN Group AG reported customer advances of CHF 123.80 million at the end of 2025 and CHF 132.20 million by mid-2026. These deposits significantly strengthen the balance sheet by providing stable funding during active fabrication phases. Consequently, these liabilities represent a primary driver of operating cash before project deliveries are officially recognized.
3.
Inventory expansion buffers: SKAN Group AG proactively increases its inventories of critical components to mitigate global supply chain disruptions. In the first half of 2023, inventories were held at higher levels than usual to avoid production halts. However, this strategy temporarily absorbs cash reserves before stock levels normalize.
4.
Contrary evidence: SKAN Group AG sometimes experiences negative free cash flows when capital expenditures outpace customer deposits. During the execution of the intensive Pre-Approved Services investment phase, cash outflows remained elevated. This shows that advances do not always fully cover rapid multi-site expansion programs.

Section 5 – Regulatory Embedding and Pre-Approved Services Adoption Loops

Section summary
  1. Regulatory embedding through inspector training and guidelines creation establishes an incredibly sticky feedback loop that channels commercial orders directly to SKAN.
  2. Software validation hurdles have delayed the launch of Pre-Approved Services under Maeven AG, highlighting the execution risks of shifting to pay-per-use business models.
  3. Maintaining a regulatory-approved status creates strong switching friction that protects SKAN's service segment margins from cyclical capital equipment downturns.

Regulatory Training and Technical Trust

1.
Profound regulatory training: SKAN Group AG leverages its unmatched scientific status to educate global health authority inspectors on aseptic manufacturing techniques. The company was invited by the European Commission to train around 700 inspectors globally on robotics and Annex 1 implementation in Brussels in 2024. This active educational role builds a strong foundation of deep technical trust between SKAN and key global drug regulators. Consequently, inspectors are trained to expect SKAN's specific design parameters during official facility reviews, which directly influences customer equipment selections.
2.
Strategic hosting positioning: SKAN Group AG further solidified its position as a central authority in compliance by hosting official regulatory training events. The company hosted the official GMP inspector training at its headquarters in Allschwil in October 2025 to showcase its latest barrier systems. Hosting these events ensures that regulatory requirements align with SKAN's technology portfolio from the very beginning. This regulatory alignment creates a powerful feedback loop that drives long-term process-isolator orders from global biopharma clients.
3.
Unrivaled industry presence: SKAN Group AG continues to stand out from its peers by maintaining direct access to regulatory bodies. The company secured the sole industry presence at the critical 2024 European Union GMP Annex 1 training session. This exclusive access allows SKAN to anticipate regulatory developments very early compared to traditional machinery manufacturers. By understanding these changes first, SKAN designs and delivers compliant machinery before competitors can adapt.
4.
Rigorous safety standards: SKAN Group AG designs its advanced containment solutions to meet the most stringent global safety requirements. The company's pure² isolators are engineered to comply fully with GMP Grade A and ISO 5 standards for sensitive applications. These systems are qualified using rigorous SMEPAC containment testing to handle highly toxic substances up to OEB 6 levels. Meeting these strict standards helps pharmaceutical customers secure fast, risk-free product approvals from local health authorities.

Guidance Drafting and Specifications Loop

5.
Active industry leadership: SKAN Group AG shapes international cleanroom standards through its extensive involvement in leading industry organizations. The company is a key member of the International Society for Pharmaceutical Engineering Containment Group. Within this professional group, SKAN contributes directly to drafting the official ISPE Containment Manual used worldwide. This continuous contribution ensures that SKAN's technical standards are embedded directly into international containment guidelines.
6.
