Reinvesting gross profit gains back into brand building will ultimately secure long-term market share and underwrite stable regional earnings growth through fiscal 2029.

Section 7 – If the Answer Differs from the Common View

Section summary
  1. Divergent growth trajectories in the newly standalone India segment could offset persistent consumer spending headwinds across North American spirits.
  2. Severe tariff risks and aggressive category price wars represent key downside scenarios that would compress international operating margins.

Potential Regional Upside Cases

1.
Standalone India outperformance: Standalone India could exceed conservative corporate growth targets as urban incomes rise and consumer demographics expand. Jefferies Research upgraded United Spirits to a buy in late October 2026, forecasting a robust 10% sales compound annual growth rate. This premiumisation is supported by the recovery of McDowell's No. 1 and Scotch duty benefits from the UK-India trade agreement. Higher volumes in India would boost consolidated gross profit dollars and cushion soft North American performance.
2.
Promising tequila recovery: Casamigos price resets could stimulate faster volume growth and secure long-term tequila category participation in the United States. Bernstein Research reported in November 2026 that Casamigos Blanco returned to a positive 4% volume growth following recent price cuts. However, aged tequila expressions like Reposado remained weak with a 6% volume decline over the same trailing period. Analysts believe resolving these demand dynamics is vital for restoring premium tequila margins.
3.
Stronger self-help measures: Accelerated productivity gains from the massive corporate restructuring program could fully protect Diageo's operating margins from near-term top-line headwinds. Sir Dave Lewis implemented a plan to deliver one billion dollars in annual savings by fiscal 2028. Jefferies Research noted that these overhead reductions de-risk the investment case by creating a firm floor on earnings. This structural margin support would offset the impact of high regional promotion costs.
4.
Contrary evidence: Nielsen scanner data compiled by Deutsche Bank in late 2026 indicates that spirits-industry demand remains structurally weak across both North America and Europe. Despite Diageo's surgical price adjustments, overall category value continued to decline with volume gains failing to offset negative price-mix trends. This suggests that lower prices may dilute divisional margins without generating sustainable volume growth. This trend would lead to further earnings downgrades.

Disruptive Downside Cases and Actors

5.
Severe tariff impacts: Proposed United States import tariffs of 25% on Mexican and Canadian imports pose a substantial downside threat to Diageo's large North American profit pool. Morgan Stanley estimated in early 2025 that these tariffs cover approximately 48% of the company's US revenues. Unmitigated tariff expenses would raise cost of goods sold and reduce operating margins. This disruption would compress group earnings before tax by mid-to-high-single digits.
6.
Intense local competition: Competitor price promotions in mature spirit categories could trigger aggressive price wars that erode premium brand equity. Local Mexican competitors like Becle SAB de CV are taking a more aggressive stance on tequila pricing in response to industry oversupply. Morgan Stanley warns that rising third-party promotional intensity limits Diageo's pricing power. This competition would cap operating profit growth.
