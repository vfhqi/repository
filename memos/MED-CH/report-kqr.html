US TOUCH Clinical Uptake and Adoption

1.
Accelerated adoption: Medartis reports that US clinical uptake of the KeriMedical TOUCH thumb prosthesis has exceeded initial executive targets because of strong surgeon demand. The Basel-based orthopaedic firm now expects to complete approximately 3,000 procedures in the United States during the 2026 fiscal period. This upgraded projection represents a massive acceleration from the initial launch target of 1,200 units established in early 2026. This rapid volume expansion is critical to achieving upgraded revenue growth guidance of 17% to 19%.
2.
Strategic training: Medartis has actively expanded its medical education footprint in the United States to drive long-term surgeon loyalty. The company acquired the Miami-based M.A.R.C. Institute in September 2026 to establish its first dedicated surgical education center in the region. Over the first six months of 2026, the company trained more than 180 hand surgeons on the TOUCH technique. This extensive training network aims to cover approximately 10% of the US hand surgeon population by the end of 2026 to ensure sustained product utilization across major clinics.
3.
Strategic cross-selling: Medartis aims to leverage the rapid clinical adoption of the TOUCH prosthesis to expand sales of its broader upper extremities portfolio. The company's sales-force strategy focuses on winning new surgeon relationships with the TOUCH joint implant and subsequently introducing them to APTUS plates and screws. Although the contribution from this cross-selling approach was still building in the first half of 2026, it remains a primary driver of long-term US growth. This portfolio expansion is critical to offsetting ongoing distributor transitions in legacy regions like Florida.
4.
Regional penetration: Medartis is rapidly expanding its direct sales representation in the United States to support the TOUCH rollout and offset past distributor disruptions. Core sales in the US region reached a record CHF 30.3 million in the first half of 2026, representing organic growth of 27.4% despite significant currency headwinds. The group has integrated 10 exclusive field experts to support the commercial rollout and facilitate the value analysis committee approvals. This focused commercial structure is designed to accelerate high-margin arthroplasty sales growth.
5.
Contrary evidence: Clinical adoption in Australia has been severely constrained because Medartis has experienced prolonged delays in securing dedicated reimbursement. Although surgeon feedback is positive, the basal thumb prosthesis has remained without formal pricing on the Prescribed List throughout the first half of 2026. This international delay shows that reimbursement approval is a prerequisite for turning clinical interest into commercial cash flows.

Medicare Reimbursement and Pricing Longevity

6.
Favorable coding: Medartis secured a new dedicated CPT code in the United States for the TOUCH prosthesis, providing a standardized pathway for hospital billing. Management has established a specialized insurance coverage team to help hospitals navigate local Medicare reimbursement channels. However, the current sellside narrative lacks detailed projections regarding the average selling price of TOUCH once temporary Medicare pass-through status eventually expires. This information gap creates significant uncertainty for long-term margins because post-pass-through pricing is highly sensitive to hospital budget cuts.
7.
Regulatory precedent: Precedents from the US Medicare system demonstrate that medical devices and therapies face substantial pricing resets when transitional reimbursement programs conclude. In the case of Akebia Therapeutics, the transition of its drug Vafseo out of the transitional TDAPA program in 2027 is forcing a commercial reset that requires significant volume growth to offset lower pricing. This precedent highlights that companies must secure broad hospital formulary adoption during the pass-through period to avoid severe revenue contraction after reimbursement rates decline.
8.
Negative pressure: Broader healthcare trends indicate that a higher mix of Medicare volumes often results in lower average selling prices. For instance, Morgan Stanley research on Wegovy in November 2026 highlights how a negative channel mix toward Medicare and cash-pay options can drive average price declines of 20%. If Medartis' TOUCH prosthesis experiences a similar shift in its patient demographic, its US margins could face substantial pressure once reimbursement limits are established by payers.

Comparing Bonesupport Reimbursement Dynamics

9.

The Swedish medtech firm's commercial momentum is heavily supported by an improved CMS reimbursement framework that took effect on October 1, 2026. This regulatory update is expected to provide greater long-term predictability for hospital administrators.
10.
Systemic reclassification: The Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services finalized reimbursement changes that integrate Bonesupport's product code into standard diagnosis-related groups. According to DNB Carnegie research in August 2026, the use of code XW0V0P7 allows eligible cases without major complications to receive higher-severity reimbursement. This change effectively creates a severity trigger that can increase hospital payments by USD 5,000 to USD 15,000 per procedure. This structural adjustment helps Bonesupport bypass local hospital budget caps by aligning reimbursement with higher resource consumption.
11.
Market penetration: Bonesupport's commercial history shows that building a substantial US market share is a multi-year process requiring extensive surgeon education. By mid-2024, DNB Carnegie estimated that Cerament G had achieved approximately 4% market share of all US bone infection and open trauma procedures. The company has steadily expanded its hospital system approvals, reaching over 140 Level 1 trauma centers by the end of 2025 compared to just 14 in 2024. This long-term trajectory confirms that continuous clinical education is essential for deep market penetration.
12.
Persistent barriers: Despite favorable reimbursement codes, hospital administrative approval remains the primary bottleneck for Bonesupport's US sales expansion. Distributors report that navigating local hospital value analysis committees is a slow and demanding process that often delays product adoption. To address these cost-benefit concerns, Bonesupport leverages extensive clinical evidence published in prestigious scientific journals. This comparison highlights that Medartis' TOUCH rollout will likely face similar hospital administrative hurdles during its US expansion.
13.
Other side of the story: While hospital administrative barriers and post-pass-through pricing resets represent real commercial risks, the unique clinical profile of the TOUCH prosthesis may insulate Medartis from severe pricing pressure. Physician feedback indicates that thumb joint arthroplasty provides significantly faster rehabilitation and superior functional outcomes compared to traditional resection surgery. If clinical demand remains exceptionally high, hand surgeons may exert substantial pressure on hospital administrators to approve TOUCH despite budget constraints.

Unhedged Intercompany FX Exposure

1.
Substantial currency volatility: Medartis faces substantial foreign exchange volatility on its balance sheet due to its extensive intercompany financing structure. The Basel-based group maintains between CHF 200 million and CHF 250 million in unhedged intercompany loans to finance foreign accounts receivables and sets. Fluctuations in key currencies like the US dollar, euro, and Australian dollar generate significant non-cash gains or losses that directly influence reported net income. This structural exposure represents a persistent source of bottom-line volatility for investors.
2.
Significant non-cash losses: Medartis reported a core net profit decline in fiscal year 2025 that was primarily driven by massive currency headwinds. The group recorded unrealized foreign exchange losses of CHF 5.3 million in 2025 due to the weakening of the US dollar and Australian dollar. These non-cash fluctuations directly depressed reported net results despite stable underlying operations. Investors must monitor these movements since they obscure Medartis' true operating earnings power.
3.
Improving translation effects: Medartis experienced a significant financial reversal in the first half of 2026 as currency pressures temporarily eased. The group's finance income was supported by foreign exchange gains of CHF 2.9 million in the first six months of 2026. This positive movement stood in sharp contrast to the CHF 5.2 million foreign exchange loss recorded in the prior-year period. Fewer foreign currency losses on intercompany loans drove a strong bottom-line improvement for the period.
4.
Contrary evidence: Medartis occasionally registers positive foreign exchange translation impacts during Swiss franc weakness, as seen in the minor currency gain of CHF 0.1 million in fiscal year 2024. This positive movement indicates that currency translation is a two-sided operational variable rather than a permanent drag. However, because the Swiss franc historically tends to strengthen against major global currencies, these positive translation phases are typically short-lived and unpredictable.

Strategic Decisions Against Hedging

5.
Deliberate cash prioritization: Medartis management deliberately chooses not to implement currency hedging programs for its translation exposures to prioritize cash preservation. Financial Chief Dirk Kirsten explained that hedging balance sheet translation risks would result in costly financial derivatives. The group explicitly places cash impact over non-cash P&L translation impacts. This strategic preference means that P&L earnings will remain volatile while protecting the group's net cash position.
6.
Prudent transfer adjustments: Medartis has explored operational adjustments to mitigate these translation risks without resorting to costly financial derivatives. Management announced plans to review the group's internal transfer pricing concept to find currency risk mitigation potential. Additionally, simplifying the global supply chain represents another operational pathway to reduce implied currency risks. These internal measures aim to stabilize financial results organically over the long term.
7.
Sustained translation risks: Medartis continues to operate with a high proportion of costs in Swiss francs while generating revenues in foreign currencies. The Basel-headquartered company maintains its primary manufacturing and headquarter activities in Switzerland, leaving its cost base exposed. Because translation risks are not hedged, the group relies entirely on its strong equity base to absorb short-term currency movements. This unhedged posture heightens the risk of EPS downward revisions during sudden Swiss franc appreciation.

Core Tax Rate Dynamics

Financial Chief Peter Hackel confirmed that this range represents the normal operating expectation for the group. This guidance is key for analysts attempting to project Medartis' long-term EPS trajectory. Achieving this targeted range is critical to supporting sustainable net income growth over the multi-year planning horizon.
9.
Rising tax expenses: Medartis recorded a core income tax expense of CHF 5.7 million in the first half of 2026. This expense represented a significant increase compared to the core tax expense of CHF 2.1 million reported in the first half of 2025. The increase was consistent with the group's stronger operating results and the consolidation of KeriMedical. This rising tax burden will moderate near-term net margin expansion as operations scale up.
10.
Fluctuating historical rates: Medartis has experienced highly variable statutory tax impacts over the past several fiscal years. The company reported a core income tax expense of CHF 5.2 million in 2025, compared with an exceptional tax credit of CHF 1.0 million in 2024. These shifts often stem from deferred tax adjustments and negative operating results in expansion regions like the United States. This historical volatility makes longterm EPS modeling more challenging for investment analysts.

Stakeholder Disruptions and Indicators

8.
Regulatory bottlenecks: National regulators and healthcare authorities possess both the motive and authority to disrupt current consensus growth assumptions. Delays in securing clinical registrations from Brazil's ANVISA have historically caused double-digit sales declines in key Latin American metropolitan markets. These regulatory delays represent a persistent hurdle for value-segment expansion and global product rollouts.
9.
Payer restrictions: Corporate and government payers represent another major disruption risk through mandated price adjustments and hospital administrative barriers. Government-mandated price cuts in Thailand and reimbursement delays in Australia severely restricted APAC regional growth in late 2026. These systemic payer actions demonstrate how administrative price pressure can erode underlying volume growth benefits.
10.
Shareholder leverage: Major shareholder Dr. h.c. Thomas Straumann, who holds 39.9% of voting rights, possesses significant power to influence corporate strategy. If commercial growth decelerates, major capital providers could pressure management to curtail costly direct sales transitions in favor of cash preservation. This strategic shift would alter long-term reinvestment patterns and protect short-term operating profit margins.
11.
Early divergence: Early indicators of strategic divergence are starting to appear in regional sales mix and localized production programs. The successful listing of the CMC1 prosthesis on Australia's Prescribed List in July 2026 serves as a key indicator of international reimbursement progress. Additionally, the local manufacturing transition in Indiana under Project Flash highlights a proactive effort to eliminate tariffs and protect operating margins.
12.
Contrary evidence: Although localized production is designed to restore gross margins to 80%, unexpected logistics disruptions could undermine these projected savings. Global supply chain shifts and climate-related operational risks could raise manufacturing costs despite onshore transition efforts. These persistent operational variables indicate that tariff mitigation is not guaranteed to automatically translate into sustainable core EBITDA margin expansion.
13.
Other side of the story: Some commentators argue that Medartis is well-positioned to maintain its premium valuation even if growth moderates because of its highly diversified multi-brand structure. The integration of Brazilian value brand NeoOrtho opens up an estimated CHF 450 million market segment that premium offerings cannot address. However, managing a complex dual-brand strategy across multiple jurisdictions increases operational compliance risks and could dilute consolidated profit margins if integration execution falters.
