Bank Polska Kasa Opieki (PEO-PL): what the Q3 2026 results do to the case

These results strengthen the existing case modestly at the operating line and leave the park where it was; the larger movement is in the share price, which has taken the case's own return arithmetic from an F toward a D without reaching the level at which the case re-opens.

What changed

Bank Pekao reported results for the quarter to 30 September 2026 on 29 October 2026. Net profit of PLN 1,517m beat the company-compiled consensus of PLN 1,457m by 4.12% and the Polish market consensus of PLN 1,410m by more, but was down 20.41% on the PLN 1,906m of Q3 2025. The beat sat below the operating line. Morgan Stanley called the quarter in line: pre-provision profit was virtually unchanged against expectations, and the net profit beat came from lower-than-expected corporate taxes and a PLN 9m reversal of Swiss-franc legal provisions.

The operating lines, set against what the case assumed:

  • Net interest income PLN 3,525m, up 6.75% quarter on quarter from PLN 3,302m, in line with the PLN 3,519m consensus. On the Earnings Review's measure the net interest margin was 3.89%, against 3.91% the quarter before and 4.15% a year earlier. The case's margin driver was set on a basis that read Q2 2026 at 3.65%, so the two series do not join and the case's 3.55% alarm cannot be read off this print. What can be read is the direction: stable quarter on quarter, with the largest revenue line growing again.
  • Net fee and commission income PLN 853m, up 11.21% year on year, about 1% above the PLN 842m expected. The case's modal fee growth was 9% to 10%.
  • Gross customer loans PLN 201,629m, up 9.08% year on year, corporate loans up 13.4%, retail cash-loan sales a record PLN 2.3bn, up 16%; customer deposits PLN 285,447m, up 9.61%, retail deposits up 7.6%. The case's modal loan growth was 8% to 9%.
  • Total provisions PLN 322m against a PLN 341m consensus, but underlying credit provisions of PLN 332m were 7.10% above the PLN 310m consensus and annualise to a cost of risk of 66 basis points. The corporate segment's cost of risk exceeded 100 basis points on two large write-offs, in transport infrastructure and food retail. Nine-month cost of risk was 49 basis points. The case's modal cost of risk was 55 to 60 basis points, with an alarm above 65.
  • Operating expenses PLN 1,467m, broadly in line; cost-to-income 34% excluding the Bank Guarantee Fund. Operating expenses rose 7.5% year on year, total expenditure including amortisation more than 11%, and IT and telecommunications costs 20.16% to PLN 465m for the nine months. A Q4 reserve of PLN 70m to PLN 80m funds voluntary redundancies for more than 500 volunteers. The case's modal cost ratio was 33% to 35%.
  • CET1 14.7%, total capital 17.8%, leverage 6.9%. Management said dividend discussion is premature until full-year results. The case's modal payout was 70%.
  • Regulatory burden up nearly PLN 800m year on year; bank corporate tax 30% in 2026, legislated to 26% in 2027 and 23% in 2028, unchanged. The PZU merger has been suspended by the Ministry of Finance, the required legal amendments not having passed. A PLN 119m competition fine over credit holidays is under appeal.
  • Targets reiterated: return on equity above 18% and cost-to-income below 35% in 2027, cost of risk 65 to 75 basis points, payout 50% to 75%. Management added that the targets are not set in stone, with flexibility particularly on cost-to-income, and projected a margin rebound under current conditions.

The sell-side response was quiet. Morgan Stanley kept Equal-weight at what it calls a fair 1.80 times 2027 tangible book, then on 26 November raised its target from PLN 305 to PLN 316, 3.61%, by rolling the valuation base to 2029 and modestly lifting 2028 net interest income. It had cut its FY2026 earnings per share from PLN 23.22 in August to PLN 20.79 on 20 October, about 10%, before the print, so the beat was against a lowered bar. The consensus target range was PLN 225 to PLN 305 with a median of PLN 265.70 on 20 October. Deutsche Bank published nothing on the quarter and no expert-call coverage was found; the post-results evidence rests on one broker.

The price has done more than the quarter. The shares closed at PLN 248.6 on 26 November 2026 against the PLN 276.5 the case was written at, about 10% lower: below the 50-day average of PLN 265.77, just above the 150-day of PLN 246.33, 5.1% above the 200-day, down 2.7% over three months and 13% below the 52-week high of PLN 285.9. Consensus earnings per share 24 months forward have risen to PLN 29.44 from the case's PLN 28.28, so the multiple is 8.4x at the 45th percentile of the stock's ten-year history, against the case's 9.78x at the 92nd percentile of five years. Consensus earnings revisions are positive, +5% over three months and +9% over six, but the buy share is 31% against the case's 38%, with a 15-point fall over three months, and the revision-momentum score is 5 from the case's 8. The pinned letters read technical momentum A, sell-side earnings momentum B, valuation C.

The challenge

Three readings of these results that the case does not hold, in descending order of force, and two obvious readings that fail.

First: the sign of the margin debate may have flipped, and the case has no branch for it. Every rate branch in the case points one way: a hold to the April 2027 meeting, then 50 basis points of cuts, with alarms on an earlier or deeper cut. The evidence now admits the opposite. The Monetary Policy Council held the reference rate at 3.75% with a hawkish tone and the central bank's staff lifted the 2027 inflation forecast by 145 basis points to 4.2%; ING contemplates a 50 basis point tightening in Q1 2027; the bank's own economists said rates could rise in the early months of next year; management projects a margin rebound; and net interest income rose 6.75% in a single quarter while the margin held. On the company's sensitivity of 10 to 15 basis points of margin per 100 basis points of rates, a hike is worth little directly, but it removes the compression that the sell-side models (Morgan Stanley's 48 basis points cumulative to 2028) and the case's modal path both carry, and it does so on the line the case puts at about 75% of operating revenue. For this reading to be right, energy-driven inflation would have to pass into wages, the central bank would have to act rather than talk, promotional deposit pricing (up to 5.7% in the quarter) would have to stop short of eating the asset-yield gain, and the POLSTR drag from 1 January 2027 would have to be the 10 to 15 basis points on new mortgages rather than the 2% to 3% of interest income a broad voluntary switch could cost. If it is right, operating earnings growth returns to a bank the case says grows only by tax refund, and FY2028 consensus heads toward the PLN 33 at which the case re-opens on earnings rather than on price. We think this reading plausible, not probable; its weakness is that a tightening cycle into GDP growth of 2.9% feeds the second reading.

Second: the growth engine is buying volume with credit risk, and the case's asset-quality concern is pointed at the wrong book. The case's Demand rating of B rests on corporate lending with two-plus years of visibility, and its asset-quality concern is the retail and mortgage gap to PKO BP. The quarter says the corporate book is where the risk sits: corporate loans up 13.4% and business real estate up 27.2% while working-capital lending grew only 5.6% on what the bank called cautious business sentiment; corporate cost of risk above 100 basis points on two large single-name write-offs; underlying provisions 7.10% above consensus, annualising to 66 basis points, above the case's 65 basis point alarm in the first quarter it was tested. Beside a sector loan-to-deposit ratio at a record low of 59.9% and the case's own note that corporate spreads are being competed down, the reading is that excess liquidity is pushing the system to reach for corporate volume as the EU-funded capital cycle fades from mid-2027, and that the write-offs are the leading edge rather than noise. For it to be right, Q4 2026 and Q1 2027 would have to show further large-ticket losses and a cost of risk holding above 65 basis points; the company's reiterated 65 to 75 basis point range for 2027 would be a floor, not a ceiling. The case's own drop-through puts a 65 basis point cost of risk at about 1.3% off FY2028 earnings per share, so the earnings damage is modest; the damage is to the Foundations claim of a well-provisioned number two and to the return-on-equity target. Against the reading: management calls the losses two names, nine-month cost of risk is 49 basis points, and the non-performing loan ratio on the August 2026 figure was 3.3%. We put it at possible to plausible, and it is the one the second review cannot settle.

Third, weaker because the case half-holds it: the operator has told us the cost target is negotiable. The case's internal forces are strong on strategy and standards, and the output evidence for its 'Major Corporate Change' setup is the cost ratio. Management now says the targets are not set in stone with flexibility particularly on cost-to-income, that promotional and marketing spending will likely stay high next year, and that digital capability lags ambition with 2027 the breakthrough year, while IT costs rose 20.16% and total expenditure more than 11%, and PKO BP added over 470k Allegro Klik customers in the same quarter on a 4.46% margin. The reading is that Pekao is entering a catch-up investment cycle in which the cost ratio becomes aspirational, and the self-help that defines the setup turns into spend. For it to be right the ratio would have to pass the case's 35.5% alarm in Q4 2026 or Q1 2027 after the PLN 70m to PLN 80m redundancy reserve, with the savings from more than 500 leavers absorbed by marketing and technology rather than falling through. The case already asks for proof the ratio holds through the IT catch-up; what is new is that management volunteered the flexibility. Possible, not yet plausible, at 34%.

Two readings fail on the evidence. "The beat was low quality, so the thesis is weaker" fails because the case never leaned on quarterly net profit; it leaned on the operating lines, and those were in line or better, the 20.41% year-on-year fall being the tax step the case already carries. "Pekao is losing the deposit franchise" cannot be rested on this quarter: the broker peer table shows Pekao deposit growth of 5.2% while the company reported 9.61%, a conflict the Earnings Review itself flags. Promotional pricing at 5.7% presses on the case's 36% deposit beta, but that is a pressure the case names, not a new reading.

What would settle it

The second review, four working days on, carries the broker estimates and targets that land after the print: whether FY2027 and FY2028 consensus move toward or away from the case's PLN 26.36 and PLN 28.90, whether anyone follows Morgan Stanley's FY2026 cut, whether a second broker publishes at all, and where the sell-side puts the 2027 cost of risk against three unreconciled ranges (the company's 65 to 75 and 40 to 50 basis points, the broker's 55). That settles the earnings-trigger half of the first reading: if FY2028 consensus is heading toward PLN 33, the case re-opens on its own terms. It does not carry a reconciliation of the margin basis, a central-bank decision, a Q4 cost of risk, or anything on POLSTR take-up.

The rate sign settles outside the company: the Monetary Policy Council's decisions through Q1 2027 against ING's tightening call and the case's dated April 2027 meeting, with the Q4 2026 net interest income and margin at the results in late January 2027 showing whether the quarter's 6.75% was volume or the start of a rebound.

The corporate credit reading settles at the Q4 2026 results in late January 2027 (cost of risk, any further single-name losses) and the Q1 2027 results in late April 2027, the case's own two-quarter test. The company has not committed to reconcile its 40 to 50 and 65 to 75 basis point ranges or to disclose single-name exposures; that is a question to put to it, not one the calendar answers.

The cost reading settles at the same two dates: the Q4 ratio after the redundancy reserve, and the Q1 2027 ratio the case already names as a dated step.

POLSTR's effect on the margin is unlikely to be visible before the Q1 2027 results; the company has given a sensitivity for rates but none for voluntary switching, and nothing dated supplies it.

The PZU option has no date. The merger is suspended; the case's alarm falls at Q1 2027 without a bill tabled. A ministerial alternative structure is the only thing that would move it before then, and none is scheduled.

The price is observable daily, and the case's three fixed lines re-base nightly; PLN 225 is the number.
