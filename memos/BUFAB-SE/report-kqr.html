Section 2 – Regulatory Scope and Sourcing Exposure Mapping 20 findings

Section summary
  1. Bufab faces material CBAM regulatory exposure as non-EU procurement accounts for over half its purchasing volume, concentrated in high-carbon Asian manufacturing jurisdictions.

Non-EU Sourcing and Country Risk Allocations

1.
Concentrated Asian procurement: Non-EU purchasing accounts for 51.7% of total procurement, leaving the group heavily exposed to carbon border adjustments on foreign C-part manufacturing
2.
Dominant Chinese footprint: China represents Bufab's single largest sourcing origin at 29.2% of purchasing volume, exposing goods to high domestic grid emission factors
3.
Significant Taiwanese volume: Taiwan supplies 15.4% of purchasing volume, offering a lower-tariff alternative to mainland China but remaining subject to CBAM default values
4.
Emerging market exposures: India and Vietnam account for 3.1% and 1.3% of purchasing volume respectively, where high carbon-intensity manufacturing triggers punitive default markups
5.
Mitigating European base: European suppliers handle approximately 45% of group procurement volume, providing a sheltered local sourcing core completely exempt from border tariffs

Product Portfolio CN Code Mappings and Scope Coverage

6.
Heavy steel exposure: Steel components represent more than 90% of Bufab's total material volume, placing the vast majority of product lines directly under CBAM scope
7.
Core fastener classification: Mainstream steel fasteners are classified under Combined Nomenclature heading 7318, covering threaded bolts, screws, nuts, and washers
8.
Expanded rod categories: Galvanised threaded steel rods without heads fall under Combined Nomenclature code 7318 15 42, incurring standard iron and steel embedded carbon calculations
9.
Secondary aluminum coverage: Aluminum C-parts fall under Combined Nomenclature headings 7607 through 7610, encompassing foils, tubes, pipe fittings, and structural profiles
10.
Non-consensus downstream risk: Consensus underestimates the impact of proposed regulatory scope extensions to 180-200 downstream metal-intensive products planned for 2028

Direct Versus Indirect Emissions Accounting Mechanics

11.
Scope 1 coverage limitation: Definitive CBAM rules for iron, steel, and aluminum currently apply exclusively to Scope 1 direct manufacturing process emissions
12.
Excluded indirect power emissions: Scope 2 indirect emissions from electricity consumption remain excluded for steel and aluminum imports to match domestic EU Emissions Trading System indirect cost compensation
13.
Punitive default values: Importers relying on non-verified supplier data face steep default values, such as 4.7 tonnes CO2 per tonne for Indian steel imports
14.
Escalating default markups: The regulatory markup on default emissions values escalates from 10% in 2026 to 20% in 2027 and 30% from 2028 onward
15.
Non-consensus verification edge: Consensus assumes high default penalty costs, but major foreign suppliers are moving en masse to third-party verified primary emissions data to eliminate default markups

Carbon Price Trajectories and Benchmark Sensitivity

16.
Baseline carbon assumption: Modelled CBAM certificate liabilities assume an EU Emissions Trading System allowance price baseline of €80 to €100 per tonne of CO2
17.
Range-bound near-term outlook: Carbon market analysts expect Emissions Trading System prices to remain range-bound between €60 and €90 per tonne through 2026
18.
Long-term price inflation: European carbon prices are projected to break above €100 per tonne after 2030 as annual allowance caps continue to tighten structurally
19.
Medium confidence rating: Confidence in exact liability mapping remains medium, anchored by preliminary H1 2026 financial accruals and verified European Commission benchmark updates
20.
Other side of the story: A rapid surge in carbon prices toward €120 per tonne, combined with failure to secure verified supplier emissions data, could double Bufab's unmitigated border compliance liabilities
