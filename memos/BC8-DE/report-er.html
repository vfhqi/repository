Section 16 – Conclusion

The H1/Q3 2026 results changed Bechtle's earnings narrative. Before the update, the market's debate was about margin compression. Analysts expected internal IT migration projects to create an absolute pre-tax earnings headwind of roughly three percent in fiscal 2026, a fifteen basis point margin drag on business volume. They also questioned whether public-sector demand had sustainably resumed.

The Q2 and Q3 prints answered the second question and largely neutralised the first. Q3 business volume grew nineteen percent to €2,438 million, with organic growth of 17.5% and German business volume up 20.8% organically. EBT rose 13.9% to €91.7 million even though the internal IT spend continued. The Q3 EBT margin held at 3.8%, so the cost drag was absorbed rather than passed through to profitability.

Guidance was raised for the second time in November, to EBT growth of more than ten percent from five to ten percent. Taken together, the 18-month to threeyear EPS debate moves from "can margins survive the investment cycle?" to "how long can volume-led operating leverage run?". The consensus deviations were material and mostly positive. Q3 business volume beat consensus by seven percent, and EBT of €92 million beat the company-collected €85 million by nine percent.

Estimates followed. Deutsche Bank raised pre-tax forecasts by about five percent for fiscal 2026–2028, and Jefferies lifted EPS estimates by three to five percent over the same horizon. Jefferies went further, arguing that consensus FY2027 pre-tax earnings of €382 million look too conservative given the backlog and accelerating public demand. It also argued that the €10 billion business volume target for 2030 is outdated, with that level reachable as early as fiscal

Cancom lowered its fiscal 2026 sales guidance after Q3 sales fell three percent, while Computacenter raised its profit guidance. The second was the absence of Baader Bank, which did not publish updated commentary after the ad-hoc guidance lift. That limits how fully the revision cycle can be read from the domestic sell-side.

The single most important change is that visibility, not margin, now anchors the earnings outlook. The record order backlog of €3,607 million, up thirty-three percent, combined with public sector demand up thirty-five percent in Q3 and a €250 million Bavarian judiciary contract and an €80 million education contract, supports revenue conversion well into fiscal 2027. At the same time, nine-month EBT of €233.5 million is up 15.3% year-on-year, while full-year guidance asks only for growth above ten percent.

By my arithmetic, a ten percent increase on fiscal 2025 EBT of €324 million needs only marginal Q4 growth over the prior-year quarter. That leaves a margin of safety, or conservatism, in the guide. The step-change therefore lies in the base from which fiscal 2027 and 2028 EPS are built, not just in the 2026 number. Management's Ambition 2030 targets of €10 billion in business volume and a five percent EBT margin now look less like a stretch on volume and more like a test of margin. Sell-side analysts expect the new leadership era to spark upgrades to the medium-term targets in early 2027.

The contradictory views deserve weight, and two of them cut at the quality of the beat. The bullish case rests on the backlog, public frameworks, share gains and German stimulus, with Deutsche Bank expecting the benefits of fiscal spending to become more visible from fiscal year 2027. The bearish case has four parts. First, the growth is partly price-driven. Q2 revenue growth was attributed to higher price levels, and analysts estimated PC prices up 15–20% and server and storage prices up 50–100%, prompting customers to pull forward orders.

Second, the volume headline overstates what reaches the income statement. Q3 revenue grew only 6.1% against 19% business volume, as software growth drives deferred revenue. Gross margin on business volume fell by a full percentage point to 13.3% on large, below-average-margin deals. Third, cash conversion is weak.

Nine-month operating cash flow was negative €132 million, and inventories rose 74.8% to €667.2 million, with eighty percent reportedly backed by firm customer contracts. Fourth, the private sector has not recovered, with SME customers showing high investment reluctance and postponing major IT upgrades. I weigh the bullish case as stronger for fiscal 2026 and the first half of 2027, because backlog and framework call-offs are contractual.

Beyond that, durability depends on private-sector hardware demand and on whether price inflation reverses. Leadership change is a further uncertainty: the report notes Konstantin Ebert takes over as CEO on January 1, 2027 alongside a reduction of the Executive Board to three members, which could be seamless or could slow integration of new acquisitions such as ITQuotes and Bespoke XYZ.
