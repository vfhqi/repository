Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. Divergent AI forces will dictate whether European software majors can successfully re-rate, as the initial valuation de-rating has concluded while structural monetization proofs remain highly localized and unproven at scale. High plausibility exists for long-term structural differentiation between highly resilient transaction-based software platforms and seat-dependent application developers, though near-term direct monetization trends remain moderately unproven. The European software sector now confronts a dual-force paradigm where premium AI pricing opportunities collide with deflationary seat compression dynamics, creating an unresolved tension that will determine earnings trajectories through the remainder of the decade. Investors' fears of artificial intelligence disruption pushed global software multiples to decade-plus lows earlier in 2026, yet the UK software sector has staged a strong recovery of approximately 42% from its late March lows. This valuation rebound reflects nascent evidence of execution resilience rather than definitive proof of sustainable AI monetization at scale. While Business AI is strategic, meaningful agentic revenue remains difficult to identify, and the core debate centers on whether premium tiers and agents can raise prices while seat compression and commoditization cap revenue growth. & Co Redburn On the value-accretive side of the equation, tangible premium pricing mechanisms are beginning to materialize across the peer group. Resellers negotiating SAP RISE contracts in late 2026 are facing a 20% commercial uplift over prior on-premises baselines, while Morgan Stanley's August 2026 survey found that 33% of SAP resellers report paid Business AI adoption. Sage is successfully monetizing AI through higher pricing on premium bundles, and Dassault expects to shift toward token-driven pricing linked to engineering complexity. However, deployment breadth remains constrained, with fewer than a third of pilot customers able to quantify any concrete ROI benefit.

On the value-capping side, evidence of seat compression and feature commoditization is accumulating rapidly. Enterprise customers may execute smaller seat renewals during contract negotiations due to productivity efficiencies, and AI-induced coding efficiencies could compress the absolute number of developer licenses sold to corporate clients. Drawing automation tools can slash manual drafting times from 66 hours to two minutes per component, allowing enterprises to execute engineering projects with fewer human CAD operators.

Meanwhile, AI risk is concentrated in low-end drafting tools where feature commoditization forces defensive integration rather than accretive premium monetization. Business model polarization is driving divergent outcomes across the European software landscape. Morgan Stanley assigns SAP a class-leading 91% net AI exposure score, reflecting its irreplaceable role as the transactional system of record for complex enterprise operations.

Travel software giant Amadeus remains insulated from front-end search disruption because its economics are anchored in backend booking execution rather than simple discovery. In contrast, TeamViewer faces severe structural AI pressure because automated workflows displace human-intensive remote support and help-desk ticketing volumes. The valuation trajectory from trough to partial recovery underscores persistent uncertainty regarding terminal growth assumptions.

When European software stocks sold off in February and March of 2026, compressed valuations implied flat to negative terminal growth rates from 2030, yet the sector-wide spring rebound pushed average European software valuations higher to price in roughly 2% terminal growth from 2030. Current multiples now embed cautious optimism that incumbents can adapt, though Dassault Systèmes still possessed the worst growth-to-valuation algorithm in the sector, trading on an expensive valuation relative to its peers despite delivering the slowest organic revenue expansion. & Co Redburn Critical data gaps prevent definitive mid-term resolution of the monetization question.

SAP's disclosures lack a detailed cost bridge to determine if accelerating AI infrastructure costs are structurally dilutive to cloud gross margins, and actual customer transaction volumes and long-term token consumption metrics remain unmapped by Dassault. Without transparent disclosure of consumption economics, attach rates, and margin structures, investors must rely on indirect proxies and management commentary to assess the sustainability of emerging pricing models.

Looking forward, second-order implications will reshape competitive boundaries beyond traditional software vendors. Hyperscaler Forward Deployed Engineering teams are increasingly competing with traditional integrators to capture highly complex AI implementations, while systems integrators are reorienting toward data governance and clean-core S/4HANA migrations, where human process orchestration remains critical. The ultimate resolution of the AI monetization question will depend on whether transaction-based platforms can sustain premium pricing power as agentic capabilities mature, or whether seat compression and feature commoditization erode revenue growth across the entire software value chain.
