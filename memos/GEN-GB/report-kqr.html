Section 3 – Pricing power, capacity constraints and competitive windows

Section summary
  1. Strong local pricing power and structured cost pass-throughs allow European building product leaders to capture regulatory rent despite volatile input costs.
  2. Structural capacity constraints in glass, extrusion, and local plumbing networks secure a competitive window of 18 to 24 months before supply catch-up or imports erode margins.

Contract structures and raw material pass-through

1.
Rolling-contract lags: Lindab International operates on three-month average maturity contracts for steel purchasing, which provides pricing stability but delays the margin benefit of falling raw material costs during market downturns.
2.
Extended inventory pipelines: Managing a large safety stock means it takes four to six months for raw materials to flow through Lindab, exposing it to short-term pricing pressure from spot-market competitors.
3.
Surcharges protecting margins: Geberit successfully implemented high single-digit and double-digit price increases on plastic piping products in June 2026 to offset raw material cost inflation.
4.
Lagged pricing dynamics: Despite experiencing direct material price inflation of 7% in Q2 2026, Geberit expects its pricing actions to fully offset raw material headwind pressures by the second half of the year.
5.
Responsible price adjustments: Genuit Group implemented double-digit price increases in May 2026 across 60% of its product portfolio, successfully turning its like-for-like sales trajectory positive.
6.
Rational industry behavior: Genuit's management noted that competitor pricing followed similar upward trajectories on the same day, demonstrating highly rational pricing discipline across developers and peers.

Capacity constraints and domestic supply limits

7.
Extrusion capacity shortages: Former Eurocell management expects that when the housing market recovers, severe capacity shortages in extruders and fabricators will protect manufacturer profit margins.
8.
Sash window backlogs: Inwido's recent UK acquisitions, including Victorian House Window Group, operate near-capacity at 1,750 windows weekly, but face unprecedented backlogs from UK planning relaxations allowing uPVC replacements.
9.
Glass supply vulnerabilities: Anticipated triple-glazed requirements of 0.8 U-values in UK new builds are expected to trigger a severe shortage of glass supply because major suppliers dormantized float plants.
10.
Scalable manufacturing flex: Genuit operates with substantial volume headroom, as capacity is not expected to be a bottleneck since the group can easily add production shifts as demand recovers.
11.
Wholesaler relationship barriers: Industry leaders like Geberit face margin limits despite volume recoveries, as they must consistently invest in installer relationships and marketing to protect their behind-the-wall moats.

12.

13.
Localized product standards: UK-specific building codes and imperial product sizing create a formidable barrier to import penetration from metric-based Continental European manufacturers.
14.
Erosion of Polish imports: While Polish uPVC window imports achieved massive growth historically, Italian residential data shows that PVC frame imports plummeted fifty-six percent in 2024.
15.
Chinese competitor threats: Asian manufacturers are executing aggressive market share strategies in the European heat pump sector, creating severe downward pricing pressure on distributors.
16.
Temporary pre-buying windows: Zehnder warns that its strong 7% ventilation sales growth in early 2026 was partially driven by pre-buying ahead of price increases, indicating a temporary margin window.
17.
Other side of the story: If raw material costs rapidly deflate, manufacturers holding expensive contract inventories will suffer persistent gross margin compression as agile, spot-buying peers aggressively underbid them on pricing.
