Section 1 – Brief company description and history

Section summary
  1. Wise went into its earnings with depressed investor sentiment following regulatory overhangs, despite
1.
its highly disruptive cross-border payments infrastructure continuing to deliver solid organic customer growth. Core mission and infrastructure advantages: Wise operates as a major cross-border payments disruptor in the global financial technology sector. The company's core mission is centered on building money without borders by facilitating payments that are instant, convenient, transparent, and low-cost. This strategy allows Wise to offer cross-border transfers that are significantly cheaper than leading traditional banks globally.

2.
Multi-product segment architecture: The company delivers its services through three primary product segments, consisting of the Wise Account for personal users, Wise Business for small-to-medium enterprises, and the Wise Platform. The Wise Platform enables commercial banks and larger fintech enterprises to integrate Wise's proprietary infrastructure directly into their own systems via an application programming interface. This multiproduct strategy allows the company to monetize global consumer activity on a highly favorable cost structure.
3.
Infrastructure-led competitive moat: Rather than relying on the traditional and expensive correspondent banking network, Wise connects local payment rails and central banks into a single proprietary network. By bypassing correspondent intermediaries, Wise has established a highly defensible structural cost advantage over traditional finance players. The company's global payment infrastructure is currently powered by over 80 licenses and direct access to nine domestic payment systems worldwide.
4.
Fragmented target addressable market: Wise estimates the total addressable market for its cross-border payment products at approximately $43 trillion in annual transaction volume. This massive market is highly fragmented and still dominated by legacy players, with traditional banks controlling more than 70% of global cross-border flows. In comparison, Wise processed a record $243 billion in cross-border transactions in fiscal year 2026, representing a still-low overall market penetration of around 5% for individuals and less than 1% for smalland-medium businesses.
5.
Solid historical volume growth: Over the last three fiscal years, the company has driven rapid organic expansion by reinvesting its efficiency gains back into lower prices. In fiscal year 2026, Wise's cross-border volume grew by 31% year-over-year to $243.5 billion, up from $185.2 billion in fiscal year 2025. This volume expansion was fueled by a 21% increase in its active customer base, which reached 19 million active customers by the end of fiscal year 2026.
6.
Evolving profit margin framework: Following the rise in global interest rates, Wise transitioned to a new US GAAP financial reporting framework, introducing an updated medium-term target structure. The company guided to a medium-term income before tax margin of 15% to 20%, but expects an elevated near-term target of 20% to 25% until it can return 80% of its interest income above a 1% yield to its customers. For fiscal year 2026, the company recorded a margin of c. 26.4% on net revenues of $2.50 billion.
7.
Peer historical operational stress: In contrast to Wise's robust organic growth, its legacy competitor Western Union has struggled with persistent operational challenges and margin retreats. In 2026, Western Union cut its full-year adjusted earnings expectations due to persistent headwinds in North American retail corridors. This deterioration reflects broader macroeconomic and immigration policy headwinds that have heavily strained legacy cash-remittance corridors.
8.
Competitor margin erosion: Analysts at Deutsche Bank highlighted that Western Union experienced a massive margin and earnings miss in the middle of calendar year 2026. The miss was driven by a structural transaction shift away from high-profit cash payouts and towards lower-margin digital wallets. Consequently, legacy operators are launching aggressive opex-reduction programs to protect earnings from structural volume declines across the industry.
9.
Share price performance trends: Despite its strong underlying fundamentals, the share price of Wise experienced significant volatility over the 12 months preceding its October 2026 results. The stock's 52-week trading range fluctuated widely, leaving the valuation relative to the broader payments sector highly discounted. This performance is largely attributable to European money-laundering allegations in June and US charter refusals in September.

Section 3 – Key moving parts and changes

Section summary
  1. Wise is transitioning through stabilizing leadership changes and upgraded short-term revenue guidance, but must navigate prolonged regulatory overhangs and transient dual-listing friction.

Leadership and Corporate Structuring

1.
Planned board transition: Non-Executive Director Barak Eilam has been appointed as Chair Designate to succeed David Wells. David Wells will officially step down from his position in April 2027. Barak Eilam brings extensive experience building global technology businesses, which will help scale the enterprise platform business over the next three years.
2.
Dual listing friction: Wise executed a dual listing process in H1 FY27 to broaden its global investor base. This corporate action incurred approximately 15 million dollars in non-recurring administrative expenses. These transitional listing costs compressed the company's near-term profitability by about two percentage points in the first half.
3.
CFO presentation focus: Newly appointed Chief Financial Officer Emmanuel Thomassin participated in his first major post-listing earnings call. He outlined the company's multi-year investment framework to the investment community. This leadership addition helps formalize the company's US GAAP financial reporting transition going forward.

Margin Profiles and Capital Allocation

4.
Elevated near term margins: Wise expects to report an elevated near-term income before tax margin of 20% to 25%. This temporary margin bump is driven by the company retaining a portion of net interest income. Over the next three years, management plans to return eighty percent of interest above a one percent yield back to customers.
5.
Medium term margin compression: The company continues to target a lower long-term income before tax margin of 15% to 20%. Reinvesting efficiency gains back into lower prices will eventually bring profit margins down from their current high levels. This long-term margin adjustment is a central pillar of Wise's customer-acquisition strategy.
6.
Massive share buyback: Wise launched a substantial share purchase program of over $500 million in FY27. Jefferies analyst Hannes Leitner noted that the buyback was 39% complete as of mid-October 2026. This capital allocation program represents an opportunistic reduction in diluted share count of around 4%.
7.
Retained earnings preference: Wise continues to enforce a strict no-dividend distribution policy. Management intends to retain all future earnings to fund the development and expansion of its cross-border infrastructure. This cash preservation policy distinguishes Wise from legacy payments peers who prioritize returning capital via dividends to shareholders.

Volume, Revenue, and Growth Dynamics

8.
Upgraded short term guidance: Wise upgraded its constant-currency net revenue growth guidance for FY27 to 17.5-20%. This represents an increase from the previous expectation of growth around the middle of the 15-20% range. The upgrade reflects resilient customer acquisition and good card spend momentum heading into the second half.
9.
Constant currency revenue momentum: Net revenue grew by 21% constant-currency year-over-year to $739.1 million in Q2 FY27. This delivery was supported by strong active customer growth of 20% yearover-year. The total active customer base reached 12.5 million during the quarter.
10.
Shifting segment growth mix: Cross-border transaction volume reached $71.7 billion in the second quarter. Morgan Stanley analyst Russell Quelch highlighted that business volumes grew 32% year-over-year, significantly outperforming personal volumes which only grew 18% year-over-year. This shift indicates that the Business segment is becoming a major growth engine for the firm.

Regulatory and Macro Overhangs

11.
US trust charter rejection: The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency denied Wise US's application for a national trust bank charter in July 2026. The regulator cited pre-existing deficiencies in the company's Bank Secrecy Act and anti-money laundering programs. This rejection delays Wise's strategic goal of obtaining direct master account access to the US Federal Reserve.
12.
Ongoing Belgian investigation: Belgian prosecutors are concluding a criminal investigation into potential anti- money laundering compliance failures at Wise Europe. The probe involves approximately €500 million in suspicious transactions processed through the European subsidiary. This ongoing compliance inquiry creates a material regulatory overhang on valuation multiples.
13.
Compliance cost escalation: To address these global regulatory challenges, Wise is allocating approximately one-third of its ongoing investments to compliance. This increased operational spend is designed to prevent financial crime and strengthen internal risk controls. William Blair analyst Cristopher Kennedy estimated that about one third of the workforce is currently dedicated to financial crime prevention.
14.
Currency conversion headwinds: A stronger US dollar during the second quarter created a localized growth headwind. Morgan Stanley analyst Russell Quelch noted that USD strength slowed down large-ticket transfers in the US. This macro factor contributed to a slight two percent total volume miss against consensus expectations.
15.
Payment rail expansion: Wise successfully went live with its ninth direct domestic payment rail integration in Malaysia during Q2 FY27. Connecting directly to local infrastructure allows the company to bypass intermediary correspondent banks. This expansion enhances transaction speeds and reinforces its structural price leadership in emerging markets.
16.
Other side of the story: While bulls focus on the upgraded near-term net revenue guidance, Morgan Stanley analyst Russell Quelch warned that prolonged regulatory scrutiny could permanently slow down Platform adoption among major financial institutions. Potential bank partners may require longer due diligence cycles, which would restrict Wise's ability to reach its long-term target of Platform representing over fifty percent of total volumes.
