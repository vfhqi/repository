Robertet Derivative Hedging Programs

1.

The company restricts the use of these derivative instruments solely to hedging future transactions or firm commitments and prohibits speculative trading. These forward contracts are entered into with top-tier financial institutions to keep counterparty risk minimal. This disciplined approach ensures that commercial derivative contracts protect the group's gross margins without introducing speculative volatility.
2.
Rising volume: Robertet SA expanded its currency hedging portfolio significantly in 2025 as currency volatility intensified. The nominal amount of USD currency forwards rose to $17,000k in 2025, compared with $10,500k in 2024. This nominal position was substantially higher than the $5,430k held by the group at the end of 2023. This progressive volume increase shows that the group is stepping up its transactional protection during periods of euro strengthening.
3.
Unhedged purchasing: Robertet SA does not implement any derivative hedging instruments for its raw material purchases. Sourcing managers cannot hedge these transactions because the vast majority of natural materials are not listed on any organized exchange. This lack of derivative coverage leaves the purchasing cost base highly exposed to localized inflation and spot FX market moves. Consequently, raw material cost fluctuations must be managed through commercial negotiations rather than financial derivative hedging programs.
4.
Strategic swaps: Robertet SA actively manages its floating-rate debt exposure through interest rate swap contracts. The company set up a swap in September 2022 to cover 50% of its OPAS loan at a fixed rate of 2.605%. A final swap in August 2024 extended coverage to 75% for both facilities, helping mitigate interest rate risks on earnings per share.
