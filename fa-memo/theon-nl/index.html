The operating drivers behind the inputs

  • Night vision is capped by tube supply.
  • Supply: Exosens agreed "over 400,000" tubes for 2026 to 2030 (3 December 2025), about 80,000 to 90,000 a year. Harder Digital adds about 13,000 a year for internal use (15,000 capacity in 2025, 20,000 in 2027, about 66% internal).
  • Demand on that supply: the OCCAR programme (100,000 binoculars, about 1 EURbn, deliveries 2027 to 2029) needs about 200,000 Exosens tubes, about 67,000 a year (Bernstein, 10 December 2025).
  • Night-vision revenue therefore grows at tube volume plus price: +11% in FY2027, then +4% to +7% a year. Price is about +1.5% a year blended: zero on OCCAR's fixed price and +3% to +4% on bilateral and export contracts, passing through Exosens' tube price rises (Bernstein: 2,531 EUR in 2025 to 2,898 EUR in 2029).
  • Options (902 EURm) and pre-selections lengthen the backlog rather than raising annual revenue.
  • Non-night-vision organic growth runs at 26%, 25%, 20% and 17%. The sources:
  • IRIS-C thermal clip-ons (OCCAR IRCOD: 50 EURm initial order plus 150 EURm of options);
  • Kappa (42 EURm SAFE-funded order) and PHYLAX for Rheinmetall (over 40 EURm initial);

How to read these figures

  • Amounts are in millions of the reporting currency; per-share figures are in units of that currency. Negative figures are shown in brackets.
  • Historical figures use FactSet's standardised lines, which can differ from the company's own presentation.
  • Earnings per share appears on four bases, which must not be mixed: reported basic; FactSet's adjusted diluted, before unusual items; the Viewforth definition, which is the model's standing convention; and the brokers' adjusted basis, on which consensus is based.
  • Consensus is FactSet's median of the broker estimates it holds. Where FactSet's line-by-line consensus differs from the broker-by-broker detail, the line-by-line table takes precedence.
  • Viewforth's forecasts are the Excel model's base case. There are no bull or bear cases.
