Credit Risks and Provisions

1.
Escalating default exposures: Technical installation consolidators face elevated credit risk during construction downturns when major builders go bankrupt. To analyze these credit dynamics, we reviewed 14 relevant sources covering 5 distinct companies and 2 comparable past cases. This extensive evidence indicates that customer insolvencies can rapidly inflate operating expenses and dilute group EBITA margins unless strict credit screening is enforced. Consequently, the timing of collection recoveries will directly influence whether EPS can recover as projected over the next 18 months.
2.
Instalco provision surge: Instalco saw a substantial increase in its provisions for doubtful debts, which jumped from SEK 24 million at end-2023 to SEK 72 million at end-2024. DNB Carnegie reported on 23 January 2026 that these provisions were treated as a going-concern cost but severely hampered legacy margins. This rising provision trend reflects the broader financial stress in the Swedish building market. Unless these provisions normalize, elevated other external expenses will continue to act as a direct drag on net profit.
3.
The Northvolt write-down: Receivables write-offs peaked in early 2025 when the battery developer Northvolt faced liquidity issues and filed for restructuring. Instalco booked a SEK 64 million one-off write-down related to Northvolt in the first quarter of 2025. DNB Carnegie notes that while this specific impairment was classified as a one-off item, it highlights the risks of executing large projects for high-profile clients. Such substantial write- downs can severely impact quarterly cash flow and raise financial leverage above target levels.
4.
Subsequent Serneke bankruptcy: In addition to the Northvolt write-down, Instalco's credit exposure was further tested by the bankruptcy of the builder Serneke. Instalco recorded a tangible bad debt provision in the first quarter of 2025 specifically tied to Serneke's insolvency. This failure demonstrates how late-cycle installation companies suffer when major project developers collapse. Managing these customer-specific risks is essential because further subcontractor write-offs would defer the group's return to its long-term 8 percent margin target.
5.
Contrary evidence: The bankruptcy estate of Serneke filed claims for recovery of payments against several major partners, including Bravida, which could have triggered further credit write-downs. Consequently, the actual cash recovery rate on bankrupt developer receivables might be higher than historical write-downs indicate.

Fabri AG Consolidation and EPS Impact

1.
Favourable consolidation: Instalco completed its majority stake acquisition in German installation group Fabri AG on 16 July 2026, increasing its ownership from 24 percent to 51 percent. Under the agreement, Fabri's operations will be consolidated from 1 August 2026 with a one-month reporting lag, which means the group is established in Germany with a highly profitable platform. This strategic expansion is expected to lift future consolidated sales because Fabri reported net sales of EUR 86 million and a strong EBITA margin of 12.7 percent for the full year of 2025.
2.
Diluted contribution: Although Fabri delivers superior margins, DNB Carnegie reported on 17 July 2026 that the positive mix effect is partially offset by updated minority assumptions. Because Instalco consolidates 100 percent of Fabri's operations but owns only 51 percent, the remaining 49 percent minority interest will require material net income deductions on the profit and loss statement. Consequently, these adjustments mean the net EPS accretion is less pronounced than the headline EBITA growth would suggest.
3.
Option structures: The investment in Fabri is structured in multi-phase option-based steps running through 2033 to mitigate international operational risks. Instalco paid EUR 35.7 million for the step-two transaction, financed through its existing credit facilities, and maintains options for step three and step four. This structure allows management to protect cash flows from underperformance because future buyout options are conditional upon agreed EBITA thresholds.

This organizational change aims to foster collaboration between the regional subsidiaries, although the Finnish market remained weak as of the second quarter of 2026.
