Executive Summary

1.
Overall answer: the Key Question can be answered partly, not fully. The report's confidence mapping rates the convertible bond (Section 2) and NeoOrtho/financing inputs (Section 5) as High, currency (Section 3) and tax/capitalisation (Section 4) as Medium, and the broker bridge and scenarios (Section 6) as Low. The contractual terms are known. The below-EBIT outcomes that turn those terms into EPS depend on currency, tax and broker assumptions that are only partly disclosed. Convertible bond. The terms are well documented. The coupon is 3.0%, paid semi-annually, and the bond matures on 11 April 2031. The initial conversion price is CHF 100.725, a 27.5% premium to the concurrent share placement. Medartis can redeem at par from 2 May 2029 if the volume-weighted average price stays at or above CHF 130.94 for 20 of 30 trading days. Full conversion would create 1,149,665 shares, about 8.43% dilution, and conditional capital for bonds of 5,284,786 shares is ample. 2025 interest comprised a CHF 3.5m cash coupon and CHF 3.3m non-cash accretion, so conversion would remove a combined CHF 6.8m cost. The bond was excluded from diluted EPS as antidilutive, so reported diluted figures do not yet reflect it. Currency sensitivities. Medartis is largely unhedged. It has a Swiss-franc cost base, global revenues without formal hedging, and main exposures in the US dollar, Australian dollar and euro. A 10% USD/CHF move changes pre-tax profit by about CHF 1.9m, a 10% AUD/CHF move by about CHF 1.3m, and a 10% EUR/CHF move by only CHF 0.4m. The larger swing comes from the balance sheet. Intercompany loans exceed CHF 200m, and their revaluation produced CHF 5.2m of FX losses in 2025, mostly unrealised. This reversed into CHF 7.0m of gains in H1 2026, driven by the Brazilian real and Australian dollar.

Bank FX forecasts. The forecasts point to a modest drift, not a decisive tailwind. Morgan Stanley sees USD/CHF at 0.82 through 2027, rising to 0.87 by Q4 2028. Because the loan terms are undisclosed, these paths cannot be translated precisely into reported FX results. Tax rate. The normal rate is guided but not proven. The 2025 effective rate was 27%, against negative 41% in 2024, driven by CHF 5.3m of current tax and transfer-pricing adjustments. Management expects 18%–19% and regards sell-side rates above 19% as too conservative. Two points temper this. No advance pricing agreements were in place as of late 2025, and disclosure on the specific transfer-pricing adjustments is limited. / CSR reports Development cost capitalisation. The cost is small today but growing. Capitalised development rose to CHF 4.32m in 2025 from CHF 2.03m in 2024, while amortisation was only CHF 0.82m and the net book value reached CHF 12.46m. This points to higher future amortisation, but Medartis does not publish the future amortisation schedule. Medacta, by contrast, expenses comparable items immediately, and Straumann amortises straightline over no more than three years.

, financing and share count. NeoOrtho is a defined but not fully quantified drag. Medartis owns 51%, and it is contracted to buy the remaining 49% in FY2029 based on FY2028 sales. The recognised liability was CHF 16.4m at end-2025, with an undiscounted payment of up to CHF 24.8m.

This is somewhat above the roughly CHF 15.4m cited in the Key Question. Changes in the liability's fair value go through retained earnings rather than the income statement. On liquidity, cash fell from CHF 138.7m to CHF 33.0m. CHF 12m of the CHF 40m committed facilities was drawn, and management secured an additional CHF 50m of headroom.

Covenants are not disclosed. Shares outstanding rose from 13,632,277 to 13,646,113 by June 2026 through employee issuance.

Peer comparisons. Peers show different choices, with Medartis at the more exposed end. Sonova has issued no convertible bonds and hedges transaction risk with short-dated forwards. Straumann is shifting manufacturing to Andover and Shanghai to lower its CHF cost exposure and carries negative net debt of CHF 173m. Medacta is also unhedged but runs higher operating margins. Ypsomed shows Swiss peers also face unrealised intercompany-loan translation losses (CHF 7.8m).

Broker bridge and scenarios. This is the weakest link. Consensus FY2027 EPS of CHF 2.50 demands a sharp improvement in margins and financial results, and the underlying currency and tax assumptions are not fully disclosed. The history shows how fragile such bridges are. FY2025 consensus EPS fell from above CHF 1 to CHF 0.18, driven by CHF 5.3m of FX losses and CHF 5.2m of core tax expense. The report's illustrative scenarios place FY2027 EPS between CHF 0.41 and CHF 1.57, all below the consensus figure. FX and tax are the widest swing factors.

Interpretation and limits. The evidence supports a clear view of the bond's mechanics and a bounded view of NeoOrtho. The FX and tax channels are the largest and least predictable below-EBIT items. Organic growth of 17% in H1 2026, the planned Titan Nail transition away from the third-party NX nail and sell-side models that assume flat currency could shift outcomes either way. The main gaps are undisclosed intercompany loan rates and maturities, the NeoOrtho payment timing, non-public broker models and the absence of a hedging programme. Further work should focus on the FY2026 and FY2028 audited disclosures, tax reconciliations and the intangibleasset note. It should also cover any treasury changes, such as restructuring intercompany debt or adopting selective hedging, to move the Key Question from partly answered toward fully answered.

Section 2 – Convertible Bond Terms and Dilution Impact

Section summary
  1. Medartis's convertible bond terms protect the company via call options but threaten substantial dilution if shares exceed the conversion price.

Bond terms and option provisions

1.
Favorable pricing: Medartis announced in its annual report published on 17 March 2025 that it successfully placed a senior unsecured convertible bond totaling CHF 115.8 million. The company set the annual cash interest coupon at a rate of 3.0% paid semi-annually. This long-term debt matures on 11 April 2031, which shields the firm from near-term principal refinancing risks while keeping its funding costs predictable.
2.
High premium: Medartis confirmed in its 17 March 2025 annual report that the initial conversion price was set at CHF 100.725 per share. This conversion price represents a 27.5% premium over the company's concurrent share placement price. This structure protects current investors from immediate dilution because the conversion price sits well above current trading levels.
3.
Flexible redemption: Medartis specified in its annual report on 17 March 2025 that it can redeem all outstanding bonds at par on or after 2 May 2029. This call option triggers if the volume-weighted average share price remains at or above CHF 130.94 for at least 20 out of 30 consecutive trading days. Management can use this provision to force conversion into equity if the stock performs exceptionally well.
4.
Strategic funding: Medartis stated in its annual report dated 17 March 2025 that the bond proceeds were intended to fund general corporate purposes and expansion. The long-term nature of this instrument matches the extended developmental cycle of its titanium medical implants. Securing this capital allowed the firm to accelerate its product launches across key regions.
5.
Maturity structure: Medartis confirmed in its annual report on 17 March 2025 that unless converted or repurchased early, the bonds are redeemable at par in 2031. This gives the company a seven-year runway from the initial issuance in April 2024. This long term ensures that the business has ample time to generate cash from its investments.

Financial expenses and accounting treatment

6.
Split accounting: Medartis reported in its 17 March 2025 annual report that the convertible bond is divided into liability and equity components. The company recognized the liability component at an initial amortized cost of CHF 92.97 million. This split-accounting treatment means the firm must record non-cash accretion charges, which materially reduces reported net profit before conversion.
7.
Heavy interest: Medartis faced annual interest costs in 2025 composed of a CHF 3.5 million cash coupon and CHF 3.3 million of non-cash accretion. The non-cash accretion reflects the amortized cost adjustment required to pull the liability component back to par over its term. Eliminating this combined CHF 6.8 million expense upon conversion would directly boost future earnings per share.
8.
Contrary evidence: Medartis stated in its annual report published on 17 March 2026 that its convertible bonds were completely antidilutive for the fiscal year. The company excluded these potential shares from its diluted earnings per share calculations as their inclusion would increase EPS. This means that current reported diluted EPS figures completely ignore the potential dilution of the bond.
9.
Accretion mechanics: Medartis explained in its annual report dated 17 March 2025 that the effective interest rate method determines the non-cash accretion expense. This method ensures that the carrying value of the liability increases gradually toward its face value of CHF 115.8 million. This accounting treatment guarantees that financing costs will remain high unless conversion occurs early.
10.
Amortized expenses: Medartis noted in its annual report on 17 March 2025 that bond transaction costs were capitalized and offset against the bond liability. These capitalized costs are amortized over the seven-year term of the bond, increasing the annual interest expense. This amortization further weighs on net profit margins until the instrument is retired.

Dilution modeling and capital structure

11.
Substantial dilution: Medartis would create 1,149,665 registered shares if the bondholders execute their conversion rights in full. This would lead to a dilution of 8.43% based on the 13,632,277 ordinary shares outstanding as of 31 December 2025. Such dilution would permanently dilutive to earnings per share unless operating income expands rapidly.
12.
Adequate headroom: Medartis reported in its annual report on 17 March 2026 that it maintains sufficient conditional capital to cover bond conversions. The company's conditional capital for bonds stands at 5,284,786 shares, which represents up to 38.77% of the total outstanding share capital. This large reserve guarantees that the company does not face issuance constraints from its capital band.
13.
Alternative diluters: Medartis noted in its 20 March 2026 annual report that it maintains conditional capital of 484,476 shares for employee benefit plans. The company issued 36,737 registered shares from this conditional capital pool in 2025 to satisfy employee compensation. Investors must monitor these combined pools because they expand the total share count over time.
14.
Dilution comparison: Medartis's potential dilution of 8.43% from the convertible bond is relatively high for a mid- cap medical technology firm. This dilution level exceeds the typical dilution from annual employee share programs. Investors must account for this dilution when calculating long-term equity value and price targets.
15.
Capital band: Medartis stated in its annual report published on 17 March 2026 that the board is authorized to adjust share capital within a capital band. This band ranges from 12,432,612 shares to 17,859,474 shares and is authorized until 20 April 2028. This mechanism provides the board with significant financial flexibility to manage capital.

Peer comparisons and restructuring precedents

16.
Peer differences: Sonova Holding AG reported in its annual report published on 18 May 2026 that it has not issued any convertible bonds. Sonova maintains conditional capital of 3,301,120 registered shares to cover potential options and conversion rights granted for debt instruments. This lack of active convertible debt means Sonova avoids complex non-cash interest expenses that weigh on Medartis.
17.
Valuation comparisons: Straumann Holding AG was noted in a Jefferies broker report on 19 February 2026 as trading at 36 times forecast earnings. Unlike Medartis, Straumann relies on high valuation and strong execution to fund growth rather than issuing dilutive convertibles. This strategy prevents the dilutive overhang and interest burden that currently characterizes Medartis's financial profile.
18.
Comparable precedent: Idorsia Ltd illustrated the risks of convertible debt in its annual report published on 20 March 2025, describing a major restructuring. In May 2024, Idorsia's bondholders voted to slash the conversion price from CHF 33.95 to CHF 6 and extend maturity. This extreme restructuring highlights how volatile share prices can disrupt standard convertible debt structures.
19.
Sonova capital: Sonova Holding AG stated in its annual report on 18 May 2026 that its conditional share capital represents up to 8.92% of existing share capital. Sonova's conditional capital can increase by a maximum of CHF 266,106.65 through the issuance of 5,322,133 registered shares. This conservative capital structure relies on equity rather than potentially dilutive convertible debt instruments.
20.
Other side of the story: Bondholders are highly unlikely to convert their debt before the 2031 maturity unless Medartis's share price rises dramatically above CHF 100.725. This keeps the capital structure stable and prevents immediate share dilution for existing shareholders. However, this delayed conversion forces Medartis to continue paying high annual interest expenses, which limits short-term EPS growth under its current leverage.

Section 3 – Currency Composition and Foreign Exchange Sensitivities

Section summary
  1. Medartis remains highly exposed to foreign exchange fluctuations because it operates without currency hedging for its main exposures.
  2. Large intercompany loans exceeding CHF 200 million amplify profit volatility through significant unrealized gains and losses.
  3. Contrast analysis with Swiss peers reveals that Medartis's unhedged risk profile represents an outlier in corporate risk management.

Currency composition and sensitivities

1.
Unhedged exposure: Medartis operates with a Swiss-franc cost base and generates revenues globally in several currencies without formal hedging. Management stated in its 17 March 2025 annual report that the main exposures are the US dollar, Australian dollar, and euro. This unhedged posture means that fluctuations in key currency pairs will directly impact reported pre-tax profit and equity.
2.
Quantifiable volatility: Medartis confirmed in its 17 March 2025 annual report that a 10% movement in the USD/CHF exchange rate affects pre-tax profit by CHF 1.9 million. This same USD/CHF movement impacts the group's equity by CHF 2.0 million. These sensitivity analyses demonstrate how vulnerable the company's bottom line is to currency shifts.
3.
Australian exposure: Medartis reported in its annual report on 17 March 2025 that a 10% change in the AUD/CHF rate impacts pre-tax profit by CHF 1.3 million. This 10% AUD/CHF movement also impacts consolidated equity by CHF 2.3 million. These levels are close to the prior year when a 10% move impacted profit by CHF 1.3 million and equity by CHF 2.4 million.
4.
Euro impact: Medartis reported in its 17 March 2025 annual report that EUR/CHF sensitivities are much lower than other major currencies. A 10% shift in the EUR/CHF exchange rate only impacts pre-tax profit by CHF 0.4 million. This demonstrates that the company's European operations are less sensitive to transaction risks relative to its overseas businesses.

Intercompany loans and FX results

5.
Massive funding: Medartis utilizes a large intercompany financing structure exceeding CHF 200 million to support its global subsidiaries. The Chief Financial Officer explained on 12 March 2024 that these loans finance accounts receivables and inventory sets. Because these loans are denominated in foreign currencies, they create substantial balance sheet risk during periods of Swiss franc strength.

Section 4 – Effective Tax Rates and Development Cost Capitalization

Section summary
  1. Medartis's tax rate remains volatile due to transfer-pricing adjustments, while rising development cost capitalization shields short-term margins at the expense of future amortization.

Effective tax rates and transfer pricing

1.
Substantial volatility: Medartis recorded an effective tax rate of 27% under IFRS in fiscal year 2025, which represents a massive swing from the negative 41% rate reported in 2024. This elevated tax rate was driven by the recognition of CHF 5.3 million of current tax expenses alongside transfer-pricing adjustments on intercompany profits. This volatility directly eroded bottom-line profitability and contributed to the decline in core net profit to CHF 6.1 million.
2.
Heavy burden: Medartis recorded core income tax expenses of CHF 5.2 million in 2025 compared to an exceptional tax credit of CHF 1.0 million in 2024. The loss of this tax credit represents a major headwind for the company's core earnings performance. This sudden shift underscores how sensitive Medartis's net profit remains to the expiration of one-time tax benefits in Switzerland.
3.
Favorable outlook: Medartis management expects the long-term underlying tax rate to normalize between 18% and 19% over the next several years. Chief Financial Officer Peter Hackel noted that sell-side models projecting tax rates above 19% are likely too conservative relative to internal plans. This guidance suggests that the corporate tax burden will act as a smaller drag on future diluted earnings.
4.
153 154 - Earnings Calls Structured allocation: Medartis implements a global transfer-pricing model that allocates profits across subsidiaries based on arm's length value creation. The company has not entered into any advance pricing agreements with tax authorities as of late 2025. This open posture means that future transfer-pricing audits remain an unhedged risk for core earnings per share.
5.
156 157 / CSR reports Prudent management: Medartis noted that its corporate tax affairs are overseen by global finance teams and external Big Four advisory firms. This structured oversight is designed to ensure complete compliance with the OECD-G20 BEPS framework and international standards. However, compliance does not eliminate the risk that local jurisdictions may unilaterally adjust transfer pricing models, impacting earnings.
6.
159 160 / CSR reports Contrary evidence: Medartis admitted in its 2025 annual report that unrecognized tax losses and temporary differences totaling millions of francs continue to distort its tax calculations. These uncapitalized tax assets could prevent the company from sustaining its guided tax rate of 18% if international tax rules tighten.

Development cost capitalization and amortization

7.
Increasing investment: Medartis capitalized CHF 4.32 million in development costs during fiscal year 2025, representing a significant increase from the CHF 2.03 million capitalized in 2024. This higher capitalization rate reflects accelerated research and development efforts across the group's medical brand portfolio. Capitalizing these costs instead of expensing them helps insulate reported core operating income in the short term.
8.
Stable amortization: Medartis recorded an intangible development amortization charge of CHF 0.82 million in 2025, which was flat compared to the CHF 0.84 million recorded in 2024. The net book value of the group's capitalized development assets consequently rose to CHF 12.46 million at the end of 2025. This growing intangible asset balance will require higher future amortization charges, which will weigh on EPS.

This is a substantial increase from the cost basis of CHF 10.90 million recorded at the start of 2024. If these assets do not yield commercial success, they could trigger severe write-down charges that heavily drag down EPS.

This comparison shows that Medartis faces a higher structural tax headwind than some of its Swiss medtech peers.

Straumann also utilizes straight-line amortization for development costs over a period of expected sales not exceeding three years. Medacta's decision to expense these items immediately contrasts with Medartis's practice of capitalizing substantial development additions. This conservative accounting policy makes Medacta's operating income less vulnerable to future amortization spikes or impairment write-downs.
