Section 2 – NTI Sensitivity to Sterling, Euro, and US Dollar Policy Rates

Section summary
  1. LSEG exhibits lower direct policy rate sensitivity than peers because the majority of its clearing net treasury income is derived from structural fees.
  2. Clearstream is highly rate-geared whereas Euronext has structurally indexed its clearing operations to achieve complete euro rate neutrality.

1.

Morgan Stanley highlighted on January 26, 2022, that about two-thirds of LCH's net treasury income represents rate-insensitive cash handling charges, while only one-third is sensitive to the overnight to three-month curve. This structural layout means that sterling policy rate cuts do not automatically result in proportional treasury income declines.
3.
Cyclical contrast: LSEG's interest rate sensitivity remains highly defensive when compared directly with the high cyclical gearing of its primary peers. This indicates that Clearstream's treasury performance is highly dependent on Federal Reserve rate decisions in addition to ECB actions.

Consequently, even under aggressive rate-cutting cycles by the Federal Reserve or the Bank of England, LSEG's EPS remains insulated by this stable, asset-based fee baseline.

Section 3 – Collateral Mix and the LCH SA Minimum Cash Collateral Framework

Section summary

This shift occurred because clearing members aggressively optimized their portfolios by transitioning from cash to cheaper non-cash margin instruments. As a result, this collateral optimization served as a structural headwind that depressed LSEG's net treasury income by reducing high-margin cash balances held at LCH.

LSEG's non-cash collateral revenues increased by 4.9% on a constant currency basis in 2024 as members optimized their collateral mix. Therefore, the organic growth of non-cash handling fees remains insufficient to fully offset the contraction in cash-reinvestment treasury income.

LCH levies a fee of approximately 6bps to 7bps on non-cash collateral balances, which grew slower than clearing revenues due to netting offsets. Thus, a return to low-volatility market conditions typically incentivizes clearing members to substitute cash with non-cash assets, hurting earnings per share.

This regulatory change effectively establishes a systemic floor on cash liquidity across LSEG's European clearing operations.

This minimum is calculated each day as a specific percentage of the clearing member's exposure or overall margin requirement. By checking requests intraday, LCH SA prevents withdrawals that would breach the liquidity safety floor, protecting the CCP from sudden cash drains.
