Section 2 – Contract Pass-Through and Margin Protection

Section summary
  1. Kerry Group's index-linked, back-to-back contracts protect margins by passing raw material changes through rapidly, in contrast to Givaudan's slower-adjusting formula contracts.
  2. Peer structures vary from Corbion's automated pass-through on lactic acid to AAK's risk-hedged specialty fats with six to nine month contractual lags.

Kerry Contract Structures

1.
Rapid pricing: Kerry Group utilizes highly automated pricing contracts that allow the company to protect its profitability during volatile commodity cycles. According to Deutsche Bank, over 70% of Kerry's products are sold with an automatic pass-through pricing mechanism that features a very short lag. This contractual setup ensures that raw material cost fluctuations are transferred directly to customers to protect absolute profitability.
2.
Flexible pricing: Kerry Group passes through both raw material inflation and deflation to pricing far more quickly than its competitors. Analysts at Bernstein report that these contract structures allow the company to pass on input cost inflation in real time, preserving margins through inflationary periods. This characteristic keeps EBITDA margins highly stable but contributes to increased top-line volatility during deflationary commodity cycles.
3.
Automated adjustments: Former Kerry finance directors confirm that contractual mechanisms are highly integrated with raw material commodity price structures. The expert reports that direct pass-through is automatic for commodities like dairy, sugar, and starches, while other contracts undergo structured price adjustments. These volatile operating conditions mean that contracts have become shorter in duration to incorporate automatic raw material pass-through clauses.
4.
Deflationary impact: Kerry Group's pricing record closely mirrors the deflationary raw material trend that characterized the market from 2024 through early 2026. Company filings show that net pricing was -0.3% in FY2025, -1.3% in Q1 2026, and -0.7% in Q2 2026, reflecting the deflationary input cost environment. However, pricing returned to positive territory at +0.2% in Q3 2026 as input cost deflation began turning to inflation.
5.
Contrary evidence: Former Kerry commercial directors reveal that commercial pricing adjustments are not always completed in real time. The expert notes that raw-material cost declines took at least two months to affect actual customer pricing. This delay suggests that pricing transitions are not instantaneous and can create temporary margin mismatches during rapid deflationary periods.
6.
Risk mitigation: Kerry Group manages raw material volatility through a comprehensive combination of operational and financial hedging strategies. According to Kerry's bond prospectus, the group utilizes back-to-back arrangements with customers alongside forward purchasing contracts. The group's policy enforces the targeted use of raw material hedges to mitigate residual risks on commodity exposures.

Analysts at Deutsche Bank report that Tate & Lyle has retained two-thirds of its top thirty customers over the last five years. 4.

Bernstein Research notes that transitioning a formulation to a new ingredient supplier typically takes 12 to 18 months to ensure compliance. This timeline is driven by rigorous regulatory, quality, and audit requirements that food manufacturers must satisfy. Consequently, these extended timelines serve as formidable structural barriers to switching for any established brand.
5.
Rigorous testing: Food manufacturers prioritize supply reliability and quality consistency over cost when evaluating potential ingredient partners. According to expert interviews, multinational food manufacturers require at least two years of auditing and testing to qualify new suppliers for specialty lipids. These rigorous qualification cycles protect incumbent suppliers like AAK because customers avoid costly operational disruptions unless price differentials are extreme. Thus, high technical requirements effectively insulate margins from spot price competition.
6.
Contrary evidence: Some segment contracts allow customers to shift volumes with minimal notice, exposing suppliers to sudden competitive losses. Kerry Group's own prospectus admits that the company tends not to enter into long-term contracts, enabling customers to switch to alternative suppliers quickly. This lack of contractual lock-in means Kerry must constantly innovate to defend its accounts. Therefore, operational barriers may be less protective when commodity alternatives are readily available.
