  2. Strategic nuclear power purchase agreements, such as the landmark 22-year Google contract, are de-risking the Nordic clean generation fleet and reinforcing EPS predictability.

Corporate restructuring: Fortum announced on November 26, 2026, a sharpened organizational structure that will renew its leadership team starting February 1, 2027. Executive Vice President Simon-Erik Ollus has decided to leave the group after an agreed transition period. This governance shift will group corporate customers and markets under Kati Levoranta to improve execution.

These key management transitions were initiated to support the company's evolving Nordic growth strategy and operational execution.
3.

The transaction involved an aggregate consideration of approximately EUR 482 million, equal to NOK 47 per share. Consequently, Elmera was delisted from Euronext Oslo Børs on November 6, 2026, and is consolidated into Consumer Solutions.

These commercial and operational synergies are projected to be realized gradually starting in 2028 and fully from 2030 onwards. The initial cash payment was approximately EUR 40 million, with potential earn-outs bringing the total estimated purchase price to EUR 61 million. Any future investments in these projects will depend directly on demand from industrial customers and prevailing market conditions.

This updated target compares to the 10.9% comparable return on net assets recorded for the last twelve months ending September 30, 2026. Management believes this level is achievable with the existing portfolio and planned efficiency measures.
7.
Capital discipline: Fortum updated its leverage framework by setting a maximum Net Debt to Comparable EBITDA ratio ceiling of 2.5 times. This credit metric replaced the previous financial net debt ceiling of 2.0 to 2.5 times in the first quarter of 2026. Section 4 – Financial delivery

Section summary
  1. Fortum delivered a solid top-line performance in Q3 2026 with actual group revenues beating broker consensus expectations by 17.26%.
  2. Group comparable operating profit missed consensus expectations slightly by 2.86% due to retail margin compression and elevated administrative expenses.
  3. Financial leverage remains extremely low and conservative with net debt-to-comparable EBITDA of 1.4 times, well below the group's ceiling of 2.5 times.
  4. Medium-term electricity price hedging for 2028 was initiated at EUR 42 per megawatt-hour, falling below sell-side expectations and signaling potential pressure on future EPS.
  5. Fortum's management is prioritizing long-term industrial partnerships, nuclear lifetime extensions, and retail expansion to de-risk its Nordic generation portfolio.
  6. The landmark 22-year Google power purchase agreement provides economic certainty for the Loviisa plant and is expected to lift return on net assets.
  7. The Elmera acquisition consolidates Fortum's retail footprint and is projected to deliver immediate scale benefits and operational synergies.
  8. Capital discipline remains central to the group's strategy, with growth investments guided at EUR 750 million through 2030 to preserve a strong credit profile.

Heightened risk: Fortum is exposed to labor constraints and scheduling delays during specialized maintenance campaigns due to tight contractor availability across the Nordic nuclear fleet. South African engineering firm Lesedi Nuclear Services provides specialized outage services at Fortum's Loviisa plant to mitigate local workforce limitations. Extended maintenance outages at co-owned plants like Oskarshamn 3 and Olkiluoto 3 limit nuclear generation volumes and raise operating expenses.
6.
Other side of the story: Fortum's nuclear supply chain and hydrology risks could be quickly offset by a sudden return to wet hydrology and favorable fuel market conditions. A rapid normalization of precipitation would instantly restore Nordic reservoir levels and maximize flexible hydro generation earnings. Supportive regional water conditions would also alleviate power grid bottlenecks and reduce the need for expensive fuelcycle mitigation measures.

Verdict on the EPS trajectory. Over the next 18 months to 3 years, we judge Fortum's EPS trajectory to be directionally constructive but back-end loaded and uneven. Visible catalysts sit in 2028–2030: the 22-year Google nuclear PPA starts in 2028 and covers 50% of Loviisa's output, Elmera synergies are realised gradually from 2028, and management targets a EUR 330 million comparable operating profit improvement by 2030. The nearer period looks more exposed to outer-year estimate pressure than to upgrades. Morgan Stanley estimated that the 2028 hedge disclosure could trigger a 5% to 10% downgrade to consensus EPS.

Q3 2026 was a mix, not a clean beat. The Generation segment was the engine: comparable operating profit of EUR 121 mm beat the broader consensus of EUR 108 mm. The group still fell 2.86% below the EUR 105 mm consensus on comparable operating profit. Consumer Solutions came in 30% short of its EUR 20 mm consensus, and Other Operations posted a larger loss than the EUR 24 mm expected. Group EPS quality therefore depends on a single segment offsetting two weaker ones.

7.
Consensus anomaly 1: the same hedge disclosure was read in opposite ways. Fortum disclosed 2028 cover of 45% at EUR 42 per MWh. Morgan Stanley argued this mechanically reduces realised prices relative to prior projections. Deutsche Bank viewed it as comparable to its own model, which assumed 43% coverage at EUR 43.70. DNB Carnegie had estimated EUR 44 and believed the market expected more. This dispersion suggests outer-year consensus is not yet anchored.
8.
Consensus anomaly 2: revisions run in opposite directions across time horizons. Morgan Stanley raised its 2026 EPS estimate from EUR 1.06 to EUR 1.13 but trimmed 2028 from EUR 1.20 to EUR 1.16 on lower hedge pricing. DNB Carnegie's near-term upgrade reflected the higher near-term hedge price and the inclusion of Elmera's retail earnings. Part of the near-term lift is therefore acquired rather than organic. Before the release, Deutsche Bank had cut its full-year EPS estimate from EUR 0.99 to EUR 0.92, placing it ten percent below the Bloomberg consensus. Dispersion between brokers on the near-term base is wide.
9.
Consensus anomaly 3: nuclear volumes rose but guidance was not lifted. Q3 nuclear volumes grew to 5.6 TWh, up 21.74% from 4.6 TWh. Even so, full-year nuclear guidance was set at about 23 TWh, the lower bound of the prior 23 to 23.5 TWh range, because nine-month volumes lagged historical averages after extended outages at co-owned facilities. A strong quarter does not yet signal a durable fleet recovery.
10.
Consensus anomaly 4: the share price ran ahead of contract evidence. Absolute share price returns were 19.70% over three months and 53.65% over twelve months. Bernstein argued the shares had re-rated excessively without material progress on new PPAs. Morgan Stanley warned the share price already implies an aggressive long-term power price. The market is capitalising future contracts that have not yet been signed. The EUR 330 million plan is more volume than price. The improvement is self-led, assumes no power price increases, and relies on Generation for EUR 260 million. Bernstein calculated that two-thirds of the target rests on simple volume normalisation. This is encouraging because it limits price dependence. However, nuclear availability fell to 75% in 2025 against a 90% objective that management expects only gradually by 2030. Delivery therefore hinges on execution at jointly owned plants, including Oskarshamn 3, which the sell-side links to constrained Fortum generation volumes. The Google PPA is a real but bounded EPS lever. Morgan Stanley calculated a premium of about EUR 30 per MWh. Management expects a return on net assets uplift of about 1.4 percentage points over time. Deutsche Bank noted it locks in higher prices for almost 10% of Fortum's output, so group EPS sensitivity is meaningful but limited. Replicability is the open point: subsequent deals may not match the premium, and Fortum did not participate in Microsoft's PPA tender, which raises doubts about signing lucrative PPAs for existing plants.

Elmera is modestly accretive but sits on a weak retail base. Morgan Stanley projected a 5% lift to Group EBITDA and a 2% to 3% lift to EPS. Management expects about EUR 30 million of annual synergies, phased in from 2028 and fully from 2030. The acquired platform joins a segment where comparable operating profit fell 39.13% to EUR 14 mm and where the customer base shrank from 2.36 million to 2.3 million. Bernstein warned that integration expenses could weigh heavily on margins. The EPS contribution is likely positive but slow to prove out.

Cost drift and leadership change add execution noise. Other Operations' loss widened 83.33% to EUR 33 mm, with one-off restructuring and higher administrative costs cited. The leadership team will be renewed from February 1, 2027, and the report flags substantial transition risk during a period of volatile commodity prices. We treat this as a modest negative for the 2027 EPS base rather than a thesis-breaker.

Hydrology and demand realisation drive the upside and downside. Nordic hydro reservoirs were thirteen terawatthours below normal, which restricted Fortum's ability to capture high merchant prices. Morgan Stanley cautioned that higher price expectations are vulnerable to rising reservoir levels and lower gas prices, which would compress unhedged margins. On the demand side, Fortum itself describes near-term industrial demand realisation as highly gradual, and green industrial electrification investments have been delayed by a weak macroeconomic environment.

The strongest bear case is a convergence of three exposures. First, the hedge book locks in legacy pricing: the 2028 weakness is a function of timing and a legacy drag on outer-year EPS. Second, commodity curves could fall as hydrology normalises, exposing unhedged volumes when the share price already embeds an aggressive longterm power price. Third, the offsets are slow or unproven: retail margins are compressed, Elmera synergies arrive from 2028, and two-thirds of the profit plan depends on fleet availability.

A fall in curves, further nuclear JV outages and a stalled second PPA would leave EPS growth dependent on volume recovery alone. Policy and regulation can reshape the bear case. Finland imposed a windfall tax on hydro and nuclear in 2013 before repealing it in 2015, and Bernstein argues the state, as majority owner, is incentivised to favour local consumers. Higher Swedish property tax costs about EUR 30 million annually through 2030.

For demand, the Finnish supervisor ordered Google to halt construction at two data center sites, although management assured analysts the PPA is unaffected. - Earnings Calls Flexibility economics are shifting under Fortum's optimisation premium. Finnish battery additions pushed reserve prices from EUR 10 to 30 per MWh to far below EUR 10. Fortum's EUR 8 to 10 per MWh optimisation premium guidance was unchanged, but uncontrolled battery additions could suppress the scarcity pricing and volatility that historically enhanced integrated portfolios.

On the other side, the NuCore state aid application for SMRs offers a long-dated option that does not affect the 3-year EPS window. The balance sheet is a support, not a constraint. Net debt to comparable EBITDA of 1.4 times sits comfortably below the 2.5 times ceiling, although net debt exceeded the broker consensus by 6.53% and Morgan Stanley sees Elmera lifting net debt to about EUR 2.3 billion.
