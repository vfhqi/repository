Sector Note N.2: Real Estate – Debt and Refinancing Schedules for European Commercial Landlords (Q3 2026 Analysis)

Sector Refinancing Dynamics: Winners and Losers

The transition of commercial landlords through the current interest rate environment is highly bifurcated, with individual capital structures, hedging ratios, and debt maturity profiles determining whether interest bills will rise or fall over the next two years. Across the European real estate sector, landlords are managing the gradual feed-through of higher interest rates depending on their in-place cost of debt, hedging ratios, and debt durations.

The "Losing Cheap Hedges" Group: Landlords like Aroundtown, Atrium Ljungberg, Fastighets Balder, Fabege, Wihlborgs Fastigheter, and FastPartner are set to suffer over the next two years. They are rolling off exceptionally cheap legacy hedges (often fixed at 1% to 2.5%) and refinancing into a higher-rate modern debt environment. This creates a direct headwind that drives up interest bills and compresses FFO or CEPS.

The "Replacing Expensive Debt" / Insulated Group: Landlords like CA Immobilien Anlagen, Diös Fastigheter, and NP3 Fastigheter present a more resilient or positive outlook. CA Immo has pre-funded near-term bond maturities at reasonable rates, while NP3 Fastigheter and Castellum present mixed structures where they simultaneously benefit from replacing highly expensive, high-coupon legacy/floating debt while managing rolling hedges.
