APM Mini-Memo: Bufab

Written by the Assistant Portfolio Manager, 9 October 2026. Ideas Bench rank 9 in the lists drawn from the Pool of Stocks Eligible for Middle Innings Portfolio Selection. Evidence: the Investment Analyst's Analysis and Judgement memo dated 24 September 2026 and published 1 October, the ratings store and weekly readings of 9 October 2026, the research digest and the earnings review of 10 August 2026.

The one-line view. Neutral with medium conviction: a genuinely good business whose corporate change has already travelled the whole way into the share price, so the earnings case is sound and the return is not.

What the company does

Bufab is a Swedish-listed supply chain partner that sources and manages C-parts, mostly fasteners and similar low-value components, for industrial customers. It serves 20,000 customers from about 65 sister companies in 31 countries. Net sales in 2025 were 8,072 million Swedish kronor at an adjusted margin on earnings before interest, tax and amortisation of 13.3 percent, rising to 15.0 percent in the first half of 2026 on sales up 6.3 percent.

The product is cost removal, not parts. The component is 15 to 20 percent of a customer's fastening cost and the rest is hidden administration, so Bufab sells the removal of the other 85 percent and embeds itself through vendor-managed inventory that takes twelve to eighteen months to migrate.

A plausible investment thesis

Bullish on the business, and the mechanism is fully visible. A new chief executive from August 2022 repriced on value delivered rather than cost, raised the margin target from 12 to 14 percent in December 2023 and sold the capital-hungry manufacturing units in 2024 and 2025. Gross margin reached 33.2 percent in the second quarter of 2026 against 29.3 percent at the end of 2023, and drops through a flexible cost base to an adjusted margin of 15.0 percent. The Investment Analyst's modal path takes reported earnings per share from 3.30 kronor in 2025 to about 5.30 kronor in 2028, carried by the first year and settling at 10 to 12 percent a year.

Four things to track: gross margin above about 32.5 percent; organic growth above about 4 percent into 2027, against 5.3 percent in the second quarter; the acquisition pace, modelled at 3 to 4 percent of sales a year against the company's 6 to 8 percent ambition; and cash conversion, 58 then 90 percent in the first two quarters of 2026. The third-quarter report lands on 23 October.

The Target Setup

The Target Setup is 'Big Corporate Change-driven EPSU', with 'Delivering - Serial Acquirer - Mechanical EPS Upgrader' combining behind it. The Investment Analyst's memo of 24 September rated the major corporate change pattern B and the serial-acquirer pattern C, the other four D or F, and rated Setups' Fit/Crispness B. The match is crisp and the entry is late: the pattern pays at input and early output, and this case is at full output, with consensus earnings per share for 2028 up 23.9 percent and the shares up 53.0 percent.

Case Riskiness and simplicity

The Investment Analyst's memo of 24 September, re-formed by Watson on 1 October, rated Case Riskiness C, where A is simple and low-risk. Nine of the ten combination red flags are absent, so the binding risk is the multiple: on unchanged earnings the price is 107 kronor at the five-year median and 78.5 kronor at the ten-year conservative band. The one fundamental shock is a 938 million kronor unhedged US dollar mismatch, where a 30 percent move removes over 26 percent of 2025 operating profit. Simplicity holds in the mechanism and fails in the perimeter of 31 countries and quarterly indicators.

Business quality

High quality for its category, short of the premium Swedish compounders on capital. Twelve consecutive quarters of gross margin expansion, an adjusted margin of 15.0 percent against Bossard's 10.5 percent, an operating profit every year since 1977, and over 50 acquisitions at 6.0 to 7.5 times operating earnings. Against that, return on capital employed is 14.3 percent against Lagercrantz at 17.4 percent, working capital is 37.9 percent of sales and goodwill is over 70 percent of net assets. The digest treats that capital intensity as the price of the moat, since Bossard's ratio is near-identical. Foundations' Robustness is B, set by the Investment Analyst as at 24 September.

Plausible returns and valuation

This is where the case fails. TSR/Valuation reads F, set by the Investment Analyst on 9 October on the memo's modal return rebased to the price of 139.6 kronor: about 4.9 percent a year over three years and 1.7 percent a year over eighteen months, against the D anchor of about 12 percent a year. The legs are 1 percent of cash, 8 percent of earnings growth and minus 4.6 percent of de-rating. FactSet on 9 October has the shares at 24.7 times 24-month-forward earnings per share of 5.64 kronor, the 98th percentile of their own history against a five-year median near 19 times. Even holding the multiple gives only about 8.9 percent a year.

Value chain and thematics: the pros and the cons

The direction is a pro and the shape is a con. Customers want fewer suppliers and outsourced logistics, the carbon border adjustment mechanism and the sustainability reporting directive raise barriers that favour the compliant scaled distributor, and defence and semiconductor demand is strong. Against that, the top five distributors hold about 15 percent of the market and Bufab about 2 percent globally while leading only in Sweden, and over half of revenue is tied to European industrial production with purchasing managers' indices below 50. Thematic Fit/Momentum is B, read by the script on 9 October, where the memo had it pinned at C on 1 October.

The strongest counter-case

In its strongest form the counter-case is bullish, and the market is voting for it. On this reading Bufab has joined the Swedish serial acquirers: a decade of 17 percent compounding, three consecutive profit beats, and outer-year consensus revenue growth of 5.8 percent for 2028 that is mechanically too low for an acquirer. If the deal pace doubles, the Analyst's own earnings path moves to about 13 percent a year and the three-year return to about 18 to 22 percent a year. The momentum readings of 9 October agree with that view.
