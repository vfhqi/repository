Campari (CPR-IT) — what the third-quarter 2026 results do to the existing case

These results leave the Park call standing but move the case modestly in the stock's favour, and they do it by changing what the false-friend worry is about: the US demand decay the case feared is now visible, and the group grew through it anyway, on Europe and on lower-margin formats.

1. What changed

  • Group. Organic net sales grew 3.4% in the third quarter of 2026 (reported 28 October 2026), exactly in line with company-compiled consensus. Reported net sales were €777 million against €764 million expected. Nine-month organic growth is 2.9%. It is the sixth consecutive quarter of organic growth.
  • The regions swapped places. Europe grew 7.7% organically against 3.0% expected. North America fell 2.0% against an expected rise of 2.8%. Asia-Pacific and travel retail fell 1.3%.
  • The US guide was cut. Management now expects a marginal full-year topline decline in the US. Tequila and aperitifs grew there over nine months; the rest of the portfolio declined. Before the print, Jefferies reported US Nielsen data for summer 2026 showing Aperol sales down 4% while Bacardi's St-Germain grew 40%.
  • Aperitifs and formats. The House of Aperitifs grew 12.7% in the quarter and 6.7% over nine months. Convenience formats drove one-third of that nine-month growth. The finance chief said on the call that these formats carry a lower gross margin than bottles.
  • Guidance held. Circa 3% organic growth and organic accretion in the adjusted EBIT margin for 2026, with a 70 basis point SG&A benefit. The US tariff headwind is now €20 million, down from €30 million. A €15 million refund of 2025 tariffs was collected in the quarter, part reinvested in advertising, part offsetting second-half cost of goods. Morgan Stanley reads the net effect on the margin as neutral.
  • No margin, no leverage. Under the reporting policy adopted in 2026 the first and third quarters carry net sales and outlook only. Gross margin and leverage were not reported.
  • Sell-side. Bernstein cut adjusted EPS by 0.8% for 2026 and 1.5% for 2027 and trimmed its target to €10.50 from €10.80, Outperform kept. Morgan Stanley kept Overweight and €6.50, Deutsche Bank Buy and €7.30, Jefferies Hold and €6.70. The review carries no evidence on changes to Campari's strategic goals; the passage under that heading describes a different company and we have disregarded it.

The case assumed the opposite shape: US Aperol as the fulcrum, with agave and SG&A savings flattering margins while that demand slowed.

2. The challenge

Reading one: the case has the right disease in the wrong organ, and the fulcrum is no longer the US. The case's lead indicator is US Aperol depletion velocity. On the scanner evidence that indicator has already failed: Aperol down 4% against a copycat up 40%, and a US guide cut to decline. Yet the group met consensus and held guidance, because Europe delivered more than twice what was expected. In its strongest form this reading says Campari is now a European aperitif and new-format growth company for which the US is a drag to be managed, not the variable that decides the case. If so, the case's two reassessment triggers, both written around US re-acceleration and Aperol or Espolòn volume, are probably waiting for evidence that is unlikely to arrive and is no longer the evidence that matters. For this to be right, European growth has to be execution and innovation, as Deutsche Bank argues, and not peak-season weather or sell-in: Italy and the UK led while Germany was weak, UK Aperol ready-to-drink velocity ran at two to three times forecast, and the formats have to scale beyond pilot markets in 2027. The evidence against is real: Europe is only 3.8% over nine months, so 7.7% is one summer quarter, and Morgan Stanley itself flags unresolved trading disruption in France and Italy.

Reading two: a second false friend, the mirror image of the one the case holds. The case worries that cost tailwinds mask demand decay. These results admit the reverse: that volume from lower-margin formats masks margin decay. One-third of aperitif growth now comes from products management says are gross-margin dilutive. The margin-accretion guide is being held with help that does not repeat: a €15 million refund and a €10 million lower tariff bill. And the quarter in which the mix shifted is one in which no margin was disclosed. The case treated Campari as structurally behind in ready-to-drink and scored that as a headwind; it never considered that catching up could be the problem. For this reading to be right, the formats have to substitute for bottle occasions more than they add new ones, and the mix shift has to be large enough to outrun agave and SG&A relief. Nothing in the review measures either.

A third point presses on the recommendation, not the thesis. The shares stood at 6.82 on 13 November 2026 in the home-exchange quote, against the EUR 5.984 at which the case was written. That is 13.8% above the 200-day average, close to the 52-week high of 6.91, and above the Morgan Stanley and Jefferies targets the review carries. The market has been paying for relative resilience in a sector where NielsenIQ shows Campari's US value up 0.90% in the four weeks to 31 October 2026 while Diageo fell 6.10%. Parking has had a cost. Equally, the cheap-multiple support the case leaned on is thinner: the case's 15.6x at the 4th percentile, which it flagged as stale, now reads 18.2x at the 9th percentile. Still bottom-quartile, but re-rated without the capitulation the clearing-event setups require.

3. What it does to the case

  • E8.RA8, false-friend fit [F]: same weight, different content. The demand leg is confirmed in the US, which is more pressure. The case's own disconfirming condition is half-approached: Espolòn grew high-single digits and aperitifs 12.7%, but gross margin is unobserved. The source's re-pre-mortem trigger, US Aperol below a flat trend, is plausibly met on scanner sales, though scanner sales are not depletion velocity.
  • E1.RA1 [B] and E8.RA2's dominant-position question [C]: more pressure. The US under-penetration runway and "no major rivals" both sit badly beside St-Germain.
  • E1.RA2 [C]: mixed. Less pressure on headwinds (the case's EUR 30m tariff figure has moved to the smaller broker figure it noted) and on delivery congruency. More pressure on the low-multiple credit.
  • E11.RA4, political and regulatory [D]: less pressure, offset in part by a new Brazilian excise framework. E11.RA2's ready-to-drink entrant question [D]: less pressure. E11.RA1 [C]: the same, with the mix inverted.
  • E7 [D]: marginally less pressure on perimeter and change count, with disposals at about 4% of net sales pro forma and more due to close in the fourth quarter. E7.RA5, conservative investor relations [C]: more pressure. Guidance was held by swapping regions, and quarterly disclosure has narrowed.
  • MR2, sell-side earnings momentum [D]: less pressure. The pinned letter now reads C (snapshot of 14 November 2026), and this was one of the case's four stated Park reasons. MR1, technical momentum [C]: the pinned letter reads D, which we report as given.
  • Group 4 [D], MR4 [B], MR5 [D]: the same. The review says nothing on the governance overhang.

4. What would settle it

  • The second review, four working days on. It is likely to carry settled broker estimates, targets and rating changes, and so whether the trims stay as small as Bernstein's. It cannot carry margin, leverage or depletion evidence, because the company did not report any.
  • Monthly US Nielsen and NABCA data. Aperol against St-Germain, and the Casamigos-to-Espolòn price gap. This is the fastest test of reading one's premise and it arrives monthly.
  • The full-year 2026 results. Last year's came on 4 March. This is the first gross margin, EBIT margin and leverage print since the half-year, and the only near-term test of reading two.
  • The 2027 European summer. Morgan Stanley's own condition is that a European decline in the peak season would invalidate the constructive view. That is the durability test for reading one, and it is two to three quarters away.
  • What nothing committed will answer. The company has not committed to disclosing the margin of convenience formats or their cannibalisation of bottles, and the first quarter of 2027 is also sales-only. Reading two can only be inferred from group gross margin twice a year. That is a weaker kind of open question, and the case should be written knowing it.
