Group judgement

External change forces

Layer 1 · External change forces

Written last by the Investment Analyst for its group. The rating above stands for it, so it carries no number of its own.

The external picture is a strong, broad and contracted demand tailwind (three large operators, a structural data curve, a coverage gap measured in tens of millions of people) set against headwinds that do not reach EBITDA: the global discount rate, frontier sovereign stress (Senegal, eastern DRC, the DRC cash rule), currency and tax. The one change I would stress to the Consumer is in the composition of demand: the 2026 surge in high-return colocations has already normalised and is being replaced by capital-heavy new-site coverage builds, which keeps tenancy additions high into FY2027 while raising capex per tenancy and slowing the rise in the tenancy ratio. The visibility is good to end-2027 and poor beyond, where operator capex front-loading, active sharing and customer-aligned tower ventures all point to additions normalising around 2,000 to 2,500 a year.

C-02

To what extent, and in which direction, is competitive intensity changing?

D4247th percentile

Layer 1 · External change forces

Judgement. Competitive intensity is rising slowly, and its nature is changing: rivalry among independent tower companies is stable or easing, while competition from customer-aligned builders is rising. The latter does not touch the installed base (contracted, all-or-nothing, 1% churn cap) but competes for new-site building and will be used as bargaining leverage at the DRC renewals in 2028 to 2029.

Reasoning. Easing: American Tower is prioritising developed markets; IHS Towers is being taken over by MTN and has exited Latin America; SBA (Minara in Tanzania) builds selectively; incumbent independents build only on ten-year anchor contracts. Rising: Esengo Towers (Vodacom and Orange, up to 2,000 solar rural sites in the DRC over six years); Eastcastle growing towards 1,800 DRC sites; Indus Towers (Bharti, with Airtel as anchor tenant) building in Zambia and Uganda and planning Nigeria from Q3 of its fiscal 2027; MTN re-integrating IHS's towers (vertical re-integration of the passive layer by an operator). Helios's own markets are largely untouched so far: no independent rival in Senegal (bar Al Karama's 450 sites), Madagascar or Malawi; 60% of Tanzania's towers; 42% of all and 75% of independent towers in the DRC.

Why it matters. The marginal competitor is now the customer itself (or a vehicle it controls) rather than another tower company. That caps long-run pricing and BTS share more than it threatens volumes; the effect shows at renewals and in the share of new sites Helios wins, not in near-term revenue. The most important single watch item is whether Indus Towers follows Airtel (28% of Helios revenue) into a Helios market (Tanzania, DRC, Congo Brazzaville, Madagascar, Malawi), because that would turn a theoretical option into an alternative for Airtel's new builds.

C-05

How are input costs, input availability and supplier concentration changing, and is the net effect a headwind or a tailwind?

C+5072nd percentile

Layer 1 · External change forces

Judgement. Net neutral for EBITDA in dollars, a mild headwind for the reported margin percentage, and a mild tailwind for the economics of new and hybrid sites. The input that most distorts the forecasts is diesel, and its effect runs through revenue, not profit.

Reasoning. Power (about 200 USDm of a roughly 420 USDm cash cost base) is passed through by contract (about half of contracts escalate quarterly, half annually, with a 6 to 12 month lag between global fuel moves and local prices). High fuel inflated Q3 2026 revenue (+16.9% year on year, a 4.7% beat) while EBITDA rose 12.6% and the margin fell to 53.5% from 55.5% in Q1. Oil fell below 100 USD on 5 November 2026 from about 115 at end-October; if it stays lower, FY2027 reported revenue growth will be lower than tenancy growth implies and the margin percentage will rise, with little change to EBITDA. Construction cost per tower is stable at 100k to 150k USD; lithium battery prices fall 8% to 15% a year, improving hybrid-site returns; hybrid sites are 56% of the estate; procurement is spread across more than 1,000 suppliers with 81% of spend local; Standard Bank provided a 29 USDm sustainable trade facility (July 2026). For the model. Model revenue as tenancy revenue plus a separate power pass-through line at near-zero margin, so that the oil price changes revenue and margin percentage but not EBITDA. Do not read a fall in the margin percentage as deterioration.

To what extent is a macro force acting on the company, on its sector and on its industry, and at which level does it bite hardest?

C+4568th percentile

Layer 1 · External change forces

Judgement. The macro forces bite hardest at the company level, through the equity line and the multiple (frontier risk appetite, local currencies, sovereign credit), rather than at the sector level (bond yields), from which Helios has largely decoupled. Local macro is a tailwind to demand.

Reasoning. For the model. Macro does not change the EBITDA forecast materially (71% of EBITDA in hard or pegged currencies; a 10% adverse move in local currencies costs less than 14 USDm of EBITDA). C-01

To what extent is end-market demand changing, structurally or cyclically, and with how much visibility? (A cyclical swing counts when it has two-plus years of visibility.)

B+7687th percentile

Layer 1 · External change forces

Judgement. Demand is changing in two ways at once: a structural uplift that is intact and long, and a cyclical surge in operator capex that peaked in volume terms in H1 2026 but has unusually good visibility through FY2027. The cyclical swing qualifies as a two-year visible swing (2026 to 2027) because it is already in contracted tenancies and in management's stated FY2027 pipeline; beyond FY2027 visibility is poor because no customer has published capex plans past its FY2027.

Reasoning. Structural: wireless carries about 75% of African data against 15% in Europe; population coverage is 35% in the DRC and 45% in Tanzania; smartphone penetration rises from about 50% to over 80% by 2030; Airtel Africa data traffic grew 56% year on year; management sizes the organic runway at 72,000 further tenancies by 2040, about double today's base. Cyclical: Airtel Africa's cash capex was 389 in its June 2026 quarter against 121 a year earlier, with 1.1 USDbn guided for its year to March 2027, which implies about 220 a quarter in its second half, a third below its first half (Airtel itself calls this "frontloading"); the top four African operators raised capex guidance by about a third; Orange Middle East and Africa capex is about 1.6 EURbn; Vodacom targets 13.5% to 14.5% capex intensity and names Tanzania and the DRC. Helios additions went from 2,538 in FY2025 to about 4,000 in FY2026, and management set FY2027 at more than 3,000 on the strength of "solid volume visibility" from customer orders.

The insight the Consumer needs. The composition of demand is changing faster than its level. Colocations on existing towers (about 1,160, 827, 506 in Q1 to Q3) have already normalised; new-site coverage builds (about 246, 278, 418) are accelerating. Demand is moving from capacity (densifying existing sites, high return, little capex) to coverage (new rural and suburban sites, about 100k to 150k USD each, about 12% initial return). For the model this means: the volume of tenancies stays high into FY2027, but capex per tenancy and the incremental EBITDA margin per tenancy both move adversely, and the tenancy ratio rises more slowly than the IMPACT 2030 path assumes. Coverage builds are, however, the seed of future colocations (a second tenant typically arrives after about 2 to 2.5 years), so the 2026 to 2027 build cohort supports 2028 to 2030 colocation demand.

Implications for the model. Visible: FY2027 tenancies (about 38,900 at end-2027 if additions are 3,000, average tenancies about 10% above FY2026). Not visible: FY2028 onwards; base it on management's informal "about 6% tenancy growth a year" (about 2,100 to 2,400 additions) unless operators extend their cycles.
