Section 2 – Non-recurring Gains, Clean Earnings Base, and Minority Holdings

Section summary
  1. Indra's reported FY25 net income is distorted by a massive non-recurring gain from its TESS Defence stake revaluation.
  2. Sell-side treatment of this gain varies, creating significant inconsistencies in consensus adjusted earnings models.
  3. Consolidated space and land vehicle assets introduce material minority interest profit leaks that dilute parent EPS.

Revaluation of TESS Defence

1.
Revaluation financial impact: Indra recorded a massive one-off revaluation gain during the second quarter of 2025 to reflect its increased ownership in its land systems joint venture. According to Indra's interim financial report on 23 July 2025, the parent company acquired an additional 26.335% stake in Tess Defence, S.A., increasing its holding to 51.001% for a total consideration of EUR 106.66m. This purchase triggered a mandatory accounting update under IFRS, resulting in a non-recurring financial gain of EUR 99.64m from revaluing its historical 24.666% interest. This accounting entry artificially boosted the reported net profit for the period, meaning the underlying organic earnings power of the business was lower than the headline results.

Q y p Indra 1H25 and 2Q25 financial results showing the net profit spike from the TESS revaluation

2.
Artificially boosted net income: Reported net income for the full year 2025 was heavily influenced by this accounting adjustment, masking the true operational trajectory of the defense business. Indra's 2025 annual report released on 25 February 2026 highlights that consolidated net profit attributable to the parent reached EUR 435.8m, compared with EUR 277.5m in the prior fiscal year. If we exclude the EUR 99.64m revaluation gain, clean net income stood at approximately EUR 336.16m, representing a more modest organic improvement. This demonstrates how headline profitability measures can mislead investors unless they separate oneoff transactional events from recurring cash-generating activities.
3.
Operational consolidation dynamics: Consolidating Tess Defence added significant revenues to Indra's balance sheet but introduced a lower margin mix to the segment. Indra's annual report on 27 April 2026 shows that Tess Defence contributed EUR 143.25m in revenue from its consolidation date in June. However, Bernstein Research reports on 24 July 2026 that land vehicle program execution diluted the Defense EBIT margin down to 17.0% from 21.3% excluding TESS. This margin dilution will persist over the next 18 months unless the company can increase its proprietary sensor content.

Broker Treatment and Adjusted EPS

4.
Morgan Stanley adjusted methodology: Morgan Stanley took a technical approach to the revaluation benefit, incorporating the one-off gain into their near-term adjusted models while keeping outer years unchanged. In a research update on 24 July 2025, Morgan Stanley notes that the EUR 100m revaluation gain was the primary driver behind a massive net income beat in the second quarter. Consequently, the broker raised its fiscal year 2025 adjusted diluted EPS forecast by approximately 25% to EUR 2.58, but left outer-year forecasts broadly flat because of the non-recurring nature of the transaction. This highlights that their adjusted EPS base for 2025 remains high, which could distort year-on-year growth comparisons for fiscal year 2026.
5.
Bernstein Research clean base: Bernstein Research maintained an alternative perspective on Indra's adjusted earnings, focusing on the company's long-term valuation discount relative to pure-play defense peers. In a report published on 24 July 2026, Bernstein notes that Indra's adjusted EPS stood at EUR 2.48 for 2025, which matches the reported IFRS figure including the revaluation gain. This suggests that some sell-side analysts failed to strip out the paper gain from their historical valuation base, which creates an artificially low entry multiple for backward-looking quantitative screens. Investors must therefore scrutinize individual consensus models to ensure they are comparing clean operating performance.
6.
Contrary evidence: Some brokers argue that the revaluation gain should not be completely ignored because it reflects the crystallization of substantial industrial value in land systems. Morgan Stanley argues on 24 July 2025 that while the gain is technically non-recurring, the consolidation of TESS Defence is highly strategic as Spain's defense modernization cycle accelerates. This means that ignoring the gain underestimates the underlying value of the asset portfolio, although it remains critical to separate the cash and non-cash elements of the transaction.

Minority Holdings and Profit Share

7.
Tess Defence shareholder structure: Minority shareholdings in Tess Defence are tightly held by Spanish defense industrial partners, creating a complex governance and profit-sharing structure. Indra's consolidated annual accounts on 27 April 2026 disclose that the non-controlling interest in Tess Defence stands at exactly 49.0%. The remaining equity is split equally among Santa Bárbara Sistemas, S.A., Escribano Mechanical & Engineering, S.L., and SAPA Operaciones, S.L., with each holding a 16.33% stake in the venture. Consequently, almost half of the profits generated by Spain's flagship 8x8 land vehicle program will leak out to these partners rather than flowing to Indra's shareholders.
8.
Sovereign space non-controlling interests: Space consolidation has introduced significant minority profit leaks through Indra's acquisitions of Hispasat and Hisdesat. According to Indra's 2025 annual report, the group formalised its 89.68% stake in Hispasat on 30 December 2025, leaving a 10.3% non-controlling interest held by Spanish government-backed institutions. More importantly, because Hispasat owns over 43% of Hisdesat, Indra consolidated Hisdesat but faces a massive 54.3% non-controlling interest leak in that unit. This means that more than half of Hisdesat's defense satellite profits are attributed to minority partners, significantly reducing the EPS accretion of the space strategy.
9.
Hispasat institutional partners: Minority stakes in Hispasat are held by Spanish public entities, aligning the business with state-led defense and space priorities. Bernstein Research reports on 3 February 2025 that Spain's state holding company SEPI holds approximately 7.0% of Hispasat, while the Ministry of Science's CDTI holds around 3.0%. This close alignment with national security institutions provides strong backlog visibility for the IRIS2 secure satellite program, but it also limits Indra's operational flexibility regarding capital allocation and cost rationalization. This institutional structure confirms that the space division operates more as a sovereign utility than a commercial enterprise.

Comparative Peer Analysis

10.
Defense peer associate profits: European defense peers utilize diverse joint venture and associate structures, which impacts how they report clean earnings and minority interest leaks. Leonardo SpA's Helicopters and Electronics segments operate via extensive partnerships, with broker research from Jefferies on 10 July 2026 highlighting that Leonardo benefits from substantial equity-accounted associate income from MBDA and ATR. In comparison, Saab AB's annual report on 22 October 2026 reports MSEK 136 in associate income offset by MSEK 42 in minority leaks. This suggests that Indra's highly consolidated but heavily leaked Space structure is structurally riskier than the equity-accounted models of its peers.
11.
Satellite operator depreciation comparison: Satellite operators require massive capital expenditure, which drives high depreciation charges and depresses consolidated net income margins. Bernstein Research reports on 4 February 2025 that Hispasat and Hisdesat plan to spend EUR 400m in capital expenditure over the 2025-2030 period. This high capital intensity, driven by the IRIS2 satellite program, means that depreciation charges will step up sharply, dragging down the net income margin. Consequently, the space segment's contribution to parent EPS will be less accretive than gross EBIT trends suggest.
12.
Thales Alenia Space joint venture: Thales SA manages its satellite business through a joint venture model that minimizes direct minority profit leaks on its own balance sheet. Deutsche Bank Research notes on 22 September 2026 that Thales owns 67.0% of Thales Alenia Space, with Leonardo holding the remaining 33.0%. Because this business operates as a consistently equity-accounted associate for Leonardo, the operational risks and capital requirements are shared. This contrast highlights that Indra's choice to fully consolidate Hisdesat with a 54.3% minority leak exposes its parent net income to higher volatility than Thales's structured partnership.
13.
Other side of the story: While the revaluation gain and minority interest leaks are technical headwinds, the absolute volume of defense spending could render these accounting nuances irrelevant over the long term. Morgan Stanley points out on 29 October 2026 that Indra's record defense backlog of EUR 22.2bn, driven by Spanish modernization programs, provides unprecedented operational visibility. If top-line growth accelerates to double digits and operating leverage improves, the dilutive impact of minority leaks will be easily absorbed, leading to substantial EPS growth that exceeds historical broker expectations.

Sustainability

Net Financial Result and Prepayments

1.
Temporary financial improvement: Indra's net financial result reached a positive €4m in the first half of 2026 due to specific accounting adjustments. This positive result was primarily supported by a €17m gain from the accounting reclassification of contract assets and liabilities, alongside €11m of higher cash remuneration. However, these non-operating effects mask an underlying increase in financial expenses driven by expanding debt obligations, which suggests that this positive contribution will not persist over the next 18 months.
2.
Prepayment cash dynamics: Prepayments from Spain's Special Modernisation Programmes have artificially inflated Indra's liquidity position while masking underlying financing requirements. Bernstein Research reports on 12 October 2026 that out of €1.49bn in free cash flow, only €15m was generated excluding these program advances and assuming historical factoring. Because these prepayments are restricted to project execution, they reduce short-term bank borrowings but create substantial future program liabilities as cash is consumed.
3.
Pre-financing interest terms: Pre-financing cash carries strict regulatory and operational constraints that prevent Indra from optimizing its net treasury yields. Bernstein Research notes on 12 October 2026 that although these down-payments reduce intermediate funding risks, they cannot be deployed freely for corporate actions. This restricted cash profile limits interest income opportunities, which means the company cannot offset its gross debt costs as efficiently as its unconstrained commercial peers.

Debt Cost and Factoring

4.
Gross debt cost: Indra succeeded in reducing its average cost of gross debt, even as absolute borrowing volumes expanded during 2026. The average gross cost of debt improved from 3.3% in the first half of 2025 to 3.0% in the first half of 2026. This financing cost was highly competitive compared to historical cycles, helping protect net profit margins from the full impact of higher benchmark interest rates.
5.
Factoring elimination dynamics: Indra decided to eliminate its factoring program, which led to a significant transitional impact on reported net debt and liquidity. The group's 9M26 financial presentation shows the complete elimination of the historical €187m factoring program in September 2026. This operational adjustment means that reported free cash flow was reduced by a €187m factoring effect, reversing a key source of short-term working capital funding.
6.
Factoring impact on consensus: Analysts monitoring Indra's leverage ratios have focused on the underlying debt metrics after adjusting for the factoring exit. Bernstein Research notes on 24 July 2026 that excluding the impact of modernization programs and factoring, organic free cash flow remains highly constrained. This analytical perspective confirms that the elimination of factoring temporarily inflates leverage ratios unless offset by further Spanish defense down-payments.

Section 4 – Capitalised R&D, Intangible Asset Risks, and PwC Audit Judgements

Section summary
  1. Indra's rising R&D capitalization rate masks potential operating margin risks as deferred costs accumulate.
  2. The cancellation of the European FCAS fighter program introduces material write-down and team absorption risks.
  3. The auditor transition to PricewaterhouseCoopers could lead to stricter judgements on percentage-of-completion revenue.

R&D Capitalisation and Spikes

1.
Rising capitalisation rate: Indra experienced a dramatic surge in capitalized development costs during the first half of 2026 as its strategic industrial transformation gathered momentum. According to Indra's interim financial disclosures on 23 July 2026, capitalized R&D rose to EUR 41m in 1H26 from EUR 13m in 1H25. This shift deferring current expenses to the balance sheet artificially supports near-term operating margins but increases future amortization risks. Unless commercial milestones are met, this trend will eventually dilutive parent earnings.
2.
Accelerating amortization deferral: Indra's full-year financial accounts confirm that the company has steadily increased its reliance on R&D capitalization over successive fiscal periods. The group's annual report published on 27 April 2026 shows that development capitalization rose from EUR 46m in 2024 to EUR 88.9m in 2025, out of total development expenses of EUR 113.3m and EUR 144.8m respectively. This represents a capitalization rate jump to 61.4% from 40.6% in the prior year. This high rate creates a substantial asset base that must be written down if programs fail.

FCAS Halt and Intangible Risks

3.
Severe program risk: The abrupt cancellation of the Next-Generation Fighter element of the Future Combat Air System program presents a direct threat to Indra's capitalized asset base. Bernstein Research reported on 9 June 2026 that France and Germany agreed to halt the core fighter program. This halt means that Indra faces direct write-downs on capitalized costs if specific work packages cannot be repurposed. This structural disruption could trigger non-cash impairment charges that will depress reported earnings per share.
4.
Concentration of revenues: The financial materiality of the FCAS contract highlights the high stakes of any balance sheet adjustments for Indra's defense segment. Bernstein Research notes on 9 June 2026 that FCAS contributed EUR 214m in revenue during 2025, which accounted for approximately 15% of Defense division sales. Although Spain signed a separate EUR 700m five-year national contract with Indra, the loss of European consortium scale increases the overhead absorption risk for its specialized engineering teams. If these teams are underutilized, self-funded R&D expenses will rise, ultimately restricting operating margins.
5.
Contrary evidence: Some analysts argue that the halt of the European fighter program will have a negligible impact on Indra's mid-term financial performance because its focus remains on high-value systems. Bernstein Research argues on 9 June 2026 that resource reallocation toward combat cloud, sensors, and Command and Control domains will offset lost airframe volumes. This shift to software-led systems preserves underlying profit margins and backlog utility, meaning that major balance sheet write-downs are unlikely.

Auditor Transition and PoC Revenue

6.
Tightening accounting scrutiny: The transition to PricewaterhouseCoopers as the group's sole statutory auditor for the 2026 to 2028 period introduces potential changes in accounting judgements.
