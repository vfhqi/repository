EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

1.
INVESTMENT VERDICT: ZIGUP presents a compelling self-help story anchored by Spain's structural usership transition and the £20 million UK&I simplification programme serving as primary earnings growth engines, while normalisation of fleet replacement capex drives toward £200.

Materiality of Internal Change

2.
Strategic CFO Transition: Rachel Coulson was appointed as CFO on August 18, 2025, bringing extensive experience in digital transformation from her role as Deputy CFO at Pearson, positioning the group to execute ambitious efficiency programmes.
3.
Controversial Value Creation Plan: The VCP received material opposition at the AGM, passing with only 65.27% votes in favor and 34.73% against, introducing governance friction despite aligning leadership with shareholder value creation.
4.
Aggressive UK&I Simplification: The group launched a UK&I simplification programme to merge disparate historical brands and unlock £20 million in annualised savings by FY2028, with Jefferies noting that the £20 million efficiency programme will deliver £6 million to £7 million in net benefits in FY2027.
5.
Strategic Portfolio Rationalization: The group decided to exit the non-core NewLaw personal injury business to permanently improve Claims & Services margins, though the NewLaw personal injury market exit resulted in exceptional impairment and closure costs of £12.8 million.
6.
Cultural and Operational Enablement: Growing employee satisfaction with Group-wide employee survey engagement scores remaining strong and growing at 75%, while ZIGUP rolled out Microsoft 365 Copilot to 3,500 employees to enhance productivity.

Materiality of External Change

7.
Structural Demand Shift in Spain: Spanish corporate customers are increasingly transitioning from direct vehicle ownership to flexible rental models, with flexible rental penetration of the total Spanish light commercial vehicle fleet currently standing at only 5.0%, providing substantial runway for expansion.
8.
Defensible Competitive Positioning: ZIGUP commands approximately 20.0% of the highly fragmented UK LCV rental market and represents a market-leading 33.0% share of the regional LCV rental fleet in Spain, creating scale advantages.
9.
EV Transition Disruption Opportunity: ZIGUP is actively executing its Drive to Zero programme to assist corporate clients with vehicle electrification, successfully installing over 6,000 electric vehicle charging points during the fiscal year.
10.
Supply Chain Normalization: Original equipment manufacturer delivery schedules have fully normalised following years of acute pandemic shortages, helping lower the average fleet age to 28-30 months, reducing maintenance costs and improving disposal economics.
11.
Macroeconomic Divergence: The Spanish economy exhibits notable resilience, supporting double-digit flexible fleet expansion, while the UK consumer landscape remains structurally challenging, dampening broader commercial and construction activity.

Top 5-8 Ranked Key Drivers of EPS

Driver 1 – Spain Rental Usership Expansion (HIGH Impact, HIGH Plausibility): Management highlights that the Spanish LCV rental segment represents only 5% of the total vehicle parc, with average Spanish vehicles on hire increasing by 11% year-on-year to 66.10k units in H1 2026, supported by Deutsche Bank forecasting a long-term 6% compound annual growth rate for Spanish rental revenues.

Driver 2 – UK&I Simplification Cost Savings (HIGH Impact, HIGH Plausibility): The reorganisation aims to deliver at least £20 million of annualised savings by FY2028, with Jefferies expecting the business simplification to generate £6 million to £7 million in net benefits during FY2027, underpinned by cash costs to fully implement the 18-month reorganisation program capped at a modest £5 million.

Driver 3 – Fleet Replacement Capex Normalisation (HIGH Impact, HIGH Plausibility): Berenberg highlights ZIGUP's path to achieving £200 million in steady-state cash flow by FY2028, with steady-state cash flow more than doubling from £20.30 million to £48.60 million during H1 2026, as Deutsche Bank projects net replacement capex to fall by c.£60 million to c.£320 million by FY2028.

Driver 4 – Claims & Services Contract Renewals (MODERATE Impact, MEDIUM Plausibility): Management expects H2 volumes and contract renewals to lift segment margins closer to 5%, with the division successfully launching its partnership with global broker Howden Insurance in October 2025, though personal lines claims volumes softened over summer 2025.

Driver 5 – Residual Value Stabilization (MODERATE Impact, MEDIUM Plausibility): Management expects vehicle disposal profits to settle around £10 million to £15 million per segment, with UK residual values remaining stable since October 2024, though uncertain electric vehicle residual values could compress disposal margins.
