EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF) 3 findings

Sandvik operates as a dominant underground mining equipment duopoly controlling 90% of the global hard-rock market alongside Epiroc, with Mining and Rock Solutions generating nearly 70% of revenues from high-margin recurring aftermarket services that stabilize earnings through capital cycles. The Machining division commands a leading 20% global market share in premium metal cutting tools, delivering operating margins roughly double those of nearest peer Kennametal through superior pricing power and proprietary tungsten supply integration. Management has strategically repositioned the group toward higher-margin software and downstream filtration niches, including the transition of Intelligent Manufacturing to SaaS subscriptions and the acquisition of Diemme Filtration to access a 20 billion SEK dewatering addressable market.

1.
Financial Performance and Peer Benchmarking: Sandvik displays highly cyclical organic growth trajectories compared to peers, with flat organic revenue growth in early 2026 following a 2% contraction in late 2024 driven by weakening automotive and general engineering demand. Group operating margins remain resilient above 19% supported by high-margin aftermarket sales and active cost-cutting programs that delivered 2 billion SEK in run-rate savings. Return on invested capital reached 12.2% in 2025, lagging Atlas Copco's exceptional 24% ROIC due to goodwill dilution from recent software acquisitions and higher working capital intensity at 27.5% of sales. Prudent financial leverage of 0.9x net debt to EBITDA provides strong balance sheet headroom, with strong cash

conversion of 95% and unutilized credit facilities of 11 billion SEK supporting future M&A without stretching the capital structure.

2.
Operator Quality and Strategic Execution: CEO Stefan Widing transitioned the group from decentralization to a growth-led framework starting in 2020, successfully divesting lower-margin steel divisions while acquiring highergrowth digital mining assets, though the 2019-2025 Shift to Growth plan materially underdelivered in non-mining segments where Machining achieved only 1.9% CAGR against a 5% target. Capital allocation remains balanced with capex at 4% of sales and a 50% dividend payout, while management demonstrated cost flexibility by cutting 3,000 jobs during the 2019 downturn and delivering 2 billion SEK in run-rate savings to defend margins. Return on capital employed reached 15.2% in 2025, trailing Epiroc's 19.3% due to integration drag from mid-sized acquisitions. The business retains immense structural pricing power through its underground hard-rock duopoly holding 50% share in loaders and haulers, premium tooling leadership delivering double the margins of peers supported by R&D spend four times larger than Kennametal, and a strong competitive edge in underground battery-electric fleets with deployed volumes estimated at 2.4x larger than Epiroc

- Analyst/Investor Competitive Moat and Aftermarket Lock-In: Upstream mining companies capture mission-critical pricing power because original equipment placement generates high-margin, structurally embedded aftermarket streams over a 30-year platform lifecycle, with lifecycle aftermarket value reaching 3x to 4x the original equipment price. This recurring service multiplier mechanically locks in aftermarket streams worth 3x to 4x the initial price, representing nearly 70% of total mining revenues, while underground battery-electric vehicle platforms require 1.5x to 2x more technical service hours than diesel machinery.

Sandvik mitigates raw material risk through captive tungsten smelting at Wolfram Bergbau und Hütten in Austria producing SEK 2 billion annually, covering just over 10% of cutting tools' global tungsten demand, while recycling programs retrieve used inserts to supply roughly 55% of the group's global tungsten requirements. Machining customers prioritize cost-per-use over product price, making customer stickiness structurally high for premium cutting tools that deliver superior lifetime under extreme material feeds and prevent customer substitution with cheaper Chinese copies. & Co Redburn Long-Term Structural Tailwinds and Disruption Resilience: Sandvik and Epiroc control a combined 90% of the underground mining equipment market, with a dense onsite service network in remote regions creating an insurmountable entry barrier for new competitors and severe technical and safety demands preventing low-cost Asian players from entering premium niches.

Record copper and gold prices incentivize miners to increase capex and maximize production rates, while declining ore grades force miners deeper underground which structurally favors Sandvik's 80% underground mining exposure, with stringent safety regulations driving adoption of fully autonomous and remote-controlled haulage fleets and electrified underground fleets drastically reducing ventilation energy costs representing 50% of mine energy.

Sandvik's Deswik platform provides advanced mine planning across more than 900 global mine sites, though the closed software ecosystem faces integration headwinds relative to hardware-agnostic rivals. While 3D printing represents a long-term threat to cutting tools by potentially reducing early-stage roughing operations, high-precision semi-finishing and finishing tools remain essential due to printed part variability.

3.
Foundation Verdict: Strong – the business foundation is anchored by an impregnable underground mining duopoly capturing structural aftermarket multipliers worth 3x to 4x original equipment sales, resilient 19%+ operating margins supported by proprietary tungsten integration, and deep paradigm fit with multi-decade mining electrification and deeper ore grade trends, though software monetization execution and mid-market machining share defense against Chinese competitors remain unproven
