Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Evaluating Telia pricing power across diverse Nordic conditions remains critical for predicting mid-term earnings per share.
  2. Investors must weigh robust consumer convergence against localized open-access fibre competition and emerging technological switching threats.

Central Query and

1.
Market Parameters Central inquiry: "how much pricing power does this company have, and how does it hold up across different operating conditions, specifically: low Swedish consumer inflation (Riksbank core inflation forecast of 0.6% for 2026) with a central bank now raising rates; higher Norwegian inflation and rising household income; recurring promotional campaign seasons (Black Friday and Christmas, and the expiry of discounted twelve-month contracts in Finland); a price war in one market (Finland in late 2025); price competition on Swedish municipal open-access fibre networks; possible four-to-three consolidation in Sweden; artificial-intelligence agents that automate tariff comparison and switching; regulated wholesale caps (EU roaming at \u20ac1 per gigabyte from 2027)?"
2.
Extensive research: This investigation analyzed 20 different documents including broker notes, company filings, and expert calls. The scope encompasses 10 distinct companies and evaluates three historical precedents to establish a comprehensive overview. This evidence is essential for evaluating the stability of Telia across its geographic segments.

EPS Relevance and

3.
Macro Dynamics Significant leverage: Telia earnings are highly sensitive to real price adjustments because service revenue drives the bulk of operating cash flow. Analysts emphasize that lapping cost cuts means Telia must accelerate service revenue growth to support its dividend goals. Consequently, pricing power represents the primary lever for expanding EPS over the next 18 to 36 months.
4.
Macro headwinds: Swedish core inflation is projected to drop to 0.6% in 2026 while interest rates remain volatile. Telia faces pressure to implement back-book price increases in a low-inflation environment without triggering customer churn. Achieving real-term price increases under these conditions is critical for maintaining Swedish mobile margins.
5.
Norwegian opportunities: Rising household incomes and higher inflation in Norway have historically allowed Telia to lift average revenue per user. However, sustaining these gains depends on how aggressively Telenor and Lyse respond to Telia's tariff adjustments. Any aggressive discounting by competitors could erode the benefits of Telia's convergence strategy in Norway.

Operational Disruptors and

6.

In Finland, the expiry of 12-month contracts forces Telia to defend its base against aggressive competitor campaigns from DNA and Elisa. Section 2 – Swedish Mobile Postpaid and Open-Access Fiber Network Dynamics

Section summary
  1. Flat Swedish mobile ARPU reflects persistent low-end migration and aggressive B2B price competition.
  2. Fixed broadband ARPU is heavily diluted by low-value consolidation and open-network wholesale pricing pressure.

Postpaid Mobile ARPU Constraints

1.
Stagnant performance: Telia Sweden has experienced static mobile postpaid ARPU which reached SEK 238 in the second quarter of 2026. DNB Carnegie reported in September 2025 that postpaid ARPU has been essentially flat since first quarter 2019 due to low-end brand migration. This lack of growth limits Telia ability to expand its margins in mature areas. Squeezing realterm revenue increases out of this footprint remains a critical challenge.
2.
Intense tendering: Swedish B2B mobile markets suffer from severe pricing pressure as operators compete aggressively for volume. Rothschild and Co Redburn noted in September 2025 that public sector clients are regularly trading down to cheaper tariffs during contract renewals. Consequently, these B2B pricing concessions drag down blended ARPU. This commercial friction directly reduces overall Swedish service revenue margins.

Brand migration: Swedish consumers are increasingly switching from premium mobile offerings to cheaper no- frills alternatives to reduce monthly expenditures. DNB Carnegie explained in July 2026 that overall market ARPU is being negatively impacted by no-frills brand migration which dilutes premium service revenues. 4.
Stable competition: Tele2 Sweden has managed to report slightly more stable mobile postpaid ARPU trends in recent quarters. DNB Carnegie reported in October 2026 that Tele2 mobile B2C postpaid ARPU was SEK 218 in Q3 2026, up from SEK 212 in Q2 2026. This moderate improvement suggests that market discipline is slowly returning to Sweden postpaid segment. This stabilization should ease competitive pressures on Telia.

Section 3 – Norwegian Mobile Convergence and Competitive Responses

The Telia Q3 2026 interim report published on October 22, 2026, showed postpaid mobile ARPU growing 2.8% to NOK 303 while broadband ARPU rose 9.4% to NOK 275. These consecutive gains indicate that retail tariff adjustments held firm through autumn 2026 without experiencing immediate price erosion. Improving volume additions: Telia Norway successfully turned around its customer intake trends by registering positive subscriber additions during middle 2026. This alliance allows both companies to pool their spectrum and base station resources while maintaining separate core networks. Section 5 – Contract Indexation, Inflation Lags, and Premium Precedents

Section summary
  1. Telia contractual pricing structures and inflation indexation lags limit its ability to offset rising costs in low Swedish inflation environments.
  2. Swisscom and Sunrise precedents show that aggressive back-book premium adjustments frequently trigger dilutive subscriber transitions to low-tier flanker brands.
  3. KPN demonstrates that modifying contractual terms and implementing strategic front-book price steps provides superior revenue protection compared to standard indexation.

Contractual Indexation and Inflation Lags

1.
Sluggish adjustments: Telia Towers faced lower inflation-linked price increases in the first quarter of 2026 compared to the prior year. DNB Carnegie reported on April 13, 2026, that these price adjustments are linked directly to consumer price inflation measures. This lag in indexation directly restricts near-term earnings growth until new contracts reflect updated economic realities.
2.
Fragile pricing: Swedish telecom operators find it difficult to implement direct back-book adjustments due to weak domestic macroeconomic backdrops. Morgan Stanley reported on November 15, 2026, that Riksbank policy rate hikes are expected to reach 2.25% while underlying inflation remains depressed through the year. Consequently, Telia cannot easily leverage high inflation indices to automatically raise B2C tariffs in Sweden.

Precedents from KPN Dutch Market

3.
Capped indices: KPN serves as a critical precedent for structured contractual indexation clauses in European telecom contracts. Deutsche Bank Research reported on September 17, 2026, that KPN utilizes contractual clauses allowing CPI-based price increases up to a strict limit of two percent for mobile. This cap restricts the company's ability to fully pass through high inflation rates to customers during periods of severe macroeconomic pressure.
4.
Strategic revisions: KPN modified its standard terms to provide greater flexibility for ad-hoc tariff adjustments beyond normal inflation parameters. Deutsche Bank Research reported on October 30, 2026, that KPN revised its customer contracts in June and July of 2026 to enable price increases beyond CPI trends. Although this structural change triggered a temporary spike in customer churn, it ultimately established stronger mid-term pricing flexibility.
5.
Historical indexation: KPN relied heavily on contractual CPI adjustments during periods of elevated consumer price inflation. Bernstein Research reported on January 23, 2025, that KPN faced lower indexation contribution trends entering 2025 as general inflation slowed down. This shift illustrates how indexingdependent business models face revenue deceleration when macroeconomic price trends begin to normalize.
6.
Front-book elevation: KPN pivoted toward front-book price steps as a strategic alternative to purely indexation- driven revenue growth. Deutsche Bank Research reported on September 17, 2026, that KPN eliminated its cheapest broadband tier to raise entry-level pricing by nearly six percent. This deliberate shift forces incoming subscribers onto higher value packages and helps prevent back-book erosion from out-of-contract customers spinning down.
7.
Contractual modifications: KPN demonstrated that changing agreement structures can offer long-term commercial agility despite immediate subscriber friction. Deutsche Bank Research noted on October 29, 2026, that KPN's contract term changes gave the operator increased flexibility to adjust its consumer propositions. This structural revision positions the company to implement ad-hoc rate increases and protect underlying cash flow from inflationary erosion.

Swiss Premium Operator Case Studies

This aggressive backbook adjustment represents a significant test of customer loyalty and pricing power in a mature market.

Deutsche Bank Research noted on October 15, 2026, that Swisscom's Wingo brand implemented a targeted price increase of CHF 1 to support service revenue. This multi-brand strategy helps the operator defend its volume base while slowly repairing the market's entry-level price floor.

Morgan Stanley reported on November 5, 2026, that the proportion of Swiss B2C mobile customers on second and third brands increased to thirty-nine percent in the third quarter of 2026.
