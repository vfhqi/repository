Section 2 – ROFA Industrial Automation's Historical Financial Performance

Steady expansion: ROFA Industrial Automation achieved a revenue CAGR of approximately 3.6% between fiscal years 2017 and 2025, according to Morgan Stanley on 2 March 2026. Compared to SGS, ROFA operates at more than double the financial scale with its EUR 430 million in revenue. This substantial scale enables SPIE to fulfill its annual German expansion targets.

These discrepant statutory filing numbers suggest that public records are either incomplete or reflect a reorganization of German entities.

Segmental distribution: ROFA derives approximately 80% of its total sales from factory automation, with the remaining 20% coming from warehouse and logistics automation. Section 4 – Provisional Purchase Price Allocation and Acquired Assets

Section summary
  1. SPIE postponed detailed provisional purchase price allocation disclosures for ROFA Industrial Automation because the consolidation process only started in the third quarter of 2026.
  2. Group goodwill accounts for nearly sixty percent of non-current assets, aligning with acquisition-heavy balance sheet structures seen across other European technical service consolidators.
  3. Non-cash amortization from historic purchase price allocations creates a continuous drag on reported net income, while cash flow metrics remain unaffected.

Provisional PPA Status

1.
Delayed consolidation: SPIE postponed the detailed consolidation of ROFA Industrial Automation until the third quarter closing of fiscal year 2026. Consequently, the first-half interim report of July 2026 did not contain specific figures for acquired goodwill or intangible assets. The company's management stated that they will perform a purchase price allocation process to identify the principal acquired intangible assets. Therefore, investors must wait for the full-year disclosures to see the provisional purchase price allocation values.
2.
Strategic identification: SPIE expects to identify significant intangible assets under IFRS 3R guidelines once the ROFA transaction is fully consolidated. These principal acquired assets will include the ROFA brand, customer relationships, and the outstanding order backlog. By segregating these items, the company will amortize them over their respective useful economic lives. Thus, this accounting treatment will reduce reported operating income through non-cash amortization charges in future quarters.
3.
Rising goodwill: SPIE reported that its total German segment goodwill increased to EUR 1,790.1 million as of June 30, 2026. This segment's goodwill stood at EUR 1,773.2 million at the end of December 2025 before recent bolt-on additions. Because the ROFA deal was not consolidated at the end of the first half, this increase reflects other minor German acquisitions. Consequently, the German division remains the largest recipient of goodwill across the entire group footprint.
4.
Extended evaluation: SPIE benefits from a contractually permitted twelve-month evaluation period to finalize its purchase price allocations under IFRS rules. During this window, the company can adjust the temporary fair value of assets and liabilities acquired. This flexibility allows management to refine the initial figures based on new operational data. Consequently, these adjustments may materially alter the provisional goodwill reported at consolidation.
5.
Indefinite brand life: SPIE treats its core corporate brand as an intangible asset with an indefinite useful life. Unlike customer relationships and backlogs, indefinite-life brands are not subject to systematic annual amortization. Instead, the group tests these brand assets for impairment at least once per year. This accounting policy helps protect operating profit margins from continuous non-cash brand depreciation charges.
6.
Contrary evidence: SPIE omitted the detailed provisional purchase price allocation table for ROFA from its Q3 2026 financial sales publication. Although the company consolidated ROFA since June 3, 2026, the interim sales report focused primarily on organic growth rather than balance sheet updates. This lack of detailed data prevents analysts from immediately calculating the net cash acquired from ROFA at the closing date.

This represents a goodwill-to-non-current-assets ratio of approximately 59.44% (calculated) as of June 30, 2026. Such a high ratio indicates that SPIE's expansion model is highly dependent on acquisition premiums rather than physical capital. Therefore, the company must maintain strong operating returns to justify these balance sheet valuations.

This high percentage confirms that European technical service consolidators consistently allocate the majority of consideration to goodwill. Consequently, SPIE's heavy goodwill ratio is typical for the technical services industry.

Goodwill represented EUR 4,295 million out of a total balance sheet of EUR 10,295 million at the end of 2025. For the first half of 2026, this non-cash amortization expense amounted to EUR 45.7 million. Although this charge does not affect free cash flow, it reduces the GAAP net income.
