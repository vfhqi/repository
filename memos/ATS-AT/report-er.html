Section 16 – Conclusion

AT&S delivered a highly encouraging turnaround in its F1Q26/27 financial results, driven by strong volumes and improved substrate pricing that outperformed expectations. This earnings print marks a critical inflection point for the investment case, validating management's strategic pivot from commoditized PCB manufacturing toward high-value artificial intelligence packaging partnerships. The group achieved reported revenue of €548.7 million, representing 37.5% year-over-year growth and 40% growth on a constant-currency basis, which exceeded initial market expectations due to strong volume expansion and improved utilization.

More significantly, EBITDA soared by 134% year-over-year to €165.0 million, pushing the EBITDA margin up by 12.4 percentage points to 30.1%, driven by higher capacity utilization and better pricing in the Microelectronics segment.

The single most important change to the investment case is the successful de-risking of AT&S's massive capital expansion program through customer-financed capacity agreements. To protect its stretched balance sheet, AT&S has secured full customer financing for its newly announced Chongqing capacity expansion, with the high double-digit million range investment fully financed by long-term customer agreements. Furthermore, in mid- June 2026, AT&S announced a major capacity expansion of €1.5 billion to €2.0 billion at its Kulim site in Malaysia, involving close partnerships with AMD and another leading technology company to co-develop next-generation substrates. This structural shift from balance-sheet-constrained capacity expansion to customer-backed co-investment fundamentally transforms the risk-reward profile, eliminating the primary historical concern of idle capacity charges during cyclical downturns.

The investment case now rests on three core hypotheses that deviate materially from consensus expectations. First, Deutsche Bank's Brown indicated that the midpoint of company guidance remains conservative relative to ongoing margin momentum, pointing out that underlying EBITDA margins of 39% to 40% in Q4-26 and Q1-27 (excluding client payments) indicate strong fundamental pricing strength. This suggests that pricing power is structurally stronger than the market currently models, driven by acute global substrate shortages and the exponential complexity scaling of advanced packaging.

Second, Deutsche Bank's Brown highlights that the Kulim K1 ramp for AMD will increase customer diversification, with this ramp expected to elevate AMD to approximately

20% of sales in FY27 from less than 5% historically. This customer diversification materially reduces the concentration risk that has historically depressed AT&S's valuation multiple. Third, despite its rapid operational turnaround, AT&S trades at an attractive forward valuation of 5.3x EV/EBITDA, representing a 50% to 60% discount to its closest Asian substrate peers, while peers Ibiden and Unimicron trade at a historical average multiple of approximately 12x to 15x EV/EBITDA.

This valuation gap appears unjustified given the company's improving execution, customer-backed expansions, and accelerating margin trajectory. However, the strongest counter-hypothesis centers on the structural risk of industry-wide overcapacity emerging from synchronized competitor capital spending. Market leader Unimicron is aggressively accelerating its capital deployment to capture dominant high-end substrate allocations, with Masterlink Securities reporting that Unimicron raised its 2026 capital expenditure budget for the second time to NT$53.7 billion from NT$19.7 billion to fit out its Guangfu Plant II and Yangmei Plant II, targeting a massive 40% year-over-year capacity expansion by the end of 2026.

Furthermore, second-tier Taiwanese substrate suppliers are also executing substantial capacity expansions, with Kinsus approving an additional NT$19.6 billion in capital expenditure, more than doubling its original NT$8.0 billion budget, with this capital earmarked for facility construction at its K6 plant to double monthly capacity to 80 million units by fiscal year 2029. If this synchronized capacity build-out coincides with a sudden deceleration in hyperscaler AI capital spending, the industry could rapidly revert to severe pricing erosion and margin compression, particularly punishing highly leveraged players like AT&S.

A detailed 'WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND' framework requires monitoring five critical variables. First, any delay exceeding two quarters in the Kulim K2 customer qualification timeline would invalidate the FY28-29 revenue inflection thesis, as Deutsche Bank's Brown adjusted his model to reflect a delayed ramp-up schedule for the empty Kulim K2 facility, pushing it to Q1-29 from Q3-28. Second, a decline in substrate pricing of more than 10% quarter-over-quarter would signal a premature return to overcapacity, contradicting the current structural shortage narrative.

Third, any increase in net debt/EBITDA leverage above 3.0x would indicate that operating cash generation is insufficient to fund the aggressive capex program, as cash flow from operating activities declined by 78.3% year-over-year to €39.9 million due to inventory building and a significant increase in receivables, reflecting supply chain resilience efforts and higher revenue volumes. Fourth, a reduction in customer concentration below 70% of revenues from the top five accounts would materially de-risk the geopolitical exposure, as the company's high customer concentration represents a significant vulnerability as 76.5% of total revenue is now attributable to its top five customers, representing an increase from 71.3% in the prior fiscal year, intensifying AT&S's sensitivity to geopolitical shocks.

Fifth, any acceleration in the commercialization timeline of glass core substrates or CoWoP architectures before 2029 would represent a disruptive technological threat, as emerging packaging platforms that bypass the substrate entirely represent a long-term structural tail risk, with Chip-on-Wafer-on-PCB architecture seeking to replace Chip-on-Wafer-on- Substrate packaging, and a successful transition to CoWoP would eliminate the need for ABF substrates, potentially driving industry-wide oversupply risks in late 2027 to 2030.
