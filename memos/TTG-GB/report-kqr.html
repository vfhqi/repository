The overarching inquiry

1.
Overarching query: "For TT Electronics plc (TTG-GB), whose shares rose about 95% in the twelve months to October 2026 to a 24-month-forward price-to-earnings multiple of about 10.5x against a five-year median of about 7.2x and a ten-year median of about 9.8x, what do the share-price and forwardmultiple histories of UK and European engineered-electronics and components companies show about how the multiple behaved in the 24 months after the first set of results that followed a profit-warning cycle and a change of chief executive, specifically at Volex plc (2017 to 2021), XP Power Ltd (2024 to 2026), Senior plc (2023 to 2025), discoverIE Group plc (2019 to 2021), Solid State plc (2024 to 2026) and TT Electronics itself (2016 to 2018 and 2020 to 2021), including the multiple at the first clean results, its peak and trough within the following 24 months, whether the re-rating was sustained once cash conversion recovered and dividends were reinstated, how the multiple responded to a disposal of a non-core division and to the presence of a blocking shareholder or a lapsed bid, and how sell-side target-price methodologies and the covering brokers' published FY2027 building blocks (tax rate, Power and EMS margins, working capital, dividend) at Investec, Stifel and RBC compare with those of Jefferies, so that it is clear which broker's model drives the FactSet median and what multiple the sell-side regards as fair for a stock at this stage of recovery?"

This means the stock's premium rating could rapidly unwind unless working capital requirements ease and dividends are reinstated to support investor confidence.

Section 3 – Impact of Cash Conversion and Dividend Reinstatement

Section summary
  1. Strong operational cash conversion and progressive dividend profiles typically underpin the sustainability of post-turnaround multiple expansions.
  2. Delays in reinstating dividends or working capital swings can depress valuation multiples even when underlying cash metrics begin recovering. Net read: Historical peer turnarounds show that sustained rating premiums require a proven track record of converting operating profits into free cash flow.

Turnaround cash conversion profiles

1.
Divergent cash performance: TT Electronics experienced a sharp contraction in operational cash flow during the first half of 2026 because of working capital builds. Company filings on 2 September 2026 revealed that cash conversion fell to 42% compared to 135% in the prior half-year. This deterioration was driven by a substantial £12.7 million working capital outflow from targeted inventory investment to support second-half deliveries. Consequently, the group must deliver on its full-year conversion target of 70% to 80% to restore investor faith in its balance sheet.
2.
Peer cash metrics: Peer companies have demonstrated far more robust cash generation profiles during their respective recovery phases. Volex's preliminary results on 25 June 2026 showed that the group delivered a consistent underlying free cash flow of $42.3 million. Although its cash conversion of 63.9% was slightly lower than the prior year's 67.2% due to data center inventory builds, its overall cash position remained strong. This highlights how larger peers can sustain consistent capital allocation priorities even when experiencing minor working capital pressures.
3.
Compounding cash model: The cash generation profile of discoverIE Group provides an excellent precedent of operational cash flow supporting a premium valuation multiple. Group annual reports from June 2026 show that discoverIE achieved an operating cash conversion of 91%, which is comfortably ahead of its long-term 85% target. Furthermore, its ten-year cash conversion has averaged approximately 100% across multiple economic cycles. This exceptional cash generation has enabled discoverIE to self-fund acquisitions and maintain its highgrowth compounding model.
4.
Aerospace cash recovery: Senior plc provides another compelling case study of cash flow recovery driving post- turnaround multiple performance. Senior's annual results on 2 March 2026 reported an outstanding operating cash conversion of 90%, which exceeded its medium-term financial targets. This strong cash performance drove free cash flow from continuing operations to £35.8 million, up from £26.1 million in 2024. This cash recovery allowed Senior to substantially deleverage its balance sheet to a comfortable 0.9x net debt to EBITDA.
