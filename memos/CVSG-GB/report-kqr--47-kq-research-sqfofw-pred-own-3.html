Volume pressure and footfall trends

1.
Depressed traffic: CVS Group witnessed persistent volume headwinds in its companion-animal division through the end of its financial year 2026. The company reported veterinary practice like-for-like sales growth of just 1.0% in its main division. Management attributed this domestic volume weakness to softer UK consumer confidence and extreme fourth-quarter hot weather. Consequently, near-term organic growth depends heavily on recovering routine pet clinic footfall.
2.
Market contraction: Industry-wide veterinary clinic visits continue to experience negative momentum due to cumulative inflation and macroeconomic pressure. Piper Sandler projected UK veterinary visits to drop 1% to 2% in 2026 and remain flat to down 1% in 2027. More than half of surveyed practitioners now blame weak growth on consumer demand rather than staffing constraints. This weak volume environment implies that consolidators must rely on pricing power to sustain earnings.
3.
Owner affordability: Financial anxiety among pet owners has directly restricted routine and preventative clinical visits since mid-2026. A national consumer survey revealed that 52% of UK pet owners express deep concern regarding veterinary healthcare affordability. This financial stress has forced lower-income households to defer routine maintenance treatments. If these inflationary pressures persist, companion-animal volumes will remain constrained across corporate practice networks.
4.
Cohort aging: The aging of the post-pandemic pet population represents a key structural driver for future clinic visit volumes. CVS Group expects the large puppy and kitten cohorts born during the pandemic to require more clinical care as they age. These animals are currently in their young, healthy adult years, visiting the vets less often. Consequently, this demographic shift should drive higher care intensity over the next three years.
5.
Contrary evidence: Deutsche Bank reports that Pets at Home achieved sequential operational improvement, with first-quarter veterinary consumer revenue increasing 1.9% to £215 million. This clinical revenue growth occurred despite the broader UK veterinary services market remaining subdued. Furthermore, the group's veterinary joint-venture network has maintained clinical full-time equivalent headcount growth of 4% year-on-year. This demonstrates that differentiated corporate joint-venture models can outperform fully corporate veterinary clinics during regulatory transitions.

Transaction values and service-led inflation

6.
Price protection: Veterinary practices are successfully defending their operating revenues from regulatory price caps by increasing service fees. Following the Competition and Markets Authority's final decision, written prescription fees were capped. Dr. Cees Bennett explained that clinics are recovering lost drug margins through higher consultation fees. This strategic shift ensures that overall transaction values remain high despite regulatory pressure.
7.
Diagnostic intensity: Corporate practice groups are increasing diagnostic testing penetration to protect their historical operating turnover. CVS Group's Laboratories division reported revenue growth of 11.5%, driven by a 4.3% increase in test volumes. Management continues to target diagnostic capability expansion to support clinical care. This diagnostic intensity will be crucial for stabilizing companion-animal division margins.
8.
Insurance dynamics: Moderate claims inflation has helped UK pet insurers absorb rising veterinary transaction costs without triggering severe policy cancellations. Jefferies reported that annual pet insurance claims inflation stabilized at approximately 5.0% in 2026. Meanwhile, insurers like Policy Expert grew their UK customer base by 5.2% to cover 1.6 million customers. This steady insurance coverage supports resilient high-value clinical transaction volumes.

E-prescribing platforms: The administrative burden associated with written prescriptions has prompted practices to adopt specialized digital infrastructure. Petscribers developed a secure platform to simplify the creation and sharing of written prescriptions. Dr. Angela Jones noted that manual prescription processes currently strain busy clinical teams. Section 4 – Online Prescription Migration and Competitive Pricing Pressures

Section summary
  1. The regulatory push for pricing transparency since September 2026 is accelerating the structural shift of chronic veterinary medicine sales toward low-cost online channels.
  2. Corporate veterinary practice margins are insulated because clinical service fees and diagnostic revenues are being raised to offset lost prescription product sales.
  3. Competitors like Molecule Group are building scaled ePharmacy platforms to capture this migrating market, while CVS leverages Animed Direct to retain digital spend.

Acceleration of digital medicine migration

1.
High-migrating clinical products: High-margin chronic pharmaceutical sales are migrating rapidly from physical veterinary practices to online platforms. Harrison Family Vets' Group Clinical Director stated in September 2026 that UK practices have already lost over 50% of their long-term medication sales online over the last five years. This leakage is expected to accelerate because the September 2026 regulatory order mandates that veterinarians actively inform clients about cheaper online alternatives. Consequently, clinics face margin dilution unless professional service fees are adjusted upward.
2.
Growing administrative friction: The administrative friction of issuing written prescriptions represents a growing operational burden for clinical teams. Jefferies reported in February 2026 that written prescriptions remain a major operational bottleneck following the Competition and Markets Authority's review. Dr. Cees Bennett noted in February 2026 that manual prescription processing is highly inefficient and creates significant friction because clinics must verify and process external requests. This administrative load will ultimately increase client transaction costs because busy clinics will charge administrative fees to cover staff time.
3.
Stable historic reliance: The proportion of medicine sales in total clinic revenue has stabilized at historically low levels. A former director at VetPartners explained in June 2024 that small animal practices typically derive only 15% to 20% of their total revenue from in-clinic pharmaceutical sales. Similarly, a former director at Linnaeus confirmed in September 2026 that medicines account for just 15% to 18% of modern practice turnover. This limited reliance on drug sales means that the earnings per share risk from the final regulatory pricing order remains highly manageable.
4.
Contrary evidence: CVS Group successfully digitized its workflow using Nordhealth's Provet Cloud practice management software to streamline operations across 348 clinics. DNB Carnegie reported in August 2024 that this rapid software implementation was completed in just six weeks, illustrating the group's technical integration capabilities. This technical agility suggests that large veterinary chains can utilize advanced software to streamline prescription-handling and keep clients within their ecosystem. Consequently, the threat of rapid client leakage to third-party online retailers may be smaller if integrated clinical digital tools are deployed.

CVS Group online retail performance

5.
Strategic defense mechanism: CVS Group is leveraging its Animed Direct online retail platform to capture price- sensitive customers migrating away from physical clinics. The company reported in its 2026 Annual Report that Animed Direct represents its dedicated online pharmacy and pet retailer, supplying pet food and prescription medicines directly to consumers. This digital division allows the group to protect its overall market share by offering a low-cost option for clients who shop around. Consequently, this online channel helps stabilize group-level revenues and protect CVS from competitive pricing transparency pressures.
6.
Rebounding revenue performance: Animed Direct achieved solid double-digit top-line growth in the financial year 2026 despite facing severe cost-of-living pressures. CVS Group reported that its online retail business grew revenue by 11.1% to £51.0 million in the financial year 2026, up from £45.9 million in the prior year. This strong recovery represents a significant turnaround from the financial year 2025, when online retail revenues declined by 8.2% due to legacy website issues and reduced discretionary spending. This performance suggests that online medicine demand is accelerating post-pandemic.
