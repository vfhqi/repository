Section 1 – Key question and brief

Section summary
  1. Tightening biofuel mandates are highly resolvable regarding volume growth, but structural overcapacity and feedstock cost inflation make mid-term refiner margins and EPS trajectory highly volatile

Mandate transmission to earnings

Uncertain margin outlook: The core question is whether tightening biofuel mandates will restore high- margin environments, or if overcapacity will compress margins through 2030.
2.
Expanding volume growth: Tightening mandate schedules drive high physical demand volumes, which directly expands low-carbon segment sales and increases group revenues for early-moving refiners.
3.
Rising feedstock costs: Refiners face volatile raw material costs, as feedstock competition for waste oils can inflate operating costs and squeeze refinery operating margins significantly.
4.
Premium multiple justification: High margin visibility from mandated volumes allows pure-play biofuel stocks to command premium valuation multiples over traditional fossil-fuel refining peers.

Section 2 – Mandate schedules and policy dynamics

Section summary
  1. Tightening European and US regulatory mandates guarantee substantial volume growth for HVO and SAF through 2030, though uneven national transpositions create regional margin variations.

European Union regulatory roadmap

1.
Mandated HVO targets: Under the European Union RED III directive formally enacted in December 2025, member states must achieve either a 29% renewable share or a 14.5% greenhouse gas intensity reduction in transport by 2030.
2.
Binding sub-targets: The RED III framework establishes a binding 5.5% combined sub-target for advanced biofuels and non-biological renewable fuels by 2030, including a 1% minimum share for renewable fuels of non-biological origin.
3.
Phasing out double-counting: A major structural shift from January 2026 is the elimination of double- counting mechanisms for advanced biofuels, which effectively forces fuel suppliers to purchase double the physical volume.
4.
ReFuelEU SAF ramp-up: The ReFuelEU Aviation regulation mandates fuel suppliers to blend a minimum 2% SAF in 2025, rising to 6% by 2030 and 70% by 2050.
5.
Synthetic fuel mandates: Within the ReFuelEU framework, a sub-mandate for synthetic aviation fuels (e-fuels) starts at synthetic aviation fuel target of 1.2% in 2030 and rises to 35% by 2050.
6.
FuelEU maritime integration: The FuelEU Maritime regulation adds a smaller demand pool, requiring a 2% shipping emissions intensity reduction in 2025, rising to 6% by 2030.

National transpositions in Europe

7.
Germany's aggressive quota: Germany's April 2026 RED III transposition raises the greenhouse gas quota from 12% to quota increase to 17.5% by 2027 and 65% by 2040.
8.
German transport scope: The German draft bill broadens the greenhouse gas quota scope to encompass the entire transport sector, including road, rail, and aviation.
9.
Netherlands mandate structures: The Netherlands launched a draft bill with a 4% industrial hydrogen mandate for 2030, which begins in 2027 and utilizes a book-and-claim regime.
10.
Dutch marine fuel treatment: Under the Dutch transposition, used cooking oil supplied to maritime shipping receives no compliance credit value, forcing suppliers to charge a premium.
11.
Spanish compliance transition: Spain formalized its RED III transposition from 2027, introducing ambitious road transport mandates and transitioning to a greenhouse gas-based quota system.
12.
French penalty frameworks: France's draft transposition outlines tougher greenhouse gas reduction targets, enforcing non-compliance fines of double the average SAF price gap multiplied by the deficit volume.
13.
Austria's demanding rules: Austria's transposition of RED III imposes requirements that are far more demanding than previous regulations, compelling OMV to accelerate local blending capabilities.

United States policy framework

14.
Federal volume targets: Under the US Renewable Fuel Standard, the EPA set Renewable Volume Obligations at 24.02 billion gallons for 2026 and 24.46 billion gallons for 2027.
15.
Clean fuel credits: The Section 45Z Clean Fuel Production Credit provides a tax credit of up to $1 per gallon for HVO and $1.75 per gallon for SAF from 2025 to 2027.
16.
Feedstock origin limitations: Under the OBBBA amendments, Section 45Z eligibility is restricted to fuels derived from domestic feedstocks starting in 2026, which structurally disadvantages European and Asian imports.
17.
36 10Q California LCFS tightening: California's Low Carbon Fuel Standard was tightened in July 2025, increasing the carbon intensity reduction target to 30% by 2030.
18.
Automatic acceleration mechanism: The California LCFS framework incorporates an Automatic Acceleration Mechanism triggering in 2027 to automatically tighten carbon intensity benchmarks if credit oversupply persists.
19.
Other side of the story: Sceptical analysts warn that because biofuels are highly expensive, governments under cost-of-living pressures may soften or delay soften ambitious long-term targets as Swedish and Finnish reversals have already demonstrated.

Section 9 – Second and third-order implications

Section summary
  1. Tightening biofuel mandates spark inflationary agricultural feedstock pressures that spill over to food systems and accelerate maritime and aviation cost pass-through.
  2. Non-consensus alternatives like insect lipids and plastic-derived synthetic fuels emerge as disruptive long-term hedges to lipid supply scarcity.

Agricultural and food-vs-fuel dynamics

1.
Inflated crop demand: Tightening mandates consume 28% of global palm oil and 28% of canola oil, creating massive domestic markets for growers.
2.
Escalating food inflation: Sustained energy shocks and feedstock demand drive up agricultural production costs globally, establishing food as an independent inflationary force.
3.
Surging seed sales: Higher crop prices from biofuel demand can add 250 to 325 basis points to seed sales for suppliers like Corteva.
4.
Fertilizer market exposure: Biofuel mandates drive crop acreages, making fertilizer providers highly vulnerable to potential mandate reversals or policy changes.
5.
Nuanced oilseed balance: Abundant prospective oilseed supplies coexist with tighter palm oil balances, leaving prices highly sensitive to policy shifts and weather.
6.
Severe land constraints: Sourcing corn-based alcohol-to-jet fuels requires over nine acres of arable land for a single transcontinental US flight.

Maritime shipping and aviation economics

7.
Emerging compliance trading: Early maritime adopters of biofuels generate substantial FuelEU compliance surpluses, enabling commercial pooling and trading of excess credits.
8.
/ CSR reports Escalating maritime costs: Inclusion of shipping in the EU ETS and FuelEU Maritime introduces severe cost burdens that strain operators' cash flows.
9.
Severe fare inflation: A 10% SAF blend is projected to increase jet fuel costs by 20%, forcing airlines to pass carbon abatement costs to passengers.
10.
Compressed airline margins: Transitioning to SAF without corresponding supply-side support may materially increase airline operating costs and erode operating cash flows.
11.
Punitive private aviation: The European Commission proposes applying stricter EU ETS rules to departing non-commercial flights regardless of distance thresholds.

Non-consensus outcomes and outlier signals

12.
Disruptive insect lipids: Startup BugEra is genetically modifying flies to double their lipid content, offering cost-competitive alternatives to waste oils.
13.
Innovative plastic upcycling: Clean Planet Technologies launched the first pilot converting hard-to-recycle waste plastics into SAF to bypass lipid feedstock bottlenecks.
14.
Potential mandate dilution: Airlines have the motive to lobby against high costs, and governments facing cost-of-living pressures have the opportunity to dilute ambitious blending targets.
15.
Other side of the story: Sceptical analysts argue that instead of driving agriculture and aviation to green parity, high biofuel costs will simply trigger severe demand destruction across transport sectors, stalling the transition.
