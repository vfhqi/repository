Emerging combination threats: GlaxoSmithKline is developing its antibody-drug conjugate Blenrep in combination with BorDex as a potential treatment for relapsed multiple myeloma. If oncologists shift clinical preferences away from Darzalex regimens toward competitor combinations, Genmab's core royalty stream would face downward pressure.

This dual-payload technology represents a disruptive threat to conventional single-payload products like Tivdak, potentially intensifying market competition.

Regulatory Environment

13.
Heightened pricing pressure: United States policy reforms under the Inflation Reduction Act allowed Medicare to negotiate list price reductions ranging from 38% to 79% for selected high-volume drugs in late 2026. Major developers face expanding lists of negotiated therapies, which caps peak commercial revenue potential and limits price increases. The regulatory intervention indirectly impacts Genmab's long-term royalty potential as blockbuster oncology therapies eventually qualify for price negotiations.
14.
Restructured drug benefits: The Medicare Part D redesign shifts a substantial portion of financial liability from the federal government to plan administrators and drug manufacturers. This regulatory restructuring incentivizes insurance plans to enforce tighter formulary management and prioritize lower-cost generic alternatives. Expensive novel oncology treatments may face severe utilization restrictions unless developers present compelling real-world efficacy evidence.
15.
Constrained launch pricing: Pharma manufacturers are increasingly forced to evaluate regional price corridors and launch strategies in advance because of the rigid Inflation Reduction Act pricing framework. The legislation penalizes retroactive price adjustments, which limits executive commercial flexibility during subsequent international expansions. These global pricing constraints could squeeze early-stage launch margins, representing a mid-term risk to Genmab's product sales.
16.
Gradual regulatory streamlining: The United States Food and Drug Administration continues to utilize expedited development pathways, including Fast Track and Breakthrough Therapy designations, to accelerate oncology reviews. For instance, the regulator granted Breakthrough Therapy designation to zanidatamab, enabling rolling reviews of critical clinical sections. These expedited regulatory pathways help developers shorten drug development cycles, accelerating the timeline to market and subsequent royalty generation for Genmab.

Sourcing and Manufacturing

17.
Geopolitical supply reshuffle: The United States enacted the BIOSECURE Act on December 18, 2025, restricting federal contracts and procurements involving specified biotechnology companies of concern. This legislation drives global pharmaceutical companies to diversify their manufacturing partners away from China to secure supply chains. Non-Chinese contract development and manufacturing organizations stand to capture substantial outsourced volume, ensuring long-term supply safety for Genmab's pipelines.
18.
Constrained CDMO capacity: Contract development and manufacturing organizations require substantial initial capital expenditures, ranging from $500 million to $2 billion, to establish commercial-scale mammalian bioreactor capacity. Ramping up complex antibody production takes up to five years because of intensive quality validation and personnel training. Slower capacity expansion among major Western CDMOs could create near-term manufacturing bottlenecks, representing a clinical-scale risk for Genmab's late-stage candidates.
19.
Complex biologic manufacturing: Biopharmaceutical production demands advanced technical processes to synthesize multispecific antibodies and antibody-drug conjugates from production cell lines. Ramping up conjugation processes requires rigorous quality controls to ensure stable drug-to-antibody ratios and prevent batch failures.
