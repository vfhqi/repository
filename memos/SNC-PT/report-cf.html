Outliers worth noting 1 findings

Sonaecom maintains an extreme net cash position of EUR 246.6 million with zero gross debt at the holding company level in H1 2026, contrasting sharply with its primary operating asset NOS which carries net debt of EUR 1.58 billion for FY26E, creating an unusual structural mismatch where the parent exhibits defensive liquidity while the underlying telecom business operates with sector-typical leverage of approximately 2.0x.

Total board remuneration paid by Sonaecom reached just EUR 177,132 in 2025 with only three remunerated directors, representing an exceptionally depressed corporate overhead that sits multiple standard deviations below comparable European investment holding companies and reflects the complete absence of independent nonexecutive directors, a governance outlier that presents structural risks for minority investors given the 88.84% parent ownership concentration.

Sonaecom's reported H1 2026 EBITDA margin of 441.9% is structurally inflated because equity-method results from NOS are recognized within consolidated EBITDA, generating a statistically nonsensical profitability ratio that renders traditional margin peer comparisons meaningless and forces investors to deconstruct the consolidated financials to assess standalone holding company economics, where underlying EBITDA remained negative at EUR 5.8 million in H1 2026.

Bright Pixel's technology venture portfolio maintained an implied cash-oncash multiple of approximately 1.4x, representing a materially subdued return profile compared to traditional venture capital fund benchmarks of 2.5x to 3.0x at comparable lifecycle stages, raising questions about either conservative fair value marking practices or structural underperformance in capital deployment execution across the 50+ active company portfolio.

Sonaecom secured EUR 86.6 million in cash dividends from NOS in May 2026, consisting of a EUR 0.35 ordinary plus EUR 0.10 extraordinary per share payment, while the parent company distributed only EUR 25.4 million in dividends during H1 2026, creating a dividend retention rate of 70.7% that is unusually high for a pure holding company structure and suggests either latent capital deployment plans or conservative balance sheet management that diverges from typical investment holding dividend policies targeting 80% to 90% cash distribution ratios to ultimate shareholders.
