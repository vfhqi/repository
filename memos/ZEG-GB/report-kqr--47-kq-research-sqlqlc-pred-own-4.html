Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. The Key Question can be partly answered. The mathematical mechanics, accrued value, dividend and buyback adjustments, and broker dilution treatments are well documented in audited accounts and covering research, but critical gaps remain regarding board settlement discretion, exact redemption timing within the threeyear window, fifth calculation period baseline mechanics, and the interaction between operational turnaround execution and management redemption decisions. The fourth calculation period of Zegona Communications' Management Incentive Scheme has accrued to £412.31 million as of 31 March 2026, representing a substantial increase from the third cycle's £195 million payout settled through 55 million new shares. This accrued value is based on a baseline of £3.57135 per share established on 15 October 2024 and entitles management to 15% of value growth above a compounded 5% annual preferred return. The scheme's mathematical treatment of capital returns is unambiguous in company filings: dividends and buybacks are added directly to deemed market capitalisation to calculate total value created rather than reducing the baseline capitalisation, meaning the £1.23 billion special dividend and £121 million buyback both amplify the surplus available for management participation.
  2. The management incentive scheme is structurally critical to Zegona's investment thesis, driving deep founder alignment but presenting material dilution risks that could weigh heavily on future per-share earnings.

Settlement methodology represents the most significant uncertainty affecting future dilution. Historical precedent from the third cycle shows a 90% shares and 10% cash split, with the cash component designed to facilitate tax payments, yet covering brokers Morgan Stanley and Deutsche Bank now model the upcoming payout as 100% share-settled to avoid cash depletion and protect leverage ratios.

This difference matters profoundly: cash settlement of the estimated EUR 700 million incentive would severely pressure Zegona's balance sheet and target leverage ratios, while share settlement would dilute ordinary equity holders but preserve financial flexibility. The redemption window itself spans from 15 October 2027 to 15 October 2029, yet no public information clarifies what operational or market triggers would prompt management to redeem early versus late within this range. Broker treatments of dilution show methodological alignment but different emphasis. Morgan Stanley employs a DCF model with a 9% WACC and 0.5% terminal growth rate, arriving at a 2,200p target price, while Deutsche Bank maintains an identical 2,200p target using unlevered FCF yield as its preferred metric.

Both brokers explicitly incorporate dilution from share settlement in their models, with Morgan Stanley forecasting EPS of €0.51 in FY27, rising to €1.39 in FY28 and €1.64 in FY29, with the FY28 figure reflecting the dilution impact. Despite the material dilution headwind, investors broadly accept the scheme because it creates clear alignment with management to drive equity value growth. Post-redemption dynamics introduce another layer of structural complexity.

When the fourth calculation period ends, a new calculation period automatically begins with remaining shares retaining the incentive entitlement, with the baseline set at the higher of the 30-day VWAP or Net Shareholder Invested Capital. However, each new calculation period requires shareholder approval at the subsequent AGM, and if shareholders representing seventy-five percent or more vote against renewal, the management shares are redeemed for zero value.

Historical voting patterns show overwhelming support, with the fourth period ratified with 95% approval and the third with 98%, suggesting strong shareholder confidence in the alignment benefits despite dilution concerns. The analysis reveals critical information gaps that prevent full resolution of the Key Question. Undisclosed elements include employee-level allocation details, specific internal metrics guiding the board's cash versus share settlement choice, and precise individual tax rates dictating the cash portion.

Furthermore, operational triggers that would lead management to redeem early in October 2027 remain unspecified, and the prospective baseline calculation for the fifth period is unconfirmed until the fourth period is redeemed. These gaps matter because non-consensus resolution scenarios carry substantial ramifications: cash settlement would push leverage above the target 2.75x EBITDAaL, while share price underperformance could lead management to defer redemption beyond October 2029, locking them into the existing fourth period baseline.

Operational turnaround execution at Vodafone Spain creates asymmetric upside that could exponentially increase the accrued incentive value. Delivering on the RanCo mobile network joint venture unlocks up to EUR 150 million in annual synergies, while leaving Vantage Towers in 2028 could save EUR 50 million in rental fees. These operational improvements would drive share price appreciation above the baseline, compounding management's participation.

Conversely, rapid turnaround would pressure competitors MasOrange and Telefonica to defend market share, while price competition from Digi Spain continues to depress mobile and broadband ARPU. Consolidation scenarios introduce further complexity, as reports that Telefonica is exploring a buyout of Zegona could drive exponential valuation gains for management, though such a merger faces stringent European antitrust scrutiny.

The scheme's structural importance to Zegona's investment thesis cannot be overstated. The incentive plan gives management a 15% share of value growth, resulting in founders collectively holding about 23% ownership, creating what investors broadly agree is highly positive alignment despite the dilution. However, the potential dilution headwind directly threatens to suppress future EPS development and reduces projected per-share returns. This tension between alignment and dilution defines the core investment debate around Zegona: whether the operational value creation enabled by the incentive structure sufficiently exceeds the dilutive impact on ordinary shareholders. Current evidence suggests the alignment benefits have historically prevailed, with the third cycle creating £1.5 billion of shareholder value, but the fourth cycle's substantially larger accrued value and uncertain settlement method introduce heightened execution risk.

The analysis establishes that while the mathematical mechanics and current accrued value are precisely quantified in audited accounts, the forward-looking elements governing settlement, redemption timing, and postredemption dynamics remain subject to board discretion, market conditions, and operational execution. These uncertainties prevent full resolution of the Key Question but do not impair the ability to model base case, bull case, and bear case scenarios with reasonable confidence bands around dilution impacts and EPS trajectories through 2029.
