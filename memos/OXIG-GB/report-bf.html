EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF) 1 findings

Oxford Instruments operates a two-division structure generating 74% of revenue and 96% of profit from highmargin Imaging & Analysis ( 24%+ operating margins ), while Advanced Technologies faces 2.6% margins due to cleanroom depreciation. The company successfully de-emphasized China exposure to 22% of orders while driving 32.2% North American revenue growth and shifting 58% of sales toward commercial customers. Management achieved 90% platform standardization across plasma systems, delivering a 40% assembly time reduction, offsetting cyclical academic research softness with robust commercial demand momentum.

Oxford Instruments sustains exceptional capital returns with 27.1% ROCE and 20.3% ROIC in FY26, supported by 89% operational cash conversion and a debt-free balance sheet holding £94 million net cash. The company occupies profit-rich metrology and scientific imaging chokepoints commanding 55-60% gross margins in commercial R&D and 22-25% EBIT margins, substantially outperforming volume capital equipment peers. However, the capital-intensive Advanced Technologies segment suffered a margin collapse to 2.6% in FY26, weighed down by £2.5 million elevated Severn Beach depreciation despite tripling production capacity.

Oxford commands impregnable customer lock-in via Process of Record status, proprietary processing recipes, and 12-18 month qualification barriers that render supplier switching functionally impossible in commercial semiconductor fabrication. Service revenue expanded to 18.8% of group sales, with plasma service orders surging 34% year-on-year as high-volume manufacturers demand 24/7 premium uptime contracts worth up to €1.4 million. The state-of-the-art Severn Beach facility tripled compound semiconductor capacity and delivered 60% labor productivity gains in Belfast cameras through decisive workforce restructuring and product line rationalization.

Strategic reference partnerships with Coherent Corp on 6-inch indium phosphide fab ramps and multiple fully automated system orders from major US chipmakers validate Oxford's transition from academic niche to volume commercial production supplier. Semiconductor process control metrology commands high pricing leverage due to stringent qualification barriers and extreme customer risk aversion to yield-disrupting tool changes, insulating margins from competitive pressure. Engineering teams successfully designed out heavy rare earth requirements and expanded alternative material sourcing, fully resolving supply chain bottlenecks and enabling robust H2 shipment acceleration.

Cross-value-chain signals confirm commercial semiconductor demand strength offsetting academic funding delays, with Coherent Corp partnership on indium phosphide and post-FY26 long-term purchase agreements signaling sustained volume manufacturing traction. Divesting the low-margin NanoScience business for £60 million at 60x EBITA structurally removes historical Advanced Technologies drag and adds 100 basis points to pro-forma group operating margins. Platform standardization and anticipated high-volume commercial deliveries underpin management's 12-15% Advanced Technologies margin target, representing a 4-5x expansion from current trough levels.

The dominant structural risk remains customer facility delays halting expensive tool shipments, creating severe revenue lumpiness and leaving the newly expanded Severn Beach cleanroom under-utilized during prolonged cyclical downturns in data center capex. Both major production facilities face complex, high-risk ERP system upgrades over the next 24 months, threatening operational disruptions that could delay finished goods shipments and compress near-term cash conversion.

1.
Bottom-line verdict: Oxford Instruments demonstrates strong foundation quality driven by impregnable Process of Record switching costs, world-class cleanroom validation leverage, and exceptional 20%+ ROIC returns, structurally insulated by proprietary recipe lock-in and 12-18 month customer qualification barriers that render competitive displacement functionally impossible in high-value semiconductor metrology and commercial R&D applications
