Section 1 – Brief company description and history

Section summary
  1. Sell-side analysts held highly conservative expectations for SCHOTT Pharma prior to the second-quarter results due to persistent polymer syringe weakness and a critical customer-specific volume reduction.
  2. The market prioritized clarity regarding the resolution of the glass syringe customer dispute and the utilization rates of newly installed capacity.
1.
Established global leader: SCHOTT Pharma serves as a leading provider of high-quality drug containment solutions and drug delivery systems for injectable medicines. The company supplies critical packaging such as vials, ampoules, cartridges, and pre-fillable syringes to the top 30 global pharmaceutical manufacturers. Deep integration across the sector makes the business a highly trusted partner within healthcare.

2.
Deep historical roots: The company trace its glass technology legacy back to Otto Schott and Carl Zeiss who founded the original laboratory in 1884. The business launched its standard-setting borosilicate glass pharmaceutical packaging in 1911. The carve-out of SCHOTT Pharma was finalized in 2022 ahead of its September 2023 public listing.
3.
Controlled governance structure: The Carl Zeiss Foundation maintains full indirect ownership of the parent organization SCHOTT AG. A partnership limited by shares structure allows SCHOTT AG to retain strategic control while admitting public equity investors. Independent board representation ensures that minority shareholders remain protected under this setup.
4.
Resilient operational segmentation: SCHOTT Pharma operates two distinct divisions consisting of Drug Containment Solutions and Drug Delivery Systems. The drug containment business specializes in high-volume vials, cartridges, and ampoules. Glass and polymer prefillable syringes constitute the core drug delivery solutions offered.
5.
Steady financial expansion: SCHOTT Pharma expanded its annual revenues from €821 million in fiscal year 2022 to €957 million in fiscal year 2024. The top-line performance expanded further to reach €986 million in fiscal year 2025. Expanding sales of high-value solutions underpinned this solid growth trajectory during the historical period.
6.
Lagging relative scale: SCHOTT Pharma generated lower absolute revenues than some of its primary packaging sector competitors. Peer organization Gerresheimer recorded total revenues of €2.036 billion in fiscal year 2024. Similarly, competitor Stevanato Group delivered revenues of €1.186 billion in fiscal year 2025.
7.
Strong relative profitability: SCHOTT Pharma achieved a strong EBITDA margin of 28.4% in fiscal year 2025. Stevanato Group reported an adjusted EBITDA margin of 25.1% during the same fiscal year. Higher profitability levels supported superior return on capital for the company.
8.
Extreme valuation pressure: SCHOTT Pharma experienced a severe share price contraction of 35.1% over the 12- month period leading up to February 2026. The absolute decline widened to 43.8% for the 12 months ending in March 2026. Persistent destocking dynamics and declining investor confidence dragged down the company's valuation.
9.
Underperforming broader equity: The market valuation of the company diverged significantly from the broader European equity benchmark. The STOXX Europe index gained 6.2% over the 12-month period ending in March 2026. Severe underperformance during this timeframe left the stock trading at discounts to peers.
10.
Disappointing target reductions: Deutsche Bank downgraded the company to Hold in December 2025 while reducing its target price to €19 from €29. The downgrade followed a disappointing reduction in medium-term growth guidance to a 6% to 8% range. Analysts cut consensus earnings expectations because of sudden weakness in glass syringes.
11.
Continued broker caution: Deutsche Bank lowered its price target further to €16 from €19 in February 2026. The revision reflected a lower target P/E multiple of 15x to account for unreliable target delivery. Unresolved leadership changes and extremely low trading liquidity remained key overhangs.
12.
Persistent sell-side discount: Jefferies maintained a Hold rating on the stock with a target price of €17.30 in May 2026. Near-term operational headwinds and customer-specific glass syringe hurdles overshadowed the company's long-term growth potential. A highly conservative terminal valuation multiple reflected ongoing visibility concerns.
13.
Other side of the story: The bearish sentiment preceding the Q2 2026 results could have been overly pessimistic given the underlying structural shift toward high-value ready-to-use solutions. Expanding GLP-1 drug delivery demand and capacity additions at new sites represents powerful long-term growth engines. Early stabilization of vial demand suggests that the destocking cycle is approaching its end.
