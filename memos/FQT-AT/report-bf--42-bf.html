Appendix

Plausible problems to monitor

Outliers worth noting

Frequentis operates a highly unusual business model anchored by exceptionally high recurring maintenance revenues, with 54% of total revenues derived from Installed Base Business in fiscal 2025, of which approximately 40% consists of long-term maintenance contracts. The company maintains market leadership with a 30% global share in safety-critical air traffic control voice systems, yet this defensive installed base concentration appears statistically extreme when compared to typical systems integration peers.

Peer defense systems integrators like Thales and L3Harris typically derive a lower proportion of revenues from pure maintenance streams, instead generating revenue from broader military platform portfolios. This outlier revenue structure creates superior cash flow predictability but simultaneously exposes the group to acute risks if sovereign clients consolidate maintenance in-house or migrate to cloud-native platforms that reduce physical hardware servicing needs.

The maintenance-heavy model has not been fully reconciled with the stated strategic shift toward pure software license delivery, raising questions about whether the group can sustain its 54% installed base share as cloud migrations accelerate [Cross-ref: Section 1, Section 4].

Frequentis exhibits a severe working capital anomaly, with trade accounts receivable surging 80.6% to EUR 144.7 million in fiscal 2025 alongside a 57.4% inventory buildup to EUR 51.8 million. In stark contrast, peer Indra Sistemas achieved a remarkable negative 124 working capital days of sales in March 2026, aided by receivables falling to negative 173 days from customer prepayments. Frequentis' working capital days of approximately 72 days place it in the bottom quartile of comparable defense systems integrators, suggesting the company holds materially weaker billing and cash collection leverage with sovereign clients.

Management attributes the receivables inflation to milestone payment delays and cost-to-cost revenue recognition practices, yet the widening gap versus peers indicates Frequentis may lack the negotiating power to secure the prepayment structures that competitors command. This working capital outlier represents a hidden financing cost and signals potential sovereign customer leverage that could compress future pricing power and free cash flow conversion [Cross-ref: Section 2, Section 8].
