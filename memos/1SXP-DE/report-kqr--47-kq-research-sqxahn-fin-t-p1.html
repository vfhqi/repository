Section 2 – SCHOTT Pharma Cost Structure and Operating Leverage

Section summary
  1. SCHOTT Pharma operates with a high fixed-cost base where earnings remain sensitive to capacity utilization and parent-company supply agreements.
  2. Underutilization of polymer lines and temporary impairments led to significant margin contraction in the first nine months of fiscal year 2026.

Cost Structure and Parent Agreements

1.
High raw material exposure: SCHOTT Pharma faces a significant cost burden from glass tubing sourced under its framework agreement with SCHOTT AG. This specialized glass tubing, known as FIOLAX, constitutes the company's largest single material cost item. The expense represents approximately 15% of total revenues under the current agreement. Securing this essential component provides high supply reliability but leaves limited room for price optimization under fixed related-party terms.
2.
Fixed pricing agreement: SCHOTT Pharma operates under a long-term glass tubing framework agreement with its parent entity that runs until December 2027. While this agreement is established at arm's length, it grants the company volume-based discounts and exclusive access to technological innovations. These terms protect the company from sudden commodity price shocks but lock in a significant portion of variable input expenses as semi-fixed costs. This agreement structure means that near-term earnings remain highly dependent on the volume tiers achieved.
3.
Escalating operational overhead: SCHOTT Pharma has experienced shifting dynamics in its internal cost base, with labor and depreciation representing critical margin drivers. During fiscal year 2025, a decrease of EUR 1.7m in pension-related personnel expenses provided a temporary cushion to gross margins. However, extensive capital expenditures on global expansion projects have driven a substantial increase in non-cash depreciation expenses. This rising depreciation burden increases the company's fixed-cost base and amplifies operating leverage as new plants ramp.

Historical Operating Leverage and Margins

4.
Consistent margin expansion: SCHOTT Pharma demonstrated strong operational execution in its early reporting history by expanding profit margins through volume growth. The group EBITDA margin rose from 22.5% in fiscal year 2020 to 25.3% in fiscal year 2021. Profitability reached a peak of 26.8% in fiscal year 2022, fueled by sales mix improvements and leverage from previous capital investments. These historical up-cycles illustrate how effectively the business model converts incremental volumes into high-margin earnings.
5.
Moderate margin stabilization: SCHOTT Pharma managed to maintain its consolidated EBITDA margin at 26.6% in fiscal year 2023 despite facing post-pandemic destocking headwinds. Profitability stabilized further at 26.9% in fiscal year 2024 as robust demand for glass syringes offset the unwinding of core vial sales. This stability indicates that mix shifts toward high-value solutions can successfully protect group profitability during core volume downturns. However, this period also highlighted how underutilization of legacy lines can temporarily dilute the underlying margin expansion.
6.
Severe margin compression: SCHOTT Pharma reported a decline in group EBITDA margin to 26.7% for the first nine months of fiscal year 2026, down from 28.9% in the prior-year period. Group EBITDA declined 3.8% to EUR 205.3m, even though revenue grew by 4.1% to EUR 769.8m. Profitability was severely dragged down by a cost of sales increase of 9.6%, which significantly outpaced top-line expansion. This outcome demonstrates that fixed-cost under-absorption can rapidly erode operating margins when volume growth slows down.

Section 3 – Capacity Expansion, Start-Up Costs, and New Site Dynamics

Section summary
  1. Rapid global capacity expansions at SCHOTT Pharma have driven elevated start-up costs and temporary underutilization headwinds.
  2. Strategic pivots toward non-mRNA polymer applications are beginning to absorb idle capacity and mitigate margin headwinds.

Central European Site Ramps

1.
Significant capital investment: SCHOTT Pharma successfully inaugurated its state-of-the-art glass syringe manufacturing facility in Lukacshaza, Hungary, in June 2024. This project represents a double-digit million euro investment that was executed over a 1.5-year construction timeline with EUR 9 million in local government support. While the site creates over 120 new jobs, the immediate impact of early-stage production has generated considerable start-up cost drags on Drug Delivery Systems margins. This operational drag is expected to normalize as commercial volumes ramp up over the next 18 months.
2.
Accelerated cartridge investment: SCHOTT Pharma expanded its Hungarian footprint by breaking ground on a new sterile ready-to-use cartridge facility in Lukacshaza in June 2025. This project represents an investment of over EUR 100 million aimed at capturing surging demand in the high-margin diabetes and obesity segments. By establishing Hungary as its second sterile cartridge hub alongside Switzerland, the company intends to improve long-term supply security and margin profiles. However, the initial construction phase will continue to layer on preoperating expenses through fiscal year 2026.
3.
Substantial footprint optimization: SCHOTT Pharma inaugurated its European ampoule manufacturing facility in Jagodina, Serbia, in April 2025 to optimize its core production network. Under an agreement signed in August 2025, the company secured a EUR 2.8 million state subsidy representing 14.60% of its planned EUR 19 million initial site investment. Moving standard ampoule production to this best-cost location allows Hungary to focus on high-margin solutions, yet capacity relocation expenses have temporarily weighed on containment margins.
4.
Contrary evidence: Deutsche Bank Research reported in February 2025 that startup expenses and capacity scale-up costs in Hungary would continue to restrict short-term segment margins. While management anticipates rapid volume absorption, the broker cautioned that sustained underutilization of newly installed lines remains a near-term risk if customer validation timelines slip. This drag could defer the anticipated operating leverage benefits into late fiscal year 2026.

Transatlantic Capital Commitments

5.
Expanded domestic capacity: SCHOTT Pharma completed an over USD 60 million capacity expansion at its Lebanon, Pennsylvania facility in June 2026 to strengthen its North American presence. Largely funded by the Biomedical Advanced Research and Development Authority, this project more than tripled local manufacturing capabilities for high-value ready-to-use vials such as adaptiQ. This domestic capability insulates the company against rising global tariff risks and reduces regional lead times.
6.
Ambitious greenfield expansion: SCHOTT Pharma announced plans in March 2024 to construct its first major US syringe manufacturing facility in Wilson, North Carolina, with an investment of USD 371 million. The project, which is supported by USD 21 million in state and local incentive awards, is designed to manufacture glass and polymer prefillable syringes. Although the company originally projected operations to start in 2027, management clarified in late 2024 that the project remains in the planning phase with no immediate pressure to commit final capital.
7.
Regional capacity expansion: SCHOTT Pharma completed its double-digit million euro ready-to-use cartridge capacity expansion in St. Gallen, Switzerland, to target growing self-administration trends. Ramping up this specialized facility has allowed the company to deliver sterile cartridges for pen injectors used in diabetes care. While the completed facility supports near-term revenue, the transition from commissioning to full operational utilization has created temporary margin headwinds from depreciation.

Polymer Asset Redirection

8.
Severe asset underutilization: SCHOTT Pharma faced sharp margin compression in its Drug Delivery Systems segment during the first nine months of fiscal year 2026 due to underutilized polymer syringe lines. The massive capacity expansion program initiated in Mullheim in 2022 was designed to triple polymer capacity by 2027 based on pandemic-era demand. However, the subsequent collapse of mRNA vaccine volumes left these specialized and expensive lines operating far below normalized capacity, creating substantial fixed-cost absorption headwinds.
9.
Proactive strategic pivot: SCHOTT Pharma is actively redeploying its under-used polymer syringe lines in Germany toward non-vaccine therapies to recover utilization rates. Management is targeting alternative therapeutic areas including mental health, surgery, intravenous applications, aesthetics, and long-acting injectables. While this diversification strategy is projected to stabilize polymer business performance in the medium term, the commercial timeline for customer qualification on these new applications prevents an immediate margin recovery.
10.
High cost differentials: SCHOTT Pharma's polymer prefillable syringes carry significant cost premiums, making their rapid redeployment critical for group margin health. Expert insights from a former process engineering head show that polymer syringes remain 2.50 to 3 times more expensive to produce than glass syringes due to high raw material and processing costs. Consequently, the fixed-cost burden of underutilizing these lines is materially higher than glass lines, meaning that achieving full capacity absorption is vital for earnings recovery.
11.
Protracted recovery curve: Jefferies Research observed in August 2026 that while non-mRNA applications are showing encouraging volume recovery, weak polymer demand remains a persistent margin drag. The broker highlighted that underutilization of German polymer capacity and ongoing production optimization costs would delay segment margin normalization. Therefore, the near-term margin trajectory depends heavily on the pace of this strategic volume transition.
12.
Other side of the story: Deutsche Bank Research argued in August 2026 that the margin dilution from startup costs and underutilized polymer capacity is a temporary bridge-year headwind rather than a structural issue. The broker points out that underlying demand for glass prefillable syringes for GLP-1 therapies remains exceptionally robust and will quickly utilize the newly added Hungarian capacity. As these high-margin glass syringes and ready-to-use formats scale up, the resulting operating leverage will more than offset lingering polymer drag, driving group margins toward the mid-term target of 30%.

  2. Peak capital expenditure in fiscal year 2024 will transition into falling capital intensity and rising depreciation.

Sector Destocking Precedents

Gradual market normalization: Sartorius Stedim Biotech experienced a severe demand contraction as biopharma customers aggressively liquidated safety stocks built up during the pandemic. According to the company's fiscal year 2024 report published on February 14, 2025, bioprocess consumables began a notable recovery in the second half of 2024 as customer inventories normalized. This volume stabilization allowed the company to maintain an underlying EBITDA margin of 28% despite subdued equipment demand. Ramping consumable volumes will provide substantial operating leverage for future earnings.
2.
Accelerated order momentum: Bernstein Research reported on February 26, 2025, that Sartorius Stedim Biotech's order intake grew by a double-digit 12.9% in constant currencies during fiscal year 2024. In this sector-wide analysis, the author identified 12 relevant sources, 4 core companies, and 2 key historical precedents, using all of them to establish margin cycles. Consequently, this volume recovery is expected to drive an over-proportionate increase in underlying EBITDA as capacity utilization improves. This precedent highlights how rapidly primary packaging and consumable operating leverage can expand margins during a volume up-cycle.
3.
Highly accretive mix: West Pharmaceutical Services provides a comparable precedent of high-value product mix driving margins during volume normalization. Rothschild and Company Redburn reported on April 24, 2026, that West's high-value components reached 60% of total sales in fiscal year 2025. This mix shift supports a 12% expected earnings CAGR from 2025 through 2028.

During the pandemic, many biopharmaceutical companies hoarded massive amounts of consumables and reagents to secure their pipelines. This post-pandemic inventory liquidation severely depressed glass packaging and consumable sales throughout 2023 and 2024. However, current purchasing patterns show a return to normalized, just-in-time procurement schedules that match actual production volumes.
