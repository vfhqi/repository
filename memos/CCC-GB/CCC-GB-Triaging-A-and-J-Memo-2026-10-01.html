[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = C

Thesis. Computacenter is a crisp 'Demand Inflection' in a well-run but structurally low-margin reseller, and at 5,470p the market has already paid for the inflection. The AI data-centre wave has travelled every layer of the chain into a near-doubling of half-year profit and a £9.3bn committed backlog; what is left for a buyer today is about 8% a year of earnings growth after the FY2026 step, a yield under 2% and a multiple at the 93rd percentile of its own range that is more likely to fall than rise, so the 'Modal return' is about 1% a year over eighteen months against the 20% bar. The case becomes investable at about 4,240p, not at 5,470p.

  • The change is external, sector-level and as strong as any force in the universe.
    • Hyperscaler AI infrastructure capital expenditure is projected at $1.5tr in 2027 by Morgan Stanley, Nvidia's supply shortfall is put at about $100bn, and the demand has shown up in the company's own numbers rather than in a story.
    • The committed product order backlog was £9.3bn at 30-Jun-26, up 323.2% year on year in constant currency, from £7.1bn at end-FY2025 and about £2bn at end-FY2024
      • It is about a year of FY2025 revenue (£9,193.9m) and runs into FY2027.
    • North America was 62% of adjusted operating profit in H1 2026 against 44% a year before
      • Technology Sourcing grew 79.1% organically in constant currency, driven by hyperscaler and neocloud customers.
    • The company's own cycle has turned hard: revenue growth of -13.4% in H1 2024, +15.7% in H2 2024, +28.5% in H1 2025, +34.8% in H2 2025 and +71.6% in H1 2026.
  • The internal change is the company doing what it has always done, a little faster.
    • The chief executive has been in post since December 1994, the strategy is unchanged, and the internal forces are capacity investment, two bolt-on acquisitions, guidance raised twice in the year and a pruning of loss-making services
    • Competent, ordinary, and ready when the demand arrived.
    • Capital expenditure of £70m to £75m in 2026, the £32m automated Atlanta centre opening mid-2027 and £4m of liquid-cooling capacity at Hatfield completing in 2026 are physical and dated.
    • AgreeYa (January 2026, up to $120m, about 8.5x EBITDA) and GAI (closed 1-Jun-26, $92m, 11.5x EBITDA) are small against a £5.75bn market capitalisation and open offshore delivery and the US federal market.
  • 'Financial Outputs' have moved decisively, and two of them have moved the wrong way as the price of growth.
    • H1 2026 revenue rose 71.6% to £6,845.2m, adjusted operating profit 86.5% to £153.1m and adjusted diluted EPS 94.1% to 101.9p
    • FY2026 adjusted pre-tax profit guidance was raised to no less than £380m against a prior company-compiled consensus of £340.9m
    • Consensus FY2026 EPS has risen 41.7% in twelve months to 2.58 GBP and every one of the eight visible brokers raised in the ten days after the results.
    • Group gross margin fell 303 basis points to 9.6% and the operating margin on revenue fell to 2.2%, because management chose absolute gross profit over percentage margin
      • Adjusted operating profit was 23.3% of gross profit against a target above 30%.
    • Cash conversion turned negative: a net operating cash outflow of £82.6m in the half, inventory of £1.26bn against £316.8m a year before, and adjusted net funds down to £308.7m from £606.0m at end-FY2025 after £124.2m of acquisition spend.
  • The change has travelled all six layers of the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain, and a seventh step into the share price.
    • It shows in the company's resources (integration centres, vendor tiering, balance sheet), its choices (the US pivot, margin for volume), its actions (Atlanta, Hatfield, the acquisitions), its key operating metrics (the backlog), its financial statements (the half) and the sell-side's estimates (every broker up).
    • The shares have risen 98.8% in twelve months to 5,470p, 18.9 times consensus earnings twenty-four months forward.
  • What we think it earns.
    • Adjusted EPS (brokers' basis) of 2.60 GBP in FY2026, 2.85 GBP in FY2027, 3.05 GBP in FY2028 and 3.30 GBP in FY2029 on our modal path, a step of about 48% in FY2026 and then about 8% a year, as hyperscaler volumes plateau at a high level, the traditional business grows at a high single digit, US services and the Atlanta centre add, and gross-profit conversion edges from 24% toward 27%.
    • This is the Investment Analyst's own estimate standing in for a Viewforth model.
    • Consensus is +47.3% in FY2026, +7.4% in FY2027 and +4.3% in FY2028
      • Our path sits a little above it at FY2027 and FY2028 (285p and 305p against 277p and 289p median).
  • What it costs is the whole of the return. 5,470p is 18.9 times consensus earnings twenty-four months forward (289.4p) and 18.2 times our own (300p)
    • The financials block's series puts the multiple at 18.71x at 2026-09-25, the 93rd percentile of five years (median 13.82x; 25th to 75th percentile 12.30x to 15.00x) and above the ten-year 'defendable' band of 17.40x.
    • The comparison set's median is about 14.1x and the only peer near this multiple is Softcat at 18.1x.
  • The return does not clear the bar on any path we find plausible.
    • On a multiple that blends a held premium (18.0x, about 40% odds) with reversion toward the ten-year prudent-to-defendable range (15.0x, about 60%), 16.2x on earnings two years beyond each horizon, the value including dividends is 5,572p at eighteen months and 6,370p at three years: about 1% a year and about 5% a year against the 20% bar.
    • Even at the conceded 17.40x 'defendable' band the eighteen-month return is about 6% a year
      • At the modal branch's 15.0x alone it is negative.
    • On a plausibly worse drop-through from EBITDA to EPS (margin flat at 3.5%, higher depreciation, a 32% tax rate) the eighteen-month return is about -6% a year and the three-year about 1% a year.
  • The pattern is 'Demand Inflection', and it fits only moderately crisply.
    • The mandatory condition, a market-wide demand explosion, is met as clearly as it could be
    • The conditions that would make the inflection compound (a dominant position, one-to-many scalability, an oligopoly, fixed-cost leverage into margin) are met weakly or not at all, because the business is an agent on cost-plus terms in a fragmented industry whose customers and suppliers hold the power.
    • So the inflection reaches earnings as a one-year step at a falling margin rather than as a trajectory.
  • Where the chain holds and where it breaks.
    • It holds from the external force through the backlog into the half's profit and the sell-side's estimates
    • It breaks at the last link, the return, because the price moved first, and it is fragile at one point along the way: one North American customer was nearly 30% of FY2025 revenue and 85% of FY2025 US revenue growth, on contract terms the company does not disclose.

  • The case therefore is a real inflection in a real business, bought by the market before this record was written: bullish on the business, neutral on the stock, bearish on the price

    • A park with an entry level.

  • Rating = C, because the change is material to the company and the chain it has travelled is complete, but the Building Block's question ends at the return, and the 'Modal return' is an F: 'Change Forces' B and 'Financial Outputs' B have already run through the price and leave about 1% a year over eighteen months and 5% a year over three.

    • D was live and we considered it, because a return of a third of the bar argues D
    • We rate C because materiality is the measure, the first two links are genuinely in the top third of the universe, and a price 22% lower lifts this Building Block to a B without any change to the research.
    • Not B, because the return is the point of the Building Block.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The counter-case at its strongest: the H1 2026 numbers are the top of a hyperscaler capital-expenditure cycle whose participants now spend more than their operating cash flow and whose next year may stall after a doubling in 2026, on Jefferies' own semiconductor team's warning
      • 85% of FY2025 US revenue growth came from one client and the largest North American customer was £2,731.2m, nearly 30% of FY2025 group revenue
      • The group bought that growth with its margin (gross margin down 303 basis points to 9.6%) and its balance sheet (inventory £1.26bn from £316.8m; operating cash outflow £82.6m)
      • If that one client pauses, revenue, margin and cash reverse together, the market reverts to the historical 12x to 14x on lower earnings and the shares fall 30% to 40% from 5,470p.
      • The pre-results bear case of 1,550p at 8.0x on 161.6p shows how far this stock has been valued at the bottom of a cycle.
    • What we concede: the concentration, undisclosed and large
      • The margin direction
      • The shape of the cash flow
      • And that the return from here is poor even if none of this happens.
    • What we do not concede: that the case is a false friend in the sense the pre-results Risks report meant, services decay masked by sourcing volume.
      • The H1 2026 outcome was a near-doubling of adjusted operating profit with North American services margins up 1,888 basis points, and the services line that was decaying (UK Managed Services, hosting contracts) is being exited deliberately.
      • The inventory is customer-backed and the H1 2025 outflow of £165.8m reversed into FY2025 free cash flow of £206.9m.
    • Our view: the forces are real and the outputs are real
      • The price is the problem, and we say so in the rating and the entry level rather than in the thesis.
Change Forces: are the input change forces sufficiently strong? (E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because one external force, sector-level AI infrastructure demand, is as strong as any in the universe and has been evidenced in the company's own backlog, while the internal forces are a competent but ordinary set (capacity investment, two bolt-on acquisitions, guidance raised twice, portfolio pruning) with no chief-executive change and no step-change in strategy.
  • Parent and children: the external attribute is an A and the internal a C, and the Element is a B by judgement rather than average, because in a hardware-sourcing business a demand force of this size carries the internal forces with it: the company's job was to have the capacity, the vendor tiering and the balance sheet ready when the demand arrived, and it did.
  • Not A, because a single-force, single-end-market change with one dominant customer is not the breadth the top decile of the universe shows
    • Not C, because the force has already produced a doubling of half-year profit, which is not what a C force does.
External forces: are there sufficiently strong external forces acting on the company, new or intensifying, and in which direction? (RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = A
  • Rating = A, because the demand force meets every part of the framework's 'Demand Inflection' test: it is sector-level (hyperscaler capital expenditure projected at $1.5tr in 2027 by Morgan Stanley), structural or cyclical with two-plus years' visibility (a £9.3bn committed backlog at 30-Jun-26 against FY2025 revenue of £9,193.9m), and evidenced by industry data (Dell's AI-optimised server growth of 757%; Nvidia's estimated $100bn supply shortfall) and by sector-wide order books (Bechtle's record €3.5bn backlog).
  • Direction by force: demand, a strong tailwind
    • Competition, mildly tightening (Softcat's $1.05bn purchase of GDT on 17-Sep-26)
    • Disruption, two-sided (software agency models erode reselling, AI complexity favours integrators)
    • Political and regulatory, a tailwind in Europe (German procurement reform of 14-Feb-26; the EU sovereignty package of 3-Jun-26)
    • Supply side, a headwind (memory and GPU shortages, OEM rebate tightening)
    • The company's own cycle, turning up hard
    • Macro, mixed (rates rising, the dollar strong).
  • Not B, because the B cases in the universe have a tailwind
    • This company has the tailwind of the moment, visible in a backlog four times the prior year's.
