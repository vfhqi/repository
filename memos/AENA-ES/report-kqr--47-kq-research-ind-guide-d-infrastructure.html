Section 3 – Share Price Drivers and Cyclical Dynamics

Section summary
  1. Transport concessions are highly geared to volume recovery and political tariff reviews whereas telecom towers trade as pure long-duration bond proxies.
  2. High leverage and refinancing timelines dominate market attention today as sovereign yields reset valuation buffers and discount rates across both infrastructure sectors.

Operational Yield and Demand Drivers

1.
Fluctuating volume risk: Transport concession revenues are tightly coupled with economic activity, where airport passenger traffic historically grows at twice the rate of real gross domestic product.
2.
Capped terminal throughput: Europe holds over half of the world's most capacity-constrained Level 3 airports, meaning terminal bottlenecks limit long-term volume expansion until massive capital expenditure is deployed.
3.
Predictable tenancy expansion: Telecom tower revenues depend on tenancy additions rather than wireless traffic, with site growth driven by mandatory 5G coverage requirements where tenancy ratios remain stable.
4.
Divergent inflation protection: Transport tariffs fully adjust to consumer price changes, while tower contracts face capped escalators that limit protection if inflation exceeds average caps.

Cost and Yield Sensitivities

5.
Severe yield sensitivity: Rising sovereign bond yields directly de-rate tower companies by increasing WACCs, causing a nineteen percent target price reduction for every 100 basis point rate increase.
6.
Protected concession financing: Transport concession project finance is structured as fully amortizing debt, which insulates equity cash flows from near-term refinancing shocks.
7.
Irrelevant accounting multiples: Traditional price-to-earnings ratios fail to reflect transport concession values due to finite asset lifespans, requiring discounted cash flow models to properly assess asset IRRs.
8.
Robust opex insulation: Industrial contractors and concessions maintain strong pass-through pricing mechanisms, which shield operating margins from volatile energy and fuel inflation.

Cyclical Turns and Market Sentiment

9.
Strained lease negotiations: Stretched mobile network operator margins lead to aggressive contract renegotiations, unwinding historical tower premiums as anchor tenants challenge pricing structures.
10.
Depressed transport valuations: Political risks and fiscal uncertainty drag French concessions down to empirical crisis-level troughs, creating substantial valuation discounts to net asset value.
11.
Refinancing risk focus: High leverage remains the market's primary focus today, with de-leveraging to less than six times EBITDAaL seen as essential to attract conservative capital.
12.
Other side of the story: Other side of the story: It is highly probable that if inflation remains structurally subdued, the defensive inflation indexation of both sectors will fail to offset rising asset maintenance capex as real cash yields compress.
