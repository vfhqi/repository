Section 2 – INDUS Holding's Decentralized Trade Tax Structure

Section summary
  1. INDUS Holding AG’s decentralized municipal trade tax structure prevents the offsetting of losses between its limited partnerships, creating a structural tax penalty.
  2. This tax asymmetry added EUR 5.98m of additional tax expense in FY2025, although total tax expenses were offset by a EUR 8.36m non-recurring SMA credit.
  3. Standard German corporate groups utilize tax consolidation contracts to offset losses, but INDUS's unique model leaves individual partnerships exposed to localized earnings drag.

Decentralized Trade Tax Architecture

1.
Autonomous partnership taxation: INDUS Holding AG acquires subsidiaries structured primarily as German limited partnerships, which isolates trade tax liabilities. The company's annual report of 24 March 2026 confirms municipal trade tax is assessed at the individual GmbH & Co. KG level. This decentralized setup means profits and losses cannot be consolidated across partnerships, creating a permanent structural tax friction that limits overall group profit margins. Consequently, underperforming entities directly increase the group's effective tax rate and drag down consolidated earnings per share during cyclical periods.
2.
Asymmetric loss offsetting: Offsetting tax gains and losses between different limited partnerships is prohibited under German trade tax law. INDUS Holding AG discloses in its 2025 annual report that taxable income from these partnerships is ascribed to the holding level and then subjected to corporate income tax. This flow-through structure ensures that while profitable entities generate taxable corporate income, loss-making partnerships cannot transfer their losses to offset those gains. The resulting asymmetry increases cash tax outflows and depresses overall net income margins relative to consolidated peers.
3.
Organschaft compared: Standard consolidated corporate groups in Germany utilize Organschaft tax group contracts to pool profits and losses across subsidiaries. INDUS Holding AG's 2025 annual report indicates that such tax group contracts exist only with its stock corporations to a limited extent. This structural choice isolates the limited partnerships, preventing them from achieving the tax efficiency of a standard consolidated group. This limitation means that INDUS’s decentralized business model inherently tolerates higher tax leakage than a typical German corporate group.
4.
Contrary evidence: The value added from purchase price allocations for tax purposes is deductible as depreciation from supplementary tax statements. This tax depreciation reduces the trade tax assessment base at the limited partnerships, offsetting some of the structural trade tax penalty. INDUS Holding AG's 2025 annual report notes this mechanism can result in meaningful tax savings even for highly profitable subsidiaries. Therefore, the decentralized structure is not entirely disadvantageous for partnerships because localized tax depreciation shields operating earnings from municipal taxes.

Quantifying the Tax Penalty

5.
Baseline penalty quantification: The structural tax penalty from the non- offsetting of losses at autonomous subsidiaries remains a persistent drag on consolidated earnings. INDUS Holding AG’s 2025 annual report reveals this structural effect added EUR 5.98m to tax expenses in FY2025, compared to EUR 7.11m in FY2024. This expense represents actual tax leakage from entities that did not generate sufficient taxable income to utilize their localized losses. Reducing this penalty requires operational turnarounds at loss-making subsidiaries, without which reported effective tax rates will remain elevated above standard rates.
6.

According to the 2024 annual report, the non-offsetting tax expense was EUR 7.11m in FY2024 and EUR 2.88m in FY2023. In contrast, the FY2022 annual report showed a much larger non-offsetting tax penalty of EUR 10.22m. These fluctuations demonstrate how cyclical downturns in specific segments rapidly expand the tax penalty, causing severe volatility in reported earnings per share.

Portfolio cleanup dynamics: The S.M.A. Metalltechnik transaction highlights the broader tax implications of INDUS's active portfolio optimization. Exiting these loss-making entities not only stops operating losses but also allows the group to eventually realize non-recurring tax benefits. Consequently, restructuring the portfolio can provide substantial net income support even during challenging industrial downturns.

German Tax Code Context

12.
Loss utilization constraints: Utilizing trade tax loss carryforwards in Germany requires meeting strict corporate and entrepreneurial identity criteria. The Federal Tax Court's decision IV R 26/21 on 1 February 2024 ruled that trade tax losses can only be transferred to a partnership under specific continuation rules. Under Section 10a of the Trade Tax Law, the business identity must remain identical between the loss-incurring year and the utilization year. Because INDUS Holding AG's partnerships operate with high autonomy, meeting these requirements during reorganizations remains complex.
13.
Unrestricted carryforward terms: German tax regulations do not impose time limits on the utilization of trade tax loss carryforwards. INDUS Holding AG's 2025 annual report discloses that its unrecognized trade tax loss carryforwards can be carried forward indefinitely. However, actual realization of these deferred tax assets remains contingent on the municipal trade tax rates applicable at the time of future profits. This means the indefinite carryforward period provides long-term optionality but does not guarantee near-term relief.

Other side of the story: Some analysts suggest that the decentralized limited partnership structure provides long-term operational advantages that outweigh the trade tax penalty. INDUS Holding AG's 2025 annual report highlights that localized operational responsibility motivates management teams to act as true entrepreneurs. Furthermore, the localized tax depreciation from purchase price allocations provides immediate cash flow benefits that help fund local capital expenditure. This localized cash generation supports sustainable dividend payouts even when consolidated net income is temporarily pressured by the trade tax offset limitation.
