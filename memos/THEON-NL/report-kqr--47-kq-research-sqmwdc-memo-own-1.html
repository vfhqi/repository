Non-European investors face mandatory clearance when acquiring more than 10% of voting rights in any French listed entity. This rigorous oversight establishes a clear boundary for strategic defense investments in the country.

Section summary
  1. Cyprus corporate income tax law fully exempts gains from the sale of shares, shielding the potential Exosens disposal from any corporate-level tax liabilities.
  2. Capital Gains Tax does not apply to the disposal since Exosens is a French-listed company holding no real estate assets situated in Cyprus.

Special Contribution for Defence applies to local individuals' dividends but remains completely inapplicable to corporate-level capital gains on security transfers.

Corporate Income Tax Framework

1.
Strategic incorporation: Theon International was established as a Cyprus-domiciled holding entity specifically to serve as the management and finance hub of the group. The company completed its restructuring in September 2021 by acquiring Swiss-based Theon Sensors AG in a share-for-share exchange transaction. This corporate architecture ensures that foreign operational profits can flow upward to the Cyprus parent company with maximum tax efficiency. Consequently, Cyprus residency remains a cornerstone of the group's financial strategy for managing international equity holdings.
2.
Favourable treatment: Theon Sensors is domiciled in Cyprus where it benefits from a highly competitive tax framework. The parent entity, Theon International Plc, is subject to a corporate income tax rate of 12.5% in its home jurisdiction of Cyprus. This rate is significantly lower than the tax rates of 29.8% to 31.2% applied to its German operations. Therefore, maintaining the residency in Cyprus allows the company to optimize its international tax exposure effectively.
3.
Elevated guidance: Theon International's management has guided to a higher group effective tax rate in recent forecasts. Deutsche Bank Research reported on 10 November 2025 that the group guided its future income tax rate to be between 23% and 24% compared to 22% in FY2024. This increase reflects the growing portion of earnings coming from higher-tax jurisdictions like Germany. Consequently, this tax mix means the corporatelevel relief in Cyprus is critical for preserving earnings per share.
4.
Complete exemption: Theon International benefits from a full participation exemption on equity disposals under Cyprus tax law. Any gain realized from the disposal of shares or other 'securities' is fully exempt from Cyprus corporate income tax. This exemption applies regardless of the holding period, the size of the shareholding, or the trading nature of the gain. Thus, a sale of the Exosens stake would incur zero corporate income tax in Cyprus.
5.
Broad definition: The Cyprus tax code defines 'securities' widely to cover various equity-related instruments. This statutory definition explicitly encompasses common shares, options, debentures, and depositary receipts. Because the 9.8% holding in Exosens consists of standard listed common shares, it falls squarely within this protective tax definition. This broad classification ensures that any monetization path avoids complex local tax assessments in Cyprus.

Capital Gains and Defense Contribution

6.
Narrow scope: Cyprus Capital Gains Tax is highly restricted in its application to foreign assets. Under Capital Gains Tax Law 52/1980, this tax only applies to gains from real estate located in Cyprus or companies holding such property. Since Exosens is a French-domiciled entity with no Cyprus property, any disposal of its shares is completely outside the scope of this tax. This narrow scope guarantees that the disposal remains entirely exempt from capital gains duties.
7.
Safe listing: Cyprus tax regulations provide additional capital gains protections for shares listed on regulated markets. For shares listed on recognized exchanges, Cyprus law generally excludes them from local capital gains calculations even if underlying real estate conditions are met. Since Exosens is listed on Euronext Paris, this regulated status reinforces the exempt nature of the investment. Therefore, the public market listing safeguards the tax-free status of the transaction.
8.
Unrestricted flows: Cyprus tax policy encourages international investment by eliminating withholding taxes on outbound capital returns. There are no domestic withholding taxes on dividends, interest, or royalties paid to non-residents unless the underlying right is used inside Cyprus. This lack of restrictive withholding rules allows Theon International to distribute any realized cash from the Exosens monetization to global shareholders smoothly. Therefore, the Cyprus parent structure ensures frictionless capital distribution routes for international investors.
9.
Zero defense: Theon International's equity sales are not subject to the Cyprus Special Contribution for Defence. Cyprus legislation exempts capital gains on securities from the Special Contribution for Defence entirely. Although Cyprus companies must pay a 17% defense contribution on certain deemed dividend distributions, this does not apply to capital profits. This distinction ensures that the entire proceeds can be reinvested without defensive tax leakages.
10.
Contrary evidence: The Cyprus tax authorities have not issued a formal, binding list of recognized stock exchanges for tax exemptions. National Bank of Greece SA noted on 26 June 2025 that the lack of an official list of recognized stock exchanges may require companies to seek advance tax rulings for certainty. If Euronext Paris were ever deemed unrecognized, the tax treatment of the disposal could theoretically be open to challenge. While this risk is remote, the absence of explicit guidelines remains a minor source of regulatory ambiguity.
