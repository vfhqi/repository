To what extent, and in which direction, is competitive intensity changing?

C-4323rd of 46

Layer 1 · External change forces

Judgement: competitive intensity is rising slowly in the arena that matters most, the market to buy small UK and Nordic niche companies; it is broadly unchanged in the subsidiaries' product niches. The direction is mildly negative.

Teqnion competes in two markets. In its product niches, competition is fragmented and rational: subsidiaries sell custom, specification-driven products directly to B2B customers (94% of sales direct), no customer exceeds 10% of group sales, and there is no evidence of price wars. The pressure is from customers rather than rivals (Swedish builders' tendering).

In the acquisition market, competition is intensifying, and specifically in the UK, where Teqnion has placed its growth. Swedish acquirers are moving there because Sweden "is becoming unsustainably competitive" (former Bergman & Beving division head): Bergman & Beving completed six of eight deals in the UK in fiscal 2026, Beijer Alma made its first UK deal (MGA Controls, May 2026), the UK is Röko's largest market, and Addtech now completes about half its acquired volume outside the Nordics. A former Lagercrantz M&A head puts Nordic private multiples at 8x to 10x EBITDA, up from 4x to 6x; Morgan Stanley puts European private-equity business-services deals near 12x. Against that, Teqnion's evidence of discipline still holds: management claims about 5x to 6x, and 2026 acquisition cash outflows of about 230 MSEK for roughly 260 to 270 MSEK of revenue at around 20% margins imply roughly 4x to 5x upfront plus earn-outs. The small deal size (target post-tax profit 10 to 40 MSEK, companies with 30 to 50 MSEK turnover) remains below the threshold where larger peers and private equity compete hard, and such deals are "seldom subject to bidding wars" (expert).

The risk is a slow erosion of the UK entry-multiple arbitrage over 18 to 36 months rather than a sudden change. The earliest observable sign would be Teqnion's disclosed acquisition multiples or the ratio of upfront price plus earn-outs to acquired EBITA rising.

C-05

How are input costs, input availability and supplier concentration changing, and is the net effect a headwind or a tailwind?

B4710th of 46

Layer 1 · External change forces

Judgement: a mild headwind from metals, freight, energy and wages, largely offset by the China sourcing office and local price increases; supplier concentration is a unit-level risk, not a group risk.

Cost pressure has re-emerged in 2026: the Idun review records copper wire up 25% to 30% and hot-rolled steel coil up nearly 45% since mid-2025, and European road-freight contract rates up 17.6%; the Fitness for Fighting digest records producer energy prices up 10.8% on the Iran re-escalation. For the model: no gross-margin tailwind beyond a few MSEK of further sourcing savings; a 3- to 6-month margin lag if metal prices keep rising.

C-07

To what extent is a macro force acting on the company, on its sector and on its industry, and at which level does it bite hardest?

D3632nd of 46

Layer 1 · External change forces

Judgement: interest rates are the dominant macro force, and they bite hardest at the company level through financing cost and valuation, rather than through demand.

At the industry level, high rates suppress construction and capital spending; at sector level (Nordic serial acquirers) they compress multiples, since the Sector digest names the earnings-yield gap to swap rates as the warning sign; at company level they hit Teqnion three ways. First, financing cost: debt is floating, parent-company credit liabilities rose to 627 from 500 a year earlier, a growing share is in sterling at a Bank of England rate of 3.75% with hikes being pulled forward, and the Riksbank has strengthened its tightening signal (Fitness for Fighting digest, 16 October 2026). Third, currency: a stronger krona, which Morgan Stanley and Nordea forecast for 2026 to 2027, cuts the translated value of Väst's sterling profits, now about 45% of segment EBITA.

Demand is affected too (Swedish housing), but that is already in the base. The skew of the macro force over twelve months is negative.
