Section 2 – CPI-Linked Pricing and Inflation Contractual Durability

Section summary
  1. Contractual pricing mechanisms face increasing legal, political, and competitive pressures across European telecom markets.
  2. Hellenic Telecom benefits from favorable pricing dynamics in Greece because peers provide constructive market pricing protection.

Pricing Durability and Regulatory Risks

1.
Favorable domestic pricing: Hellenic Telecom operates within a constructive three-player Greek mobile market that allows for consistent tariff adjustments. Deutsche Bank reports that both Vodafone Greece and Nova raised their retail prices in line with consumer price inflation in early 2026. This second consecutive year of rival price hikes provides critical air cover for OTE to implement similar value-protecting measures without risking severe market share loss. Therefore, Greek pricing durability remains high unless competitive intensity unexpectedly escalates.
2.
Shrinking indexation steps: Telekom Austria demonstrates how inflation-linked price adjustments are fading as macroeconomic pressures cool down. Deutsche Bank highlights that Telekom Austria implemented a modest 2.9% indexation step linked to 2025 CPI starting April 2026. This adjustment is significantly lower than the 7.8% price increase enacted in April 2024, which means that indexation will support organic service revenues far less. Consequently, regional incumbents must rely on commercial upselling strategies to sustain average revenue per user.
3.
Competitive market dynamics: Telekom Austria faces severe retail competition in its domestic market which undermines the benefit of indexation. Deutsche Bank notes that Austria remains a very tough market for Telekom Austria because of intense competition and weak macroeconomic conditions. This deterioration occurred because competitive promotions and lower incoming rates offset positive indexation. This indicates that indexation alone cannot guarantee revenue growth when the underlying market environment is intensely promotional.
4.
Historical pricing comparisons: European telecom operators have historically struggled to generate positive top-line growth through pricing actions alone. Morgan Stanley indicates that European telcos averaged a negative 0.8% year-on-year sales growth over the past fifteen years. This long-term deflation was driven by price comparison websites, e-SIMs, and customer-friendly regulations that eroded historical backbook premium pricing. Thus, Hellenic Telecom's current ability to sustain annual price steps represents a major departure from broader European trends that may face medium-term reversion.

Regional Legal and Regulatory Precedents

5.
Polish indexation scrutiny: Orange Polska's contractual indexation is subject to intense regulatory oversight by local competition authorities. The company's annual report states that the Office of Competition and Consumer Protection is actively examining if Orange's inflation clauses violate collective consumer interests. Although Orange Polska successfully implemented a 3.6% price indexation on open-ended contracts in early 2026, the ongoing probe forced the company to recognize financial provisions. Thus, legal scrutiny creates material balance sheet risks for operators relying on contractual indexation.
6.
Austrian indexation litigation: Telekom Austria has faced persistent legal challenges from consumer protection groups regarding its historical indexation clauses. The Association for Consumer Information filed a lawsuit contesting these clauses, but the Commercial Court ruled in favor of A1 Austria in September 2025. Despite this initial victory, Telekom Austria stopped offering indexation clauses to new customers after February 2024 to mitigate risk. This shift demonstrates how legal uncertainty can dismantle long-term pricing structures.
7.
Eastern European ARPU trends: The effectiveness of indexation clauses varies widely across Eastern European operating segments. Telekom Austria reports that while it implemented indexation in Slovenia and Croatia, it avoided indexation in Bulgaria for competitive reasons. Instead, the Bulgarian business successfully grew ARPU by upselling the existing customer base to premium packages. This variance demonstrates that operators must tailor pricing mechanisms to local regulatory and competitive environments to sustain profitability.
8.
German pricing difficulties: Vodafone Germany illustrates the severe revenue consequences when regulatory changes and price constraints collide. Light Reading reports that Vodafone Germany's third-quarter revenue fell by 7.6% because of legislative changes that outlawed bulk television contracting in multi-dwelling units. This structural reform halving Vodafone's MDU TV customer base shows that regulatory interventions can easily erase stable revenue streams. Therefore, European telcos struggle to recoup infrastructure costs in markets where direct price increases are legally blocked or reversed.

Consumer Behavior and Cost of Living

9.
Prepay to postpaid migration: Hellenic Telecom actively mitigates retail pricing pressures by steering consumers toward higher-value contracts. Deutsche Bank indicates that OTE consistently grows mobile service revenue by migrating customers from prepay to postpay plans, which generates a €3 to €4 monthly ARPU uplift. OTE's postpaid mobile base expanded by 7% in fiscal year 2025, while the total mobile customer count fell by 1%. This structural shift ensures that aggregate mobile service revenues grow even during cost-of-living squeezes.
10.
Cost of living constraints: Squeezed household budgets alter how European consumers choose and retain telecom services. Morgan Stanley's consumer survey highlights that the rising cost of utilities is a primary consumer concern, forcing households to trim non-essential spending. While telecom services enjoy utility-like status, price-sensitive users tend to spin down to discount brands or wholesale offerings during economic downturns. This trend means that incumbents must work harder to retain premium customers through converging family plans.
11.
Value of convergent bundles: High penetration of bundled services acts as a critical shield against customer churn during inflationary squeezes. Morgan Stanley argues that convergent bundling creates substantial customer stickiness because switching providers becomes highly complex and inconvenient. Customers risk losing multi-product discounts, content subscriptions, and the convenience of a unified bill if they switch. Therefore, OTE's emphasis on fixed-mobile convergence helps insulate its contractual pricing power from economic shocks.
12.
Rising retention expenses: European operators face escalating commercial costs as they focus on preserving their premium customer base. Deutsche Bank notes that as telecom markets saturate, operators must transition promotional spending from customer acquisition to long-term subscriber retention. This shift protects the high-value backbook but inevitably compresses margins because marketing costs and retention subsidies rise. Consequently, OTE's earnings per share expansion depends on balancing price steps against customer churn in a tight economy.

Section 3 – Public ICT, Systems Integration, and the RRF Cliff

Section summary
  1. Hellenic Telecom's public ICT revenue faces transition risks as the EU-funded Recovery and Resilience Facility wind-down approaches in late 2026.
  2. Domestic IT peers are building robust private sector backlogs and securing long-term service agreements to offset potential public sector spending cliffs.

OTE's ICT Performance and Post-RRF Outlook

1.
Accelerating ICT growth: OTE has delivered remarkable short-term growth in its Systems Solutions division by executing critical digital projects. According to Hellenic Telecom's Q2 2026 report, other revenues grew by 37.8%, driven by a 64.3% surge in Systems Solutions revenue. Morgan Stanley's note of 29 July 2026 confirms this momentum was bolstered by the acceleration of public sector digitalization before the RRF deadline. This rapid frontloading supports intermediate earnings but sets a high baseline that could trigger a revenue correction when funding expires.
2.
Advanced digital offerings: OTE is leveraging artificial intelligence and private cloud solutions to cultivate non- public sector revenue streams. In its Q2 2026 report, OTE announced the launch of advanced Data Center services, including GPU-as-a-Service for AI workloads. Management's goal is to transition the group into an AInative organization to improve operational cost structures and attract high-value enterprise IT spend. This technological pivot helps insulate OTE from public spending declines by capturing growing corporate demand for AI infrastructure.
3.
Lower margin profile: The system solutions business remains structurally less profitable than Hellenic Telecom's core connectivity business. Deutsche Bank's report of 21 June 2024 notes that OTE's ICT revenue historically generates a 20% EBITDA margin, which is far lower than the 42% margin of OTE's Greek operations. Management is actively focusing on shifting its business mix toward private enterprises and expanding its cybersecurity portfolio. This product transition is crucial because lower public contract volumes will dilute aggregate corporate margins unless OTE scales high-margin managed IT services.
4.
Mitigation through expansion: Hellenic Telecom is leveraging non-Greek markets and recurring maintenance contracts to soften the post-RRF drop. Deutsche Bank's research on 24 April 2026 highlights that OTE's international ICT revenue grew 25% in 2025, representing 22% of its total ICT turnover. Additionally, the company will continue earning recurring revenue from maintaining infrastructure built during the RRF phase. If international demand or public maintenance contracts fail to materialize, OTE's overall revenue growth will stagnate.
5.
Contrary evidence: The anticipated RRF cliff edge may be entirely avoided due to significant execution delays that push actual spending into future years. Morgan Stanley's report of 22 May 2026 highlights a substantial two-year lag between the receipt of RRF funds and their actual utilization by final beneficiaries. In fact, the broker estimates that only 56% of received funds had been utilized by late 2025, which means that an extensive pipeline of project spending will persist through 2027 and 2028. Consequently, the near-term risk of a sharp revenue drop in 2027 remains highly exaggerated.

Domestic Competitors and Backlog Durability

6.
Robust contract backlogs: Quest Holdings is utilizing a deep backlog of contracted projects to secure long-term digital services revenue. Quest Holdings reported in its 2025 financial statements that its IT services unit, Uni Systems, has accumulated a backlog exceeding €750 million. Out of this total backlog, only €100 million is directly tied to RRF public contracts, with the remainder spread across private and international clients. This diversified backlog ensures that the company's IT services segment remains highly insulated from public funding cliffs.
7.
Funding transition strategies: Space Hellas utilizes long-term subsidized financing to transition its operations toward advanced private sector technologies. According to the Space Hellas annual report of 24 July 2026, the company issued a ten-year common bond loan funded by the RRF and Alpha Bank to finance its internal digital transformation. This long-term subsidized capital allows the integrator to upgrade its network security capabilities and broaden its private enterprise portfolio before public subsidies expire. Consequently, Space Hellas maintains high financial flexibility to adapt to changing domestic demand.
8.
Delayed project pipelines: The slow initial execution of public digitalization contracts serves as a revenue stabilizer during transition periods. Quest Holdings' management reported during its Q3 2023 earnings call that less than 10% of its early revenues were derived from active RRF contracts. This administrative delay means that the bulk of contracted public IT projects are only now being fully executed and will support top-line revenues through late 2026. Thus, initial administrative bottlenecks have paradoxically distributed the public spending peak over a longer period.
9.
Shifting market demand: The domestic Greek IT market has already begun adjusting as massive public sector RRF projects reach normalization. In Quest Holdings' H1 2026 report, management noted that the Greek IT market contracted by 13% compared to a highly elevated base in early 2025. Despite this market-wide contraction, Uni Systems grew its first-half sales by 21.1% to €159.4 million. This divergence shows that wellpositioned integrators can outperform broader public digitalization roll-offs by capturing private market share.
10.
Extended public contracts: Space Hellas is mitigating post-RRF cliff risks by securing multi-year public service and maintenance contracts. Space Hellas signed a contract with the Ministry of Foreign Affairs in July 2025 for the technical support of the National Visa Information System. The contract is valued at €7.4 million and runs until December 31, 2029, with an option for an additional €7.4 million. Securing long-term service agreements allows integrators to generate highly visible recurring revenue that smooths out the decline in new public construction work.

Section 4 – Wholesale Migration, Fiber Overbuild, and Challenger Choices

Section summary
  1. Competing network migrations pose a long-term challenge but the PPC-Vodafone joint venture consolidates the wholesale landscape.
  2. Italian precedents demonstrate that wholesale-only overbuild deadlocks often resolve through cooperative geographic commercial agreements.

Greek Wholesale Landscape and PPC Joint Venture

1.
Strategic joint venture: In June 2026, Vodafone Greece and Public Power Corporation signed heads of terms to create a 50:50 fiber joint venture. This partnership combines Vodafone's Fiber2All and PPC's FiberGrid networks, covering 1.6 million homes on a combined basis. It intends to provide open wholesale access to other internet service providers across Greece. This joint venture helps de-risk competitor network deployment costs while creating a formidable wholesale competitor to Hellenic Telecom.
2.
Competitor network rollout: PPC FiberGrid continues its aggressive fiber-to-the-home expansion using existing electricity distribution infrastructure to lower costs. PPC reports that its network reached 2.05 million households and businesses in H1 2026. With an average cost of only €160 per home passed, PPC targets reaching 3.8 million households by 2028. This rapid rollout means competitors can bypass OTE's copper and fiber networks much faster.
3.
Wholesale volume discounts: Hellenic Telecom protected its wholesale revenues by signing volume-based FTTH agreements in late 2024. Deutsche Bank notes that OTE's pricing framework offers up to 28% wholesale discounts to Vodafone Greece and Nova. This commercial framework binds these operators to OTE's fiber footprint, making it less attractive to migrate to PPC's network. Unless competitors completely write off their OTE commitments, OTE's near-term wholesale revenue remains protected.

A new owner of Nova could re-evaluate network sharing and wholesale agreements, potentially accelerating volume migration off OTE to maximize proprietary cash flows.

Italian Precedents and Wholesale Deadlocks

7.
Italian NetCo separation: Telecom Italia provides a critical historical precedent after completing the sale of its NetCo business unit on July 1, 2024. Following this separation, FiberCop became the exclusive wholesale provider of ADSL and FTTC and the leading provider of FTTH in Italy. TIM signed a Master Services Agreement to acquire wholesale fixed-line access from FiberCop to serve its retail base. This structural separation proves that incumbents can successfully transition to wholesale-purchasing entities without losing operational viability.
8.
Quality of service risks: Transitioning to third-party wholesale networks exposes retail operators to severe operational and customer relationship risks. Deutsche Bank highlights that Telecom Italia's Q2 2026 service revenues were negatively impacted by higher fixed churn and net line losses. TIM management blamed this performance on worsening service quality from FiberCop. This operational friction shows that outsourcing network operations can damage retail broadband market shares.
9.
Commercial deadlock alternatives: Instead of a full merger, Italian fiber operators have explored geographical commercial agreements to resolve infrastructure duplication. Deutsche Bank reports that FiberCop and Open Fiber discussed a deal in April 2026 to focus FiberCop on grey areas and Open Fiber on subsidized white areas. This commercial compromise suggests that Greek operators may avoid costly network overbuild by dividing competitive territories.
10.
Fiber investment hurdles: Pure wholesale models face severe deployment and monetization hurdles that delay network targets and depress financial returns. Deutsche Bank reports that KKR's initial investment assumptions for FiberCop were 1.6 million active lines below target by mid-2026. Similarly, Open Fiber faced significant delays, upgrading only half of its targeted EU-funded buildings by mid-2025. These hurdles indicate that wholesale-only platforms struggle to monetize massive fiber-to-the-home capital expenditures.

If Vodafone and Nova migrate their customer bases off OTE's network faster than anticipated, OTE stands to lose up to €250 million of high-margin wholesale revenue. This acceleration would severely dent OTE's projected earnings per share expansion.

Morgan Stanley argues that this joint venture will ease investor worries about chaotic fiber overbuild in Greece. However, if the joint venture triggers aggressive commercial wholesale discounts to attract Nova as an anchor tenant, OTE will face severe wholesale volume losses. Consequently, the consolidation of OTE's rivals on a neutral wholesale network remains a major medium-term threat that could undermine OTE's long-term profitability and EPS growth.

Section 5 – Financial Optimization and Balance Sheet Drivers

Section summary
  1. Positive balance sheet optimization enablers that previously drove Hellenic Telecom's earnings are now shifting as cheap debt matures.
  2. Upcoming domestic spectrum renewals are expected to be highly cost-effective and fully offset by exceptional tax benefits.
  3. Ongoing fiber-to-the-home rollouts will require elevated and steady capital expenditure before stepping down after 2028.

Balance Sheet and Refinancing Pressures

1.
Slowing interest savings: OTE's long-term trend of declining net interest expenses is reaching a critical inflection point as historical low-cost financing matures. Management reported that a €500 million bond carrying a very low interest rate of 0.875% is scheduled to mature in September 2026. Refinancing this debt in the current higher interest rate environment means that interest cost savings have run their course. This shift will pressure earnings per share unless OTE offsets higher coupon rates through stronger operational cash flow.
2.
Debt cost transition: Refinancing matured debt will mark a structural transition in OTE's capital cost structure. OTE's weighted average cost of debt declined previously because of favorable bond redemptions, such as a €374.6 million bond repaid in 2022. However, replacing ultra-low-coupon debt with fresh issuances will end the era of cost optimization. This means the balance sheet will no longer provide passive support to OTE's net margins.
3.
Normalizing depreciation benefits: Hellenic Telecom's historical depreciation and amortization declines are stabilizing as domestic infrastructure investments accelerate. OTE's historical D&A trend reflected previous asset write-downs and portfolio pruning, including the Romanian mobile segment separation. However, the massive ongoing domestic fiber buildout is expanding the tangible asset base and stabilizing the future depreciation run rate. This stabilization means that D&A reductions will no longer act as material passive earnings drivers.
4.
Comparative leverage strength: Hellenic Telecom maintains a highly conservative balance sheet that provides substantial protection compared to international sector peers.
