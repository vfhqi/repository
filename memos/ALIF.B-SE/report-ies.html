Addlife (ALIF.B-SE): what the Q3 2026 results do to the case

These results leave the case where it was, a park on the return, and press on it from both sides at once: the balance sheet and the deal count came in a little better than the case assumed, the quality of the earnings beat and the Medtech margin came in worse, and the share price has moved about 12 percent further from the case's entry level, so the net effect is slightly negative and nothing in the record would move a full grade today.

The challenge

Three readings the case does not hold. Two cut against it, and the third cuts for it in a way that still does not help at this price. The obvious bull reading, that cash conversion has turned, is not one of them: the case already holds that Q4 is the cash quarter and that nine-month cash tells you little, and a flat SEK 505m against SEK 504m is exactly that.

First, the earnings beat is an earn-out gain, and under the case's own terms an earn-out gain is an invalidation signal, not good news. Profit was SEK 23m ahead of consensus; adjusted EBITA was SEK 1m ahead; DNB Carnegie identifies SEK 32m of earn-out revaluation inside reported EBITA. On those three figures the whole of the beat is the revaluation. A reduction in contingent consideration is booked when the acquired company is now expected to earn less than the earn-out assumed. The case's invalidation level for the acquisition driver reads "acquisition multiples above 8x EV/EBITDA, or earn-out targets missed", and the case's own earnings-quality note records that earn-out reversals credited SEK 101m in 2023 and SEK 85m in Q3 2022 to operating profit, the years in which the 2021 deals went wrong. The strongest form of this reading is that the first sign of a current-programme deal under-earning has arrived dressed as a 22 percent beat, and that the case's post-amortisation EPS path now carries about SEK 0.26 a share before tax (SEK 32m on the case's 122.1m shares, our arithmetic) of non-repeating gain in Q3 that its modal arithmetic does not contain. For it to be right, the revaluation has to reflect a lowered profit expectation at an acquired company rather than a discount-rate or currency effect, and it has to relate to a deal of the current vintage rather than a 2021 residual. The review does not say which deal, why, or whether management was asked. That silence is itself a finding: the one line that connects this quarter to the case's invalidation test is the one line nobody has explained.

Second, the margin engine has narrowed to one segment and to acquisition mix, which is the reading the case conceded as its strongest competitor and held off only because both segments were rising. The case's margin item rests on "ten consecutive quarters of expansion, with both segments rising". Medtech, 62 percent of sales, fell year on year in the first half (the case's record: 12.8 percent against 13.5 percent) and fell again in Q3 (the review: 11.3 percent against 11.6 percent), on 5 percent organic growth, so volume came without operating leverage. Labtech's 1.7-point gain came on 2 percent organic growth and 5 percent acquired growth, and management attributed the group's unusually high operating costs to the specialists needed to support advanced products. The strongest form of this reading is the pre-mortem's "False Friend", which the case named and rejected: the pruning-led margin story is finished in the larger segment, and group margin expansion from here is bought, not earned. The arithmetic adds weight. The case's modal FY2026 margin of 12.6 percent on SEK 10,800m of revenue requires Q4 adjusted EBITA of about SEK 389m on about SEK 2,835m of sales, a 13.7 percent quarter, against 12.4 percent in Q4 2025 (our arithmetic from the review's nine-month figures and the case's full-year figures). That is improbable. It also means the case's alarm level for the margin, below 12.5 percent in any quarter other than Q3, is live for Q4, since last year's Q4 was 12.4 percent. For the reading to be right, Medtech's decline has to be structural (the commoditised supplies mix and the cost of advanced-product support) rather than Spanish strikes and summer surgical timing; the review carries both explanations and quantifies neither.

Third, and cutting the other way, the compounder branch is arriving earlier than the case allowed, and the market is already paying for it. The case put the odds of reaching and holding 6 to 8 deals a year at about 40 percent and the compounder multiple branch at about 14 percent. The review shows six deals in twelve months, four in 2026, a decentralised deal funnel that is a structural change in sourcing capacity, stated margins above 12 percent at below 7x, SEK 36m of EBITA from the 2026 acquisitions on SEK 114m of sales, leverage at 2.5x where the sell-side feared 2.8x, and the refinancing done. The case's own higher-price re-entry condition, four or more deals announced in the six months to March 2027 at stated margins above segment averages with leverage at or below 2.5x, has one deal (Lehnen, 13 October) and the leverage condition met exactly at the September balance sheet. The strongest form of this reading is that the pace is now probable rather than possible and the compounder framing is being adopted (the price up about 12 percent since the memo, relative strength 78, the consensus target SEK 201). For it to be right the Q4 cash has to arrive (DNB Carnegie expects 2.3x at year-end), Lehnen's consideration has to land without pushing leverage back above 2.5x, and the pace has to hold through 2027 at sub-7x multiples. But the reading changes the odds, not the verdict. On the case's own branch arithmetic, the compounder branch's three-year value of SEK 249.6 plus SEK 5.80 of dividends from SEK 190.6 is about 10 percent a year (our arithmetic), the bottom of the case's D band, where at SEK 170.7 it was about 14 percent, a C. The reading the case rated unlikely has, if it is right, already been priced past the point where it pays.

What would settle it

  • The earn-out revaluation. Which deal, what changed in its expected earnings, and whether the SEK 32m is a performance revision or a discounting effect. The company has not disclosed this and has not committed to. If the second review does not surface it from the Q3 report's notes or a broker, it stays an open question of the kind nothing answers until the annual report, and it should be put to management directly.
  • The year-end report on 3 February 2027. It carries the four tests the case and the review both name: Q4 operating cash flow against the roughly SEK 584m the case's FY2026 conversion alarm needs and the SEK 888m of Q4 2025; year-end leverage against the case's 2.5x alarm and DNB Carnegie's 2.3x; the Q4 group margin against the case's 12.5 percent non-Q3 alarm, with Q4 2025 at 12.4 percent; and Medtech's margin against its own 12.5 percent alarm. It also tests the first reading: a further revaluation, or a disclosed earn-out shortfall, would settle it one way; a clean quarter the other.
  • Deal announcements between now and March 2027. The case's re-entry count stands at one of four. Each announcement should carry a stated margin against segment and, where given, a multiple; the company usually gives the first and not the second.
  • The second review, four working days on. It can carry what this one could not: post-results estimates and targets from Redeye and the third broker, the post-amortisation consensus series on the case's basis (the only post-results EPS figures to hand are DNB Carnegie's on the adjusted basis), and the dashboard's revision data with the 22 October note inside it. It cannot carry Q4 cash, the identity of the revalued earn-out, Medtech's next margin, or any company disclosure, because the company issues nothing between now and 3 February except deal announcements. Only after it should a memo update be considered, and on this evidence the update would most probably re-state the park at a higher price with a lower return, not change it.
